From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 6.2.3 Continuity of invariants

In this section, we prove the continuity of a few invariants of the singularities with respect to $d_ S$.

Theorem 6.2.29. Let $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then for any prime divisor $E$ over $X$, we have

6.77
\begin{equation} \lim _{j\in I}\nu (\varphi _ j,E)=\nu (\varphi ,E). \end{equation}

Proof. First observe that since $d_ S$ is a pseudometric, it suffices to prove (6.77) when $I=\mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ as partially ordered sets.

By corollary 6.2.19, after adding a Kähler form to $\theta $, we may assume that the masses of $\varphi _ j$ and of $\varphi $ are bounded from below by a positive constant.

By theorem 6.2.27, we may assume that $\varphi _ j$ and $\varphi $ are both $\mathcal{I}$-model and hence model. When proving (6.77), we are free to pass to subsequences.

By proposition 6.2.12 and a sandwich argument, we may assume that the sequence $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is either increasing or decreasing. In the increasing case, the Lelong numbers $\nu (\varphi _ j,E)$ decrease to a limit at least $\nu (\varphi ,E)$, while $L^1$-convergence gives the opposite inequality by upper semicontinuity of Lelong numbers. In the decreasing case, (6.77) follows from proposition 3.1.26. $\square$

Theorem 6.2.30. Let $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Assume that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Then

6.78
\begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ j}\to \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi },\quad \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\to \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \end{equation}
Recall the volume is defined in definition 3.2.9. In fact, we do not have to assume the positivity of the mass of $\varphi $. The proof of the general statement is slightly more involved. See corollary 7.3.7 below.

Proof. The latter part of (6.78) is just a special case of theorem 6.2.17. It remains to prove the former part.

We may therefore assume that $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n>0$ for all $j\in I$ after passing to a tail. Then by theorem 6.2.27, we have

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Therefore, the first part of (6.78) follows again from theorem 6.2.17. $\square$

Next we show that $d_ S$-convergent sequences have a sort of quasi-equisingular property (cf. (1.40)).

Theorem 6.2.31. Let $\varphi _ j,\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ ($j\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$). Assume that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Then for each $\lambda '>\lambda >0$, there is $j_0>0$ so that for $j\geq j_0$,

6.79
\begin{equation} \mathcal{I}(\lambda ' \varphi _ j)\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi ). \end{equation}

Proof. Fix $\lambda '>\lambda >0$. We want to find $j_0>0$ so that for $j\geq j_0$, (6.79) holds.

Step 1. We first assume that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities.

Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a log resolution of $\varphi $ and let $E_1,\ldots ,E_ N$ be all prime divisors in the polar locus of $\varphi $ on $Y$. Recall that by theorem 1.4.15, a non-zero local holomorphic function $f$ lies in the right-hand side of (6.79) if and only if for all $i=1,\ldots ,N$,

6.80
\begin{equation} \operatorname{ord}_{E_ i}(f)>\lambda \nu (\varphi ,E_ i)-A_ X(E_ i), \end{equation}

where $A_ X$ denotes the log discrepancy. Similarly, if $f$ lies in the left-hand side of (6.79), then for all $i=1,\ldots ,N$,

\[ \operatorname{ord}_{E_ i}(f)> \lambda '\nu (\varphi _ j,E_ i)-A_ X(E_ i). \]

As Lelong numbers are continuous with respect to $d_ S$ by theorem 6.2.29, we can find $j_0>0$ so that when $j\geq j_0$, $\lambda '\nu (\varphi _ j,E_ i)\geq \lambda \nu (\varphi ,E_ i)$ for all $i$. In particular, (6.80) follows.

Step 2. We handle the general case.

By corollary 6.2.19, we are free to increase $\theta $ and assume that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current.

Take a quasi-equisingular approximation $(\psi _ k)_ k$ of $\varphi $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The existence is guaranteed by theorem 1.6.10. Take $\lambda ''\in (\lambda ,\lambda ')$. Then by definition of quasi-equisingular approximations (cf. (1.40)), we can find $k>0$ so that

\[ \mathcal{I}(\lambda ''\psi _ k)\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi ). \]

Observe that $\psi _ k\geq \varphi $ for all $k>0$. By proposition 6.2.23, we then find that for any $k>0$, we have $\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ k\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \lor \psi _ k=\psi _ k$ as $j\to \infty $. By Step 1, we can find $j_0>0$ so that for $j\geq j_0$,

\[ \mathcal{I}\Bigl (\lambda ' (\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ k)\Bigr )\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda ''\psi _ k). \]

It follows that for $j\geq j_0$,

\[ \mathcal{I}(\lambda ' \varphi _ j)\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi ). \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Note. Adrien Douady (1935–2006) was a French mathematician known for his pioneering work in complex dynamics and fractal geometry. Along with John H.
