Hessian mass and global comparison

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 12.12 Hessian mass and global comparison

The maximum inequality compares measures on complementary Borel sets. Integrating it leaves a difference of total masses. The recursive currents express this difference through the complex Hessians of the background powers, extending the mass calculation of chapter 2 to bounded admissible potentials. Their vanishing characterizes the unmodified comparison principle. When they do not vanish, a small sublevel set limits the change made by the maximum; the resulting torsion error can still be absorbed after rescaling the upper potential.

For bounded admissible $t_1,\ldots ,t_ m$, put

\[ \mathcal M_ m(t_1,\ldots ,t_ m)=\int _ XQ_ m(t_1,\ldots ,t_ m), \qquad H_ m(t)=Q_ m(t,\ldots ,t). \]

Here $H_ m(t)$ is a finite positive Radon measure and $\mathcal M_ m$ is the total mass of a mixed positive measure, by 12.8.3. For a nonempty subset $I=\{ i_1<\cdots <i_ q\} \subset \{ 1,\ldots ,m\} $, put $i(I)=i_ q$ and $I^-=I\setminus \{ i_ q\} $. Use $D_{I^-}$ for the recursive current with those ordered inputs, with $D_\varnothing =1$.

Lemma 12.12.1. For every bounded admissible list,

12.32
\begin{equation} \label{eq:hessian-bounded-mass-expansion} \mathcal M_ m(t_1,\ldots ,t_ m) =\int _ X\omega ^ n+ \sum _{\varnothing \ne I\subset \{ 1,\ldots ,m\} } \int _ X t_{i(I)}D_{I^-}\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-|I|}). \end{equation}

Every integral on the right is an integral of bounded canonical values against signed measure coefficients.

Proof. Expand $Q_ m$ using (12.8). The term for $I$ pairs $D_ I$ with the smooth form $\omega ^{n-|I|}$. By (12.4),

\[ \langle D_ I,\omega ^{n-|I|}\rangle =\langle t_{i(I)}D_{I^-},\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-|I|})\rangle . \]

Since $|I^-|\leq m-1$, 12.8.2 gives measure coefficients for $D_{I^-}$; their bounded product with $t_{i(I)}$ is well defined. Thus the displayed pairing is exactly the integral in the sum, and is finite on compact $X$. The empty subset contributes $\int _ X\omega ^ n$. $\square$

Let $\mu _ r$ be the repeated-slot mixed measure from 12.11.2, for fixed $r$ and $z_1,\ldots ,z_{m-r}$. The total-mass difference below therefore has the explicit expression (12.32) for the two indicated lists.

Proposition 12.12.2. If $u,v$ are bounded admissible and $w=\max \{ u,v\} $, then

\[ \int _{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm d\mu _ r(v) \leq \int _{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm d\mu _ r(u) +\int _ X\mathrm d\mu _ r(w)-\int _ X\mathrm d\mu _ r(u). \]

Proof. Set $E=\{ u<v\} $. The contact-set version of 12.11.2 gives

\[ \mu _ r(w)\geq \mathbf1_{X\setminus E}\mu _ r(u) +\mathbf1_ E\mu _ r(v). \]

All these measures are finite and positive. Integrating on $X$ and using $\mu _ r(u)(X\setminus E)=\mu _ r(u)(X)-\mu _ r(u)(E)$ gives exactly the assertion. No boundary or closed-background hypothesis enters this calculation. $\square$

The unmodified global comparison principle holds precisely when the powers occurring in this mass formula are $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed. Only the powers $n-m,\ldots ,n-1$ occur at Hessian degree $m$.

Theorem 12.12.3. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-q})=0$ for every $1\leq q\leq m$;

  2. $\int _ XH_ m(u)$ is independent of the smooth admissible potential $u$;

  3. $\mathcal M_ m(t_1,\ldots ,t_ m)=\int _ X\omega ^ n$ for every bounded admissible list;

  4. every pair of bounded admissible $u,v$ satisfies

    \[ \int _{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(v)\leq \int _{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(u). \]

Under these conditions, for every $1\leq r\leq m$ and every fixed bounded admissible list $z_1,\ldots ,z_{m-r}$, one also has

\[ \int _{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm d\mu _ r(v) \leq \int _{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm d\mu _ r(u). \]

Proof. The first condition implies the third by (12.32). The third implies the second by taking all inputs equal to a smooth potential.

To prove that the second implies the first, fix any smooth real $f$. For all sufficiently small real $t$ of either sign, $\omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ is positive, so $tf$ is strictly admissible. The polynomial

\[ \int _ X(\omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m} =\sum _{q=0}^ m\binom mq t^ q \int _ X(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ q\wedge \omega ^{n-q} \]

is constant on an interval. Consequently every coefficient with $q\geq 1$ vanishes. Polarizing that coefficient by replacing $f$ with $\sum _{a=1}^ q s_ af_ a$ gives

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ q \wedge \omega ^{n-q}=0 \]

for all smooth real $f_ a$. Integration by parts in the first factor, using lemma 2.1.1, gives

\[ \int _ Xf_1\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_2\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ q \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-q})=0. \]

Since $f_1$ is arbitrary, the smooth top-degree form paired with it vanishes pointwise. At each point the other complex Hessians can be prescribed by real quadratic functions times a cutoff. Complexifying this multilinear identity gives all coordinate $(q-1,q-1)$-forms, as in the proof of theorem 2.2.2. They detect every coefficient of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-q})$, so that form vanishes. When $q=1$, there are no other Hessians and the same conclusion follows directly from the arbitrariness of $f_1$.

Under the third condition, $\mu _ r(w)$ and $\mu _ r(u)$ have the same total mass. 12.12.2 proves the mixed comparison assertion, and $r=m$ gives the fourth condition. Finally, suppose the fourth condition holds and fix smooth admissible $u,v$. Choose $C$ so large that $u<v+C$ everywhere. Constant invariance gives $H_ m(v+C)=H_ m(v)$, so comparison gives $\int _ XH_ m(v)\leq \int _ XH_ m(u)$. Exchanging $u,v$ gives equality, which is the second condition. $\square$

The same varying background that obstructs Monge–Ampère comparison also obstructs comparison at each intermediate Hessian degree.

Example 12.12.4. Let $X=(\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z))^ n$, with $z_ j=x_ j+\mathrm i y_ j$, and put

\[ \beta _ j=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j, \qquad b=2+\cos (2\pi x_1), \qquad \omega =\beta _1+b\beta _2+\sum _{j=3}^ n\beta _ j. \]

For $|t|<\pi ^{-2}$ the potential $u_ t=t\cos (2\pi x_1)$ is smooth strictly admissible, and, with $B_ n=\int _ X\beta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \beta _ n$,

\[ \int _ X H_ m(u_ t)=n!\left(2-\frac{m\pi ^2t}{2n}\right)B_ n. \]

Thus every $-\pi ^{-2}<t<0$, together with a constant $C>|t|$, gives a pair $u=0$, $v=u_ t+C$ that violates the unmodified global comparison inequality.

Proof. As in example 2.3.1, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t=-\pi ^2t\cos (2\pi x_1)\beta _1$. The coefficient of $\beta _1$ in $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t$ is strictly positive in the stated range, and all the other coefficients are positive. Since $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^2=0$, expansion gives

\[ H_ m(u_ t)=\omega ^ n+m\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t\wedge \omega ^{n-1} =n!b\left(1-\frac mn\pi ^2t\cos (2\pi x_1)\right) \beta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \beta _ n. \]

The period averages of $b$ and $b\cos (2\pi x_1)$ are respectively $2$ and $1/2$, proving the mass formula. For $t<0$ this mass is strictly larger than $\int _ XH_ m(0)=2n!B_ n$. Meanwhile $C>|t|$ gives $\{ 0<u_ t+C\} =X$, so the comparison inequality for this pair would assert the opposite ordering of these masses. $\square$
