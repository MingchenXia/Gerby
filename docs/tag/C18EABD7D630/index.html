Curvature pairs and positive lower mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.16 Curvature pairs and positive lower mass

On a threefold, the lower mass can be bounded below by a curvature condition. Consider a principal elliptic bundle over a Kähler surface, with a connection form $\eta $ whose curvature has real and imaginary parts $\alpha $ and $\beta $. If $\alpha ^2+\beta ^2$ is semipositive, the split metric $g$ built from the base metric and $\eta $ satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g^2)=0$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\leq 0$. A cubic energy identity for bounded potentials then shows that no bounded potential has mass below $\int _ Xg^3$, and the determinant estimate of theorem 26.8.2 transfers this lower bound to every Hermitian form on the manifold. The same curvature sign excludes pluriclosed metrics on these manifolds.

In this section $X$ is a connected compact complex threefold and $\mathrm dV$ is a smooth positive probability volume form. Products of bounded potentials are those of proposition 1.7.1. For a Hermitian form $h$, $\mathscr G_ h$ denotes the compact convex set of normalized Gauduchon currents of section 4.4, and $k_ T$ is the determinant density of $T\in \mathscr G_ h$ relative to $\mathrm dV$, as in theorem 26.8.1.
