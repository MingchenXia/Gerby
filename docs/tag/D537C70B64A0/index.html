27.6 Partial kernels under a modificationD537C70B64A0

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.6 Partial kernels under a modification

A modification identifies holomorphic sections, but it changes the integrability condition that defines multiplier ideals; the correction is a twist by the relative canonical bundle, through the Jacobian. If the relative canonical bundle carries the metric determined by two volume forms, the same Jacobian factor makes the identification of section spaces an isometry. The partial kernel measures downstairs are then exactly the pushforwards of those upstairs, and the convergence theorem on compact Kähler manifolds descends to every compact complex manifold that admits a Kähler modification.

Let $\pi :Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds. We use the exceptional set $S\subset X$, its inverse image $D=\pi ^{-1}(S)\subset Y$, the relative canonical bundle $R_\pi $ and its Jacobian section $J_\pi $ from section 1.22.7, together with the transformation of multiplier ideals in lemma 1.22.15 and the invariance of the multiplier-ideal order in lemma 1.22.16.

Proposition 27.6.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For $v\in C(X)$ let $E_ X(v)=E_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(v)$ be the partial envelope with obstacle $v$ on the whole of $X$. Then

\[ E_{\pi ^*\theta ,Y}[\varphi \circ \pi ]_{\mathcal I}(v\circ \pi ) =E_ X(v)\circ \pi . \]

Moreover, for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ the non-pluripolar measures satisfy

\[ \pi _*\mathrm{MA}_{\pi ^*\theta }(u\circ \pi ) =\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u) \]

as positive Borel measures, possibly of infinite total mass.

Proof. By proposition 1.22.12, $a\mapsto a\circ \pi $ is a bijection from $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ onto $\mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$. For a continuous obstacle on the whole manifold, the quasi-everywhere and the pointwise obstacle conditions are equivalent, as recalled in section 1.19, and since $\pi $ is surjective, $a\leq v$ on $X$ if and only if $a\circ \pi \leq v\circ \pi $ on $Y$. Together with lemma 1.22.16, this identifies the two families of competitors. By theorem 27.3.1, each partial envelope is the largest member of its family, which proves the envelope identity; no interchange of pullback and regularized supremum is needed.

The map $\pi $ is a biholomorphism from $Y\setminus D$ onto $X\setminus S$, so locality of the non-pluripolar product identifies the two measures there. Neither measure charges the analytic sets $D$ and $S$. Testing against nonnegative Borel functions, or exhausting the complements by increasing compact sets, proves the pushforward identity, including the case of infinite total mass. $\square$

Choose smooth positive probability volumes $\nu _ X$ on $X$ and $\nu _ Y$ on $Y$. They determine a smooth Hermitian metric $h_ R$ on $R_\pi $ such that

27.23
\begin{equation} |J_\pi |_{h_ R}^2\, \nu _ Y=\pi ^*\nu _ X. \end{equation}

Explicitly, let $f_ X$ and $f_ Y$ be the densities of $\nu _ X$ and $\nu _ Y$ in local coordinates. The local frame of $R_\pi $ given by the quotient of the coordinate canonical frames is given the squared norm $(f_ X\circ \pi )/f_ Y$. This function is smooth and strictly positive, also where $J_\pi $ vanishes, and the transformation rules of the coordinate densities show that it defines a metric. Equation (27.23) is an identity of smooth top-degree forms; it does not define the pullback of an arbitrary measure.

Proposition 27.6.2. Let the line bundle $L$ with metric $h$, the selecting potential $\varphi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$ and the continuous weight $v\in C(X)$ be as in section 27.1, and use the volume $\nu _ X$ on $X$. On $Y$, use the line bundles $\pi ^*L^ m\otimes R_\pi $ with the metrics $\pi ^*h^ m\otimes h_ R$, the selecting potential $\varphi \circ \pi $, the continuous weight $v\circ \pi $ and the volume $\nu _ Y$, and define the kernels and kernel measures as in section 27.1, with the fixed factor $(R_\pi ,h_ R)$ added. Then the identification $s\mapsto J_\pi \pi ^*s$,

\[ H^0(X,L^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\varphi )) \simeq H^0(Y,\pi ^*L^ m\otimes R_\pi \otimes \mathcal I(m\varphi \circ \pi )), \]

is an isometry for the continuous weighted norms. The kernels and kernel measures on $Y$ and $X$ satisfy

\[ B_{Y,m}=|J_\pi |_{h_ R}^2(B_{X,m}\circ \pi ), \qquad \pi _*\beta _{Y,m}^{(r)}=\beta _{X,m}^{(r)} \]

for the same normalization integer $r$, also when the section spaces are zero.

Proof. The identification is lemma 1.22.15 with $M=L^ m$ and $a=m\varphi $. By (27.23) and the change of variables formula,

\[ \int _ Y|J_\pi \pi ^*s|_{\pi ^*h^ m\otimes h_ R}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv\circ \pi }\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ Y =\int _ X|s|_{h^ m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ X. \]

The selecting potential has already determined the section spaces and does not enter the norms. Hence the identification maps an orthonormal basis to an orthonormal basis, and summing the squared pointwise norms of the basis vectors gives the kernel formula, also at the zeros of $J_\pi $. Multiplying by $r!/m^ r$ and integrating a continuous function pulled back from $X$ proves the measure formula. $\square$
