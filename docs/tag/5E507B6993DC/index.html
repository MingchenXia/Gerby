1.6 Local products and capacity5E507B6993DC

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.6 Local products and capacity

Bedford–Taylor products of bounded plurisubharmonic functions, and the capacity defined through them, measure the exceptional sets of plurisubharmonic functions and control bounded approximation. We recall these classical facts of local pluripotential theory from [SGPT, GZ17]. The plurifine topology is the coarsest topology that makes all local plurisubharmonic functions continuous. It is finer than the ordinary topology; in particular, strict superlevel sets of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are plurifine open.

Theorem 1.6.1 (Bedford–Taylor). Let $1\leq k\leq n$. If $p_1,\ldots ,p_ k$ are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\mathbb C^ n$, their mixed product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_ k$ is a positive closed current of order zero. It is symmetric, multilinear in its current factors, and continuous under simultaneous locally bounded decreasing approximation. It does not charge pluripolar sets and is local in the plurifine topology.

Here plurifine locality means that if two families of such functions agree on a plurifine open Borel set, then their products have the same restriction to that set.

Products multiplied by a bounded potential need more than the weak convergence of the unweighted products in theorem 1.6.1. An auxiliary complex variable recovers the weight from an ordinary mixed product in one more dimension, including at top degree.

Lemma 1.6.2. Let $a_ j\downarrow a$ and $b_{i,j}\downarrow b_ i$, $1\leq i\leq r\leq n$, be locally uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\mathbb C^ n$. Then

\[ a_ j\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_{r,j} \longrightarrow a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ r \]

weakly as currents. The same holds for quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that become plurisubharmonic after fixed smooth corrections, and after wedging with smooth forms that converge locally in $C^\infty $.

Proof. First let $r=n$, and write $\mu _ j=\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_{i,j}$ and $\mu =\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ i$. Work over a relatively compact ball on which $-M\leq a_ j,a\leq M$. On the product of this ball with a disk of radius $R$ in an auxiliary variable $w$, where $\log R^2>M+1$, set

\[ v_ j(z,w)=\max \{ a_ j(z),\log |w|^2\} ,\qquad v(z,w)=\max \{ a(z),\log |w|^2\} . \]

These are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions, also at $w=0$, and $v_ j\downarrow v$. Pulling back the functions $b_{i,j}$ and $b_ i$ to the product and applying theorem 1.6.1 in dimension $n+1$, we obtain

\[ T_ j=\mu _ j\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j\longrightarrow T=\mu \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v. \]

Since $\mu $ is closed, $T=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v\mu )$. As $\mu $ has top degree in the base variables, only derivatives in $w$ contribute. Testing against product test functions and applying Fubini's theorem therefore identifies $T$ with $\mu (\mathrm dz)$ times the circle measure

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_ w\max \{ a(z),\log |w|^2\} =\sigma _{\exp (a(z)/2)}. \]

Here $\sigma _ t$ is the uniform probability measure on the circle $|w|=t$: the radial derivative jumps from zero to $2/t$ there, so the Euclidean Laplacian has total mass $4\pi $, and our normalization of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ divides that mass by $4\pi $. This distributional computation is valid for every bounded Borel function $a$; no continuity in $z$ is needed. The same description holds for $T_ j$.

Choose a smooth radial compactly supported function $\vartheta (w)$, supported away from zero and from the circle $|w|=R$, that equals $\log |w|^2$ on all circles with $-M\leq \log |w|^2\leq M$. For every smooth compactly supported test function $\xi $ on the base,

\[ \langle T_ j,\xi \vartheta \rangle =\int \xi a_ j\, \mathrm d\mu _ j, \qquad \langle T,\xi \vartheta \rangle =\int \xi a\, \mathrm d\mu . \]

The convergence $T_ j\to T$ therefore proves the top-degree assertion.

For $r<n$, wedge with $n-r$ constant positive $(1,1)$-forms, each the Hessian of a positive quadratic function, and apply the top-degree case. By polarization, the products of positive constant $(1,1)$-forms span the real constant forms of bidegree $(n-r,n-r)$, and multiplying them by smooth test functions recovers every coefficient of the currents. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, add the fixed smooth corrections to the decreasing sequences, apply the result to the corrected functions, and expand; the term involving the correction to $a_ j$ converges by the ordinary decreasing continuity of the products. Finally, the local Chern–Levine–Nirenberg bounds show that these finitely many products have uniformly bounded variation on compact sets. This allows smooth forms converging locally in $C^\infty $ to replace fixed forms in the tests. $\square$

For concentric coordinate balls $B'\Subset B$ and a Borel set $E\subset B'$, the relative Bedford–Taylor capacity is

\[ \operatorname {cap}(E,B)=\sup \Bigl \{ \int _ E(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n:v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B),\ 0\leq v\leq 1\Bigr \} . \]

If $v$ is bounded, nonconstant and plurisubharmonic on $B$, applying the definition to $(v-\inf _ Bv)/(\sup _ Bv-\inf _ Bv)$ gives

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n(E)\leq (\sup _ Bv-\inf _ Bv)^ n\operatorname {cap}(E,B); \]

for constant $v$ both sides vanish. Write $\operatorname {Vol}(E)=\int _ E\mathrm{d}V$, where $\mathrm{d}V$ is a fixed smooth positive volume form. We use two standard local estimates on these balls, with constants $C,a>0$ depending only on the nested balls and on $\mathrm{d}V$:

  1. If $w\leq 0$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$ and $v$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $B$, then

    \[ \int _{B'}|w|(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n \leq C\| w\| _{L^1(B)}\| v\| _{L^\infty (B)}^ n. \]

    Since adding a constant to $v$ does not change $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$, one may first normalize $v$ by such a constant. This is the weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality.

  2. For every Borel set $E\subset B'$,

    \[ \operatorname {Vol}(E)\leq C\exp \bigl (-a\operatorname {cap}(E,B)^{-1/n}\bigr ), \]

    where the right-hand side is zero when the capacity is zero. This is the classical local volume–capacity estimate.

Both are classical results of local pluripotential theory [Chapters 3 and 4, GZ17]; the conventions for bounded products and capacity in [Chapter 2, SGPT] agree with these up to fixed constants.

The dependence on the range of the potential can be read off from the usual cutoff argument. Let $M>0$ and let $p$ be plurisubharmonic on $B$ with $0\leq p\leq M$. Let $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ be the Euclidean form, and choose a cutoff function $\chi $ supported in $B$, with $0\leq \chi \leq 1$, equal to one on a smaller ball and satisfying $-C\kappa \leq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \leq C\kappa $. Integration by parts gives, for $1\leq j\leq n$,

\[ \begin{split} \int \chi (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} & =\int p\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^{j-1}\wedge \kappa ^{n-j}\\ & \leq CM\int _{\operatorname {supp}\chi }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^{j-1}\wedge \kappa ^{n-j+1}. \end{split} \]

Iterating this estimate with nested cutoffs between $B'$ and $B$, and stopping at degree one or at degree zero, we obtain respectively

1.4
\begin{equation} \begin{split} \int _{B'}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} & \leq C_ jM^{j-1}\int _ B\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p\wedge \kappa ^{n-1},\\ \int _{B'}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} & \leq C_ jM^ j. \end{split} \end{equation}

The constants depend only on the nested balls and on $j$. For bounded $p$, the integrations by parts are justified by local decreasing regularization.

Definition 1.6.3. For a compact set $E$ in an open Euclidean ball $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$, the relative extremal function is

\[ h_{E,B}=\sup \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(B):-1\leq u\leq 0,\ u|_ E\leq -1\} . \]

The constant $-1$ is a competitor, and the family is bounded above by zero. The local upper-envelope theorem therefore gives $h_{E,B}^*\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $-1\leq h_{E,B}^*\leq 0$. Allowing all competitors $u\leq 0$ with $u|_ E\leq -1$ gives the same supremum, because the truncation $\max (u,-1)$ of such a competitor is a competitor in the original family.

A sequence of functions $f_ j$ converges to $f$ in local capacity if $\operatorname {cap}(K\cap \{ |f_ j-f|>\epsilon \} ,B)\to 0$ for every $\epsilon >0$, every coordinate ball $B$ and every compact set $K\subset B$. An assertion holds quasi-everywhere, abbreviated q.e., if it holds outside a pluripolar set; in particular, differences of functions that may both be $-\infty $ are considered only off such a set. A positive measure is non-pluripolar if it gives zero mass to every pluripolar set.

A function is quasi-continuous if, for every $\epsilon >0$, it is continuous outside an open set of capacity less than $\epsilon $. Plurisubharmonic functions have this property for the local Bedford–Taylor capacity, and a Borel set has zero capacity if and only if it is pluripolar. A locally uniformly bounded monotone sequence of plurisubharmonic functions converges in capacity: to its limit if it decreases, and to the upper semicontinuous regularization of its limit if it increases. If uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions converge in capacity, their local products converge weakly, and this remains true after the products are multiplied by uniformly bounded quasi-continuous coefficients converging in capacity. These local results, recalled in [SGPT, GZ17], also apply to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions after a common smooth correction.

Theorem 1.6.4 (Mixed Monge–Ampère inequality). Let $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a domain, and let $\beta $ be its Euclidean Hermitian form. Let $a,b$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $U$, and let $f,g$ be nonnegative measurable functions on $U$ with

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a)^ n\geq f\beta ^ n, \qquad (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^ n\geq g\beta ^ n. \]

Then, for $0\leq k\leq n$,

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a)^ k\wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^{n-k} \geq f^{k/n}g^{(n-k)/n}\beta ^ n. \]

The comparison principle of Bedford and Taylor also has mixed forms, recalled in [SGPT]. Let $a,b$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $U$ such that $E=\{ a<b\} \Subset U$, and let $S$ be a positive closed $(n-1,n-1)$-current that is a product of Hessians of bounded plurisubharmonic functions and smooth closed positive forms. Then

1.7
\begin{equation} \int _ E\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge S\leq \int _ E\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge S. \end{equation}

Indeed, apply the Bedford–Taylor integration by parts formula to $b_\epsilon =\max \{ a,b-\epsilon \} $, and let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, using monotone continuity to pass to the limit. Replacing one factor at a time, we obtain, with $P=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ and $Q=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b$,

1.8
\begin{equation} \int _ E P^{n-j}\wedge Q^ j\leq \int _ E P^ n, \qquad 0\leq j\leq n. \end{equation}

The local envelope theorem for bounded obstacles, recalled in [SGPT, GZ17], shows where an envelope can carry Monge–Ampère mass. Let $B$ be a ball with a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic defining function $h<0$, and let $f$ be a bounded quasi-continuous obstacle with $Ah\leq f\leq 0$ for some $A>0$. Then the envelope

\[ q=\bigl (\sup \{ v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B):v\leq f\ \text{q.e.}\} \bigr )^* \]

satisfies $Ah\leq q\leq 0$, tends to zero at the boundary, and

1.9
\begin{equation} \mathbf1_{\{ q<f\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n=0. \end{equation}

For a locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$ and a smooth convex increasing function $F$ on a neighborhood of the range of $v$, the bounded Bedford–Taylor chain rule reads

1.10
\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F(v)=F'(v)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v+F''(v)\, \mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v. \end{equation}

The gradient term, and its products with positive Bedford–Taylor factors of bounded potentials, are nonnegative. These identities belong to the bounded local calculus recalled in [SGPT, GZ17]; they follow by decreasing regularization, and bounded potentials have the local Sobolev regularity needed to define the gradient term. In particular, inequalities derived from this formula survive local smoothing with a lower curvature error tending to zero.

Lemma 1.6.5. Let $B$ be a coordinate ball and let $\tau $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure with compact support in $B$. Then there is a bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ such that $\tau \ll (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$.

Proof. For $n\geq 2$, we use Cegrell's decomposition theorem. On a bounded hyperconvex domain in $\mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 2$, let $\mathcal E_0$ consist of the bounded negative plurisubharmonic functions with zero boundary values and finite total Monge–Ampère mass. The theorem states that every positive compactly supported measure $\tau $ has a decomposition

\[ \tau =g(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n+\tau _ s, \qquad v\in \mathcal E_0,\qquad 0\leq g\in L^1((\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n), \]

where $\tau _ s$ is a positive measure carried by a pluripolar set. If $\tau $ is non-pluripolar, then $\tau _ s=0$, so $\tau \ll (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$.

In dimension one, the assertion follows directly from the Green potential. Let $a\leq 0$ be the Green potential of $\tau $ on the disk, normalized by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a=\tau $. Then $v=\mathrm{e}^ a$ is bounded and subharmonic, and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq \mathrm{e}^ a\tau . \]

To justify this inequality, set $a_ M=\max (a,-M)$. The chain rule (1.10) in dimension one gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{e}^{a_ M}\geq \mathrm{e}^{a_ M}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ M$, and the right-hand side is at least $\mathrm{e}^ a\tau $ on $\{ a>-M\} $. Let $M\to \infty $, using the decreasing convergence of the bounded subharmonic functions $\mathrm{e}^{a_ M}$ on the left and monotone convergence on the right. Since $\{ a=-\infty \} $ is polar and $\tau $ charges no polar set, $\mathrm{e}^ a>0$ almost everywhere for $\tau $. Thus $\tau \ll \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$. $\square$

For a signed measure $\nu $, write $|\nu |$ for its total variation measure and $\| \nu \| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}=|\nu |(B)$. On a curve, the Green representation controls capacity by the total variation of the Laplacian, together with an $L^1$ term for the harmonic remainder.

Lemma 1.6.6. Let $B'\Subset B$ be concentric disks in $\mathbb C$. There is a constant $C$, depending only on these disks, such that for all quasi-subharmonic functions $u,v$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and all $t>0$,

1.11
\begin{equation} \operatorname {cap}(B'\cap \{ |u-v|>t\} ,B) \leq \frac Ct\left(\| u-v\| _{L^1(B)} +\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-v)\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right). \end{equation}

Here the difference $u-v$ is formed off the pole sets of $u$ and $v$.

Proof. Put $f=u-v$ and write the Jordan decomposition on $B$ as $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\alpha ^+-\alpha ^-$. Let $g^\pm \leq 0$ be the Green potentials of $\alpha ^\pm $ on $B$ with zero boundary values. The $L^1(B)$ norm of the Green kernel of the disk is bounded uniformly in its pole, so

\[ \| g^+\| _{L^1(B)}+\| g^-\| _{L^1(B)} \leq C\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}. \]

Indeed, on the unit disk the kernel is $\log |(z-\zeta )/(1-\overline\zeta z)|^2$; its logarithmic singularity is uniformly integrable, and $|1-\overline\zeta z|\leq 2$. Scaling gives the bound on $B$.

The distribution $h=f-g^++g^-$ is harmonic, and the interior mean-value estimate gives

\[ \| h\| _{L^\infty (B')} \leq C\left(\| f\| _{L^1(B)}+\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right). \]

The identity $u+g^-=v+g^++h$ holds almost everywhere, hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Thus $|u-v|\leq -g^+-g^-+|h|$ off the polar sets of $u$ and $v$. Let $0\leq a\leq 1$ be a subharmonic test function on $B$. Since the measure $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ charges no polar set, this pointwise bound, the weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate and (1.4) yield

\[ \int _{B'}|u-v|\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a \leq C\left(\| f\| _{L^1(B)}+\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right). \]

Restricting the integral to $B'\cap \{ |u-v|>t\} $, dividing by $t$ and taking the supremum over $a$ proves (1.11). $\square$
