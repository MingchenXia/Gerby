Pluripolar sets and the plurifine topology

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · B.10 Pluripolar sets and the plurifine topology

Josefson proved that locally pluripolar subsets of $\mathbb C^ n$ are globally pluripolar [Jos78]; see theorem 10.2.1. The approach through capacity is due to Bedford and Taylor [BT82]; for relative extremal functions compare [Theorem 4.32, pp. 118–119, Theorem 4.34, pp. 119–121, and Theorem 4.40 and Corollary 4.41, pp. 124–125, GZ17]. The identities for the masses of relative extremal functions of compact and of open sets (proposition 10.1.2, proposition 10.1.3) are proved with the homogeneous balayage of corollary 9.6.4; together with approximation by decreasing open sets they give the characterization of pluripolar sets by capacity, theorem 10.1.4.

For the local base of plurifine neighborhoods in proposition 10.3.2 compare [arXiv version, Lemma 3.1, pp. 3–4, EMW06]. The main theorem of that paper, the local connectedness of the plurifine topology, is not treated here; its proof also uses finely subharmonic functions. The plurifine quasi-Lindelöf principle, theorem 10.4.1, follows from Choquet's lemma and the pluripolarity of the exceptional sets of upper envelopes (corollary 10.2.2). The plurifine locality of Bedford–Taylor products, theorem 10.4.3, is deduced from their behavior under maxima (proposition 9.2.7), up to exceptional sets that products of bounded functions do not charge. In section 10.5 the difference of two plurisubharmonic functions, and its quasi-extrema, are considered only outside their common pole set, where the difference is defined.
