Proposition 13.1.8B1F342A8772A

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 13.1.8

Proposition 13.1.8. The regularized envelope $G_\delta ^*$ on $\mathbb P^ n$ is bounded and $\theta _\delta $-plurisubharmonic. On the affine chart,

13.1
\begin{equation} V_\delta =\tfrac \delta 2\chi +G_\delta ,\qquad F_\delta =\tfrac \delta 2\chi +G_\delta ^*\in \mathcal L_\delta ^+. \end{equation}

Moreover, $V_\delta =F_\delta $ almost everywhere.

Proof. By lemma 13.1.7, the map $u\mapsto u-\delta \chi /2$ is a bijection between the two families of competitors, which proves the identity for $V_\delta $. Constants below $\inf _ K(\psi -\delta \chi /2)$ are competitors for $G_\delta $.

Since all competitors for $G_\delta $ lie below the constant $\sup _ K(\psi -\delta \chi /2)$ on $K$, lemma 1.19.1 gives a common upper bound for them. Together with a constant competitor, the upper-envelope theorem shows that $G_\delta ^*$ is bounded and $\theta _\delta $-plurisubharmonic. Addition of the smooth function $\delta \chi /2$ in (13.1) commutes with regularization, and the two-sided bounds for $G_\delta ^*$ give $F_\delta \in \mathcal L_\delta ^+$.

Locally, adding a potential of $\theta _\delta $ makes the competitors plurisubharmonic. The upper-envelope theorem recalled in chapter 1 shows that regularization changes their supremum only on a pluripolar set. Thus $G_\delta =G_\delta ^*$ almost everywhere, and adding $\delta \chi /2$ gives the same assertion for $V_\delta $. $\square$
