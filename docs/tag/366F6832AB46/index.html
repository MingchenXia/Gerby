Lemma 14.26.2366F6832AB46

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Lemma 14.26.2

Lemma 14.26.2. Fix $p>1$. If nonzero nonnegative densities $q_ j,q\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$ satisfy $q_ j\to q$ in $L^ p$, then $c_ h(q_ j)\to c_ h(q)$.

Proof. We first show that the scalars $c_ h(q_ j)$ lie in a compact subset of $(0,+\infty )$. Fix a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $ on $X$ (definition 4.1.1; in dimension one, any Hermitian metric). Pairing the equation for $q_ j$ with $\gamma ^{n-1}$, as in proposition 14.13.2 with $\theta =h$ and barrier $\rho =0$, gives

\[ c_ h(q_ j)^{1/n}\int _ X\left(\frac{q_ j\, \mathrm{d}V}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n \leq \int _ Xh\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}. \]

Since $q_ j^{1/n}\to q^{1/n}$ in $L^ n$, the integrals on the left converge to the corresponding integral for $q$, which is positive. Hence the scalars are bounded above. The lower-density estimate of theorem 10.3.3 gives $c_ h(q_ j)\| q_ j\| _{L^ p}\geq c_ p>0$, so they are also bounded away from zero.

Consequently, the normalized continuous solutions $u_ j$ of $\mathrm{MA}_ h(u_ j)=c_ h(q_ j)q_ j\, \mathrm{d}V$ have uniformly bounded $L^ p$ densities. By theorem 10.3.4, every subsequence has a further subsequence along which $u_ j$ converges uniformly to some $u$ and $c_ h(q_ j)$ converges to some $c>0$. Continuity of bounded Monge–Ampère products under uniform convergence gives $\mathrm{MA}_ h(u)=cq\, \mathrm{d}V$, and uniqueness of the scalar gives $c=c_ h(q)$. Since every subsequence of $(c_ h(q_ j))$ has a further subsequence converging to $c_ h(q)$, the whole sequence converges to $c_ h(q)$. $\square$
