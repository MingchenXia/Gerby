Theorem 27.3.104BABC8924B6

Theorem 27.3.1. The function $E=E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and it is the largest member of its defining family. Let $q=P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$, and let $C$ be a constant as in lemma 1.19.1, so that $\sup _ Xa\leq C$ for every $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $a\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Then

\[ q+\min _ Kw\leq E\leq q+C. \]

In particular, $E\sim q$ and

\[ E\sim _{\mathcal I}\varphi . \]

Proof. By theorem 16.2.2, $q\leq 0$ and $q\sim _{\mathcal I}\varphi $. Hence $q+\min _ Kw$ belongs to the defining family. Lemma 1.19.1 bounds the entire family above by $C$, so its regularized supremum $E$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and $E\geq q+\min _ Kw$.

For the upper bound, let $a$ be a member of the family. Since $\sup _ Xa\leq C$, proposition 16.2.4, proposition 16.2.6 give

\[ a-C\leq a-\sup _ Xa\leq P_\theta [a]_{\mathcal I} \leq P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}=q. \]

Taking the regularized supremum over $a$ proves $E\leq q+C$.

The two bounds show that $E-q$ is bounded. Hence $E$ and $q$ have the same multiplier ideals, and these are the multiplier ideals of $\varphi $.

It remains to show that $E$ itself satisfies the obstacle condition, which is not automatic after regularization. By Choquet's lemma, $E$ is the regularization of the supremum of a countable subfamily. Replace its members by their successive finite maxima. Finite maxima are again members of the family: the multiplier-ideal order is preserved by theorem 1.16.3, and a finite union of pluripolar exceptional sets is pluripolar. We obtain an increasing sequence $a_ j$ in the family whose limit has regularization $E$. Outside a countable union of pluripolar subsets of $K$, every $a_ j$ is at most $w$, and hence so is $\lim _ ja_ j$. By the local upper-envelope theorem recalled in section 1.19, $E$ differs from $\lim _ ja_ j$ only on a pluripolar set. Thus $E\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$.

We have shown that $E$ belongs to its defining family. It dominates every member by construction, so it is the largest member. In particular, the supremum in (27.4) equals $E$ at every point even before regularization. $\square$
