1.5 Weak convergence of measuresA1AF2491CD1A

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 1.5 Weak convergence of measures

The families of measures whose limits we shall need are not always sequences, so we work with nets. A directed set is a nonempty set $I$ with a preorder $\leq $ in which every two elements have a common upper bound. A net $(x_ i)_{i\in I}$ in a topological space $X$ is a map $i\mapsto x_ i$ from a directed set $I$ to $X$; it converges to $x\in X$ if for every neighborhood $U$ of $x$ there is $i_0\in I$ with $x_ i\in U$ for all $i\geq i_0$. For a net $(a_ i)$ in $[-\infty ,\infty ]$, $\varliminf _ i a_ i=\sup _{i_0}\inf _{i\geq i_0}a_ i$ and $\varlimsup _ i a_ i=\inf _{i_0}\sup _{i\geq i_0}a_ i$.

The weak topology on $\mathcal D'(\Omega )$ is the weakest topology for which all maps $T\mapsto \langle T,\chi \rangle $, $\chi \in C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$, are continuous. A set containing $T$ is a neighborhood of $T$ for this topology if and only if it contains a set $\{ S:|\langle S-T,\chi _ l\rangle |<r\text{ for }1\leq l\leq k\} $ with finitely many test functions $\chi _ l$ and $r>0$. Since the index set of a net is directed, it follows that a net $(T_ i)$ converges to $T$ exactly when $\langle T_ i,\chi \rangle \to \langle T,\chi \rangle $ for every test function $\chi $, and we then say that $T_ i\to T$ in the sense of distributions.

Likewise, the vague topology on the set of Radon measures on $\Omega $ is the weakest topology for which all maps $\mu \mapsto \int f\, \mathrm d\mu $, $f\in C_ c(\Omega )$, are continuous. For the same reason, a net $(\mu _ i)$ converges to $\mu $ in this topology, and we say that $\mu _ i\to \mu $ vaguely, if and only if $\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _ i\to \int f\, \mathrm d\mu $ for every $f\in C_ c(\Omega )$. A net of finite Radon measures converges narrowly to a finite Radon measure $\mu $ if the same holds for every bounded continuous function $f$ on $\Omega $. Narrow convergence implies vague convergence, but the converse fails, because mass can escape to infinity or to the boundary of $\Omega $ (example 1.5.4).

Proposition 1.5.1. Let $(T_ i)$ be a net of positive distributions on $\Omega $ converging in the sense of distributions to $T\in \mathcal D'(\Omega )$. Then $T$ is positive, and the Radon measures associated with the $T_ i$ by theorem 1.4.5 converge vaguely to the Radon measure associated with $T$. Conversely, if a net of Radon measures converges vaguely, the associated distributions converge in the sense of distributions.

Proof. For a nonnegative test function $\chi $, the numbers $\langle T_ i,\chi \rangle $ are nonnegative, hence so is their limit $\langle T,\chi \rangle $; likewise $\langle T,\chi \rangle $ is real for real $\chi $. Thus $T$ is positive, and $T$ and the $T_ i$ are given by Radon measures $\mu $ and $\mu _ i$. Let $f\in C_ c(\Omega )$. By lemma 1.4.1(5), $f$ is the uniform limit of test functions supported in a fixed compact set $L$; in particular, $f$ vanishes outside $L$. If $\eta $ is a smooth cutoff function for $L$ in $\Omega $, then $\mu _ i(L)\leq \langle T_ i,\eta \rangle \to \langle T,\eta \rangle $. A convergent net of real numbers need not be bounded, but it is bounded from some index on; hence there is $i_0$ with $\mu _ i(L)\leq C=\langle T,\eta \rangle +1$ for $i\geq i_0$, and also $\mu (L)\leq \langle T,\eta \rangle <C$. For every test function $\chi $ supported in $L$ and every $i\geq i_0$,

\[ \left|\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _ i-\int f\, \mathrm d\mu \right|\leq 2C\| f-\chi \| _\infty +|\langle T_ i,\chi \rangle -\langle T,\chi \rangle |, \]

so $\varlimsup _ i|\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _ i-\int f\, \mathrm d\mu |\leq 2C\| f-\chi \| _\infty $, which can be made arbitrarily small. Hence $\mu _ i\to \mu $ vaguely. The converse holds because test functions belong to $C_ c(\Omega )$. $\square$

Thus, by the uniqueness in theorem 1.4.5, a net of Radon measures converges vaguely to a Radon measure if and only if it converges to it in the sense of distributions. In 2, where measures appear as currents of top degree, this mode of convergence is called weak convergence.

Proposition 1.5.2. Let $(\mu _ i)$ be a net of Radon measures on $\Omega $ converging vaguely to a Radon measure $\mu $. Then for every open set $U\subset \Omega $ and every compact set $K\subset \Omega $,

\[ \mu (U)\leq \varliminf _ i\mu _ i(U),\qquad \varlimsup _ i\mu _ i(K)\leq \mu (K). \]

Consequently, if $E\Subset \Omega $ is a Borel set with $\mu (\partial E)=0$, then $\mu _ i(E)\to \mu (E)$.

Proof. If $f\in C_ c(\Omega )$, $0\leq f\leq 1$ and $\operatorname{Supp}f\subset U$, then $\int f\, \mathrm d\mu =\lim _ i\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _ i\leq \varliminf _ i\mu _ i(U)$; taking the supremum over such $f$ and using (1.4) gives the first inequality. For the second, let $\varepsilon >0$, choose by outer regularity an open set $V\supset K$ with $\mu (V)<\mu (K)+\varepsilon $, and let $\eta $ be a cutoff function for $K$ in $V$. Then $\mu _ i(K)\leq \int \eta \, \mathrm d\mu _ i\to \int \eta \, \mathrm d\mu \leq \mu (V)$, so $\varlimsup _ i\mu _ i(K)\leq \mu (K)+\varepsilon $. Finally, for $E$ as in the last assertion, the closure $\overline E$ is a compact subset of $\Omega $, and $\overline E\setminus \operatorname{Int}E=\partial E$ is $\mu $-null, so $\mu (\operatorname{Int}E)=\mu (E)=\mu (\overline E)$. Since $\mu _ i(\operatorname{Int}E)\leq \mu _ i(E)\leq \mu _ i(\overline E)$, the two inequalities, applied to $\operatorname{Int}E$ and $\overline E$, give

\[ \mu (E)=\mu (\operatorname{Int}E)\leq \varliminf _ i\mu _ i(E)\leq \varlimsup _ i\mu _ i(E)\leq \mu (\overline E)=\mu (E). \]
$\square$

Proposition 1.5.3. Let $(\mu _ i)$ be a net of finite Radon measures on $\Omega $ converging vaguely to $\mu $. Suppose that the net is eventually uniformly tight: for every $\varepsilon >0$ there are a compact set $K\subset \Omega $ and an index $i_0$ such that $\mu _ i(\Omega \setminus K)<\varepsilon $ for all $i\geq i_0$. Then $\mu $ is finite and $\mu _ i\to \mu $ narrowly. In particular, $\mu _ i(\Omega )\to \mu (\Omega )$.

Proof. Let $\varepsilon >0$, and choose $K$ and $i_0$ as above. Since $\Omega \setminus K$ is open, proposition 1.5.2 gives $\mu (\Omega \setminus K)\leq \varepsilon $, and since $\mu (K)<\infty $, the measure $\mu $ is finite. Let $f$ be a bounded continuous function on $\Omega $ and $\eta $ a cutoff function for $K$ in $\Omega $. Then $f\eta \in C_ c(\Omega )$, so $\int f\eta \, \mathrm d\mu _ i\to \int f\eta \, \mathrm d\mu $, while $1-\eta $ takes values in $[0,1]$ and vanishes on $K$, so that for $i\geq i_0$

\[ \left|\int f(1-\eta )\, \mathrm d\mu _ i\right|\leq \| f\| _\infty \varepsilon , \qquad \left|\int f(1-\eta )\, \mathrm d\mu \right|\leq \| f\| _\infty \varepsilon . \]

Hence $\varlimsup _ i|\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _ i-\int f\, \mathrm d\mu |\leq 2\| f\| _\infty \varepsilon $. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ proves narrow convergence, and $f=1$ gives the last assertion. $\square$

Example 1.5.4. On $\mathbb R$, the Dirac measures $\delta _ j$, $j\geq 1$, converge vaguely to zero, since every $f\in C_ c(\mathbb R)$ vanishes at $j$ for large $j$; yet each has total mass one. On $\Omega =(0,1)$ the measures $\delta _{1/j}$, $j\geq 2$, behave in the same way, the mass escaping through the boundary point $0$, which does not belong to $\Omega $. Neither sequence is eventually uniformly tight: a compact subset of $\mathbb R$ does not contain $j$ for large $j$, and a compact subset of $(0,1)$ does not contain $1/j$ for large $j$, so the mass outside a fixed compact set is eventually equal to one.

Theorem 1.5.5. Let $(\mu _ j)$ be a sequence of Radon measures on $\Omega $ such that $\sup _ j\mu _ j(K)<\infty $ for every compact set $K\subset \Omega $. Then $(\mu _ j)$ has a vaguely convergent subsequence. If, moreover, the $\mu _ j$ are finite and the sequence is eventually uniformly tight, then it has a narrowly convergent subsequence with a finite limit.

Proof. Step 1: a countable dense family. Let $\varphi (t)=\max \{ 0,1-|t|\} $, so that $\sum _{n\in \mathbb Z}\varphi (t-n)=1$ for $t\in \mathbb R$. For $k\geq 1$ and $n\in \mathbb Z^ d$ put $\varphi _{k,n}(x)=\prod _{l=1}^ d\varphi (2^ kx_ l-n_ l)$. These are nonnegative continuous functions with $\sum _ n\varphi _{k,n}=1$, and $\varphi _{k,n}(x)\neq 0$ only if $|x-2^{-k}n|_\infty <2^{-k}$, where $|\cdot |_\infty $ is the maximum norm. Let $\mathcal F$ be the countable family of all finite sums $\sum _ nq_ n\varphi _{k,n}$, $k\geq 1$, with rational coefficients $q_ n$, whose support is a compact subset of $\Omega $. Given $f\in C_ c(\Omega ,\mathbb R)$, extended by zero to $\mathbb R^ d$, choose rational numbers $q_{k,n}$ with $|q_{k,n}-f(2^{-k}n)|\leq 2^{-k}$ and $q_{k,n}=0$ if $f(2^{-k}n)=0$, and put $f_ k=\sum _ nq_{k,n}\varphi _{k,n}$. Then

\[ |f_ k(x)-f(x)|\leq \sum _ n|q_{k,n}-f(x)|\varphi _{k,n}(x)\leq 2^{-k}+\sup \{ |f(y)-f(x)|:|y-x|_\infty <2^{-k}\} , \]

which tends to zero uniformly in $x$ because $f$ is uniformly continuous. Moreover, $f_ k$ vanishes at points of $|\cdot |_\infty $-distance more than $2^{-k}$ from $\operatorname{Supp}f$, so for large $k$ all the $f_ k$ belong to $\mathcal F$ and are supported in a fixed compact set $L\subset \Omega $.

Step 2: diagonal extraction. Enumerate $\mathcal F=\{ g_1,g_2,\ldots \} $. For each $l$, the numbers $\int g_ l\, \mathrm d\mu _ j$ are bounded by $\| g_ l\| _\infty \sup _ j\mu _ j(\operatorname{Supp}g_ l)$. Choosing successively convergent subsequences for $g_1,g_2,\ldots $ and passing to the diagonal subsequence, we find $(\mu _{j_ k})$ such that $\int g_ l\, \mathrm d\mu _{j_ k}$ converges for every $l$. If $f\in C_ c(\Omega ,\mathbb R)$ and $L$, $f_ m$ are as in Step 1, then with $C_ L=\sup _ j\mu _ j(L)$,

\[ \left|\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _{j_ k}-\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _{j_{k'}}\right|\leq 2C_ L\| f-f_ m\| _\infty +\left|\int f_ m\, \mathrm d\mu _{j_ k}-\int f_ m\, \mathrm d\mu _{j_{k'}}\right|, \]

so $(\int f\, \mathrm d\mu _{j_ k})_ k$ is a Cauchy sequence. Its limit is a positive linear functional of $f$, hence integration against a Radon measure $\mu $ by theorem 1.2.2, and $\mu _{j_ k}\to \mu $ vaguely; complex $f$ are handled by linearity. If the sequence is eventually uniformly tight, so is every subsequence, and proposition 1.5.3 gives the last assertion. $\square$
