Theorem 21.11.33A57F525B7ABC

Theorem 21.11.33.A57F525B7ABC Suppose that $F\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$. Then the following hold:

  1. 8BEAD5D03F60

    For any $n\not\in \operatorname{Dom}F$, $\nabla F(n)=\varnothing $;

  2. C38BB55E74AA

    for any $n\in \operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F$, $\nabla F(n)\neq \varnothing $; moreover, for any $n'\in N$, we have

    \[ \partial _{n'} F(n)=\sup \left\{ \langle n', m \rangle : m\in \nabla F(n)\right\} ; \]
  3. 891D0FE828BD

    for $n\in N$, the set $\nabla F(n)$ is non-empty and bounded if and only if $n\in \operatorname{Int}\operatorname{Dom}F$.

Proof. Subgradients at points outside the effective domain are empty by definition. On the relative interior, existence and the directional formula follow from local convex regularity applied in the affine span $A$ of the domain. To view a subgradient as a functional on $N$, extend its restriction to the direction space of $A$ by a linear functional. Any functional vanishing on that direction space may be added without changing the subgradient inequality. Thus for a direction outside that space the supremum in the directional formula is $+\infty$, just as the one-sided directional derivative is; for tangent directions it is the relative formula.

At an interior point of the domain, local Lipschitz continuity bounds all subgradients: if $|F(x+v)-F(x)|\leq L|v|$ for small $v$, the subgradient inequality for $v$ and $-v$ gives $|\langle p,v\rangle|\leq L|v|$, hence $|p|\leq L$. Conversely, suppose the subgradient set is nonempty at a point $x$ of the domain which is not interior. A supporting hyperplane to the closure of the convex domain at $x$ supplies a nonzero $q$ with $\langle q,y-x\rangle\leq0$ for every $y$ in the domain. If $p$ is a subgradient, then so is $p+tq$ for every $t\geq0$, so the set is unbounded. Supporting hyperplanes also exist when the domain lies in a proper affine subspace, by taking an annihilating functional. This proves the final equivalence.
