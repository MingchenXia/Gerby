20.3.72 Pullback and strict representativesD36C5D62DF35

The lower volume above is positive, and its type contains a strict current on $Y$. A modification preserves both optimized masses but can destroy this last property when the ordinary type is fixed.

Proposition 20.3.73.55D866148BAF For every modification $\pi :Y'\to Y$ and $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$ with $v\sim r$,

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\pi ^*\theta }(\pi ^*v) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (v),\qquad \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\pi ^*\theta }(\pi ^*v) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (v). \]

These masses and the ideal orders are also unchanged by a smooth gauge $(\theta ,v,r)\mapsto (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f,v-f,r-f)$.

Proof. By proposition 5.43.18, every $\pi ^*\theta $-plurisubharmonic potential descends. Since $\pi $ is surjective, ordinary inequalities are equivalent before and after pullback. Thus the competitors are in bijection. On the locus where $\pi $ is an isomorphism, locality identifies their products. Both extended products give the excluded analytic subsets zero mass, so

\[ \pi _*\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\pi ^*\theta }(\pi ^*v) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v). \]

Taking the infimum and supremum proves the identities. Smooth gauge preserves the actual currents and measures; it preserves multiplier ideals because $\mathrm e^{sf}$ is locally bounded above and below. $\square$

Proposition 20.3.74.E91643D06F3E Suppose $b<c$ and let $\pi :Y'\to Y$ be a nonempty finite composition of point blowups. There is a strict current $\pi ^*\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v'$ with $v'\leq \pi ^*r+C$, but none with $v'\sim \pi ^*r$. Both ordinary-type volumes retain the values in (20.3.68). Blowup centers may lie on $D$ or on earlier exceptional curves.

Proof. Put $\beta =(c-b)w_1+aw_2$. The representatives of lemma 20.3.65 show that $U$ satisfies (18.27.1); by lemma 18.27.2 so does $\pi ^*U$. The logarithmic divisor is $b\pi ^*D$, whose smooth curvature is $b\pi ^*w_1$, so its residual background is $\pi ^*\beta $. If a strict current existed in the ordinary type of $\pi ^*r$, theorem 18.27.4 would give a smooth $f$ with $\pi ^*\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f>0$. The exceptional curve $E$ of the last blowup is contracted by the composite map. Restriction to this smooth curve would give $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(f|_ E)>0$, contrary to its zero integral by Stokes. Only the smooth function $f$ is restricted, so this argument applies also when the original potential has a pole along $E$.

For the one-sided assertion, start with the strict representative $v_0=bG_ p+U_ L$ from theorem 20.3.66. Suppose $v_ j$ is strict with lower bound $\delta _ jh_ j>0$ on the current surface, and let $\pi _{j+1}$ be the next point blowup. For its exceptional divisor $E_{j+1}$, choose a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(E_{j+1})$ with curvature $\eta _{j+1}$ negative along the vertical tangent, and write $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ell _{j+1}=[E_{j+1}]-\eta _{j+1}$. For all sufficiently small $t_ j>0$, $\pi _{j+1}^*h_ j-t_ j\eta _{j+1}>0$. To see this, in a blowup chart $(x,z)\mapsto (x,xz)$ the pullback has a uniformly positive horizontal coefficient, mixed coefficient $O(|x|)$ and Schur complement at least $c_1|x|^2$. The perturbation adds a positive vertical term at least $t_ jc_2$, while its error in the Schur complement is bounded by $C(t_ j|x|+t_ j|x|^2+t_ j^2)$. Shrink the exceptional neighborhood and then choose $t_ j$ small; on its compact complement positivity persists under small perturbations. Thus

\[ v_{j+1}=\pi _{j+1}^*v_ j+\delta _ jt_ j\ell _{j+1} \]

is strict. These genuine quasi-plurisubharmonic pullbacks are defined even over pole centers. The logarithmic terms are bounded above, so induction gives $v'\leq \pi ^*r+C$. Finally proposition 20.3.73 preserves both volumes in the pulled-back ordinary type. The additional exceptional poles of the one-sided witness are not competitors in that fixed type. $\square$

For example take $c=1$, $b=1/4$, $\alpha =3/4$ and $a=2+\tau $, where $\tau =(|z|^2-1)/(|z|^2+1)$ is the height on the first sphere. The lower and upper volumes are $3/2$ and $9/2$. The choice $L=31$ has $\epsilon _ L=(3/4)32^{-1/4}<3/8$ and gives a strict representative with infinite closed first moment. The intrinsic ideal orders are $\frac14\operatorname {ord}_ E(\pi ^*D)$. Every nonempty finite composition of point blowups, including centers on $D$, preserves both volumes and permits one-sided strictness while excluding a strict representative in the same ordinary type.
