Theorem 6.10.1DFD10C71D607

Theorem 6.10.1.DFD10C71D607 Let $Z\subset X$ be a complex submanifold and $\varphi $ a weight on $X$. Then

5BDE1812FA47 6.10.2
\begin{equation} \mathcal I(\varphi |_ Z)\subset \mathcal I(\varphi )|_ Z^{\mathrm{im}}; \end{equation}

on a connected component of $Z$ on which $\varphi |_ Z\equiv -\infty $, the left side is zero. If $\varphi _1$ and $\varphi _2$ are weights on complex manifolds $X_1$ and $X_2$, and $p_ i\colon X_1\times X_2\to X_ i$ are the projections, then

01EC6A2D5998 6.10.3
\begin{equation} \mathcal I(\varphi _1\circ p_1+\varphi _2\circ p_2) =(p_1^{-1}\mathcal I(\varphi _1)\cdot \mathcal O) (p_2^{-1}\mathcal I(\varphi _2)\cdot \mathcal O). \end{equation}

In particular, for weights $\varphi $ and $\psi $ on $X$,

0EA6BB9CDFD8 6.10.4
\begin{equation} \mathcal I(\varphi +\psi )\subset \mathcal I(\varphi )\mathcal I(\psi ). \end{equation}

Proof. The inclusion (6.10.2) is a restatement of corollary 6.5.9: if $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi |_ Z)_ x$, that corollary provides a holomorphic germ $F$ at $x$ with $F|_ Z=f$ and $F\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$.

The product formula is local. Near a point $(x_1,x_2)$ we choose bounded polydiscs $\Omega _ i$ around $x_ i$ in charts of $X_ i$ on which $\varphi _ i$ is bounded above; subtracting constants, which changes none of the multiplier ideals, we may assume that $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\leq 0$. We write $\Phi =\varphi _1\circ p_1+\varphi _2\circ p_2$, a nonpositive weight on the polydisc $\Omega _1\times \Omega _2$. Let $(e_ j)$ and $(g_ k)$ be orthonormal bases of $\mathcal H_{\varphi _1}(\Omega _1)$ and $\mathcal H_{\varphi _2}(\Omega _2)$, which exist by theorem 6.8.9(2). By Fubini's theorem the functions $e_ j(z)g_ k(w)$ form an orthonormal family in $\mathcal H_\Phi (\Omega _1\times \Omega _2)$. We show that its linear span is dense. Let $F\in \mathcal H_\Phi (\Omega _1\times \Omega _2)$. By Fubini's theorem, for almost every $w\in \Omega _2$ we have $\varphi _2(w)>-\infty $ and $F(\cdot ,w)\in \mathcal H_{\varphi _1}(\Omega _1)$, and for such $w$ we put

\[ a_ j(w)=\int _{\Omega _1}F(z,w)\overline{e_ j(z)} \mathrm e^{-\varphi _1(z)}\, \mathrm dV_ z. \]

We claim that each $a_ j$ agrees almost everywhere with an element of $\mathcal H_{\varphi _2}(\Omega _2)$ of norm at most $\| F\| _\Phi $. Choose compact sets $K_\ell \uparrow \Omega _1$ and put $E_\ell =K_\ell \cap \{ \mathrm e^{-\varphi _1}\leq \ell \} $; these sets increase and exhaust $\Omega _1$ up to the pole set of $\varphi _1$, which has measure zero. The function $a_{j,\ell }$ obtained by integrating over $E_\ell $ instead of $\Omega _1$ is holomorphic on $\Omega _2$. Indeed, let $L\subset \Omega _2$ be compact. For $z\in E_\ell \subset K_\ell $ and $w\in L$, the integrand $F(z,w)\overline{e_ j(z)}\mathrm e^{-\varphi _1(z)}$ and all its partial derivatives in $w$ are bounded, because $F$ and its derivatives are continuous on $K_\ell \times L$, $e_ j$ is bounded on $K_\ell $, and $\mathrm e^{-\varphi _1}\leq \ell $ on $E_\ell $. Hence we may differentiate under the integral sign any number of times: $a_{j,\ell }$ is smooth, and $\partial a_{j,\ell }/\partial \bar w_ m=0$ for all $m$, because $F$ is holomorphic in $w$. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in $z$, followed by integration in $w$ against $\mathrm e^{-\varphi _2}$,

\[ \| a_ j-a_{j,\ell }\| _{\varphi _2}^2 \leq \| F\| _\Phi ^2 \int _{\Omega _1\setminus E_\ell }|e_ j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi _1}\, \mathrm dV_ z \longrightarrow 0 \]

by dominated convergence, since the sets $\Omega _1\setminus E_\ell $ decrease to a null set and $|e_ j|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi _1}$ is integrable. Since $\varphi _2\leq 0$, the holomorphic functions $a_{j,\ell }$ therefore converge to $a_ j$ in $L^2(\Omega _2)$, and hence in the sense of distributions; so $\bar\partial a_ j=0$, and $a_ j$ agrees almost everywhere with a holomorphic function, by lemma 6.4.10(1). The same Cauchy–Schwarz estimate over all of $\Omega _1$ gives $\| a_ j\| _{\varphi _2}\leq \| F\| _\Phi $, which proves the claim.

Now suppose that $F$ is orthogonal to all the products $e_ jg_ k$. By Fubini's theorem, whose use is justified by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, $\langle a_ j,g_ k\rangle _{\varphi _2}=\langle F,e_ jg_ k\rangle _\Phi =0$ for all $k$. Since the linear span of the $g_ k$ is dense in $\mathcal H_{\varphi _2}(\Omega _2)$, the element $a_ j$ of this space is orthogonal to itself, so $a_ j=0$ almost everywhere, for every $j$. Hence for almost every $w$ all the coefficients $\langle F(\cdot ,w),e_ j\rangle _{\varphi _1}$ vanish, and $F(\cdot ,w)=0$, because $F(\cdot ,w)$ is the limit of the partial sums of its expansion (theorem 6.8.9(2)). Thus $F=0$ almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere, since $F$ is continuous. Consequently, the closure $M$ of the linear span of the products $e_ jg_ k$ satisfies $M^\perp =0$, and $M=\mathcal H_\Phi (\Omega _1\times \Omega _2)$ by proposition 6.2.1.

We can now prove (6.10.3). Denote its right side by $\mathfrak b$; it is the ideal generated by the products $(f_1\circ p_1)(f_2\circ p_2)$ of local sections $f_ i$ of $\mathcal I(\varphi _ i)$. Each product $e_ jg_ k$ is a section of $\mathfrak b$. Every $F\in \mathcal H_\Phi (\Omega _1\times \Omega _2)$ is the norm limit, hence by (6.8.7) the locally uniform limit, of a sequence of finite linear combinations of these products, so its germs lie in $\mathfrak b$ by lemma 6.8.1. Since $\mathcal I(\Phi )$ is generated by the germs of the elements of $\mathcal H_\Phi (\Omega _1\times \Omega _2)$ (theorem 6.8.9(3)), we obtain $\mathcal I(\Phi )\subset \mathfrak b$. Conversely, if $|f_ i|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi _ i}$ is integrable near $x_ i$ for $i=1,2$, then $|f_1(z)f_2(w)|^2\mathrm e^{-\Phi (z,w)}$ is integrable near $(x_1,x_2)$ by Fubini's theorem; since $\mathcal I(\Phi )$ is an ideal, $\mathfrak b\subset \mathcal I(\Phi )$.

Finally, let $\varphi $ and $\psi $ be weights on $X$, and apply (6.10.2) to the weight $\varphi \circ p_1+\psi \circ p_2$ on $X\times X$ and the diagonal, which we identify with $X$. The restriction of the weight is $\varphi +\psi $, and by (6.10.3) the restriction of its multiplier ideal is generated by the products $f_1f_2$ of local sections $f_1$ of $\mathcal I(\varphi )$ and $f_2$ of $\mathcal I(\psi )$, that is, it equals $\mathcal I(\varphi )\mathcal I(\psi )$. This proves (6.10.4). $\square$
