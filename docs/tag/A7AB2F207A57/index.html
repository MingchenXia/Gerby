Chapter 13 · Singularity types, envelopes, and models · Tag A7AB2F207A57

Penalized third order

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Lemma 16.2.17

Lemma 16.2.17. Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$, let $\beta \in \mathbb R$ and $0\leq t\leq 1$, and let $a$ be a smooth real function with $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a>0$ and

\[ (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a)^ n=\mathrm e^{\beta (a-tf)}\kappa ^ n. \]

Suppose that $C_0^{-1}h\leq g\leq C_0h$ on $X$ for a constant $C_0\geq 1$, and let $\Psi $ be the tensor of lemma 16.2.16(2) for $g$. Then

\[ S=|\Psi |_ g^2=g^{i\bar p}g^{j\bar q}g_{m\bar l}\, \Psi ^ m_{ij}\overline{\Psi ^ l_{pq}}\leq C, \]

where $C$ depends only on $X$, $\omega $, $\kappa $, $\beta $, $C_0$ and $f$, but not on $t$ or $a$. Consequently, in each chart of a fixed finite atlas, the first derivatives of the functions $g_{i\bar j}$ are bounded on every compact subset by a constant of the same kind.

Proof. In this proof $C,C_1,C_2,\ldots $ denote constants depending only on $X$, $\omega $, $\kappa $, $\beta $, $C_0$ and $f$. We show that $D_ gS\geq -C_1(S+1)$ and $D_ gH\geq C_0^{-1}S-C_2$, where $H=\operatorname {tr}_ hg$, and apply the maximum principle to $S+KH$ for a large constant $K$.

By lemma 16.2.16(1),(2), $S$ is a smooth function on $X$. The fixed smooth tensors $\widehat R$ (the tensor of lemma 16.2.16(3) for $h$), $\widehat\nabla \widehat R$, $\tau =\operatorname {Ric}(k)+\beta k+\beta t(f_{i\bar j})$ and $\widehat\nabla \tau $ have $h$-norms bounded on $X$ independently of $t\in [0,1]$, because they are smooth on the compact manifold $X$ and depend affinely on $t$; by the assumption on $g$, their $g$-norms are bounded as well.

The Laplacian of $\Psi $. Let $R$ be the tensor of lemma 16.2.16(3) for $g$. Since $\Psi $ has only holomorphic indices,

\[ \nabla _{\bar q}\Psi ^ m_{ij}=\partial _{\bar q}\Psi ^ m_{ij}=\widehat R^ m{}_{i\bar qj}-R^ m{}_{i\bar qj}. \]

Put $D_ gT=g^{p\bar q}\nabla _ p\nabla _{\bar q}T$ and $\overline D_ gT=g^{p\bar q}\nabla _{\bar q}\nabla _ pT$ for tensors $T$. On a function $u$ both operators reduce to $D_ gu=g^{p\bar q}\partial _ p\partial _{\bar q}u$, since $\nabla _ p$ adds no term to the antiholomorphic index of $\partial _{\bar q}u$, and $\nabla _{\bar q}$ none to the holomorphic index of $\partial _ pu$. Applying $g^{p\bar q}\nabla _ p$ to the last identity and using $\nabla g^{-1}=0$ and lemma 16.2.16(3), we obtain

16.18
\begin{equation} \label{eq:elliptic-calabi-tensor} D_ g\Psi ^ m_{ij} =g^{p\bar q}\nabla _ p\widehat R^ m{}_{i\bar qj} -\nabla _ iR_ j{}^ m. \end{equation}

By lemma 16.2.16(4) and the first identity of (3), $\overline D_ g\Psi =D_ g\Psi +\mathcal R\Psi $, where

\[ (\mathcal R\Psi )^ m_{ij} =-R_ a{}^ m\Psi ^ a_{ij}+R_ i{}^ a\Psi ^ m_{aj}+R_ j{}^ a\Psi ^ m_{ia}. \]

The Laplacian of $S$. By lemma 16.2.16(1) and $\nabla _ r\overline{\Psi ^ l_{pq}}=\overline{\nabla _{\bar r}\Psi ^ l_{pq}}$, which holds because $\nabla _ r$ adds no term to antiholomorphic indices,

\[ \partial _ rS=g^{i\bar p}g^{j\bar q}g_{m\bar l}\bigl (\nabla _ r\Psi ^ m_{ij}\, \overline{\Psi ^ l_{pq}}+\Psi ^ m_{ij}\, \overline{\nabla _{\bar r}\Psi ^ l_{pq}}\bigr ). \]

The right side is a tensor with one lower holomorphic index $r$, on which $\nabla _{\bar s}$ acts as $\partial _{\bar s}$. Applying lemma 16.2.16(1) once more and contracting with $g^{r\bar s}$, we obtain, with the inner products and norms defined by $g$,

\[ D_ gS=|\nabla \Psi |_ g^2+|\overline\nabla \Psi |_ g^2+\langle \overline D_ g\Psi ,\Psi \rangle _ g+\langle \Psi ,D_ g\Psi \rangle _ g, \]

where $\nabla \Psi =(\nabla _ r\Psi ^ m_{ij})$ and $\overline\nabla \Psi =(\nabla _{\bar s}\Psi ^ m_{ij})$; for the last term we used $\overline{g^{r\bar s}}=g^{s\bar r}$. Inserting $\overline D_ g\Psi =D_ g\Psi +\mathcal R\Psi $, we get

16.19
\begin{equation} \label{eq:elliptic-calabi-norm} D_ gS=|\nabla \Psi |_ g^2+|\overline\nabla \Psi |_ g^2 +2\operatorname{Re}\langle D_ g\Psi ,\Psi \rangle _ g +\langle \mathcal R\Psi ,\Psi \rangle _ g; \end{equation}

in particular $\langle \mathcal R\Psi ,\Psi \rangle _ g$ is real.

Bounds from the equation. In every chart, $\log \det g=\log \det k+\beta (a-tf)$, so

\[ R_{i\bar j}=-\partial _ i\partial _{\bar j}\log \det g=\operatorname {Ric}(k)_{i\bar j}-\beta a_{i\bar j}+\beta tf_{i\bar j}=\tau _{i\bar j}-\beta g_{i\bar j}. \]

Hence $R_ i{}^ m=g^{m\bar l}\tau _{i\bar l}-\beta \delta ^ m_ i$ is bounded in $g$-norm, and so $|\langle \mathcal R\Psi ,\Psi \rangle _ g|\leq CS$. Moreover $\nabla _ iR_ j{}^ m=g^{m\bar l}\nabla _ i\tau _{j\bar l}$, because $\nabla g^{-1}=0$ and $\nabla \delta =0$. For a tensor the difference $\nabla _ p-\widehat\nabla _ p$ consists of the connection terms with $\Gamma -\widehat\Gamma =\Psi $ in place of $\Gamma $; thus $\nabla _ i\tau _{j\bar l}=\widehat\nabla _ i\tau _{j\bar l}-\Psi ^ a_{ij}\tau _{a\bar l}$ and

\[ \nabla _ p\widehat R^ m{}_{i\bar qj}=\widehat\nabla _ p\widehat R^ m{}_{i\bar qj}+\Psi ^ m_{pa}\widehat R^ a{}_{i\bar qj}-\Psi ^ a_{pi}\widehat R^ m{}_{a\bar qj}-\Psi ^ a_{pj}\widehat R^ m{}_{i\bar qa}. \]

By (16.18) it follows that $|D_ g\Psi |_ g\leq C(1+S^{1/2})$. Discarding the two nonnegative gradient terms in (16.19), we conclude that

\[ D_ gS\geq -2C(1+S^{1/2})S^{1/2}-CS\geq -C_1(S+1). \]

A compensating term. We use the trace $H=\operatorname {tr}_ hg$. Let $x\in X$, and take the coordinates of lemma 16.2.14 at $x$. By that lemma, identity (16.12) holds at $x$, with $|E|\leq C_ EH\operatorname {tr}_ gh\leq C$ by the assumption on $g$, and $D_ h\log \det g=D_ h\log \det k+\beta (H-\operatorname {tr}_ hk-tD_ hf)$ is bounded by $C$. At $x$ the coefficients $\widehat\Gamma $ vanish, so $\Psi ^ m_{ij}=\lambda _ m^{-1}\partial _ ig_{j\bar m}$ and

\[ S=\sum _{i,p,q}\frac{|\partial _ pg_{i\bar q}|^2}{\lambda _ p\lambda _ i\lambda _ q}. \]

Hence the quadratic sum in (16.12) is at least $(\min _ p\lambda _ p)S\geq C_0^{-1}S$, and $D_ gH\geq C_0^{-1}S-C_2$ at $x$. Both sides are functions on $X$, so this inequality holds everywhere.

The maximum principle. Choose $K$ with $KC_0^{-1}\geq C_1+1$, and let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $S+KH$. There the complex Hessian of $S+KH$ is negative semidefinite, so

\[ 0\geq D_ g(S+KH)\geq (KC_0^{-1}-C_1)S-C_1-KC_2\geq S-C_1-KC_2. \]

Thus $S\leq C_1+KC_2$ at $x_0$. Since $H\leq nC_0$ on $X$, the function $S+KH$, and hence $S$, is bounded on $X$.

First derivatives of $g$. In a chart of the fixed atlas, $\partial _ pg_{i\bar q}=\widehat\nabla _ pg_{i\bar q}+\widehat\Gamma ^ l_{pi}g_{l\bar q}=g_{m\bar q}\Psi ^ m_{pi}+\widehat\Gamma ^ l_{pi}g_{l\bar q}$ by lemma 16.2.16(2). On a compact subset of the chart, $h$ lies between two positive multiples of the identity matrix and $\widehat\Gamma $ is bounded, so both terms are bounded, by the bounds for $S$ and $g$. Finally $\partial _{\bar p}g_{i\bar q}=\overline{\partial _ pg_{q\bar i}}$, so all first derivatives of the $g_{i\bar j}$ in the underlying real coordinates are bounded. $\square$
