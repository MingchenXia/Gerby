Properties of non-pluripolar products

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 2.4 Properties of non-pluripolar products

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta ,\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ be closed real smooth $(1,1)$-forms on $X$.

We write

2.19
\begin{equation} \label{eq:PSHpos} \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}=\left\{ \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n>0 \right\} . \end{equation}

The non-pluripolar product $\theta _{\varphi }^ n$ is well-defined thanks to proposition 2.2.7.

Remark 2.4.1. Suppose that $X$ is a connected complex manifold of dimension $0$, namely, $X$ is a single point. In this case, by definition, the non-pluripolar product $\theta _{\varphi }^ n$ is given by the current of integration at the unique point. So $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}=\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cong \mathbb {R}$ in this case and $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n=1$ for all $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Recall the following basic result:

Proposition 2.4.2. Assume that $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ is non-empty. Then the cohomology class $\{ \theta \} $ is big.

See [Proposition 1.22, BEGZ10]. We recall a few basic facts about the non-pluripolar masses.

Proposition 2.4.3. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold $Y$ and $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then

\[ \int _ Y (\pi ^*T_1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\pi ^*T_ n)=\int _ X T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n. \]

Similarly, if $S_1,\ldots ,S_ n$ are closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $Y$, then

\[ \int _ Y S_1\wedge \cdots \wedge S_ n=\int _ X \left(\pi _*S_1\right)\wedge \cdots \wedge \left(\pi _*S_ n\right). \]
Here $\pi ^*T_ i$ is defined as follows: Write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$ for some closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta _ i$ on $X$ and $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$, then $\pi ^*T_ i=\pi ^*\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\pi ^*\varphi _ i$. The pull-back $\pi ^*T_ i$ is clearly independent of the choices of $\theta _ i$ and $\varphi _ i$.

Proof. The assertions follow from proposition 2.2.2 1 on the Zariski open set where $\pi $ is an isomorphism. The exceptional locus is pluripolar, and the relevant non-pluripolar products put no mass on pluripolar sets by proposition 2.2.2 4. $\square$

Theorem 2.4.4. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then the map

\[ [0,1] \ni t\mapsto \log \int _ X \theta _{t\varphi _1+(1-t)\varphi _0}^ n \]

is concave.

More generally, given closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ on $X$, we have

\[ \int _ X T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n\geq \prod _{i=1}^ n \left( \int _ X T_ i^ n \right)^{1/n}. \]
See [DDNL19log] for the proof.

Remark 2.4.5. Here and in the sequel, when we write expressions like $t\varphi +(1-t)\psi $ for $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$, we will follow the convention that when $t=0$, the value is $\psi $ and when $t=1$, the value is $\varphi $.

We shall write

2.20
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Vtheta} V_{\theta }=\sup \left\{ \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):\varphi \leq 0 \right\} . \end{equation}

Note that the upper semicontinuous regularization $V_{\theta }^*$ of $V_{\theta }$ is itself a candidate of (2.20) if $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $ as follows from proposition 1.2.2, therefore, $V_{\theta }=V_{\theta }^*\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ if $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $.

The function $V_{\theta }$ should be regarded as a canonical representative of the least singular potentials in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We recall the following result:

Lemma 2.4.6. Suppose that $\{ \theta \} $ is big. The potential $V_{\theta }$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$.

Recall that $\operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$ is the non-Kähler locus introduced in definition 1.7.8.

Proof. By definition of $\operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$, it suffices to show that if $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has analytic singularities and $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current, then $V_{\theta }$ is locally bounded outside $\{ \varphi =-\infty \} $. After replacing $\varphi $ by $\varphi -\sup _ X\varphi $, the function $\varphi $ is a candidate in (2.20); hence $V_{\theta }\geq \varphi $ and $V_{\theta }\leq 0$. Since $\varphi $ is locally bounded outside its pole set, so is $V_{\theta }$. $\square$

The non-pluripolar product has a lower semicontinuity property.

Theorem 2.4.7 (Semicontinuity theorem). Let $\varphi _ j, \varphi _ j^ k\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ ($k\in \mathbb {N}$, $j=1,\ldots ,n$). Let $\chi _ k,\chi $ ($k\in \mathbb {N}$) be non-negative uniformly bounded quasi-continuous functions on $X$ such that $(\chi _ k)_ k$ converges to $\chi $ in capacity. Assume that for any $j=1,\ldots ,n$, as $k\to \infty $, either $\varphi _ j^ k$ decreases to $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ or increases to $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ almost everywhere. Then

2.21
\begin{equation} \label{eq:semicon1} \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\int _ X\chi _ k \, \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k} \geq \int _ X \chi \, \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}. \end{equation}

If in addition,

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty }\int _ X\theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k} =\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}, \]

then

\[ \chi _ k \, \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\rightharpoonup \chi \, \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n} \]
weakly as measures
Note. Recall that the weak convergence of Radon measures is stronger than the weak convergence as currents in general. When the Radon measures have uniformly bounded total variation, they are equivalent.
. In particular, the limit in (2.21) exists and equality holds in (2.21).
See [Theorem 2.6, DDNLsurv] for the proof. The non-pluripolar mass is a monotone quantity with respect to the singularity type.
Theorem 2.4.8 (Monotonicity theorem). Let $\varphi _ j,\psi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ for $j=1,\ldots ,n$. Assume that $\varphi _ j \succeq \psi _ j$
Note. See definition 1.5.2 for the notation.
for every $j$, then
\[ \int _{X} \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n} \geq \int _{X} \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\psi _ n}. \]

In particular, if $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\varphi \succeq \psi $, then

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n\geq \int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

In other words, the non-pluripolar mass increases as the potentials become less singular.

See [Theorem 1.1, DDNL18mono]. We will prove a vast extension of this theorem in proposition 6.1.16.

By this theorem, the non-pluripolar mass $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n$ can be used as a rough measure of the singularities of $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. In section 3.1, we shall refine this measure by defining the notion of $P$-envelope.

As a corollary, we obtain that

Corollary 2.4.9. Fix a directed set $I$. For each $j=1,\ldots ,n$, take an increasing net $(\varphi _ j^ i)_{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$, uniformly bounded from above. Set

\[ \varphi _ j\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi _ j^ i,\quad j=1,\ldots ,n. \]

Then

2.22
\begin{equation} \label{eq:increseqnppcont} \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ i}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ i} = \int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}. \end{equation}

In particular, if the nets are indeed sequences, we have

\[ \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ i}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ i} \rightharpoonup \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}. \]

Proof. The final assertion follows from (2.22) together with theorem 2.4.7. It remains to establish (2.22).

We may assume that $I$ is infinite as there is nothing to prove otherwise. Thanks to theorem 2.4.8, we already know the $\leq $ inequality in (2.22). We prove the reverse inequality. When $I\cong \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ as directed sets, the reverse inequality follows from theorem 2.4.7. In general, by Choquet's lemma proposition 1.2.3, we can find a countable infinite subset $R\subseteq I$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{r\in R}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi ^ r_ j=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi ^ i_ j \]

for all $j=1,\ldots ,n$. We fix a bijection $R\cong \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. For any $j=1,\ldots ,n$, we will then denote elements $\varphi _ j^ r$ ($r\in R$) by $\varphi _ j^1,\varphi _ j^2,\dots $. We shall write

\[ \psi _ j^ a=\varphi _ j^1\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _ j^ a \]

for each $a\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$.

For every $a$ we may choose $i_ a\in I$ dominating the finitely many indices corresponding to $\varphi _ j^1,\ldots ,\varphi _ j^ a$ for all $j$. It then follows from the monotonicity theorem that

\[ \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ i}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ i}\geq \lim _{a\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{1,\psi _1^ a}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\psi _ n^ a}. \]

From the special case mentioned above, we know that the right-hand side is exactly the right-hand side of (2.22), so we conclude. $\square$

We prove an interesting inequality about the Monge–Ampère measure of the maximum of two potentials.

Lemma 2.4.10. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

2.23
\begin{equation} \label{eq:thetavarphilorpsi1} \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n\geq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \geq \psi \} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi <\psi \} }\, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \end{equation}

In particular, if $\varphi \leq \psi $, then

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =\psi \} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =\psi \} }\, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]
At a first sight, (2.23) might seem trivial, and it is if we replace $\{ \varphi \geq \psi \} $ by $\{ \varphi >\psi \} $. The difficulty really lies on understanding the contact set $\{ \varphi =\psi \} $.

Proof. For each $k\in \mathbb {N}$, we set

\[ \psi _ k=\psi \lor (V_{\theta }-k),\quad \varphi _ k=\varphi \lor (V_{\theta }-k). \]

For each $t>0$ and each $k\in \mathbb {N}$, we have

\[ \begin{split} \theta _{\psi _ k\lor (\varphi _ k+t)}^ n\geq & \mathds {1}_{\{ \psi _ k>\varphi _ k+t\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ k\lor (\varphi _ k+t)}^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \psi _ k<\varphi _ k+t\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ k\lor (\varphi _ k+t)}^ n\\ =& \mathds {1}_{\{ \psi _ k>\varphi _ k+t\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ k}^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \psi _ k<\varphi _ k+t\} }\, \theta _{\varphi _ k}^ n, \end{split} \]

where the equality follows from the plurifine locality of non-pluripolar products proposition 2.2.2 1. We observe that for any $t>0$, $\psi _ k\lor (\varphi _ k+t)\sim \psi _ k\lor \varphi _ k$. Therefore, the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 guarantees that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\psi _ k\lor (\varphi _ k+t)}^ n=\int _ X\theta _{\psi _ k\lor \varphi _ k}^ n. \]

As $t\to 0+$,

\[ \theta _{\psi _ k\lor (\varphi _ k+t)}^ n\rightharpoonup \theta _{\psi _ k\lor \varphi _ k}^ n \]

by theorem 2.4.7.

Letting $t\to 0+$, we conclude that

\[ \theta _{\psi _ k\lor \varphi _ k}^ n\geq \mathds {1}_{\{ \psi _ k>\varphi _ k\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ k}^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \psi _ k\leq \varphi _ k\} }\, \theta _{\varphi _ k}^ n. \]

In particular, multiplying both sides by $\mathds {1}_{\{ \min \{ \varphi ,\psi \} >V_{\theta }-k\} }$ and applying proposition 2.2.2 1 again, we find that

\[ \begin{split} \mathds {1}_{\{ \min \{ \varphi ,\psi \} >V_{\theta }-k\} }\, \theta _{\psi \lor \varphi }^ n\geq & \mathds {1}_{\{ \min \{ \varphi ,\psi \} >V_{\theta }-k\} \cap \{ \psi \leq \varphi \} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n \\ & +\mathds {1}_{\{ \min \{ \varphi ,\psi \} >V_{\theta }-k\} \cap \{ \psi >\varphi \} }\, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \end{split} \]

Letting $k\to \infty $, we conclude (2.23). $\square$

Corollary 2.4.11. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ so that $\varphi _ i\to \varphi $ in $L^1$. Assume that $\varphi _ i\leq 0$ for all $i\in I$. Then

2.28
\begin{equation} \label{eq:contact_int_usc} \varlimsup _{i\in I} \int _{\{ \varphi _ i=0\} }\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n\leq \int _{\{ \varphi =0\} } \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. Observe that $\varphi \leq 0$ by proposition 1.5.4.

Step 1. We first assume that $I\cong \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ as directed sets. Namely, $(\varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ is a sequence.

For each $k>0$, let

\[ \psi _ k=\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq k}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi _ j. \]

Then $(\psi _ k)_{k}$ is a decreasing sequence in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with limit $\varphi $, see corollary 1.2.8 for example.

Thanks to 2.4.10, for each $k>0$, we have

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ k=\psi _ k\} }\, \theta _{\varphi _ k}^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ k=\psi _ k\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ k}^ n. \]

Multiplying both sides with $\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ k=0\} }$, we find that

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ k=0\} }\, \theta _{\varphi _ k}^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ k=0\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ k}^ n. \]

Take $b,C>0$. For each $k>0$, we have

\[ \begin{split} \int _{\{ \varphi _ k=0\} } \theta _{\varphi _ k}^ n\leq \int _{\{ \varphi _ k=0\} } \theta _{\psi _ k}^ n\leq \int _{\{ \psi _ k=0\} } \theta _{\psi _ k}^ n\\ =\int _{\{ \psi _ k=0\} } \theta _{\psi _ k\lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n\leq \int _ X \mathrm{e}^{b\psi _ k}\, \theta _{\psi _ k\lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n, \end{split} \]

where the equality part follows from proposition 2.2.2 1.

Letting $k\to \infty $ with the help of theorem 2.4.7, we conclude that

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\int _{\{ \varphi _ k=0\} } \theta _{\varphi _ k}^ n\leq \int _ X \mathrm{e}^{b\varphi }\, \theta _{\varphi \lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n. \]

Letting $b\to \infty $ and then $C\to \infty $, we conclude (2.28).

Step 2. We now handle the general net case.

Fix a volume form on $X$, so that we can talk about the $L^1$-norm on $X$ in the sequel.

Suppose that (2.28) fails. Then there is $\delta >0$ such that, for every $i_0\in I$, one can find $i\geq i_0$ satisfying

\[ \int _{\{ \varphi _ i=0\} }\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n>\int _{\{ \varphi =0\} }\theta _{\varphi }^ n+\delta . \]

Since $\varphi _ i\to \varphi $ in $L^1$, for every $k>0$ there is $a_ k\in I$ such that

\[ \| \varphi _ i-\varphi \| _{L^1}<k^{-1},\quad i\geq a_ k. \]

We can then recursively choose an increasing sequence $(i_ k)_{k>0}$ in $I$ with $i_ k\geq a_ k$ and

\[ \int _{\{ \varphi _{i_ k}=0\} }\theta _{\varphi _{i_ k}}^ n>\int _{\{ \varphi =0\} }\theta _{\varphi }^ n+\delta . \]

This sequence converges to $\varphi $ in $L^1$, contradicting Step 1. $\square$

Next we introduce an envelope construction, which will be repeatedly used in the sequel.

Definition 2.4.12. Given a function $f\colon X\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$, we define $P_{\theta }(f)$ as follows:

2.31
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Pthetafdef} P_{\theta }(f)\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\left\{ \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \varphi \leq f\textup{ quasi-everywhere} \right\} \footnotemark . \end{equation}
Note. In the original article [DDNLsurv], the authors required that $\varphi \leq f$ everywhere. But in the proof of their Theorem 2.7 (theorem 2.4.14 below), they actually relied on the current definition. In the most relevant case, if $f$ is $\mathcal{F}$-upper semicontinuous (for example, if $f$ is upper semicontinuous), then these two definitions coincide. This is due to 1.3.17 and proposition 1.3.15.
The function $P_{\theta }(f)$ is either constantly $\pm \infty $ or lies in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Moreover, given another function $g\colon X\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$, equal to $f$ quasi-everywhere, we have $P_{\theta }(f)=P_{\theta }(g)$. In particular, it makes sense to talk about $P_{\theta }(f)$ even if $f$ is only defined outside a pluripolar set.

We also observe that

2.32
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Pthetafleqfqe} P_{\theta }(f)\leq f\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \end{equation}

This is a consequence of proposition 1.2.3 and proposition 1.2.5.

Lemma 2.4.13. Fix a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Let $f\colon X\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ be a lower semicontinuous function. Assume that $P_{\theta }(f)\not\equiv \infty $. Then $P_{\theta }(f)$ is locally bounded outside $\operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$. Set $U\coloneqq \{ P_{\theta }(f)<f\} \setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$, then

2.33
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bal_ddcn0} \Bigl (\theta |_ U+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta }(f)|_ U\Bigr )^ n=0. \end{equation}

Proof. First observe that $V_{\theta }+\inf _ X f$ is a candidate in the definition (2.31). So

\[ P_{\theta }(f)\geq V_{\theta }+\inf _ X f, \]

and in particular $P_{\theta }(f)\not\equiv -\infty $.

In particular, $P_{\theta }(f)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. It is therefore bounded from above. By 2.4.6, it is locally bounded away from $\operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$.

Now fix $x\in U$, and $\epsilon >0$ so that

\[ P_{\theta }(f)(x)<f(x)-\epsilon . \]

Take holomorphic balls $B\Subset B'\Subset U$ containing $x$. After shrinking $B$, we can guarantee that

2.34
\begin{equation} \label{eq:PthetafBp_temp1} \left.P_{\theta }(f)\right|_{B}<f(x)-\epsilon ,\quad f|_ B> f(x)-\epsilon . \end{equation}

Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be the balayage of $P_{\theta }(f)$ on $B$, as constructed in proposition 1.5.9. By the domination principle proposition 1.2.15, we find that

\[ \varphi |_{B}\leq f(x)-\epsilon <f|_{B}. \]

Since $\varphi =P_{\theta }(f)$ outside $B$, we conclude that

\[ \varphi \leq f\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]

From this it follows that $\varphi \leq P_{\theta }(f)$, and hence equality holds. But then (2.33) holds automatically on $B$ since $\varphi $ is constructed by balayage. Since $x$ and $B$ are arbitrary, we conclude (2.33). $\square$

Theorem 2.4.14. Let $f\colon X\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ be a quasi-continuous function.

Assume that $P_{\theta }(f)\not\equiv \pm \infty $. Then $P_{\theta }(f)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and

2.35
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Pthetafconc} \int _{\{ P_{\theta }(f)<f\} } \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n=0. \end{equation}

Thanks to (2.32), we can rewrite (2.35) as

\[ \int _{\{ P_{\theta }(f)\neq f\} } \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n=0. \]
We shall say that $\theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n$ is carried by the contact set $\{ P_{\theta }(f)=f\} $.
Note. It is tempting to say that $\theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n$ is supported on the contact set $\{ P_{\theta }(f)=f\} $, as people are already doing in the literature. It should be mentioned that this is an abuse of the language, since the support of $\theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n$ (as a closed subset of $X$) could be much larger in general. One could probably introduce a notion of plurifine support, similar to what Fuglede did in the classical potential theory [Fug72].

Proof. Step 1. We first reduce to the case where $f$ is bounded.

Step 1.1. Reduce to the case where $f\leq 0$.

Take $C\in \mathbb {R}$ so that $P_{\theta }(f)\leq C$. Then

\[ P_{\theta }(f)=P_{\theta }\Bigl ( \min \{ f,C\} \Bigr ). \]

Thus we can replace $f$ by $\min \{ f,C\} $. Replacing $f$ by $f-\sup _ X f$, we reduce easily to the case where $f\leq 0$.

Step 1.2. Reduce to the case where $f$ is bounded. Assume that in this case, the theorem is known. We prove (2.35) for $f\leq 0$.

For each $j>0$, we have

2.36
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Pthetafjint0_temp1} \int _{\{ P_{\theta }(f_ j)<f_ j\} } \theta _{P_{\theta }(f_ j)}^ n=0, \end{equation}

where $f_ j=f\lor (-j)$.

Now fix $C>0$ and two open sets $G'\Subset G\Subset X\setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$. Fix a smooth function $\chi \colon X\rightarrow [0,1]$ so that $\chi |_{G'}\equiv 1$ and $\chi $ is supported in $G$.

Define

\[ U_ C\coloneqq G \cap \left\{ P_{\theta }(f)>V_{\theta }-C \right\} ,\quad U'_ C\coloneqq G' \cap \left\{ P_{\theta }(f)>V_{\theta }-C \right\} . \]

Then $U_ C$ is $\mathcal{F}$-open. It follows from proposition 2.2.2 1 that for any $j>0$,

\[ \mathds {1}_{U_ C}\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f_ j)\lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n=\mathds {1}_{U_ C}\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f_ j)}^ n. \]

In particular, thanks to (2.36),

2.37
\begin{equation} \label{eq:intU1mexpPthetaf_temp1} \int _{U_ C} \left( 1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(f_ j)-f_ j} \right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f_ j)\lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n=0. \end{equation}

Note that the $P_{\theta }(f_ j)\lor (V_{\theta }-C)$'s for various $j$ are uniformly bounded from below on $U_ C$, thanks to 2.4.6.

Next we claim that $(P_{\theta }(f_ j))_ j$ is decreasing and converges to $P_{\theta }(f)$.

The sequence is decreasing; let us compute its limit. In fact, $P_{\theta }(f_ j)\leq f_ j$ quasi-everywhere. It follows that $\inf _ j P_{\theta }(f_ j)\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and hence

\[ \inf _{j>0} P_{\theta }(f_ j)\leq P_{\theta }(f). \]

The reverse inequality is trivial, and our assertion follows.

Since $P_{\theta }(f)\not\equiv -\infty $, we know that the set $\{ f=-\infty \} $ is pluripolar. It follows that $P_{\theta }(f_ j)-f_ j$ converges to the following function $g\colon X\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ in capacity:

\[ g(x)=\begin{cases} P_{\theta }(f)(x)-f(x),& \textup{if } f(x)\neq -\infty ;\\ -\infty ,& \textup{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

Therefore,

\[ 1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(f_ j)-f_ j}\xlongrightarrow {\mathrm{C}} 1-\mathrm{e}^{g}. \]

Next we claim that

2.40
\begin{equation} \label{eq:intU1mexpPthetaf_temp2} \int _{U'_ C} \left( 1-\mathrm{e}^{g} \right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n=0. \end{equation}

Since $g\leq 0$ quasi-everywhere, the left-hand side of (2.40) is non-negative. It suffices to prove the $\leq $ direction.

We wish to let $j\to \infty $ directly in (2.37), but since $U_ C$ is not open in general, this cannot be done directly. In the sequel, we shall slightly enlarge $U_ C$ to get an open set and then take the limit.

Fix $\epsilon >0$. We can find an open subset $W\Subset X\setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$ so that

2.41
\begin{equation} \label{eq:CapCinvthetaWmusmall_temp1} \operatorname{Cap}_{C^{-1}\theta }(W\setminus U_ C)<\epsilon . \end{equation}

For example, we can construct $W$ as follows. By example 2.3.5, we can find an open set $A\subseteq G$ so that $\operatorname{Cap}_{C^{-1}\theta }(A)<\epsilon $ and $P_{\theta }(f)-V_{\theta }$ is continuous on $G\setminus A$. Then $U_ C\cap (G\setminus A)$ is relatively open in $G\setminus A$, so we may choose an open set $W_0\subseteq G$ such that

\[ W_0\cap (G\setminus A)=U_ C\cap (G\setminus A). \]

Taking $W=W_0\cup A$, we get $U_ C\subseteq W$ and $W\setminus U_ C\subseteq A$, hence (2.41).

Thanks to (2.41) and (2.37), we have

\[ \int _ W \chi \left( 1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(f_ j)-f_ j} \right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f_ j)\lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n\leq C^ n\epsilon . \]

Letting $j\to \infty $ and applying theorem 2.3.8, we find that

\[ \int _{U'_ C} \left( 1-\mathrm{e}^{g} \right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)\lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n\leq \int _ W \chi \left( 1-\mathrm{e}^{g} \right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)\lor (V_{\theta }-C)}^ n\leq C^ n\epsilon . \]

Since $\epsilon >0$ is arbitrary, (2.40) follows.

Now letting $C\to \infty $ in (2.40), we find

\[ \int _{G'}\left( 1-\mathrm{e}^{g} \right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n=0. \]

Since $G'\Subset X\setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$ is arbitrary, and $\operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )\subseteq X$ is a proper analytic subset, we finally conclude

\[ \int _ X \left( 1-\mathrm{e}^{g} \right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n=0. \]

In other words, $P_{\theta }(f)=f$ almost everywhere with respect to $\theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n$. This proves (2.35).

Step 2. We now assume that $-C'\leq f\leq 0$ for some $C'>0$.

For each $j>0$, take an open subset $V_ j\subseteq X$ so that

  1. $\operatorname{Cap}_{C'^{-1}\theta }(V_ j)\leq 2^{-j-1}$, and

  2. $f|_{X\setminus V_ j}$ is continuous.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the $V_ j$'s are decreasing. Take a continuous function $f_ j\colon X\rightarrow [-C',0]$ extending $f|_{X\setminus V_ j}$. This is always possible thanks to Tietze's extension theorem. For each $j>0$, we let

\[ h_ j\coloneqq \sup _{k\geq j} f_ k. \]

Then $h_ j$ is lower semicontinuous and $h_ j$ agrees with $f$ outside $V_ j$. Set

\[ h=\inf _{j>0} h_ j. \]

Then the set $\{ h\neq f\} $ is contained in the intersection of the $V_ j$'s and hence is a pluripolar set, thanks to theorem 2.3.4. In particular, $P_{\theta }(f)=P_{\theta }(h)$.

Now we can apply 2.4.13 to conclude that

\[ \int _{X\setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )} \left(1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(h_ j)-h_ j} \right) \theta _{P_{\theta }(h_ j)}^ n=0 \]

for each $j>0$.

Fix two open subsets $G'\Subset G\Subset X\setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$. Note that $-C'\leq h_ j\leq 0$. In particular,

\[ V_{\theta }-C'\leq P_{\theta }(h_ j)\leq V_{\theta }. \]

Hence,

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _ G \left| 1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(h_ j)-f}\right|\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(h_ j)}^ n\\ =& \int _{G\cap V_ j} \left|1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(h_ j)-f}\right|\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(h_ j)}^ n+\int _{G\setminus V_ j} \left|1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(h_ j)-h_ j}\right|\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(h_ j)}^ n\\ =& \int _{G\cap V_ j} \left|1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(h_ j)-f}\right|\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(h_ j)}^ n\\ \leq & \int _{G\cap V_ j} \left( 1+\mathrm{e}^{C'}\right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(h_ j)}^ n\\ \leq & \left( 1+\mathrm{e}^{C'}\right) \cdot 2^{-j-1} C'^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Now we can apply the same arguments as in Step 1.2 to conclude that

\[ \int _{G'}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{P_{\theta }(f)-f}\right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta }(f)}^ n=0. \]

Since $G'\Subset X\setminus \operatorname{nK}(\{ \theta \} )$, (2.35) follows. $\square$

The following lemma is striking in that we begin only with an upper bound of $\varphi $, but at the end of the day, we get a lower bound almost for free. This powerful method will be employed again and again in the whole book. A more general version will be proved in theorem 6.1.17, after introducing the terminology of $P$-preorder in chapter 6.

Lemma 2.4.15. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $\varphi \preceq \psi $ and $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$. Then for any

2.47
\begin{equation} \label{eq:arangetemp} a\in \left( 1, \left(\frac{\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n}{\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n}\right)^{1/n} \right)\footnotemark , \end{equation}

there is $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ such that

2.48
\begin{equation} \label{eq:path_env} a^{-1}\eta +\left(1-a^{-1}\right)\psi \leq \varphi . \end{equation}
Note. The fraction in (2.47) is understood as $\infty $ if either $\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n$ or $n=0$. Thanks to the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8, the interval (2.47) is non-empty.

We write

2.49
\begin{equation} \label{eq:perversePtheta} \begin{aligned} P_{\theta }\Bigl (a\varphi +(1-a)\psi \Bigr )\coloneqq & \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\left\{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): a^{-1}\eta +\left(1-a^{-1}\right)\psi \leq \varphi \right\} \\ \in & \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \end{aligned} \end{equation}

Note that if we regard $a\varphi +(1-a)\psi $ as a function defined outside the pluripolar set $\{ \varphi =\psi =-\infty \} $ on $X$, then (2.49) coincides with the envelope in the sense of (2.31).

Observe that

2.52
\begin{equation} \label{eq:P_path_everywhere_ineq} a^{-1}P_{\theta }\Bigl (a\varphi +(1-a)\psi \Bigr )+(1-a^{-1})\psi \leq \varphi . \end{equation}

In fact, this equation holds outside a pluripolar set by proposition 1.2.5, hence it holds everywhere by proposition 1.2.6.

Finally, observe that

\[ P_{\theta }\Bigl (a\varphi +(1-a)\psi \Bigr )\preceq P_{\theta }\Bigl (a\varphi +(1-a)\varphi \Bigr )=\varphi . \]

If $\varphi \leq \psi $, the same argument gives

\[ P_{\theta }\Bigl (a\varphi +(1-a)\psi \Bigr )\leq \varphi . \]

Proof. Without loss of generality, we may assume that $\varphi \leq \psi \leq 0$.

Step 1. We show the existence of $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (2.48).

Step 1.1. We first consider the case where $\psi \sim \varphi $. In this case, we can take $\eta =\varphi -C$ enough $C>0$. But since by (2.52), we also have $P_{\theta }(a\varphi +(1-a)\psi )\leq \psi $, we find that

\[ P_{\theta }\Bigl (a\varphi +(1-a)\psi \Bigr )\sim \varphi \sim \psi \]

in this case.

Step 1.2. We make a first reduction of the problem.

Now for each $\gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we let

\[ P_{\theta }[\gamma ]\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta \leq \min \{ \gamma +C,0\} \textup{ for some }C>0 \Bigr \} \footnotemark . \]
Note. This is precisely the $P$-envelope to be introduced later in definition 3.1.4.

Observe that due to corollary 2.4.9 and theorem 2.4.8, we have

2.53
\begin{equation} \label{eq:intthetaP_pr_mass_temp1} \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\gamma ]}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\gamma }^ n. \end{equation}

Further, $0\geq P_{\theta }[\gamma ]\geq \gamma $ if $\gamma \leq 0$.

We now define an increasing transfinite sequence $(\psi _{\alpha })_{\alpha }$ consisting of non-positive functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ as follows:

  1. $\psi _0=\psi $;

  2. $\psi _{\alpha +1}=P_{\theta }[\psi _{\alpha }]$;

  3. if $\alpha $ is a limit ordinal, we define

    \[ \psi _{\alpha }=\operatorname *{sup}_{\beta <\alpha }{}\! {}^{*} \psi _{\beta }. \]

Observe that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\psi _{\alpha }}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n \]

by corollary 2.4.9 and (2.53). Since $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is indeed a set, the transfinite sequence must stabilize, say at some $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Note that $0 \geq \phi \geq \psi $, and $P_{\theta }[\phi ]=\phi $.

Note that the interval (2.47) remains the same if we replace $\psi $ by $\phi $. Take $a$ in this interval, it suffices to prove the existence of $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ so that

2.54
\begin{equation} \label{eq:path_env_temp1} a^{-1}\eta +\left(1-a^{-1}\right)\phi \leq \varphi . \end{equation}

Let us record the following observation for later use. Suppose that $\tau \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\tau \preceq \phi $. Then

\begin{equation} \label{eq:modelpot_sup_temp1} \sup _{\{ \phi \neq -\infty \} } (\tau -\phi )=\sup _ X \tau . \end{equation}

Observe that $\phi \leq 0$, so on the set $\{ \phi \neq -\infty \} $, we have

\[ \tau -\phi \geq \tau . \]

So the $\geq $ direction in (2.55) follows from corollary 1.3.18. Conversely, by assumption, we can find a constant $C>0$ so that

\[ \tau -\sup _ X \tau \leq \min \{ \phi +C,0 \} . \]

It follows that $\tau -\sup _ X \tau \leq P_{\theta }[\phi ]=\phi $. Therefore, the reverse inequality follows, and (2.55) follows.

Since $\varphi _ k\sim \phi $, using Step 1.1, we have $\eta _ k\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\eta _ k\sim \phi $ as well.

Note that $(\eta _ k)_ k$ is decreasing in $k$. Let

Finally, it follows from theorem 2.4.14 that

Thanks to (2.64), for each $a'>\epsilon ^{-1}$, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n>\left(\frac{a'-\epsilon ^{-1}}{a'}\right)^ n\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n\geq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n= \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n= \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

Therefore, 1 holds as well. $\square$

Here $\omega _{f_ i}\coloneqq \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ i\in L^ n(X)$, since $f_ i\in C^{1,\overline{1}}(X)$. Therefore, $\omega _{f_ i}^ n$ makes sense as an absolutely continuous Radon measure.

Proof. Set $\varphi \coloneqq P_\omega (\min \{ f_1,f_2\} )$. Since $\min \{ f_1,f_2\} \in C^0(X)$, we have $\varphi \leq \min \{ f_1,f_2\} $. By proposition 2.4.22, $\varphi \in C^{1,\overline{1}}(X)$. As $f_1,f_2,\varphi \in C^{1,\overline{1}}(X)$, the measures $\omega ^ n_{f_1}$, $\omega ^ n_{f_2}$ and $\omega ^ n_\varphi $ are all absolutely continuous.

Step 1: We show that at almost every contact point, $\varphi $ and $f_ i$ agree to order two.

For $i\in \{ 1,2,3\} $ set

\[ \Psi _1\coloneqq \varphi -f_1,\quad \Psi _2\coloneqq \varphi -f_2,\quad \Psi _3\coloneqq f_1-f_2. \]

Each $\Psi _ i$ lies in $C^{1,\overline{1}}(X)$.

We claim that for $i=1,2,3$, we have

2.76
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DNTroof_step1claim} \Psi _ i\textup{ vanishes to second order at almost every point of }\{ \Psi _ i=0\} . \end{equation}

To prove this, write $\{ \Psi _ i=0\} $ as the disjoint union $A_ i\sqcup B_ i\sqcup C_ i\sqcup D_ i$ of the following four sets:

  • $A_ i\coloneqq \{ \Psi _ i=0\} \cap \{ \mathrm{d}\Psi _ i\neq 0\} $;

  • $B_ i\coloneqq \{ \Psi _ i=0\} \cap \{ \textup{the classical real Hessian of }\Psi _ i\textup{ does not exist}\} $;

  • $C_ i\coloneqq \{ \Psi _ i=0\} \cap \{ \mathrm{d}\Psi _ i=0\} \cap \{ \textup{the classical real Hessian exists and is non-zero}\} $;

  • $D_ i\coloneqq \{ \Psi _ i=0\} \cap \{ \mathrm{d}\Psi _ i=0\} \cap \{ \textup{the classical real Hessian exists and equals zero}\} $.

It suffices to prove $A_ i$, $B_ i$, $C_ i$ are Lebesgue null sets.

The set $A_ i$ is a $C^1$ real hypersurface in $X$, hence has Lebesgue measure zero.

Since $\Psi _ i\in W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, the classical pointwise real Hessian of $\Psi _ i$ exists almost everywhere, so $B_ i$ is Lebesgue null.

Finally $C_ i$ is Lebesgue null by Stampacchia's lemma. This proves (2.76).

Specialising (2.76) to $\Psi _1,\Psi _2$, the functions $\varphi $ and $f_ i$ agree to order two at almost every point of the contact set $\{ \varphi =f_ i\} $. Consequently $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ i$ have the same matrix of coefficients almost everywhere on $\{ \varphi =f_ i\} $, and therefore so do their $n$-fold wedge powers:

2.77
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DNTroof_step1eq} \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_ i\} }\, \omega ^ n_\varphi = \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_ i\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_ i},\qquad i\in \{ 1,2\} . \end{equation}

The analogous statement applied to $\Psi _3$ gives

2.78
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DNTroof_step1eqf12} \mathds {1}_{\{ f_1=f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_1} = \mathds {1}_{\{ f_1=f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_2}. \end{equation}

Step 2. The barrier $\min \{ f_1,f_2\} $ is continuous, hence quasi-continuous, so theorem 2.4.14 yields

2.79
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DNTroof_step2conc} \int _{X\setminus \left(\{ \varphi =f_1\} \cup \{ \varphi =f_2\} \right)}\omega ^ n_\varphi = \int _{\left\{ \varphi <\min \{ f_1,f_2\} \right\} }\omega ^ n_\varphi =0. \end{equation}

Set $V_1\coloneqq \{ f_1<f_2\} $ and $V_2\coloneqq \{ f_1>f_2\} $, both open. We may decompose

\[ \{ \varphi =f_1\} \cup \{ \varphi =f_2\} = \bigl (V_1\cap \{ \varphi =f_1\} \bigr ) \sqcup \bigl (V_2\cap \{ \varphi =f_2\} \bigr ) \sqcup \{ \varphi =f_1=f_2\} . \]

This combined with (2.79) and (2.77) yields

\[ \begin{aligned} \omega ^ n_\varphi ={}& \mathds {1}_{V_1\cap \{ \varphi =f_1\} }\, \omega ^ n_\varphi + \mathds {1}_{V_2\cap \{ \varphi =f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_\varphi + \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_1=f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_\varphi \\ ={}& \mathds {1}_{V_1\cap \{ \varphi =f_1\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_1} + \mathds {1}_{V_2\cap \{ \varphi =f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_2} + \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_1=f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_ j}. \end{aligned} \]

On the other hand,

\[ \mathds {1}_{V_ j\cap \{ \varphi =f_ j\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_ j} = \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_ j\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_ j} - \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_1=f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_ j}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \omega ^ n_\varphi = \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_1\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_1} + \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_2\} }\, \omega ^ n_{f_2} + \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f_1=f_2\} } \, \bigl (\omega ^ n_{f_ j}-\omega ^ n_{f_1}-\omega ^ n_{f_2}\bigr ). \]

Together with (2.78) we obtain (2.72). $\square$

Theorem 2.4.24 (Di Nezza–Trapani). Let $\theta _1,\dots ,\theta _ n$ be closed real smooth $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ representing pseudo-effective cohomology classes. For each $i=1,\dots ,n$, let $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ and $f_ i\in C^{1,\overline{1}}(X)$ be functions with $\varphi _ i\leq f_ i$. Then

2.82
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DNTcontact} \mathds {1}_{\bigcap _ i\{ \varphi _ i=f_ i\} }\, \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n} = \mathds {1}_{\bigcap _ i\{ \varphi _ i=f_ i\} }\, \theta _{1,f_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,f_ n}. \end{equation}

Proof. Step 1. We first reduce to the case where $\theta _1=\cdots =\theta _ n$, $\varphi _1=\cdots =\varphi _ n$ and $f_1=\cdots =f_ n$.

Assume for the moment that this special case is known. Let us handle the general case.

For $\lambda =(\lambda _1,\dots ,\lambda _ n)\in (\mathbb {R}_{>0})^ n$, we set

\[ \theta _\lambda \coloneqq \sum _{j=1}^ n\lambda _ j\theta _ j,\quad \varphi _\lambda \coloneqq \sum _{j=1}^ n\lambda _ j\varphi _ j,\quad f_\lambda \coloneqq \sum _{j=1}^ n\lambda _ j f_ j. \]

Then $\varphi _\lambda \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _\lambda )$, $f_\lambda \in C^{1,\overline{1}}(X)$, $\varphi _\lambda \leq f_\lambda $, and

\[ \bigcap _{j=1}^ n\{ \varphi _ j=f_ j\} \subseteq \{ \varphi _\lambda =f_\lambda \} . \]

So

\[ \mathds {1}_{\bigcap _ j\{ \varphi _ j=f_ j\} }(\theta _\lambda )^ n_{\varphi _\lambda } = \mathds {1}_{\bigcap _ j\{ \varphi _ j=f_ j\} }(\theta _\lambda )^ n_{f_\lambda }. \]

Both sides are polynomials in $\lambda _1,\dots ,\lambda _ n$; comparing the coefficient of $\lambda _1\cdots \lambda _ n$ gives (2.82).

From now on, we assume

\[ \theta =\theta _1=\dots =\theta _ n,\quad \varphi =\varphi _1=\cdots =\varphi _ n\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ), \]

and

\[ f=f_1=\cdots =f_ n\in C^{1,\overline{1}}(X) \]

with

\[ \varphi \leq f. \]

We wish to prove that

2.83
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DNT_temp1} \mathds {1}_{\left\{ \varphi =f\right\} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\mathds {1}_{\left\{ \varphi =f\right\} }\, \theta _{f}^ n. \end{equation}

Step 2. We first handle the special case where $f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Step 2.1. We assume that $\theta $ is a Kähler form, denoted by $\omega $ for clarity. Pick a decreasing sequence $(\phi _ j)_ j$ in $C^{\infty }(X)$

Note. The functions $\phi _ j$ are not required to be $\omega $-psh.
with pointwise limit $\varphi $ and set
\[ \psi _ j\coloneqq P_{\omega }\left(\min \{ \phi _ j,f\} \right). \]

Then $\psi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, and

\[ \varphi \leq \psi _ j\leq \min \{ \phi _ j,f\} \leq f, \]

and hence $\psi _ j\searrow \varphi $. Applying 2.4.23 with $f_1=\phi _ j$ and $f_2=f$ gives

2.84
\begin{equation} \label{eq:omegapsij_temp1} \omega ^ n_{\psi _ j}\geq \mathds {1}_{\{ \psi _ j=f\} }\, \omega ^ n_ f \geq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f\} }\, \omega ^ n_ f. \end{equation}

Fix $C>0$ with $\min _ X f>-C$ and $\epsilon >0$. Let

\[ h_ j^{C,\epsilon }\coloneqq \frac{(\psi _ j+C)\lor 0}{\left((\psi _ j+C)\lor 0 \right)+\epsilon },\qquad h^{C,\epsilon }\coloneqq \frac{(\varphi +C)\lor 0}{\left((\varphi +C)\lor 0\right)+\epsilon }. \]

These functions are both quasi-continuous with values in $[0,1]$. Observe that $h_ j^{C,\epsilon }$ converges in capacity to $h^{C,\epsilon }$ as $j\to \infty $.

Now fix $g\in C^0(X)$, $g\geq 0$. Note that $h_ j^{C,\epsilon }=0$ on the set $\{ \psi _ j\leq -C\} $ and $h^{C,\epsilon }=0$ on the set $\{ \varphi \leq -C\} $. Using locality of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 1, we have

\[ h_ j^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\psi _ j}^ n=h_ j^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\psi _ j\lor (-C)}^ n,\quad h^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\varphi }^ n=h^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\varphi \lor (-C)}^ n. \]

Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ X g\, \omega _{\varphi }^ n\geq & \int _ X h^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\varphi }^ n & \\ =& \int _ X h^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\varphi \lor (-C)}^ n & \\ =& \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X h_ j^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\psi _ j\lor (-C)}^ n & \textup{by theorem \href{../../tag/272876B00528/}{2.3.8}}\\ =& \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X h_ j^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{\psi _ j}^ n & \\ \geq & \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _{\{ \varphi =f\} } h_ j^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{f}^ n & \textup{by }(\href{../../tag/88AC8C43CD82/}{2.84})\\ =& \int _{\{ \varphi =f\} }h^{C,\epsilon }g \, \omega _{f}^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where the last equality follows from the dominated convergence theorem. By our choice of $C$, we have $h^{C,\epsilon }>0$ on $\{ \varphi =f\} $, and hence when $\epsilon \to 0+$, $h^{C,\epsilon }\to 1$ everywhere on this set. Letting $\epsilon \to 0+$, we find

\[ \int _ X g\, \omega _{\varphi }^ n\geq \int _{\{ \varphi =f\} }g \, \omega _{f}^ n \]

by the dominated convergence theorem.

By the Riesz representation theorem, we conclude that

\[ \omega _{\varphi }^ n\geq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi =f\} }\, \omega _{f}^ n. \]

Then by 2.4.10, the reverse inequality also holds, and (2.83) follows in this case.

Step 2.2. We no longer assume that $\theta $ is a Kähler form.

Choose a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$ so that $\theta +\omega $ is a Kähler form and $f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\omega )$. Fix $g\in C^0(X)$ and consider, for $t\geq 0$,

\[ Q(t)\coloneqq \int _{\{ \varphi =f\} }g\, (\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n - \int _{\{ \varphi =f\} }g\, (\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ n. \]

By multilinearity of the non-pluripolar product, $Q$ is a polynomial in $t$. Step 2.1 applied to $\theta +t\omega $ for $t>1$ gives $Q(t)\equiv 0$ for $t>1$, hence $Q\equiv 0$, and in particular $Q(0)=0$. Since $g$ is arbitrary, (2.83) holds.

Step 3. We handle the general case. We no longer assume that $f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Choose a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$ so that $\theta +\omega $ is a Kähler form. Then

\[ \varphi \leq P_{\theta +\omega }(f)\leq f. \]

By 2.4.23 applied to $f_1=f_2=f$, we find that $f$ and $P_{\theta +\omega }(f)$ agree to the second order on the contact set $\{ P_{\theta +\omega }(f)=f \} $, so

\[ \mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta +\omega }(f)=f \right\} }\, \theta _{P_{\theta +\omega }(f)}^ n=\mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta +\omega }(f)=f \right\} }\, \theta _{f}^ n.\footnotemark \]
Note. This expression looks slightly unusual, as $P_{\theta +\omega }(f)$ is not $\theta $-psh in general, but it does make sense.
However, Step 2 implies that
\[ \mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta +\omega }(f)=\varphi \right\} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta +\omega }(f)=\varphi \right\} }\, \theta _{P_{\theta +\omega }(f)}^ n. \]

Combining these results, we conclude (2.83). $\square$

Corollary 2.4.25. Assume that $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $. For any $f\in C^{1,\overline{1}}(X)$, we have

\[ \theta ^ n_{P_\theta (f)} = \mathds {1}_{\{ P_\theta (f)=f\} }\, \theta ^ n_ f. \]

In particular,

\[ \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n=\mathds {1}_{\{ V_{\theta }=0\} }\, \theta ^ n. \]

Proof. From theorem 2.4.24, we find

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ P_\theta (f)=f\} }\, \theta ^ n_{P_\theta (f)}=\mathds {1}_{\{ P_\theta (f)=f\} }\, \theta ^ n_ f. \]

By the concentration property theorem 2.4.14, our assertion follows. $\square$
