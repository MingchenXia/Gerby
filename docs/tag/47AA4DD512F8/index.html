Divisorial classes and infinite mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 24.4 Divisorial classes and infinite mass

Now let the background be a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $Y$, with upper mass $v_+(\omega )$ (definition 6.5.1) and extended Monge–Ampère measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega $ (theorem 6.1.2). When $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $, the potential of theorem 6.6.1 has the same multiplier ideals as a smooth potential. The two potentials therefore have the same residual classes on every model, while their extended masses differ.

Proposition 24.4.1. If $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $, there is $U\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\omega )$ such that

\[ \mathbb D_\omega (U)=\mathbb D_\omega (0),\qquad \int _ Y\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)=+\infty , \qquad \int _ Y\omega ^ n<+\infty . \]

The common family has the nef Cartier realization $(Y,\{ \omega \} )$.

Proof. Take $U$ from theorem 6.6.1. All its multiplier ideals at positive weights are trivial, so every divisorial order in $D_{m,Z}$ vanishes on every model. The self-trace identity gives $N_ Z(U)=0$, which is also the divisorial part of the zero potential. Both families therefore consist of the pullbacks of the class of $\omega $. Since $\omega $ is smooth and positive, its affine class is nef and realizes the family on $Y$ itself. The extended mass of $U$ is infinite by construction, whereas the smooth measure $\omega ^ n$ has finite mass because $Y$ is compact. $\square$
