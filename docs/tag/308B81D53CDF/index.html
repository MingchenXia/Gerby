From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 14.1 The trace equation and global potentials

Throughout this chapter, except in section 14.8, $(X,\omega )$ is a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. (In dimension zero all the notions below are trivial: $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ and all positivity cones are $\{ 0\} $, both positivity loci are empty and normalized potentials vanish. On a disconnected compact Kähler manifold the results apply to each of its finitely many components, with normalizations imposed on each component.) The goal of this section is the $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-lemma (theorem 14.1.7). We first develop the Sobolev spaces of $X$ and the elliptic theory of its Laplacians (lemma 14.1.3), and then express the trace of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ through the Laplacian of $f$ (lemma 14.1.6).

Let $J$ be the complex structure of $X$. The Kähler form defines the Riemannian metric $g(v,w)=\omega (v,Jw)$ and the volume form $\mathrm dV=\omega ^ n/n!$. Forms carry the pointwise inner product induced by $g$, and the $L^2$ inner product $\langle u,v\rangle =\int _ X\langle u,v\rangle \, \mathrm dV$. The Hodge star operator is the complex-linear bundle map characterized by $u\wedge *\overline v=\langle u,v\rangle \, \mathrm dV$ for forms $u,v$ of the same degree. Explicitly, if $e_1,\ldots ,e_{2n}$ is a positively oriented orthonormal basis of the real cotangent space at a point and $I'$ denotes the increasing multi-index complementary to $I$, then $*e_ I=\varepsilon _ Ie_{I'}$, where $e_ I\wedge e_{I'}=\varepsilon _ Ie_1\wedge \cdots \wedge e_{2n}$ and $\varepsilon _ I=\pm 1$; indeed $e_ I\wedge \varepsilon _ Je_{J'}$ is $\delta _{IJ}$ times the volume form. Hence $*$ is a real isometry, and since $e_{I'}\wedge e_ I=(-1)^{k(2n-k)}e_ I\wedge e_{I'}$ for $|I|=k$, we have $**=(-1)^{k(2n-k)}=(-1)^ k$ on $k$-forms, the real dimension $2n$ being even.

Now let $u$ be a $(k-1)$-form and $v$ a $k$-form, one of them with compact support. Since $\mathrm d*\overline v$ has degree $2n-k+1$, on which $**=(-1)^{k-1}$, we have $u\wedge \mathrm d*\overline v=(-1)^{k-1}u\wedge *\overline{(*\mathrm d*v)}=(-1)^{k-1}\langle u,*\mathrm d*v\rangle \, \mathrm dV$. Integrating $\mathrm d(u\wedge *\overline v)=\mathrm du\wedge *\overline v+(-1)^{k-1}u\wedge \mathrm d*\overline v$ by Stokes' formula (proposition 7.1.3), we obtain $\int _ X\langle \mathrm du,v\rangle \, \mathrm dV=-\int _ X\langle u,*\mathrm d*v\rangle \, \mathrm dV$. Thus the formal adjoint of $\mathrm d$ is $\mathrm d^*=-*\mathrm d*$. More generally, a linear differential operator $P$ on forms with smooth coefficients has a formal adjoint $P^*$, a differential operator of the same kind characterized by $\langle P\alpha ,\phi \rangle =\langle \alpha ,P^*\phi \rangle $ for smooth forms $\alpha ,\phi $; in a chart it is obtained by integrating by parts, and the local expressions agree on overlaps by this characterization.

A distributional $k$-form is a current of degree $k$ (section 7.2). It is paired with smooth $k$-forms $\phi $ by $\langle u,\phi \rangle =u(*\overline\phi )$, which equals $\int _ X\langle u,\phi \rangle \, \mathrm dV$ when $u$ is a locally integrable form. For a scalar distribution $f$ and a smooth function $\chi $ the pairing reads $\langle f,\chi \rangle =f(\overline\chi \, \mathrm dV)$, and we say that $f$ has mean zero if $\langle f,1\rangle =0$. We let $P$ act on distributional forms by $\langle Pu,\phi \rangle =\langle u,P^*\phi \rangle $. For $P=\mathrm d$ this is the exterior derivative of currents, by the formula for $\mathrm d^*$; for $*$, for the exterior product with a smooth form and for its pointwise adjoint, it is the action on the distribution coefficients in local frames. Since $(PQ)^*=Q^*P^*$, and since an operator is determined by its formal adjoint, every identity between such operators that holds on smooth forms also holds on distributional forms.

On functions we use the Laplace–Beltrami operator $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$. For the Euclidean metric on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ we have $L=-\sum _ j\partial ^2/\partial x_ j^2$, so $L$ is the negative of the Laplacian $\Delta $ of Part I; we use this sign convention throughout the chapter because it makes $L$ a nonnegative operator: $\int _ XfLf\, \mathrm dV=\int _ X|\mathrm df|^2\, \mathrm dV$ for smooth real $f$. On forms we use the Hodge Laplacian $\mathcal L=\mathrm d\mathrm d^*+\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$, and a form or current $u$ is harmonic if $\mathcal Lu=0$. For smooth $u$, $\langle \mathcal Lu,u\rangle =\| \mathrm du\| ^2+\| \mathrm d^*u\| ^2$, so $u$ is harmonic if and only if $\mathrm du=0$ and $\mathrm d^*u=0$. On functions $\mathcal L=L$, so for the Euclidean metric this notion of harmonic function agrees with that of section 2.1; on the compact connected manifold $X$, however, the only solutions of $Lf=0$ are the constants (lemma 14.1.3), and the harmonic objects of interest here are forms.

We use the following Sobolev spaces. Choose a finite cover of $X$ by coordinate charts, frames of the bundle of differential forms over them, and smooth functions $\chi _ k\geq 0$ with compact supports in the charts and $\sum _ k\chi _ k=1$ (proposition 7.1.3). Using the frames, $\chi _ ku$ is a vector-valued function on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ for every form $u$. For an integer $r\geq 0$, let $H^ r$ be the completion of the space of smooth forms for the norm whose square is

\[ \sum _ k\int _{\mathbb R^{2n}}(1+|\xi |^2)^ r |\widehat{\chi _ k u}(\xi )|^2\, \mathrm d\xi , \]

and let $H^{-r}$ be the space of distributional forms $u$ for which $\phi \mapsto \langle u,\phi \rangle $ is bounded on smooth forms for the norm of $H^ r$, with the norm of this functional. By lemma 14.1.1 below, these spaces do not depend on the choices made or on $\omega $. The same notation is used for functions.

Lemma 14.1.1. Let $r,l\geq 0$ be integers. For a $\mathbb C^ N$-valued function $v$ on an open set $W\subset \mathbb R^{2n}$ whose distributional derivatives of order at most $r$ are square integrable, put $\| v\| _{(r),W}^2=\sum _{|\alpha |\leq r}\| \partial ^\alpha v\| _{L^2(W)}^2$, and $\| v\| _{(r)}=\| v\| _{(r),\mathbb R^{2n}}$. For a form $u$ of class $C^ l$, let $\| u\| _{C^ l}$ be the maximum over $k$ of the suprema on $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$ of the derivatives of order at most $l$ of the coefficients of $u$ in the $k$th chart and frame.

  1. On smooth forms, the norm of $H^ r$ is equivalent to the norm $\bigl (\sum _ k\| \chi _ ku\| _{(r)}^2\bigr )^{1/2}$, and other choices of the charts, frames and cutoff functions give equivalent norms. Multiplication by a smooth function, or by a smooth endomorphism of the bundle of forms, is bounded for the norm of $H^ r$, and $\| \phi \| _{H^ r}\leq C\| \phi \| _{C^ r}$ for smooth forms $\phi $.

  2. The norm of $H^ r$ dominates a multiple of the $L^2$ norm, and the continuous extension to $H^ r$ of the identity map of smooth forms is an injective map into the space of $L^2$ forms; we regard $H^ r$ as a subspace of the $L^2$ forms. An $L^2$ form $u$ belongs to $H^ r$ if and only if, for every $k$, the distributional derivatives of order at most $r$ of $\chi _ ku$ are square integrable, and then $\bigl (\sum _ k\| \chi _ ku\| _{(r)}^2\bigr )^{1/2}$ is equivalent to $\| u\| _{H^ r}$. In particular, $H^0$ is the space of all $L^2$ forms, with an equivalent norm, and the subspace $H^ r$, with its norm up to equivalence, does not depend on the choices of the charts, frames and cutoffs. Moreover $H^{r'}\subset H^ r$ for $r\leq r'$, and if $W$ is an open set whose closure is a compact subset of a chart with a frame, then the coefficient vector of every $u\in H^ r$ on $W$ satisfies $\| u\| _{(r),W}\leq C_ W\| u\| _{H^ r}$.

  3. Let $r>l+n$. Every $u\in H^ r$ agrees almost everywhere with a form of class $C^ l$, and for this form $\| u\| _{C^ l}\leq C\| u\| _{H^ r}$. Likewise, a compactly supported $\mathbb C^ N$-valued function $v$ on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ with $\| v\| _{(r)}<\infty $ agrees almost everywhere with a function of class $C^ l$ whose derivatives of order at most $l$ are bounded by $C\| v\| _{(r)}$. Consequently, a form that lies in every $H^ r$ is smooth.

  4. For $u\in H^{-r}$ the functional $\phi \mapsto \langle u,\phi \rangle $ extends uniquely to a bounded conjugate-linear functional on $H^ r$, with norm $\| u\| _{H^{-r}}$, and every bounded conjugate-linear functional on $H^ r$ arises in this way from a unique $u\in H^{-r}$; thus $H^{-r}$ is the dual space of $H^ r$. Multiplication by smooth functions and endomorphisms is bounded on $H^{-r}$, and $H^{-r}\subset H^{-r'}$ for $r\leq r'$. The space $H^{-r}$, with its norm up to equivalence, depends neither on the choices of the charts, frames and cutoffs nor on the Kähler form $\omega $ that defines the pairing; neither does $H^ r$.

  5. Every distributional form belongs to $H^{-r}$ for some $r$.

Proof. In this proof, the constants $C$ depend on $r$, $l$ and the data indicated, but not on the forms or functions involved. We first compare the coefficient vectors of a form in two charts, and then prove (1)–(5) in turn.

(a) A change of charts. Let $\kappa \colon U\to \mathbb R^{2n}$ and $\kappa '\colon U'\to \mathbb R^{2n}$ be charts of $X$ with frames of the bundle of forms over $U$ and $U'$, let $K\subset U$ be compact, and let $W\subset U'$ be an open set whose closure is a compact subset of $U'$. For a smooth form $w$ that vanishes outside $K$, let $w_\kappa $ and $w_{\kappa '}$ be its coefficient vectors in the two charts and frames, the first one extended by zero to $\mathbb R^{2n}$. We claim that

14.1
\begin{equation} \| w_{\kappa '}\| _{(r),\kappa '(W)}\leq C\| w_\kappa \| _{(r)}, \end{equation}

with $C$ depending on the charts, frames, $K$ and $W$. Indeed, on $\kappa '(U\cap U')$ we have $w_{\kappa '}(y)=A(y)\, w_\kappa (\tau (y))$, where $\tau =\kappa \circ \kappa '^{-1}$ and $A$ is the smooth matrix of the change of frames. By the chain rule, the Leibniz rule and induction on $|\alpha |$,

\[ \partial ^\alpha w_{\kappa '}(y)=\sum _{|\beta |\leq |\alpha |}c_{\alpha \beta }(y)\, (\partial ^\beta w_\kappa )(\tau (y)),\qquad y\in \kappa '(U\cap U'), \]

where the matrices $c_{\alpha \beta }$ are polynomials in the derivatives of order at most $|\alpha |$ of $A$ and of $\tau $; they are smooth on $\kappa '(U\cap U')$ and therefore bounded on its compact subset $\kappa '(\overline W\cap K)$. Since $w$ vanishes on the open set $X\setminus K$, all derivatives of $w_{\kappa '}$ vanish on $\kappa '(W\setminus K)$, and every point of $\kappa '(W)$ lies in this set or in $\kappa '(W\cap K)$. Hence, for $|\alpha |\leq r$,

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _{\kappa '(W)}|\partial ^\alpha w_{\kappa '}|^2\, \mathrm dy & \leq C\sum _{|\beta |\leq r}\int _{\kappa '(W\cap K)}|(\partial ^\beta w_\kappa )(\tau (y))|^2\, \mathrm dy\\ & \leq C\sum _{|\beta |\leq r}\int _{\mathbb R^{2n}}|\partial ^\beta w_\kappa (x)|^2\, \mathrm dx, \end{aligned} \]

by the change of variables $x=\tau (y)$, whose Jacobian determinant $|\det D\tau ^{-1}|$ is bounded on the compact set $\kappa (\overline W\cap K)$. This proves (14.1). We also use the Leibniz rule in the following form. If $M$ is a smooth matrix-valued function on an open set $V\subset \mathbb R^{2n}$ whose derivatives of order at most $r$ are bounded on $V$, then $\| Mv\| _{(r),V}\leq C\| v\| _{(r),V}$ for smooth $v$ on $V$, with $C$ depending on these bounds. If $v$ vanishes outside a compact set $L\subset V$, it suffices that $M$ be smooth on $V$, the bounds being taken on $L$, and then $\| Mv\| _{(r)}\leq C\| v\| _{(r)}$ for the extensions by zero.

(b) Proof of (1). For a test function $v$ on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ we have $\widehat{\partial ^\beta v}(\xi )=(2\pi \mathrm i\xi )^\beta \widehat v(\xi )$, and $(1+|\xi |^2)^ r=\sum _{|\beta |\leq r}c_\beta |\xi ^\beta |^2$ with positive multinomial coefficients $c_\beta $. Hence Plancherel's theorem (theorem 6.1.2) gives

14.4
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb R^{2n}}(1+|\xi |^2)^ r|\widehat v(\xi )|^2\, \mathrm d\xi =\sum _{|\beta |\leq r}c_\beta (2\pi )^{-2|\beta |}\| \partial ^\beta v\| _{L^2}^2, \end{equation}

and the norm of $H^ r$ is equivalent on smooth forms to $\bigl (\sum _ k\| \chi _ ku\| _{(r)}^2\bigr )^{1/2}$. Let charts with domains $U'_ m$, frames over them, and cutoffs $\chi '_ m$ with $\sum _ m\chi '_ m=1$ be other choices, and choose open sets $W_ m$ with $\operatorname{Supp}\chi '_ m\subset W_ m$ and $\overline{W_ m}$ compact in $U'_ m$. For a smooth form $u$ we have $\chi '_ mu=\sum _ k\chi '_ m\chi _ ku$, and the form $\chi '_ m\chi _ ku$ vanishes outside the compact set $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$ and outside $\operatorname{Supp}\chi '_ m\subset W_ m$. By (14.1), with $K=\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$ and $W=W_ m$, its norm $\| \cdot \| _{(r)}$ in the $m$th new chart is at most $C$ times its norm in the $k$th old chart, and by the Leibniz rule, applied to the factor $\chi '_ m$ on the compact set $\kappa _ k(\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k)$, where $\kappa _ k$ is the $k$th chart, the latter is at most $C\| \chi _ ku\| _{(r)}$. Summing over $k$ and $m$, we see that the new norm is bounded by a constant times the old one, and by symmetry the two norms are equivalent.

If $M$ is a smooth function or endomorphism, then $\chi _ kMu=M\chi _ ku$ vanishes outside $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$, so the Leibniz rule gives $\| \chi _ kMu\| _{(r)}\leq C\| \chi _ ku\| _{(r)}$. Finally, $\| \chi _ k\phi \| _{(r)}$ is bounded, by the Leibniz rule and since $\kappa _ k(\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k)$ has finite measure, by a constant times the suprema on $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$ of the derivatives of order at most $r$ of the coefficients of $\phi $; hence $\| \phi \| _{H^ r}\leq C\| \phi \| _{C^ r}$.

(c) Proof of (2). For a smooth form $u$ we have $|u|\leq \sum _ k|\chi _ ku|$ pointwise, since $\chi _ k\geq 0$ and $\sum _ k\chi _ k=1$. On the compact sets $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$ the pointwise norm of forms is comparable with the Euclidean norm of the coefficient vectors, and $\mathrm dV$ with Lebesgue measure in the charts; hence $\| u\| _{L^2}\leq C\sum _ k\| \chi _ ku\| _{(0)}\leq C\| u\| _{H^ r}$, and conversely $\| \chi _ ku\| _{(0)}\leq C\| u\| _{L^2}$ because $0\leq \chi _ k\leq 1$. Therefore the identity map of smooth forms extends to a bounded map $j$ from $H^ r$ to the space of $L^2$ forms. Let $u\in H^ r$ be the limit in $H^ r$ of smooth forms $u_ i$. By (1), for every $k$ and $|\alpha |\leq r$ the functions $\partial ^\alpha (\chi _ ku_ i)$ form a Cauchy sequence in $L^2(\mathbb R^{2n})$, with a limit $g_{k,\alpha }$, while $\chi _ ku_ i\to \chi _ kj(u)$ in $L^2$. For every test function $\phi $ on $\mathbb R^{2n}$,

\[ \begin{aligned} \int g_{k,\alpha }\phi \, \mathrm dx& =\lim _ i\int \partial ^\alpha (\chi _ ku_ i)\phi \, \mathrm dx=\lim _ i(-1)^{|\alpha |}\int \chi _ ku_ i\, \partial ^\alpha \phi \, \mathrm dx\\ & =(-1)^{|\alpha |}\int \chi _ kj(u)\, \partial ^\alpha \phi \, \mathrm dx, \end{aligned} \]

so $g_{k,\alpha }$ is the distributional derivative $\partial ^\alpha (\chi _ kj(u))$. Hence these derivatives are square integrable, and $\sum _ k\| \chi _ kj(u)\| _{(r)}^2=\lim _ i\sum _ k\| \chi _ ku_ i\| _{(r)}^2$, which by (1) lies between two positive multiples of $\lim _ i\| u_ i\| _{H^ r}^2=\| u\| _{H^ r}^2$. In particular, $j(u)=0$ implies $u=0$, so $j$ is injective, and we identify $u$ with $j(u)$. This proves the first assertion, the necessity of the condition and the equivalence of the norms.

Conversely, let $u$ be an $L^2$ form such that every $\chi _ ku$ has square integrable distributional derivatives of order at most $r$, and let $\rho _\varepsilon $ be the mollifier of section 1.4. Since $\chi _ ku$ vanishes outside the compact set $\kappa _ k(\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k)$, the functions $v_{k,\varepsilon }=\rho _\varepsilon *(\chi _ ku)$ are smooth and, for small $\varepsilon $, vanish outside a fixed compact subset $L_ k$ of $\kappa _ k(U_ k)$, where $U_ k$ is the domain of the $k$th chart; moreover $\partial ^\alpha v_{k,\varepsilon }=\rho _\varepsilon *\partial ^\alpha (\chi _ ku)$ by proposition 1.4.3, and this converges to $\partial ^\alpha (\chi _ ku)$ in $L^2$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$, by lemma 1.4.1, for $|\alpha |\leq r$. Let $w_{k,\varepsilon }$ be the smooth form on $X$ whose coefficient vector in the $k$th chart and frame is $v_{k,\varepsilon }$, extended by zero outside $U_ k$, and put $u_\varepsilon =\sum _ kw_{k,\varepsilon }$. Choose open sets $W_ m$ with $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ m\subset W_ m$ and $\overline{W_ m}$ compact in $U_ m$. By (14.1), with $K=\kappa _ k^{-1}(L_ k)$ and $W=W_ m$, and by the Leibniz rule for the factor $\chi _ m$, we have $\| \chi _ m(w_{k,\varepsilon }-w_{k,\varepsilon '})\| _{(r)}\leq C\| v_{k,\varepsilon }-v_{k,\varepsilon '}\| _{(r)}$. By (1), $(u_\varepsilon )$ is therefore a Cauchy sequence in $H^ r$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$ along a sequence, and it converges in $L^2$ to $\sum _ k\chi _ ku=u$, by the comparison of pointwise norms on the compact sets $\kappa _ k^{-1}(L_ k)\cup \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k\subset U_ k$. Hence $u\in H^ r$.

For $r=0$ the condition holds for every $L^2$ form, so $H^0$ is the space of all $L^2$ forms, and its norm is equivalent to the $L^2$ norm by the first paragraph. Other choices of the charts, frames and cutoffs give, by (1), equivalent norms on smooth forms, hence the same Cauchy sequences and the same limits in $L^2$; so they give the same subspace $H^ r$ with an equivalent norm. If $r\leq r'$, then $\| \phi \| _{H^ r}\leq \| \phi \| _{H^{r'}}$ for smooth $\phi $, so every Cauchy sequence in $H^{r'}$ is a Cauchy sequence in $H^ r$ with the same limit in $L^2$; hence $H^{r'}\subset H^ r$.

Finally, let $W$ be open with closure compact in a chart with a frame. For smooth $u$, we have $u=\sum _ m\chi _ mu$, and (14.1), applied to $w=\chi _ mu$ with $K=\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ m$, gives $\| u\| _{(r),W}\leq C\sum _ m\| \chi _ mu\| _{(r)}\leq C\| u\| _{H^ r}$. If $u\in H^ r$ is the limit in $H^ r$ of smooth forms $u_ i$, the coefficient vectors of the $u_ i$ on $W$ form a Cauchy sequence for $\| \cdot \| _{(r),W}$ and converge in $L^2(W)$ to that of $u$. Testing against test functions on $W$ as above, we find that their derivatives of order at most $r$ converge in $L^2(W)$ to those of the coefficient vector of $u$, and the bound passes to the limit.

(d) Proof of (3). Let first $v$ be a smooth $\mathbb C^ N$-valued function with compact support, and let $|\alpha |\leq l$. The inversion formula of theorem 6.1.2, applied to $\partial ^\alpha v$, whose Fourier transform is $(2\pi \mathrm i\xi )^\alpha \widehat v$, and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give

\[ \begin{aligned} \sup |\partial ^\alpha v|& \leq \int _{\mathbb R^{2n}}|2\pi \xi |^{|\alpha |}|\widehat v(\xi )|\, \mathrm d\xi \\ & \leq (2\pi )^ l\Bigl (\int _{\mathbb R^{2n}}(1+|\xi |^2)^{l-r}\, \mathrm d\xi \Bigr )^{1/2}\Bigl (\int _{\mathbb R^{2n}}(1+|\xi |^2)^ r|\widehat v(\xi )|^2\, \mathrm d\xi \Bigr )^{1/2}, \end{aligned} \]

where the first integral on the right is finite, by polar coordinates, because $2(r-l)>2n$. By (14.4), $\sup |\partial ^\alpha v|\leq C\| v\| _{(r)}$. If $v$ is only compactly supported with $\| v\| _{(r)}<\infty $, then, as in (c), the mollifications $v_\varepsilon =\rho _\varepsilon *v$ are smooth with compact support and $\| v_\varepsilon -v\| _{(r)}\to 0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$. Along a sequence $\varepsilon _ i\to 0$, the estimate shows that the derivatives of order at most $l$ of the $v_{\varepsilon _ i}$ converge uniformly; hence the $v_{\varepsilon _ i}$ converge uniformly to a function of class $C^ l$ whose derivatives of order at most $l$ are bounded by $C\| v\| _{(r)}$. This function agrees with $v$ almost everywhere, since $v_{\varepsilon _ i}\to v$ in $L^2$ and a subsequence converges almost everywhere.

Now let $u$ be a smooth form. On $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$, the coefficient vector of $u$ in the $k$th chart and frame is the sum over $m$ of the coefficient vectors of the forms $\chi _ mu$. We apply the formula for $\partial ^\alpha w_{\kappa '}$ in (a) with $\kappa $ the $m$th chart, $\kappa '$ the $k$th chart and $K=\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ m$. The derivatives of the coefficient vector of $\chi _ mu$ in the $k$th chart vanish outside $\kappa _ k(\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ m)$, and the coefficients $c_{\alpha \beta }$ are bounded on the compact set $\kappa _ k(\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k\cap \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ m)$. Hence the derivatives of order at most $l$ of this coefficient vector are bounded on $\kappa _ k(\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k)$ by $C$ times the suprema of the derivatives of order at most $l$ of the coefficient vector of $\chi _ mu$ in the $m$th chart, and the Euclidean estimate and (1) give $\| u\| _{C^ l}\leq C\sum _ m\| \chi _ mu\| _{(r)}\leq C\| u\| _{H^ r}$.

If $u\in H^ r$ is the limit in $H^ r$ of smooth forms $u_ i$, then $\| u_ i-u_{i'}\| _{C^ l}\leq C\| u_ i-u_{i'}\| _{H^ r}$, so in every chart the coefficients of the $u_ i$ and their derivatives of order at most $l$ converge uniformly on $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$, and hence on the open sets $\{ \chi _ k>0\} $, which cover $X$. The limit is a form of class $C^ l$ with $\| \cdot \| _{C^ l}\leq C\| u\| _{H^ r}$, and it agrees with $u$ almost everywhere, because $u_ i\to u$ in $L^2$. Finally, if $u$ lies in every $H^ r$, then for each $l$ it agrees almost everywhere with a form of class $C^ l$; these continuous forms agree almost everywhere, hence everywhere, so $u$ agrees almost everywhere with a smooth form.

(e) Proof of (4). Let $u\in H^{-r}$. Since smooth forms are dense in $H^ r$, the bounded functional $\phi \mapsto \langle u,\phi \rangle $ on smooth forms extends uniquely to a bounded conjugate-linear functional on $H^ r$, with the same norm. Conversely, let $\ell $ be a bounded conjugate-linear functional on $H^ r$. Since $*$ is a real bundle isomorphism with smooth coefficients, the formula $T(\alpha )=\ell (\overline{*^{-1}\alpha })$ defines a complex-linear functional $T$ on smooth forms. If $\alpha $ is supported in a compact subset $K$ of a chart, then, by (1) and the chain rule on the compact sets $K\cap \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$, we have $|T(\alpha )|\leq \| \ell \| \, \| *^{-1}\overline\alpha \| _{H^ r}\leq C\| *^{-1}\overline\alpha \| _{C^ r}\leq C_ K\sum _ Jp_{K,r}(\alpha _ J)$ in the notation of section 7.2. Hence the components of $T$ of the various degrees are currents, and $T$ is a distributional form $u$ with $\langle u,\phi \rangle =u(*\overline\phi )=\ell (\phi )$ for smooth $\phi $. Thus $u\in H^{-r}$ and $\| u\| _{H^{-r}}=\| \ell \| $. It is unique, because $*$ maps the smooth forms onto the smooth forms, so that a distributional form is determined by its pairings with smooth forms.

For a smooth function or endomorphism $M$, with pointwise adjoint $M^\dagger $, we have $\langle Mu,\phi \rangle =\langle u,M^\dagger \phi \rangle $ for distributional forms $u$ and smooth $\phi $, so $|\langle Mu,\phi \rangle |\leq \| u\| _{H^{-r}}\| M^\dagger \phi \| _{H^ r}\leq C\| u\| _{H^{-r}}\| \phi \| _{H^ r}$ by (1). The inclusion $H^{-r}\subset H^{-r'}$ for $r\leq r'$ follows from $\| \phi \| _{H^ r}\leq \| \phi \| _{H^{r'}}$, and by (1) other choices of the charts, frames and cutoffs change the norm of $H^ r$ on smooth forms, and hence $H^{-r}$, only up to equivalence.

The definition of $H^ r$, $r\geq 0$, does not involve $\omega $. Let $\omega '$ be another Kähler form, with pointwise inner product $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle '$ and volume form $\mathrm dV'$. At each point, the Hermitian form $\langle a,b\rangle '\, \mathrm dV'/\mathrm dV$ on forms is $\langle a,Sb\rangle $ for a unique endomorphism $S$, which is positive definite, hence invertible, and depends smoothly on the point. For smooth $u$ and $\phi $ we then have $u\wedge *'\overline\phi =\langle u,\phi \rangle '\, \mathrm dV'=\langle u,S\phi \rangle \, \mathrm dV=u\wedge *\overline{S\phi }$, where $*'$ is the Hodge star of $\omega '$; since $u$ is arbitrary, $*'\overline\phi =*\overline{S\phi }$. Hence the pairing defined by $\omega '$ is $u(*'\overline\phi )=\langle u,S\phi \rangle $ for every distributional form $u$. Since multiplication by $S$ and by $S^{-1}$ is bounded on $H^ r$ by (1), the condition defining $H^{-r}$ is the same for both pairings, and the two norms are equivalent.

(f) Proof of (5). Let $u$ be a distributional form. Since $\sum _ k\chi _ k=1$, we have $\langle u,\phi \rangle =\sum _ ku(\chi _ k\, {*\overline\phi })$ for smooth $\phi $. The forms $\chi _ k\, {*\overline\phi }$ are supported in the compact sets $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k$ of the charts, so the definition of currents (section 7.2) gives integers $m_ k$ and constants $C_ k$ with $|u(\chi _ k\, {*\overline\phi })|\leq C_ k\sum _ Jp_{\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ k,m_ k}\bigl ((\chi _ k\, {*\overline\phi })_ J\bigr )$. By the Leibniz rule, and since $*$ and the change from the coordinate frames to the given frames have smooth coefficients, the right side is at most $C\| \phi \| _{C^ l}$ with $l=\max _ km_ k$. By (3), $\| \phi \| _{C^ l}\leq C\| \phi \| _{H^ r}$ for $r>l+n$, and therefore $u\in H^{-r}$. $\square$

Lemma 14.1.2. Let $U\subset \mathbb R^{2n}$ be a bounded open set and let $N\geq 1$. For an open set $W\subset \mathbb R^{2n}$ and an integer $k\geq 0$ let $H^ k(W)$ be the space of $\mathbb C^ N$-valued functions on $W$ whose distributional derivatives of order at most $k$ are square integrable on $W$, with the norm $\| v\| _{(k),W}$ of lemma 14.1.1. For $v\in H^1(U)$ and $\mathbb C^ N$-valued $w\in C^\infty _ c(U)$ put

\[ \mathcal A(v,w)=\int _ U\Bigl (\sum a^{ij}_{IJ}\, \partial _ jv_ I\, \overline{\partial _ iw_ J} +\sum b^ j_{IJ}\, \partial _ jv_ I\, \overline{w_ J}+\sum c_{IJ}\, v_ I\, \overline{w_ J}\Bigr )\mathrm dx, \]

where the sums run over $1\leq i,j\leq 2n$ and $1\leq I,J\leq N$, the coefficients are smooth on a neighborhood of $\overline U$, and the principal coefficients satisfy, for some $\lambda >0$,

\[ \sum a^{ij}_{IJ}(x)\, \zeta _{jI}\, \overline{\zeta _{iJ}}\geq \lambda |\zeta |^2 \qquad \text{for all }x\in U\text{ and }\zeta \in \mathbb C^{2n\times N}. \]

(A term of the form $\int e^ i_{IJ}v_ I\overline{\partial _ iw_ J}$ can be integrated by parts into terms of the last two kinds, so it is also allowed.) Let $k\geq 0$, $F\in H^ k(U)$ and $v\in H^1(U)$, and suppose that $\mathcal A(v,w)=\int _ U\sum _ JF_ J\overline{w_ J}\, \mathrm dx$ for all $w\in C^\infty _ c(U)$. Then, for every $\chi \in C^\infty _ c(U)$, the function $\chi v$, extended by zero, belongs to $H^{k+2}(\mathbb R^{2n})$, and $\| \chi v\| _{H^{k+2}(\mathbb R^{2n})}\leq C(\| F\| _{H^ k(U)}+\| v\| _{H^1(U)})$, where $C$ depends only on $\chi $, $k$, $\lambda $ and bounds for the coefficients and their derivatives.

Proof. In this proof $H^ j$ stands for $H^ j(\mathbb R^{2n})$. We prove the case $k=0$ with difference quotients and then argue by induction on $k$. Let $0\leq \chi \leq 1$, let $h_0$ be half the distance from $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $ to $\partial U$, and for a coordinate direction $e_ l$ and $0<|h|<h_0$ put $\delta _ hv(x)=(v(x+he_ l)-v(x))/h$. For smooth $f$ we have $\delta _ hf(x)=\int _0^1\partial _ lf(x+the_ l)\, \mathrm dt$, so the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in $t$ and Fubini's theorem bound the $L^2$ norm of $\delta _ hf$ on a set by that of $\partial _ lf$ on a slightly larger set; by mollification the same holds for $f\in H^1$. The function $w=-\delta _{-h}(\chi ^2\delta _ hv)$ lies in $H^1$ and has compact support in $U$. Its mollifications are test functions converging to $w$ in $H^1$ (lemma 1.4.1), and both sides of the equation are continuous in $w$ for the norm of $H^1$, so the equation also holds for $w$.

Discrete integration by parts, $\int f\, \overline{\delta _{-h}g}=-\int (\delta _ hf)\, \overline g$, turns the principal term into $\int \sum \delta _ h(a^{ij}_{IJ}\partial _ jv_ I)\, \overline{\partial _ i(\chi ^2\delta _ hv_ J)}$. Here $\delta _ h(a\, \partial _ jv)=a(\cdot +he_ l)\, \partial _ j\delta _ hv+(\delta _ ha)\, \partial _ jv$ and $\partial _ i(\chi ^2\delta _ hv)=\chi ^2\partial _ i\delta _ hv+2\chi \, \partial _ i\chi \, \delta _ hv$. The ellipticity condition at $x+he_ l$ bounds the main term $\int \sum a^{ij}_{IJ}(x+he_ l)\chi ^2\partial _ j\delta _ hv_ I\overline{\partial _ i\delta _ hv_ J}$ from below by $\lambda \| \chi \nabla \delta _ hv\| _{L^2}^2$. The remaining terms, the lower-order terms and the right-hand side are estimated with $|\delta _ ha|\leq C$, with $\| \delta _ hv\| _{L^2(\operatorname{Supp}\chi )}\leq \| \partial _ lv\| _{L^2(U)}$, and with $\| w\| _{L^2}\leq \| \partial _ l(\chi ^2\delta _ hv)\| _{L^2}\leq \| \chi \nabla \delta _ hv\| _{L^2}+C\| v\| _{H^1(U)}$, where the difference quotients are bounded as above. This gives

\[ \lambda \| \chi \nabla \delta _ hv\| _{L^2}^2 \leq C\bigl (\| F\| _{L^2(U)}+\| v\| _{H^1(U)}\bigr ) \bigl (\| \chi \nabla \delta _ hv\| _{L^2}+\| v\| _{H^1(U)}\bigr ), \]

and hence $\| \chi \nabla \delta _ hv\| _{L^2}\leq C(\| F\| _{L^2(U)}+\| v\| _{H^1(U)})$, independently of $h$.

If $\phi \in C^\infty _ c$ is supported in the interior of $\{ \chi =1\} $, then $-\int \partial _ jv\, \partial _ l\phi =\lim _{h\to 0}\int (\delta _ h\partial _ jv)\, \phi $ is bounded by this constant times $\| \phi \| _{L^2}$. By the Riesz representation theorem in $L^2$ (proposition 11.1.1), applied to the extension of $\phi \mapsto -\int \partial _ jv\, \partial _ l\phi $ to $L^2$, the distributional derivatives $\partial _ l\partial _ jv$ are square integrable there, with the same bound. Since $\chi $ may be chosen equal to one near the support of any given cutoff, this proves the lemma for $k=0$.

Suppose the lemma holds for some $k\geq 0$, let $F\in H^{k+1}(U)$, and fix $\chi $ and an open set $U'\Subset U$ containing $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $. By the lemma for $k$, $v\in H^{k+2}(U')$, with norm at most $C(\| F\| _{H^ k(U)}+\| v\| _{H^1(U)})$. Fix $m$ and test the equation with $-\partial _ mw$, $w\in C^\infty _ c(U')$. Integrating by parts in $x_ m$, we find that $u=\partial _ mv\in H^1(U')$ satisfies $\mathcal A(u,w)=\int \sum _ JF'_ J\overline{w_ J}\, \mathrm dx$ for all $w\in C^\infty _ c(U')$, where, with the indices $I,J$ suppressed,

\[ F'=\partial _ mF+\sum _{i,j}\partial _ i\bigl ((\partial _ ma^{ij})\partial _ jv\bigr )-\sum _ j(\partial _ mb^ j)\partial _ jv-(\partial _ mc)v. \]

Since $v\in H^{k+2}(U')$, we have $F'\in H^ k(U')$ with $\| F'\| _{H^ k(U')}\leq C(\| F\| _{H^{k+1}(U)}+\| v\| _{H^{k+2}(U')})$. The lemma for $k$ on $U'$ gives $\chi \, \partial _ mv\in H^{k+2}$ for every $m$, hence $\chi v\in H^{k+3}$ with the asserted bound. $\square$

Lemma 14.1.3. On $X$ the following hold.

  1. Every distributional form $u$ with $\mathcal Lu=0$ is smooth, and the space of harmonic forms is finite dimensional.

  2. For every scalar distribution $a$ of mean zero there is a unique distribution $f$ of mean zero with $Lf=a$. If $a$ is smooth, then $f$ is smooth, and if $a$ is real, then $f$ is real.

  3. For every constant $c>0$, the operator $\mathcal L+c$ is a bijection of the space of distributional forms onto itself, and it maps $H^{r+2}$ isomorphically onto $H^ r$ for every integer $r$.

  4. There is a constant $C$ such that $\| v\| _{L^2}\leq C\| \mathrm dv\| _{L^2}$ for every scalar function $v\in H^1$ of mean zero.

Proof. Throughout, we use the properties of the spaces $H^ r$ proved in lemma 14.1.1. We show that the inclusion $H^1\subset L^2$ is compact, prove Gårding's inequality, solve the equation $(\mathcal L+c)v=b$ weakly with the Riesz representation theorem, and obtain the regularity of the solution from lemma 14.1.2. This gives (3); we then prove (1), (4) and (2) in turn.

Step 1: the inclusion $H^1\subset L^2$ is compact. Let $(u_ j)$ be bounded in $H^1$, fix $k$, and put $v_ j=\chi _ ku_ j$, a coefficient vector in the $k$th chart that vanishes outside a fixed compact set $S$. For $R>0$, the part of $\widehat{v_ j}$ on $|\xi |>R$ has $L^2$ norm at most $R^{-1}\| v_ j\| _{H^1}\leq C/R$. The inverse Fourier transforms $w_{j,R}$ of the parts on $|\xi |\leq R$ are uniformly bounded with uniformly bounded derivatives, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on the ball $|\xi |\leq R$, so by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem (proposition 1.6.4) a subsequence converges uniformly on $S$. Since $v_ j$ vanishes outside $S$,

\[ \| v_ j-v_ i\| _{L^2}\leq \| w_{j,R}-w_{i,R}\| _{L^2(S)}+2CR^{-1}. \]

A diagonal subsequence over $R=1,2,\ldots $ is therefore Cauchy in $L^2$. Repeating this for the finitely many charts and using $u_ j=\sum _ k\chi _ ku_ j$ proves compactness.

Step 2: Gårding's inequality. We first compute $\mathcal L$ in a chart with real coordinates $x_1,\ldots ,x_{2n}$ and the coordinate frame $\mathrm dx_ I$ of the forms. Let $(g^{ij})$ be the inverse of the matrix $(g(\partial _ i,\partial _ j))$, so that $g^{ij}$ is the inner product of $\mathrm dx_ i$ and $\mathrm dx_ j$, write the pointwise inner product of forms as $\sum _{I,J}G_{IJ}u_ I\overline{v_ J}$ and the volume form as $\rho \, \mathrm dx$, and let $\epsilon _ i$ be the exterior multiplication by $\mathrm dx_ i$ and $\epsilon _ i^\dagger $ its pointwise adjoint.

If $e_1,\ldots ,e_{2n}$ is an orthonormal basis of the cotangent space at a point, then exterior multiplication $\epsilon (e_ a)$ by $e_ a$ and its adjoint $\epsilon (e_ a)^\dagger $, which maps $e_ a\wedge e_ K$ to $e_ K$ for $a\notin K$ and kills the basis forms $e_ J$ with $a\notin J$, satisfy $\epsilon (e_ a)\epsilon (e_ b)^\dagger +\epsilon (e_ b)^\dagger \epsilon (e_ a)=\delta _{ab}$. Indeed, for $a=b$, exactly one of the two terms maps a given basis form $e_ J$ to itself, namely the first if $a\in J$ and the second if $a\notin J$, and the other term kills it. For $a\neq b$, both terms kill $e_ J$ unless $b\in J$ and $a\notin J$; in that case, writing $e_ J=\pm e_ b\wedge e_ K$, they map $e_ b\wedge e_ K$ to $e_ a\wedge e_ K$ and to $\epsilon (e_ b)^\dagger (e_ a\wedge e_ b\wedge e_ K)=-e_ a\wedge e_ K$, respectively. Writing $\mathrm dx_ i=\sum _ aM_{ia}e_ a$, so that $\sum _ aM_{ia}M_{ja}=g^{ij}$, we obtain by bilinearity

14.9
\begin{equation} \epsilon _ i\epsilon _ j^\dagger +\epsilon _ j^\dagger \epsilon _ i=g^{ij}. \end{equation}

In the coordinate frame $\mathrm du=\sum _ i\epsilon _ i\partial _ iu$, where $\partial _ i$ acts on the coefficients. Integrating by parts in $\int \sum _{I,J}G_{IJ}(\epsilon _ i\partial _ iu)_ I\overline{v_ J}\, \rho \, \mathrm dx$, we find $\mathrm d^*v=-\sum _ i\epsilon _ i^\dagger \partial _ iv+B_0v$, where $B_0$ is a matrix whose entries are linear combinations, with smooth coefficients, of first derivatives of the $G_{IJ}$ and of $\rho $. Since the $\epsilon _ i$ have constant coefficients in the coordinate frame, (14.9) gives

\[ \mathcal L=\mathrm d\mathrm d^*+\mathrm d^*\mathrm d=-\sum _{i,j}g^{ij}\partial _ i\partial _ j+B_1 \]

on each coefficient, where $B_1$ is a differential operator of order at most one with smooth coefficients. On functions $\mathrm d^*$ vanishes, so that

14.10
\begin{equation} Lf=\mathrm d^*\mathrm df=-\sum _{i,j}g^{ij}\partial _ i\partial _ jf+B_0\Bigl (\sum _ i\partial _ if\, \mathrm dx_ i\Bigr ); \end{equation}

at a point where the first derivatives of the metric coefficients vanish, $Lf=-\sum _{i,j}g^{ij}\partial _ i\partial _ jf$ there.

Now let $u$ be smooth with compact support in the chart. Since $\langle \mathcal Lu,u\rangle =\| \mathrm du\| ^2+\| \mathrm d^*u\| ^2$, integrating the second-order term by parts once gives

\[ \| \mathrm du\| ^2+\| \mathrm d^*u\| ^2 =\int \sum _{i,j,I,J}g^{ij}G_{IJ}\, \partial _ ju_ I\, \overline{\partial _ iu_ J}\, \rho \, \mathrm dx+E(u), \]

where $|E(u)|\leq C\| u\| _{L^2}\| u\| _{H^1}$, with $C$ depending on a compact set containing the support of $u$. For $\zeta =(\zeta _{jI})\in \mathbb C^{2n\times N}$, where $N$ is the number of multi-indices $I$, write $\zeta _ j=(\zeta _{jI})_ I$ and $G(\zeta _ j,\zeta _ i)=\sum _{I,J}G_{IJ}\zeta _{jI}\overline{\zeta _{iJ}}$. Diagonalizing the positive definite real symmetric matrix $(g^{ij})$ by an orthogonal matrix $O$, we get $\sum _{i,j}g^{ij}G(\zeta _ j,\zeta _ i)=\sum _ ad_ aG(\eta _ a,\eta _ a)$ with eigenvalues $d_ a>0$ and $\eta _ a=\sum _ jO_{aj}\zeta _ j$; hence this Hermitian form is positive definite, uniformly on compact subsets of the chart, and the integral is at least $\lambda \| \nabla u\| _{L^2}^2$ for some $\lambda >0$. Applying this to the $\chi _ ku$, whose images under $\mathrm d$ and $\mathrm d^*$ differ from $\chi _ k\, \mathrm du$ and $\chi _ k\, \mathrm d^*u$ by terms bounded by $C|u|$, summing over $k$, using $\sum _ k\chi _ k^2\leq 1$ and absorbing the error terms by $ab\leq \tau a^2+b^2/(4\tau )$ with small $\tau >0$, we obtain

14.11
\begin{equation} \| u\| _{H^1}^2\leq C\bigl (\| \mathrm du\| _{L^2}^2+ \| \mathrm d^*u\| _{L^2}^2+\| u\| _{L^2}^2\bigr ) \end{equation}

for smooth forms $u$ on $X$, and hence for all $u\in H^1$, since both sides are continuous for the norm of $H^1$.

Step 3: weak solutions. Fix $c>0$ and put $P=\mathcal L+c$, with the quadratic form $Q(v,w)=\langle \mathrm dv,\mathrm dw\rangle +\langle \mathrm d^*v,\mathrm d^*w\rangle +c\langle v,w\rangle $. Since $\mathrm d$ and $\mathrm d^*$ are first-order operators with smooth coefficients, $|Q(v,w)|\leq C\| v\| _{H^1}\| w\| _{H^1}$ for smooth forms, so $Q$ extends to a bounded sesquilinear form on $H^1$, and by (14.11) the norm $Q(u,u)^{1/2}$ is equivalent to the norm of $H^1$. For $b\in H^{-1}$, the map $w\mapsto \langle b,w\rangle $ is a bounded conjugate-linear functional on $H^1$, so the Riesz representation theorem (proposition 11.1.1) gives a unique $v\in H^1$ with $Q(v,w)=\langle b,w\rangle $ for all $w\in H^1$, and $\| v\| _{H^1}\leq C\| b\| _{H^{-1}}$. For smooth $\phi $ we have $Q(v,\phi )=\langle v,P\phi \rangle $, first for smooth $v$ and then for all $v\in H^1$, since both sides are continuous in $v$ for the norm of $H^1$; hence $Pv=b$ as distributional forms. We write $v=P^{-1}b$.

Step 4: interior regularity. We apply lemma 14.1.2 to $P$. In a chart, with the notation of Step 2, one integration by parts in the expression of $\mathcal L$ shows that $\langle Pv,w\rangle =\mathcal A(v,w)$ for smooth $v$ and $w$, the latter with compact support in the chart, where $\mathcal A$ is a form as in lemma 14.1.2 with principal coefficients $a^{ij}_{IJ}=g^{ij}G_{IJ}\rho $ and smooth lower-order coefficients. These principal coefficients satisfy the ellipticity condition on compact subsets of the chart, as shown in Step 2. Both sides are continuous in $v\in H^1$, so the equation $Q(v,w)=\langle b,w\rangle $ of Step 3 reads $\mathcal A(v,w)=\int \sum _ JF_ J\overline{w_ J}\, \mathrm dx$ with $F_ J=\rho \sum _ IG_{IJ}b_ I$. Let $b\in H^ r$, $r\geq 0$, and $v=P^{-1}b$. Applying lemma 14.1.2 with $\chi =\chi _ k$ on relatively compact open subsets of the finitely many charts, and using lemma 14.1.1(2), we obtain $\| v\| _{H^{r+2}}\leq C(\| b\| _{H^ r}+\| v\| _{H^1})$, and since $\| v\| _{H^1}\leq C\| b\| _{H^{-1}}\leq C\| b\| _{L^2}$,

\[ \| P^{-1}b\| _{H^{r+2}}\leq C_ r\| b\| _{H^ r},\qquad r\geq 0. \]

In particular, if $b$ is smooth, then $P^{-1}b$ lies in every $H^ r$ and is therefore smooth by lemma 14.1.1(3), and $PP^{-1}b=b$; and if $u$ is smooth, then $P^{-1}Pu=u$, because $Q(u,w)=\langle Pu,w\rangle $ for $w\in H^1$ and the solution in Step 3 is unique. For smooth $b$ and $\phi $, with $\psi =P^{-1}\phi $, we get $\langle P^{-1}b,\phi \rangle =\langle P^{-1}b,P\psi \rangle =Q(P^{-1}b,\psi )=\langle b,P^{-1}\phi \rangle $.

We therefore extend $P^{-1}$ to all distributional forms by $\langle P^{-1}u,\phi \rangle =\langle u,P^{-1}\phi \rangle $ for smooth $\phi $. If $u\in H^{-s}$ with $s\geq 2$, then $|\langle u,P^{-1}\phi \rangle |\leq \| u\| _{H^{-s}}\| P^{-1}\phi \| _{H^ s}\leq C\| u\| _{H^{-s}}\| \phi \| _{H^{s-2}}$, so $P^{-1}u\in H^{-s+2}$ and $P^{-1}$ is bounded from $H^ r$ to $H^{r+2}$ for $r\leq -2$; for $u\in H^{-1}$ the new definition agrees with that of Step 3, by the same computation with $b=u$. Since $P$ is formally self-adjoint, $\langle PP^{-1}u,\phi \rangle =\langle u,P^{-1}P\phi \rangle =\langle u,\phi \rangle $ and $\langle P^{-1}Pu,\phi \rangle =\langle u,PP^{-1}\phi \rangle =\langle u,\phi \rangle $ for every distributional form $u$, so $P$ is a bijection of the space of distributional forms onto itself with inverse $P^{-1}$. Conversely, $P$ is bounded from $H^{r+2}$ to $H^ r$ for every integer $r$: for $r\geq 0$ because it is a differential operator of order two with smooth coefficients, for $r=-1$ because $|\langle Pv,\phi \rangle |=|Q(v,\phi )|\leq C\| v\| _{H^1}\| \phi \| _{H^1}$, and for $r\leq -2$ by transposition, since $|\langle Pu,\phi \rangle |=|\langle u,P\phi \rangle |\leq C\| u\| _{H^{r+2}}\| \phi \| _{H^{-r}}$. Hence $P$ maps $H^{r+2}$ isomorphically onto $H^ r$. As $c>0$ was arbitrary, this proves (3).

Step 5: harmonic forms. If $\mathcal Lu=0$ distributionally, then $Pu=cu$, so $u=cP^{-1}u$. By lemma 14.1.1(5), $u\in H^{-r}$ for some $r$; iterating this identity raises the Sobolev order by two at each step, so $u$ lies in every $H^ r$ and is smooth by lemma 14.1.1(3). For smooth harmonic $u$, (14.11) gives $\| u\| _{H^1}\leq C\| u\| _{L^2}$. An $L^2$ limit of harmonic forms is distributionally harmonic, hence smooth and harmonic, so the closed unit ball of the space of harmonic forms in $L^2$ is bounded in $H^1$ and therefore compact by Step 1. A normed space with compact closed unit ball is finite dimensional: an infinite orthonormal sequence would have no convergent subsequence. This proves (1).

Step 6: the scalar equation. We first prove (4). If it fails, there are scalar functions $v_ j\in H^1$ of mean zero with $\| v_ j\| _{L^2}=1$ and $\mathrm dv_ j\to 0$ in $L^2$. Since $\mathrm d^*$ vanishes on functions, (14.11) shows that $(v_ j)$ is bounded in $H^1$, so by Step 1 a subsequence converges in $L^2$ to some $v$ of norm one and mean zero, and $\mathrm dv=\lim _ j\mathrm dv_ j=0$ in the sense of distributions. In every chart $v$ is locally constant by corollary 1.4.4, so $v$ is constant on the connected manifold $X$, which contradicts $\| v\| _{L^2}=1$ and mean zero. This proves (4).

Consequently $\| \mathrm dv\| _{L^2}$ is an equivalent Hilbert norm on the closed subspace of $H^1$ of functions of mean zero. Let $a$ be smooth of mean zero. The Riesz representation theorem (proposition 11.1.1) gives $f$ in this subspace with $\langle \mathrm df,\mathrm dw\rangle =\langle a,w\rangle $ for all $w$ of mean zero, hence for all $w\in H^1$, since both sides vanish for constant $w$. Testing with $w=f$ gives $\| \mathrm df\| ^2=\langle a,f\rangle \leq \| a\| \, \| f\| \leq C\| a\| \, \| \mathrm df\| $, hence $\| f\| _{H^1}\leq C\| a\| _{L^2}$. With $c=1$ the function $f$ satisfies $Q(f,w)=\langle a+f,w\rangle $ for all $w\in H^1$, so $f=P^{-1}(a+f)$ by the uniqueness in Step 3; if $f\in H^ r$, $r\geq 0$, then Step 4 gives $f\in H^{r+2}$ with $\| f\| _{H^{r+2}}\leq C(\| a\| _{H^ r}+\| f\| _{H^ r})$. By induction $f$ lies in every $H^ r$, so it is smooth, and $\| f\| _{H^{r+2}}\leq C_ r\| a\| _{H^ r}$ for every $r\geq 0$.

Let $\Pi a=\langle a,1\rangle /\int _ X\mathrm dV$ be the average of $a$, and let $\mathcal G$ be the operator $a\mapsto f$ applied to $a-\Pi a$, for smooth $a$. Then $\| \mathcal Ga\| _{H^{r+2}}\leq C_ r\| a\| _{H^ r}$ for $r\geq 0$, and $L\mathcal G=1-\Pi $ by construction. If $a$ is smooth, then $La$ has mean zero, because $\langle La,1\rangle =\langle a,L1\rangle =0$, and both $\mathcal GLa$ and $a-\Pi a$ are smooth solutions $f$ of mean zero of $Lf=La$. The difference $f_0$ of two such solutions satisfies $\int _ X|\mathrm df_0|^2\, \mathrm dV=\langle Lf_0,f_0\rangle =0$, so it is constant, and it vanishes since it has mean zero; hence $\mathcal GL=1-\Pi $. For smooth $a$ and $b$, since $\mathcal Ga$ has mean zero and $L$ is formally self-adjoint,

\[ \langle \mathcal Ga,b\rangle =\langle \mathcal Ga,L\mathcal Gb+\Pi b\rangle =\langle L\mathcal Ga,\mathcal Gb\rangle =\langle a-\Pi a,\mathcal Gb\rangle =\langle a,\mathcal Gb\rangle , \]

so $\mathcal G$ is formally self-adjoint, and so is $\Pi $. Extend $\mathcal G$ to distributions by transposition, $\langle \mathcal Ga,\chi \rangle =\langle a,\mathcal G\chi \rangle $. This defines a distribution: if $a\in H^{-s}$ with $s\geq 2$, then $|\langle a,\mathcal G\chi \rangle |\leq C\| a\| _{H^{-s}}\| \chi \| _{H^{s-2}}$. The two identities $L\mathcal G=\mathcal GL=1-\Pi $ remain valid, by transposition of the same identities for smooth functions. Hence $f=\mathcal Ga$ solves $Lf=a$ for every distribution $a$ of mean zero, and $\langle \mathcal Ga,1\rangle =\langle a,\mathcal G1\rangle =0$. If $Lf=0$ for a distribution $f$, then $\mathcal Lf=0$, so $f$ is smooth by Step 5, and $\int _ X|\mathrm df|^2\, \mathrm dV=\langle Lf,f\rangle =0$ shows that $f$ is constant; this proves uniqueness. If $a$ is real, then $\overline f$ is another solution of mean zero, since $L$ has real coefficients, so $f$ is real. $\square$

The next lemma solves a scalar equation in which the Laplacian is replaced by a mixed trace with respect to several Kähler forms; it is used for the mixed Hodge index theorem (theorem 14.3.3). We use the real operator $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ of (7.6), for which $\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$.

Lemma 14.1.4. Let $\omega _1,\ldots ,\omega _{n-1}$ be Kähler forms on $X$ and put $A=\omega _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \omega _{n-1}$, with $A=1$ when $n=1$. For every smooth real $(n,n)$-form $\eta $ with $\int _ X\eta =0$, there is a unique smooth real function $f$ of mean zero such that

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge A=\eta . \]

Proof. For smooth real functions $f,g$ put $B(f,g)=\int _ X\mathrm{d}f\wedge \mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}g\wedge A$. Only the $(1,1)$-part of $\mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g$ contributes, and this part equals $\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi }(\partial f\wedge \bar\partial g+\partial g\wedge \bar\partial f)$, which is symmetric in $f$ and $g$; in particular $\mathrm{d}f\wedge \mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}f$ has $(1,1)$-part $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial f\wedge \bar\partial f$. Thus $B$ is symmetric. Since $A$ is closed, Stokes' formula (proposition 7.1.3) applied to $\mathrm d(g\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge A)$ gives

\[ B(f,g)=B(g,f)=-\int _ Xg\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge A. \]

On the compact manifold there are constants $0<c\leq C$ with $c\, \omega \leq \omega _ j\leq C\omega $ for all $j$, by lemma 13.1.1. For $(1,1)$-forms $\gamma _ j,\gamma '_ j$ we have the telescoping identity

14.12
\begin{equation} \gamma _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \gamma _{n-1}-\gamma '_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \gamma '_{n-1}=\sum _{m=1}^{n-1}\gamma _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \gamma _{m-1}\wedge (\gamma _ m-\gamma '_ m)\wedge \gamma '_{m+1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \gamma '_{n-1}. \end{equation}

Applying it with $\gamma _ j=\omega _ j$, $\gamma '_ j=c\, \omega $ and with $\gamma _ j=C\omega $, $\gamma '_ j=\omega _ j$, we see that the forms $A-c^{n-1}\omega ^{n-1}$ and $C^{n-1}\omega ^{n-1}-A$ are sums of products of semipositive $(1,1)$-forms. Such products, and their products with the semipositive form $\mathrm i\partial f\wedge \bar\partial f$, are strongly positive (proposition 7.2.1). Hence $B(f,f)$ lies between $c^{n-1}$ and $C^{n-1}$ times $\int _ X\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial f\wedge \bar\partial f\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$. At a point, if $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ is a unitary coframe, that is, $\omega =\mathrm i\sum _ j\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ j$, and $\partial f=\sum _ ja_ j\theta _ j$, then $\mathrm i\partial f\wedge \bar\partial f\wedge \omega ^{n-1}/(n-1)!=\sum _ j|a_ j|^2\omega ^ n/n!$ and $|\mathrm df|^2=2\sum _ j|a_ j|^2$; so the last integral is $\frac{(n-1)!}{4\pi }\| \mathrm df\| _{L^2}^2$. By lemma 14.1.3(4), $B$ is therefore an equivalent inner product on the closed subspace of real functions of mean zero in $H^1$.

The sesquilinear extension $B_{\mathbb C}(f_1+\mathrm if_2,g_1+\mathrm ig_2)=B(f_1,g_1)+B(f_2,g_2)+\mathrm i\bigl (B(f_2,g_1)-B(f_1,g_2)\bigr )$ to complex-valued functions is, by the symmetry of $B$, a Hermitian form with $B_{\mathbb C}(f,f)=B(f_1,f_1)+B(f_2,f_2)$, hence an equivalent inner product on the complex Hilbert space of functions of mean zero in $H^1$. The Riesz representation theorem (proposition 11.1.1), applied to the bounded conjugate-linear functional $g\mapsto -\int _ X\overline g\, \eta $, gives $F$ in this space with $B_{\mathbb C}(F,g)=-\int _ X\overline g\, \eta $ for all $g$. For real $g$ the right side is real and $B_{\mathbb C}(F,g)=B(\operatorname{Re}F,g)+\mathrm iB(\operatorname{Im}F,g)$, so $f=\operatorname{Re}F$ is a real function of mean zero in $H^1$ with $B(f,g)=-\int _ Xg\eta $ for every real mean-zero $g\in H^1$. Since $\int _ X\eta =0$ and $B(f,1)=0$, the identity holds for all $g\in H^1$; this is the equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge A=\eta $ in the weak sense.

It remains to prove that $f$ is smooth. In a chart, with $\eta =\tilde\eta \, \mathrm dx$, the weak equation reads $\mathcal A(f,w)=-\int \tilde\eta \, \overline w\, \mathrm dx$ for $w\in C^\infty _ c$, where $\mathcal A(f,w)=B(f,\overline w)=\int \sum _{i,j}a^{ij}\partial _ jf\, \overline{\partial _ iw}\, \mathrm dx$ and the real symmetric matrix $(a^{ij})$ is the density of the quadratic form $\mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge A$. By the bounds just proved, $(a^{ij})$ is uniformly positive definite on compact subsets of the chart. This is a form of the kind considered in lemma 14.1.2, with $N=1$, and since $\tilde\eta $ is smooth, that lemma, applied with the cutoffs $\chi _ k$ on relatively compact open subsets of the charts, shows that every $\chi _ kf$ has square integrable distributional derivatives of all orders. Hence $f$ lies in every $H^ r$ by lemma 14.1.1(2), and it is smooth by lemma 14.1.1(3). If $f$ and $f'$ are two solutions, then $B(f-f',f-f')=0$, so $f-f'$ is constant on the connected manifold $X$, and it vanishes since it has mean zero. $\square$

Let $\Lambda $ be the pointwise adjoint of the Lefschetz operator $u\mapsto \omega \wedge u$; thus $\Lambda \omega =|\omega |^2=n$. Let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ be a unitary coframe of $(1,0)$-forms at a point, so that $\omega =\mathrm i\sum _ j\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ j$ there. A real $(1,1)$-form $T=\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}T_{j\bar k}\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ k$ corresponds to the Hermitian matrix $(T_{j\bar k})$, and $\Lambda T=\sum _ jT_{j\bar j}$ is its trace. Indeed, the covectors $\theta _ j,\bar\theta _ k$ form an orthonormal basis of the complexified cotangent space: in linear coordinates with $\mathrm dz_ j=\sqrt2\, \theta _ j$ at the point we have $\omega =\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$ there, so $g$ is Euclidean in the real coordinates at the point and $\langle \mathrm dx_ j+\mathrm i\, \mathrm dy_ j,\mathrm dx_ k\pm \mathrm i\, \mathrm dy_ k\rangle =(1\pm 1)\delta _{jk}$. Hence the forms $\mathrm i\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ k$ are orthonormal, and $\Lambda T=\langle T,\omega \rangle $ for $2$-forms $T$. Since $\omega ^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ is the sum of the products of $n-1$ distinct forms $\mathrm i\theta _ l\wedge \bar\theta _ l$, we have $\mathrm i\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ k\wedge \omega ^{n-1}/(n-1)!=\delta _{jk}\, \omega ^ n/n!$; equivalently,

\[ T\wedge \frac{\omega ^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=(\Lambda T)\frac{\omega ^ n}{n!}. \]

The last formula defines $\Lambda T$ for a current $T$, as a distribution; it is the action of $\Lambda $ on currents described at the beginning of this section.

To compute the trace of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$, we use holomorphic coordinates adapted to a Kähler form at a point.

Lemma 14.1.5. Let $\sigma $ be a Kähler form on a complex manifold $M$ of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $x\in M$. There are holomorphic coordinates $w$ centered at $x$ in which $\sigma =\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum _{k,l}\tilde g_{k\bar l}\, \mathrm dw_ k\wedge \mathrm d\bar w_ l$ with $\tilde g_{k\bar l}(0)=\delta _{kl}$ and with all first derivatives of the functions $\tilde g_{k\bar l}$ vanishing at $0$.

Proof. Choose holomorphic coordinates $z$ centered at $x$. Since the matrix of $\sigma $ at $x$ is positive definite Hermitian, after a linear change of coordinates $\sigma =\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum _{k,l}g_{k\bar l}\, \mathrm dz_ k\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ l$ with $g_{k\bar l}(0)=\delta _{kl}$; moreover $\overline{g_{l\bar k}}=g_{k\bar l}$, since $\sigma $ is real. The $(2,1)$-part of $\mathrm d\sigma =0$ is $\partial \sigma =\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum _{k,l,m}\partial _ mg_{k\bar l}\, \mathrm dz_ m\wedge \mathrm dz_ k\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ l=0$, which gives $\partial _ mg_{k\bar l}=\partial _ kg_{m\bar l}$.

By the holomorphic inverse function theorem (proposition 7.1.2(1)), the equations $z_ l=w_ l-\frac12\sum _{k,m}\partial _ mg_{k\bar l}(0)w_ kw_ m$ define holomorphic coordinates $w$ near $0$, since the Jacobian matrix of this map at $0$ is the identity. Substituting $\mathrm dz_ l=\mathrm dw_ l-\sum _{k,m}\partial _ mg_{k\bar l}(0)w_ m\, \mathrm dw_ k$, which uses the symmetry in $k$ and $m$, and $g_{k\bar l}(z)=\delta _{kl}+\sum _ m\partial _ mg_{k\bar l}(0)w_ m+\sum _ m\partial _{\bar m}g_{k\bar l}(0)\bar w_ m+O(|w|^2)$, we find that the coefficient of $\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\mathrm dw_ k\wedge \mathrm d\bar w_ l$ in $\sigma $ is $\delta _{kl}$ plus terms of order two in $w$: the terms $\sum _ m\partial _ mg_{k\bar l}(0)w_ m$ of $g_{k\bar l}$ cancel against the correction in $\mathrm dz_ k$, and their conjugates $\sum _ m\partial _{\bar m}g_{k\bar l}(0)\bar w_ m=\sum _ m\overline{\partial _ mg_{l\bar k}(0)}\, \bar w_ m$ cancel against the correction in $\mathrm d\bar z_ l$. Hence the first derivatives of the new coefficients vanish at $0$. $\square$

Lemma 14.1.6. For a scalar distribution $f$ and a real $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$,

14.13
\begin{equation} \Lambda \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=-\frac{Lf}{4\pi },\qquad *T=(\Lambda T)\frac{\omega ^{n-1}}{(n-1)!} -T\wedge \frac{\omega ^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}\quad (n\geq 2). \end{equation}

If $\mathrm dT=0$ and $\Lambda T$ is constant, then $\mathcal LT=0$.

Proof. Both sides of each identity are differential operators, or bundle maps, with smooth coefficients, acting on distributions and currents as explained at the beginning of this section. Hence it suffices to verify the identities for smooth $f$ and $T$, at an arbitrary point. By lemma 14.1.5, there are holomorphic coordinates centered at the point in which $\omega =\frac{\mathrm i}{2}\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$ at the point and the first derivatives of the coefficients of $\omega $, hence of the metric coefficients, vanish there. Thus (14.10) gives $Lf=-\sum _ j(\partial _{x_ j}^2+\partial _{y_ j}^2)f=-4\sum _ j\partial _ j\partial _{\bar j}f$ there, while $\theta _ j=\mathrm dz_ j/\sqrt2$ is a unitary coframe and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{j,k}f_{j\bar k}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k =\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}\frac{f_{j\bar k}}{\pi }\, \theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ k . \]

Hence $\Lambda \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\pi ^{-1}\sum _ jf_{j\bar j}=-Lf/(4\pi )$. For the second identity, diagonalize the Hermitian matrix of $T$ by a unitary change of coframe; by linearity it suffices to treat $T=\mathrm i\theta _1\wedge \bar\theta _1$. Write $\sqrt2\theta _ j=e_{2j-1}+\mathrm ie_{2j}$ with real covectors $e_ a$; they form an orthonormal basis, positively oriented since $\omega ^ n/n!=e_1\wedge \cdots \wedge e_{2n}$, and $\omega _ j=\mathrm i\theta _ j\wedge \bar\theta _ j=e_{2j-1}\wedge e_{2j}$. By the explicit formula for $*$, the star of $\omega _1$ is $\omega _2\wedge \cdots \wedge \omega _ n$. On the other hand $\omega ^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ is the sum of all products of $n-1$ distinct $\omega _ j$, while $\omega _1\wedge \omega ^{n-2}/(n-2)!$ is the sum of those products that contain $\omega _1$; their difference is exactly $\omega _2\wedge \cdots \wedge \omega _ n$, and $\Lambda \omega _1=1$.

If $\mathrm dT=0$ and $\Lambda T$ is constant, the second identity and $\mathrm d\omega =0$ give $\mathrm d*T=0$, hence $\mathrm d^*T=-*\mathrm d*T=0$, and $\mathcal LT=\mathrm d\mathrm d^*T+\mathrm d^*\mathrm dT=0$. In dimension one a $(1,1)$-current is $T=(\Lambda T)\omega $, so $*T=\Lambda T$ is constant and the same conclusion holds. $\square$

Since $\omega $ is an arbitrary Kähler form on $X$, lemma 14.1.3, lemma 14.1.6 hold for every Kähler form $\omega '$ on $X$ in place of $\omega $, with the same Sobolev spaces $H^ r$; we write $L_{\omega '}$ and $\Lambda _{\omega '}$ for the corresponding operators. We record a consequence for the complex Laplacian, which is used in chapter 16. Write $\omega '$ in local holomorphic coordinates as $\omega '=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{j,k}g_{j\bar k}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$, let $(g^{j\bar k})$ be the inverse matrix, normalized by $\sum _ kg^{j\bar k}g_{l\bar k}=\delta _{jl}$, and put $D_ gf=\sum _{j,k}g^{j\bar k}\partial _ j\partial _{\bar k}f$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{j,k}f_{j\bar k}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$, the function $D_ gf$ is the trace of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ relative to $\omega '$, and (14.13) for $\omega '$ gives

14.14
\begin{equation} D_ gf=\Lambda _{\omega '}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=-\frac{L_{\omega '}f}{4\pi }. \end{equation}

Consequently, by lemma 14.1.3 for $\omega '$, for every constant $\beta >0$ the operator $D_ g-\beta =-(4\pi )^{-1}(L_{\omega '}+4\pi \beta )$ is a bijection of the space of distributions onto itself and maps $H^{r+2}$ isomorphically onto $H^ r$ for every integer $r$. For $\beta =0$, the equation $D_ gf=a$ for a distribution $a$ has a solution if and only if $a$ has mean zero with respect to the volume form $\omega '^ n/n!$ (that is, $\int _ Xa\, \omega '^ n=0$ when $a$ is integrable); the solution is then unique up to an additive constant, and it is smooth if $a$ is smooth. The condition is necessary because $\langle L_{\omega '}f,1\rangle =\langle f,L_{\omega '}1\rangle =0$, with the pairing through $\omega '^ n/n!$.

Theorem 14.1.7 (The $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-lemma). Every closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$ has a unique decomposition

14.15
\begin{equation} T=H+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f, \end{equation}

where $H$ is a smooth harmonic real $(1,1)$-form and $f$ is a real distribution of mean zero. If $T$ is smooth, then $f$ is smooth. If $T$ is positive, then $f$ has a canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representative. Moreover, $T$ is exact as a current if and only if $H=0$.

Proof. Put $a=\int _ XT\wedge \omega ^{n-1}/\int _ X\omega ^ n$. Since $T\wedge \omega ^{n-1}=(\Lambda T)\omega ^ n/n$, the distribution $\Lambda T-na$ has mean zero, and lemma 14.1.3 gives a real distribution $f$ of mean zero with $Lf=-4\pi (\Lambda T-na)$. By lemma 14.1.6, $\Lambda \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\Lambda T-na$, so the closed current $U=T-a\omega -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ has $\Lambda U=0$. By lemma 14.1.6 again, $U$ is harmonic, and by lemma 14.1.3 it is smooth. The Kähler form is harmonic as well, since $\mathrm d\omega =0$ and $*\omega =\omega ^{n-1}/(n-1)!$ is closed. Hence $H=a\omega +U$ and $f$ give the decomposition (14.15). If $T$ is smooth, then $\Lambda T$ is smooth, and so is $f$ by lemma 14.1.3.

An exact harmonic form $B$ vanishes: if $B=\mathrm dS$ for a current $S$, then

\[ \| B\| _{L^2}^2=\int _ X\mathrm dS\wedge *B=\pm \, S(\mathrm d*B)=0, \]

since $\mathrm d*B=0$. If $T=H'+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f'$ is a second decomposition, then $H-H'=\mathrm d\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(f'-f)$ is exact and harmonic, hence zero. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(f-f')=0$, so $L(f-f')=0$ by lemma 14.1.6; thus $f-f'$ is constant by lemma 14.1.3, and it vanishes because both have mean zero. The same argument proves the exactness criterion: if $T$ is exact, so is $H=T-\mathrm d\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f$, whence $H=0$; conversely, if $H=0$, then $T=\mathrm d\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f$.

Suppose now that $T\geq 0$. By theorem 7.3.2, theorem 8.2.1, lemma 13.1.2, every point of $X$ has a coordinate ball on which $T=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $u$ plurisubharmonic and $H=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ with $h$ smooth. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(f+h-u)=0$, so the scalar Laplacian of the distribution $f+h-u$ vanishes, and Weyl's lemma (theorem 2.1.4) shows that it is a smooth pluriharmonic function $p$. Thus $f=u-h+p$ is, on the ball, the class of a plurisubharmonic function minus a smooth function. On the overlap of two balls, the two local representatives $u_1-h_1+p_1$ and $u_2-h_2+p_2$ agree almost everywhere; after adding $h_1-p_1$ to both, both become plurisubharmonic, because $h_1-p_1-h_2+p_2$ is smooth with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(h_1-p_1-h_2+p_2)=H-H=0$, and plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere (theorem 8.2.1). Hence the local representatives define a global quasi-plurisubharmonic representative of $f$. It is the only one, since two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere (lemma 13.1.4(1)). $\square$

Definition 14.1.8. The real $(1,1)$-cohomology space of $X$ is

\[ H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)= \frac{\{ \text{closed smooth real $(1,1)$-forms}\} }{\{ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f:f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)\} }. \]

The class of a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ is denoted $\{ \theta \} $. For a closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ we put $\{ T\} =\{ H\} $, where $H$ is the harmonic form in (14.15).

By theorem 14.1.7, every class contains exactly one harmonic form, so $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is isomorphic to the space of harmonic real $(1,1)$-forms; in particular it is finite dimensional, and we give it its vector-space topology. The real de Rham cohomology in degree two is the quotient of the closed smooth real $2$-forms by the forms $\mathrm d\beta $, with $\beta $ a smooth real $1$-form. A closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is exact in this sense has $H=0$ and is therefore of the form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ with $f$ smooth; hence $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is identified with the subspace of de Rham classes represented by $(1,1)$-forms.

Choosing a basis of harmonic forms gives a linear choice $\alpha \mapsto \theta _\alpha $ of smooth representatives. Each basis form $H_ k$ satisfies $-c_ k\omega \leq H_ k\leq c_ k\omega $ for some $c_ k$, by lemma 13.1.1, applied with $K=X$ to $H_ k$ and to $-H_ k$, and all norms on the finite-dimensional space $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ are equivalent; hence for every norm there is a constant $C$ such that

14.16
\begin{equation} -C\| \alpha \| \omega \leq \theta _\alpha \leq C\| \alpha \| \omega . \end{equation}

The class of a closed current does not change when $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a distribution is added to it, by the uniqueness in theorem 14.1.7. If $H_1,\ldots ,H_ N$ is an $L^2$-orthonormal basis of harmonic real $(1,1)$-forms, the coefficients of the harmonic part of $T$ are the pairings $\int _ XT\wedge *H_ k$, because $*H_ k$ is closed and therefore $\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge *H_ k=0$. Hence the map $T\mapsto \{ T\} $ on closed currents is continuous for weak convergence.

Positive currents in a class are thus described by quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials.

Corollary 14.1.9. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. A closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$ satisfies $\{ T\} =\{ \theta \} $ if and only if $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ for some $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and then $\varphi $ is unique up to an additive constant. In particular, the class $\{ \theta \} $ contains a closed positive current if and only if $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $.

Proof. Let $T\geq 0$ be closed with $\{ T\} =\{ \theta \} $. Write $T=H+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ as in (14.15); since $\{ T\} =\{ \theta \} $, also $\theta =H+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ with $g$ smooth. By theorem 14.1.7, $f$ has a canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representative, and the difference $\varphi $ of this representative and $g$ satisfies $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $; thus $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Conversely, if $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ is a closed positive current, and it lies in $\{ \theta \} $ because adding $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a distribution does not change the class. If $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi '$ with $\varphi ,\varphi '\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, then $\varphi -\varphi '$ is constant almost everywhere by the uniqueness part of theorem 14.1.7, say equal to $c$, and the quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi $ and $\varphi '+c$, which agree almost everywhere, agree everywhere by lemma 13.1.4(1). $\square$

If $\theta $ is replaced by $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ with $h$ smooth, then $\varphi $ is replaced by $\varphi -h$, up to a constant. Consequently the pole set, the Lelong numbers and the comparison of singularities of potentials of currents in $\{ \theta \} $ do not depend on the choice of the smooth representative $\theta $.
