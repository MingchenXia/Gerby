Uniformly controlled complex Hessians

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.8.2 Uniformly controlled complex Hessians

The preceding construction bounds the solution form and its Gauduchon factor by controlling derivatives of the logarithmic density. A bound on the solution form itself leads to a different estimate. If $\Omega =\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$, then $d\Omega =d\omega $, so the divergence of its cofactor depends only on the fixed torsion and the size of $\Omega $. This permits uniform bounds for the factor even when derivatives of the varying complex Hessian are unbounded.

We continue to assume $n\geq 3$ and that $\mathrm{d}V$ is a probability volume form. We first give the real elliptic estimate used below. A cube denoted by $tQ$ has the same center as $Q$ and $t$ times its side length, and $\fint _ Q$ denotes its Euclidean average.

Lemma 26.8.7. Let $Q\subset \mathbb R^ m$, $m>2$, be a cube. Suppose that $u$ is a smooth strictly positive solution on a neighborhood of $3Q$ of

\[ \operatorname {div}(A\nabla u+Bu)=0, \qquad \lambda I\leq A\leq \Lambda I,\qquad |B|\leq K, \]

where $A$ is a smooth symmetric matrix field and $B$ is a smooth vector field. Then

\[ \sup _{Q/2}u\leq C\inf _{Q/2}u, \]

where $C$ depends only on $m,\lambda ,\Lambda ,K$ and the side length of $Q$. Derivatives of $A$ and $B$ do not enter $C$.

Proof. We first estimate positive and negative powers, then compare their small moments by an estimate for $\log u$.

For $p\ne 0,1$, test the weak equation by $\eta ^2u^{p-1}$, where $\eta $ is a smooth cutoff. The principal term is

\[ (p-1)\int \eta ^2u^{p-2}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle . \]

The other terms contain respectively $2\eta u^{p-1}A\nabla u\cdot \nabla \eta $, $(p-1)\eta ^2u^{p-1}B\cdot \nabla u$, and $2\eta u^ pB\cdot \nabla \eta $. Taking absolute values after moving the principal term to one side and applying Young's inequality absorbs half of its energy. Thus, for either sign of $p$,

26.48
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-harnack-power} \int \eta ^2|\nabla u^{p/2}|^2 \leq C_1p^2(1+|p-1|^{-2}) \int (|\nabla \eta |^2+K^2\eta ^2)u^ p, \end{equation}

with $C_1$ depending only on the ellipticity bounds. Each test is legitimate since this individual smooth positive solution is bounded above and away from zero on the cutoff support.

Put $\beta =m/(m-2)>1$ and choose $p_0=\beta ^{-k-1/2}$, with $k$ to be fixed later. Apply the Euclidean Sobolev inequality to $\eta u^{p/2}$ on nested cubes shrinking from $Q$ to $Q/2$. Choose the cutoff gaps proportional to $2^{-j}$ and the powers $p_ j=p_0\beta ^ j$. The positive powers never equal one: the closest two are $\beta ^{-1/2}$ and $\beta ^{1/2}$. Consequently the constants in (26.48), together with the cutoff terms from $\nabla (\eta u^{p/2})$, grow at most polynomially in $p_ j$ and exponentially in $j$. Since $\sum _ j(j+1)\beta ^{-j}<\infty $, their iteration product converges. Negative powers give the same calculation for $u^{-1}$. We obtain

26.49
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-harnack-moments} \sup _{Q/2}u\leq C_2\left(\fint _ Q u^{p_0}\right)^{1/p_0}, \qquad \inf _{Q/2}u\geq C_2^{-1} \left(\fint _ Q u^{-p_0}\right)^{-1/p_0}. \end{equation}

Here $C_2$ also depends on $p_0$ and the fixed cube size.

Testing instead by $\eta ^2/u$ gives

\[ \int \eta ^2|\nabla \log u|^2 \leq C_3\int (|\nabla \eta |^2+K^2\eta ^2). \]

For every subcube $S\subset Q$ of side length $\ell $, a cutoff supported in $2S\subset 3Q$, equal to one on $S$, has derivative bounded by $C/\ell $. Poincaré's inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz therefore give

\[ \fint _ S|\log u-(\log u)_ S| \leq C_4(1+K\ell )\leq K_0, \]

where $K_0\geq 1$ is uniform and $(\log u)_ S$ denotes the mean. We spell out the exponential consequence of this mean oscillation bound.

Write $f=\log u$ and fix $a>2$. Select the maximal dyadic subcubes $S$ of $Q$ on which $\fint _ S|f-f_ Q|>aK_0$. Their total measure is at most $|Q|/a$. The parent of each selected cube has average at most $aK_0$, hence $|f_ S-f_ Q|\leq 2^ maK_0$. Outside their union, differentiation of dyadic averages gives $|f-f_ Q|\leq aK_0$ almost everywhere. Repeat this selection inside each chosen cube, subtracting its own mean. The union at generation $j$ has measure at most $a^{-j}|Q|$, and outside generation $j+1$ the accumulated mean changes and last oscillation give

\[ |f-f_ Q|\leq (j2^ ma+a)K_0. \]

The intersection of all generations is null. Choosing $c>0$ so that $\exp (c2^ ma)<a$ and summing over the generations yields

\[ \fint _ Q\exp \! \left(\frac{c|f-f_ Q|}{K_0}\right)\leq C_5, \]

with dimensional constants. Now fix $k$ large enough that $p_0<c/K_0$. Multiplying the estimates for the positive and negative exponentials cancels $f_ Q$ and gives

\[ \left(\fint _ Q u^{p_0}\right) \left(\fint _ Q u^{-p_0}\right)\leq C_5^2. \]

Combining this with (26.49) proves the result. Every constant comes from the stated coefficient bounds and the fixed cube. $\square$

Proposition 26.8.8. Fix $0<\delta \leq \Lambda $. For every smooth real $v$ with

\[ \delta \omega \leq \Omega _ v:=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\leq \Lambda \omega , \]

let $\rho _ v$ be its Gauduchon factor normalized by

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ v\Omega _ v^{n-1})=0, \qquad \int _ X\rho _ v\, \mathrm{d}V=1. \]

There is $C_ H\geq 1$, depending only on $X,\omega ,\mathrm{d}V,\delta ,\Lambda $, such that $C_ H^{-1}\leq \rho _ v\leq C_ H$ on $X$ for every such $v$.

Proof. Existence and uniqueness of $\rho _ v$ follow from theorem 4.1.2. Fix a real reference metric on $X$, and put $Q_ v=\Omega _ v^{n-1}$ and $J_ v=\Omega _ v^ n/\mathrm{d}V$. Since $d\Omega _ v=d\omega $,

26.50
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-hessian-cofactor-divergence} dQ_ v=(n-1)d\omega \wedge \Omega _ v^{n-2}. \end{equation}

Both $Q_ v$ and $dQ_ v$ have uniformly bounded coefficients in fixed charts.

Define a real tensor $A_ v$ and vector field $b_ v$ by

\[ \langle A_ vd\phi ,d\psi \rangle \, \mathrm{d}V =n\, d\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge Q_ v, \qquad (b_ v\cdot d\phi )\, \mathrm{d}V =-n\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge dQ_ v. \]

Only the $(1,1)$ part of $d\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi $ contributes to the first identity; it is

\[ \frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi } (\partial \psi \wedge \bar\partial \phi +\partial \phi \wedge \bar\partial \psi ). \]

Thus $A_ v$ is symmetric and uniformly positive definite, with a uniform upper bound. The second identity and (26.50) give a uniform bound for $b_ v$.

For $L_{\Omega _ v}\phi =n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi \wedge Q_ v/\Omega _ v^ n$, Stokes' theorem gives

\begin{align*} \int _ X\psi J_ vL_{\Omega _ v}\phi \, \mathrm{d}V & =-n\int _ Xd\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge Q_ v +n\int _ X\psi \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge dQ_ v\\ & =-\int _ X\langle A_ vd\phi ,d\psi \rangle \, \mathrm{d}V -\int _ X\psi b_ v\cdot d\phi \, \mathrm{d}V. \end{align*}

In particular, the adjoint equation for $\rho _ v$ is

\[ \operatorname {div}_{\mathrm{d}V}(A_ vd\rho _ v+b_ v\rho _ v)=0. \]

In each coordinate chart, multiplying the coefficients by the fixed smooth volume density converts this to Euclidean divergence form while preserving uniform ellipticity and a uniform bound for the vector coefficient. Lemma 26.8.7 applies. A fixed finite cover by inner cubes has a connected overlap graph, since $X$ is connected. Chaining its local estimates gives $\sup _ X\rho _ v\leq C_ H\inf _ X\rho _ v$. The normalization and $\mathrm{d}V(X)=1$ imply the claimed two-sided bound. $\square$

For a fixed $0<\varepsilon <1$, consider the full family

26.51
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-controlled-hessian-family} \mathcal V_\varepsilon = \{ v\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R):\sup _ Xv=0, -\varepsilon \omega \leq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\leq \varepsilon \omega \} . \end{equation}

Set $c_ v=\int _ X\Omega _ v^ n$ and $F_ v=\log (J_ v/c_ v)$, so that $\int _ X\mathrm e^{F_ v}\mathrm{d}V=1$. The density $F_ v$ is defined by an actual solution form, rather than prescribed before solving an equation.

Theorem 26.8.9. Let

\[ j_-=(1-\varepsilon )^ n\min _ X\frac{\omega ^ n}{\mathrm{d}V}, \qquad j_+=(1+\varepsilon )^ n\max _ X\frac{\omega ^ n}{\mathrm{d}V}, \]

and take $C_ H$ from proposition 26.8.8 with $\delta =1-\varepsilon $ and $\Lambda =1+\varepsilon $. For every $v\in \mathcal V_\varepsilon $, the exact maximizing current $T_ v\in \mathscr G_\omega $ for $F_ v$ is unique. Its determinant density $k_{T_ v}$ and the probability density $r_ v=\mathrm d\nu _{T_ v,F_ v}/\mathrm{d}V$ satisfy

26.52
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-hessian-adjoint-bounds} \frac{j_-}{C_ Hj_+}\leq r_ v\leq \frac{C_ Hj_+}{j_-}, \qquad j_+^{-1/n}\frac{j_-}{C_ Hj_+} \leq k_{T_ v}\leq j_-^{-1/n}\frac{C_ Hj_+}{j_-}. \end{equation}

In particular both densities are uniformly bounded at the critical exponent $n/(n-1)$, without an assumption on the lower mass. Moreover,

\[ \| F_ v-F_0\| _\infty \leq n\log \frac{1+\varepsilon }{1-\varepsilon }, \]

and this normalized family contains infinitely many distinct nonconstant densities on the fixed manifold $X$.

Proof. Use the normalization of proposition 26.8.8 and put

\[ N_ v=\int _ XJ_ v\rho _ v\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad T_ v=\frac{\rho _ v}{N_ v}\Omega _ v^{n-1}. \]

The factor equation and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-Stokes give

\[ \int _ X\omega \wedge \rho _ v\Omega _ v^{n-1} =\int _ X\Omega _ v\wedge \rho _ v\Omega _ v^{n-1}=N_ v. \]

Hence $T_ v\in \mathscr G_\omega $. It is the $(n-1)$st power of the Gauduchon metric $\gamma _ v=(\rho _ v/N_ v)^{1/(n-1)}\Omega _ v$. The equation $\Omega _ v^ n=c_ v\mathrm e^{F_ v}\mathrm{d}V$ gives $B_0(F_ v)=\log c_ v$. Thus theorem 26.6.2 identifies $T_ v$ as the unique exact maximizer, and $M(F_ v)=c_ v^{-1/n}$. The determinant and probability normalizations are therefore

26.53
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-hessian-adjoint-densities} k_{T_ v}=\frac{\rho _ v}{N_ v}J_ v^{(n-1)/n}, \qquad \Gamma _{F_ v}(T_ v)=c_ v^{-1/n}\frac{J_ v\rho _ v}{N_ v}\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad r_ v=\frac{J_ v\rho _ v}{N_ v}. \end{equation}

Here $j_-\leq J_ v\leq j_+$ and, since $\int _ X\rho _ v\, \mathrm{d}V=1$, also $j_-\leq N_ v\leq j_+$. The factor bound gives the first pair of inequalities in (26.52); the identity $k_{T_ v}=J_ v^{-1/n}r_ v$ gives the second. The critical norms are bounded by the respective upper constants because $\mathrm{d}V$ is a probability volume.

The inequalities

\[ (1-\varepsilon )^ n\leq \frac{J_ v}{J_0}\leq (1+\varepsilon )^ n, \qquad (1-\varepsilon )^ n\leq \frac{c_ v}{c_0}\leq (1+\varepsilon )^ n \]

give the bound for $F_ v-F_0$ by subtracting logarithms. To prove nontriviality, choose a nonconstant smooth real $\phi $. For small $t$, the potential $t\phi -\sup _ X(t\phi )$ belongs to $\mathcal V_\varepsilon $ and gives the same density as $t\phi $. Differentiation at zero gives

\[ \left.\frac{d}{dt}F_{t\phi }\right|_{t=0} =L_\omega \phi - \frac{\int _ XJ_0L_\omega \phi \, \mathrm{d}V}{\int _ XJ_0\, \mathrm{d}V}. \]

This function is nonconstant: otherwise $L_\omega \phi $ would be constant, its values at a maximum and a minimum would force that constant to be zero, and the strong maximum principle would make $\phi $ constant. Choose two points where the displayed derivative differs. The difference of the densities at these points is strictly monotone for $t$ in a small interval. These give infinitely many distinct densities, with at most one parameter having equal values at the chosen points. Discarding that parameter gives the nonconstant family. $\square$

Uniform bounds for the solution forms need not give derivative bounds, or even equicontinuity, for their normalized logarithmic densities.

Example 26.8.10. For every fixed $0<\varepsilon <1$, there are $v_ j\in \mathcal V_\varepsilon $ with $\| v_ j\| _{C^0}\to 0$ such that their normalized densities $F_{v_ j}$ are pairwise distinct, bounded in $C^0$, and unbounded in $C^1$. This family is not equicontinuous. Nevertheless, both densities of its exact maximizers satisfy the uniform bounds of theorem 26.8.9.

Proof. Choose holomorphic coordinates centered at a point $p$, put $x=\operatorname {Re}z_1$, and take a smooth real cutoff $\zeta $ supported in the chart and equal to one near $p$. We may assume $0\leq \zeta \leq 1$. On the chart set

\[ \beta =dx\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(x^2/2) =\frac{\mathrm i}{8\pi }dz_1\wedge d\bar z_1. \]

This is a nonzero semipositive form of rank one. For a constant $a>0$ to be chosen, extend

\[ w_ j=\frac a{j^2}\zeta \sin (jx) \]

by zero outside the chart, and put $v_ j=w_ j-\sup _ Xw_ j$. The product rule gives

\begin{align*} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j={}& -a\zeta \sin (jx)\beta +\frac aj\cos (jx) (d\zeta \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}x+dx\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\zeta )\\ & +\frac a{j^2}\sin (jx)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\zeta . \end{align*}

All fixed forms in this expression have compact support after multiplication by the indicated cutoff or its derivatives. Thus a fixed $C>0$ bounds the operator norm of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j$ relative to $\omega $ by $aC$ for every $j\geq 1$. Choose $a$ so small that $aC\leq \varepsilon /2$. Then $v_ j\in \mathcal V_\varepsilon $, and $\| v_ j\| _{C^0}\leq 2a/j^2$.

Write $s=\operatorname {tr}_{\omega (p)}\beta >0$. Since $x(p)=0$ and $\zeta =1$ near $p$, we have $\Omega _{v_ j}(p)=\omega (p)$ and

\[ \partial _ x\Omega _{v_ j}(p)=\partial _ x\omega (p)-aj\beta . \]

Differentiating the logarithmic determinant, and observing that its normalizing integral is constant in the spatial variable, gives the exact identity

\[ \partial _ xF_{v_ j}(p) =\partial _ x\log \frac{\omega ^ n}{\mathrm{d}V}(p)-ajs. \]

These derivatives are pairwise different and their absolute values tend to infinity. Hence the densities are pairwise distinct and their $C^1$ norms are unbounded. Their uniform $C^0$ bound is part of theorem 26.8.9.

To check failure of equicontinuity, take points $p_ j^\pm $ with $x=\pm \pi /(2j)$ and all other real coordinates zero. For large $j$ they lie where $\zeta =1$, and $\Omega _{v_ j}(p_ j^\pm )=\omega (p_ j^\pm )\mp a\beta $. The integral normalizing $F_{v_ j}$ cancels from the difference, so

\[ F_{v_ j}(p_ j^-)-F_{v_ j}(p_ j^+) \longrightarrow \log \frac{1+as}{1-as}>0. \]

Indeed, $\beta ^2=0$ gives $(\omega (p)\pm a\beta )^ n=(1\pm as)\omega (p)^ n$, and $as<1$ by the chosen form comparison. Both points tend to $p$, which proves the failure of equicontinuity. The exact-adjoint bounds still apply because the entire sequence lies in $\mathcal V_\varepsilon $. $\square$

The same estimate applies to the family with $\delta \omega \leq \Omega _ v\leq \Lambda \omega $ by replacing $j_-$ and $j_+$ with $\delta ^ n\min _ X(\omega ^ n/\mathrm{d}V)$ and $\Lambda ^ n\max _ X(\omega ^ n/\mathrm{d}V)$. Applying the estimate to a prescribed family of densities requires uniformly comparable solution forms. Thus this construction provides critical estimates when $v_-(\omega )=0$ for a larger smooth family, but leaves the question for arbitrary bounded families of continuous densities open.
