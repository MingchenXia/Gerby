Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 1.5 Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

In practice, it is important to consider a variant of plurisubharmonic functions. We will fix a complex manifold $X$.

Definition 1.5.1. Let $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$.

A $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function or $\theta $-psh function for short on $X$ is a function $\varphi \colon X\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ such that for each $x\in X$ and each open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$ satisfying the condition that $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ for some smooth function $g$ on $U$, we have $g+\varphi |_ U\in \mathrm{PSH}(U)$. The set of $\theta $-psh functions on $X$ is denoted by $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

A quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ is a function $\varphi \colon X\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ such that there exists a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta '$ on $X$ such that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$. The set of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ is denoted by $\mathrm{QPSH}(X)$.

There is a natural preorder on $\mathrm{QPSH}(X)$ defined as follows:
Definition 1.5.2.Assume that $X$ is compact. Given $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$, we say that $\varphi $ is more singular than $\psi $ and write $\varphi \preceq \psi $
Note. Some people write $\psi \preceq \varphi $.
if there is $C\in \mathbb {R}$ such that $\varphi \leq \psi +C$. We also say $\psi $ is less singular than $\varphi $ and write $\psi \succeq \varphi $.

In case $\varphi \preceq \psi $ and $\psi \preceq \varphi $, we say $\varphi $ and $\psi $ have the same singularity type. We write $\varphi \sim \psi $ in this case.

It is sometimes convenient to include the constant function $-\infty $ as well. Then we always write $-\infty \preceq \varphi $ for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$. Formally, we write $-\infty \sim -\infty $ as well. When $X$ is not compact, one can still define similar notions, but the generalization is not unique, and we shall not consider them in this book.

Remark 1.5.3.The preceding results concerning plurisubharmonic functions can be extended mutatis mutandis to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions. We will apply these extensions without further explanations.

Proposition 1.5.4.Assume that $X$ is compact. Let $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then for any $a,b\in \mathbb {R}$, $a\leq b$, the set

\[ \left\{ \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \sup _ X \varphi \in [a,b]\right\} \]

is compact with respect to the $L^1$-topology. Moreover, $\varphi \mapsto \sup _ X \varphi $ is $L^1$-continuous for $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

This is an immediate consequence of [Proposition 8.5, Exercise 1.20, GZ17]

Remark 1.5.5. More generally, if $K\subseteq X$ is a closed non-pluripolar subset, then

\[ \left\{ \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \sup _ K \varphi \in [a,b]\right\} \]

is relatively compact with respect to the $L^1$-topology. See [Corollary 4.3, GZ05].

As a consequence, if $\sup _ K \varphi \leq C_0$ for some $C_0\in \mathbb {R}$, then $\sup _ X \varphi \leq C_1$ for some $C_1\in \mathbb {R}$ as well.

To see this, we may assume that $C_0>0$. When $\sup _ K \varphi \in [0,C_0]$, our assertion follows from the above. If $\sup _ K \varphi <0$, it suffices to observe that

\[ \varphi \leq \varphi -\sup _ K \varphi . \]

Corollary 1.5.6. Assume that $X$ is compact. Let $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\sup _ X \varphi _ i$ is uniformly bounded from below. Then $\varphi =\inf _{i\in I} \varphi _ i$ is a $\theta $-psh function and

1.35
\begin{equation} \label{eq:decreasingnetL1conv} \adjustlimits \lim _{i\in I}\sup _ X \varphi _ i=\sup _ X \varphi ,\quad \varphi _ i\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi . \end{equation}

Proof. We may assume that $X$ is connected.

It suffices to show that $\varphi \not\equiv -\infty $. From this, it follows that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi _ i\to \varphi $ in $L^1$ by proposition 1.2.2. The $L^1$-continuity of $\eta \mapsto \sup _ X\eta $ on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ from proposition 1.5.4 then gives the first equality in (1.35).

Fix $i_0\in I$ and replace $I$ by the cofinal tail $I_{\geq i_0}$. This does not change the infimum. On this tail we have

\[ \sup _ X\varphi _ i\leq \sup _ X\varphi _{i_0}, \]

while the suprema are bounded from below by assumption. Hence proposition 1.5.4 shows that the net $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is relatively compact with respect to the $L^1$-topology. We may therefore take a subnet $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in J}$ converging in $L^1$ to some $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Since $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in J}$ is a Willard subnet of $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$, we know that it is also decreasing. Therefore, $\psi =\inf _{j\in J} \varphi _ j$ due to proposition 1.2.2 and proposition 1.2.6.

Fix $i_0\in I$. For all sufficiently large $j\in J$, the corresponding index of the subnet dominates $i_0$, hence

\[ \varphi _ j\leq \varphi _{i_0}. \]

Therefore, $\psi \leq \varphi _{i_0}$. As $i_0$ was arbitrary, $\psi \leq \varphi $ everywhere. In particular, $\varphi $ is not identically $-\infty $. $\square$

Proposition 1.5.7. Assume that $X$ is compact. Let $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $E$ be a prime divisor over $X$. Then

\[ \sup \Bigl \{ \nu (\varphi ,E): \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ) \Bigr \} <\infty . \]

Proof. It follows from the proof of [Exercise 2.7, GZ17] that $\nu (\bullet ,E)$ is upper semicontinuous with respect to the $L^1$-topology on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Since adding a constant does not change Lelong numbers, we may restrict to the compact set $\{ \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):\sup _ X\varphi =0\} $ from proposition 1.5.4. The desired upper bound follows. $\square$

Proposition 1.5.8. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a compact Kähler manifold $Y$. Let $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then the pull-back gives a bijection

\[ \pi ^*\colon \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\xlongrightarrow {\sim }\mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta ). \]
This follows from a more general result theorem B.1.4.

Since we shall constantly need the balayage argument of proposition 1.2.14, we explain explicitly the global version of it.

Proposition 1.5.9. Fix a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $B\Subset B' \subseteq X$ be two holomorphic balls. Assume that $\varphi $ is locally bounded on $B'$. Then there is a unique $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ so that $\psi $ is bounded on an open neighborhood of $\overline{B}$, and

\[ \begin{cases} \left( \theta |_ B+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi |_ B \right)^ n = 0,\\ \psi = \varphi \quad \textup{on } X\setminus B. \end{cases} \]

Moreover, $\psi \geq \varphi $.

We shall call $\psi $ the balayage of $\varphi $ on $B$.

Proof. By the Dolbeault–Grothendieck lemma, after possibly shrinking $B'$, we may write $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ for some bounded function $g\in C^{\infty }(B')$. We can then use $g$ to translate $\theta $-psh functions to psh functions, and our assertion follows immediately from proposition 1.2.14. $\square$
