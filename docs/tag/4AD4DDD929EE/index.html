Envelope systems over Hermitian perturbations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 25 Envelope systems over Hermitian perturbations

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and fix real numbers $a<c$.

For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the multiplier-ideal envelope $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ of definition 16.2.1 is the regularized supremum of the nonpositive $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions with the multiplier ideals of $u$. It depends on the background $\theta $. When $\theta $ is perturbed by Hermitian forms, the envelopes of one multiplier-ideal class in the perturbed backgrounds form a compatible family, and as the perturbation tends to zero these normalized representatives decrease to a potential in the original background. Projecting the limit back to the perturbed backgrounds recovers the family exactly when the limit keeps the multiplier ideals of the family; otherwise it gives the greatest family below it that comes from the original background.

We apply this to test curves that are constant for parameters $\tau \leq a$ and have cutoff $c$. Recovery can fail even in a Kähler background: on a ruled surface, at zero volume, the limit acquires an additional divisorial pole. On the other hand, for big backgrounds on manifolds with the bounded mass property, recovery holds by theorem 30.5.6 when the lower volumes $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}$ of the initial slices (definition 13.3.1) have a positive limit. On the Hopf threefold, scalar invariance along the elliptic fibers instead allows recovery from positive lower volume on the projective base, by theorem 25.6.3, even though ambient upper mass is unbounded. Smooth changes of the background commute with recovery, whereas the limiting Monge–Ampère mass can change when the background is not closed.

We also use the ordinary singularity envelope $P_\theta [u]$ of definition 1.19.4, the ordinary and multiplier-ideal singularity orders of section 1.12, and the extended Monge–Ampère measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}$ of theorem 6.1.2. A potential $u$ with $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}=u$ is called $P_\theta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed.
