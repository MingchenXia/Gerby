16.7 Singularity constraints and relative capacity2B1B1CB179EE

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 16.7 Singularity constraints and relative capacity

We now combine a singularity constraint with an obstacle. For a bounded obstacle the resulting envelope stays within a bounded distance of the singularity envelope $P_\theta [u]$, and it satisfies a contact bound. We then introduce the capacity relative to a reference potential $\phi $, defined through the potentials in the strip $\phi -1\leq v\leq \phi $, and use it to measure the deep sublevel sets of potentials of full mass relative to $\phi $. Throughout this section $\{ \theta \} $ is big.

Definition 16.7.1. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and a Borel obstacle $f$, define

\[ P_\theta [u](f)=\left(\sup \{ v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): v\preceq u,\ v\leq f\text{ quasi-everywhere}\} \right)^* \]

when this family is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, and put $P_\theta [u](f)=-\infty $ when it is empty. For Borel obstacles $g$ and $f$ we write $P_\theta (g,f)=P_\theta (\min \{ g,f\} )$, under the same conditions on the family of competitors.

By the rooftop representation in proposition 16.7.2 below, this is consistent with definition 16.4.9 when $f$ is a potential $\phi $ with $u\preceq \phi $.

Proposition 16.7.2. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and let $f$ be a Borel obstacle for which the family in definition 16.7.1 is nonempty and uniformly bounded above.

  1. $P_\theta [u](f)\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. The envelope increases with $f$ and with the singularity type of $u$, does not change when $f$ is modified on a pluripolar set, and satisfies $P_\theta [u](f+c)=P_\theta [u](f)+c$ for $c\in \mathbb R$.

  2. For all sufficiently large $k$, the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta (u+k,f)$ are potentials, and

    \[ P_\theta [u](f)=\left(\lim _{k\to \infty }P_\theta (u+k,f)\right)^*. \]

  3. If $|f|\leq C$ quasi-everywhere, then

    \[ P_\theta [u]-C\leq P_\theta [u](f)\leq P_\theta [u]+C, \]

    and $P_\theta [u](f)$ has the same mass as $u$.

In particular, $P_\theta [u](0)=P_\theta [u]$ and $P_\theta [V_\theta ](f)=P_\theta (f)$ whenever these envelopes are defined.

Proof. We begin with the final identities. A competitor for $P_\theta [u](0)$ is a nonpositive potential more singular than $u$, exactly as in definition 16.3.1; and every potential is more singular than $V_\theta $, by minimality, so the families defining $P_\theta [V_\theta ](f)$ and $P_\theta (f)$ coincide.

(1) The maximum of two members of the family in definition 16.7.1 is again a member. By theorem 8.3.2, applied on finitely many coordinate balls on which $\theta $ has smooth local potentials, there are members $v_1,v_2,\ldots $ with $(\sup _ kv_ k)^*=P_\theta [u](f)$, and the maxima $w_ k=\max \{ v_1,\ldots ,v_ k\} $ form an increasing sequence of members with the same regularized supremum. By corollary 10.2.2, $\sup _ kw_ k=P_\theta [u](f)$ outside a pluripolar set, and each $w_ k$ lies below $f$ outside a pluripolar set; since countable unions of pluripolar sets are pluripolar (theorem 10.1.4), $P_\theta [u](f)\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. (The envelope itself need not be more singular than $u$; see example 16.4.8.) The other assertions follow from the corresponding properties of the families of competitors.

(2) A competitor $h$ for the obstacle $\min \{ u+k,f\} $ satisfies $h\leq u+k$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, and $h\leq f$ quasi-everywhere; so it belongs to the family of definition 16.7.1. In particular these competitors are uniformly bounded above, $P_\theta (u+k,f)$ is defined, and when it is a potential, it belongs to the family. Conversely, every member $v$ of the family satisfies $v\leq u+k$ for all sufficiently large integers $k$, hence $v\leq \min \{ u+k,f\} $ quasi-everywhere and $v\leq P_\theta (u+k,f)$. Thus a fixed member shows that the rooftop envelopes, which increase with $k$, are potentials for all large $k$, and the family and the rooftop envelopes have the same supremum. This proves the representation.

(3) Normalize $u\leq 0$. If $|f|\leq C$ quasi-everywhere, then $u-C$ is a competitor, and for large $k$ each rooftop envelope $P_\theta (u+k,f)$ lies between $u-C$ and $u+k$, so it has the singularity type of $u$ and mass $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle $. By (2) and corollary 15.7.6, $P_\theta [u](f)$ also has this mass. Comparing $f$ with the constants $-C$ and $C$, and using (1) together with $P_\theta [u](0)=P_\theta [u]$, gives the two bounds. $\square$

Proposition 16.7.3. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and let $f$ be a bounded quasi-continuous obstacle. Then $w=P_\theta [u](f)$ is a potential, and

\[ \mu _ w\leq \mathbf1_{\{ w=P_\theta (f)\} }\mu _{P_\theta (f)}, \qquad \mu _ w(X\setminus \{ w=f\} )=0. \]

In particular $\int _ X(f-w)\, \mu _ w=0$, where the integrand may be given any value at the poles of $w$.

Proof. Write $p=P_\theta (f)$ and choose $C$ with $|f|\leq C$. Then $V_\theta -C$ is a competitor for $f$, and every competitor $v$ satisfies $v-C\leq 0$, hence $v\leq V_\theta +C$; thus $V_\theta -C\leq p\leq V_\theta +C$. Consequently $p$ has minimal singularities, and its mass is that of $V_\theta $, namely $\operatorname {vol}(\{ \theta \} )>0$, by definition 15.8.1, proposition 15.8.2. A potential lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere if and only if it lies below $p$: one direction is the maximality of $p$, the other holds because $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Hence the two constrained families coincide, and $w=P_\theta [u](p)$. Since $u\preceq p$, this is the relative envelope of definition 16.4.9, by proposition 16.7.2. The measure bound in proposition 16.4.10, which applies because $p$ has positive mass, gives the first inequality. By theorem 16.2.5, $\mu _ p$ is carried by $\{ p=f\} $, so $\mu _ w$ is carried by $\{ w=p\} \cap \{ p=f\} \subset \{ w=f\} $. Finally, $f-w$ is nonnegative quasi-everywhere and vanishes $\mu _ w$-almost everywhere, which gives the integral identity. $\square$

Proposition 16.7.4. Let $u,v,\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, let $p$ be an integer with $1\leq p\leq n$, and let $T_1,\ldots ,T_{n-p}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. For $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ put

\[ \lambda _ q=\left\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ p \wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-p}\right\rangle . \]

If $u,v\preceq \phi $, then

\[ \lambda _ v(\{ u<v\} )\leq \lambda _ u(\{ u<v\} ) +\lambda _\phi (X)-\lambda _ u(X). \]

If moreover $\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle >0$ and $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $\lambda _ u(X)=\lambda _\phi (X)$, and the inequality also holds with $\{ u\leq v\} $ in place of $\{ u<v\} $.

Proof. For $\varepsilon >0$ put $q=\max \{ u,v-\varepsilon \} $. Write $T_ i=\theta _ i'+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$ with smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta _ i'$ and $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i')$ (theorem 14.1.7). Since $q\preceq \phi $, the monotonicity of mixed masses in corollary 15.7.5, applied with $n$ factors to the potentials $q,\ldots ,q,\varphi _1,\ldots ,\varphi _{n-p}$ and $\phi ,\ldots ,\phi ,\varphi _1,\ldots ,\varphi _{n-p}$, gives $\lambda _ q(X)\leq \lambda _\phi (X)$. On the disjoint plurifine open sets $\{ u<v-\varepsilon \} $ and $\{ u>v-\varepsilon \} $, $q$ equals $v-\varepsilon $ and $u$ respectively, so plurifine locality gives

\[ \lambda _ v(\{ u<v-\varepsilon \} ) \leq \lambda _\phi (X)-\lambda _ u(\{ u>v-\varepsilon \} ). \]

Writing $\lambda _ u(\{ u>v-\varepsilon \} )=\lambda _ u(X)-\lambda _ u(\{ u\leq v-\varepsilon \} )$, enlarging $\{ u\leq v-\varepsilon \} $ to $\{ u<v\} $ up to the common pole set of $u$ and $v$, which carries no mass, and letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, we obtain the first inequality.

Now let $\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle >0$ and $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. By proposition 16.4.10, $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [v]=P_\theta [\phi ]$. With the potentials $\varphi _ i$ of the currents $T_ i$ chosen above, the preservation of mixed masses in proposition 16.3.2, applied to the $n$ potentials $u,\ldots ,u,\varphi _1,\ldots ,\varphi _{n-p}$ and to the corresponding tuples for $v$ and $\phi $, gives $\lambda _ u(X)=\lambda _ v(X)=\lambda _\phi (X)$. Thus the last two terms in the inequality cancel. Applying the inequality with $u$ and $v$ interchanged, we get $\lambda _ u(\{ v<u\} )\leq \lambda _ v(\{ v<u\} )$, and subtracting this from $\lambda _ v(X)=\lambda _ u(X)$ gives $\lambda _ v(\{ u\leq v\} )\leq \lambda _ u(\{ u\leq v\} )$. $\square$

Definition 16.7.5. For a reference potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and a Borel set $A\subset X$, the Monge–Ampère capacity relative to $\phi $ is

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(A) =\sup \{ \mu _ v(A):v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\ \phi -1\leq v\leq \phi \} . \]

The potentials $v$ in this supremum are called competitors for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$. For $\phi =V_\theta $ we obtain the $\theta $-capacity.

Proposition 16.7.6. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The relative capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ is monotone, countably subadditive, zero on pluripolar Borel sets and continuous along increasing sequences of Borel sets. Its total value is $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(X)=\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle $, and it is inner regular on Borel sets:

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(A) =\sup _{K\subset A\text{ compact}}\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(K). \]

It does not change when a constant is added to $\phi $. If $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies $|\phi -\psi |\leq C$ outside the pole sets of $\phi $ and $\psi $, for a constant $C\geq 0$, then

\[ (2C+1)^{-n}\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(A) \leq \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(A) \leq (2C+1)^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(A). \]

Replacing $(\theta ,\phi )$ by $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g,\phi -g)$ for a smooth real function $g$ leaves the capacity unchanged.

Proof. Every competitor has the singularity type of $\phi $ and hence mass $\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle $; since $\phi $ itself is a competitor, the capacity is finite and $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(X)=\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle $. Monotonicity, countable subadditivity and continuity along increasing sequences hold for each measure $\mu _ v$ and pass to the supremum; for the last property, interchange the supremum over the sequence with the supremum over competitors. Inner regularity follows in the same way from the inner regularity of each finite measure $\mu _ v$ (lemma 16.2.1): $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(A)=\sup _ v\sup _ K\mu _ v(K)=\sup _ K\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(K)$, the inner suprema being taken over the compact sets $K\subset A$. Each $\mu _ v$ gives no mass to pluripolar sets. Adding a constant $c$ to $\phi $ corresponds to the bijection $v\mapsto v+c$ of competitors, which does not change $\mu _ v$.

For the comparison, note first that $\phi \leq \psi +C$ and $\psi \leq \phi +C$ hold almost everywhere, hence everywhere; in particular $\phi $ and $\psi $ have the same pole set. Put $t=(2C+1)^{-1}$. If $\phi -1\leq v\leq \phi $, the potential $q=(1-t)\psi +tv-tC$ satisfies

\[ q\leq (1-t)\psi +t(\psi +C)-tC=\psi ,\qquad q\geq (1-t)\psi +t(\psi -C-1)-tC=\psi -1, \]

so it is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$, and $\mu _ q\geq t^ n\mu _ v$. This proves the upper comparison, and exchanging $\phi $ and $\psi $ proves the lower one. The last assertion follows from the bijection $v\mapsto v-g$ between the two families of competitors, which preserves the current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$. $\square$

Proposition 16.7.7. Let $u,\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi $, and let $s\in \mathbb R$ and $0<t\leq 1$. Then

\[ t^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(\{ u<\phi -s-t\} ) \leq \mu _ u(\{ u<\phi -s\} )+\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle -\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle . \]

In particular, if $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(\{ u<\phi -s\} )\to 0$ as $s\to \infty $.

Proof. Let $v$ be a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$, and put $q=(1-t)\phi +tv-s$. Then $\phi -s-t\leq q\leq \phi -s$, so

\[ \{ u<\phi -s-t\} \subset \{ u<q\} \subset \{ u<\phi -s\} . \]

Moreover $q\sim \phi $ and $\mu _ q\geq t^ n\mu _ v$. Proposition 16.7.4, with $p=n$, applied to $u$ and $q$, gives

\[ t^ n\mu _ v(\{ u<\phi -s-t\} )\leq \mu _ q(\{ u<q\} ) \leq \mu _ u(\{ u<\phi -s\} )+\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle -\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle , \]

and taking the supremum over $v$ proves the inequality. If $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle $. With $t=1$, the right side tends to zero as $s\to \infty $ by dominated convergence, because the sets $\{ u<\phi -s\} $ decrease to the pole set of $u$, which is $\mu _ u$-null. $\square$
