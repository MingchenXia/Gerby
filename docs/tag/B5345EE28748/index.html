Comparison and mass invariance

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 2.4 Comparison and mass invariance

The global comparison principle of Kähler geometry compares the Monge–Ampère measures of two potentials on the set where one lies below the other. Adding a constant to a potential leaves its Monge–Ampère measure unchanged, and after a large constant shift this set becomes the whole manifold. Such an inequality would therefore compare total masses, and the varying mass of example 2.3.1 rules it out.

Proposition 2.4.1. Suppose that every pair $u,v\in \mathcal H_\omega $ satisfies

2.4
\begin{equation} \label{eq:naive-comparison} \int _{\{ u<v\} }\omega _ v^ n \leq \int _{\{ u<v\} }\omega _ u^ n. \end{equation}

Then $\mathrm M_\omega $ is constant on $\mathcal H_\omega $.

Proof. Fix $u,v\in \mathcal H_\omega $. By compactness there is a constant $C$ such that $u<v+C$ everywhere. Since $\omega _{v+C}=\omega _ v$, applying (2.4) to $u$ and $v+C$ gives $\mathrm M_\omega (v)\leq \mathrm M_\omega (u)$. Exchanging the roles of $u$ and $v$ gives the opposite inequality. $\square$

Corollary 2.4.2. The inequality (2.4) does not hold for every Hermitian form on a compact complex manifold.

Proof. Let $X$, $\omega $, $u_ t$ and $B=\int _ X\beta _1\wedge \beta _2$ be as in example 2.3.1. For $-\pi ^{-2}<t<0$, set $u=0$ and $v=u_ t+C$ with $C>|t|$. Then $\{ u<v\} =X$, but

\[ \int _ X\omega _ v^2=(4-\pi ^2t)B>4B=\int _ X\omega _ u^2. \]

Thus this pair violates (2.4). $\square$

The modified comparison principle of theorem 10.2.1 compensates for the derivatives of the background. Conversely, under the equivalent conditions of theorem 2.2.2, the ordinary comparison principle holds for bounded potentials, by 11.5.2.
