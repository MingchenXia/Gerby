Proposition 6.2.23FDEBDB1E8A22

Proposition 6.2.23. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ (resp. $(\psi _ i)_{i\in I}$) be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\varphi _ i \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ (resp. $\psi _ i \xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$). Then

\[ \varphi _ i\lor \psi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \lor \psi . \]

Proof. Since $d_ S$ is a pseudometric, we may assume that both nets are actually sequences and $I=\mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. By corollary 6.2.19, we may assume that the masses $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$, $\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n>0$.

By proposition 6.2.4 and proposition 6.1.20, replacing all potentials by their $P$-envelopes changes neither the hypotheses nor the conclusion. Thus we may assume that the $\varphi _ j$'s, the $\psi _ j$'s, $\varphi $ and $\psi $ are all model. After discarding finitely many terms, the masses of the two sequences are bounded below by a positive constant. Applying proposition 6.2.12 to both sequences and using the sandwich criterion corollary 6.2.21, it is enough to treat the case where each of $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ and $(\psi _ j)_ j$ is monotone. In this reduced situation, $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ (resp. $(\psi _ j)_ j$) converges to $\varphi $ (resp. $\psi $) almost everywhere due to proposition 6.2.26, corollary 6.2.9 and proposition 3.1.28.

We handle three cases separately.

Step 1. Assume that both sequences are increasing.

In this case, we have $\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j\nearrow \varphi \lor \psi $ almost everywhere. Therefore, $\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S} \varphi \lor \psi $ by corollary 6.2.9.

Step 2. Assume that one sequence, say $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is increasing while the other is decreasing. Then we have

\[ \varphi _ j \lor \psi \leq \varphi _ j \lor \psi _ j \leq \varphi \lor \psi _ j. \]

Thanks to corollary 6.2.21, it suffices to show that both sides converge to $\varphi \lor \psi $ with respect to $d_ S$. So we reduce to the case where both sequences are decreasing.

Step 3. Assume that both sequences are decreasing.

In this case, due to corollary 6.2.11, it suffices to show that

6.68
\begin{equation} \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n. \end{equation}

The $\geq $ direction follows from theorem 2.4.8; it remains to argue the $\leq $ direction.

Thanks to 2.4.15, we may find a sequence $(\epsilon _ j)_ j$ in $(0,1)$ with limit $0$ and a sequence $(\eta _ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ such that

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)\varphi _ j+\epsilon _ j \eta _ j \leq \varphi ,\quad \eta _ j \leq \varphi _ j. \]

It follows that for each $j\geq 1$, we have

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)(\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j)+\epsilon _ j \eta _ j \leq \varphi \lor \psi _ j. \]

Therefore by theorem 2.4.8,

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)^ n \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j}^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi _ j}^ n. \]

Letting $j\to \infty $, we find that

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j}^ n\leq \lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi _ j}^ n. \]

Therefore, in order to prove (6.68), we may assume that one of the sequences is constant; let us say $\psi _ j=\psi $ for all $j$. Repeating the same argument as before and constructing $(\epsilon _ j)_ j$, $(\eta _ j)_ j$ as above, we get

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)^ n \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi }^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n. \]

Letting $j\to \infty $, we conclude (6.68). $\square$
