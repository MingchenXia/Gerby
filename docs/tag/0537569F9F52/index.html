From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 26.6.2

Theorem 26.6.2. For every $F\in C(X,\mathbb R)$,

26.40
\begin{equation} M(F):=\max _{T\in \mathscr G_\omega }\mathfrak D_ F(T) =\mathrm{e}^{-B_0(F)/n}>0. \end{equation}

The maximum equals the supremum over the smooth strictly positive members of $\mathscr G_\omega $. The set $\mathscr M(F)$ of maximizing currents is nonempty, compact and convex.

If $F$ is smooth, there is exactly one maximizing current, namely the normalized $(n-1)$st power of the Gauduchon metric conformal to the smooth solution form $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ of $\mathrm{MA}_\omega (u)=\mathrm{e}^{B_0(F)+F}\mathrm{d}V$.

Proof. Upper semicontinuity of $\mathfrak D_ F$ on the compact set $\mathscr G_\omega $ gives attainment, and testing a smooth positive member gives a positive value. Concavity makes the set of maximizers convex, and upper semicontinuity makes it closed.

Suppose first that $F$ is smooth, and let $\Omega =\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$ be the smooth solution form with $\Omega ^ n=\mathrm{e}^{B_0(F)+F}\mathrm{d}V$. The form $\beta =\mathrm{e}^{-B_0(F)/n}\Omega $ is an admissible test in the preceding lemma, so Stokes' theorem for currents gives, for every $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $,

\[ \mathfrak D_ F(T)\leq \int _ X\beta \wedge T =\mathrm{e}^{-B_0(F)/n}\int _ X\omega \wedge T=\mathrm{e}^{-B_0(F)/n}. \]

If $\gamma $ is the Gauduchon metric conformal to $\Omega $, equality holds in the pointwise matrix inequality for $T$ proportional to $\gamma ^{n-1}$; normalizing $T$ so that $\int _ X\omega \wedge T=1$, we obtain the formula and its attainment by a smooth test.

For uniqueness, let $T$ be a maximizer. Equality in the integrated inequalities forces equality in the matrix arithmetic–geometric mean inequality $\sigma _ T$-almost everywhere. Since the matrix of $\beta $ is positive definite and $\operatorname {tr}P_ T=n$, this means that the coefficient measures of $T$ are a nonnegative scalar measure times the smooth coefficients of $\gamma ^{n-1}$. Thus $T=a\gamma ^{n-1}$, where $a$ is a positive measure, regarded as a distribution. The equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a\gamma ^{n-1})=0$ is the adjoint of a scalar elliptic equation, so elliptic regularity makes $a$ smooth. Since $\gamma $ is Gauduchon, proposition 4.8.1 identifies the kernel of this adjoint operator with the constants: the trace operator $v\mapsto n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}/\gamma ^ n$ has the constants as kernel and index zero, and integration against $\gamma ^ n$ annihilates its range. Thus $a$ is constant, and the normalization fixes the constant. This proves uniqueness.

For continuous $F,G$, the pointwise definition gives

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\| F-G\| _\infty /n}\mathfrak D_ G(T) \leq \mathfrak D_ F(T) \leq \mathrm{e}^{\| F-G\| _\infty /n}\mathfrak D_ G(T). \]

The same bounds hold for the maxima, and for the suprema over smooth positive members. We approximate $F$ uniformly by smooth functions. Since $B_0$ is $1$-Lipschitz for the uniform norm by proposition 15.1.1, the formula just proved for smooth data gives (26.40) in the limit, including the equality with the smooth supremum. Note that only $F$ is approximated here: some normalized Gauduchon currents are not limits of smooth positive ones, as example 28.8.2 shows. $\square$
