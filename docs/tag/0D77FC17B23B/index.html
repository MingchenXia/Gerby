1.13 Analytic types and restriction0D77FC17B23B

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.13 Analytic types and restriction

The simplest singularities of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are logarithmic poles along the common zeros of finitely many holomorphic functions. The logarithmic growth of a general singularity at a point is measured by its Lelong number. Let $u$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on a complex manifold and let $x$ be a point. Choose holomorphic coordinates centered at $x$ and a smooth function $q$ such that $p=u+q$ is plurisubharmonic near $x$, and write $B(x,r)$ for the Euclidean coordinate ball of radius $r$. The Lelong number of $u$ at $x$ is

\[ \nu (u,x)=\lim _{r\downarrow 0} \frac{\sup _{B(x,r)}p}{\log r^2}. \]

By the local growth theorem recalled in [SGPT], the limit exists and does not depend on the coordinates or on the correction $q$. With this normalization, $\log |z|^2$ has Lelong number one at the origin. For an irreducible analytic subset $Z$, the generic Lelong number of $u$ along $Z$ is

\[ \nu (u,Z)=\inf _{x\in Z}\nu (u,x). \]

By Siu's theorem, recalled in 1.13.1, $\nu (u,x)=\nu (u,Z)$ for all $x\in Z$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$.

For analytic singularities we follow the convention of [SGPT]: we say that a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ has analytic singularities if near every point there are holomorphic functions $f_1,\ldots ,f_ N$, not all identically zero, and a rational number $c>0$ such that

\[ u=c\log \bigl (|f_1|^2+\cdots +|f_ N|^2\bigr )+O(1), \qquad c\in \mathbb Q_{>0}. \]

The remainder is only required to be bounded; if it can be chosen smooth, we say that $u$ has analytic singularities with smooth remainder. Given a coherent ideal sheaf $\mathfrak a$ and a common coefficient $c$, we say that $u$ has analytic type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ if this holds with local generators $f_1,\ldots ,f_ N$ of $\mathfrak a$. The choice of generators does not matter: two finite systems of generators of the same ideal change the logarithm only by a bounded amount on smaller relatively compact open sets.

Proposition 1.13.1. Analytic singularities are preserved by addition, finite maxima, and multiplication by positive rational numbers. Let $u$ have analytic type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, and let $Y\subset X$ be a connected complex submanifold on which $\mathfrak a$ does not vanish identically. Then the restriction $u|_ Y$ is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$, in particular not identically $-\infty $, and it has analytic type

\[ (c,\mathfrak a\mathcal O_ Y). \]

Here $\mathfrak a\mathcal O_ Y$ is the image ideal, generated by the restrictions to $Y$ of local sections of $\mathfrak a$.

Proof. For multiplication by a positive rational number, multiply the coefficient. Consider two presentations with coefficients $c_1,c_2$ and ideals $\mathfrak a_1,\mathfrak a_2$, and choose a positive rational number $d$ with $c_1=k d$ and $c_2=\ell d$, where $k,\ell $ are positive integers. Raising a sum of squared moduli of generators of $\mathfrak a_1$ to the power $k$ gives, up to bounded factors, the corresponding sum for $\mathfrak a_1^ k$; similarly for $\mathfrak a_2^\ell $. Products of generators of $\mathfrak a_1^ k$ and $\mathfrak a_2^\ell $ therefore present the sum with coefficient $d$, since a product of sums of squares is a sum of squares of pairwise products.

For the maximum, apply the elementary inequalities

\[ \max \{ A,B\} \leq A+B\leq 2\max \{ A,B\} ,\qquad A,B\geq 0, \]

to the two sums of squared moduli of generators of $\mathfrak a_1^ k$ and $\mathfrak a_2^\ell $. Taking logarithms shows that the maximum differs from $d$ times the logarithm of their total by a bounded amount. These are local assertions; on the compact manifold, finitely many charts give the corresponding comparisons of ordinary singularity types.

For the restriction, restrict to $Y$ the two-sided bounds that define the remainder. Since $Y$ is connected and is not contained in the zero set of $\mathfrak a$, the common zero set of the restricted generators is a proper analytic subset of $Y$, so $u|_ Y$ is finite on a dense open subset of $Y$. After a smooth local correction, the restriction theorem for plurisubharmonic functions shows that $u|_ Y$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, and the restricted bounds give the asserted presentation. $\square$

For an arbitrary quasi-plurisubharmonic function, a vanishing generic Lelong number along a submanifold does not prevent the restriction from being identically $-\infty $. For example, on a small polydisk in $\mathbb C^ n$ with coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots ,z_ n)$ and $|z_1|<\mathrm{e}^{-1}$, the function

\[ u(z)=-\sqrt{-\log |z_1|^2} \]

is plurisubharmonic, because $t\mapsto -\sqrt{-t}$ is increasing and convex for $t<0$. Its Lelong number vanishes at every point of $\{ z_1=0\} $, but its restriction to this hyperplane is identically $-\infty $. For the analytic approximants constructed below, the analytic hypothesis in proposition 1.13.1 excludes this behavior.
