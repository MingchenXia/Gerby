From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 17.5 Singularity envelopes at arbitrary mass

Throughout this section $\theta $ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ with big class, and we put $V=V_\theta $ and $\Omega =\int _ X\omega ^ n$; volumes of Borel sets are taken with respect to $\omega ^ n$. No model potential is fixed in this section.

For a potential $u$ of positive mass, theorem 16.4.7 describes $P_\theta [u]$ as the largest potential $v\leq 0$ with $u\preceq v$ and $\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle $, and it shows that $P_\theta [P_\theta [u]]=P_\theta [u]$. Its proof relies on the domination principle, which requires positive mass. For arbitrary $u$ we replace the mass by the collection of all mixed masses of $u$ against $V$; this leads to the singularity ceiling of definition 17.5.2. We prove that the ceiling equals $P_\theta [u]$ and that $P_\theta [\cdot ]$ is idempotent, without any assumption on the mass of $u$.

The main step, proposition 17.5.1, perturbs $u$ to $w_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )u+\varepsilon V$, which has positive mass $m_\varepsilon $, and shows that $P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]$ decreases to $P_\theta [u]$. Since $m_\varepsilon $ tends to zero when $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =0$, the estimates must not deteriorate as the mass degenerates. We therefore use a tail bound normalized by $m_\varepsilon $, which is uniform in $\varepsilon $, and convert it into a volume bound by means of constant-density solutions of Monge–Ampère equations.

Proposition 17.5.1. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\leq 0$. For $0<\varepsilon <1$ put

\[ w_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )u+\varepsilon V, \qquad \phi _\varepsilon =P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]. \]

Then $\phi _\varepsilon $ decreases to $P_\theta [u]$, pointwise and in $L^1(X)$, as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$.

Proof. The envelopes $\phi _\varepsilon $ decrease to a $\theta $-psh limit $q\geq P_\theta [u]$; the point is the reverse inequality, which we obtain by showing that $\{ q>P_\theta [u]+\delta \} $ has arbitrarily small volume for every $\delta >0$.

Step 1: the limit exists. Put $\phi =P_\theta [u]$. Since $u\leq 0$, we have $u\leq V$, so $w_\varepsilon =u+\varepsilon (V-u)$ decreases to $u$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ (at the poles of $u$ all these functions equal $-\infty $). In particular $u\leq w_\varepsilon \leq 0$. By the monotonicity of $P_\theta [\cdot ]$ in proposition 16.3.2(1) and the bound $P_\theta [\cdot ]\leq V$ of definition 16.3.1, we get $\phi \leq \phi _\varepsilon \leq V$, and $\phi _\varepsilon $ decreases as $\varepsilon $ decreases. Hence $q=\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}\phi _\varepsilon $ exists pointwise; as a decreasing limit of $\theta $-psh potentials bounded below by $\phi $, it is $\theta $-psh (corollary 14.2.5), and $\phi \leq q\leq V$. Since $\phi $ and $V$ are integrable, dominated convergence gives $\phi _\varepsilon \to q$ in $L^1(X)$. It remains to prove that $q\leq \phi $.

Step 2: masses and a normalized tail bound. For $0\leq k\leq n$ consider the finite non-pluripolar measures

\[ \nu _ k=\langle \theta _ u^ k\wedge \theta _ V^{n-k}\rangle , \qquad a_ k=\nu _ k(X). \]

Since $\theta _{w_\varepsilon }=(1-\varepsilon )\theta _ u+\varepsilon \theta _ V$, multilinearity (proposition 15.1.5) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \mu _{w_\varepsilon } & =\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(1-\varepsilon )^ k\varepsilon ^{n-k}\nu _ k, \\ m_\varepsilon :=\int _ X\langle \theta _{w_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle & =\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(1-\varepsilon )^ k\varepsilon ^{n-k}a_ k. \end{aligned} \]

Here $a_0=\int _ X\langle \theta _ V^ n\rangle >0$ by proposition 15.8.2, so $m_\varepsilon \geq \varepsilon ^ na_0>0$. By the mass preservation in proposition 16.3.2(3) and by theorem 16.4.7, $\phi _\varepsilon $ is a model potential of mass $m_\varepsilon $.

In the rest of the proof, sets defined by inequalities between potentials, such as $\{ u<V-A\} $, are understood pointwise, with values in $[-\infty ,+\infty )$. The pole sets of all potentials involved are pluripolar, hence null for all the non-pluripolar measures below and for $\omega ^ n$. For $A>0$ put

\[ r(A)=\max _{k:\, a_ k>0}\frac{\nu _ k(\{ u<V-A\} )}{a_ k}. \]

The maximum is over a finite set which contains $k=0$. As $A\to \infty $, the sets $\{ u<V-A\} $ decrease to a subset of the pole set of $u$, which is $\nu _ k$-null; since the measures $\nu _ k$ are finite, continuity from above gives $r(A)\downarrow 0$. Moreover $w_\varepsilon -V=(1-\varepsilon )(u-V)$, so $\{ w_\varepsilon <V-A\} \subset \{ u<V-A\} $. Using the expansion of $\mu _{w_\varepsilon }$ and the bound $\nu _ k(\{ u<V-A\} )\leq r(A)a_ k$, valid for every $k$ (when $a_ k=0$ both sides vanish), we obtain the estimate

17.46
\begin{equation} \frac{\mu _{w_\varepsilon }(\{ w_\varepsilon <V-A\} )}{m_\varepsilon } \leq r(A),\qquad 0<\varepsilon <1, \end{equation}

which is uniform in $\varepsilon $.

Step 3: rooftops. For fixed $A>0$ put

\[ p_{\varepsilon ,A}=P_\theta (w_\varepsilon +A,V), \qquad p_ A=P_\theta (u+A,V). \]

The potential $w_\varepsilon $ is a common minorant of $w_\varepsilon +A$ and $V$, so $w_\varepsilon \leq p_{\varepsilon ,A}$. On the other hand $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\leq V\leq 0$ and $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\leq w_\varepsilon +A$, so $p_{\varepsilon ,A}$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]$. Hence $w_\varepsilon \leq p_{\varepsilon ,A}\leq \phi _\varepsilon $, and since $w_\varepsilon $ and $\phi _\varepsilon $ both have mass $m_\varepsilon $, the monotonicity of mass (theorem 15.7.4) shows that $p_{\varepsilon ,A}$ has mass $m_\varepsilon $ as well; thus $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$. As $w_\varepsilon +A$ decreases to $u+A$, proposition 16.1.6 gives $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\downarrow p_ A$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Likewise $u\leq p_ A\leq V\leq 0$ and $p_ A\leq u+A$, so $p_ A$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [u]$, and $p_ A\leq \phi $.

Step 4: auxiliary solutions. Apply theorem 17.4.5 with the model potential $\phi _\varepsilon $ of mass $m_\varepsilon $ in place of $\phi $, the volume form $\omega ^ n$ and the constant density $m_\varepsilon /\Omega $. It gives $\chi _\varepsilon \in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$ with

\[ \mu _{\chi _\varepsilon }=\frac{m_\varepsilon }{\Omega }\, \omega ^ n, \qquad \sup _ X\chi _\varepsilon =0. \]

By lemma 16.4.2, $\chi _\varepsilon \leq \phi _\varepsilon $. By proposition 14.2.1, normalized $\theta $-psh potentials are uniformly bounded in $L^1$, so there is a constant $C_1$, independent of $\varepsilon $, with $\int _ X(-\chi _\varepsilon )\, \omega ^ n\leq C_1$.

Step 5: a volume bound uniform in $\varepsilon $. Fix $\delta >0$ and $B>1$, put $\tau =\min \{ 1/2,\delta /(4B)\} $, and define

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{\varepsilon ,B}& =\{ \chi _\varepsilon >\phi _\varepsilon -B\} ,\\ \psi _\varepsilon & =(1-\tau )\phi _\varepsilon +\tau \chi _\varepsilon -\delta /2,\\ E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }& =\{ \phi _\varepsilon >p_{\varepsilon ,A}+\delta \} . \end{aligned} \]

Since $\phi _\varepsilon \leq 0$, the complement of $G_{\varepsilon ,B}$ lies in $\{ \chi _\varepsilon \leq -B\} $, whose volume is at most $C_1/B$ by Markov's inequality. Moreover,

\[ \chi _\varepsilon -\delta /2\leq \psi _\varepsilon \leq \phi _\varepsilon -\delta /2, \]

and since $\chi _\varepsilon $ and $\phi _\varepsilon $ have the same mass, the monotonicity of mass places $\psi _\varepsilon $ in $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$. On $G_{\varepsilon ,B}$ we have $\psi _\varepsilon -\phi _\varepsilon =\tau (\chi _\varepsilon -\phi _\varepsilon )-\delta /2>-\tau B-\delta /2\geq -3\delta /4$, whereas $p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\phi _\varepsilon -\delta $ on $E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }$. Hence

\[ E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }\cap G_{\varepsilon ,B}\subset \{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} . \]

By multilinearity, $\mu _{\psi _\varepsilon }\geq \tau ^ n\mu _{\chi _\varepsilon }=\tau ^ n(m_\varepsilon /\Omega )\omega ^ n$. As $p_{\varepsilon ,A}$ and $\psi _\varepsilon $ both belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$, the comparison principle of proposition 16.7.4 applies to them, and we obtain

\[ \tau ^ n\frac{m_\varepsilon }{\Omega } \int _{E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }\cap G_{\varepsilon ,B}}\omega ^ n \leq \int _{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} }\mu _{\psi _\varepsilon } \leq \int _{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} }\mu _{p_{\varepsilon ,A}}. \]

To estimate the right side, we use the contact inequality for rooftops, corollary 16.2.6:

\[ \mu _{p_{\varepsilon ,A}} \leq \mathbf1_{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}=w_\varepsilon +A\} }\mu _{w_\varepsilon } +\mathbf1_{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}=V\} }\mu _ V. \]

The second contact set does not meet $\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} $: at a common point we would have $V<\psi _\varepsilon \leq \phi _\varepsilon -\delta /2\leq V-\delta /2$. At a point of $\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} $ in the first contact set, $w_\varepsilon +A<\psi _\varepsilon \leq V-\delta /2$, hence $w_\varepsilon <V-A$. Therefore, by (17.46),

\[ \int _{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} }\mu _{p_{\varepsilon ,A}} \leq \mu _{w_\varepsilon }(\{ w_\varepsilon <V-A\} )\leq m_\varepsilon r(A). \]

Dividing by the positive number $\tau ^ nm_\varepsilon /\Omega $ and adding the volume of the complement of $G_{\varepsilon ,B}$, we arrive at

17.50
\begin{equation} \int _{E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }}\omega ^ n \leq \frac{C_1}{B}+\Omega \tau ^{-n}r(A). \end{equation}

Step 6: passage to the limit. Take a sequence $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$. If $q(x)>p_ A(x)+\delta $, then $\phi _{\varepsilon _ j}(x)\geq q(x)$ and $p_{\varepsilon _ j,A}(x)\downarrow p_ A(x)$, so $x\in E_{\varepsilon _ j,A,\delta }$ for all large $j$. Thus

\[ \{ q>p_ A+\delta \} \subset \varliminf _ j E_{\varepsilon _ j,A,\delta }, \]

and Fatou's lemma together with (17.50) shows that the volume of $\{ q>p_ A+\delta \} $ is at most $C_1/B+\Omega \tau ^{-n}r(A)$. Since $p_ A\leq \phi $, the same bound holds for the set $\{ q>\phi +\delta \} $, which depends neither on $A$ nor on $B$, whereas $\tau $ depends on $B$ and $\delta $ but not on $A$. Letting first $A\to \infty $, so that $r(A)\to 0$, and then $B\to \infty $, we see that $\{ q>\phi +\delta \} $ has zero volume. As $\delta >0$ was arbitrary, $q\leq \phi $ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere, because both are $\theta $-psh (see the beginning of section 16.1). Together with $\phi \leq q$, this gives $q=\phi $. $\square$

Definition 17.5.2. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ let $\mathcal F_ u$ be the set of potentials $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $v\leq 0$, $u\preceq v$ and

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ k\wedge \theta _ V^{n-k}\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ k\wedge \theta _ V^{n-k}\rangle \qquad \text{for }0\leq k\leq n. \]

The singularity ceiling of $u$ is the function $(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*$.

The family $\mathcal F_ u$ contains $u-\sup _ Xu$ and is bounded above by zero, so the singularity ceiling $(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*$ is a $\theta $-psh potential. Both the singularity ceiling of $u$ and $P_\theta [u]$ are unchanged when a constant $c$ is added to $u$. Indeed, $\theta _{u+c}=\theta _ u$, and a potential $v$ satisfies $u+c\preceq v$ if and only if $u\preceq v$; hence $\mathcal F_{u+c}=\mathcal F_ u$, and $P_\theta [u+c]$ and $P_\theta [u]$ have the same competitors.

Theorem 17.5.3. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ with big class, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then the singularity ceiling of $u$ is its singularity envelope, that is,

\[ \Big(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v\Big)^*=P_\theta [u], \]

and $P_\theta [P_\theta [u]]=P_\theta [u]$. In particular, both identities hold when $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =0$.

Proof. By the remark following definition 17.5.2, both identities are unchanged when a constant is added to $u$, so we may assume $u\leq 0$. Put $\phi =P_\theta [u]$.

We first show that $\phi $ lies below the singularity ceiling of $u$. By definition 16.3.1, $\phi \leq 0$, and $u\leq \phi $ because $u$ is itself a competitor. Applying the mixed mass preservation of proposition 16.3.2(3) to the tuple consisting of $k$ copies of $u$ and $n-k$ copies of $V$, and using $P_\theta [V]=V$ (proposition 16.3.2(4)), we see that $\phi $ has the same mixed masses against $V$ as $u$. Hence $\phi \in \mathcal F_ u$, and therefore $\phi \leq (\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*$.

Conversely, let $v\in \mathcal F_ u$, and for $0<\varepsilon <1$ put

\[ w_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )u+\varepsilon V, \qquad z_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )v+\varepsilon V. \]

Expanding by multilinearity and using the mixed mass identities that define $\mathcal F_ u$, we get

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _{w_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _{z_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle \geq \varepsilon ^ n\int _ X\langle \theta _ V^ n\rangle >0. \]

Moreover $w_\varepsilon \preceq z_\varepsilon $, since $u\preceq v$. Let $\zeta _\varepsilon =P_\theta [z_\varepsilon ]$. By theorem 16.4.7, $\zeta _\varepsilon $ is a model potential with the same mass as $z_\varepsilon $, hence as $w_\varepsilon $. As $w_\varepsilon \preceq z_\varepsilon \leq \zeta _\varepsilon $, the potential $w_\varepsilon $ belongs to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\zeta _\varepsilon )$, and proposition 16.4.10 gives

\[ P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]=P_\theta [\zeta _\varepsilon ]=\zeta _\varepsilon =P_\theta [z_\varepsilon ]. \]

Now let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Proposition 17.5.1, applied to $u$ and to $v$, gives $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [v]$. Since $v\leq 0$, the potential $v$ is itself a competitor for $P_\theta [v]$, so $v\leq P_\theta [v]=\phi $. Taking the supremum over $v\in \mathcal F_ u$ and its upper semicontinuous regularization, we obtain $(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*\leq \phi $.

For idempotence, let $\phi _\varepsilon =P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]$ be as in proposition 17.5.1. Since $\phi \leq \phi _\varepsilon $, the monotonicity of $P_\theta [\cdot ]$ and theorem 16.4.7, which applies to $w_\varepsilon $ because $\int _ X\langle \theta _{w_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle >0$, give

\[ P_\theta [\phi ]\leq P_\theta [\phi _\varepsilon ] =\phi _\varepsilon . \]

Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and using the proposition, we obtain $P_\theta [\phi ]\leq \phi $. Conversely, $\phi \leq 0$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [\phi ]$, so $\phi \leq P_\theta [\phi ]$. This proves idempotence. $\square$
