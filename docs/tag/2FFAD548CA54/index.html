11.45.14 Weak adjoints on a product surface2FFAD548CA54

On the product of two projective lines, the averaging probability of (11.45.8) is the full supporting probability. The continuous positive residual defines a weak linearization, and spherical harmonics give invariant-measure uniqueness without a regularity assumption.

In this subsection take $Y=\mathbb P^1_ y$, $\kappa =\omega _2$ and $k=1$. Keep $a,d,b,H,\theta ,\eta ,\psi $ and $\mathrm{d}W=\omega _1\wedge \omega _2$ as above, and write $\phi =\phi _{a,d}$, $A=A_0=c_0H$ and $u=\psi +w_0$. Choose a smooth positive probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V=q\, \mathrm{d}W$ and put $F=\log (2bH/q)$. Then

\[ \Omega =A\omega _1+b\omega _2,\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\Omega +d[D],\qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\Omega ^2=c_0\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V. \]

The model is admissible by proposition 11.45.3, and $u\sim \phi $ gives $B_\phi (F)=\log c_0$. Nonconstant $b$ cannot be harmonic, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta =(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)\wedge \omega _2\ne 0$. Every smooth potential perturbation has the same $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta $ and is therefore non-Gauduchon.

For a smooth function $h$ on the product, define its partial Laplacians by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_ i h=(\Delta _ i h)\omega _ i$. With the mass-one Fubini–Study normalization, $\Delta _ i$ is the Laplacian of the unit round sphere.

Lemma 11.45.15.85744C3971DC Let $V>0$ be continuous on $\mathbb P^1$, and let $T$ be a finite signed measure such that

\[ \int _{\mathbb P^1}(\Delta f-Vf)\, \mathrm{d}T=0 \qquad (f\in C^\infty (\mathbb P^1)). \]

Then $T=0$.

Proof. Let $H_ t$ be the sphere heat operator. On spherical harmonics of degree $\ell $, it acts by $\mathrm{e}^{-t\ell (\ell +1)}$. The harmonic series defines a smooth heat solution for $t>0$; for smooth initial functions, the maximum principle gives positivity and contraction in the uniform norm. Density of smooth functions extends $H_ t$ to a positive, strongly continuous contraction on $C(\mathbb P^1)$. Choose $M\geq \max V$ and define

\[ R_ Mg=\int _0^\infty \mathrm{e}^{-Mt}H_ tg\, \mathrm{d}t. \]

Then $\| R_ M\| \leq M^{-1}$ and $(M-\Delta )R_ Mg=g$ in distributions. For any $g\in C(\mathbb P^1)$, the map

\[ f\longmapsto R_ M((M-V)f-g) \]

contracts by at most $1-\min V/M<1$. Its continuous fixed point satisfies $\Delta f=Vf+g$ in distributions, with a continuous right-hand side. The functions $f_\varepsilon =H_\varepsilon f$ are smooth and satisfy

\[ f_\varepsilon \longrightarrow f,\qquad \Delta f_\varepsilon =H_\varepsilon (Vf+g)\longrightarrow Vf+g \]

uniformly. Thus $(\Delta -V)f_\varepsilon \to g$ uniformly. Testing against the finite measure $T$ gives $\int g\, \mathrm{d}T=0$ for every continuous $g$, hence $T=0$. $\square$

Theorem 11.45.16.23BDF62163E8 The weight and current

\[ \rho =\frac1{2ab},\qquad Q=\rho \Omega =\frac{A}{2ab}\omega _1+\frac1{2a}\omega _2 \]

satisfy $\mathrm{d}Q=0$ and $\int _ X\rho \Omega ^2=1$. For smooth real $h$, the measure-valued and logarithmic linearizations at $u$ are

\[ D_ u\mathrm{MA}_\theta (h)=2\Omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h,\qquad L_ uh=\frac{2\Omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h}{\Omega ^2} =\frac{\Delta _1h}{A}+\frac{\Delta _2h}{b}. \]

The only Borel probability satisfying $\int _ XL_ uh\, \mathrm{d}\mu =0$ for every smooth real $h$ is

6EEDC24A3093 11.45.17
\begin{equation} \nu =\rho \Omega ^2=\frac Aa\, \mathrm{d}W,\qquad \mathcal A_\phi (F)=\{ \nu \} . \end{equation}

If $r\geq 0$ is measurable, $\int _ Xr\Omega ^2=1$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(r\Omega )=0$, then $r=\rho $ almost everywhere.

Proof. We first define the weighted current and variation, then prove invariant-measure uniqueness and apply the supporting inequality on the full space $C(X)$.

The current and operator. Since $A/b$ depends only on the first curve, $\mathrm{d}((A/b)\omega _1)=0$ distributionally; this follows also by uniform smooth approximation of its coefficient. Thus $Q$ is closed, and $\int \rho \Omega ^2=\int A\omega _1/a=1$. The coefficient $\rho $ is smooth and $\Omega $ is continuous, so the product defining $Q$ is well defined. Uniform positivity of $\Omega $ gives $\Omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h>0$ for sufficiently small signed $t$. The bounded-product calculation used in theorem 11.45.9, now with the smooth perturbation $th$, gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u+th) =\Omega ^2+2t\Omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h+t^2(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^2. \]

This proves the two linearizations; mixed components of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ have zero wedge with the diagonal $\Omega $. Distributional Stokes gives $\int L_ uh\, \mathrm{d}\nu =2\int \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge Q=0$.

Invariant probabilities. Choose real spherical harmonics $e_ j$ on the second sphere, with $e_0=1$ and $\Delta _2e_ j=-\lambda _ je_ j$. Only the constant mode has eigenvalue zero. For an invariant probability $\mu $, define finite signed measures $T_ j$ on the first sphere by

\[ \int f\, \mathrm{d}T_ j =\int _ X\frac{f(x)e_ j(y)}{A(x)}\, \mathrm{d}\mu . \]

Testing $L_ u$ on $f(x)e_ j(y)$ gives

\[ \int \left(\Delta _1f-\lambda _ j\frac Ab f\right)\, \mathrm{d}T_ j=0. \]

For $j=0$, testing on spherical harmonics of the first sphere shows that $T_0=C\omega _1$. Their finite span is uniformly dense, because restrictions of polynomials decompose into harmonics and polynomials are dense on the sphere. The first marginal of $\mu $ is therefore $CA\omega _1$, and probability normalization gives $C=1/a$. For every nonconstant mode, lemma 11.45.15 with $V=\lambda _ jA/b>0$ gives $T_ j=0$. As these are identities of measures, their test functions may then be multiplied by the continuous function $A$. They determine all spherical harmonic moments of $\mu $. Uniform density of harmonics and of finite product tests gives $\mu =(A/a)\omega _1\wedge \omega _2$.

Supporting probabilities and weights. Let $\mu \in \mathcal A_\phi (F)$ and, for smooth real $h$, set

\[ F_ t=F+\log \frac{(\Omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^2}{\Omega ^2}. \]

This is continuous, and $u+th$ solves the ordinary-type equation with density $\mathrm{e}^{F_ t}$ and scalar $c_0$. Thus $B_\phi (F_ t)=B_\phi (F)$, by uniqueness of the scalar. Uniform positivity gives $F_ t-F=tL_ uh+O(t^2)$ in the uniform norm. Apply (11.34.1) for both signs of $t$ to obtain $\int L_ uh\, \mathrm{d}\mu =0$. The perturbed density need not depend on the first curve alone: the inequality is the one on all of $C(X)$. Invariant uniqueness and nonemptiness of $\mathcal A_\phi (F)$ from theorem 11.34.2 now prove (11.45.17).

Finally, $\Omega ^2/\mathrm{d}W$ is bounded above and below by positive constants, so a normalized $r$ as in the statement belongs to $L^1(\mathrm{d}W)$ and $r\Omega $ is an order-zero current. Distributional Stokes makes $r\Omega ^2$ invariant. The probability uniqueness just proved gives $r\Omega ^2=\rho \Omega ^2$, hence $r=\rho $ almost everywhere. $\square$

Proposition 11.45.18.132C5E1825BF Let $w_ j\in C^\infty (X)$ be real and suppose

\[ \Omega _ j=\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j>0,\qquad \Omega _ j\longrightarrow \Omega \quad \text{uniformly as }(1,1)\text{-forms}. \]

Put $u_ j=\psi +w_ j$ and $F_ j=\log (\Omega _ j^2/(c_0\mathrm{d}V))$. Let $\rho _ j>0$ be the smooth residual adjoint weight, normalized by

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ j\Omega _ j)=0,\qquad \int _ X\rho _ j\Omega _ j^2=1. \]

Then $F_ j\to F$ uniformly, and the entire sequence satisfies

\[ \rho _ j\Omega _ j^2\rightharpoonup \nu ,\qquad \rho _ j\Omega _ j\rightharpoonup Q,\qquad \rho _ j\, \mathrm{d}W\rightharpoonup \rho \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

Such approximations exist with $w_ j\to w_0$ uniformly and can depend on both curve variables.

Proof. Existence of the smooth positive weights is theorem 11.4.2, applied to the residual metrics $\Omega _ j$ on the surface. Put $\nu _ j=\rho _ j\Omega _ j^2$ and $L_ jh=2\Omega _ j\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h/\Omega _ j^2$. Distributional Stokes gives $\int L_ jh\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j=0$. Uniform convergence to the positive form $\Omega $ supplies a common determinant lower bound and gives $L_ jh\to L_ uh$ uniformly for every fixed smooth $h$. Every weak cluster point of the probabilities $\nu _ j$ is therefore invariant for $L_ u$. By theorem 11.45.16, it equals $\nu $, proving convergence of the whole sequence. The same determinant convergence gives $F_ j\to F$ uniformly.

For a smooth real $(1,1)$ test form $\xi $, the ratios $\Omega _ j\wedge \xi /\Omega _ j^2$ converge uniformly to $\Omega \wedge \xi /\Omega ^2$. Consequently,

\[ \int _ X\rho _ j\Omega _ j\wedge \xi =\int _ X\frac{\Omega _ j\wedge \xi }{\Omega _ j^2}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \longrightarrow \int _ X\rho \Omega \wedge \xi . \]

Similarly, the positive densities $p_ j=\Omega _ j^2/\mathrm{d}W$ converge uniformly to $p=\Omega ^2/\mathrm{d}W$. For every $g\in C(X)$,

\[ \int _ Xg\rho _ j\, \mathrm{d}W =\int _ X(g/p_ j)\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \longrightarrow \int _ X(g/p)\, \mathrm{d}\nu =\int _ Xg\rho \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

These arguments require no pointwise control of the weights.

For existence, heat-smooth $A$ on the first sphere to positive smooth functions $A_ j$ of the same mean $a$, converging uniformly to $A$. Green inversion gives smooth mean-zero $w_ j(x)$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j=(A_ j-a)\omega _1$ and $w_ j\to w_0$ uniformly. Their residuals converge uniformly to $\Omega $. Adding $\varepsilon _ j h(x,y)$ for a fixed smooth function depending on both variables and $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$ preserves positivity for all sufficiently small coefficients and supplies nonsymmetric examples. Each $u_ j$ retains the fixed logarithmic divisor, and its non-pluripolar equation has scalar $c_0$ and logarithmic density $F_ j$. $\square$

Example 11.45.19.3C22B09DCF87 Take $a=1$, $d=1/4$, $q=1$, and let $t$ be the sphere height

\[ t=\frac{|Z_0|^2-|Z_1|^2}{|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2},\qquad b=2+t,\qquad H=A=\tfrac 12+|t|. \]

Here $c_0=1$ and $F=\log (2bA)$ is Lipschitz but not $C^1$. The residual has continuous positive coefficients with the same loss of differentiability. Its supporting probability differs from normalized Monge–Ampère volume:

\[ \int _ X\Omega ^2=4,\qquad \int _ Xt\, \mathrm{d}\nu =0,\qquad \int _ Xt\, \frac{\Omega ^2}{4}=\frac5{24}. \]

Even for smooth constant $H$, approximation of the pole by globally smooth potentials need not recover the weak adjoint. For $H=a$, so that $w_0=0$ and $\nu =\mathrm{d}W$, put

\[ \psi _\delta =d\log (\ell +\delta (1-\ell )),\qquad P_\delta =\omega _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\psi _\delta /d),\qquad 0<\delta \leq 1. \]

The smooth potentials $\psi _\delta $ decrease pointwise to $\psi $, converge in $L^1$ and smoothly off $D$, but the classical adjoint probabilities of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _\delta $ converge to

\[ \frac{a\omega _1+d\delta _ p}{a+d}\wedge \omega _2, \qquad p=\{ Z_0=0\} . \]

This limit assigns mass $d/(a+d)>0$ to $D$, and the weighted currents also retain a nonzero divisor component.

Proof. The height $t$ is uniform on $[-1,1]$, so $\int A\omega _1=1$ and $\int tA\omega _1=0$. Since $\Omega ^2=2bA\, \mathrm{d}W$, its mass is four and its normalized height moment is

\[ \frac12\int _{-1}^1 t^2(\tfrac 12+|t|)\, \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{2} =\frac5{24}. \]

The weight is $1/(2b)$ and $\nu =A\, \mathrm{d}W$. Moreover, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta =-2t\, \mathrm{d}W$, so the background has no smooth positive Gauduchon potential perturbation.

For the smoothing family, direct differentiation gives

\[ \frac{P_\delta }{\omega _1} =\frac{4\delta }{((1+\delta )+(1-\delta )t)^2}. \]

It is positive of mass one and converges weakly to $\delta _ p$. Thus the positive smooth forms are $\widehat\Omega _\delta =a\omega _1+dP_\delta +b\omega _2$. Their classical weights and probabilities are

\[ \widehat\rho _\delta =\frac1{2(a+d)b},\qquad \widehat\nu _\delta =\frac{a\omega _1+dP_\delta }{a+d}\wedge \omega _2. \]

The weighted forms are closed, so these are the normalized classical adjoints. Their limits follow from the curve measure convergence. In particular, the weighted-current limit contains $d[D]/(2(a+d)b(p))$. These smoothings fill the pole and do not satisfy the fixed-divisor and uniform-residual hypotheses of proposition 11.45.18. $\square$
