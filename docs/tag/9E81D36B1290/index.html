Proposition 13.11.49E81D36B1290

Proposition 13.11.4.9E81D36B1290 Let $\varphi _1,\ldots ,\varphi _ p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X)$.

  1. 4C9D393253D0

    The product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ is local with respect to the plurifine topology in the following sense: Let $O\subseteq X$ be an $\mathcal{F}$-open subset and $\psi _1,\ldots ,\psi _ p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X)$. Assume that

    \[ \varphi _ j|_{O}=\psi _ j|_{O},\quad j=1,\ldots ,p, \]

    and that

    \[ \bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j\textup{ and } \bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ j \]

    are both well-defined, then

    C3F3FF3F504B 13.11.5
    \begin{equation} \left.\left(\bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j\right)\right|_{O}=\left.\left(\bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ j\right)\right|_{O}. \end{equation}

    If furthermore $O$ is open in the usual topology, then the product

    \[ \bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j|_{O} \]

    on $O$ is well-defined and

    7E91D53462AC 13.11.6
    \begin{equation} \left. \left(\bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j\right)\right|_{O}=\bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j|_{O}. \end{equation}

    Let $\mathcal{U}$ be an open covering of $X$. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ is well-defined if and only if each product

    \[ \bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j|_{U},\quad U\in \mathcal{U}. \]
  2. 13DFC0FA2988

    The current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ and the fact that it is well-defined depend only on the currents $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j$, not on the choice of the $\varphi _ j$'s nor on the ordering of the $\varphi _ j$'s.

  3. 1353BEBCB3BB

    When $\varphi _1,\ldots ,\varphi _ p\in L^{\infty }_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)$, the non-pluripolar product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ is well-defined and is equal to the Bedford–Taylor product. More generally, if $q\leq p$, and we only assume that $\varphi _1,\ldots ,\varphi _ q\in L^{\infty }_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)$, and that the non-pluripolar product $T\coloneqq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _{q+1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ is well-defined, then the non-pluripolar product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ is also well-defined, and is equal to the Bedford–Taylor product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ q\wedge T$ as in 13.10.2.

  4. F4627B44F775

    Assume that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ is well-defined. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ puts no mass on pluripolar sets.

  5. 90ED277A28AD Assume that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ p$ is well-defined. Then $\bigwedge _{j=1}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j$ is a closed positive $(p,p)$-current on $X$.
    Note. This current is in fact strongly positive, as shown in [Xiabdiv2].
  6. 87188EAC5CDB

    The product is multilinear: Let $\psi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X)$, $a,b>0$ then

    A1F77BA03A26 13.11.7
    \begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a\varphi _1+b\psi _1)\wedge \bigwedge _{j=2}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j =a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\wedge \bigwedge _{j=2}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j + b\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1\wedge \bigwedge _{j=2}^ p \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j \end{equation}

    in the sense that the left-hand side is well-defined if and only if both terms on the right-hand side are well-defined, and the equality holds in that case.

Proof. For bounded potentials, Bedford--Taylor plurifine locality identifies their products on every plurifine open set on which the potentials agree. Apply it to the bounded truncations $\max\{\varphi_j,-k\}$ and $\max\{\psi_j,-k\}$, on the intersection of $O$ with the finite-level loci of all the potentials. The truncated products agree there. Let $k\to\infty$ in the increasing restriction measures defining the non-pluripolar product. The omitted pole sets have zero mass by construction, so equality holds on all of $O$. This limiting argument, including invariance under changes of local potentials, is established in the local calculus of non-pluripolar products.
