Exponential equations for an individual density

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 14.34 Exponential equations for an individual density

We return to the Hermitian background $h$ and a fixed nonzero density $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, and consider, for $\lambda >0$, the exponential equation

14.123
\begin{equation} \label{eq:individual-exponential-equation} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h). \end{equation}

A solution is a function $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$ satisfying (14.123), and an exponential subsolution at exponent $\lambda $ is a function $s\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$ with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(s)\geq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}f\, \mathrm{d}V$. For the truncations $f_ k=\min (f,k)$, theorem 10.1.2 gives continuous solutions $u_ k$ of the truncated equations, and these decrease as $k$ increases. Strict domination shows that every exponential subsolution is a common lower bound for the $u_ k$. Hence, as soon as one subsolution exists, the decreasing limit is not identically $-\infty $, and theorem 14.33.1 shows that it solves (14.123); it is the greatest subsolution. A positive threshold $b_ h(f)$ provides exponential subsolutions for every exponent, and the normalized greatest solutions then recover $b_ h(f)$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$.

Theorem 14.34.1. Fix $\lambda >0$, and suppose that (14.123) has an exponential subsolution $s\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$. For $k\geq 1$, let $u_ k$ be the continuous solution of

\[ \mathrm{MA}_ h(u_ k)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ k}f_ k\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad f_ k=\min (f,k), \]

given by theorem 10.1.2. Then $u_ k$ decreases to a solution $u_\lambda $ of (14.123), and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_\lambda )$ has finite mass. Moreover, $u_\lambda $ is the greatest exponential subsolution: every exponential subsolution $v$ at exponent $\lambda $ satisfies $v\leq u_\lambda $. In particular, $u_\lambda $ is the greatest solution of (14.123).

Proof. Since $f_ k\leq f_{k+1}$, lemma 10.4.2 gives $u_{k+1}\leq u_ k$.

We first show that every exponential subsolution $v$ lies below each $u_ k$. Let $t>0$. On the set $\{ u_ k<v-t\} $,

\[ \mathrm{MA}_ h(u_ k)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ k}f_ k\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v-t). \]

Since $u_ k$ is bounded, $P_{h/2}(u_ k)\geq \inf _ Xu_ k$ is not identically $-\infty $, and $v-t\preceq u_ k$. Strict domination, theorem 14.1.2, therefore gives $u_ k\geq v-t$. Letting $t\downarrow 0$ gives $u_ k\geq v$.

In particular $s\leq u_ k\leq u_1$ for every $k$, so the decreasing limit $u_\lambda \geq s$ is not identically $-\infty $ and belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$. By lemma 1.8.12, $u_ k\to u_\lambda $ in capacity. Choose a constant $A\geq \sup _ Xu_1$. The nonpositive functions $u_ k-A$ satisfy

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_ k-A)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (u_ k-A)}\, \mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f_ k\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

and $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f_ k\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. Theorem 14.33.1, applied with $\mu =\mathrm{d}V$ and constant exponents, shows that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_\lambda )$ is finite and that $u_\lambda $ solves (14.123). Finally, every exponential subsolution lies below all $u_ k$, hence below $u_\lambda $, which is itself a subsolution. $\square$

Being the greatest subsolution, $u_\lambda $ does not depend on the approximation used to construct it. The equation may have other, smaller solutions with different singularities. If $f\in L^ p$ for some $p>1$, the continuous solution of theorem 10.1.2 is a subsolution and therefore lies below $u_\lambda $. Thus $u_\lambda $ is bounded, and bounded exponential comparison (corollary 10.2.4) shows that it coincides with the continuous solution.

Corollary 14.34.2. Suppose $b=b_ h(f)>0$. Then (14.123) has a greatest solution $u_\lambda $ for every $\lambda >0$. If $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$ satisfies $\sup _ Xv=0$ and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V$, then

\[ u_\lambda \geq v+\lambda ^{-1}\log b. \]

Put

\[ M_\lambda =\sup _ Xu_\lambda ,\qquad v_\lambda =u_\lambda -M_\lambda ,\qquad c_\lambda =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda M_\lambda }. \]

Then, as $\lambda \downarrow 0$,

\[ c_\lambda \longrightarrow b_ h(f),\qquad \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f\longrightarrow b_ h(f)f \quad \text{in }L^1(\mathrm{d}V). \]

Proof. By theorem 14.27.2, a function $v$ as in the statement exists. Since $v\leq 0$, the function $s=v+\lambda ^{-1}\log b$ satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(s)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V \geq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda s}f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

so it is an exponential subsolution. Theorem 14.34.1 gives the greatest solution $u_\lambda $ and the bound $u_\lambda \geq s$; in particular, $c_\lambda \geq b$.

Set $g_\lambda =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }f$. By lemma 1.23.3, applied to the finite measure $f\, \mathrm{d}V$, we have $g_\lambda \to f$ in $L^1$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$. Each $g_\lambda $ is nonzero, since $v_\lambda $ is finite almost everywhere. The equation for $u_\lambda $ reads $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v_\lambda )=c_\lambda g_\lambda \, \mathrm{d}V$, so theorem 14.27.2, applied to the density $g_\lambda $, gives

\[ c_\lambda \leq b_ h(g_\lambda ). \]

The upper semicontinuity of the threshold (theorem 14.31.1) therefore gives

\[ b\leq \liminf _{\lambda \downarrow 0}c_\lambda \leq \limsup _{\lambda \downarrow 0}c_\lambda \leq b_ h(f)=b. \]

This proves $c_\lambda \to b$. Since $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f=c_\lambda g_\lambda $ and $g_\lambda \to f$ in $L^1$, it also proves $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f\to bf$ in $L^1$. $\square$

Since $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v_\lambda )=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f\, \mathrm{d}V$, the Monge–Ampère measures of the normalized greatest solutions converge in total variation to $b_ h(f)f\, \mathrm{d}V$. This answers the questions about the scalar and the measure raised in section 14.33; the potentials $v_\lambda $ themselves are harder to control. Under positive lower mass, corollary 31.7.1 shows that every $L^1$-convergent sequence $v_{\lambda _ j}$ with $\lambda _ j\downarrow 0$ converges in capacity to a solution of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v)=b_ h(f)f\, \mathrm{d}V$. For $L^ p$ data with $p>1$, proposition 14.19.1 gives uniform precompactness. In both cases, convergence of the whole family $(v_\lambda )$ requires that all its cluster limits coincide. This holds when the dual problem for $f^{1/n}$ over Gauduchon currents, studied in section 26.9, has a smooth strictly positive maximizer: the whole family then converges uniformly (corollary 26.9.7). If the maximizer is smooth and semipositive, and strictly positive outside a proper analytic set, proposition 26.9.11 gives $L^1$ convergence of the whole family and a fixed lower barrier, and corollary 26.9.18 strengthens this to uniform convergence for $L^ p$ data with $p>1$.

For a general positive threshold, the corollary gives the lower bound

\[ v_\lambda \geq v-\lambda ^{-1}\log (c_\lambda /b_ h(f)) \]

for every normalized threshold subsolution $v$. The next subsection estimates the scalar error $\log (c_\lambda /b_ h(f))$ when $f^{1/n}$ can be approximated by bounded functions in the norm of $\mathcal D_ h$. When $b_ h(f)=0$, theorem 14.34.1 still applies whenever an exponential subsolution exists; examples are constructed in 14.34.2.
