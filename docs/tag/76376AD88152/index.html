Theorem 12.7.376376AD88152

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 12.7.3

Theorem 12.7.3. Let $A<B$ and let $f$ be smooth on a neighborhood of $X\times [A,B]$. Suppose that, for every $x\in X$,

\[ f(x,A)\leq 0\leq f(x,B),\qquad 0<\kappa \leq \partial _ t f(x,t)\leq L\quad (A\leq t\leq B). \]

There is a unique continuous admissible solution with values in $[A,B]$ of

\[ H_ m(w)=e^{f(x,w)}\omega ^ n. \]

This solution is smooth and strictly admissible.

Proof. Choose $a\geq L$ and let $\mathcal C=\{ v\in C(X):A\leq v\leq B\} $, a complete subset of $C(X)$ in the uniform norm. For $v\in \mathcal C$ put $H_ v(x)=f(x,v(x))-av(x)$ and let $T(v)$ be the continuous proper solution with exponent $aT(v)+H_ v$, furnished by 12.7.1. The function $f(x,t)-at$ is nonincreasing in $t$, so

\[ -aB\leq f(x,B)-aB\leq H_ v(x) \leq f(x,A)-aA\leq -aA. \]

The zero-order bounds in that proposition give $A\leq T(v)\leq B$. The mean-value theorem also gives

\[ \| H_ v-H_ z\| _\infty \leq (a-\kappa )\| v-z\| _\infty , \qquad \| T(v)-T(z)\| _\infty \leq (1-\kappa /a)\| v-z\| _\infty . \]

Since $0\leq 1-\kappa /a<1$, the contraction theorem gives a unique fixed point $w\in \mathcal C$. It is admissible because it equals $T(w)$, and its equation is exactly the stated equation. Every continuous admissible solution in this interval is a fixed point, by uniqueness of the continuous proper equation with its prescribed $H_ w$. This proves uniqueness in the stated class. 12.7.2 makes $w$ smooth. Its form lies in the closed $m$-cone and has positive $m$th symmetric polynomial; Maclaurin's inequalities then put it in the open cone. Thus it is strictly admissible. $\square$
