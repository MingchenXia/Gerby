From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Lemma 15.13.2

Lemma 15.13.2. For every $\sigma >0$, the equation

15.70
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}_\theta (s_\sigma )=\mathrm{e}^{\sigma s_\sigma }f\, \mathrm{d}W \end{equation}

has a solution of ordinary type $\phi _{a,d}$, unique in that type. It is the greatest exponential subsolution among all $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi _{a,d}$, and its measure has finite mass.

Proof. The one-sided ordinary class has the description

15.71
\begin{equation} u\preceq \psi \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad u=\psi +r,\quad r\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\eta ),\qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi +r) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\eta (r). \end{equation}

Indeed, $u\preceq \psi $ gives $\nu (u,D)\geq d$. Divisorial removal, lemma 1.17.1, shows that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-d[D]\geq 0$. Thus the locally integrable difference $u-\psi $ has an $\eta $-plurisubharmonic representative $r$. The equality $u=\psi +r$ holds off $D$ by canonical equality and on $D$ because both sides equal $-\infty $. Conversely, adding $\psi $ to $r$ adds the positive current $d[D]$; since $r$ has a finite compact upper bound, the one-sided comparison follows. Off $D$ the currents agree, so finite-level locality identifies their extended products, including at any additional poles of $r$. Both measures give $D$ zero mass, proving the global equality. The bounded ordinary type corresponds exactly to bounded $r$, as in theorem 15.9.1.

The function $g_\sigma =\mathrm{e}^{\sigma \psi }f$ extends continuously by zero on $D$ and is positive elsewhere. Since $\eta >0$, theorem 10.1.2 gives a continuous solution $R_\sigma $ of

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\eta (R_\sigma )=\mathrm{e}^{\sigma R_\sigma }g_\sigma \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

For this bounded density, the greatest solution of theorem 14.34.1 is bounded and coincides with $R_\sigma $ by corollary 10.2.4. Thus it dominates every $\eta $-plurisubharmonic exponential subsolution. The correspondence (15.71) transfers this order to the entire one-sided ordinary class and gives $s_\sigma =\psi +R_\sigma $. Bounded residual uniqueness gives uniqueness in the ordinary type. The bounded residual equation has finite mass, so the singular equation does too. $\square$
