16.6.12 Auxiliary compact complex factors42B1A5F53675

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.6.12 Auxiliary compact complex factors

So far, the auxiliary factor that makes the background nonclosed has been a curve. The arguments only used that the auxiliary term in the background is semipositive and vanishes on the slices parallel to the curve product. The auxiliary factor can therefore be any compact complex manifold of positive dimension, Kähler or not, and the potentials and their polar metric still depend only on the curve coordinates and the fixed divisor of weight one.

Keep $Y$, $\sigma _ i$, $B_ i$ and $\gamma _\sigma $, as well as the data $(Z,\kappa ,D,\psi )$ of the divisor of weight one. Let $T$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $\ell \geq 1$, and put $B=Y\times T$, with projections $\pi $ to $Y$ and $\operatorname {pr}_ T$ to $T$. Choose a smooth semipositive real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha $ on $B$ whose restriction to every slice $Y\times \{ t\} $ is zero. By semipositivity, the tangent spaces of these slices lie in the kernel of $\alpha $, so in product coordinates $\alpha $ has only coefficients in the $T$-directions. On $M=B\times Z$, let $\varpi ,r$ be the projections, and put $p=\pi \circ \varpi $ and

\[ \Gamma _\alpha =\pi ^*\gamma _\sigma +\alpha ,\qquad \Theta _\alpha =\varpi ^*\Gamma _\alpha +r^*\kappa , \qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r. \]

We assume neither that $\alpha $ is closed nor that $M$ has the bounded mass property.

Lemma 16.6.42. For $u_ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(C_ i,\sigma _ i)$ put

\[ u=\sum _ i u_ i\circ \pi _ i,\qquad U=\Psi +u\circ p, \qquad q_ i=P_{\sigma _ i}[u_ i],\quad r_ i=P_{\sigma _ i}[u_ i]_{\mathcal I}. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} P_{\Theta _\alpha }[U]& =\Psi +\sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ p,\\ P_{\Theta _\alpha }[U]_{\mathcal I} & =\Psi +\sum _ ir_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ p. \end{aligned} \]

Every ordinary representative of either of these envelopes has zero extended mass, so their upper and lower singularity volumes are zero.

Proof. On $B$, sums of ordinary competitors on the curves give the lower bound $P_{\Gamma _\alpha }[u\circ \pi ]\geq \sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ \pi $. For the upper bound, replace a competitor by its maximum with a sufficiently low translate of $u\circ \pi $. Its restriction to every slice $Y\times \{ t\} $ is then not identically $-\infty $, and since $\alpha $ restricts to zero there, it is a $\gamma _\sigma $-plurisubharmonic ordinary competitor for $u$. The case of the zero obstacle in proposition 16.6.30 bounds it by $\sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i$. This proves the identity for the ordinary envelope on $B$.

Take the atomic Green representatives $g_ i$ of the proof of proposition 16.6.30. The pullback $g$ of their sum to $B$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(B,\Gamma _\alpha )$, is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u\circ \pi $, and is ordinarily equivalent to $\sum _ ir_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ \pi $. Its complex Hessian is a countable current of divisors along coordinate fibers, minus a smooth form; this divisor current has finite mass, as one sees with a fixed product Hermitian metric and the finite atomic masses on the curves. By lemma 1.17.4, every ideal competitor is therefore at least as singular as $g$; if the divisor current is zero, $g$ is smooth and this is immediate by compactness. Thus every such competitor is bounded by $P_{\Gamma _\alpha }[g]=P_{\Gamma _\alpha }[\sum _ ir_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ \pi ]$, which equals $\sum _ ir_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ \pi $ by the ordinary identity just proved and $P_{\sigma _ i}[r_ i]=r_ i$. This sum is itself an ideal competitor, which proves the identity for the ideal envelope on $B$.

Apply proposition 16.6.13 to $M\to B$ and the divisor $B\times D$ with weight one. Its induced zero obstacle is zero because $\sup _ Z\psi =0$, which gives both identities upstairs. The mass assertion of the same proposition, applied to each of the two envelopes, shows that every representative of its ordinary type has zero mass, so both singularity volumes are zero. $\square$

For normalized ordinary curve models $q_ i$, write

\[ \Phi _\alpha (q)=\Psi +\sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ p, \]

let $\mathcal S_\alpha $ be the locus of these potentials, and give it the metric

\[ \rho _\alpha (\Phi _\alpha (q),\Phi _\alpha (\widetilde q)) =\sum _ i\| \mu _ i-\widetilde\mu _ i\| _{\mathrm{TV}}, \]

where $\mu _ i,\widetilde\mu _ i$ are the polar curvatures of $q_ i,\widetilde q_ i$.

Proposition 16.6.43. The map $\Phi _\alpha (q)\mapsto \Phi _\sigma (q)$ is an isometry from $\mathcal S_\alpha $ onto the locus $\mathcal S_\sigma $ of proposition 16.6.37, and it maps the ideal subspaces and the models with analytic singularities onto each other. Consequently $\mathcal S_\alpha $ is complete and geodesic, with diameter $2\sum _ iB_ i$. Its ideal subspace is closed, geodesically convex and equal to the metric closure of the models with analytic singularities. Ideal projection is the unique nearest-point retraction onto this subspace and is $1$-Lipschitz, and the distance to the subspace is the sum of the nonatomic polar masses.

Changing $T$ or $\alpha $ does not change this abstract metric space. All the area comparisons and the contractions commuting with ideal projection of proposition 16.6.37 hold, even for different auxiliary data. The upper-volume comparison vanishes on the locus, and metric convergence implies $L^1(M)$ convergence for every smooth positive volume form. If $T$ and $Z$ are fixed and the curve backgrounds converge uniformly to backgrounds of zero area, then, for any choice of the semipositive terms $\alpha $, the potentials converge in $L^1(M)$, uniformly over the model tuples, to the sum of $\Psi $ and the pullbacks of the normalized smooth potentials of the limiting curve backgrounds, as in corollary 16.6.38.

Proof. Applied to model tuples, the lemma shows that every $\Phi _\alpha (q)$ is a normalized ordinary model and computes its ideal projection coordinatewise. Restrictions to curve sections on which the other potentials are finite determine the tuple up to constants, and the normalization of the coordinates determines these constants. Thus the map is well defined and bijective, and the formulas for the polar curvatures make it an isometry preserving ideal projection.

Each potential in either locus is pulled back from $Y\times Z$. Analytic presentations pull back under the projection that forgets the auxiliary factor. Conversely, by proposition 1.13.1, the restriction of a potential with analytic singularities to a section with fixed auxiliary coordinate has analytic singularities and is not identically $-\infty $. Hence a potential in either locus has analytic singularities exactly when the common potential on $Y\times Z$ does, and the isometry maps the analytic models onto each other. The statements on the complete metric, the ideal closure, geodesics, nearest points and area comparisons now follow from proposition 16.6.37.

A pairwise maximum is $\Psi $ plus the pullback of the maximum of two $\Gamma _\alpha $-plurisubharmonic functions on $B$. The reduction along the divisor of weight one gives zero upper volume for it and for both models, so the upper-volume comparison vanishes. For $L^1$ convergence, integration along the fixed compact fibers bounds the norm of each pulled-back curve potential by a constant times its norm on the curve. The same estimate and theorem 1.8.15 give the asserted uniform limit at zero area. Neither estimate involves $\alpha $. $\square$

Corollary 16.6.44. For the arbitrary curve potentials $u_ i$ of the lemma, put $R=\Psi +\sum _ ir_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ p$. The following are equivalent: $P_{\Theta _\alpha }[U]=P_{\Theta _\alpha }[U]_{\mathcal I}$; for each $i$, the polar part of the curvature measure of $u_ i$ equals its atomic part; $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta _\alpha ,R)$; and the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $U$ agree below every smooth real obstacle on $M$.

Proof. By the lemma and the normalization of the coordinates, equality of the envelopes below zero is equivalent to $q_ i=r_ i$ for every $i$. By corollary 30.13.2, this is equivalent to equality of the polar and atomic measures, and in that case $u_ i\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(C_ i,\sigma _ i,r_ i)$. Choose stretching competitors in these curve classes. The sum of their pullbacks plus $\Psi $ has positive current, since the contributions of the curves, the form $\varpi ^*\alpha $ and the current of the divisor are all positive. Adding the convex stretching inequalities, which is possible also at poles, gives a stretching competitor relative to $R$ for every finite stretch, and adding the ordinary bounds gives $U\preceq R$. Thus $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta _\alpha ,R)$, and proposition 16.6.8 gives equality below every smooth obstacle. Taking the zero obstacle recovers the first condition, which proves the equivalence. $\square$

Corollary 16.6.45. If $\alpha =0$ or some $B_ i=0$, the background $\Theta _\alpha $ is not big. If all $B_ i>0$ and $\alpha $ is positive on every nonzero vector tangent to the factor $T$, it is big. In particular, for a Hermitian form $\omega _ T$ on $T$ and a positive nonconstant smooth function $h$ on $Y$, the choice $\alpha =(h\circ \pi )\operatorname {pr}_ T^*\omega _ T$ gives a big background that is not closed whenever all $B_ i>0$.

Proof. For $\alpha =0$, the background is pulled back from $Y\times Z$, and by proposition 1.22.3 every potential in it descends to $Y\times Z$. The corresponding currents have no component in the $T$-directions, so they cannot dominate a positive multiple of a Hermitian form on $M$. Thus the background is not big.

If $B_ i=0$, choose any Hermitian form $\omega _ T$ on $T$ and write $s:M\to T$ for the projection. The positive $(m+\ell +k-1,m+\ell +k-1)$-form

\[ L_ i=p^*\left(\bigwedge _{j\ne i}\pi _ j^*\eta _ j\right) \wedge s^*(\omega _ T^\ell )\wedge r^*(\kappa ^ k) \]

is closed, because $\omega _ T^\ell $ has top degree on $T$. Its pairing with $\Theta _\alpha $ is zero, since the only possible contribution contains the integral of $\sigma _ i$, whereas its pairing with a positive product Hermitian form is strictly positive. By Stokes' theorem, no current in the background is bounded below by a positive multiple of a Hermitian form.

When all areas are positive, write $\sigma _ i=B_ i\eta _ i/A_ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ i$. Subtracting the smooth pullback of $\sum _ i\chi _ i\circ \pi _ i$ gives the current $p^*\sum _ i(B_ i/A_ i)\pi _ i^*\eta _ i+\varpi ^*\alpha +r^*\kappa $. Positivity of $\alpha $ in the $T$-directions makes this form strictly positive, which proves bigness. For the stated choice of $\alpha $, the exterior derivative of $\Theta _\alpha $ has the component $\mathrm dh\wedge \omega _ T$, with one direction in $Y$ and two in $T$. It is nonzero for nonconstant $h$ and cannot be canceled by the component with three directions in $T$. Hence $\mathrm d\Theta _\alpha \ne 0$. $\square$

The proofs use no bound on the ambient masses, so the auxiliary manifold may fail the bounded mass property; an explicit example follows. The complete metric and the criterion for all obstacles concern only the specified product locus.
