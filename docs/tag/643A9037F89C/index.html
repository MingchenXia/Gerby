15.37.31 Normalization at exponent zero643A9037F89C

In this section the background is a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$ with $v_-(h)>0$, and the reference is zero; thus $\theta =h$ and $\varphi =0$ in the notation of the chapter, and $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ is the minimal envelope class. Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero. Recall from (15.28.1) that the subsolution threshold $b_ h(f)$ is the limit of the normalizing constants of the truncations $\min (f,k)$; by theorem 15.28.4, it is the largest $a\geq 0$ for which some $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u)\geq af\, \mathrm{d}V$. We show that $b_ h(f)$ is exactly the scalar of the normalized equation in the minimal envelope class: scalar comparison bounds every subsolution coefficient by this scalar. Dominated continuity also identifies the equation satisfied by every limit of normalized greatest exponential solutions; whether these limits coincide is a question about the potentials.

Corollary 15.37.32.74BE83BA167D Let $h$ be a Hermitian form on $X$ with $v_-(h)>0$, and let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero. There is $v\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v)=b_ h(f)f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

The number $b_ h(f)>0$ is the unique positive scalar for which this equation has a solution in that envelope class.

For $\lambda >0$, let $u_\lambda $ be the greatest solution of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_\lambda )=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f\, \mathrm{d}V$ from theorem 15.35.2, and set $v_\lambda =u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda $. Each $u_\lambda $ belongs to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$. If $\lambda _ j\downarrow 0$ and $v_{\lambda _ j}\to v$ in $L^1$, then the convergence holds in capacity, and $v$ belongs to the same envelope class and satisfies the equation above.

Proof. By theorem 15.37.28, applied with $\theta =h$, there are $c>0$ and $w\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,h,0)$ with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(w)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$. If $s\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(s)\geq af\, \mathrm{d}V$ with $a>0$, then

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(w) \leq \frac ca\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(s). \]

Every $h$-plurisubharmonic $s$ satisfies $s\preceq 0$, so the scalar assertion of corollary 15.37.11, with reference zero, gives $c/a\geq 1$. Taking the maximum over all subsolution coefficients $a$, theorem 15.28.4 yields $b_ h(f)\leq c$. The reverse inequality holds because $w$ itself is a subsolution with coefficient $c$. The same argument identifies the scalar of every solution in this envelope class with $b_ h(f)$.

For $\lambda >0$, theorem 15.37.28 also gives a solution $s_\lambda $ of the exponential equation in the envelope class. Since $s_\lambda $ is in particular a subsolution, the greatest-solution property gives $s_\lambda \leq u_\lambda $. Upward stability (proposition 16.28.62) places $u_\lambda $ and its translate $v_\lambda $ in that class.

Write $c_\lambda =\exp (\lambda \sup _ Xu_\lambda )$. By corollary 15.35.3, $c_\lambda \to b_ h(f)>0$ and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v_\lambda ) =c_\lambda \mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }f\, \mathrm{d}V \longrightarrow b_ h(f)f\, \mathrm{d}V \quad \text{in total variation}. \]

Since $v_\lambda \leq 0$ and $c_\lambda $ stays bounded for small $\lambda $, these measures are dominated by one finite non-pluripolar measure $C f\, \mathrm{d}V$. Applying theorem 15.37.19 to the given $L^1$-convergent sequence $v_{\lambda _ j}$, we obtain class membership, convergence in capacity and the equation for its limit. $\square$

By theorem 5.25.2, every sequence $\lambda _ j\downarrow 0$ has a subsequence along which $v_{\lambda _ j}$ converges in $L^1$. The corollary identifies the equation of every such limit; the examples below show that this equation alone need not determine the normalized potential.
