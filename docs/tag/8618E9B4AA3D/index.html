5 Fine topology and balayage8618E9B4AA3D

Throughout this chapter $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^ d$ is open and $d\geq 2$. Subharmonic functions are upper semicontinuous but in general not continuous. For instance, let $e_1$ be the first coordinate unit vector, and let $u=E_ d*\nu $ be the fundamental potential of the probability measure $\nu =\sum _{j\geq 1}2^{-j}\delta _{e_1/j}$, which is supported on the compact set $\{ 0\} \cup \{ e_1/j:j\geq 1\} $. By proposition 3.2.1, $u$ is subharmonic on $\mathbb R^ d$. At each atom we have $u(e_1/j)=-\infty $, because the integrand $E_ d(e_1/j-\cdot )$ is bounded above on the support of $\nu $ and equals $-\infty $ at $e_1/j$. On the other hand, $u(0)=\sum _ j2^{-j}E_ d(e_1/j)$ is finite, since $|E_ d(e_1/j)|$ equals $(2\pi )^{-1}\log j$ if $d=2$ and a constant multiple of $j^{d-2}$ if $d\geq 3$. Hence $u$ is not continuous at $0$.

By theorem 4.5.2, the discontinuities of subharmonic functions are nevertheless controlled by capacity: on a compact subset of a ball, a nontrivial subharmonic function becomes continuous once an open set of arbitrarily small capacity has been removed. In this chapter we study two further constructions, the fine topology and balayage, in which the polar sets of chapter 3, chapter 4 appear as exceptional sets. Both have counterparts in the pluripotential theory of 2.

The fine topology is the coarsest topology that contains the Euclidean topology and makes every subharmonic function continuous. In this topology, thinness of a set at a point becomes an ordinary neighborhood condition (proposition 5.1.3). Polar sets are finely closed, and the fine topology induces the discrete topology on them (proposition 5.1.5). Since there are uncountable compact polar sets, the fine topology is neither Lindelöf nor second countable (example 5.1.7). Up to polar sets, however, it has the Lindelöf property: by Choquet's lemma and theorem 4.4.4, every union of finely open sets agrees with a countable subunion outside a polar set (theorem 5.4.1). Consequently finely open sets are measurable, and together with quasi-continuity this shows that they are open up to sets of small capacity (proposition 5.4.2).

Balayage is based on reduction. For a nonnegative superharmonic function $s$ and a set $A$, the reduction $R_ s^ A$ of $s$ on $A$ is the infimum of all nonnegative superharmonic functions that majorize $s$ on $A$: it keeps the values of $s$ on $A$ and removes as much of $s$ elsewhere as superharmonicity permits. Its lower semicontinuous regularization $\widehat R_ s^ A$ is superharmonic and agrees with $R_ s^ A$ outside a polar set. On a ball, when $A$ is compact and $s$ is the Green potential of a measure $\mu $ whose potential is bounded on $A$ or whose Green energy is finite, $\widehat R_ s^ A$ is the Green potential of a measure $\mu ^ A$ carried by $A$, the balayage of $\mu $ onto $A$. We characterize $\mu ^ A$ by an orthogonality relation for the Green energy, which is a positive definite quadratic form on signed measures of finite energy (proposition 5.2.2, theorem 5.3.1, theorem 5.3.2), and express the balayage of a Dirac mass through harmonic measure (example 5.3.3).

Finally, we compare the fine topology of $\mathbb C^ n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ with the topology generated by the functions that are subharmonic on complex lines, which becomes the plurifine topology of chapter 10; for $n\geq 2$ the two topologies differ (proposition 5.5.1).

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in section B.5.
