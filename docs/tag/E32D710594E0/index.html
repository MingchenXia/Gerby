Flag dimension section field

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 21.3.2

Proposition 21.3.2. The transcendence degree of $K(W_\bullet )$ over $\mathbb C$ is exactly $d$. If $\Delta (W_\bullet )$ has nonempty interior in $\mathbb R^ n$, then $X$ has $n$ algebraically independent meromorphic functions. Moreover, $d$, the period $e$ and the intrinsic volume in theorem 21.3.1 are the same for any two complete flags on $X$.

Proof. Suppose first that $d>0$. Choose sections $s_0,\ldots ,s_ d$ of positive degrees $m_0,\ldots ,m_ d$ whose normalized valuations $\nu (s_ i)/m_ i$ are affinely independent. Let $M$ be a common multiple of their degrees and put $t_ i=s_ i^{M/m_ i}\in W_ M$. The ratios $f_ i=t_ i/t_0$ belong to $K(W_\bullet )$. The flag valuation extends to quotients by subtraction, giving

\[ \nu (f_ i)=M\left(\frac{\nu (s_ i)}{m_ i} -\frac{\nu (s_0)}{m_0}\right), \qquad 1\leq i\leq d. \]

These vectors are linearly independent. Distinct monomials in the $f_ i$ therefore have distinct valuations, so the term of smallest valuation in a nonzero polynomial expression cannot cancel, and the ratios are algebraically independent. Thus the transcendence degree is at least $d$; this is trivial when $d=0$.

Conversely, let $f_1,\ldots ,f_ r$, with $r\geq 1$, be algebraically independent elements of the field. Using the product of their denominators, write $f_ i=A_ i/B$ with $A_ i,B\in W_ M$ and $B\neq 0$ for a common positive degree $M$. For every integer $q\geq 1$ and every multi-index $a=(a_1,\ldots ,a_ r)\in \mathbb N^ r$ with $|a|=a_1+\cdots +a_ r\leq q$, the sections

\[ B^{q-|a|}A_1^{a_1}\cdots A_ r^{a_ r}\in W_{Mq} \]

are linearly independent, because division by $B^ q$ turns a linear relation among them into a polynomial relation among the $f_ i$. There are $\binom {q+r}{r}$ such sections. Since $W_ M\neq 0$, the degree $M$ is a multiple of $e$, and theorem 21.3.1 gives $\dim W_{Mq}=O(q^ d)$. Comparing with the binomial lower bound forces $r\leq d$, which proves equality of the transcendence degree with $d$. A body with nonempty interior has $d=n$, and this gives the stated consequence for $X$.

The field and the set of degrees with nonzero sections do not depend on the flag, so neither $d$ nor $e$ does. By theorem 21.3.1, the lattice volume is, for either flag, the limit of the same dimensions with the same normalization. It is therefore independent of the flag as well, although the bodies and their lattices may differ. $\square$
