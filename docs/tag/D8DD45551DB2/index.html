A convex functional on probability measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 15.3 A convex functional on probability measures

To pass to the limit in the supporting inequality, we need to control the mass that supporting probabilities can put on small sets. The convex dual of $B_\varphi $ provides this control: the measures in each of its sublevel sets are uniformly absolutely continuous. This allows the logarithmic density and the supporting measure to vary simultaneously.

Let $\mathcal P(X)$ denote the set of Borel probability measures on $X$, with the weak topology. Define

15.20
\begin{equation} \label{eq:normalization-dual-definition} H_\varphi (\nu ) =\sup _{G\in C(X)}\left(B_\varphi (G)+\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu \right), \qquad \nu \in \mathcal P(X). \end{equation}

The value $+\infty $ is allowed, while taking $G=0$ shows that $H_\varphi \geq B_\varphi (0)>-\infty $.

Proposition 15.3.1. The functional $H_\varphi $ is convex and weakly lower semicontinuous. For every $F\in C(X)$,

15.21
\begin{equation} \label{eq:normalization-dual-minimum} B_\varphi (F) =\min _{\nu \in \mathcal P(X)} \left(H_\varphi (\nu )-\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu \right). \end{equation}

The minimizers are exactly $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$.

Proof. Each function of $\nu $ in the supremum defining $H_\varphi $ is affine and weakly continuous, so their supremum is convex and lower semicontinuous. By definition, $H_\varphi (\nu )-\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu \geq B_\varphi (F)$, with equality precisely when

\[ B_\varphi (G)+\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq B_\varphi (F)+\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu \quad \text{for every }G\in C(X). \]

This is the defining inequality of $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$, and the minimum is attained because $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is nonempty by theorem 15.2.1. $\square$

Theorem 15.3.2. For every finite real $L$, the set

\[ \mathcal K_ L=\{ \nu \in \mathcal P(X):H_\varphi (\nu )\leq L\} \]

is weakly compact. Its measures are uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$: for every $\epsilon >0$ there is a $\delta >0$ such that

15.22
\begin{equation} \label{eq:normalization-dual-uniform-ac} \mathrm{d}V(E)<\delta \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sup _{\nu \in \mathcal K_ L}\nu (E)<\epsilon \end{equation}

for every Borel set $E$. In particular, writing $\mathrm{d}\nu =h_\nu \mathrm{d}V$, the densities in this set are uniformly integrable in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. On $\mathcal K_ L$, weak convergence of measures also implies weak convergence of their densities in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$.

Proof. Weak compactness follows from lower semicontinuity and compactness of $\mathcal P(X)$. We may assume that the sublevel set is nonempty, so that $L\geq B_\varphi (0)$. Fix $t>0$. By theorem 15.1.3, there is $\delta _ t>0$ such that

\[ 0\leq \chi \leq 1,\quad \chi \in C(X),\quad \int _ X\chi \, \mathrm{d}V<\delta _ t \quad \Longrightarrow \quad B_\varphi (t\chi )>B_\varphi (0)-1. \]

Indeed, otherwise there would be a sequence of such functions $\chi $ tending to zero in $L^1$ with $B_\varphi (t\chi )\leq B_\varphi (0)-1$, contrary to that theorem. For $\nu \in \mathcal K_ L$, the defining supremum gives

\[ t\int _ X\chi \, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq L-B_\varphi (t\chi ) <L-B_\varphi (0)+1. \]

Choose $t$ so large that $(L-B_\varphi (0)+1)/t<\epsilon /2$. If $\mathrm{d}V(E)<\delta _ t/2$, outer regularity gives an open neighborhood $O$ of $E$ with $\mathrm{d}V(O)<\delta _ t$. For any compact $K\subset E$, choose a continuous cutoff $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ equal to one on $K$ and supported in $O$. The last inequality gives $\nu (K)<\epsilon /2$, uniformly in $\nu \in \mathcal K_ L$, and inner regularity yields $\nu (E)\leq \epsilon /2<\epsilon $. This proves (15.22).

In particular, every measure in the sublevel set is absolutely continuous, with a density of integral one. Together with the uniform small-set estimate, this is exactly uniform integrability on the finite measure space $(X,\mathrm{d}V)$.

Finally, suppose that $\nu _ j\to \nu $ weakly within the sublevel set. For a bounded measurable $G$, choose continuous $G_ k$ with $|G_ k|\leq \| G\| _\infty $ and $G_ k\to G$ in $\mathrm{d}V$-measure; such approximations exist by Lusin's theorem. Uniform absolute continuity gives

\[ \sup _{\sigma \in \mathcal K_ L}\int _ X|G_ k-G|\, \mathrm{d}\sigma \longrightarrow 0. \]

To see this, split $X$ into $\{ |G_ k-G|\leq a\} $ and its complement; the integral over the first set is at most $a$, and the integral over the second is at most $2\| G\| _\infty $ times a uniformly small measure. For fixed $k$, weak convergence applies to $G_ k$. Letting first $j\to \infty $ and then $k\to \infty $ proves convergence of the integrals against $G$, which is exactly weak $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ convergence of the densities. In particular, every sequence in the sublevel set has a subsequence whose densities converge weakly in $L^1$. $\square$
