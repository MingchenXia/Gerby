Chapter 11 · Non-pluripolar measures and mass · Tag C0F29EF85157

Small pole envelope residual

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 30.3.5

Theorem 30.3.5. Let $\omega $ be a Hermitian form on $X$, and let $S\subset X$ be a compact set of Hausdorff dimension zero. Suppose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus S$, has pole set $\{ u=-\infty \} =S$, and has positive lower singularity volume. Put $p=P_\omega [u]$ and $p_ m=P_\omega (u+m,0)$ for real $m>0$. For $v=u$, $v=p$ and $v=p_ m$, the weak limit

\[ \Phi _ v=\lim _{k\to \infty } (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (v,-k))^ n \]

of the bounded truncations exists and is a finite positive measure, which we call the full Monge–Ampère measure of $v$. On each coordinate chart, its restriction $\mathbf1_ S\Phi _ v$ equals $\mathbf1_ S(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v+q))^ n_{\mathrm{full}}$ for every smooth function $q$ making $v+q$ plurisubharmonic there; in particular, it does not depend on $q$. Moreover,

\[ \mathbf1_ S\Phi _ p=\mathbf1_ S\Phi _ u, \]

and $\Phi _{p_ m}\to \Phi _ p$ weakly as $m\to \infty $.

Proof. Step 1: the rooftops. Adding a constant to $u$ changes neither $p$ nor the family $(p_ m)_{m>0}$, apart from a shift of the parameter $m$. We may therefore assume $u\leq 0$. Then

\[ u\leq p_ m\leq \min (u+m,0),\qquad p_ m\uparrow p, \]

where the increasing limit is understood after upper semicontinuous regularization. Thus $p_ m\sim u$, and $p_ m$ and $p$ are locally bounded off $S$. By theorem 30.3.4, $p$ is an ordinary model with the same volumes as $u$. By lemma 1.8.12, $p_ m\to p$ in capacity. On compact subsets of $X\setminus S$ the potentials have a common bounded range, so all bounded local products converge there, by the bounded product theory of section 1.6.

Step 2: full measures. Let $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ be locally bounded off $S$, and put $v_ k=\max (v,-k)$. On each chart choose a smooth $q$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \omega $, so that the functions $v_ k+q$ are plurisubharmonic and decrease to $v+q$. Theorem 1.6.9 then gives full weak limits in every degree,

\[ W_ j(v)=\lim _{k\to \infty }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ k)^ j, \qquad \Phi _ v=\lim _{k\to \infty }(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ k)^ n. \]

Indeed, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ k=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v_ k+q)-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$, and the powers can be expanded in the closed positive powers of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v_ k+q)$ and smooth forms. The local limits patch together because the approximating bounded currents are global. The limits are currents of order zero with finite mass on the compact manifold $X$, and $\Phi _ v$ is positive. Stokes' theorem for the bounded potentials $v_ k$ gives $\int _ XW_ n(v)=0$. Consequently

30.5
\begin{equation} \label{eq:small-pole-full-total} J(v):=\Phi _ v(X) =\int _ X\omega ^ n+ \sum _{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj \int _ XW_ j(v)\wedge \omega ^{n-j}. \end{equation}

The measure $\Phi _ v$ equals $\mathrm{MA}_\omega (v)$ off $S$. Every closed positive power of degree less than $n$ in this expansion has coefficient measures vanishing on $S$, by corollary 1.13.2. Thus only the top closed power contributes to $\mathbf1_ S\Phi _ v$. This identifies $\mathbf1_ S\Phi _ v$ with $\mathbf1_ S(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v+q))^ n_{\mathrm{full}}$ and proves independence of $q$. These assertions are local on each chart and do not require a single chart containing $S$. Finally, $J(v)\leq v_+(\omega )$, since every truncation $v_ k$ has mass at most $v_+(\omega )$.

Step 3: convergence of the lower-degree powers. We need local trace bounds for the closed powers of $p_ m$ that are uniform in $m$. Fix a relatively compact coordinate neighborhood and write $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$. Choose $B>0$ with $B\kappa \geq \omega $ there, and $A>1$ with $A\omega -B\kappa \geq \kappa $. For $p_{m,k}=\max (p_ m,-k)$, the closed current $T_{m,k}=B\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_{m,k}$ is positive and

\[ Q_{m,k}:=A\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_{m,k}\geq T_{m,k}+\kappa . \]

Expanding $Q_{m,k}^ n$ with the smooth positive difference $Q_{m,k}-T_{m,k}\geq \kappa $ gives, for $1\leq j\leq n$,

\[ \binom nj T_{m,k}^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} \leq Q_{m,k}^ n, \qquad \int _ XQ_{m,k}^ n\leq A^ n v_+(\omega ). \]

The last bound holds because $Q_{m,k}=A(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p_{m,k}/A))$ and $p_{m,k}/A$ is a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function. Letting $k\to \infty $ gives trace bounds, independent of $m$, for every full closed power of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p_ m+B|z|^2)$.

For $j<n$, every subsequential weak limit of these closed positive $(j,j)$-currents puts no mass on $S$, again by corollary 1.13.2. Off $S$ the bounded capacity convergence identifies the limit with the corresponding full power of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p+B|z|^2)$, which also puts no mass on $S$. Hence the limit is unique. Expanding back in the smooth correction and using a finite coordinate cover yields

\[ W_ j(p_ m)\longrightarrow W_ j(p),\qquad 1\leq j<n. \]

Hence (30.5) gives $J(p_ m)\to J(p)$.

Step 4: the residual measures. Let $R=\mathbf1_ S\Phi _ u$. Since $p_ m-u$ is bounded, the equality case of lemma 1.6.10, applied on each chart after a common smooth correction, gives $\mathbf1_ S\Phi _{p_ m}=R$ for every $m$. The total masses $J(p_ m)$ are bounded by $v_+(\omega )$, so along any sequence $m_ i\to \infty $ a subsequence of $\Phi _{p_{m_ i}}$ converges weakly to a measure $\eta $. Since $\Phi _{p_ m}-R$ is positive, so is $\eta -R$. Off $S$, $\eta $ equals $\mathrm{MA}_\omega (p)$, by the bounded convergence of Step 1, and its total mass is $\lim _ mJ(p_ m)=J(p)$ by Step 3. The measure $\Phi _ p$ has the same total mass and the same restriction to $X\setminus S$, so

\[ \Phi _ p(S)=\eta (S)\geq R(S). \]

On the other hand, $u\leq p$, so the inequality in lemma 1.6.10 gives $\mathbf1_ S\Phi _ p\leq R$ on each chart. Together with the previous inequality, this forces $\Phi _ p(S)=R(S)$, hence $\mathbf1_ S\Phi _ p=R$, which is the asserted equality of residual measures. It also gives $\mathbf1_ S\eta =R$, since $\eta -R\geq 0$ and $\eta (S)=R(S)$. As $\eta $ and $\Phi _ p$ also agree off $S$, we conclude that $\eta =\Phi _ p$. Every weak subsequential limit of $\Phi _{p_ m}$ is therefore $\Phi _ p$, which proves the convergence $\Phi _{p_ m}\to \Phi _ p$. $\square$
