Chapter 10 · Hermitian geometry, torsion, and Gauduchon duality · Tag B6CA5669E402

Gaud volume mass extrema

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 26.14.3

Theorem 26.14.3. One has

\[ \inf _{\mu \in \mathscr M}\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )=v_-(\omega ), \qquad \sup _{\mu \in \mathscr M}\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )=v_+(\omega ), \]

where the supremum may be infinite. Moreover,

26.89
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-lower-mass-formula} v_-(\omega )^{1/n} =\inf _{\gamma \text{ Gauduchon}} \frac{\displaystyle \int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}}{\displaystyle \left(\int _ X\gamma ^ n\right)^{(n-1)/n}}. \end{equation}

Proof. For each $\mu \in \mathscr M$, the smooth normalized equation realizes $\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )$ as the mass $\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$ of a smooth potential $v$ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v>0$. Conversely, every such potential realizes its mass as $\mathcal V_{\mu _ v}(\omega )$ for the measure $\mu _ v$ in the proof of proposition 26.14.2. Thus the set of volumes $\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )$, $\mu \in \mathscr M$, coincides with the set of these smooth masses.

It remains to show that the smooth masses have the same extrema as the masses of bounded potentials, which define $v_\pm (\omega )$. If $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty $, theorem 1.11.1 gives uniformly bounded smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u_ j$ decreasing to $u$. Their Monge–Ampère measures converge by the monotone continuity of bounded products, and so do their total masses, since $X$ is compact. Every bounded mass is therefore a limit of smooth strictly positive masses. Conversely, smooth potentials are bounded. This proves the first two identities; no uniform bound on the masses is needed.

For the last formula, take $n$th roots and interchange the infima over $\mu $ and over $\gamma $ in theorem 26.1.3. For a fixed Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, Hölder's inequality and $\int _ X\mu =1$ give

\[ D_\mu (\gamma ) \leq \left(\int _ X\gamma ^ n\right)^{(n-1)/n}, \]

with equality for the smooth probability measure $\mu =\gamma ^ n/\int _ X\gamma ^ n$. The numerator is positive and does not depend on $\mu $, so the infimum of the quotient over $\mu $ is obtained by maximizing the denominator. This gives (26.89). For $n=1$ both denominators equal one, and the same argument applies. $\square$
