27.18 Three monomial bands from a multiplier-ideal selection435C250083A1

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.18 Three monomial bands from a multiplier-ideal selection

For an isolated logarithmic pole of coefficient one on a complex surface, the multiplier ideal in degree $m$ requires vanishing of order $m-1$, whereas domination by the pole would require order $m$. At the two base points of the pencil below, the multiplier-ideal conditions select three neighboring bands of monomials. All three contribute to the limit of the kernel measures, even though the competitor classes defined by multiplier ideals and by domination coincide.

Let $X=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, and let $L=\mathcal O(1,1)$ carry its product Fubini–Study metric $h$, with $\theta =c_1(L,h)$. Let $[T_ j:Z_ j]$ be homogeneous coordinates on the $j$th factor, write $z_ j=Z_ j/T_ j$, and set

\[ \psi =\max \{ \log |T_1T_2|_ h^2,\log |Z_1Z_2|_ h^2\} , \qquad p_+=(0,\infty ),\quad p_-=(\infty ,0). \]

The two sections $T_1T_2$ and $Z_1Z_2$ of $L$ vanish simultaneously only at $p_\pm $, so these are the only poles of $\psi $. In local frames the potentials of $\psi $ are maxima of logarithms of squared moduli of holomorphic functions, so $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. On the torus $(\mathbb C^*)^2$ put

\[ x_ j=\log |z_ j|^2,\qquad s=x_1+x_2,\qquad \varphi _ X=\log (1+\mathrm e^{x_1})+\log (1+\mathrm e^{x_2}). \]

Then $\psi =\max (0,s)-\varphi _ X$.

In this model the norm is computed on the compact set $K=\{ x\in [-1,2]^2\} \subset (\mathbb C^*)^2$, which has nonempty interior. On $K$ let

\[ w=\frac{x_1^2+x_2^2}{2}-\varphi _ X, \qquad \mathrm d\nu =\frac19\, \mathrm dx_1\, \mathrm dx_2\, \mathrm d\tau _1\, \mathrm d\tau _2, \]

where $\mathrm d\tau _ j$ is the Haar probability measure in the angle of $z_ j$. Thus $\nu $ is a probability measure supported on $K$, not a smooth positive volume form on $X$. With $\psi $ as selecting potential, set

\[ \begin{aligned} V_ m& =H^0(X,L^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\psi )), & N_ m& =\dim V_ m,\\ \| \sigma \| _ m^2& =\int _ K|\sigma |_{h^ m}^2\mathrm e^{-mw}\, \mathrm d\nu , & \mathcal B_ m& =\sum _ j|\sigma _{m,j}|_{h^ m}^2, \end{aligned} \]

where $(\sigma _{m,j})$ is an orthonormal basis. The norm is positive definite by the identity theorem on the interior of $K$. As in section 27.1, we use $U_ m=m^{-1}\log \mathcal B_ m$ on $X$ and $B_ m=\mathrm e^{-mw}\mathcal B_ m$ on $K$. Only the values of $w$ on $K$ enter the norm and the envelope.

Lemma 27.18.1. For every $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$,

\[ v\preceq _{\mathcal I}\psi \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad v\leq \psi +C_ v \quad \hbox{for some finite }C_ v, \]

where $\preceq _{\mathcal I}$ is the multiplier-ideal order of section 1.12. Consequently, for every obstacle, the envelopes defined with these two conditions agree. At either pole, for every real $t>0$,

\[ \mathcal I(t\psi )=\mathfrak m_{p_\pm }^{\max \{ \lfloor t\rfloor -1,0\} }. \]

Away from the poles, $\mathcal I(t\psi )$ is the full structure sheaf.

Proof. Near $p_+$ use the coordinates $\zeta _1=z_1$, $\zeta _2=1/z_2$ and the frame $T_1Z_2$. In this frame the two pencil sections $T_1T_2$ and $Z_1Z_2$ have coefficients $\zeta _2$ and $\zeta _1$, so

\[ \psi =\log \max (|\zeta _1|^2,|\zeta _2|^2) -\log (1+|\zeta _1|^2)-\log (1+|\zeta _2|^2) =\log |\zeta |^2+O(1). \]

The same calculation applies at $p_-$. The formula for the ideals is lemma 1.16.1 with normal dimension two and coefficient one, including the case of equality in the integrability condition.

Now suppose that $v\preceq _{\mathcal I}\psi $. By theorem 1.16.3, the Lelong numbers satisfy $\nu (v,p_\pm )\geq 1$; here $\nu (v,p)$ is the Lelong number of section 1.13, not the measure $\nu $. Let $\phi $ be the plurisubharmonic local potential of $v$ in a frame near $p_\pm $, and consider its radial maximum

\[ M(a)=\sup _{|\zeta |\leq \mathrm e^{a/2}}\phi (\zeta ) \]

inside a fixed coordinate ball. This function is finite and convex: by the three-circles theorem, the corresponding maximum on each complex line through the pole is convex, and $M$ is the supremum of these functions; lines on which $\phi $ is identically $-\infty $ do not affect the supremum. The limiting slope of $M$ as $a\to -\infty $ is the Lelong number. For $a<a_0$, convexity therefore gives

\[ M(a)\leq M(a_0)+\nu (v,p_\pm )(a-a_0)\leq a+C. \]

Consequently $v\leq \psi +C$ near both poles. After $C$ is increased, the same bound holds on the compact complement, because $v$ is bounded above and $\psi $ is finite and continuous there. Conversely, domination $v\leq \psi +C$ implies the inclusions of multiplier ideals at all weights, by the definition through integrability. Thus the two competitor classes coincide before any obstacle is imposed. $\square$

The pencil also describes the competitors that are not torus-invariant. Let $\omega _{\rm FS}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^1$, normalized to have integral one.

Lemma 27.18.2. On $X\setminus \{ p_+,p_-\} $ set

\[ f=[T_1T_2:Z_1Z_2],\qquad \psi _{\rm FS}=\log (|T_1T_2|_ h^2+|Z_1Z_2|_ h^2). \]

Every $v$ in the class of lemma 27.18.1 has the form

\[ v=\psi _{\rm FS}+u\circ f, \qquad u\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^1,\omega _{\rm FS}), \]

on $X\setminus \{ p_+,p_-\} $, and $v(p_\pm )=-\infty $. Conversely, for every such $u$, the function $\psi _{\rm FS}+u\circ f$, extended by $-\infty $ at the two poles, belongs to that class.

Proof. For $\xi \in \mathbb C^*$, the curve of the pencil

\[ C_\xi =\{ Z_1Z_2=\xi T_1T_2\} \]

is a smooth rational curve of bidegree $(1,1)$ through both poles, and its $\theta $-degree is two. The restriction of $v$ to this connected curve is either identically $-\infty $ or $\theta |_{C_\xi }$-plurisubharmonic. In the latter case, its curvature measure has total mass two by Stokes' theorem. The local upper bounds of lemma 27.18.1 give Lelong numbers at least one at the two poles, hence, by the identification of Lelong numbers with atoms in lemma 1.16.11, atoms of mass at least one there. These atoms exhaust the measure. The curvature of $\psi |_{C_\xi }$ consists of the same two unit atoms. Thus $v-\psi $ is harmonic, in the sense of distributions, on the compact curve $C_\xi $, and therefore constant.

On the torus use the holomorphic coordinates $(z_1,\xi =z_1z_2)$. Local integrability and Fubini's theorem show that, for almost every $\xi $, the restriction of $v$ to $C_\xi $ is not identically $-\infty $; by the preceding paragraph it is then finite off the poles. Hence the restriction of $v$ to the transverse curve $S=\{ z_1=1\} \simeq \mathbb P^1$ is not identically $-\infty $. Put $u=v|_ S+\log 2$. Since $\theta |_ S=\omega _{\rm FS}$ and $\psi _{\rm FS}|_ S=-\log 2$, the constants on the curves $C_\xi $ give the asserted identity almost everywhere on the torus. Both sides have plurisubharmonic local potentials, so they agree everywhere off the poles. The logarithmic upper bounds give the value $-\infty $ at the poles.

Conversely, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\psi _{\rm FS}+u\circ f)=f^*(\omega _{\rm FS}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\geq 0$ off the poles. In a chart at a pole, the local potential is bounded above by $\log (|\zeta _1|^2+|\zeta _2|^2)+C$, since $u$ is bounded above. It therefore extends across the pole as a plurisubharmonic function with value $-\infty $ there, which gives a global $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function dominated by $\psi +C$. $\square$

Theorem 27.18.3. For these $K$ and $w$, the partial envelope (27.4) is

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta ,K}[\psi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)& =P=g(s)-\varphi _ X,\\ g(s)& =\sup _{0\leq t\leq 1}(ts-t^2) =\begin{cases} 0,& s\leq 0,\\ s^2/4,& 0\leq s\leq 2,\\ s-1,& s\geq 2. \end{cases}\end{aligned} \]

Here $P$ is given by this formula on the torus and extends to a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ with $P(p_\pm )=-\infty $ and $\psi -1\leq P\leq \psi $.

Proof. For an arbitrary competitor $v$, lemma 27.18.2 gives, in terms of the affine coordinate $\xi $ of $\mathbb P^1$,

\[ \chi (\xi )=\log (1+|\xi |^2)+u(\xi ), \qquad v=\chi (z_1z_2)-\varphi _ X. \]

On the interior of $K$, the obstacle condition, which holds quasi-everywhere, becomes an inequality everywhere, by the submean inequality and the continuity of $w+\varphi _ X$. Evaluating at $x_1=x_2=s/2$, with $s=\log |\xi |^2$, gives

\[ \chi (\xi )\leq s^2/4\quad (-2<s<4) \]

for every argument of $\xi $. At $s=0$ this bounds $\chi $ by zero on $|\xi |=1$, and the maximum principle for the subharmonic function $\chi $ bounds it by zero on the whole disk $|\xi |\leq 1$. At infinity use the local potential

\[ \chi _\infty (\eta )=\chi (1/\eta )+\log |\eta |^2. \]

The obstacle at $s=2$ bounds it by $-1$ on $|\eta |=\mathrm e^{-1}$, and the same maximum principle gives $\chi (\xi )\leq s-1$ for $s\geq 2$. Together with the obstacle for $0\leq s\leq 2$, this proves $\chi \leq g$, without any invariance assumption on the competitor.

Conversely, $g$ is convex with slopes in $[0,1]$, so $g(\log |\xi |^2)-\log (1+|\xi |^2)$ is an $\omega _{\rm FS}$-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^1$. By lemma 27.18.2, it defines the global function $P$. Directly,

\[ \max (0,s)-1\leq g(s)\leq \max (0,s),\qquad g(s)\leq s^2/4\leq (x_1^2+x_2^2)/2. \]

Thus $P$ belongs to the competitor class of lemma 27.18.1 and satisfies the obstacle condition everywhere on $K$. Since it dominates every competitor, it is the envelope, including its values $-\infty $ at the poles. $\square$

Proposition 27.18.4. For every $m\geq 1$ the selected space is

\[ V_ m=\operatorname {span}\{ Z_1^ aT_1^{m-a}Z_2^ bT_2^{m-b}: 0\leq a,b\leq m,\ |a-b|\leq 1\} , \qquad N_ m=3m+1. \]

In particular, $N_ m$ grows linearly in $m$. These spaces are not multiplicative. If the sections were instead required to satisfy $|\sigma |_{h^ m}^2\leq C\mathrm e^{m\psi }$, only the monomials with $a=b$ would remain. The measure $\nu $ is Bernstein–Markov for $(K,w)$.

Proof. At $p_+$ the monomial with exponents $(a,b)$ has local exponents $(a,m-b)$, and at $p_-$ it has local exponents $(m-a,b)$. By lemma 27.18.1, both total orders must be at least $m-1$. Distinct exponent pairs cannot cancel, so these conditions select exactly the monomials with $|a-b|\leq 1$. The three bands $a=b$, $a=b+1$ and $a=b-1$ have $m+1$, $m$ and $m$ elements. The bound $|\sigma |_{h^ m}^2\leq C\mathrm e^{m\psi }$ would require total order $m$ at both poles and leave only the diagonal. The product of two sections with offset $a-b=1$ has offset two and lies outside the selected space of the sum degree; such products fail for every pair of positive degrees. Thus the linear growth of $N_ m$ is not the growth of a graded section ring.

Integration over the angles makes all global monomials orthogonal, including those outside the selected space. For $t\in [0,1]$ set

\[ I_ m(t)=\int _{-1}^2\mathrm e^{m(tx-x^2/2)}\, \mathrm dx =\mathrm e^{mt^2/2}J_ m(t),\qquad J_ m(t)=\int _{-1}^2\mathrm e^{-m(x-t)^2/2}\, \mathrm dx. \]

The squared norm of the monomial with exponents $(a,b)$ is

27.133
\begin{equation} A_ m(a,b)=\frac19 I_ m(a/m)I_ m(b/m). \end{equation}

With $c=\int _{-1}^1\mathrm e^{-y^2/2}\, \mathrm dy$, uniformly in $t\in [0,1]$,

\[ \frac c{\sqrt m}\leq J_ m(t)\leq \sqrt{\frac{2\pi }m},\qquad J_ m(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }m} \bigl (1+O(m^{-1/2}\mathrm e^{-m/2})\bigr ). \]

For the lower bound, integrate over the interval of radius $m^{-1/2}$ centered at $t$; for the last assertion, note that each of the two omitted Gaussian tails is at most $\mathrm e^{-m/2}/m$. Hence

27.134
\begin{equation} \frac{c^2}{9m}\mathrm e^{(a^2+b^2)/(2m)} \leq A_ m(a,b)\leq \frac{2\pi }{9m}\mathrm e^{(a^2+b^2)/(2m)}. \end{equation}

For $x\in [-1,2]^2$, completing squares bounds the weighted contribution of each normalized monomial by $9m/c^2$. Summing over all $(m+1)^2$ global monomials and using the evaluation inequality gives

\[ \sup _ K|\sigma |_{h^ m}\mathrm e^{-mw/2} \leq C m^{3/2}\| \sigma \| _ m \quad (\sigma \in H^0(X,L^ m)). \]

This polynomial bound proves the Bernstein–Markov property. On the selected space the same argument gives the factor $Cm$. $\square$

Theorem 27.18.5. With values in $[-\infty ,\infty )$ at the poles, the potentials satisfy

27.135
\begin{equation} U_ m\geq P+\frac{\log (9m/(2\pi ))}{m}-\frac1{4m^2}. \end{equation}

They converge to $P$ locally uniformly on $X\setminus \{ p_+,p_-\} $, in $L^ p$ with respect to a smooth volume form on $X$ for every finite $p$, at the rate $O(\log m/m)$, and pointwise on $X$ with values in $[-\infty ,\infty )$. The currents $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ m$ converge weakly to $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P$. Moreover, $\mathrm e^{U_ m}\to \mathrm e^ P$ uniformly on $X$, although for each fixed $m\geq 2$ the difference $U_ m-P$ is unbounded above near either pole.

Define a probability measure $\mu $ by

\[ \int _ X f\, \mathrm d\mu =\int _0^1\int f(\mathrm e^{t/2}\tau _1,\mathrm e^{t/2}\tau _2) \, \mathrm d\tau _1\, \mathrm d\tau _2\, \mathrm dt. \]

Then, against every continuous test function on $X$,

\[ N_ m^{-1}B_ m\nu \rightharpoonup \mu ,\qquad m^{-1}B_ m\nu \rightharpoonup 3\mu . \]

The measures $2m^{-2}B_ m\nu $, normalized by the ambient dimension, tend to zero in total variation. The envelope satisfies $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P)^2\rangle =0$.

Proof. The diagonal monomials belong to $V_ m$. The upper bound for their norms in (27.134) gives

\[ \mathcal B_ m\geq \frac{9m}{2\pi }\mathrm e^{-m\varphi _ X} \max _{0\leq j\leq m}\mathrm e^{js-j^2/m}. \]

For $0\leq s\leq 2$, the grid point $j/m$ nearest to $s/2$ loses at most $1/(4m^2)$ compared with $g(s)$; outside this interval the maximizing endpoint lies on the grid. This proves (27.135) on the torus.

For a monomial with offset one, $(a,b)=(j+1,j)$, the lower bound in (27.134) shows that the exponent of its normalized contribution, apart from $-m\varphi _ X$ and constants, is at most both

\[ js-j^2/m+x_1,\qquad (j+1)s-(j+1)^2/m+1-x_2. \]

The band with $(a,b)=(j,j+1)$ is obtained by interchanging the indices. Put

\[ R(x)=1+\min (\mathrm e^{x_1},\mathrm e^{1-x_2}) +\min (\mathrm e^{x_2},\mathrm e^{1-x_1}). \]

The lower bound for the norms and the count of monomials give

27.136
\begin{equation} U_ m\leq P+\frac{C+2\log (m+1)+\log R(x)}m. \end{equation}

The function $R$ extends continuously across the coordinate divisors away from the poles. In a chart at $p_+$, put $A=|\zeta _1|^2$ and $B=|\zeta _2|^2$. The possibly large term of $R$ satisfies

\[ \min (1/B,\mathrm e/A)\leq (\mathrm e+1)/(A+B), \]

and the other term is bounded; the same holds at $p_-$. Thus $R\leq C(1+r^{-2})$ near either pole, where $r=|\zeta |$ is the local radius, and $\log R$ belongs to $L^ p$ for every finite $p$ with respect to smooth volume forms.

For $m\geq 2$, all selected sections vanish at the poles. The two diagonal sections $T_1^ mT_2^ m$ and $Z_1^ mZ_2^ m$ have no other common zeros, so $\mathcal B_ m>0$ off the poles. Away from the poles, $P$ and $U_ m$ are continuous, so both inequalities extend from the torus to $X\setminus \{ p_+,p_-\} $. They give the asserted local uniform convergence and convergence in $L^ p$, and differentiation of the $L^1$ limit in the sense of distributions gives the weak convergence of the currents. At the poles both $U_ m$ and $P$ equal $-\infty $ for $m\geq 2$, which also proves the pointwise statement on $X$.

For a fixed $m\geq 2$, the lowest-order terms of the selected sections at a pole include every binary monomial of degree $m-1$, and the sum of their squared moduli is comparable to $r^{2(m-1)}$ in all directions. Hence

\[ U_ m=(1-1/m)\log r^2+O_ m(1),\qquad P=\log r^2+O(1), \]

so the difference $U_ m-P$ tends to $+\infty $ near each pole. Nevertheless, (27.136) gives, uniformly in $m\geq 2$ and $r<1$,

\[ \mathrm e^{U_ m}\leq Cr^{2-2/m}\leq Cr,\qquad \mathrm e^ P\leq Cr^2. \]

Combined with uniform convergence on compact subsets away from the poles, these bounds give uniform convergence of $\mathrm e^{U_ m}$.

The contribution of each normalized monomial to $B_ m\nu $ is exactly the product of two truncated Gaussian probability measures in $x_1,x_2$, centered at $(a/m,b/m)$, with the Haar probability measures in the angles. Completing squares in (27.133) and using its uniform bounds proves concentration at these centers, uniformly over all three bands; for example, the probability of $|x_1-a/m|\geq \eta $ is at most $C_\eta \sqrt m\, \mathrm e^{-m\eta ^2/2}$. Uniform continuity of a test function then reduces the sum to three lattice Riemann sums. Each band contains asymptotically one third of the $N_ m$ monomials, and in each band the centers $(a/m,b/m)$ approach the diagonal point $(t,t)$, $t\in [0,1]$. This proves both positive limits. The total mass of $2m^{-2}B_ m\nu $ is $2(3m+1)/m^2\to 0$, which proves the convergence to zero in total variation.

Off the poles, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P$ is the pullback by $f$ of the current on $\mathbb P^1$ defined by $g$. The function $g$ is $C^{1,1}$, and the local complex Hessian of $g(s)$ has rank at most one. Smooth convex approximations and the product of locally bounded potentials show that its square vanishes, as in the Hessian argument of section 27.16. The non-pluripolar product assigns no mass to the two poles, so the global square is zero. In particular, the positive-mass hypothesis of theorem 27.6.3 fails in this model. $\square$

Parametrize the real three-dimensional shell $S$ carrying $\mu $ by $\Psi (t,\tau _1,\tau _2)=(\mathrm e^{t/2}\tau _1,\mathrm e^{t/2}\tau _2)$, $0<t<1$. For a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$, which need be neither closed nor torus-invariant, write $\Psi ^*\mathrm d\operatorname {vol}_{\omega ,S}=\mathcal J_\omega \, \mathrm dt\, \mathrm d\tau _1\, \mathrm d\tau _2$, as in section 27.16. Since the boundary of $S$ has zero mass, the densities of $\mu $ and $3\mu $ with respect to $\mathrm d\operatorname {vol}_{\omega ,S}$ are $1/(\mathcal J_\omega \circ \Psi ^{-1})$ and $3/(\mathcal J_\omega \circ \Psi ^{-1})$. The mixed measure $\langle \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P\rangle \wedge \omega $ has a locally bounded density off the poles and gives $S$ zero mass, so it cannot reproduce the positive limit of the kernel measures.
