Proposition 16.6.756136A0B71CE1

Proposition 16.6.75. Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$, with a Hermitian form $\omega $ satisfying

\[ 0<v_-(\omega )\leq v_+(\omega )<+\infty . \]

Fix $H,C,M$ from proposition 16.6.71. For $0<t<1/C$ with $Mt^ n<v_-(\omega )$, the normalized ordinary model $p_ t=P_\omega [tH]$ satisfies

\[ \begin{split} 0<v_-(\omega )-Mt^ n & \leq \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\omega (p_ t) \leq \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\omega (p_ t)\\ & \leq v_+(\omega )-Mt^ n. \end{split} \]

Its first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification, all its multiplier ideals are trivial, and

\[ d_\omega (p_ t,0)\geq Mt^ n>0,\qquad \delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(p_ t,0)=0 \]

for every family of modifications and every choice of Gauduchon forms. Thus $p_ t$ is not an ideal model, and it belongs to the closure of the analytic models for the polar comparison. For any $0<\delta <v_-(\omega )$, all sufficiently small positive parameters give $p_ t,0\in \mathcal M_\delta (\omega )$.

In particular, these conclusions apply to every Hermitian form on every connected compact complex surface.

Proof. Taking the infimum and the supremum in proposition 16.6.73 gives the bounds for the singularity volumes of $tH$. The lower volume is positive, so by theorem 30.3.4 ordinary projection preserves both volumes. Since $tH\leq p_ t\leq 0$ and $\sup _ XtH=0$, the model is normalized.

The type of zero consists of the bounded potentials, so its upper volume is $v_+(\omega )$. Since $p_ t\leq 0$, (30.13) gives $d_\omega (p_ t,0)=v_+(\omega )-\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\omega (p_ t)\geq Mt^ n$. In particular $p_ t\ne 0$.

The part of the proof of proposition 16.6.69 that uses supports and Lelong numbers applies to this model: its pole set lies in $S$, its polar restriction is closed and positive, and every exceptional divisor whose image lies in $S$ maps to a point. Since the curve potential $g$ has zero Lelong numbers, every divisorial coefficient vanishes. The lower bound $p_ t\geq tH$ and Skoda's integrability theorem give trivial multiplier ideals at all weights. Hence the ideal envelope of $p_ t$ is the zero model, while $p_ t\ne 0$. All first polar comparisons with zero vanish, which proves that $p_ t$ is not an ideal model and lies in the closure of the analytic models.

Given $0<\delta <v_-(\omega )$, choose $t_0<1/C$ with $Mt_0^ n<v_-(\omega )-\delta $. The lower bound for the volume then puts zero and every $p_ t$ with $0<t\leq t_0$ in $\mathcal M_\delta (\omega )$.

On a compact complex surface, a Gauduchon form has finite upper mass by theorem 4.1.2, theorem 2.2.2. By the metric independence in proposition 6.5.2, every Hermitian form then has finite upper mass, and corollary 26.18.5 gives positive lower mass. $\square$
