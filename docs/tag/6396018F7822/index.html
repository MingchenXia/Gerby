Proposition 16.6.716396018F7822

Proposition 16.6.71. Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$, with Hermitian form $\omega $. Place the polydisk of lemma 16.6.67 in a coordinate chart. Choose a smooth function $0\leq \chi \leq 1$, compactly supported there and equal to one near $S=K^ n$, and extend $H=\chi h$ and $H_ L=\chi h_ L$ by zero. There is $C>0$ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H\geq -C\omega $ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L\geq -C\omega $ for all sufficiently large $L$. Put $A=\int _ X\omega ^ n$. The limits

\[ c_ j=\lim _{L\to \infty }\int _ X H_ L(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^{j-1}\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-j}), \qquad 1\leq j<n, \]

exist and are finite, and, for $0<t<1/C$,

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\omega (tH) =A+\sum _{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_ jt^ j-Mt^ n. \]

If $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, this mass is $A-Mt^ n$. On a surface the formula is

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\omega (tH) =A+2t\int _ X H\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega -Mt^2. \]

Proof. Outside a fixed neighborhood of $S$, the truncations $h_ L$ eventually agree with $h$ on every compact set, in particular on the supports of the derivatives of $\chi $. The cutoff calculation in proposition 16.6.69 therefore gives a lower bound $-C\omega $ for $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H$, uniform in $L$.

Let $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ on the coordinate polydisk $D$, and choose $B>0$ so that $T_ L=B\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L$ is positive and closed on $D$. The potentials of $T_ L$ are locally bounded. Since $H_ L$ has compact support, integration by parts for bounded potentials gives, for $1\leq j\leq n$,

\[ \int _ D T_ L^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} =B^ j\int _ D\kappa ^ n. \]

Indeed, every nonconstant term of the binomial expansion is the complex Hessian of a compactly supported current built from bounded products, wedged with a closed power of $\kappa $. Thus the traces in all degrees are bounded independently of $L$.

For $j<n$, every subsequential weak limit of $T_ L^ j$ has coefficient measures vanishing on $S$, by corollary 1.13.2. Off $S$, local boundedness and eventual equality identify it with $(B\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ j$. These two properties determine the limit uniquely. Expanding $(T_ L-B\kappa )^ j$ therefore shows that $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^ j$ converges weakly to a finite signed current $W_ j$ with no coefficient mass on $S$. It vanishes near $\partial D$ and extends by zero to $X$. Integration by parts for bounded potentials gives

\[ \int _ X(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^ j\wedge \omega ^{n-j} =\int _ X H_ L(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^{j-1} \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-j}). \]

The left side converges to $\int _ XW_ j\wedge \omega ^{n-j}$, which proves that $c_ j$ exists and is finite.

Fix $0<t<1/C$. The positive measures $(\omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^ n$ have uniformly bounded mass, by their local expansion in powers of $T_ L$ and smooth forms. Their total masses satisfy

\[ \int _ X(\omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^ n =A+\sum _{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj t^ j \int _ X(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^ j\wedge \omega ^{n-j}, \]

since the top power $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ L)^ n$ integrates to zero by Stokes' theorem for bounded potentials. Let $\eta $ be a weak subsequential limit. Its total mass is $A+\sum _{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj c_ jt^ j$.

Near $S$, where $\chi =1$, expand $(\omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ L)^ n$. All the limits of lower degree give $S$ zero mass, while the top-degree measures have mass $M$ and supports converging to $S$, by lemma 16.6.67. Hence $\eta (S)=Mt^ n$. Off $S$, eventual equality with the locally bounded function $tH$ identifies $\eta $ with the non-pluripolar measure of $tH$. Subtracting the mass on $S$ proves the formula.

If $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ q)=0$ for $1\leq q<n$, every $c_ j$ is zero. For $n=2$, dominated $L^1$ convergence $H_ L\to H$ gives $c_1=\int _ XH\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $, which proves the formula on surfaces. These formulas give the masses of the potentials $tH$ themselves. Passing to singularity volumes in a nonclosed background requires control of the masses of all potentials ordinarily equivalent to $tH$. $\square$
