Finite curve factors

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.6.15 Finite curve factors

Keep $X$ and $C$ as above, but now allow $p:X\to C$ to have disconnected fibers. By 1.22.9, it has the component factorization

\[ X\xrightarrow {q}C'\xrightarrow {r}C, \qquad p=r\circ q, \]

where $q$ is surjective and holomorphic with connected fibers, $C'$ is a connected compact Riemann surface, and $r$ is finite and holomorphic of degree $d\geq 1$. Let $\beta $ be any smooth real form on $C$, put $A=\int _ C\beta $, and set $\sigma =r^*\beta $ and $\theta =p^*\beta =q^*\sigma $.

Proposition 16.6.51. The class $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is nonempty exactly when $A\geq 0$. For $A\geq 0$, the entire ordinary $\theta $-model space is isometric, using the $q$-fiber normalization, to the curve model space in background $\sigma $, whose area is $dA$. Its diameter is $2dA$; it has the analytic closure, ideal retraction, convergence and all-obstacle properties above. Every potential has zero extended top-degree measure and every ordinary type has zero upper and lower singularity volumes. At $A=0$ the model space is a smooth singleton.

If $\kappa _ q^\gamma $ and $\kappa _ p^\gamma $ are the constants defined by the pushforwards of $\gamma ^{n-1}$ to $C'$ and $C$, then

\[ \kappa _ p^\gamma =d\kappa _ q^\gamma . \]

For $A\geq 0$, normalizing the same polar-trace variation by $\kappa _ p^\gamma $ gives $d^{-1}$ times the $q$-normalized metric, with diameter $2A$. This rescaled metric is also independent of $\gamma $. For a model $U=v\circ q$ with polar measure $\mu _ v$,

\[ p_*\lambda _ U^\gamma =\kappa _ q^\gamma r_*\mu _ v. \]

Proof. Away from the finitely many critical values, $r$ is a covering with $d$ sheets. Change of variables on these sheets gives

\[ \int _{C'}r^*\alpha =d\int _ C\alpha \]

for every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha $; the branch points and their inverse images have smooth area zero. In particular $\int _{C'}\sigma =dA$. The envelope lemma 16.6.47, applied to $q$, gives nonemptiness exactly for $dA\geq 0$, equivalent to $A\geq 0$, and proves the zero-mass and zero-area assertions. In this nonempty case, apply 16.6.49, 16.6.50 to $q$ and the smooth background $\sigma $, which may vanish at branch points or be indefinite.

The Gauduchon trace lemma for $q$ gives $q_*(\gamma ^{n-1})=\kappa _ q^\gamma $. For a smooth test form $\alpha $ on $C$, the projection formula and the covering calculation yield

\[ \int _ Xp^*\alpha \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =\kappa _ q^\gamma \int _{C'}r^*\alpha =d\kappa _ q^\gamma \int _ C\alpha . \]

Thus $p_*(\gamma ^{n-1})$ is the constant $d\kappa _ q^\gamma $. The ratio of the two normalizations is $d^{-1}$, giving every metric assertion. Finally $q_*\lambda _ U^\gamma =\kappa _ q^\gamma \mu _ v$, so composing pushforwards proves the last identity. $\square$

Example 16.6.52. Let $X=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, let $q$ be its first projection and let $r:\mathbb P^1\to \mathbb P^1$ be $r([z_0:z_1])=[z_0^2:z_1^2]$. Put $p=r\circ q$ and $\theta =p^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$, with $\int _{\mathbb P^1}\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}=1$. There are two distinct analytic ideal $\theta $-models $U_+,U_-$ which do not descend through $p$. Their $q$-normalized polar distance is four and their $p$-normalized distance is two, yet

\[ p_*\lambda _{U_+}^\gamma =p_*\lambda _{U_-}^\gamma \]

for every Gauduchon form $\gamma $. Thus the pushforward of polar trace to the original base does not separate the models.

Proof. The finite map $r$ has degree two and is branched at zero and infinity. In the affine coordinate it is $r(z)=z^2$, so the smooth semipositive form $\sigma =r^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ has area two. For $s\in \{ 1,-1\} $, set

\[ v_ s(z)=2\log |z-s|^2-\log (1+|z|^4)-\log 8. \]

This extends smoothly across infinity and has curvature $\sigma +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ s=2\delta _ s$. The inequality $|z-s|^4\leq (|z|+1)^4\leq 8(1+|z|^4)$, with equality at $z=-s$, shows that its supremum is zero. The model of $2\delta _ s$ from proposition 30.13.1 has precisely this total curvature, since its polar measure already has the full mass two. Harmonic uniqueness and normalization identify it with $v_ s$. Write $v_+=v_1$ and $v_-=v_{-1}$. Both have analytic singularities, hence are ideal by proposition 16.2.7. The model pullback bijection makes $U_\pm =v_\pm \circ q$ analytic ideal $\theta $-models.

The function $U_+$ is $-\infty $ on $q^{-1}(1)$ and finite on $q^{-1}(-1)$, although both divisors are mapped by $p$ to the same base point $1$. It therefore cannot descend through $p$; the same argument with the divisors exchanged applies to $U_-$. The variation of $2\delta _1-2\delta _{-1}$ is four. The finite factor proposition gives the two distances and

\[ p_*\lambda _{U_\pm }^\gamma =2\kappa _ q^\gamma \delta _1. \]

The upstairs polar traces are distinct, while this common pushforward loses which component carries the pole. $\square$
