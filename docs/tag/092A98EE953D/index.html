Theorem 14.34.6092A98EE953D

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 14.34.6

Theorem 14.34.6. Let $F_ j$, $j\geq 1$, be smooth strictly positive probability densities on $X_0$ with respect to $\mathrm dV_0=V_0^{-1}h_0^ m$, whose normalization scalars on $X_0$, relative to $h_0$ and $\mathrm dV_0$, satisfy $c_{h_0}(F_ j)\leq 2^{-2j}/C$. There are smooth strictly positive probability densities $g_ j$ on $Y$ with respect to $\kappa ^ k$ such that the probability densities

\[ f_ j=RF_ jg_ j,\qquad f=\sum _{j\geq 1}2^{-j}f_ j \]

on $X$ satisfy $c_ H(f_ j)\leq 2^{-2j}$ and $b_ H(f)=0$. The function $f$ is finite everywhere, smooth off the pole fiber $X_0\times \{ o\} $, has integral one and is bounded below by a positive constant. For every $\lambda >0$, the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\, \mathrm{d}V$ has an exponential subsolution and a greatest solution, of finite mass, and every solution is unbounded.

Proof. Put $w_ j=2^{-j}$ and $M_ j=\max _{X_0}F_ j$.

Step 1: a smooth baseline. Fix $C^ j$ norms $\| \cdot \| _{C^ j}$ on $X_0$, computed in a fixed finite atlas, so that they increase with $j$. Choose $0<\varepsilon _ j\leq 1/2$ with

\[ w_ j\varepsilon _ jM_ j\leq b_0 2^{-j-2},\qquad w_ j\varepsilon _ j\| F_ j\| _{C^ j}\leq 2^{-j}. \]

Then $E=\sum _ jw_ j\varepsilon _ jF_ j$ converges in every $C^ l$ norm, so $E$ is smooth on $X_0$, and $0<E\leq b_0/4$.

Step 2: bumps escaping to the pole. Choose a smooth function $\chi :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$, supported in $[0,1]$, positive on $(0,1)$ and flat at $0$ and $1$. Starting with $T_0=0$, choose inductively $T_ j\geq 2$ with $T_ j>T_{j-1}+2$ and

\[ Z_ j=\int _{T_ j}^{T_ j+1}\chi (L-T_ j) \mathrm e^{(L^\alpha -1)/j}W(L)\, \mathrm dL \geq 2w_ jM_ j. \]

This is possible because, by lemma 14.34.4 with $\tau =1/j$, the factor $\mathrm e^{(L^\alpha -1)/j}W(L)$ is uniformly large on sufficiently distant intervals. On the annulus $\{ T_ j\leq L\leq T_ j+1\} $ put $\beta _ j=\chi (L-T_ j)B_{1/j}/Z_ j$, extend $\beta _ j$ by zero, and set $g_ j=\varepsilon _ j+(1-\varepsilon _ j)\beta _ j$. Since $\chi $ is flat at the endpoints, $\beta _ j$ is smooth; it vanishes near $o$ and near the hyperplane $\{ L=1\} $. Its integral against $\kappa ^ k$ is one, so $g_ j$ is a smooth strictly positive probability density with respect to $\kappa ^ k$. Moreover,

\[ w_ jM_ j(1-\varepsilon _ j)\beta _ j\leq B_{1/j}/2. \]

Step 3: the scalars $c_ H(f_ j)$. By theorem 9.1.1 on $Y$, there is a smooth $\phi _ j$ with $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi _ j>0$ and $(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi _ j)^ k=g_ j\kappa ^ k$; the scalar is one because both sides have mass one. On $X_0$, the same theorem gives a smooth $v_ j$ with $h_0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j>0$ and $(h_0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ m=c_{h_0}(F_ j)F_ j\, \mathrm dV_0$. Since only one term of the binomial expansion survives for dimensional reasons,

\[ (H+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v_ j+\phi _ j))^ N =C c_{h_0}(F_ j)F_ jg_ j\mu =C c_{h_0}(F_ j)f_ j\, \mathrm dV. \]

Uniqueness of the scalar gives $c_ H(f_ j)=C c_{h_0}(F_ j)\leq 2^{-2j}$.

Step 4: properties of $f$. By Tonelli's theorem, $\int _ Xf\, \mathrm dV=\sum _ jw_ j=1$. The density of $f\, \mathrm{d}V$ with respect to $\mu $ is

\[ F=f/R=\sum _ jw_ jF_ jg_ j=E+\sum _ jw_ j(1-\varepsilon _ j)F_ j\beta _ j. \]

The annuli are disjoint and escape to the pole, so only finitely many bumps meet any compact subset of $Y\setminus \{ o\} $; hence $F$, and therefore $f$, is smooth off the pole fiber. On the pole fiber all bumps vanish and $F=E$, so $f$ is finite everywhere. Also $f\geq w_1f_1\geq \frac12\min _ Xf_1>0$. If $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(u)\geq af\, \mathrm{d}V$ with $a>0$, then $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(u)\geq aw_ jf_ j\, \mathrm{d}V$, and lemma 14.26.1 gives

\[ aw_ j\leq c_ H(f_ j)\leq 2^{-2j},\quad \text{that is,}\quad a\leq 2^{-j}, \quad \text{for every }j. \]

This is impossible, so $b_ H(f)=0$ by theorem 14.27.2.

Step 5: exponential subsolutions. Fix $\lambda >0$, and choose $J$ with $1/j\leq \lambda $ for $j\geq J$. Since $L^\alpha -1\geq 0$, we have $B_{1/j}\leq B_\lambda $ for these $j$. As the bumps have disjoint supports, Step 2 bounds their total contribution $\sum _{j\geq J}w_ j(1-\varepsilon _ j)F_ j\beta _ j$ by $B_\lambda /2$. The baseline satisfies $E\leq b_0/4\leq B_\lambda /4$, because $B_\lambda \geq q_\alpha \geq b_0$. The remaining finitely many bumps are bounded by the constant

\[ K_\lambda =\max _ Y\sum _{j<J}w_ j(1-\varepsilon _ j)M_ j\beta _ j, \]

and $K_\lambda \leq (K_\lambda /b_0)B_\lambda $. Hence $F\leq A_\lambda B_\lambda $ with $A_\lambda =3/4+K_\lambda /b_0$, and theorem 14.34.5 gives the exponential subsolution $u_\alpha +\lambda ^{-1}\log (CV_0/A_\lambda )$ and the greatest solution. Finally, a bounded solution $u$ would satisfy $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(u)\geq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \inf _ Xu}f\, \mathrm{d}V$, a positive scaled subsolution, contradicting $b_ H(f)=0$. Hence every solution is unbounded. $\square$
