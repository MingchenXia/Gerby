Negative-line-bundle quotients

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.16.3 Negative-line-bundle quotients

The simplest examples of the curvature criterion come from negative line bundles. Dividing the complement of the zero section of a negative line bundle over $\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$ by a dilation produces a principal elliptic bundle, and the logarithm of the fiber norm gives a connection form whose curvature is a multiple of a Kähler form on the base.

Take $B=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, let $\omega _1,\omega _2$ be the pullbacks of the Fubini–Study forms of the two factors, normalized to have integral one, and put

\[ \begin{gathered} \Omega =a\omega _1+b\omega _2,\quad a,b\in \mathbb N_{>0},\qquad L=\mathcal O(-a,-b),\\ X=L^\times /\langle v\mapsto \rho v\rangle ,\quad 0<\rho <1, \end{gathered} \]

where $L^\times $ is the total space of $L$ with the zero section removed. Equip $L$ with the Hermitian norm induced by the standard norms on the tautological bundles of the two factors. Let $r=\log \| v\| ^2$ and set $\eta =\partial r$.

Proposition 26.16.5. The quotient $X$ is a compact complex threefold, and the projection to $B$ is a principal elliptic fibration. For $g=\pi ^*\Omega +s\mathrm i\eta \wedge \bar\eta $, $s>0$,

26.96
\begin{equation} \label{eq:elliptic-line-lower-mass} v_-(g)=24\pi s ab\log (1/\rho ). \end{equation}

Every Hermitian form on $X$ satisfies the endpoint bound of theorem 26.16.3 over its full set of normalized Gauduchon currents. The manifold has $H^2(X,\mathbb R)\ne 0$ and admits no balanced or pluriclosed metric. In particular, it is not diffeomorphic to a Hopf threefold $(\mathbb C^3\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto \lambda z\rangle $, $0<|\lambda |<1$.

Proof. In product coordinates, the squared norm of the standard local frame of $L$ is $(1+|z_1|^2)^ a(1+|z_2|^2)^ b$. Therefore

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r=\pi ^*\Omega ,\qquad \mathrm d\eta =2\pi \mathrm i\pi ^*\Omega . \]

Dilation changes $r$ by a constant, so $\eta $ descends to $X$. The function $r$ changes by the nonzero constant $\log \rho ^2$ under the generator, so the action is free and properly discontinuous; the unit circle bundle, which is compact, times a fundamental interval for $r$ maps onto $X$, which is therefore compact. Each fiber is $\mathbb C^*/\rho ^{\mathbb Z}$. Its logarithmic coordinate has periods $\log \rho $ and $2\pi \mathrm i$, and the curvature $F=2\pi \mathrm i\Omega $ has real part $0$ and imaginary part $2\pi \Omega $; hence

\[ A_ E=4\pi \log (1/\rho ),\qquad Q=4\pi ^2\Omega ^2>0, \qquad \int _ B\Omega ^2=2ab. \]

Theorem 26.16.3 now gives (26.96) and the endpoint bound, and excludes pluriclosed metrics.

To prove $H^2(X,\mathbb R)\ne 0$, put $A=\eta +\bar\eta $ and $B_1=\mathrm i(\eta -\bar\eta )$. Then $\mathrm dA=0$ and $\mathrm dB_1=-4\pi \pi ^*\Omega $. Suppose that $\pi ^*(\omega _1-\omega _2)=\mathrm d\zeta $ for a real one-form $\zeta $. Averaging $\zeta $ over the compact group of elliptic translations, we may assume $\zeta $ invariant, so that

\[ \zeta =\pi ^*\xi +(\pi ^*f)A+(\pi ^*j)B_1 \]

for a one-form $\xi $ and functions $f,j$ on $B$. The components of $\mathrm d\zeta $ with one vertical and one horizontal direction must vanish, which gives $\mathrm df=\mathrm dj=0$. Thus

\[ \omega _1-\omega _2=\mathrm d\xi -4\pi j\Omega . \]

Integrating over the two rulings gives $1=-4\pi ja$ and $-1=-4\pi jb$, which is impossible because $a,b>0$. A Hopf threefold as above is diffeomorphic to $S^5\times S^1$, whose second real cohomology vanishes.

Finally, $\pi ^*\Omega =-\mathrm dB_1/(4\pi )$ is a nonzero semipositive exact form. Its integral against the closed positive square of a balanced metric would vanish by Stokes' theorem and be strictly positive by positivity. Hence no balanced metric exists. $\square$
