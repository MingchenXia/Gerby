Theorem 32.6.39A4A36BF299F

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 32.6.3

Theorem 32.6.3. Let $u,v\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $\lambda >0$. Suppose either $v\sim \varphi $, or there are a Hermitian metric $\omega $, a bounded $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ and a finite constant $A>0$ such that

\[ \mu _ v\leq A\, \mathrm{MA}_\omega (\psi ). \]

Then there is a unique $a\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ satisfying

\[ \mu _ a=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (a-u)}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (a-v)}\mu _ v. \]

It satisfies $a\leq \min (u,v)$, and hence $\mu _ a\leq \mu _ u+\mu _ v$. If both $u,v\sim \varphi $, then also $a\sim \varphi $.

Proof. The proof has four steps: two inputs of minimal singularity type, two inputs of the ordinary type of $\varphi $, one input of that type, and finally an input with a bounded carrier measure.

It suffices to prove the result for $\lambda =1$. Indeed, replace $(\theta ,\varphi ,u,v)$ by $(\lambda \theta ,\lambda \varphi ,\lambda u,\lambda v)$: envelopes and relative classes scale with the potentials, volumes and Monge–Ampère measures scale by $\lambda ^ n$, and a bounded-carrier domination remains one after changing its constant. Dividing the resulting solution by $\lambda $ gives the desired equation. Similarly, a common downward translation of $u$, $v$ and the solution allows us to assume $u,v\leq \varphi \leq 0$; by the envelope identity, these inequalities hold after a sufficiently large common translation. We restore the translation at the end.

Step 1: inputs of minimal singularity type. First take $\varphi =V$ and $u,v\sim V$. Truncating only the weights, define the finite nonzero measures

\[ \tau _ j=\mathrm{e}^{-\max (u,-j)}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{-\max (v,-j)}\mu _ v. \]

The functions $\max (u,-j)$ need not be $\theta $-plurisubharmonic; they occur only as weights. Theorem 32.4.2 gives $a_ j\in \mathcal E_ V$ with $\mu _{a_ j}=\mathrm{e}^{a_ j}\tau _ j$.

Choose $D\geq \tfrac 12\| u-v\| _\infty +n\log 2$, where the difference is taken off the common poles, and put $w=(u+v)/2-D$. Positivity of mixed products gives

\[ \mu _ w\geq 2^{-n}(\mu _ u+\mu _ v)\geq \mathrm{e}^ w\tau _ j. \]

Exponential order in $\mathcal E_ V$ (corollary 30.3.2) gives $a_ j\geq w$. Since $\tau _ j$ increases with $j$, the same order shows that $a_ j$ decreases. The envelope identity gives $a_1\leq V+\sup _ Xa_1$, so $a_1\sim V$ by the lower bound $w$. Consequently

\[ \mu _{a_ j} \leq \mathrm{e}^{a_1-u}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{a_1-v}\mu _ v \leq C(\mu _ u+\mu _ v). \]

The decreasing limit $a\geq w$ is not identically $-\infty $ and has the ordinary type of $V$. Fixed-dominator continuity (theorem 32.2.1) gives weak convergence of the left-hand sides, and the right-hand sides converge in total variation by dominated convergence with respect to $\mu _ u+\mu _ v$. Therefore

\[ \mu _ a=\mathrm{e}^{a-u}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{a-v}\mu _ v. \]

Comparing this equation with each input measure, exponential order now gives $a\leq u,v$. This comparison uses the limiting equation; the truncated weights alone would not give it.

Step 2: two inputs of the ordinary type of $\varphi $. Suppose $u,v\sim \varphi $, and put $u_ j=\max (u,V-j)$ and $v_ j=\max (v,V-j)$. Step 1 gives solutions $a_ j\sim V$ with

\[ \mu _{a_ j}=\mathrm{e}^{a_ j-u_ j}\mu _{u_ j}+\mathrm{e}^{a_ j-v_ j}\mu _{v_ j}, \qquad a_ j\leq \min (u_ j,v_ j). \]

The differences $u_ j-v_ j$ satisfy the same uniform bound as $u-v$. With $D$ as above, $w_ j=(u_ j+v_ j)/2-D$ satisfies the reverse inequality for this equation, and exponential order gives

\[ (u+v)/2-D\leq w_ j\leq a_ j\leq \min (u_ j,v_ j). \]

The fixed lower bound $w=(u+v)/2-D$ has the ordinary type of $\varphi $, and hence lies in $\mathcal E_\varphi $. Extract an $L^1$ limit $a\not\equiv -\infty $; it satisfies $w\leq a\leq \min (u,v)$, so $a\sim \varphi $. The input masses are at most $M_ V$. Lemma 32.6.2, with $\sigma =V$ and lower bound $h=w$, proves convergence in capacity and the equation. The lemma applies although the approximating solutions have the type of $V$ and the limit has the type of $\varphi $.

Step 3: one input of the ordinary type of $\varphi $. Now let $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $v\sim \varphi $, and set $u_ j=\max (u,\varphi -j)$. Step 2 gives $a_ j\sim \varphi $ with

\[ \mu _{a_ j}=\mathrm{e}^{a_ j-u_ j}\mu _{u_ j}+\mathrm{e}^{a_ j-v}\mu _ v, \qquad a_ j\leq \min (u_ j,v). \]

We construct a common lower bound in $\mathcal E_\varphi $. Put $\delta =n\log 2>0$ and

\[ s_ j=Q_2(a_ j,u_ j)\in \mathcal E_\varphi , \qquad C_ j=\{ s_ j+u_ j=2a_ j\} . \]

By stretching and contact, $s_ j\leq a_ j$ and

32.17
\begin{equation} 2^{-n}\mu _{s_ j}+2^{-n}\mathbf1_{C_ j}\mu _{u_ j} \leq \mathbf1_{C_ j}\mu _{a_ j}. \end{equation}

On $D_ j=\{ s_ j\leq u-2\delta \} $, contact implies $a_ j\leq u_ j-\delta $. Since $\mathrm{e}^{-\delta }=2^{-n}$, the $\mu _{u_ j}$ terms cancel in (32.17). Also $v\geq \varphi -C\geq u_ j-C$ for a fixed constant $C$. Hence

32.18
\begin{equation} \mathbf1_{D_ j}\mu _{s_ j} \leq 2^ n \mathrm{e}^ C\mathbf1_{D_ j\cap C_ j} \mathrm{e}^{(s_ j-u_ j)/2}\mu _ v. \end{equation}

All measures being subtracted here are finite.

For $N\geq 1$ form the finite rooftops

\[ R_ N=P_\theta (s_1,\ldots ,s_ N),\qquad q_ N=P_\theta (u,R_ N). \]

They belong to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by the finite-rooftop proposition. On the set $\{ R_ N\leq u-2\delta \} $, partition the contact set according to the first index $i$ with $R_ N=s_ i$. Since $u_ i\geq u_ N$, (32.18) gives

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ R_ N\leq u-2\delta \} }\mu _{R_ N} \leq 2^ n \mathrm{e}^ C \mathrm{e}^{(R_ N-u_ N)/2}\mu _ v. \]

The contact pieces are disjoint, so there is no factor $N$. On $\{ q_ N\leq u-2\delta \} $ only the contact part $q_ N=R_ N$ contributes. Since $u\leq u_ N$ and $t \mathrm{e}^{-t/2}\leq 2/\mathrm{e}$ for $t\geq 0$, we obtain

\[ \int _ X(u-q_ N)\, \mu _{q_ N} \leq 2\delta M+2^{n+1}\mathrm{e}^{C-1}\mu _ v(X). \]

The first term bounds the contribution of the complementary set, including any contact with $u$. The first-moment criterion proposition 32.3.1, with fixed reference $u$, implies that the decreasing sequence $q_ N$ has a limit $h\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, not identically $-\infty $. For each fixed $j$ and every $N\geq j$, we have $q_ N\leq s_ j\leq a_ j$, so $h\leq a_ j$ for every $j$.

The $a_ j$ now have a subsequence converging in $L^1$ to a limit not identically $-\infty $, bounded above by $u$ and $v$. Lemma 32.6.2, with reference $\varphi $, fixed second input $v$ and common lower bound $h$, proves the required equation in $\mathcal E_\varphi $; here the input masses are at most $M$.

Step 4: a bounded carrier measure for the second input. Finally let $u,v\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $\mu _ v\leq A\nu $, with $\nu =\mathrm{MA}_\omega (\psi )$ and $\psi $ bounded. Again put $u_ j=\max (u,\varphi -j)$. By Step 3, applied with the inputs interchanged, there are $a_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ satisfying the same equations and bounds as in Step 3.

Let $r=P_\theta (u,v)\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. On $\{ a_ j<r\} $ one has $r\leq u_ j,v$, so the inequality $t \mathrm{e}^{-t}\leq 1/\mathrm{e}$ gives

\[ \int _ X(r-a_ j)_+\, \mu _{a_ j} \leq \mathrm{e}^{-1}\bigl (\mu _{u_ j}(X)+\mu _ v(X)\bigr )\leq 2M/\mathrm{e}. \]

Since $a_ j\leq \varphi $, the one-sided first-moment criterion gives a uniform lower bound for $\sup _ Xa_ j$. By lemma 1.23.5,

\[ \sup _{\substack {z\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\\ \sup _ Xz=0}} \int _ X(-z)\, \mathrm{d}\nu <\infty . \]

Applying this to $a_ j-\sup _ Xa_ j$ and using the lower bound for the suprema, we obtain

32.19
\begin{equation} \sup _ j\int _ X(u_ j-a_ j)\, \mu _ v \leq \sup _ j\int _ X(-a_ j)\, \mu _ v<\infty . \end{equation}

Keep $s_ j=Q_2(a_ j,u_ j)$, $C_ j$ and $D_ j$ from Step 3. The same cancellation in (32.17), now using only $a_ j\leq v$, gives

\[ \mathbf1_{D_ j}\mu _{s_ j} \leq 2^ n\mathbf1_{D_ j\cap C_ j}\mathrm{e}^{a_ j-v}\mu _ v \leq 2^ n\mu _ v. \]

Take one additional rooftop,

\[ q_ j=P_\theta (s_ j,u-2\delta )\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

On its contact set with $u-2\delta $ its measure is at most $\mu _ u$; on its other contact part, where $q_ j=s_ j\leq u-2\delta $, the preceding inequality applies. Thus

32.20
\begin{equation} \mu _{q_ j}\leq \mu _ u+2^ n\mu _ v. \end{equation}

Moreover the integrand $u-2\delta -q_ j$ vanishes on the first contact part. On the other part, the measure is carried by $C_ j$, where $u_ j-s_ j=2(u_ j-a_ j)$. Therefore

\[ \int _ X(u-2\delta -q_ j)\, \mu _{q_ j} \leq 2^{n+1}\int _ X(u_ j-a_ j)\, \mu _ v, \]

which is uniformly bounded by (32.19). The first-moment criterion with reference $u-2\delta $ prevents collapse of the $q_ j$. Extract $L^1$ limits of the $a_ j$ and of the $q_ j$, not identically $-\infty $. The fixed-dominator theorem applies to (32.20) and, after a further extraction, gives

\[ h=P_\theta \left(\inf _ jq_ j\right)\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

Since $q_ j\leq s_ j\leq a_ j$, this is a common lower bound for the extracted solutions, and lemma 32.6.2 gives the equation. Its truncation levels are the original indices of the retained subsequence.

In every step the solution lies below $u$ and $v$, so its measure is bounded by $\mu _ u+\mu _ v$. Two solutions have the same exponentially weighted measure, so corollary 30.3.2, applied in both directions, gives uniqueness, even when that weighted measure has infinite mass. Restoring the common translation and the exponent finishes the proof. $\square$
