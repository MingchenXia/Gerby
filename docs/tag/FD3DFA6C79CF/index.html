Changes of background and envelope mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.3 Changes of background and envelope mass

A smooth change of representative $(\theta ,u)\mapsto (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f,u-f)$ preserves the current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ but moves the zero obstacle. The resulting envelopes remain ordinarily equivalent after transport. In a nonclosed background, however, their masses can differ.

Proposition 16.3.1. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. Set $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$, $u'=u-f$, $m=\min _ Xf$ and $M=\max _ Xf$. Then

\[ P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}+m \leq P_{\theta '}[u']_{\mathcal I}+f \leq P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}+M. \]

The same inequalities hold for the ordinary envelopes.

Proof. If $v$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$, then $v-f+m$ is $\theta '$-plurisubharmonic, nonpositive and multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u'$. Therefore $v\leq P_{\theta '}[u']_{\mathcal I}+f-m$, and taking the envelope gives the first inequality. Conversely, a competitor $v'$ in the new background gives the competitor $v'+f-M$ in the old one, which proves the second inequality. For the ordinary envelopes, the same calculation applies with the ordinary singularity order in place of multiplier-ideal equivalence. $\square$

For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, put

\[ W_\theta (u)=\int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I})\in [0,+\infty ]. \]

This quantity depends on the decomposition of the current, even for smooth potentials.

Proposition 16.3.2. There are two smooth decompositions of the same positive current for which the corresponding values of $W$ are different.

Proof. Use the Hermitian form $\omega $ and the smooth function $g=u_ t$ of example 2.3.1, with $t\ne 0$ sufficiently small. Both $\omega $ and $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ are Hermitian, and

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}0=\omega '+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(-g). \]

For a smooth potential in a Hermitian background, all multiplier ideals are trivial and zero is a competitor for its multiplier-ideal envelope, so that envelope is zero. Consequently

\[ W_\omega (0)=\int _ X\omega ^2,\qquad W_{\omega '}(-g)=\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g)^2. \]

By the explicit formula (2.3), the two integrals differ. $\square$

The underlying torus is Kähler; the dependence comes from the chosen nonclosed background. In the closed theory, the invariance of mass under bounded changes of the potential gives the missing step after proposition 16.3.1.

Singularity volumes, on the other hand, range over the entire ordinary type, so the bijection of competitors in the gauge change preserves them. Under bounded mass, proposition 30.9.1 accordingly transports ordinary models of positive volume and preserves their upper-volume comparison.
