10 Pluripolar sets and the plurifine topologyF5A3147592B6

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 10 Pluripolar sets and the plurifine topology

The upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of a family of plurisubharmonic functions is plurisubharmonic, but it may differ from the supremum on an exceptional set (in dimension one, see example 3.4.3 with $d=2$). In chapter 9 we showed that, locally, this exceptional set has outer Monge–Ampère capacity zero (corollary 9.5.2), and that the same holds for the pole set of every plurisubharmonic function (proposition 9.3.4). The first aim of this chapter is the converse: every subset of a ball with outer capacity zero lies in the pole set of a single plurisubharmonic function on the ball. Sets that are locally contained in pole sets are called pluripolar; they play in pluripotential theory the role that polar sets play in the classical theory of chapter 3, chapter 4, chapter 5.

The link between capacity and pole sets is the relative extremal function $H_ E$ of a set $E$ in a ball $B$, the regularized upper envelope of the plurisubharmonic functions on $B$ that are nonpositive and at most $-1$ on $E$. For compact sets and for relatively compact open sets, the capacity of $E$ is the total Monge–Ampère mass of $H_ E$ (proposition 10.1.2, proposition 10.1.3). If $E$ has outer capacity zero, the extremal functions of small open neighborhoods of $E$ therefore have small mass, and the comparison principle shows that $H_ E=0$; a convergent series of functions from the envelope then has poles on all of $E$ (theorem 10.1.4). It follows that countable unions of pluripolar sets are pluripolar and that the exceptional sets of upper envelopes are pluripolar (corollary 10.2.2). On $\mathbb C^ n$ the local functions can even be glued, with the help of small multiples of $\log ^+|z|$, into a single plurisubharmonic function on the whole space; this is Josefson's theorem (theorem 10.2.1).

The second half of the chapter studies the plurifine topology, the analogue of the fine topology of chapter 5 defined by plurisubharmonic functions. Unbounded potentials force us to use it: the set $\{ u>-k\} $, on which a plurisubharmonic function $u$ coincides with its truncation $\max \{ u,-k\} $, is plurifine open but in general not open. We prove that Bedford–Taylor products of locally bounded potentials are local for the plurifine topology (theorem 10.4.3), by combining their locality on sets of the form $\{ u>v\} $ (proposition 9.2.7) with a quasi-Lindelöf theorem: every union of plurifine open sets coincides with a countable subunion outside a pluripolar set (theorem 10.4.1).

Consequently, for plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots ,u_ q$, the products of the truncations $\max \{ u_ l,-k\} $ do not depend on $k\ge m$ on the set $\bigcap _ l\{ u_ l>-m\} $ (proposition 10.4.4); this compatibility is the starting point of the non-pluripolar products of chapter 15. Finally, the difference of two plurisubharmonic functions is plurifinely continuous off their common pole set. Its quasi-supremum and quasi-infimum on a plurifine open set, which disregard pluripolar sets, are therefore its ordinary supremum and infimum off any pluripolar set containing the common poles (corollary 10.5.3).
