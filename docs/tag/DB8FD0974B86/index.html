12.3 Analytic Lelong level sets and generic valuesDB8FD0974B86

We now compare multiplier ideals with Lelong numbers. Recall from proposition 12.1.4 the order $\operatorname {ord}_ xf$ of a nonzero germ $f$ at $x$, computed in local coordinates; since $\operatorname {ord}_ xf\geq k$ if and only if $f\in \mathfrak m_ x^ k$, where $\mathfrak m_ x$ is the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ (section A.1), it does not depend on the coordinates. For a coherent ideal $\mathfrak a$ and a point $x$ with $\mathfrak a_ x\neq 0$, the order of $\mathfrak a$ at $x$ is $\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathfrak a=\min \{ \operatorname {ord}_ xf:f\in \mathfrak a_ x,\ f\neq 0\} $. If $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r$ generate $\mathfrak a$ near $x$, then $\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathfrak a=\min _ i\operatorname {ord}_ xg_ i$, the minimum being taken over the $g_ i$ with nonzero germ at $x$, since every element $\sum _ ih_ ig_ i$ has at least this order. By lemma 12.2.2, a coherent ideal on a connected complex manifold that has a nonzero stalk at one point has nonzero stalks everywhere, so its order is finite at every point.

Lemma 12.3.1. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$, let $x\in X$ be a point at which $X$ has dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $m>0$ be a real number. Then

12.12
\begin{equation} m\nu (\varphi ,x)-n <\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathcal I(m\varphi ) \leq m\nu (\varphi ,x). \end{equation}

Proof. We work on a bounded polydisc $\Omega $ around $x$ in a chart on which $\varphi $ is bounded above. Subtracting a constant changes neither side of (12.12), so we may assume $\varphi \leq 0$ on $\Omega $, and we write $\nu =\nu (\varphi ,x)$. The space $\mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$ is not zero: choose $a\in \Omega $ with $\varphi (a)>-\infty $ and apply corollary 11.4.5. By theorem 12.2.7(3), $\mathcal I(m\varphi )_ x$ is generated by germs of elements of $\mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$; since $\Omega $ is connected, a nonzero element of $\mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$ has a nonzero germ at $x$, by the identity theorem. Hence the order $k$ of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ at $x$ is the smallest vanishing order at $x$ of the nonzero elements of $\mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$.

For the lower bound, let $F\in \mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$ vanish to order exactly $k$ at $x$, and let $P_ k\neq 0$ be its homogeneous term of degree $k$. By (12.2), computed in the coordinates of the chart, and since $\varphi \leq 0$, we have $\varphi (z)\leq \nu \log (|z-x|^2/r_0^2)$ for $0<|z-x|<r_0$ and a suitable $r_0>0$; hence $\mathrm e^{-m\varphi (z)}\geq c|z-x|^{-2m\nu }$ there, with $c=r_0^{2m\nu }$. By (12.10) and polar coordinates (2.1), for small $r>0$

\[ \int _{B(x,r)}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-m\varphi }\, \mathrm dV \geq c'\int _0^ rt^{2k-2m\nu +2n-1}\, \mathrm dt \]

with $c'>0$. The left side is finite, so $2k-2m\nu +2n>0$, that is, $k>m\nu -n$.

For the upper bound, we use that every element of $\mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$ vanishes to order at least $k$ at $x$. Choose $R>0$ such that the polydisc $\Delta $ of polyradius $(2R,\ldots ,2R)$ centered at $x$ is relatively compact in $\Omega $. Let $\delta >0$ be half the distance from $\overline\Delta $ to the complement of $\Omega $. Put $A=|B(0,\delta )|^{-1/2}$. For $G\in \mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$, (12.9) gives $\sup _{\overline\Delta }|G|\leq A\| G\| _{L^2(\Omega )}\leq A\| G\| _{m\varphi }$, and the coefficient bound in (7.1) gives $|c_\alpha |\leq (2R)^{-|\alpha |}\sup _{\overline\Delta }|G|$ for the Taylor coefficients $c_\alpha $ of $G$ at $x$. Since $c_\alpha =0$ for $|\alpha |<k$ and $|(a-x)^\alpha |\leq |a-x|^{|\alpha |}$, for $|a-x|<R$ we obtain

\[ |G(a)|\leq A\| G\| _{m\varphi }\sum _{|\alpha |\geq k}\Bigl (\frac{|a-x|}{2R}\Bigr )^{|\alpha |} \leq C_ k|a-x|^ k\| G\| _{m\varphi }. \]

For the second inequality, note that there are $\binom {d+n-1}{n-1}$ multi-indices of length $d$; with $\rho =|a-x|/(2R)\leq 1/2$ we get $\sum _{d\geq k}\binom {d+n-1}{n-1}\rho ^ d\leq \rho ^ k\sum _{e\geq 0}\binom {e+k+n-1}{n-1}2^{-e}$, a convergent series. Now let $|a-x|<R$ with $\varphi (a)>-\infty $, and let $G$ be given by corollary 11.4.5, so that $G(a)=1$ and $\| G\| _{m\varphi }^2\leq C_\Omega \mathrm e^{-m\varphi (a)}$, where $C_\Omega $ depends only on $n$ and the diameter of $\Omega $. Then $1\leq C_ k^2C_\Omega |a-x|^{2k}\mathrm e^{-m\varphi (a)}$, that is,

\[ \varphi (a)\leq \frac km\log |a-x|^2+C'; \]

here $C'=m^{-1}\log (C_ k^2C_\Omega )$, and the inequality holds trivially where $\varphi (a)=-\infty $. Since it holds for all $a$ with $|a-x|<R$, the characterization in proposition 12.1.1 gives $k/m\leq \nu $. $\square$

Corollary 12.3.2. Let $x\in X$, and let $u$ be a function on a neighborhood of $x$ that near $x$ is the sum $u=v+h$ of a plurisubharmonic function $v$ and a bounded measurable function $h$. Put $\nu (u,x)=\nu (v,x)$; by proposition 12.1.1 this does not depend on the decomposition. If $\nu (u,x)=0$, then $\mathrm e^{-tu}$ is integrable on a neighborhood of $x$ for every $t>0$. This applies in particular to a function $u$ that is quasi-plurisubharmonic near $x$, that is, the sum of a plurisubharmonic function and a smooth real function near $x$. For a weight $\varphi $ on $X$ with $\nu (\varphi ,x)=0$ it says that $\mathcal I(t\varphi )_ x=\mathcal O_{X,x}$ for every $t>0$.

Proof. If $u=v+h=v'+h'$ are two such decompositions near $x$, then $v$ and $v'$ take the value $-\infty $ at the same points, and $v\leq v'+C$ and $v'\leq v+C$ near $x$, where $C$ bounds $|h|+|h'|$; so $\nu (v,x)=\nu (v',x)$ by proposition 12.1.1. If $X$ has dimension zero at $x$, then $v(x)$ is finite and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let $U$ be a connected neighborhood of $x$ on which $v$ is plurisubharmonic and $|h|\leq M$ for a constant $M$. Applying lemma 12.3.1 to the weight $v$ on $U$ and $m=t$, we obtain $\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathcal I(tv)\leq t\nu (v,x)=0$. Hence $\mathcal I(tv)_ x$ contains a germ that does not vanish at $x$; this germ is a unit, so $\mathcal I(tv)_ x=\mathcal O_{X,x}$, that is, $\mathrm e^{-tv}$ is integrable on a neighborhood of $x$. Since $\mathrm e^{-tu}\leq \mathrm e^{tM}\mathrm e^{-tv}$ on $U$, the same holds for $\mathrm e^{-tu}$. For a weight $\varphi $, the integrability of $\mathrm e^{-t\varphi }$ near $x$ means that $1\in \mathcal I(t\varphi )_ x$. $\square$

By lemma 12.3.1, the Lelong number of $\varphi $ at $x$ determines the order of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ at $x$ up to an error less than $n$. The Lelong numbers do not determine the multiplier ideals themselves, even if they are known at all points, as the following example shows.

Example 12.3.3. On $\mathbb C^2$ consider the plurisubharmonic functions (proposition 8.3.3)

\[ \psi =\log \bigl (|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2\bigr ),\qquad \varphi =\log \bigl (|z_1|^2+|z_2|^4\bigr ). \]

They have the same Lelong numbers at every point. Off the origin both functions are finite, so their Lelong numbers vanish there by proposition 12.1.1. At the origin, $\nu (\psi ,0)=1$ by proposition 12.1.4. For $|z_2|\leq 1$ we have $\varphi \leq \psi $, so $\nu (\varphi ,0)\geq 1$; conversely, the supremum of $\varphi $ over the ball $|z|<\mathrm e^{t/2}$ is at least $\sup _{|z_1|<\mathrm e^{t/2}}\log |z_1|^2=t$, so $\nu (\varphi ,0)\leq 1$ by (12.1). Nevertheless $\mathcal I(\frac32\psi )_0\neq \mathcal I(\frac32\varphi )_0$. Indeed, $\mathrm e^{-3\psi /2}=|z|^{-3}$ is integrable near $0\in \mathbb R^4$ by polar coordinates, since $\int _0^ rt^{-3}t^3\, \mathrm dt<\infty $; so $\mathcal I(\frac32\psi )_0=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^2,0}$. On the other hand, on the set $A_\delta =\{ 0<|z_2|<\delta ,\ |z_1|<|z_2|^2\} $ we have $|z_1|^2+|z_2|^4<2|z_2|^4$, hence $\mathrm e^{-3\varphi /2}>2^{-3/2}|z_2|^{-6}$, and by Fubini's theorem

\[ \int _{A_\delta }\mathrm e^{-3\varphi /2}\, \mathrm dV\geq 2^{-3/2}\int _{0<|z_2|<\delta }\pi |z_2|^4\, |z_2|^{-6}\, \mathrm dV_2(z_2)=2^{-3/2}\pi \int _0^\delta 2\pi t^{-1}\, \mathrm dt=\infty . \]

Since every neighborhood of $0$ contains $A_\delta $ for small $\delta >0$, the function $\mathrm e^{-3\varphi /2}$ is not integrable near $0$, and $1\notin \mathcal I(\frac32\varphi )_0$.

Theorem 12.3.4 (Siu). Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$ and let $c>0$. Then

\[ E_ c(\varphi )=\{ x\in X:\nu (\varphi ,x)\geq c\} \]

is an analytic subset of $X$, and its intersection with every connected component of $X$ of positive dimension is a proper subset of that component. The same holds for the Lelong numbers of a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$.

Proof. Lelong numbers vanish on components of dimension zero, so we may assume that $X$ is connected of dimension $n\geq 1$. We describe $E_ c(\varphi )$ as an intersection of analytic sets defined by the orders of the ideals $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ and then show that such an intersection is analytic. Let $\mathfrak a$ be a coherent ideal and $k$ an integer. The set of points $y$ where $\operatorname {ord}_ y\mathfrak a\geq k$ (with $\operatorname {ord}_ y\mathfrak a=\infty $ if $\mathfrak a_ y=0$) is analytic. Indeed, let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r$ generate $\mathfrak a$ on a coordinate neighborhood $W$. A germ has order at least $k$ at $y$ if and only if its derivatives of order less than $k$ vanish at $y$, since these derivatives are multiples of its Taylor coefficients, by (7.1). Hence for $y\in W$ we have $\operatorname {ord}_ y\mathfrak a\geq k$ if and only if $\partial ^\alpha g_ i(y)=0$ for all $i$ and all $|\alpha |<k$: the condition is necessary because the $g_ i$ belong to $\mathfrak a_ y$, and sufficient because, by the Leibniz rule, every element $\sum _ ih_ ig_ i$ then vanishes to order at least $k$ at $y$. This is a finite system of holomorphic equations, and for $k\leq 0$ the set is all of $W$. By (12.12),

12.13
\begin{equation} E_ c(\varphi )=\bigcap _{m\geq 1} \left\{ x:\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathcal I(m\varphi ) \geq \lfloor mc-n\rfloor +1\right\} . \end{equation}

Indeed, if $\nu (\varphi ,x)\geq c$, the lower bound in (12.12) gives the integer inequality $\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathcal I(m\varphi )>mc-n$, that is, $\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathcal I(m\varphi )\geq \lfloor mc-n\rfloor +1$, for every $m$. If $\nu (\varphi ,x)<c$, the upper bound gives $\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathcal I(m\varphi )\leq m\nu (\varphi ,x)\leq mc-n<\lfloor mc-n\rfloor +1$ as soon as $m(c-\nu (\varphi ,x))\geq n$.

Thus $E_ c(\varphi )$ is an intersection of analytic subsets of $X$. An arbitrary intersection $A=\bigcap _\lambda A_\lambda $ of analytic subsets of $X$ is analytic. Since $A$ is closed, it suffices to show that each $p\in A$ has a neighborhood $W$ such that $A\cap W$ is the intersection of $W$ with finitely many of the $A_\lambda $. Let $I_\lambda \subset \mathcal O_{X,p}$ be the ideal generated by the germs at $p$ of local defining functions of $A_\lambda $. By proposition A.1.2 the sum $\sum _\lambda I_\lambda $ is finitely generated, so it equals $I_{\lambda _1}+\cdots +I_{\lambda _ q}$ for finitely many indices; put $B=A_{\lambda _1}\cap \cdots \cap A_{\lambda _ q}$. Every local defining function of each $A_\lambda $ is then a combination of defining functions of $A_{\lambda _1},\ldots ,A_{\lambda _ q}$ near $p$, so $A_\lambda $ contains a neighborhood of $p$ in $B$. This neighborhood may depend on $\lambda $, and we now remove this dependence.

By corollary A.2.13, the irreducible components of the analytic set $B$ form a locally finite family; so some neighborhood $N$ of $p$ meets only finitely many of them. Let $B_1,\ldots ,B_ l$ be those that contain $p$. The union of the other components meeting $N$ is closed and does not contain $p$, so there is an open neighborhood $W'\subset N$ of $p$ with $B\cap W'\subset B_1\cup \cdots \cup B_ l$. Fix $\lambda $ and $i$. The regular locus $(B_ i)_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a connected manifold, dense in $B_ i$ (corollary A.2.13), and its analytic subset $A_\lambda \cap (B_ i)_{\mathrm{reg}}$ has nonempty interior in it, because $A_\lambda $ contains a neighborhood of $p$ in $B_ i$ and $p$ lies in the closure of $(B_ i)_{\mathrm{reg}}$. By lemma A.1.6, $A_\lambda \cap (B_ i)_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is therefore all of $(B_ i)_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and $B_ i\subset A_\lambda $ because $A_\lambda $ is closed. Therefore $B\cap W'\subset A_\lambda $ for every $\lambda $, and $A\cap W'=B\cap W'$. Applied to (12.13), this shows that $E_ c(\varphi )$ is analytic.

By proposition 12.1.1, $\nu (\varphi ,x)=0$ at every point where $\varphi (x)>-\infty $. Thus $E_ c(\varphi )$ is contained in the pole set of $\varphi $, which has measure zero because $\varphi $ is locally integrable (theorem 8.2.1), and therefore has no interior points; so $E_ c(\varphi )\neq X$.

Finally, let $T$ be a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$. Every point of $X$ at which $X$ has positive dimension has a coordinate ball $U$ on which $T=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $u$ plurisubharmonic, and $\nu (T,y)=\nu (u,y)$ for $y\in U$ (definition 12.1.2). By what we have proved, applied to the weight $u$ on $U$, the set $\{ y\in U:\nu (T,y)\geq c\} $ is an analytic subset of $U$ with empty interior. Since the components of $X$ of dimension zero do not meet $\{ y:\nu (T,y)\geq c\} $, and analyticity is a local property, this set is an analytic subset of $X$ whose intersection with every component of positive dimension is proper. $\square$

Proposition 12.3.5. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$ and let $Z\subset X$ be an irreducible analytic subset. The generic Lelong number of $\varphi $ along $Z$ is

\[ \nu (\varphi ,Z)=\inf _{x\in Z}\nu (\varphi ,x). \]

It is attained at every point of $Z$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$. Moreover, the complement in $Z$ of every countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$ is dense in $Z$; in particular, $\nu (\varphi ,Z)$ is attained on a dense subset of $Z$. The same definition and conclusions apply to the Lelong numbers of a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$, and give the generic Lelong number $\nu (T,Z)$. If $\pi \colon Y\to X$ is a smooth model of $X$ (section A.7), that is, a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$, and $E\subset Y$ is a prime divisor, that is, an irreducible hypersurface, we define

\[ \nu (\varphi ,E)=\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,E). \]

This number depends only on the prime divisor over $X$ represented by $E$ (definition A.7.4), not on the model.

Proof. Put $\lambda =\nu (\varphi ,Z)$. For every integer $j\geq 1$, theorem 12.3.4 shows that $Z\cap E_{\lambda +1/j}(\varphi )$ is an analytic subset of $Z$, and it is a proper subset by the definition of $\lambda $. At every point of $Z$ outside the union of these sets, $\nu (\varphi ,x)<\lambda +1/j$ for all $j$, hence $\nu (\varphi ,x)=\lambda $.

We now show that the complement in $Z$ of an arbitrary countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$ is dense in $Z$; applied to the sets $Z\cap E_{\lambda +1/j}(\varphi )$, this shows that $\nu (\varphi ,Z)$ is attained on a dense subset of $Z$. The density is clear if $Z$ is a point, for then the proper analytic subsets of $Z$ are empty. Let $\dim Z>0$. By corollary A.2.13, applied to the family consisting of $Z$ alone, $Z$ is an irreducible component of itself; so its regular locus $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a connected manifold, dense in $Z$.

Let $B$ be a proper analytic subset of $Z$. Then $B\cap Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is an analytic subset of $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and it is proper, since otherwise the closed set $B$ would contain the closure $Z$ of $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$. By lemma A.1.6, every point of $B\cap Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$ has a connected open neighborhood $U$ in $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$ with a holomorphic function $g\not\equiv 0$ on $U$ such that $B\cap U\subset \{ g=0\} $; shrinking $U$ to a coordinate ball, on which $g\not\equiv 0$ by the identity theorem, we see from lemma 7.4.1 that $B\cap U$ is a null set in the chart. Covering a chart $N$ of $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$ by countably many such balls and by open sets disjoint from $B$, we see that $B\cap N$ is a null set. Consequently, a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$ meets every chart of $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$ in a null set, which has no interior points. Its complement is therefore dense in $Z_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and hence in $Z$. For a closed positive $(1,1)$-current the same argument applies, since theorem 12.3.4 holds for its Lelong numbers.

For the second assertion, note first that $\varphi \circ \pi $ is a weight on $Y$: by proposition 8.2.3 it is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $ on each component of $Y$, and the second alternative is excluded. Indeed, since $\pi $ is bimeromorphic (section A.3), there is a nowhere dense analytic subset $A\subset X$ such that $\pi ^{-1}(A)$ is nowhere dense in $Y$ and $\pi $ maps $Y\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$ biholomorphically onto $X\setminus A$. If $\varphi \circ \pi $ were identically $-\infty $ on a component $Y_0$ of $Y$, then $\varphi $ would be $-\infty $ on the nonempty open set $\pi (Y_0\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A))$, which is impossible because $\varphi $ is locally integrable.

Now let $E\subset Y$ and $E'\subset Y'$ be prime divisors on smooth models $\pi \colon Y\to X$ and $\pi '\colon Y'\to X$ that represent the same prime divisor over $X$, and let $\rho \colon Y''\to Y$ and $\rho '\colon Y''\to Y'$ be maps from a common smooth model on which the strict transforms of $E$ and $E'$ coincide, say with $E''$. By proposition A.7.3, $\rho $ is biholomorphic over $Y\setminus S$ for an analytic subset $S\subset Y$ of codimension at least two, so it maps the nonempty open subset $E''\cap \rho ^{-1}(Y\setminus S)$ of $E''$ onto the nonempty open subset $E\setminus S$ of $E$, and $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi \circ \rho ,q)=\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,\rho (q))$ for $q$ in this set, because Lelong numbers are invariant under biholomorphisms (proposition 12.1.1). By the first part, the generic value along $E$ is attained on a dense subset of $E$, so it equals the infimum of the Lelong numbers over any nonempty open subset of $E$; the same holds for $E''$. Hence $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,E)=\nu (\varphi \circ \pi \circ \rho ,E'')$. The same argument for $\rho '$ gives $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ',E')=\nu (\varphi \circ \pi \circ \rho ,E'')$, since $\pi \circ \rho =\pi '\circ \rho '$. $\square$

Proposition 12.3.6. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$, let $Z\subset X$ be a connected closed complex submanifold of positive codimension, which is an irreducible analytic subset by corollary A.2.13, and let $z=(z',z'')$ be local coordinates near a point of $Z$ in which $Z=\{ z'=0\} $. Then near that point

\[ \varphi (z)\leq \nu (\varphi ,Z)\log |z'|^2+C,\qquad z'\neq 0, \]

for a constant $C$, and no bound of this form with a coefficient larger than $\nu (\varphi ,Z)$ holds near any point of $Z$. If $\pi \colon Y\to X$ is the blowup of a point $x$ at which $X$ has positive dimension (proposition A.5.3), and $E=\pi ^{-1}(x)$, then $\nu (\varphi ,E)=\nu (\varphi ,x)$.

Proof. Let $p\in Z$ be the given point and $\lambda =\nu (\varphi ,Z)$. Choose $r_0>0$, a constant $C$ and a neighborhood $W$ of $p$ in $Z$ such that for every $y\in W$ the closed ball $\overline{B(y,r_0)}$ lies in the coordinate domain and $\varphi \leq C$ on it. By (12.2) at $y$, we have $\varphi (z)\leq \nu (\varphi ,y)\log (|z-y|^2/r_0^2)+C$ for $0<|z-y|<r_0$. Take $z=(z',z'')$ with $y=(0,z'')\in W$ and $0<|z'|<r_0$. Then $|z-y|=|z'|$, the logarithm is negative and $\nu (\varphi ,y)\geq \lambda $, so $\varphi (z)\leq \lambda \log |z'|^2-\lambda \log r_0^2+C$. This is the asserted bound, with a constant independent of $z''$. Conversely, suppose that $\varphi (z)\leq c\log |z'|^2+C'$ for $z'\neq 0$ near a point $q$ of $Z$, where we may assume $c\geq 0$. For $y\in Z$ near $q$ we have $|z'|\leq |z-y|$, so $\varphi (z)\leq c\log |z-y|^2+C'$ for $z\notin Z$ near $y$. Since $Z=\{ z'=0\} $ is a Lebesgue null set in the chart, proposition 12.1.1 gives $\nu (\varphi ,y)\geq c$. By proposition 12.3.5 there are points $y$ arbitrarily close to $q$ with $\nu (\varphi ,y)=\lambda $, so $c\leq \lambda $.

Now let $\pi $ be the blowup of $x$, and let $n=\dim _ xX$. If $n=1$, then $\pi $ is the identity and $E=\{ x\} $, so there is nothing to prove. Let $n\geq 2$ and choose coordinates centered at $x$. The blowup is covered by $n$ charts; in the $k$th chart, $z_ k=w_ k$ and $z_ j=w_ kw_ j$ for $j\neq k$, so that

\[ |z|^2=|w_ k|^2\Bigl (1+\sum _{j\neq k}|w_ j|^2\Bigr ),\qquad E=\{ w_ k=0\} . \]

By (12.2), $\varphi \leq \nu (\varphi ,x)\log |z|^2+C$ near $x$. Pulling back, we get $\varphi \circ \pi \leq \nu (\varphi ,x)\log |w_ k|^2+C'$ on compact subsets of the $k$th chart near $E$; since $|w_ k|$ is at most the distance to any point of $E$ in that chart, $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,q)\geq \nu (\varphi ,x)$ for every $q\in E$, and $\nu (\varphi ,E)\geq \nu (\varphi ,x)$. Conversely, $E\cong \mathbb P^{n-1}$ is a compact connected submanifold of $Y$, so the first part of the proposition, applied to the weight $\varphi \circ \pi $ and $E$, gives $\varphi \circ \pi \leq \nu (\varphi ,E)\log |w_ k|^2+C$ near each point of $E$, in the coordinates of a chart containing it. Cover $E$ by finitely many such neighborhoods, on each of which $\log |w_ k|^2-\log |z|^2$ is bounded. Since $\pi $ is proper, the inverse image of a small neighborhood of $x$ lies in their union, so $\varphi (z)\leq \nu (\varphi ,E)\log |z|^2+C''$ near $x$, for $z\neq x$. Hence $\nu (\varphi ,E)\leq \nu (\varphi ,x)$. $\square$
