Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag 9E4426687FC0

Auxiliary product all obstacle criterion

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Corollary 16.6.41

Corollary 16.6.41. On $M=Y\times C_0\times Z$ with the background $\Theta _{\sigma ,f}$, for any smooth $f\geq 0$, put $U=\Psi +u\circ p$. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} P_{\Theta _{\sigma ,f}}[U]& =\Psi +\sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ p,\\ P_{\Theta _{\sigma ,f}}[U]_{\mathcal I}& =\Psi +Q\circ p. \end{aligned} \]

The following are equivalent: equality of these two envelopes; $\mu _ i=E_ i$ for every $i$; $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta _{\sigma ,f},\Psi +Q\circ p)$; and equality of the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $U$ below every smooth real obstacle on $M$. These statements include the case $f=0$ and curve backgrounds of zero area.

Proof. On $B=Y\times C_0$, with projection $\pi $ to $Y$, use the background $\gamma _{\sigma ,f}$ of the proof of lemma 16.6.36. Sums of ordinary competitors in the separate coordinates give $P_{\gamma _{\sigma ,f}}[u\circ \pi ]\geq \sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ \pi $. For the reverse inequality, replace an ordinary competitor by its maximum with a sufficiently low translate of $u\circ \pi $. Its restriction to every section $Y\times \{ z\} $ is then not identically $-\infty $, nonpositive and at least as singular as $u$, so the separation for the zero obstacle bounds it by $\sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i$. The reduction of proposition 16.6.13, applied to the divisor $B\times D$ with weight one, proves the ordinary identity upstairs.

By corollary 16.2.4, each $q_ i$ has the multiplier ideals of $u_ i$, so pullback and addition give $U\sim _{\mathcal I}\Phi _\sigma (q)$. These two potentials therefore have the same ideal envelope, which lemma 16.6.36 identifies with $\Psi +Q\circ p$.

If $\mu _ i=E_ i$ for every $i$, then $u_ i\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(C_ i,\sigma _ i,r_ i)$ by corollary 30.13.2, and we choose stretching competitors in these curve classes. The sum of their pullbacks plus $\Psi $ is $\Theta _{\sigma ,f}$-plurisubharmonic: its current is the sum of the pulled-back positive curve currents, the form $(f\circ p)s^*\tau $ and the current of the divisor with weight one. Adding the stretching inequalities gives the one for $U$ relative to $\Psi +Q\circ p$, also at poles, and the ordinary order follows in the same way. Thus $U$ belongs to that relative class, and proposition 16.6.8 gives equality below all obstacles. Taking the zero obstacle and using the two identities above recovers $q_ i=r_ i$, hence $\mu _ i=E_ i$. This closes the chain of equivalences. $\square$
