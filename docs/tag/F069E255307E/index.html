Subgeodesics

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 4.1 Subgeodesics

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class.

Definition 4.1.1. Let us fix $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. A subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ is a family $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

  1. if we define

    \[ \Phi \colon X\times \Bigl \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z\in (0,1) \Bigr \} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad (x,z)\mapsto \varphi _{\operatorname{Re}z}(x), \]

    then $\Phi $ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh, where $p_1\colon X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z\in (0,1)\} \rightarrow X$ is the natural projection;

  2. when $t\to 0+$ (resp. $t\to 1-$), $\varphi _ t$ converges to $\varphi _0$ (resp. $\varphi _1$) with respect to the $L^1$-topology.

We also say $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic.

We call $\Phi $ the complexification of the subgeodesic $(\varphi _ t)_ t$.

When we do not want to specify $\varphi _0$ and $\varphi _1$, we shall say $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ is a subgeodesic
Note. Note that it is implicit in the definition that $\varphi _ t$ as $t\to 0+$ or $t\to 1-$ admits an $L^1$-limit.
.

More generally, a family $(\psi _ t)_{t\in [a,b]}$ (resp. $(\psi _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$) in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for some $a\leq b$ is called a subgeodesic if $(\psi _{tb+(1-t)a})_{t\in [0,1]}$ (resp. $(\psi _{tb+(1-t)a})_{t\in (0,1)}$) is a subgeodesic.

Remark 4.1.2.Note that given a subgeodesic $(\psi _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$, the endpoints $\psi _ a$ and $\psi _ b$ are uniquely determined, in view of proposition 1.2.6.

Remark 4.1.3. In the literature, people sometimes regard $\Phi $ as a function defined on $X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}: \mathrm{e}^{-1}<|z|<1\} $, with $\Phi (x,z)=\varphi _{-\log |z|}(x)$. We sometimes also use this convention; the ambient strip or annulus will make the convention clear.

In general, there are no subgeodesics from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. In fact, by proposition 4.1.6 below, the existence of a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ forces $\varphi _0\land \varphi _1\not\equiv -\infty $, a condition that may fail (see example 5.2.8).

We first note that the subgeodesics are well-behaved under the change of $\theta $:

Proposition 4.1.4. Let $g$ be a smooth real function on $X$. Let $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$. Suppose that $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then $(\varphi _ t-g)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$.

Proof. This follows directly from the definition of the complexification. $\square$

Example 4.1.5. Let $\varphi _0\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Let

\[ \varphi _ t=\varphi _0+tC,\quad t\in [0,1]. \]

Then $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic. Indeed, since $\operatorname{Re}z$ is harmonic in $z$, the complexification $\Phi (x,z)=\varphi _0(x)+C\, \operatorname{Re}z$ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh.

As a consequence, the constant $(\varphi _0)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic, called the constant subgeodesic at $\varphi _0$.

A more general version is as follows: Suppose that $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $C_1,C_2\in \mathbb {R}$. Then $(\varphi _ t+C_1t+C_2)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is also a subgeodesic.

Proposition 4.1.6. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Then for each $x\in X$, $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is a convex function. Furthermore,

\[ \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\quad \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0\land \varphi _1. \]

Proof. Let $\Phi $ be the complexification of $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$.

For each $x\in X$, the map

\[ \Bigl \{ z\in \mathbb {C}: \operatorname{Re}z\in (0,1) \Bigr \} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad z\mapsto \Phi (x,z) \]

is either subharmonic or constantly $-\infty $, as follows from definition 4.1.1 1 and proposition 1.1.13. In the latter case, the convexity of $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is trivial. In the former case, the convexity on the interval $(0,1)$ follows from proposition 1.1.9.

In order to verify the convexity at the boundary, let us fix $s\in (0,1)$. We need to show that

4.1
\begin{equation} \label{eq:varphisconvextemp1} \varphi _ s(x)\leq s\varphi _1(x)+(1-s)\varphi _0(x) \end{equation}

for all $x\in X$. Thanks to proposition 1.2.6, it suffices to prove this for almost all $x$.

Choose sequences $a_ j\searrow 0$ and $b_ j\nearrow 1$ such that $\varphi _{a_ j}\to \varphi _0$ and $\varphi _{b_ j}\to \varphi _1$ almost everywhere. Outside a set $Z\subseteq X$ of zero Lebesgue measure, all these convergences hold and all the values involved are finite. For $x\in X\setminus Z$ and $j$ large enough that $a_ j<s<b_ j$, the convexity on $(0,1)$ gives

\[ \varphi _ s(x)\leq \frac{b_ j-s}{b_ j-a_ j}\varphi _{a_ j}(x)+\frac{s-a_ j}{b_ j-a_ j}\varphi _{b_ j}(x). \]

Letting $j\to \infty $ gives (4.1) almost everywhere.

Let us prove the last assertion. Let

\[ \varphi \coloneqq \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t. \]

By Kiselman's principle proposition 1.2.11, we know that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $. Since the complexification $\Phi $ is not identically $-\infty $, Fubini's theorem shows that for almost every $x\in X$, the function $t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is finite for almost every $t\in (0,1)$. For such an $x$, the convexity just proved implies that $t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is a finite convex function on $(0,1)$, hence it is bounded from below on $(0,1)$. Thus $\varphi \not\equiv -\infty $, and so $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

For any $t\in (0,1)$, using the convexity established above, we have

\[ \varphi \leq (1-t)\varphi _0+t\varphi _1. \]

It follows that $\varphi \leq \varphi _0$ and $\varphi \leq \varphi _1$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. Our assertion follows. $\square$

Corollary 4.1.7. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Then

4.2
\begin{equation} \label{eq:varphitconvexbdry} \lim _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0,\quad \lim _{t\to 1-}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _1. \end{equation}

Moreover,

\[ \lim _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t=\varphi _0,\quad \lim _{t\to 1-}\varphi _ t=\varphi _1\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]
The inequalities in (4.2) are not equalities everywhere in general. For example, consider an arbitrary subgeodesic from an unbounded $\varphi _0\in \mathcal{E}(X,\omega )$ to a bounded $\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ for some Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$.

Proof. The inequalities in (4.2) and the existence of the limits follow from the convexity in proposition 4.1.6. Let us prove the quasi-everywhere equality at $t=0$. Take a decreasing sequence $t_ j\searrow 0$ with $t_ j>0$. Then $\varphi _{t_ j}\to \varphi _0$ in $L^1(X)$ as $j\to \infty $. So by corollary 1.2.8, we have

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\varphi _{t_ j}= \varphi _0\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]

Therefore, we have

\[ \varphi _0=\varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\varphi _{t_ j}=\lim _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]

The assertion at $t=1$ is similar. $\square$

Corollary 4.1.8. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

4.3
\begin{equation} \label{eq:supconvexsubgeod} \adjustlimits \sup _ X \inf _{s\in (0,1)}\varphi _ s \leq \sup _ X \varphi _ t\leq \sup _ X \varphi _0\lor \sup _ X \varphi _1 \end{equation}

for all $t\in (0,1)$.

Furthermore, $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t$ is continuous with respect to the $L^1$-topology.

In particular, in view of proposition 1.5.4, the family $\{ \varphi _ t\} _{t\in [0,1]}$ is relatively compact with respect to the $L^1$-topology on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. (4.3) follows immediately from proposition 4.1.6. Let us verify the continuity of $t\mapsto \varphi _ t$. The continuity at $t=0$ or $t=1$ follows from the definition of subgeodesic. While the continuity on $(0,1)$ follows from the convexity of $t\mapsto \varphi _ t$, and the fact that $\varphi _ t\in L^1(X)$ for each $t\in (0,1)$. $\square$

Proposition 4.1.9. Let $(\varphi ^ i_0)_{i\in I}$, $(\varphi ^ i_1)_{i\in I}$ be two non-empty uniformly bounded from above families in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let $(\varphi ^ i_ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be subgeodesics from $\varphi _0^ i$ to $\varphi _1^ i$ for each $i\in I$. Then

\[ \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi ^ i_ t\right)_{t\in (0,1)} \]

is a subgeodesic from $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_0$ to $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_1$.

Proof. We may assume that $\varphi _0^ i,\varphi _1^ i\leq 0$ for all $i\in I$. Then it follows that $\varphi _ t^ i\leq 0$ for all $t\in (0,1)$ and all $i\in I$ by proposition 4.1.6.

We define

\[ \varphi _ t\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ) \]

for all $t\in [0,1]$. Observe that $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t$ is convex by the same argument leading to (4.1).

Let $(\psi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be the subgeodesic whose complexification $\Phi _{\psi }$ corresponds to $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \Phi _{\varphi ^ i}$, where $\Phi _{\varphi ^ i}$ is the complexification of $(\varphi ^ i_ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$. Then clearly, $\varphi _ t\leq \psi _ t$ for each $t\in (0,1)$. On the other hand, by proposition 1.2.5,

\[ \psi _ t=\sup _{i\in I}\varphi ^ i_ t=\varphi _ t\quad \textup{almost everywhere} \]

for almost all $t\in (0,1)$. Therefore, using proposition 1.2.6, we find $\psi _ t=\varphi _ t$ for almost all $t\in (0,1)$. Since both functions are convex in $t$, we conclude that $\psi _ t=\varphi _ t$ for all $t\in (0,1)$.

It remains to argue that $\varphi _ t\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _0$ as $t\to 0+$ and $\varphi _ t\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _1$ as $t\to 1-$. By symmetry, it suffices to argue the former.

By Choquet's lemma proposition 1.2.3, applied to the uniformly bounded above family of complexifications $(\Phi _{\varphi ^ i})_{i\in I}$ on the strip, there is a countable subset $I'\subset I$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Phi _{\varphi ^ i}=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Phi _{\varphi ^ i}. \]

Applying Choquet's lemma proposition 1.2.3 once more to the endpoint families if necessary, and enlarging $I'$, we may also assume

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _0^ i=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _0^ i,\quad \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _1^ i=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _1^ i. \]

Thus, after replacing $I$ by $I'$, we may assume that $I$ is countable.

We know that for any $t\in (0,1)$ and any $j\in I$,

\[ \varphi ^ j_ t\leq \varphi _ t\leq t\varphi _1+(1-t)\varphi _0. \]

Letting $t\to 0+$, we find that

\[ \varphi _0^ j\leq \varlimsup _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0 \]

almost everywhere. Since $I$ is countable, we conclude that

4.4
\begin{equation} \label{eq:varphi0limsuptemp1} \varphi _0=\varlimsup _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t \end{equation}

almost everywhere.

Fix $i_0\in I$. Recall that by proposition 4.1.6, for each $t\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ \adjustlimits \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\sup _ X \varphi _ t\geq \adjustlimits \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\sup _ X \varphi ^{i_0}_ t\geq \adjustlimits \sup _ X \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi ^{i_0}_ t>-\infty , \]

so the set $\{ \varphi _ t\} _{t\in (0,1)}$ is relatively compact with respect to the $L^1$-topology by proposition 1.5.4. Let $\psi $ be a cluster point as $t\to 0+$. It suffices to show that $\psi =\varphi _0$. By corollary 1.2.8 and (4.4), this holds almost everywhere. Therefore, it holds everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. $\square$

Proposition 4.1.10. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then for any $0\leq a\leq b\leq 1$, the segment $(\varphi _{t})_{t\in [a,b]}$ is a subgeodesic.

Proof. It suffices to show that

\[ \varphi _{tb+(1-t)a}\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _ a,\quad \varphi _{tb+(1-t)a}\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _ b \]

as $t\to 0+$ and $t\to 1-$ respectively. In other words, we need to show that for any $c\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ \varphi _ t\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _ c \]

as $t\to c$. For this purpose, observe that by proposition 4.1.6,

\[ \adjustlimits \sup _ X \inf _{s\in (0,1)}\varphi _ s\leq \sup _{X}\varphi _ t\leq \left( \sup _ X \varphi _0 \right) \lor \left( \sup _ X\varphi _1 \right) \]

for any $t\in (0,1)$. Therefore, $\{ \varphi _ t\} _{t\in (0,1)}$ is a relatively compact family with respect to the $L^1$-topology on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by proposition 1.5.4. It suffices to show that any cluster point $\psi $ of $\varphi _ t$ as $t\to c$ is equal to $\varphi _ c$. By corollary 1.2.8 and the convexity proposition 4.1.6, we have $\varphi _ c=\psi $ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. $\square$

For the purpose of this chapter, it is useful to reformulate the gluing lemma corollary 1.2.13 in the following way, which allows us to glue subgeodesics directly:

Lemma 4.1.11. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let $0\leq a<b\leq 1$ and let $(\psi _ t)_{t\in [a,b]}$ be a subgeodesic such that $\psi _ a=\varphi _ a$, $\psi _ b=\varphi _ b$, and

\[ \psi _ t\geq \varphi _ t,\quad t\in (a,b). \]

Define

\[ \gamma _ t= \begin{cases} \psi _ t,& \textup{if }t\in (a,b),\\ \varphi _ t,& \textup{if }t\in [0,1]\setminus (a,b). \end{cases} \]

Then $(\gamma _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic.

Proof. The $L^1$-convergence of $\gamma _ t$ to $\varphi _0$ at $0$ and $\varphi _1$ at $1$ follows immediately from the corresponding property for $(\varphi _ t)_ t$ and $(\psi _ t)_ t$. It remains to prove that the complexification of $(\gamma _ t)_ t$ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh.

Let

\[ S=\{ z\in \mathbb {C}:0<\operatorname{Re}z<1\} ,\quad S'=\{ z\in \mathbb {C}:a<\operatorname{Re}z<b\} . \]

Let $\Phi $ and $\Psi $ be the complexifications of $(\varphi _ t)_ t$ and $(\psi _ t)_ t$ on the corresponding strips. Since $\Psi \geq \Phi $ on $X\times S'$, the complexification of $(\gamma _ t)_ t$ is equal to $\Phi \lor \Psi $ on $X\times S'$ and to $\Phi $ on $X\times (S\setminus S')$. By the gluing lemma corollary 1.2.13, it is enough to verify that

\[ \varlimsup _{\substack {S'\ni z'\to z\\ x'\to x}}\Psi (x',z')\leq \Phi (x,z) \]

at every point $(x,z)\in X\times S$ with $\operatorname{Re}z=a$ or $\operatorname{Re}z=b$.

Suppose for instance that $a>0$, and let $(x_ j,z_ j)$ tend to $(x,z)$ with $\operatorname{Re}z=a$ and $a<\operatorname{Re}z_ j<b$. Set $t_ j=\operatorname{Re}z_ j$. Since $(\psi _ t)_ t$ is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _ a$ to $\varphi _ b$, the sequence $\psi _{t_ j}$ converges to $\varphi _ a$ in $L^1$. Applying corollary 1.2.8 to this sequence gives

\[ \varphi _ a=\inf _{m>0}\left(\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq m}{}\! {}^{*}\psi _{t_ j}\right). \]

For fixed $m$, the upper semicontinuity of $\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq m}{}\! {}^{*}\psi _{t_ j}$ gives

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\psi _{t_ j}(x_ j)\leq \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq m}{}\! {}^{*}\psi _{t_ j}\right)(x). \]

Letting $m\to \infty $, we get

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\psi _{t_ j}(x_ j)\leq \varphi _ a(x). \]

Since $\Phi (x,z)=\varphi _ a(x)$ when $\operatorname{Re}z=a$, this is the desired boundary inequality along $\{ \operatorname{Re}z=a\} $. The same argument applies along $\{ \operatorname{Re}z=b\} $ when $b<1$. Hence corollary 1.2.13 shows that the pasted complexification is $p_1^*\theta $-psh. $\square$

Definition 4.1.12. A ray $\ell =(\ell _ t)_{t\geq 0}$ is a subgeodesic ray in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ if for any $0\leq a\leq b$, the segment $(\ell _{t})_{t\in [a,b]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We say $\ell $ emanates from $\ell _0$.

The complexification of a subgeodesic ray $\ell $ is defined as the potential

\[ \Phi \colon X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z>0\} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad (x,z)\mapsto \ell _{\operatorname{Re}z}(x). \]

Note that $\Phi $ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh, where $p_1\colon X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z>0\} \rightarrow X$ is the natural projection.

Remark 4.1.13.As in 4.1.3, one may also define the complexification as a function $X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}: 0<|z|<1\} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$.
