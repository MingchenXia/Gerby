12.6.38 Lengths of the two ends96431078A25B

The interval $I$ has two ends, corresponding to $v\to \infty $ and $v\to -1/M$. Whether they lie at finite distance depends on how $a$ attains its extreme values. Assume that each of the extreme sets $\{ a=0\} $ and $\{ a=M\} $ is a finite disjoint union of smooth compact real submanifolds $Z_ i$ of positive codimension, and that near each component, respectively,

\[ a\asymp \operatorname {dist}(Z_ i)^{k_ i},\qquad M-a\asymp \operatorname {dist}(Z_ i)^{k_ i},\qquad k_ i>0, \]

where $\asymp $ means comparability up to positive constant factors and $\operatorname {dist}(Z_ i)$ is the distance to $Z_ i$ for a fixed Riemannian metric on $B$. The function $a$, respectively $M-a$, is then bounded below by a positive constant outside small disjoint tubes around these components. Define, with minima over the components $Z_ i$ of the respective extreme set,

\[ r_0=\min _{\{ a=0\} } \frac{\operatorname {codim}_{\mathbb R}Z_ i}{k_ i},\qquad r_ M=\min _{\{ a=M\} } \frac{\operatorname {codim}_{\mathbb R}Z_ i}{k_ i}. \]

Lemma 12.6.39.1EF963CF5EA2 The end $v\to \infty $ of $I$ has finite length exactly when $r_0>1$, and the end $v\to -1/M$ exactly when $r_ M>1$. More precisely, at the end $v\to \infty $ put $f=a$, $\epsilon =1/v$ and $r=r_0$; at the end $v\to -1/M$ put $f=M-a$, $\epsilon =-1/v-M$ and $r=r_ M$. Let

\[ H_ f=\int _ B\frac{\sigma }{f+\epsilon },\quad J_ f=\int _ B\frac{\sigma }{(f+\epsilon )^2},\quad K_ f=\int _ B\frac{\sigma }{(f+\epsilon )^3}. \]

Then $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ at the end, and

DB02B7FF941A 12.6.40
\begin{equation} \rho (v)\, \mathrm{d}v^2=G_ f(\epsilon )\, \mathrm{d}\epsilon ^2, \quad G_ f=\frac2n\left[\frac{K_ f}{H_ f} -\left(\frac{J_ f}{H_ f}\right)^2\right]. \end{equation}

Moreover, $G_ f\asymp \epsilon ^{-2}$ for $r<1$ and $G_ f\asymp \epsilon ^{-2}/\log (1/\epsilon )$ for $r=1$, while for $r>1$ the function $\sqrt{G_ f}$ is integrable near $\epsilon =0$.

Proof. At the end $v\to \infty $, where $\epsilon =1/v$, we have

\[ \ell _ v=\epsilon -\frac{\epsilon ^2}{a+\epsilon },\qquad \mathrm{d}v=-\epsilon ^{-2}\mathrm{d}\epsilon . \]

At the end $v\to -1/M$, where $\epsilon =-1/v-M$, we obtain

\[ \ell _ v=\frac{(M+\epsilon )^2}{M-a+\epsilon }-(M+\epsilon ), \qquad \mathrm{d}v=(M+\epsilon )^{-2}\mathrm{d}\epsilon . \]

In both cases $\ell _ v$ is an affine function of $1/(f+\epsilon )$ with coefficients depending only on $\epsilon $, and (12.6.40) expresses $\rho (v)\, \mathrm{d}v^2$ through the variance of $1/(f+\epsilon )$ under the probability $\sigma /[H_ f(f+\epsilon )]$. This probability is $\nu _ v$, the marginal in (12.6.35), because $L$ is a positive multiple of $f+\epsilon $ and positive scaling of $L$ does not change that marginal.

Integration over tubes around the components $Z_ i$, splitting the normal radius at $\epsilon ^{1/k_ i}$, gives

\[ H_ f\asymp \begin{cases} \epsilon ^{r-1}& r<1,\\ \log (1/\epsilon )& r=1,\\ 1& r>1, \end{cases} \qquad K_ f\asymp \begin{cases} \epsilon ^{r-3}& r<3,\\ \log (1/\epsilon )& r=3,\\ 1& r>3. \end{cases} \]

The analogous threshold for $J_ f$ is two. For a finite sum of tube integrals, the smallest ratio governs.

If $r<1$, choose a component attaining $r$, and two normal annuli at fixed multiples of $\epsilon ^{1/k_ i}$, so far apart that the two-sided bounds separate their ranges of $f/\epsilon $. Each annulus has probability bounded below, since its weighted mass and $H_ f$ both have order $\epsilon ^{r-1}$, and the values of $1/(f+\epsilon )$ on the two annuli differ by at least $c\epsilon ^{-1}$. The identity

\[ \operatorname {Var}_\nu T =\frac12\iint (T(x)-T(y))^2\, \mathrm{d}\nu (x)\mathrm{d}\nu (y) \]

applied to $T=1/(f+\epsilon )$ gives the lower bound, and $T\leq \epsilon ^{-1}$ gives the upper bound.

If $r=1$, the near annulus has probability at least $c/\log (1/\epsilon )$. In the same tube, the normal radii between $\epsilon ^{1/(2k_ i)}$ and a fixed small radius carry probability bounded below, by the logarithmic integral. There $1/(f+\epsilon )\leq C\epsilon ^{-1/2}$, while $1/(f+\epsilon )\geq c\epsilon ^{-1}$ on the near annulus. The variance identity and the bound $G_ f\leq 2K_ f/(nH_ f)$ prove the claimed logarithmic estimate. In both cases $r<1$ and $r=1$, the integral of $\sqrt{G_ f}$ diverges at $\epsilon =0$.

For $r>1$, $H_ f$ tends to a finite positive integral and $G_ f\leq C K_ f$. For $1<r<3$, $\sqrt{G_ f}$ is therefore bounded by $C\epsilon ^{(r-3)/2}$, whose exponent exceeds $-1$. At $r=3$ the bound is $C\sqrt{\log (1/\epsilon )}$, and for $r>3$ it is a constant. All these bounds are integrable. $\square$

Theorem 12.6.41.72BDB634BD0E Under the hypotheses of lemma 12.6.39 on the extreme sets, the metric completion of $\mathscr S$ is a closed Euclidean strip if $r_0,r_ M>1$, a closed Euclidean half-plane if exactly one of them exceeds one, and the whole Euclidean plane otherwise. Each boundary line of the completion is parametrized by $b>0$, and its points are represented by the forms

\begin{align*} \theta _+(b)& =b\kappa +\frac{H_ a}{nb^ m}a w, & H_ a& =\int _ B\frac{\sigma }{a}\quad (r_0>1),\\ \theta _-(b)& =b\kappa +\frac{H_-}{nb^ m}(1-a/M)w, & H_-& =\int _ B\frac{\sigma }{1-a/M}\quad (r_ M>1). \end{align*}

These forms are smooth, semipositive, nef, nonclosed and not big, and their variational volume is one. The completion has no other boundary points.

Proof. The completion of the convex product $\mathbb R\times I$ of proposition 12.6.36 is its Euclidean closure. Along a Cauchy sequence, $Y$ converges, so $b$ has a finite positive limit, and $R$ converges to a point of $I$ or to a finite endpoint of $I$. Lemma 12.6.39 decides which endpoints are finite and gives the three alternatives.

On $\mathscr S$ we have $e=Q(v)/(nb^ m)$ and $c=ve$. At the end $v\to \infty $, $vQ(v)\to H_ a$ by monotone convergence, which gives the coefficient limit $\theta _+(b)$. At the end $v\to -1/M$, write $1+va=1-a/M+(v+1/M)a$; dominated convergence gives $Q(v)\to H_-$, and hence the limit $\theta _-(b)$. These are the coefficient limits at all finite ends. The forms are semipositive, hence nef, and they are nonclosed. Increasing their fiber coefficient by $\epsilon >0$ makes them positive, and (12.6.33) shows that the resulting volumes tend to one as $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. Monotonicity bounds the root volume of the limit form by this limit. Conversely, each fixed Gauduchon quotient is continuous in $\epsilon $, so the limit of the root volumes is at most every quotient of the limit form, hence at most its root volume. Thus the variational volume of $\theta _\pm (b)$ is one.

Suppose that some quasi-plurisubharmonic $u$ satisfied

\[ \theta _\pm +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \delta (\kappa +w),\qquad \delta >0. \]

For every smooth nonnegative top-degree form $\eta $ on $B$, its pullback is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed for degree reasons, and $\kappa \wedge \eta =0$. Stokes' theorem for currents therefore gives

\[ \int _ B(L_\pm -\delta )\eta \geq 0. \]

where $L_\pm $ is the fiber coefficient of $\theta _\pm $. But $L_\pm $ vanishes somewhere, and a nonzero $\eta $ supported where $L_\pm <\delta $ contradicts this inequality. Thus $\theta _\pm $ is not big. $\square$
