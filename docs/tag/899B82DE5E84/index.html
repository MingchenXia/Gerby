Interior smoothness on the regular locus

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 14.17 Interior smoothness on the regular locus

Throughout this section, $\theta $ is a smooth big real $(1,1)$-form, and $\rho $, $Z=\{ \rho =-\infty \} $ and $\Omega =X\setminus Z$ are the strict analytic barrier and its regular locus fixed in section 14.12. Thus $\rho \leq 0$ has analytic singularities with smooth remainder, and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \omega $. If $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has minimal singularities, then $\varphi \geq V_\theta -C\geq \rho -C$ for a constant $C$. Hence $\varphi $ is locally bounded on $\Omega $, and its Monge–Ampère measure there is the bounded-potential product.

For such a potential, theorem 14.16.1 gives local Hölder continuity on $\Omega $ when the density belongs to $L^ p$ for some $p>1$. We show that a smooth strictly positive density on the whole of $\Omega $ then makes the potential smooth on $\Omega $. Local information alone cannot give this: example 14.20.5 below is a locally Lipschitz plurisubharmonic function with constant Monge–Ampère density that is not $C^1$. The argument therefore uses the barrier globally. By the proper gap of lemma 1.19.7, the difference between the solution and the barrier attains a minimum on the closed set where smoothness fails, and strict positivity of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ turns this minimum into a quadratic separation. Near the minimum, smooth Dirichlet approximants have traces of polynomial growth; the separation improves this growth until a uniform trace bound yields elliptic regularity.

Theorem 14.17.1. Suppose $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has minimal singularities and is locally Hölder continuous on $\Omega $. Let $c>0$ and let $f$ be smooth and strictly positive on $\Omega $. Assume

\[ (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n=cf\, \omega ^ n\quad \text{on }\Omega . \]

Then $\varphi \in C^\infty (\Omega )$ and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi >0$ there.

The Hölder hypothesis holds when, in addition, $f\in L^ p(X,\omega ^ n)$ for some $p>1$, by theorem 14.16.1; see corollary 14.17.11. The theorem also applies when local Hölder continuity is obtained by another argument.

The proof is by contradiction and occupies the rest of the section. Let $\mathcal S\subset \Omega $ be the closed set of points near which $\varphi $ is not smooth, suppose that $\mathcal S\neq \varnothing $, and let $p\in \mathcal S$ minimize $\varphi -\rho $ on $\mathcal S$. On a small ball centered at $p$ the argument has four steps.

  1. A comparison principle for continuous solutions (section 14.17.1) and the smooth Dirichlet problem give smooth solutions $u_ j$ of the equation that converge uniformly to $\varphi $ at a polynomial rate in $j$, while the traces of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$, measured in Euclidean coordinates, grow at most polynomially in $j$ (section 14.17.3).

  2. Near $p$, the quadratic separation along $\mathcal S$, together with uniform trace bounds on compact sets where $\varphi $ is already smooth, improves the trace growth to $O(j^\gamma )$ for every $\gamma >0$. Interpolation with the convergence rate then gives uniform $C^{1,\alpha }$ bounds for every $\alpha <1$ (section 14.17.4).

  3. A third-order estimate whose constants depend polynomially on the ellipticity turns uniform $C^{1,\alpha }$ bounds, for $\alpha $ close to one, into a uniform trace bound (section 14.17.5).

  4. A continuous solution with bounded trace is smooth (section 14.17.2). Hence $\varphi $ is smooth near $p$, contradicting $p\in \mathcal S$ (section 14.17.6).
