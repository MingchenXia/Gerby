Corollary 3.1.4461DB6FF8D92F

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Corollary 3.1.44

Corollary 3.1.44. Let $\varphi ,\psi ,\gamma _1,\ldots ,\gamma _ n\in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

3.109
\begin{equation} \int _ X (\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\gamma _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{\gamma _ n} \end{equation}

is well-defined and is in $\mathbb {R}$.

Proof. Step 1. We first consider the case $\psi =\phi $.

We can find $C>0$ so that $\varphi \leq \phi +C$. Therefore, $\phi -\varphi \geq -C$ almost everywhere with respect to $\theta _{\gamma _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{\gamma _ n}$. So the integral (3.109) is well-defined and takes value in $\mathbb {R}\cup \{ \infty \} $. We show that the integral is finite. For this purpose, we may replace $\varphi $ by $\varphi -C$ and assume that $\varphi \leq \phi $. Put

\[ \eta =n^{-1}(\gamma _1+\cdots +\gamma _ n). \]

Then by the multi-linearity of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 6 and positivity, we have

\[ \int _ X (\phi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\gamma _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{\gamma _ n}\leq n^ n\int _ X (\phi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\eta }^ n. \]

Thanks to corollary 3.1.42, $\eta \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Choose $A>0$ so that $\eta \leq \phi +A$. Applying 3.1.43 to $\varphi ,\eta $ and $\phi +A$, we get

\[ \int _ X(\phi +A-\varphi )\, \theta _{\eta }^ n \leq 2\int _ X(\phi +A-\varphi )\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n +2\int _ X(\phi +A-\eta )\, \theta _{\eta }^ n<\infty . \]

Hence $\int _ X(\phi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\eta }^ n<\infty $, and Step 1 follows.

Step 2. We handle the general case. Take $C>0$ so that

\[ \psi \leq \phi +C,\quad \varphi \leq \phi +C. \]

Then

\[ (\psi -C)-\phi \leq \psi -\varphi \leq \phi -(\varphi -C). \]

Step 1 applied to $\varphi -C$ and to $\psi -C$ shows that $\phi -(\varphi -C)$ and $\phi -(\psi -C)$ are integrable with respect to $\theta _{\gamma _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{\gamma _ n}$. Hence the positive and negative parts of $\psi -\varphi $ are both integrable, so (3.109) is well-defined and finite. $\square$
