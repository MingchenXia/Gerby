5.1 The trace map1699E04AE638

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 5.1 The trace map

Adding a constant to a potential does not change its current, so we normalize potentials by their mean and consider

\[ \mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V} =\left\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{d}V=0\right\} . \]

For $u\in \mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V}$ we call the measure

\[ \mathcal T_\gamma (u) =(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1} \]

the Gauduchon trace of $u$, and $\mathcal T_\gamma $ the trace map. By proposition 4.2.1 (by Stokes' theorem when $n=1$), $\mathcal T_\gamma (u)$ is a positive measure of total mass

\[ M_{\theta ,\gamma }=\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}. \]

The trace map is governed by the linear operator

\[ Af=\frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}}{\mathrm{d}V},\qquad f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R). \]

It is a real second-order elliptic operator without zeroth-order term, and $\mathcal T_\gamma (u)-\mathcal T_\gamma (v)=A(u-v)\, \mathrm{d}V$ in the sense of distributions.

Theorem 5.1.1. The trace map $\mathcal T_\gamma $ is an affine homeomorphism from the compact convex set $\mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V}$, with its $L^1$ topology, onto a compact convex set of positive measures of mass $M_{\theta ,\gamma }$, with the weak topology. In other words, for $u_ j,u\in \mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V}$,

\[ u_ j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \mathcal T_\gamma (u_ j)\rightharpoonup \mathcal T_\gamma (u). \]

If $M_{\theta ,\gamma }=0$ and $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\ne \varnothing $, both sets consist of a single point.

Proof. The set $\mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V}$ is convex. It is the image of the compact set $\mathcal K_\theta $ of theorem 1.4.2 under the continuous map $v\mapsto v-\int _ Xv\, \mathrm{d}V$, and is therefore compact.

If $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1$, then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\to \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ as currents, so the trace measures converge in the sense of distributions. Since they are positive measures of the same finite mass, and every continuous function is a uniform limit of smooth functions, they also converge weakly as measures. Thus $\mathcal T_\gamma $ is continuous.

To prove injectivity, suppose that $\mathcal T_\gamma (u)=\mathcal T_\gamma (v)$. Then $A(u-v)=0$ in the sense of distributions. Elliptic regularity for $A$ shows that $u-v$ agrees almost everywhere with a smooth function, and the maximum principle shows that this function is constant. Its mean is zero, so $u=v$ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3.

A continuous injection from a compact space into a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its image; here the target is the space of positive measures of mass $M_{\theta ,\gamma }$ with the weak topology. Since $\mathcal T_\gamma $ is affine, its image is convex. If $M_{\theta ,\gamma }=0$, every trace measure is zero, so injectivity shows that $\mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V}$ has at most one point; it has exactly one when $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is nonempty, since the mean of any $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function can be subtracted. In this case lemma 4.3.3 also gives $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=0$ for the unique normalized potential $u$, which is therefore smooth. $\square$

The inverse of the trace map can be described by a Green operator for $A$. By the maximum principle, $\ker A=\mathbb R$, and $A$ has Fredholm index zero, because the linear path joining it to a Laplace operator consists of elliptic operators. Let $A^*$ be the formal adjoint of $A$ with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$. Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ XAf\, \mathrm{d}V=0$ for all smooth $f$, that is, $A^*1=0$; hence $\ker A^*=\mathbb R$ as well. By the Fredholm alternative, $A$ maps smooth functions onto the smooth functions $g$ with $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm{d}V=0$, and the same holds for $A^*$. In particular, $A^*$ has a continuous inverse on smooth functions of mean zero, normalized to take values of mean zero. By duality with this inverse, every top-degree distribution $\sigma $ of total mass zero, and in particular every finite signed measure with $\sigma (X)=0$, determines a unique distribution $G_\gamma \sigma $ with mean zero against $\mathrm{d}V$ such that

5.1
\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(G_\gamma \sigma )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=\sigma . \end{equation}

Inverting the trace map at a positive measure therefore amounts to solving (5.1) and then checking positivity of the full current.

Proposition 5.1.2. A positive measure $\nu $ of mass $M_{\theta ,\gamma }$ belongs to the image of $\mathcal T_\gamma $ if and only if the distribution

\[ w=G_\gamma \bigl (\nu -\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}\bigr ) \]

satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\geq 0$. In that case the $\theta $-plurisubharmonic representative of $w$ is the unique $u\in \mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V}$ with $\mathcal T_\gamma (u)=\nu $.

Proof. If $\nu =\mathcal T_\gamma (u)$ with $u\in \mathcal K^0_{\theta ,\mathrm{d}V}$, then $u$ is a mean-zero solution of (5.1) with $\sigma =\nu -\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$, so uniqueness gives $w=u$ as distributions. Conversely, if $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\geq 0$, then proposition 1.1.2 gives a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic representative $u$ of $w$. It has mean zero by construction, and (5.1) gives $\mathcal T_\gamma (u)=\nu $. $\square$

In dimension one the positivity condition is automatic, because $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=\nu $; thus every positive measure of mass $M_{\theta ,\gamma }$ is the trace of a normalized potential. In higher dimensions, positivity of the trace measure $\nu $ does not imply positivity of the $(1,1)$-current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$, and the condition in proposition 5.1.2 must be checked separately.
