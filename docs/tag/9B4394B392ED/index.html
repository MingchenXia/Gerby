Locally conformally Kähler geometry

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 29 Locally conformally Kähler geometry

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$. A locally conformally Kähler (LCK) metric is a Hermitian metric that becomes Kähler after local conformal changes, and its Lee form measures the obstruction to making this change globally. On a covering on which the Lee form is exact, the Kähler metric and its admissible potentials change by the same character under deck transformations. Descending this potential calculus to $X$ gives a twisted complex Hessian with a zeroth-order term, so that even adding a constant to a potential changes the metric. The standard Vaisman metric on the Hopf manifold illustrates the difference between the two potential theories: although it is locally conformally Kähler, its ordinary potentials have unbounded Monge–Ampère masses.

Prescribing the volume while keeping the Lee class and the Gauduchon gauge couples the determinant equation with an equation for the conformal factor. For a volume invariant under the Lee and anti-Lee flows of a Vaisman metric, uniqueness for the ordinary smooth equation gives a potential that is constant along these flows. Stokes' theorem in the transverse directions fixes the normalizing constant, and changing the Lee form reconstructs the required Vaisman metric. On scalar Hopf manifolds, radial symmetry reduces the coupled equations to a periodic ordinary differential equation. Its positive solutions are ordered by their Lee periods, so the metric is determined by its Lee period and its volume; for a nonconstant radial volume, this metric is not Vaisman. A different compatibility condition gives rigidity: when an LCK metric is both Gauduchon and Chern–Ricci flat, the Bianchi and Bochner identities force its harmonic Lee form to be parallel.

The Lee form also produces a closed curvature class that can be nonzero in Bott–Chern cohomology while vanishing in de Rham cohomology. Pairing these classes with all Gauduchon metrics defines the Lee–Gauduchon cone, which detects their pseudoeffectivity and is invariant under bimeromorphic maps. Strongly Gauduchon metrics exclude all nonzero positive de Rham-exact currents and bound the linear mass of closed positive currents by their de Rham classes. The Calabi–Eckmann example separates this condition from the presence of zero in the Lee–Gauduchon cone, and a balanced elliptic fibration shows that it still allows zero lower Monge–Ampère mass.
