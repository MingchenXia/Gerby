Domination and proper equations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 12.12.2 Domination and proper equations

The weak modified comparison estimate makes strict domination possible on an arbitrary Hermitian background. A convex rescaling supplies a positive volume term, while choosing a sufficiently small sublevel set absorbs the torsion error. Canonical representatives detect its positive volume even when the set is not open.

Theorem 12.12.7. Let $u,v$ be bounded admissible. If, for some $0\leq c<1$,

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(u) \leq c\mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(v), \]

then $u\geq v$ everywhere, on an arbitrary Hermitian background. In particular, $H_ m(u)(\{ u<v\} )=0$ implies $u\geq v$.

Proof. Add the same constant to $u,v$ so that $v\geq 1$. This preserves the measure hypothesis and the desired conclusion. Choose $0<\alpha <1$ with $\alpha ^ m>c$ and put $V=\alpha v$, $\eta =1-\alpha $. Suppose $S=\inf _ X(u-V)<0$. For $0<s<-S$, the set $E_ s=\{ u<V+S+s\} $ is contained in $\{ u<V\} $ and hence in $\{ u<v\} $. It has positive volume. Indeed, if its volume were zero, then $u\geq V+S+s$ almost everywhere. Both functions are bounded admissible with their canonical representatives, so 12.1.8 would give this order everywhere, contradicting the definition of $S$. The argument does not require $E_ s$ to be open or the infimum to be attained.

Choose also $s<\eta ^3/C$, where $C$ is from 12.12.6. By 12.11.3 and the measure hypothesis,

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha ^ m H_ m(v)(E_ s)+\eta ^ m\int _{E_ s}\omega ^ n & \leq H_ m(V)(E_ s)\\ & \leq (1+Cs\eta ^{-m})H_ m(u)(E_ s)\\ & \leq c(1+Cs\eta ^{-m})H_ m(v)(E_ s). \end{aligned} \]

If $c>0$, take $s$ still smaller so that $c(1+Cs\eta ^{-m})<\alpha ^ m$. If $c=0$, this strict inequality already holds. All measures are finite and positive, so the display forces the positive-volume term to be zero, a contradiction. Hence $S\geq 0$ and $u\geq \alpha v$. Let $\alpha $ increase to one, with $\alpha ^ m>c$. Since $v$ is bounded, $\alpha v\to v$ uniformly, and therefore $u\geq v$. The final assertion is $c=0$. $\square$

Corollary 12.12.8. Fix $a>0$. If bounded admissible $u,v$ satisfy

\[ e^{-av}H_ m(v)\geq e^{-au}H_ m(u), \]

then $u\geq v$. Consequently, for any fixed finite positive Radon measure $\nu $, the equation $H_ m(u)=e^{au}\nu $ has at most one bounded admissible solution.

Proof. For $\delta >0$, put $E_\delta =\{ u<v-\delta \} $. Multiplying the assumed measure inequality by the positive bounded Borel function $e^{au}$ gives on $E_\delta $

\[ H_ m(u)\leq e^{a(u-v)}H_ m(v) \leq e^{-a\delta }H_ m(v-\delta ). \]

The last identity uses invariance under constants. Since $e^{-a\delta }<1$, 12.12.7 applied to $u,v-\delta $ gives $u\geq v-\delta $ everywhere. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ gives $u\geq v$. Two solutions of the stated equation have equal weighted measures $e^{-au}H_ m(u)=\nu $. Applying the order assertion in both directions proves uniqueness. $\square$

A strictly increasing exponent also controls changes in logarithmic data for a fixed Radon right-hand measure.

Proposition 12.12.9. Let $A<B$ and $f$ be smooth near $X\times [A,B]$, with $\partial _ t f\geq \kappa >0$ there, and let $\nu $ be a finite positive Radon measure. Suppose bounded admissible $u,v$ have values in $[A,B]$ and satisfy

\[ H_ m(u)=e^{f(x,u)+h_1}\nu ,\qquad H_ m(v)=e^{f(x,v)+h_2}\nu , \]

where $h_1,h_2$ are bounded Borel functions. Then

\[ \| u-v\| _\infty \leq \kappa ^{-1}\| h_1-h_2\| _{L^\infty (\nu )}. \]

Proof. Put $K=\| h_1-h_2\| _{L^\infty (\nu )}$ and fix $s>K/\kappa $. On $E_ s=\{ v+s<u\} $, the actual values $u,v$ lie in $[A,B]$, so $f(x,u)-f(x,v)\geq \kappa s$. The equations and the essential bound on $h_2-h_1$ therefore give, as restricted measures,

\[ \mathbf1_{E_ s}H_ m(v+s) =\mathbf1_{E_ s}H_ m(v) \leq e^{K-\kappa s}\mathbf1_{E_ s}H_ m(u). \]

The constant is smaller than one. Applying 12.12.7 to $v+s,u$ gives $u\leq v+s$ pointwise. Let $s\downarrow K/\kappa $ and exchange $u,v$ to obtain the norm bound. The exponent is evaluated only at $u,v$; the shifted comparison potential need not take its values in $[A,B]$. $\square$

Corollary 12.12.10. Under the hypotheses of 12.7.3, every bounded admissible solution with values in $[A,B]$ is its smooth solution. In particular, for every $a>0$ and smooth real $H$, the equation $H_ m(u)=e^{au+H}\omega ^ n$ has exactly one bounded admissible solution, and that solution is smooth and strictly admissible.

Proof. For a continuous admissible potential, smooth decreasing admissible approximations converge uniformly on compact $X$ by Dini's theorem. Thus 12.10.8, 12.6.1 define the same Hessian measure on the continuous class. The smooth solution of 12.7.3 therefore solves the bounded equation as well. Apply 12.12.9 with $\nu =\omega ^ n$ and $h_1=h_2=0$ to identify any other bounded solution in $[A,B]$ with it. For the final assertion, smooth existence is already 12.5.1, and 12.12.8 with $\nu =e^ H\omega ^ n$ gives uniqueness among all bounded solutions. $\square$
