7 Weighted Monge–Ampère measuresA6A92DB9F7DA

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 7 Weighted Monge–Ampère measures

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, and let $\omega $ be a Hermitian form. Capacity refers to the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ of section 1.8. For nonpositive $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ we write

\[ \mu _ u=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u) \]

for the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 6.1.2, and we put

\[ r_ n=\max (n-2,0). \]

By chapter 6, the measure $\mu _ u$ can have infinite mass when the bounded mass property fails. Its mass above depth $t$, on the superlevel set $\{ u\geq -t\} $, is nevertheless bounded by a polynomial of degree $r_ n$ in $t$, because the masses of bounded potentials grow at most polynomially in their range. A weight that decreases sufficiently fast in the depth $-u$ compensates for this growth. If $w:[0,+\infty )\to [0,+\infty )$ is a nonincreasing function with $w(t)\to 0$ as $t\to +\infty $, we call

\[ w(-u)\, \mu _ u \]

the damped Monge–Ampère measure of $u$ with weight $w$; the weight is set to zero on the pole set $\{ u=-\infty \} $, which $\mu _ u$ does not charge. The main examples are the exponential weights $\mathrm{e}^{-pt}$ and the polynomial weights $(1+t)^{-q}$. For suitable weights the damped measures are finite, uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to capacity, and continuous under convergence in capacity, a property that the extended measures themselves lack. Removing the weight requires uniform control of the mass on deep sublevel sets; we characterize this control and show by examples that such mass can persist although the potentials converge in capacity.
