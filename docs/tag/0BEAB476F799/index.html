Moving bounded carrier measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 31.4 Moving bounded carrier measures

In the approximation scheme of section 31.6, the measures of the approximating solutions are dominated by Monge–Ampère measures of uniformly bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic potentials $\psi _ j$, which vary with $j$. For a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic potential $\psi $, write $\omega _\psi =\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi $. A single dominating measure is then unavailable, but an exponential domination $\mu _{u_ j}\leq A\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\omega _{\psi _ j}^ n$ still provides a common lower envelope. A smaller-background potential and a strict big potential give comparison functions whose constants are uniform in $j$. If every $u_ j$ has the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi $, smaller-background comparison gives the same construction even when $v_-(\theta )=0$. Convergence of the potentials $\psi _ j$ in capacity then identifies the limiting equation.

Proposition 31.4.1. Fix $A,\lambda >0$, $C\in \mathbb R$ and $L\geq 0$. For $j\geq 1$, suppose that $u_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $u_ j\leq \varphi +C$, and

31.7
\begin{equation} \label{eq:exponential-bounded-reference-domination} \mu _{u_ j}\leq A \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\omega _{\psi _ j}^ n, \qquad \psi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega ),\qquad \| \psi _ j\| _\infty \leq L. \end{equation}

Assume either $v_-(\theta )>0$ or $u_ j\sim \varphi $ for every $j$. Then

\[ h=P_\theta \left(\inf _ j u_ j\right)\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

No convergence of $\psi _ j$ or $u_ j$ is assumed, and in the second alternative the constants in $u_ j\sim \varphi $ need not be uniform in $j$.

Proof. Translate all $u_ j$ by one constant so that $u_ j\leq \varphi \leq 0$; this changes $A$ by a fixed factor. Translate each $\psi _ j$ so that it takes values in $[-2L,0]$; this does not change its measure. Set $q=P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ and choose $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $a>0$ such that

\[ q\leq \varphi , \qquad \rho \leq 0,\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq a\omega . \]

Here $q\not\equiv -\infty $ by the model condition, and $\rho $ exists because $\theta $ is big. Fix $0<\delta <(1-\varepsilon )a$.

For a fixed real $b\geq 1$, put $r_{j,b}=Q_ b(u_ j,\varphi )$. The stretching lemma puts it in $\mathcal E_\varphi $, and $r_{j,b}\leq u_ j\leq 0$. Contact support and positivity imply

\[ \mu _{r_{j,b}} \leq b^ n\mathbf1_{D_{j,b}}\mu _{u_ j}, \qquad D_{j,b}=\{ b^{-1}r_{j,b}+(1-b^{-1})\varphi =u_ j\} . \]

On this contact set, $\varphi \leq 0$ gives $u_ j\leq r_{j,b}/b$. Thus (31.7) yields

31.8
\begin{equation} \label{eq:stretched-exponential-domination} \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r_{j,b}/b}\mu _{r_{j,b}} \leq b^ n A\omega _{\psi _ j}^ n, \end{equation}

where the weights are evaluated off the pluripolar pole sets.

Choose $C_ b\geq 0$ so large that

\[ \delta ^ n\exp (\lambda C_ b/b)\geq b^ n A, \]

and define

\[ s_{j,b}=q+(1-\varepsilon )\rho +\delta \psi _ j-C_ b. \]

This is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function, not identically $-\infty $, since

\[ \begin{split} \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s_{j,b} & = (\varepsilon \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q) +(1-\varepsilon )(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho ) +\delta \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ j\\ & \geq \delta \omega _{\psi _ j}. \end{split} \]

Consequently $\mu _{s_{j,b}}\geq \delta ^ n\omega _{\psi _ j}^ n$: this inequality holds away from the poles by the local positive product inequality, and then globally because the bounded reference measure does not charge pluripolar sets. Also $s_{j,b}\leq -C_ b$ and $s_{j,b}\preceq \varphi $. Together with (31.8), we conclude

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s_{j,b}/b}\mu _{s_{j,b}} \geq b^ n A\omega _{\psi _ j}^ n \geq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r_{j,b}/b}\mu _{r_{j,b}}. \]

If $v_-(\theta )>0$, apply corollary 31.2.4 with first potential $r_{j,b}$ and second potential $s_{j,b}$. It gives $r_{j,b}\geq s_{j,b}$.

In the second alternative, $r_{j,b}\sim \varphi $: if $u_ j\geq \varphi -K_ j$, the potential $\varphi -bK_ j$ is a competitor for $r_{j,b}$. Hence the smaller-background envelope $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(r_{j,b})$ is not identically $-\infty $, and $s_{j,b}\preceq r_{j,b}$. Therefore corollary 14.1.3 also gives $r_{j,b}\geq s_{j,b}$, without a lower-mass hypothesis.

Since $\psi _ j\geq -2L$, we now have a lower bound independent of $j$:

\[ H_ b=q+(1-\varepsilon )\rho -2\delta L-C_ b \leq r_{j,b}. \]

The potential $H_ b$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty $. By the definition of $r_{j,b}$, therefore,

\[ b^{-1}H_ b+(1-b^{-1})\varphi \leq u_ j \quad \hbox{for every }j. \]

For $b=1$ this shows that the infimum envelope $h$ is not identically $-\infty $. For general $b$, the left side is a competitor for $h$, so it lies below $h$; it is thus a competitor in the defining condition of the relative class for $b$. Since $h\leq \varphi $, we obtain $h\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Undoing the common translation finishes the proof. $\square$

Corollary 31.4.2. Under the hypotheses of proposition 31.4.1, if $u_ j\to u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1(X)$, then $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

Proof. The common envelope gives $h\leq u_ j\leq \varphi +C$. Along an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence, lemma 1.3.3 gives $h\leq u\leq \varphi +C$, and upward stability gives the conclusion. $\square$
