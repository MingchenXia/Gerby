From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 11 Weighted L2 estimates and holomorphic extension

Many constructions in the rest of the book need a holomorphic function $F$ on a domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ with a prescribed value at a point, or a prescribed restriction to a hyperplane section, together with a bound for the weighted $L^2$ norm $\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }\, \mathrm dV$, where $\varphi $ is a plurisubharmonic weight. Gluing by a partition of unity does not preserve holomorphicity: if $f$ is holomorphic near the support of a smooth cutoff function $\chi $, then $\chi f$ is a global smooth function, but $\bar\partial (\chi f)=f\, \bar\partial \chi $ need not vanish. If we can solve $\bar\partial u=f\, \bar\partial \chi $ with an estimate in a weighted $L^2$ norm, then $F=\chi f-u$ is holomorphic. The weight has two tasks: it must make the equation solvable with a bound on $u$, and, where values are prescribed, it must be so singular that the finiteness of the weighted norm forces $u$ to vanish there.

The basic existence theorem is Hörmander's estimate (theorem 11.3.3, theorem 11.3.4). By the Hilbert space duality of section 11.1, a solution of $\bar\partial u=\alpha $ with a norm bound exists as soon as an a priori inequality holds for the adjoint operator. For compactly supported forms this inequality follows from an integration-by-parts identity, the Bochner–Kodaira–Morrey–Kohn identity, in which the complex Hessian of the weight appears as a curvature term. To pass from compactly supported forms to the whole domain of the adjoint without any assumption on the boundary, we work on pseudoconvex open sets and modify the weight near the boundary by means of a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion function, whose existence is proved in section 11.2.

In section 11.4 we apply these estimates. Adding the logarithmic pole ${n\log |z-a|^2}$ to the weight prescribes the value of a holomorphic function at the point $a$ (proposition 11.4.1). For extension from a hyperplane, a bounded twist of the weight and an explicit one-variable weight of $\log \log $ type yield the Ohsawa–Takegoshi extension theorem, with a constant that depends only on the width of the domain in the normal direction (theorem 11.4.4). Its consequences for point values and for integrable germs (corollary 11.4.5, corollary 11.4.6) are the analytic input for the multiplier ideals of chapter 12 and the Bergman kernel approximation of chapter 13.

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in section B.11.
