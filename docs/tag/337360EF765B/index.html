Global relative capacity comparison

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Theorem 16.9.8

Theorem 16.9.8. Suppose that $\{ \theta \} $ is big and that $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has positive mass. There are continuous nondecreasing functions $F,G\colon [0,\infty )\to [0,\infty )$, vanishing at zero, such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(E)\leq F(\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)), \qquad \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)\leq G(\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(E)). \]

Consequently, any two capacities of this kind, for possibly different big backgrounds and potentials of positive mass, are bounded by such functions of each other. In particular, they define the same notion of convergence in capacity, in which Borel functions $f_ j$ converge to $f$ if the capacities of the sets $\{ |f_ j-f|>\varepsilon \} $ tend to zero for every $\varepsilon >0$. They also define the same notion of uniform absolute continuity of a family of positive Borel measures: $\lambda (A)$ is small, uniformly over the members $\lambda $ of the family, whenever the Borel set $A$ has small capacity.

Proof. Choose $A\geq 1$ with $\theta \leq \kappa =A\omega $. Every competitor $v$ for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\psi }$, and its measure for $\kappa $ dominates its measure for $\theta $: since $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v=(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)+(\kappa -\theta )$ with a smooth closed semipositive form $\kappa -\theta $, multilinearity and positivity of non-pluripolar products give $\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\rangle \geq \langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\rangle $; hence $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }\leq \operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\psi }$, and the mass of $\psi $ for $\kappa $ is at least its mass for $\theta $, hence positive. Adding a constant to $\psi $ does not change the capacities, so we may assume $\psi \leq 0$. Let $\Phi =P_\kappa [\psi ]$. By theorem 16.4.7, $\Phi $ is a model potential for $\kappa $ with $\psi \leq \Phi $ and with the same mass for $\kappa $ as $\psi $. Thus $\psi \in \mathcal E(X,\kappa ,\Phi )$, and lemma 16.9.7 and proposition 16.9.6 bound $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\psi }$ by a continuous nondecreasing function, vanishing at zero, of $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $.

Finally, rescaling the competitors gives

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega \leq \operatorname {Cap}_\kappa \leq A^ n\operatorname {Cap}_\omega : \]

every competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ is one for $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $, with a larger measure by the same argument, and if $v$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $, then $v/A$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ with measure $A^{-n}(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$. This proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality use the model potential $\phi =P_\theta [\psi ]$ for $\theta $, which has the same positive mass $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle $ as $\psi $. By (16.86), $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa ^ n\leq (C^ n/m)\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$. By theorem 16.8.1 with $a=2$, applied to $\psi $ and $\phi $, whose singularity envelopes agree, the potential $\eta =P_\theta (2\psi -\phi )$ has mass $m$ and satisfies $P_\theta [\eta ]=\phi $ and $(\eta +\phi )/2\leq \psi $.

It remains to bound $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ by a function of $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$. Put $u=(\eta +\phi )/2$. Since $P_\theta [\eta ]=P_\theta [\phi ]$, the preservation of mixed masses in proposition 16.3.2 shows that $u$ also has mass $m$. Since $u\leq \psi $, this means that $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\psi )$, and lemma 16.9.7 bounds $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,u}$ by a continuous nondecreasing function, vanishing at zero, of $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$. If $v$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$, then $h=(v+\eta )/2$ satisfies $u-1/2\leq h\leq u$, so it is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,u}$, and $\mu _ h\geq 2^{-n}\mu _ v$. Thus

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }\leq 2^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,u}. \]

Combining these inequalities with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega \leq \operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $ gives $G$; compositions, positive multiples and $n$th roots preserve continuity, monotonicity and the value zero at the origin. The remaining assertions follow by comparing both capacities with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. $\square$
