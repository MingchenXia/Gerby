From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 26.17.4

Theorem 26.17.4. Let $\beta $ be smooth positive Hermitian with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\beta ^ j)=0$ for $1\leq j\leq n-1$, $V=\int _ X\beta ^ n$, and let $h=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$ for a smooth global real function $u$. Then

\[ \mathscr G_ h=\mathscr G_\beta ,\qquad \{ \Gamma _{0,h}(T):T\in \mathscr G_ h\} =\{ \Gamma _{0,\beta }(T):T\in \mathscr G_\beta \} . \]

Every measure in this common family has a density $k_ T$ satisfying

\[ k_ T\geq 0,\qquad \| k_ T\| _{L^{n/(n-1)}(\mathrm dV)}\leq V^{-1/n}. \]

For every real or complex $p\in L^ n(\mathrm dV)$,

26.105
\begin{equation} \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_ h}\int _ X|p|\, \Gamma _0(T) =V^{-1/n}\| p\| _{L^ n(\mathrm dV)}. \end{equation}

All suprema retain singular and degenerate tests.

Proof. Distributional integration by parts gives $\int h\wedge T=\int \beta \wedge T+\int u\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T =\int \beta \wedge T$. This proves equality of the actual current sets; Lemma 26.17.3 proves equality of their measure sets.

Take a smooth strictly positive real $r$ and put $M=\int r^ n \mathrm dV$. By theorem 9.1.1 there are a smooth potential $w$, a positive form $A=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ and a positive coefficient in its prescribed-volume equation. The mass-invariance hypothesis and theorem 2.2.2 determine that coefficient, giving

26.106
\begin{equation} A^ n=\frac{Vr^ n}{M}\, \mathrm dV. \end{equation}

In particular, the total mass of $A$ is $V$. No estimates uniform in a degenerating smooth density are required.

For every $T\in \mathscr G_\beta $, integration by parts gives $\int A\wedge T=1$. In an $A$-unitary frame, matrix AM–GM gives $\det (P_ A)^{1/n}\leq 1$, including singular coefficient densities. Thus as Radon measures

\[ \Gamma _0(T)\leq (\mathrm dV/A^ n)^{1/n}(A\wedge T). \]

Multiplying by $r$ and using (26.106) yields

26.107
\begin{equation} \int r\, \Gamma _0(T)\leq V^{-1/n}\| r\| _{L^ n(\mathrm dV)}. \end{equation}

Replacing a smooth nonnegative $r$ by $r+\eta $ and letting $\eta \downarrow 0$ extends this bound to nonnegative smooth tests. For smooth real or complex $s$, smooth positive approximations $\sqrt{|s|^2+\eta ^2}$ imply $|\int s\, \Gamma _0(T)|\leq V^{-1/n}\| s\| _ n$. Consequently integration against $\Gamma _0(T)$ extends continuously from smooth functions to $L^ n$. Its positive dual representative is $k_ T\in L^{n/(n-1)}$ with the stated bound. Equality on smooth tests identifies the two Radon measures, proving absolute continuity. The integrals of arbitrary $L^ n$ roots are therefore well-defined.

For the reverse inequality with $r>0$ smooth, let $T_ r=A^{n-1}/V$. It is smooth and positive. Write $E=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$. For $j\leq n-1$, expansion and closedness of $E$ give

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(A^ j)=\sum _{l=0}^ j\binom jl\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\beta ^{j-l})\wedge E^ l=0. \]

The term with $j=l$ differentiates the constant one. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ r=0$, and smooth integration by parts gives

\[ \int _ X\beta \wedge A^{n-1} =\int _ X A^ n-\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge A^{n-1}=V. \]

Consequently $T_ r\in \mathscr G_\beta $. Its positive root is $\gamma =V^{-1/(n-1)}A$, so

\[ k_{T_ r}=(\gamma ^ n/\mathrm dV)^{(n-1)/n} =V^{-1/n}\frac{r^{n-1}}{\| r\| _ n^{n-1}}. \]

It attains equality in (26.107). For a nonnegative $p\in L^ n$, take smooth positive $r_ j\to p$ in $L^ n$. The common dual bound controls $\int (p-r_ j)k_{T_{r_ j}}\mathrm dV$, proving the lower bound in (26.105); the upper bound already holds for every current. Apply the same argument to $|p|$ for complex roots. $\square$
