Theorem 13.23.37D5F8FB5CFAB

Theorem 13.23.3.7D5F8FB5CFAB Suppose $u_ j,u\in \mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$ and $d_\theta (u_ j,u)\to 0$. After passing to a subsequence and relabeling it, there are ordinary model potentials $v_ j,w_ j\in \mathcal M_{\delta /2}(\theta )$ such that

\[ w_ j\leq u_ j\leq v_ j,\qquad w_ j\leq u\leq v_ j, \]

$v_ j$ decreases to $u$, $w_ j$ increases with $(\lim _ jw_ j)^*=u$, and

\[ d_\theta (v_ j,u)+d_\theta (w_ j,u)\longrightarrow 0. \]

The original sequence converges to $u$ in capacity.

Proof. Step 1: upper bounds. For large $j$, the rooftop envelope $r_ j=P_\theta (u_ j,u)$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic by proposition 13.23.2. It is an ordinary model: since $r_ j\leq 0$ and the envelope is monotone, $r_ j\leq P_\theta [r_ j]\leq P_\theta [u_ j]=u_ j$ and likewise $P_\theta [r_ j]\leq u$, so $P_\theta [r_ j]$ is a competitor for the rooftop and equals $r_ j$. The sequence $u_ j$ is $d_\theta $-Cauchy by proposition 13.22.5, and $d_\theta (r_ j,u)=U(u)-U(r_ j)\to 0$ by proposition 13.23.2. Choose a subsequence so that

\[ d_\theta (u_ j,u_{j+1})<\delta \, 2^{-(j+1)n},\qquad U(u)-U(r_ j)<\delta \, 4^{-(j+1)n}. \]

The tail-maximum construction in the proof of theorem 13.22.7 gives

\[ v_ j=P_\theta \left[\left(\sup _{l\geq j}u_ l\right)^*\right] \searrow u,\qquad d_\theta (v_ j,u)\longrightarrow 0. \]

Indeed, that construction produces the unique $d_\theta $-limit, which here is $u$. The models $v_ j$ satisfy $v_ j\geq u_ j$ and $v_ j\geq u$, and have lower volume at least $\delta $.

Step 2: lower bounds. By lemma 13.18.1 and the choice of the subsequence, the stretching envelopes

\[ H_ l=Q_{4^ l}(r_ l,u),\qquad K_ l=Q_{2^ l}(r_ l,u) \]

are defined. Their defining inequalities show that $(1-2^{-l})u+2^{-l}H_ l$ is a competitor for $K_ l$ and that $K_ l\leq u$. Together with addition and monotonicity, this gives

\[ (1-2^{-l})u+2^{-l}H_ l\leq K_ l\leq u, \qquad U(u)-U(K_ l)\leq nM2^{-l}. \]

Choose $j$ so large that $nM2^{1-j}<\delta $. For every $k\geq 0$, the sum of the deficits of $K_ j,\ldots ,K_{j+k}$ from $u$ is less than $\delta $, so lemma 13.23.1 shows that their rooftop envelope is not identically $-\infty $. Since $r_ l\leq u$, the stretching envelope $Q_ b(r_ l,u)$ decreases as $b$ increases; hence, for $l\geq j$,

\[ Q_{2^ j}(r_ l,u)\geq K_ l. \]

Thus $R_ j^ k=P_\theta (Q_{2^ j}(r_ j,u),\ldots ,Q_{2^ j}(r_{j+k},u))$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. The convex inequality for each obstacle gives

\[ (1-2^{-j})u+2^{-j}R_ j^ k \leq w_ j^ k:=P_\theta (r_ j,\ldots ,r_{j+k})\leq u. \]

For either volume,

\begin{equation} (1-2^{-j})^ n\operatorname {vol}_\theta (u) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\theta (w_ j^ k) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\theta (u). \end{equation}

The argument used for $r_ j$ shows that $w_ j^ k$ is an ordinary model. For fixed $j$ it decreases in $k$ and has supremum zero, so normalized compactness shows that its limit $w_ j$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. The lower-volume conclusion of theorem 13.21.2 passes the lower bound in (13.23.4) to $w_ j$. This bound is positive, so the upper-volume conclusion of the same theorem passes the bound for the upper volume as well.

Step 3: convergence. The $w_ j$ increase, since each is defined by fewer obstacles than the previous one, and $w_ j\leq r_ j\leq u_ j$, $w_ j\leq u$. After discarding finitely many indices, they have lower volume at least $\delta /2$, and (13.23.4) gives $d_\theta (w_ j,u)\leq nM2^{-j}$. By proposition 13.22.4, $w_ j\to u$ in $L^1$, so $(\lim _ jw_ j)^*=u$. The two monotone sequences $v_ j$ and $w_ j$ converge to $u$ in capacity by lemma 5.29.19, and the bounds $w_ j\leq u_ j\leq v_ j$ give $u_ j\to u$ in capacity along the chosen subsequence. Since every subsequence of the original sequence has a further subsequence of this kind, the original sequence converges to $u$ in capacity. $\square$
