Positivity cones and divisorial decomposition

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 14.3 Positivity cones and divisorial decomposition

Let $\alpha \in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ and fix a smooth representative $\theta \in \alpha $. By theorem 14.1.7, the closed currents in $\alpha $ are exactly the currents $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ with $\varphi $ a real distribution, so positivity properties of $\alpha $ become inequalities for $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $. Besides the positivity cones and the mixed Hodge index theorem, we prove Siu's decomposition of a closed positive current (theorem 14.3.7), which leads to modified nef classes.

Definition 14.3.1. A class $\alpha \in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is

  • pseudo-effective if it contains a closed positive current;

  • Kähler if it contains a Kähler form;

  • nef if for every $\varepsilon >0$ it contains a smooth form $\theta _\varepsilon $ with $\theta _\varepsilon \geq -\varepsilon \omega $;

  • big if it contains a closed current $T$ with $T\geq \delta \omega $ for some $\delta >0$.

A closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ with $T\geq \delta \omega $ for some $\delta >0$ is called a Kähler current. The cones of pseudo-effective, Kähler, nef and big classes are denoted $\mathcal E$, $\mathcal K$, $\mathcal N$ and $\mathcal B$. The zero class belongs to the pseudo-effective cone and to the nef cone.

These notions do not depend on $\omega $, because any two Hermitian forms on the compact manifold $X$ are bounded by constant multiples of each other (lemma 13.1.1). By corollary 14.1.9, $\alpha $ is pseudo-effective if and only if $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\ne \varnothing $.

Proposition 14.3.2. The pseudo-effective cone $\mathcal E$ is closed and convex, and the Kähler cone $\mathcal K$ is open and convex. Moreover $\mathcal N=\overline{\mathcal K}\subset \mathcal E$, the Kähler cone $\mathcal K$ is the interior of $\mathcal N$, and the big cone $\mathcal B$ is the interior of $\mathcal E$.

Proof. Sums and positive multiples of positive currents, Kähler forms or nef representatives are of the same kind, which proves convexity of all four cones.

Let $\alpha _ j\to \alpha $ be pseudo-effective classes, with positive currents $T_ j\in \alpha _ j$. Since the $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a distribution pairs to zero with the closed form $\omega ^{n-1}$, the trace masses are

\[ \int _ XT_ j\wedge \omega ^{n-1} =\int _ X\theta _{\alpha _ j}\wedge \omega ^{n-1}, \]

where $\theta _{\alpha _ j}$ are the representatives of (14.16); they are bounded because the classes converge. Let $K$ be a compact subset of a chart, $\chi $ a test function on the chart with $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ and $\chi =1$ on $K$, and $c'>0$ such that $\beta \leq c'\omega $ on $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $ (lemma 13.1.1), where $\beta $ is the Euclidean form (7.4) of the chart. By (14.12), applied with $\gamma _ j=c'\omega $ and $\gamma '_ j=\beta $, the form $(c'\omega )^{n-1}-\beta ^{n-1}$ is a sum of products of semipositive $(1,1)$-forms on $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $, hence strongly positive (proposition 7.2.1). Therefore $c'^{n-1}\omega ^{n-1}-\chi \beta ^{n-1}=(1-\chi )c'^{n-1}\omega ^{n-1}+\chi \bigl (c'^{n-1}\omega ^{n-1}-\beta ^{n-1}\bigr )$, with $\chi \beta ^{n-1}$ extended by zero outside the chart, is a smooth strongly positive form on $X$, so the trace measures satisfy $\mu _{T_ j}(K)\leq T_ j(\chi \beta ^{n-1})\leq c'^{n-1}\int _ XT_ j\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$, which is bounded independently of $j$. By the compactness assertion of theorem 7.2.5, a subsequence of $(T_ j)$ converges weakly to a closed positive current $T$, and $\{ T\} =\alpha $ because the class map is weakly continuous. Hence $\mathcal E$ is closed.

If $\omega _0\in \alpha $ is a Kähler form, then $\omega _0\geq c\omega $ for some $c>0$, and by (14.16) the form $\omega _0+\theta _{\alpha '-\alpha }\in \alpha '$ is Kähler whenever $C\| \alpha '-\alpha \| <c$; hence $\mathcal K$ is open. If $\alpha $ is nef and $\varepsilon >0$, a representative $\theta _\varepsilon \geq -\varepsilon \omega $ gives the Kähler form $\theta _\varepsilon +2\varepsilon \omega \in \alpha +2\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $, so $\alpha \in \overline{\mathcal K}$. Conversely, let Kähler classes $\alpha _ j$ with Kähler forms $\omega _ j\in \alpha _ j$ converge to $\alpha $. Then $\omega _ j+\theta _{\alpha -\alpha _ j}\in \alpha $ is at least $-C\| \alpha -\alpha _ j\| \omega $, so $\alpha $ is nef; thus $\mathcal N=\overline{\mathcal K}$, and $\mathcal N\subset \mathcal E$ because $\mathcal K\subset \mathcal E$ and $\mathcal E$ is closed.

Since $\mathcal K$ is open and contained in $\mathcal N$, it lies in the interior of $\mathcal N$. If $\alpha $ is nef and $t>0$, then $\alpha +t\{ \omega \} $ is Kähler: take a representative $\theta _\varepsilon \geq -\varepsilon \omega $ with $\varepsilon <t$. If $\alpha $ lies in the interior of $\mathcal N$, then $\alpha -t\{ \omega \} $ is nef for small $t>0$, and therefore $\alpha =(\alpha -t\{ \omega \} )+t\{ \omega \} $ is Kähler. Thus $\mathcal K$ is the interior of $\mathcal N$.

If $T\in \alpha $ and $T\geq \delta \omega $, then $T+\theta _{\alpha '-\alpha }\in \alpha '$ is positive whenever $C\| \alpha '-\alpha \| \leq \delta $, so a neighborhood of $\alpha $ lies in $\mathcal E$. Conversely, if $\alpha $ lies in the interior of $\mathcal E$, then $\alpha -\delta \{ \omega \} \in \mathcal E$ for some $\delta >0$, and adding $\delta \omega $ to a positive current in $\alpha -\delta \{ \omega \} $ gives a Kähler current in $\alpha $. Hence $\mathcal B$ is the interior of $\mathcal E$. $\square$

If $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ are smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms, Stokes' formula shows that $\int _ X\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _ n$ is unchanged when one $\theta _ i$ is replaced by $\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ with $f$ smooth. We therefore write $\int _ X\alpha _1\cdots \alpha _ n=\int _ X\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _ n$ for the intersection number of classes $\alpha _ i=\{ \theta _ i\} $, and use the usual notation for powers, such as $\int _ X\alpha ^ n$. In the mixed Hodge index theorem below, the mixed scalar equation of lemma 14.1.4 turns a cohomological orthogonality condition into a pointwise one, to which the signature lemma for Hermitian matrices applies.

Theorem 14.3.3 (The mixed Hodge index theorem). Let $n\geq 2$, let $\omega _3,\ldots ,\omega _ n$ be Kähler forms on $X$, and put $\Omega =\omega _3\wedge \cdots \wedge \omega _ n$, with $\Omega =1$ when $n=2$. The symmetric bilinear form

\[ Q(\alpha ,\beta )=\int _ Xa\wedge b\wedge \Omega , \qquad a\in \alpha ,\quad b\in \beta , \]

on $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ has signature $(1,\dim H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)-1)$. For every Kähler class $\gamma $, it is negative definite on the hyperplane $\{ \alpha :Q(\alpha ,\gamma )=0\} $. For Kähler classes $\alpha ,\beta $,

14.18
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mixed-intersection-cauchy} Q(\alpha ,\beta )^2\geq Q(\alpha ,\alpha )Q(\beta ,\beta ). \end{equation}

Proof. Let $\gamma $ be a Kähler class with a Kähler form $h\in \gamma $, and let $\alpha $ satisfy $Q(\alpha ,\gamma )=0$. Choose a smooth closed real form $a\in \alpha $; then $\int _ Xa\wedge h\wedge \Omega =0$. Apply lemma 14.1.4 with $A=h\wedge \Omega $, a product of $n-1$ Kähler forms, and $\eta =-a\wedge h\wedge \Omega $. The resulting $f$ makes $a'=a+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\in \alpha $ satisfy $a'\wedge h\wedge \Omega =0$ at every point. At a point, in holomorphic coordinates, the wedge products of real $(1,1)$-forms are expressed by mixed discriminants of their Hermitian matrices, so lemma 7.2.3 gives $a'\wedge a'\wedge \Omega \leq 0$, with equality only if $a'$ vanishes at that point. By Stokes' formula,

\[ Q(\alpha ,\alpha )=\int _ Xa'\wedge a'\wedge \Omega \leq 0. \]

If this integral vanishes, the smooth nonpositive density $a'\wedge a'\wedge \Omega $ vanishes everywhere, so $a'=0$ and $\alpha =0$. Thus $Q$ is negative definite on the hyperplane $\{ Q(\cdot ,\gamma )=0\} $.

Since $Q(\gamma ,\gamma )=\int _ Xh^2\wedge \Omega >0$, every class splits uniquely as a multiple of $\gamma $ plus a class in that hyperplane. The space $H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is finite dimensional by theorem 14.1.7, and $Q$ is positive on the line spanned by $\gamma $ and negative definite on its $Q$-orthogonal complement; this gives the signature. Finally, let $\alpha ,\beta $ be Kähler classes and write $\beta =c\alpha +\delta $ with $Q(\delta ,\alpha )=0$. Negative definiteness gives $Q(\beta ,\beta )=c^2Q(\alpha ,\alpha )+Q(\delta ,\delta )\leq c^2Q(\alpha ,\alpha )$, while $Q(\alpha ,\beta )=cQ(\alpha ,\alpha )$. Since $Q(\alpha ,\alpha )>0$, we obtain $Q(\alpha ,\beta )^2=c^2Q(\alpha ,\alpha )^2\geq Q(\alpha ,\alpha )Q(\beta ,\beta )$. $\square$

Corollary 14.3.4. Let $r\geq 1$, let $\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ r$ be nef classes, and let $k_1,\ldots ,k_ r\geq 0$ be integers with $\sum _ i k_ i=n$. Then

\[ \int _ X\alpha _1^{k_1}\cdots \alpha _ r^{k_ r} \geq \prod _{i:k_ i>0}\left(\int _ X\alpha _ i^ n\right)^{k_ i/n}. \]

In particular, for nef classes $\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ n$,

\[ \int _ X\alpha _1\cdots \alpha _ n \geq \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\int _ X\alpha _ i^ n\right)^{1/n}. \]

Proof. Assume first that all $\alpha _ i$ are Kähler. Then all the intersection numbers $\int _ X\prod _ i\alpha _ i^{k_ i}$ are positive, and we put $b(k)=\log \int _ X\prod _ i\alpha _ i^{k_ i}$ for $k=(k_1,\ldots ,k_ r)$ with $\sum _ ik_ i=n$. If $n=1$, only one $k_ i$ is positive and there is nothing to prove; let $n\geq 2$. Let $i\neq j$ with $k_ i,k_ j\geq 1$, let $\Omega $ be the wedge product of Kähler representatives of the remaining $n-2$ factors, counted with multiplicity, and let $e_ i$ be the $i$th coordinate vector. Then (14.18) for $\alpha _ i,\alpha _ j$ reads

\[ 2b(k)\geq b(k+e_ i-e_ j)+b(k-e_ i+e_ j). \]

Fix the coordinates other than $i,j$ and put $s=k_ i+k_ j$. The inequality says that the finite sequence $l\mapsto b(\ldots ,l,\ldots ,s-l,\ldots )$, with $l$ in the $i$th and $s-l$ in the $j$th place, $0\leq l\leq s$, is concave: its consecutive differences decrease. A concave sequence lies above the linear interpolation between its endpoints, since the average of its first $l$ differences is at least the average of all $s$ differences. Consequently

\[ b(k)\geq \frac{k_ i}{s}b(k^{(i)})+\frac{k_ j}{s}b(k^{(j)}), \]

where $k^{(i)}$ is obtained from $k$ by putting all $s$ factors in the $i$th place and $k^{(j)}$ by putting them in the $j$th place.

We now prove $b(k)\geq \sum _ i(k_ i/n)\log \int _ X\alpha _ i^ n$ by induction on the number of positive coordinates of $k$. If only one coordinate is positive, this is an equality. Otherwise choose $i\ne j$ with $k_ i,k_ j\geq 1$. The vectors $k^{(i)}$ and $k^{(j)}$ have one positive coordinate fewer, and inserting the induction hypothesis for them into the last display gives the claim: for $m\ne i,j$ the coefficient of $\log \int _ X\alpha _ m^ n$ is $(k_ i/s+k_ j/s)\, k_ m/n=k_ m/n$, and the coefficients of $\log \int _ X\alpha _ i^ n$ and $\log \int _ X\alpha _ j^ n$ are $(k_ i/s)(s/n)=k_ i/n$ and $k_ j/n$. Exponentiating proves the corollary for Kähler classes.

For nef classes, $\alpha _ i+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ is Kähler for every $\varepsilon >0$ by proposition 14.3.2, so the inequality holds for these classes. Each intersection number involved is a polynomial in $\varepsilon $ and tends to the corresponding number for the $\alpha _ i$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$; in particular the limits $\int _ X\alpha _ i^ n$ are nonnegative. Since $t\mapsto t^{k_ i/n}$ is continuous on $[0,\infty )$, the inequality passes to the limit. $\square$

The next proposition uses the cutoff-sum approximations $G_ m$ of lemma 13.4.1: for $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and integers $m\geq 1$, $G_ m$ has gentle analytic singularities (definition 13.1.11) of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi ))$ and satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m\geq -(c/m)\omega $, where $c>0$ (the constant $c'$ of that lemma) depends only on $(X,\omega )$ and not on $\theta $, $\varphi $ or $m$. In particular $G_ m$ is smooth near every point where $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ is the unit ideal.

Proposition 14.3.5. A class that contains a closed positive current with locally bounded potentials is nef. A class that contains a Kähler current with locally bounded potentials is Kähler. Every big class contains a Kähler current with gentle analytic singularities.

Proof. Write the given current as $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ with $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. If $\varphi $ is bounded, then every $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ is the unit ideal, so the approximations $G_ m$ are smooth and satisfy $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m\geq -(c/m)\omega $; hence the class is nef. If moreover $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq \delta \omega $, then $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta -\delta \omega )$, and the approximations for the background $\theta -\delta \omega $ satisfy $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m\geq (\delta -c/m)\omega $; for large $m$ the smooth form $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m$ is a Kähler form in the class. For the last assertion, let $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq \delta \omega $ be a Kähler current in a big class, with $\varphi $ not necessarily bounded. The same inequality shows that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m$ is a Kähler current for large $m$, and $G_ m$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi ))$ by lemma 13.4.1. $\square$

Corollary 14.3.6. If a closed positive current with minimal singularities is a Kähler current, then its class is Kähler. Moreover, in a Kähler class every potential with minimal singularities is bounded.

Proof. Write the current as $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq \delta \omega $, where $\varphi $ has minimal singularities, and normalize $\varphi \leq 0$. Choose $C'\geq 0$ with $-\theta \leq C'\omega $ (lemma 13.1.1). For $0<t\leq \delta /(\delta +C')$ the current

\[ (1-t)T+t\theta =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl ((1-t)\varphi \bigr )\geq \bigl ((1-t)\delta -tC'\bigr )\omega \geq 0 \]

is positive, so $(1-t)\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. By minimality, $(1-t)\varphi \leq \varphi +C$ for a constant $C$, so $\varphi \geq -C/t$ at every point where $\varphi $ is finite, hence almost everywhere. Since $\varphi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, the lower bound holds everywhere, as noted at the beginning of section 14.2. Thus $\varphi $ is bounded, and proposition 14.3.5 shows that the class is Kähler. For the second assertion, suppose that the class contains a Kähler form $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. Then $u$ is smooth and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, so a potential $\varphi $ with minimal singularities satisfies $u\leq \varphi +C$; hence $\varphi $ is bounded below, and it is bounded above as every quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$. $\square$

Recall from definition 12.1.2 that the Lelong number $\nu (T,x)$ of a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ at $x\in X$ is the Lelong number at $x$ of any local plurisubharmonic potential of $T$. If $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ with $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, then $\varphi +h$ is such a potential wherever $h$ is smooth with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $, so $\nu (T,x)=\nu (\varphi ,x)$ in the sense recalled before proposition 14.2.8; in particular, these numbers do not depend on the background $\theta $. For a prime divisor $D\subset X$, the generic Lelong number of $T$ along $D$ (proposition 12.3.5) is therefore

\[ \nu (T,D)=\inf _{x\in D}\nu (T,x)=\inf _{x\in D}\nu (\varphi ,x)=\nu (\varphi ,D), \]

and by that proposition the infimum is attained at every point of $D$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $D$. We write $[D]$ for the current of integration along $D$ (lemma A.2.16); its coefficient measures are given by integration over $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and give no mass to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$.

Theorem 14.3.7 (Siu's divisorial decomposition). Let $T$ be a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$. Then $\nu (T,D)>0$ for at most countably many prime divisors $D$, the series below converges weakly, and

14.19
\begin{equation} \label{eq:siu-divisorial-decomposition} T=R+\sum _ D\nu (T,D)[D], \end{equation}

where $R$ is a closed positive current with $\nu (R,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$. In particular $T-\nu (T,D)[D]\geq 0$ for every $D$. The decomposition is unique: if $T=R'+\sum _ Db_ D[D]$ for a weakly convergent series with coefficients $b_ D\geq 0$, at most countably many of them nonzero, and a closed positive current $R'$ with $\nu (R',D)=0$ for every $D$, then $b_ D=\nu (T,D)$ for every $D$ and $R'=R$.

Proof. We first subtract one divisor, then show that only countably many divisors occur and that the series converges, and finally identify the residual current and prove uniqueness.

Step 1: one divisor. Let $D$ be a prime divisor and $c=\nu (T,D)$; we show that $T-c[D]\geq 0$. Near a regular point of $D$ choose coordinates with $D=\{ z_1=0\} $ on a coordinate polydisc $W$ with $|z_1|<1$, and a plurisubharmonic potential $u$ of $T$ on $W$. Since the Lelong numbers of $u$ at the points of $D\cap W$ are at least $c$, proposition 12.3.6 gives $u\leq c\log |z_1|^2+C$ near the point. Off $D$ the function $u-c\log |z_1|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and it is bounded above near $D$; by theorem 8.4.1 it extends to a plurisubharmonic function $v$. Since $v=u-c\log |z_1|^2$ almost everywhere and $[D]=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z_1|^2$ near the point (theorem 7.4.5), we get $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v=T-c[D]$ there. Hence $T-c[D]$ is positive on a neighborhood of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and it equals $T$ on $X\setminus D$; so it is positive on the open set $X\setminus D_{\mathrm{sing}}$.

To extend the positivity across $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$, let $\phi $ be a strongly positive test form of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$. Since $T$ and $[D]$ have measure coefficients (theorem 7.2.5), there is a real measure $m_\phi $ on $X$, a combination of the coefficient measures with the coefficients of $\phi $ as densities, such that $(T-c[D])(\chi \phi )=\int \chi \, \mathrm dm_\phi $ for test functions $\chi $. The restriction of $m_\phi $ to the open set $X\setminus D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is a positive measure, because $(T-c[D])(\chi \phi )\geq 0$ for test functions $\chi \geq 0$ supported there; and its restriction to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is that of the positive measure $\chi \mapsto T(\chi \phi )$, because $[D]$ gives no mass to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$. Hence $(T-c[D])(\phi )=m_\phi (X)\geq 0$, that is, $T-c[D]\geq 0$.

Step 2: the series. By theorem 12.3.4, each set $E_ j=\{ x:\nu (T,x)\geq 1/j\} $ is an analytic subset of $X$ different from $X$. Being compact, $E_ j$ has finitely many irreducible components $Z$ by corollary A.2.13. We first show:

14.20
\begin{equation} \label{eq:prime-divisor-component} \begin{gathered} \text{a prime divisor $D$ contained in an analytic subset $E\neq X$}\\ \text{is an irreducible component of $E$.} \end{gathered} \end{equation}

An analytic subset of the connected manifold $X$ with an interior point is all of $X$, since the set of its interior points is open and, by the identity theorem applied to local defining functions, closed. Hence $E$ has no interior points, so its germs, and their branches, have dimension at most $n-1$, and $E_{\mathrm{sing}}$ has dimension at most $n-2$ at every point (proposition A.2.7, corollary A.2.10). At a point $y\in D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ the germ of $D$ has dimension $n-1$, so by monotonicity of dimension (see proposition A.2.7 and the remarks following it) it is not contained in the germ of $E_{\mathrm{sing}}$; thus there are points $y'\in D_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap E_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Near such a point $E$ is a complex submanifold of dimension at most $n-1$ containing the $(n-1)$-dimensional submanifold $D$, so $D$ and $E$ coincide near $y'$.

Let $F$ be the connected component of $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$ containing $y'$. The analytic subset $D\cap F$ of the connected manifold $F$ has an interior point, so it is all of $F$, and $D$ contains the closure $Z$ of $F$, an irreducible component of $E$ (corollary A.2.13). Since $D$ is compact and the components of $E$ form a locally finite family, $D$ meets only finitely many of them and is the union of its intersections with these. By induction on the number of sets, an irreducible set is not a finite union of analytic subsets different from itself; hence $D=D\cap Z'$ for a component $Z'$. Then $Z\subset D\subset Z'$, and since no component is contained in another, $D=Z$. This proves (14.20). In particular, two distinct prime divisors are not contained in one another, and every prime divisor $D$ with $\nu (T,D)\geq 1/j$, being contained in $E_ j$, is one of its finitely many components. Therefore at most countably many prime divisors have $\nu (T,D)>0$. Let $D_1,D_2,\ldots $ be an enumeration of them and put $S_ N=\sum _{k\leq N}\nu (T,D_ k)[D_ k]$.

We claim that $T-S_ N\geq 0$ for every $N$, and argue by induction; suppose that $T-S_{N-1}\geq 0$. Choose a point $x\in D_ N$ outside the proper analytic subsets $D_ N\cap D_ k$, $k<N$, and outside the exceptional sets of proposition 12.3.5 for $T$ and $T-S_{N-1}$ along $D_ N$. Such points exist, because by that proposition the complement in $D_ N$ of a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $D_ N$ is dense in $D_ N$. The currents $[D_ k]$, $k<N$, vanish near $x$, so $T-S_{N-1}=T$ near $x$, and therefore $\nu (T-S_{N-1},D_ N)=\nu (T-S_{N-1},x)=\nu (T,x)=\nu (T,D_ N)$. Step 1, applied to $T-S_{N-1}$, gives $T-S_ N\geq 0$. Consequently the trace measures of the $S_ N$ in every chart are bounded by that of $T$, and the masses $\int _ XS_ N\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$ are bounded by $\int _ XT\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$.

We now show that the sequence $(S_ N)$ converges weakly. Every test form is a finite linear combination of test forms $g\gamma $, where $g\geq 0$ is a test function supported in a chart and $\gamma $ is a strongly positive form with constant coefficients in that chart. To see this, use a partition of unity (proposition 7.1.3), the fact that strongly positive forms span the real forms (proposition 7.2.1), and write a real test function $g$ as $(g+M\psi )-M\psi $ with $\psi \geq 0$ a test function equal to one on $\operatorname{Supp}g$ and $M\geq \sup |g|$. For such $g\gamma $ the numbers $S_ N(g\gamma )$ increase with $N$, since $S_{N+1}-S_ N\geq 0$, and they are bounded by $T(g\gamma )$; hence they converge, and so does $S_ N(\phi )$ for every test form $\phi $. By the compactness assertion of theorem 7.2.5, a subsequence converges weakly to a closed positive current $S$, so $S_ N(\phi )\to S(\phi )$ for every $\phi $. Finally $R=T-S$ is closed and positive, as a weak limit of the closed positive currents $T-S_ N$.

Step 3: properties of $R$ and uniqueness. Fix a prime divisor $D$ and write $T=R+\nu (T,D)[D]+S_ D$, where $S_ D$ is the sum of the terms of the series for the other divisors. Lelong numbers are additive and nonnegative (proposition 12.1.4), and $\nu ([D],x)=\nu (\log |f|^2,x)=1$ at regular points $x$ of $D$, where $f$ is a local coordinate defining $D$. Hence, at a point of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ outside the countable union of proper analytic subsets where one of the currents $T$, $R$, $S_ D$ has a nongeneric Lelong number (such points exist by proposition 12.3.5, since $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is also a proper analytic subset of $D$),

\[ \nu (T,D)=\nu (R,D)+\nu (T,D)+\nu (S_ D,D)\geq \nu (R,D)+\nu (T,D). \]

Hence $\nu (R,D)=0$.

Now consider any decomposition $T=R'+\sum _ Db_ D[D]$ as in the statement, fix $D$, and let $S'_ D$ be the sum of the terms for the other divisors. We first show that the coefficient measures of $S'_ D$ give no mass to $D$. If $D'\neq D$ is another prime divisor, then $D\cap D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is an analytic subset of the connected manifold $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (corollary A.2.13) which is not all of it, since otherwise $D'\subset D$, and then $D'=D$ by (14.20). Hence $D\cap D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a null set for the surface measure of $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (lemma 7.4.1, in local coordinates of $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$), and $[D']$ gives no mass to $D$. Now let $K\subset D$ be a compact subset of a chart, and for large $m$ let $\chi _ m$ be test functions with $0\leq \chi _ m\leq 1$, equal to one on $K$ and supported in the $1/m$-neighborhood of $K$ (lemma 1.4.1), all supported in a fixed compact subset of the chart, on which a test function $\psi $ equals one. For the trace measures in the chart, the weak convergence of the series gives $\mu _{S'_ D}(\chi _ m)=\sum _{D'}b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(\chi _ m)$. As $m\to \infty $, $\chi _ m\to \mathbf1_ K$ pointwise, and dominated convergence, applied to the measure $\mu _{S'_ D}$ and to the series, whose terms are bounded by $b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(\psi )$ with $\sum _{D'}b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(\psi )=\mu _{S'_ D}(\psi )<\infty $, gives $\mu _{S'_ D}(K)=\sum _{D'}b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(K)=0$. Since $D$ is covered by countably many such compact sets, the trace measures of $S'_ D$, and hence its coefficient measures (theorem 7.2.5), vanish on $D$.

By Step 1, $S'_ D-\nu (S'_ D,D)[D]\geq 0$; comparing the trace measures of both sides on $D$, where $[D]$ has positive trace mass, forces $\nu (S'_ D,D)=0$. Additivity of Lelong numbers at generic points of $D$, applied to $T=R'+b_ D[D]+S'_ D$, gives $\nu (T,D)=0+b_ D+0$. Thus $b_ D=\nu (T,D)$ for every $D$, and $R'=R$. $\square$

Definition 14.3.8. A class $\alpha $ is modified nef if for every $\varepsilon >0$ there is a closed current $T_\varepsilon \in \alpha $ with $T_\varepsilon \geq -\varepsilon \omega $ and $\nu (T_\varepsilon +\varepsilon \omega ,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$. We write $\mathcal{MN}$ for the cone of modified nef classes. A closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ with $\nu (T,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$ is called non-divisorial.

The set $\mathcal{MN}$ is a convex cone. Indeed, multiplying $T_\varepsilon $ by $t>0$ shows that $t\alpha $ is modified nef, with $tT_{\varepsilon /t}$ for the allowance $\varepsilon $. If $\alpha ,\alpha '$ are modified nef, with currents $T_\varepsilon ,T'_\varepsilon $, then $T_{\varepsilon /2}+T'_{\varepsilon /2}\geq -\varepsilon \omega $ lies in $\alpha +\alpha '$. At a point of a prime divisor $D$ at which the Lelong numbers of the positive currents $T_{\varepsilon /2}+\varepsilon \omega /2$ and $T'_{\varepsilon /2}+\varepsilon \omega /2$ attain their generic values $0$ (proposition 12.3.5), the additivity of Lelong numbers (proposition 12.1.4) shows that their sum has Lelong number $0$; hence $\nu (T_{\varepsilon /2}+T'_{\varepsilon /2}+\varepsilon \omega ,D)=0$.

The cone $\mathcal{MN}$ contains $\mathcal N$, since smooth forms have zero Lelong numbers, and it is contained in $\mathcal E$: if $\alpha $ is modified nef, then $\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ contains the positive current $T_\varepsilon +\varepsilon \omega $, and $\mathcal E$ is closed. It is also closed. Indeed, let $\alpha _ j\to \alpha $ with $\alpha _ j\in \mathcal{MN}$, and let $\varepsilon >0$. By (14.16) we may choose $j$ such that $\alpha -\alpha _ j$ has a smooth representative $\eta $ with $-\varepsilon \omega /2\leq \eta \leq \varepsilon \omega /2$. If $T'\in \alpha _ j$ satisfies $T'\geq -\varepsilon \omega /2$ and $\nu (T'+\varepsilon \omega /2,D)=0$ for all $D$, then $T'+\eta \in \alpha $ satisfies $T'+\eta \geq -\varepsilon \omega $, and $\nu (T'+\eta +\varepsilon \omega ,D)=0$ for all $D$, since adding smooth forms does not change Lelong numbers.

As for the classes of definition 14.3.1, the definition of modified nef classes does not depend on $\omega $. Indeed, let $\omega '$ be another Kähler form and $C\geq 1$ with $\omega \leq C\omega '$ (lemma 13.1.1). For every $\varepsilon >0$ the current $T=T_{\varepsilon /C}$ satisfies $T\geq -(\varepsilon /C)\omega \geq -\varepsilon \omega '$, and the positive currents $T+\varepsilon \omega '$ and $T+(\varepsilon /C)\omega $ differ by a smooth form, so that $\nu (T+\varepsilon \omega ',D)=\nu (T+(\varepsilon /C)\omega ,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$.

In the definition of modified nef classes we may take $T_\varepsilon $ with gentle analytic singularities whose pole set has codimension at least two. Indeed, write $T_{\varepsilon /2}=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ with $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\varepsilon \omega /2)$ and apply lemma 13.4.1 to $\varphi $ with the background $\theta +\varepsilon \omega /2$, choosing $m$ with $c/m<\varepsilon /2$. Then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m\geq -\varepsilon \omega $. Since $\nu (\varphi ,D)=\nu (T_{\varepsilon /2}+\varepsilon \omega /2,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$, at a point $x$ of $D$ where $\nu (\varphi ,x)=\nu (\varphi ,D)$ the ideal $\mathcal I(m\varphi )_ x$ is the unit ideal by corollary 12.3.2. Hence the pole set of $G_ m$, which is the zero set of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$, contains no prime divisor. It is not all of $X$, since $\mathcal I(m\varphi )_ x$ is the unit ideal at every point $x$ with $\varphi (x)>-\infty $, where $\nu (\varphi ,x)=0$ (proposition 12.1.1); so its irreducible components have codimension at least one, those of codimension one would be prime divisors, and it has codimension at least two. Moreover $G_ m$ is smooth near such a point $x$, so the Lelong numbers of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m+\varepsilon \omega $ vanish there, and $\nu (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m+\varepsilon \omega ,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$. Thus $T_\varepsilon =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m$ has the required properties.

Corollary 14.3.9. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, let $D_1,\ldots ,D_ N$ be distinct prime divisors and $a_1,\ldots ,a_ N>0$ real numbers, and put $D=\sum _ ia_ iD_ i$. Suppose that $\varphi $ has logarithmic singularities along $D$ with bounded remainder: near each point, $\varphi =\sum _ ia_ i\log |s_ i|^2+v$ with $v$ bounded, where $s_ i$ is a local equation of $D_ i$ (lemma A.2.16), and $s_ i=1$ if the point does not lie on $D_ i$. Then $\{ \theta \} -\{ D\} $ is nef, where $\{ D\} =\sum _ ia_ i\{ [D_ i]\} $. If $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ is a Kähler current, then $\{ \theta \} -\{ D\} $ is Kähler.

Proof. Near a point, let $h$ be smooth with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $. Off the support of $D$ the function $v+h=(\varphi +h)-\sum _ ia_ i\log |s_ i|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, since $\log |s_ i|^2$ is pluriharmonic there, and it is bounded above. On a connected coordinate neighborhood of the point, the support of $D$ lies in the zero set of the holomorphic function $\prod _ is_ i\not\equiv 0$, so theorem 8.4.1 extends $v+h$ to a plurisubharmonic function $\widetilde v$. The values of $\widetilde v$ on the support of $D$ are upper limits of values of $v+h$, so $\widetilde v$ is bounded, and $\widetilde v=v+h$ outside the null set $\operatorname{Supp}D$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |s_ i|^2=[D_ i]$ (lemma A.2.16), the current $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ satisfies $T-[D]=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde v\geq 0$ near the point, where $[D]=\sum _ ia_ i[D_ i]$. Thus $T-[D]$ is a positive current in $\{ \theta \} -\{ D\} $ with locally bounded potentials. If $T\geq \delta \omega $, apply the same argument to $T-\delta \omega $, whose potential has the same logarithmic singularities; then $T-[D]\geq \delta \omega $. In both cases proposition 14.3.5 gives the assertion. $\square$
