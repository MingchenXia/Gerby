Chapter 11 · Non-pluripolar measures and mass · Tag A75B7FC1B6D9

Mass concentration

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 7.5 Mass concentration

The tail condition excludes mass carried by sublevel sets that disappear in the capacity limit. On a curve this mass can concentrate at a pole of the limit. When the bounded mass property fails, it can also persist along smooth potentials converging to zero.

Example 7.5.1. On $X=\mathbb P^1$, let $z$ be the standard affine coordinate and let $\delta _0$ denote the unit Dirac measure at its origin. Take the Fubini–Study form

\[ \omega =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+|z|^2),\qquad \int _ X\omega =1, \]

and the global nonpositive potential

\[ u(z)=\log \frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2},\qquad u_ j=\max (u,-j). \]

At infinity, in the coordinate $\zeta =1/z$, the function $u$ equals $-\log (1+|\zeta |^2)$, so it is smooth there. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\delta _0, \qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)=0. \]

The second identity holds because the extended measure discards the pole set and the current vanishes on its complement.

Each $u_ j$ is bounded and $\omega $-plurisubharmonic. Its measure $\mu _ j=\mu _{u_ j}=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$ has mass one by Stokes' theorem, and $\mu _ j=0$ on the open set $\{ u>-j\} $ by locality. Hence

\[ \operatorname {supp}\mu _ j\subset \{ u\leq -j\} =\left\{ |z|^2\leq \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-j}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-j}}\right\} . \]

These disks shrink to the origin, so $\mu _ j\rightharpoonup \delta _0$. On their supports, $u_ j=-j$. Consequently, for $j>T$,

\[ \mu _ j(\{ u_ j<-T\} )=1, \qquad \mathrm{e}^{pu_ j}\mu _ j=\mathrm{e}^{-pj}\mu _ j\rightharpoonup 0 \quad (p>0). \]

Nevertheless, $u_ j\to u$ in capacity. Indeed, outside any neighborhood of the origin the functions eventually agree with $u$, and the origin has arbitrarily small neighborhoods of arbitrarily small capacity. Thus bounded mass and capacity convergence do not prevent the undamped measures from acquiring mass at a pole of the limit. The damped limit is the extended measure of $u$, as predicted by theorem 7.3.1.

We return to a general compact Hermitian manifold $(X,\omega )$.

Corollary 7.5.2. Assume $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $ and put $V_\omega =\int _ X\omega ^ n$. Fix a real number $b>V_\omega $ and a sequence $0<\varepsilon _ j<1$ tending to zero. The smooth potentials $w_ j$ of corollary 6.7.3, chosen with mass $b$, converge to zero in capacity. For every $T>0$,

\[ \left\| \mathbf1_{\{ w_ j\geq -T\} }(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^ n-\omega ^ n \right\| _{\mathrm{TV}}\longrightarrow 0, \qquad \int _{\{ w_ j<-T\} }(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^ n\longrightarrow b-V_\omega . \]

After passage to a subsequence, there is a positive measure $\sigma $ of mass $b-V_\omega $ such that

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^ n\rightharpoonup \omega ^ n+\sigma , \qquad \mathbf1_{\{ w_ j<-T\} }(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^ n\rightharpoonup \sigma \quad \text{for every }T>0. \]

Proof. The construction in corollary 6.7.3 gives $w_ j=s_ j u_ j$, where $u_ j$ is smooth and $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$ and $0<s_ j<\varepsilon _ j$. The sublevel estimate (1.26) gives, for every fixed $\delta >0$ and all sufficiently large $j$,

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ |w_ j|>\delta \} ) =\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ u_ j<-\delta /s_ j\} ) \leq Cs_ j/\delta \longrightarrow 0. \]

Put $\mu _ j=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^ n$. Fix $T>0$ and write $E_{j,T}=\{ w_ j\geq -T\} $. The lower bound in corollary 6.7.3 gives

\[ \mathbf1_{E_{j,T}}\mu _ j\geq \lambda _{j,T}:=(1-\varepsilon _ j)^ n\mathbf1_{E_{j,T}}\omega ^ n. \]

Since $w_ j\to 0$ in $L^1$, the smooth volume of $X\setminus E_{j,T}$ tends to zero, and hence $\lambda _{j,T}\to \omega ^ n$ in total variation. For every $p>0$, theorem 7.3.1 gives $\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{pw_ j}\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ j\to V_\omega $, and therefore

\[ \limsup _ j\mu _ j(E_{j,T}) \leq \mathrm{e}^{pT}\lim _ j\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{pw_ j}\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ j =\mathrm{e}^{pT}V_\omega . \]

Letting $p\downarrow 0$ and combining with the lower bound, we obtain $\mu _ j(E_{j,T})\to V_\omega $. Positivity now yields

\[ \begin{split} \| \mathbf1_{E_{j,T}}\mu _ j-\omega ^ n\| _{\mathrm{TV}} & \leq \mu _ j(E_{j,T})-\lambda _{j,T}(X) +\| \lambda _{j,T}-\omega ^ n\| _{\mathrm{TV}}\\ & \longrightarrow 0. \end{split} \]

Subtracting this shallow mass from $\mu _ j(X)=b$ proves the assertion about the mass on $\{ w_ j<-T\} $.

The positive measures $\mu _ j$ have fixed mass on the compact manifold $X$, so a subsequence converges weakly to a measure $\nu $ of mass $b$. The inequality $\mu _ j\geq (1-\varepsilon _ j)^ n\omega ^ n$ gives $\nu \geq \omega ^ n$. Set $\sigma =\nu -\omega ^ n$. For each $T>0$, subtracting the total-variation limit of the shallow restrictions from $\mu _ j\rightharpoonup \nu $ gives the last assertion along the same subsequence, for every $T$. $\square$

Thus the defect $\sigma $ in corollary 7.5.2 carries the whole excess mass $b-V_\omega $, although the currents $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j$ converge to $\omega $ in the coefficient $L^1$ norm and their Gauduchon traces converge in total variation. The location of $\sigma $ is constrained by the limits of the intermediate powers of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j$, as we now show.
