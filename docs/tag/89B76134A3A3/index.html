From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Theorem 6.2.29

Theorem 6.2.29. Let $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then for any prime divisor $E$ over $X$, we have

6.77
\begin{equation} \lim _{j\in I}\nu (\varphi _ j,E)=\nu (\varphi ,E). \end{equation}

Proof. First observe that since $d_ S$ is a pseudometric, it suffices to prove (6.77) when $I=\mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ as partially ordered sets.

By corollary 6.2.19, after adding a Kähler form to $\theta $, we may assume that the masses of $\varphi _ j$ and of $\varphi $ are bounded from below by a positive constant.

By theorem 6.2.27, we may assume that $\varphi _ j$ and $\varphi $ are both $\mathcal{I}$-model and hence model. When proving (6.77), we are free to pass to subsequences.

By proposition 6.2.12 and a sandwich argument, we may assume that the sequence $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is either increasing or decreasing. In the increasing case, the Lelong numbers $\nu (\varphi _ j,E)$ decrease to a limit at least $\nu (\varphi ,E)$, while $L^1$-convergence gives the opposite inequality by upper semicontinuity of Lelong numbers. In the decreasing case, (6.77) follows from proposition 3.1.26. $\square$
