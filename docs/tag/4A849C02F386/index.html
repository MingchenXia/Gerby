Corollary 3.1.57

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Corollary 3.1.57

Corollary 3.1.57. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

3.176
\begin{equation} \label{eq:diam_E} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \lor \psi )+E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi )\geq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi )+E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi ). \end{equation}

Proof. Step 1. We first reduce to the case where $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Assume that (3.176) is known in that case. For each $C>0$, we let

\[ \varphi _ C=\varphi \lor (\phi -C),\quad \psi _ C=\psi \lor (\phi -C). \]

Then

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ C\lor \psi _ C)+E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ C\land \psi _ C)\geq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi _ C)+E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ C). \]

As $C\to \infty $, we have

\[ \varphi _ C\searrow \varphi ,\quad \psi _ C\searrow \psi ,\quad \varphi _ C\lor \psi _ C\searrow \varphi \lor \psi , \]

and, by the monotonicity and maximality of the rooftop,

\[ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C\searrow \varphi \land \psi . \]

Applying proposition 3.1.39 to these four decreasing sequences gives (3.176).

Step 2. We assume that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Thanks to 3.1.56 and the plurifine locality of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 1, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \lor \psi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )=& \int _0^1\int _ X (\varphi \lor \psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{(1-t)\varphi +t(\varphi \lor \psi )}^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ =& \int _0^1 \int _{\{ \psi >\varphi \} } (\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{(1-t)\varphi +t\psi }^ n\, \mathrm{d}t. \end{aligned} \]

On the other hand,

\[ \begin{aligned} & E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi )\\ =& \int _0^1 \int _ X (\psi -\min \{ \varphi ,\psi \} )\, \theta _{((1-t)\varphi +t\psi )\land \psi }^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ \leq & \int _0^1 \int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } (\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{(1-t)\varphi +t\psi }^ n\, \mathrm{d}t& \textup{by \href{../../tag/4CF7246E6E5A/}{3.1.3}}. \end{aligned} \]

Adding these equations together, we conclude (3.176). $\square$
