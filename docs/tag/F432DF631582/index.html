From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · The algebraic setting

Let $L$ be a $\mathbb {Q}$-line bundle on $X$ and $h$ be a Hermitian metric on $L$ with $\theta =c_1(L,h)$.

Lemma 13.4.14. For any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we have $\varphi ^{\mathrm{An}}\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

Proof. After replacing $L$ with a sufficiently high power, we may assume that $L$ is a line bundle. Take a very ample line bundle $H$ on $X$. By Siu's uniform global generation theorem [Siu98], [Theorem 6.27, Dem12] there exists $b>0$ large enough so that $H^{b}\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )$ is globally generated for all $k>0$. Let $\{ s_ i\} _ i$ be a finite set of global sections that generate the sheaf $H^{b}\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )$. Then

\[ v(\mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))=\min _ i v(s_ i). \]

It follows that $v\mapsto -k^{-1}v\left(\mathcal{I}(k\varphi ) \right)$ lies in $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+\frac{b}{k}H)^{\mathrm{An}})$. Using (13.39), we conclude that $\varphi ^{\mathrm{An}}\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.15. Let $\Gamma $ be a piecewise linear curve in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

Proof. The result follows from (13.42), proposition 13.4.4 and 13.4.14. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.16. Let $R$ be a commutative $\mathbb {C}$-algebra of finite type and $I$ be an ideal of $ R[t]$. If for any $a\in S^1$, $a^*I\subseteq I$, then $I$ is stable under the $\mathbb C^*$-action. Moreover, there are ideals $I_0\subseteq I_1\subseteq \cdots \subseteq I_ m$ in $R$ so that

13.44
\begin{equation} I=I_0 + I_1t+ \cdots + I_ m (t^ m). \end{equation}

Proof. It suffices to argue that $I$ can be expanded as in (13.44). To see this, assume that $a\in I$. We can write $a=a_0+a_1t+\cdots +a_ mt^ m$ with $a_ i\in R$. Then our assumption implies that $\sum _ i a_ i \rho ^ i t^ i\in I$ as well for all $\rho \in S^1$. So by the Lagrange interpolation formula, $a_ it^ i\in I$ for all $i$. Therefore, we can write $I$ as $I_0+I_1t+I_2t^2+\cdots $ for some ideals $I_0\subseteq I_1\subseteq \cdots $ in $R$. But as $R$ is noetherian, there is $m\geq 0$ so that $I_{m'}=I_ m$ for $m'>m$. (13.44) follows. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.17. Let $X$ be a complex projective variety and $p:X\times \mathbb {C}\rightarrow X$ be the natural projection. Assume that $\mathcal{I}$ is an analytic coherent ideal sheaf on $X\times \mathbb {C}$. Assume that $\mathcal{I}|_{X \times \mathbb C^*}=p^*\mathcal{J}$ for some coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal{J}$ on $X$. Then $\mathcal{I}$ is the analytification of an algebraic coherent ideal sheaf.

Proof. Let $q:X\times (\mathbb {P}^1\setminus \{ 0\} )\rightarrow X$ be the natural projection. Since $\mathbb C^* \subset \mathbb {P}^1\setminus \{ 0\} $, we can glue $q^*\mathcal{J}$ with $\mathcal{I}$ to get an analytic coherent ideal sheaf on $X\times \mathbb {P}^1$. By the GAGA principle, this ideal sheaf is necessarily algebraic, hence so is its restriction to $X\times \mathbb {C}$. $\square$

Next we point out a version of Siu's uniform global generation lemma [Siu88] that we will need in the proof of our next theorem:

Lemma 13.4.18. Let $L$ be a big line bundle on $X$ such that $c_1(L) = \{ \theta \} $ and $\Phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X\times \Delta , p_1^*\theta )$, where $\Delta $ is the unit disk. Let $G$ be an ample line bundle on $X$. Then there exists $k>0$, depending only on $X$ and $G$, such that $p_1^*(G^ k \otimes L^ m)\otimes \mathcal I(m \Phi )$ is globally generated for all $m \in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$.

Proof. The argument for this is exactly the same as the one in [Lemma 5.6, BBJ21] with Nadel's vanishing theorem replaced by the family version proved by Matsumura in [Theorem 1.7, Mat16]. $\square$

Proposition 13.4.19. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be a model potential and $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ with $\sup _ X \ell _1\leq 0$. Let $\Phi $ be the complexification of $\ell $. Then the function

\[ v\mapsto -\sigma (v)(\Phi ) \quad \text{for } v\in X^{\operatorname{div}} \]

admits a unique extension to an element of $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

Proof. We may assume that $L$ is a line bundle. Observe that the extension is unique if it exists by theorem 13.4.3.

Let $p_1\colon X\times \mathbb {C}\rightarrow X$ be the projection. For each $t>0$, we have $\ell _ t\in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, hence $\ell _ t\sim _ P\phi $ by theorem 3.1.9 and $\ell _ t\sim _{\mathcal{I}}\phi $ by corollary 6.1.9. Thanks to proposition 1.4.17 and 8.5.11, for each $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we have

\[ \mathcal{I}(m\Phi )|_{X\times \Delta ^*} = p_1^* \mathcal{I}(m\phi )|_{X\times \Delta ^*}. \]

In particular, $\mathcal{I}(m\Phi )$ admits an $S^1$-invariant coherent extension to $X\times \mathbb {C}$, obtained by gluing it with $p_1^*\mathcal{I}(m\phi )$ over $X\times \mathbb {C}^*$.

From 13.4.16 and 13.4.17, we get that

13.45
\begin{equation} \mathcal{I}(m\Phi )=\mathfrak {a}_0 +\mathfrak {a}_1 t+\cdots +\mathfrak {a}_{N-1} t^{N-1}+\mathfrak {a}_ N (t^ N)\, , \end{equation}

where the $\mathfrak {a}_ i$'s are coherent ideal sheaves on $X$. Using 13.4.18, there exists an ample line bundle $T$ over $X$ such that $p_1^* (T\otimes L^ m)\otimes \mathcal{I}(m\Phi )$ is globally generated, which is equivalent to $T\otimes L^ m\otimes \mathfrak {a}_ i$ being globally generated for all $i$.

Note. In contrast with the case where $\phi $ is bounded, explored in [BBJ21], $\mathfrak {a}_ N \neq \mathcal{O}_ X$ in general.

We define

\[ \varphi _ m(v)\coloneqq -\frac{1}{m}\sigma (v)(\mathcal{I}(m\Phi ))=-\frac{1}{m}\min _ i (v(\mathfrak {a}_ i)+i),\quad v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}. \]

From the right-hand side of the formula, $\varphi _ m$ can be extended to an element in $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+m^{-1}T)^{\mathrm{An}})$, which we denote by the same symbol.

For $v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}$,

\[ -\sigma (v)(\Phi )=\lim _{m\to \infty }-\frac{1}{2^ m}\sigma (v)(\mathcal{I}(2^ m\Phi ))=\lim _{m\to \infty }\varphi _{2^ m}(v) \]

and the right-hand side defines an element in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ by definition, since $\{ \varphi _{2^ m}\} _ m$ is decreasing by the subadditivity of multiplier ideals theorem 1.4.12. $\square$

Corollary 13.4.20. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Then $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}$ defined in definition 13.4.9 admits a unique extension to $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

The extension will be denoted by the same notation $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}$.

Proof. Observe that the extension is unique if it exists by theorem 13.4.3. We may assume that $\Gamma _{\max }=0$ without loss of generality.

When $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, our assertion follows from proposition 13.4.19 and proposition 13.4.10.

In general, fix an ample line bundle $H$ on $X$ and a Kähler form $\omega \in c_1(H)$. Then we know that

\[ \Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\left(\Gamma ^{m^{-1}\omega }\right)^{\mathrm{An}}\in \mathrm{PSH}((L+m^{-1}H)^{\mathrm{An}}) \]

for any $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. Therefore, $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ by proposition 13.4.5. $\square$
