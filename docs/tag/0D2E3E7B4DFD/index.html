7.4 Uniform tails and undamped convergence0D2E3E7B4DFD

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 7.4 Uniform tails and undamped convergence

On deep sublevel sets the damping weight tends to zero, so convergence of the weighted measures can leave their undamped mass uncontrolled. Uniformly vanishing deep tails supply the additional control: the following condition gives a finite limit measure and weak convergence of the undamped measures $\mu _{u_ j}$. It can equivalently be expressed by an increasing integrability weight.

Theorem 7.4.1. Let $u_ j,u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ be nonpositive and suppose $u_ j\to u$ in capacity. Assume

\[ \lim _{T\to \infty }\sup _ j\mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j<-T\} )=0. \]

Then $\mu _ u$ is finite and $\mu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mu _ u$.

Proof. Choose $T_0$ such that every tail $\mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j<-T\} )$ with $T\geq T_0$ is at most one. By lemma 7.1.2, $\mu _{u_ j}(X)\leq C(1+T_0)^{r_ n}+1$, so the tail condition already gives a uniform bound for the total masses.

With the cutoffs $\eta _ T$ of (7.2), the fixed-level argument in the proof of theorem 7.3.1, applied with $w\equiv 1$, gives

\[ \eta _ T(-u_ j)\mu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \eta _ T(-u)\mu _ u \quad \text{for every fixed }T>0. \]

The masses of the measures on the left are bounded by the uniform bound for the total masses. Since $\eta _ T(-u)$ increases to one off the pole set as $T\to \infty $, monotone convergence proves $\mu _ u(X)<+\infty $.

For a continuous test function $f:X\to \mathbb R$, the error made by inserting $\eta _ T(-u_ j)$ is at most $\| f\| _\infty \mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j<-T\} )$, which is small for large $T$, uniformly in $j$. For the limit potential, the corresponding error tends to zero because $\mu _ u$ is finite and does not charge the pole set. Let first $j\to \infty $ with $T$ fixed, and then $T\to \infty $. $\square$

Let $\chi :[0,+\infty )\to [1,+\infty )$ be continuous, nondecreasing and unbounded. For a nonpositive potential $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ define

\[ J_{\chi ,\omega }(u)=\int _ X\chi (-u)\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ u, \]

with the value $+\infty $ allowed. Since $\chi (T)\to +\infty $, a uniform bound for this functional forces the sublevel tails to vanish uniformly.

Corollary 7.4.2. Let $u_ j,u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ be nonpositive. If $u_ j\to u$ in capacity and $\sup _ jJ_{\chi ,\omega }(u_ j)\leq C$, then

\[ J_{\chi ,\omega }(u)\leq C,\qquad \mu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mu _ u. \]

Proof. Since $\chi \geq 1$, the total masses are at most $C$, and

\[ \mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j<-T\} )\leq C/\chi (T). \]

Thus theorem 7.4.1 applies. For the bound on $J_{\chi ,\omega }(u)$, use (7.2) with the compactly supported continuous function $s\mapsto \chi (s)\eta _ T(s)$ in place of $w\eta _ T$. The corresponding weighted integrals converge for each fixed $T$, and they are at most $C$. Let $T\to \infty $ and use monotone convergence. $\square$

Proposition 7.4.3. For a family of nonpositive potentials in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, the uniform tail condition of theorem 7.4.1 is equivalent to a uniform bound on $J_{\chi ,\omega }$ for some continuous nondecreasing unbounded function $\chi \geq 1$.

Proof. One implication was proved in corollary 7.4.2. Conversely, write the family as $(u_ i)_{i\in I}$. As in the proof of theorem 7.4.1, the tail condition and lemma 7.1.2 give a uniform bound for the total masses. Choose numbers $T_ k\to +\infty $, with $T_{k+1}>T_ k+1$, such that $\mu _{u_ i}(\{ -u_ i>T_ k\} )\leq 2^{-k}$ for every $i\in I$. Let $\rho _ k$ be a continuous nondecreasing function, equal to zero on $[0,T_ k]$ and to one on $[T_ k+1,+\infty )$, and define

\[ \chi (s)=1+\sum _{k\geq 1}\rho _ k(s). \]

The sum is locally finite, so $\chi $ is continuous, nondecreasing and unbounded. Tonelli's theorem gives

\[ J_{\chi ,\omega }(u_ i) \leq \mu _{u_ i}(X)+\sum _{k\geq 1}\mu _{u_ i}(\{ -u_ i>T_ k\} ) \leq \sup _{i\in I}\mu _{u_ i}(X)+1, \]

so this weight gives a uniform integrability bound for the whole family. $\square$
