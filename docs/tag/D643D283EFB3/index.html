Chapter 5 · Plurisubharmonic functions and positive currents · Tag D643D283EFB3

Modifications

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.22.6 Modifications

Proposition 1.22.12. Let $\pi :Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds, and let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Pullback gives a bijection

\[ \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\longrightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta ). \]

Proof. Let $S\subset X$ be a proper analytic subset over whose complement $\pi $ is an isomorphism, and put $D=\pi ^{-1}(S)$. By lemma 1.22.1, pullback preserves the curvature inequality, and the pullback of a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function is not identically $-\infty $, since $\pi $ is an isomorphism off $D$. The pullback map is injective because $\pi $ is surjective.

To prove surjectivity, let $b\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$, and define $u=b\circ \pi ^{-1}$ off $S$. On a coordinate neighborhood in $X$, choose a smooth function $\rho $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \theta $. Then $b+\rho \circ \pi $ is plurisubharmonic on the inverse image of this neighborhood. By properness, its local upper bounds give a local upper bound for $u+\rho $ near $S$, so the removable-singularity theorem extends $u+\rho $ plurisubharmonically across $S$. The resulting quasi-plurisubharmonic extensions of $u$ agree almost everywhere on overlaps, hence everywhere, and glue to a function on $X$.

The current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is positive off $S$. Locally it is the sum of the positive current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u+\rho )$ and the smooth form $\theta -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $. The smooth form gives $S$ zero mass, so the restriction of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ to $S$ is positive as well. Thus $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The plurisubharmonic functions $b+\rho \circ \pi $ and $(u+\rho )\circ \pi $ agree off $D$, hence almost everywhere, hence everywhere. Therefore $b=u\circ \pi $. $\square$

Pullback by a modification preserves $L^1$ convergence within a family with a common lower curvature bound. Compactness controls the pullbacks near the exceptional locus, where a direct change of variables would involve a vanishing Jacobian.

Lemma 1.22.13. Let $\pi :Y\to X$ be a modification between connected complex manifolds, and let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. If $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)$ and all $v_ j,v$ belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, then

\[ v_ j\circ \pi \longrightarrow v\circ \pi \quad \hbox{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(Y). \]

In particular, on compact manifolds this is convergence in $L^1$.

Proof. By lemma 1.4.1, the functions $v_ j$ are uniformly bounded above on relatively compact subsets of $X$. The pullbacks are therefore locally uniformly bounded above and have the common smooth lower curvature bound $-\pi ^*\theta $. After a smooth local correction, the compactness theorem for plurisubharmonic functions shows that every subsequence has a further subsequence that either converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ or tends locally uniformly to $-\infty $ on each connected coordinate region in $Y$.

Every such region meets the complement of the exceptional locus. There $\pi $ is a biholomorphism, and change of variables gives $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergence to $v\circ \pi $, which excludes convergence to $-\infty $. Each subsequential limit agrees with $v\circ \pi $ off the exceptional locus, hence almost everywhere, since this analytic set has smooth volume zero. Thus the limit is unique, and applying this argument on a countable cover by coordinate regions proves convergence of the whole sequence in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(Y)$. $\square$

Convergence in capacity is also preserved by modifications. The comparison takes place away from small neighborhoods of the exceptional sets, so no uniform comparison of capacities on the whole manifolds is needed.

Lemma 1.22.14. Let $\pi :Y\to X$ be a modification between connected compact complex manifolds, equipped with Hermitian forms $\omega _ Y,\omega _ X$. Write $\operatorname {Cap}_ Y=\operatorname {Cap}_{\omega _ Y}$ and $\operatorname {Cap}_ X=\operatorname {Cap}_{\omega _ X}$ for the Hermitian capacities of section 1.8. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $v_ j,v$ on $X$,

\[ v_ j\longrightarrow v\text{ in capacity on }X \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad v_ j\circ \pi \longrightarrow v\circ \pi \text{ in capacity on }Y. \]

Proof. Choose a proper analytic subset $S\subset X$ such that $\pi $ is an isomorphism outside $D=\pi ^{-1}(S)$. Both $S$ and $D$ have capacity zero. For $\epsilon >0$, choose an open neighborhood $V$ of $D$ of capacity less than $\epsilon $. By properness, there is a neighborhood $U_0$ of $S$ with $\pi ^{-1}(U_0)\subset V$; shrink it to an open set $U$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_ X(U)<\epsilon $. The inverse of $\pi $ is biholomorphic near the compact set $X\setminus U$. A finite cover by nested coordinate charts and the comparison of local and global capacities in section 1.8 give a constant $C_\epsilon $ such that, for every Borel set $E\subset X$,

\[ \begin{split} \operatorname {Cap}_ Y(\pi ^{-1}E)& \leq \epsilon +C_\epsilon \operatorname {Cap}_ X(E),\\ \operatorname {Cap}_ X(E)& \leq \epsilon +C_\epsilon \operatorname {Cap}_ Y(\pi ^{-1}E). \end{split} \]

Apply these inequalities to $E=\{ |v_ j-v|>\delta \} $ for each fixed $\delta >0$, omitting the pluripolar pole sets, and let first $j\to \infty $ and then $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. $\square$
