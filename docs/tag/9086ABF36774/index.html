Bounded type energy variation and cocycle

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Proposition 17.2.3

Proposition 17.2.3. Let $u,v\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ and put $u_ t=(1-t)u+tv$ for $0\leq t\leq 1$. Then

17.13
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bounded-type-energy-first-variation} \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t) =\int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _{u_ t}, \end{equation}

with one-sided derivatives at the endpoints, and

17.14
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bounded-type-energy-cocycle} E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u) =\frac1{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n\int _ X(v-u) \langle \theta _ v^ j\wedge \theta _ u^{n-j}\rangle . \end{equation}

In particular, the energy is increasing, and $E_\theta ^\phi (u+C)=E_\theta ^\phi (u)+Cm$ for every constant $C$.

Proof. Let $w\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$. For $0\leq r\leq 1$ the potential $\phi +r(w-\phi )=(1-r)\phi +rw$ again belongs to $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, and multilinearity gives

\[ \mu _{\phi +r(w-\phi )}=\sum _{j=0}^ n\binom nj r^ j(1-r)^{n-j}\langle \theta _ w^ j\wedge \theta _\phi ^{n-j}\rangle . \]

Since $\binom nj\int _0^1r^ j(1-r)^{n-j}\, \mathrm dr=1/(n+1)$, as one sees by integrating by parts $j$ times, we obtain

17.15
\begin{equation} \label{eq:relative-energy-segment-integral} E_\theta ^\phi (w)=\int _0^1\int _ X(w-\phi )\, \mu _{\phi +r(w-\phi )}\, \mathrm dr. \end{equation}

Now put $h=v-u$ and $q_{r,t}=\phi +r(u_ t-\phi )$. Since $\theta _{q_{r,t}}=(1-r)\theta _\phi +r(1-t)\theta _ u+rt\theta _ v$, the measures $\mu _{q_{r,t}}$ are polynomials in $(r,t)$ whose coefficients are mixed measures of total mass $m$, and the weights $u_ t-\phi =(u-\phi )+th$ are bounded. All expressions below are therefore polynomials in $(r,t)$ with finite coefficients, and the derivatives are derivatives of polynomials. Identities between such polynomials of measures follow from the corresponding identities between polynomials in commuting variables $x,y,z$, by substituting the mixed measure $\langle \theta _\phi ^ a\wedge \theta _ u^ b\wedge \theta _ v^ c\rangle $ for the monomial $x^ ay^ bz^ c$; multilinearity shows that this substitution is compatible with the products occurring here. For instance, the identities $\partial _ t\ell ^ n=nr(z-y)\ell ^{n-1}$ and $\partial _ r\ell ^ n=n\bigl ((1-t)y+tz-x\bigr )\ell ^{n-1}$ for $\ell =(1-r)x+r(1-t)y+rtz$ give

17.16
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bounded-type-measure-derivatives} \partial _ t\mu _{q_{r,t}}=nr\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle ,\qquad \partial _ r\mu _{q_{r,t}}=n\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ t-\phi )\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle , \end{equation}

in the sense of (17.1). Differentiating (17.15) with $w=u_ t$ and using the first of these identities, we obtain

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t) =\int _0^1\left[\int _ Xh\, \mu _{q_{r,t}} +nr\int _ X(u_ t-\phi )\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle \right]\mathrm dr. \]

By theorem 17.1.3, applied to the bounded quasi-psh differences $u_ t-\phi $ and $h$, the second integral equals $\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ t-\phi )\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle $, which is $\frac1n\partial _ r\int _ Xh\, \mu _{q_{r,t}}$ by the second identity. The integrand in $r$ is therefore $\partial _ r\bigl [r\int _ Xh\, \mu _{q_{r,t}}\bigr ]$, and since $q_{1,t}=u_ t$,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t)=\int _ Xh\, \mu _{u_ t}. \]

This is (17.13). Integrating it from $t=0$ to $t=1$, and expanding $\mu _{u_ t}=\sum _ j\binom nj t^ j(1-t)^{n-j}\langle \theta _ v^ j\wedge \theta _ u^{n-j}\rangle $ with the same beta integrals, gives (17.14). If $u\leq v$, every term of the cocycle formula is nonnegative, so the energy is increasing. Every mixed measure has total mass $m$, so taking $v=u+C$ in the cocycle formula gives the last assertion. $\square$
