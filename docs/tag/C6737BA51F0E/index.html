14.11 Equations in a prescribed ordinary typeC6737BA51F0E

We return to a semipositive big background $\theta $ and now require the solution to have the ordinary singularity type of a fixed reference $\varphi $. Throughout this section, $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is an envelope fixed point admitting a smaller-background envelope; that is, (13.26) holds:

\[ P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi ,\qquad P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty \quad \text{for some }0<\varepsilon <1. \]

We fix such an $\varepsilon $. Note that $\varphi \leq 0$, since every competitor for $P_\theta [\varphi ]$ is nonpositive. For a normalized solution $u$, the fixed-point property gives the upper bound $u\leq \varphi $, while the smaller-background envelope $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ yields a uniform lower bound $u\geq \varphi -C$. A supersolution and the addition theorem then prove existence in this type.

Theorem 14.11.1. Let $\theta $ and $\varphi $ be as above, and let $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$, where $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\mu (X)>0$. Then:

  1. For every $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ such that

    \[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu . \]

  2. There are $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ and a uniquely determined constant $c>0$ such that

    \[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu . \]

The potential in part (2) need not be unique up to an additive constant, even for a Hermitian background and the reference $\varphi =0$; see the counterexample in theorem 11.6.1.

The proof uses two estimates in the prescribed type. The first is a lower bound for the norm of a Monge–Ampère density.

Lemma 14.11.2. Fix $q>1$. There is $b_ q>0$ such that every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=F\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad 0\leq F\in L^ q(\mathrm{d}V), \]

satisfies $\| F\| _ q\geq b_ q$. For fixed $\theta $ and $\varepsilon $, the constant $b_ q$ is independent of $\varphi $.

Proof. Since $u\sim \varphi $, the smaller-background envelope $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(u)$ is not identically $-\infty $. If $F=0$, then theorem 14.1.2, applied to $u$ and $u+1$ with coefficient zero, would give $u\geq u+1$; hence $N=\| F\| _ q>0$.

Apply lemma 14.2.1 in the semipositive big background $(1-\varepsilon )\theta $ to $F/N$, whose $L^ q$ norm is one. It gives a bounded $s$ with $-1\leq s\leq 0$ and a constant $b_ q>0$, depending only on this fixed background and on $q$, such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}_{(1-\varepsilon )\theta }(s)\geq (b_ q/N)F\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Put $h=P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )+s$. This is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function with $h\preceq u$. Since $\theta _ h$ is the sum of the positive currents $\varepsilon \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ and $(1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s$, the same density bound holds for $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h)$. To justify this, we truncate $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ at finite levels, use positivity of bounded mixed products, and exhaust the set where $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ is finite; its pole set has zero volume.

If $N<b_ q$, we would have

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq (N/b_ q)\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h+t) \quad \text{for every }t\in \mathbb R. \]

Since $N/b_ q<1$, theorem 14.1.2 would then imply $u\geq h+t$ for every $t$, which is impossible at a point where both potentials are finite. Thus $N\geq b_ q$. The construction of $s$ used no property of $\varphi $, which proves the last assertion. $\square$

The lemma bounds the $L^ q$ norm of the density from below; it gives no positive lower bound for the total mass $\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}V$.

The second estimate bounds a normalized solution from below by $\varphi $ minus a uniform constant. It is what keeps limits of solutions in the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi $.

Theorem 14.11.3. Let $\theta $ and $\varphi $ be as above. Suppose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $u\sim \varphi $, $\sup _ Xu=0$, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq f\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad 0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V),\quad p>1. \]

There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed geometry, $\varphi $, $p$, and an upper bound for $\| f\| _{L^ p}$, such that

\[ \varphi -C\leq u\leq \varphi . \]

Proof. The upper bound follows directly from the fixed-point property: since $u\leq 0$ and $u\preceq \varphi $, the function $u$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $.

For the lower bound we construct a subsolution $\psi $ of the type of $\varphi $ whose measure dominates a multiple of $\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$, and then apply singular domination. Put $\varphi _\varepsilon =P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$, so that $\varphi _\varepsilon \leq \varphi \leq 0$. Uniform exponential integrability (lemma 1.5.1) for the normalized potentials $u$ and exponential integrability for the fixed function $\varphi _\varepsilon $ allow us to choose $\gamma >0$ and $1<q<p$ such that

\[ g=\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma (\varphi _\varepsilon +u)}f \]

has a uniform $L^ q$ bound. Explicitly, we first choose $q<p$ and apply Hölder's inequality with exponents $p/q$ and $p/(p-q)$; then we choose $\gamma $ so small that the exponential factor is controlled by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the two exponential integrability estimates.

Theorem 14.2.3, applied in the semipositive big background $(1-\varepsilon )\theta $ to the density $1+g$, gives a bounded $v$ with $\sup _ Xv=0$ and a constant $c>0$ such that

\[ ((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n=c(1+g)\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Both $\| v\| _\infty $ and $c^{-1}$ are uniformly bounded. Adding the constant one to $g$ makes the root integral of the density at least one, so no lower bound for the integral of $f$ is needed.

Put $h=\varphi _\varepsilon +v\leq \varphi $, which is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and define

\[ \chi (t)=\frac1{1-t}\quad (t\leq 0),\qquad \psi =\varphi +\chi (h-\varphi ), \]

where the formula for $\psi $ is evaluated off the pole sets. Then $0\leq \chi \leq 1$, $0<\chi '\leq 1$, and $\chi ''>0$. For bounded potentials with $h\leq \varphi $, lemma 1.7.9, applied with $G(x,y)=x+\chi (y-x)$, gives

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G(\varphi ,h) \geq (1-\chi '(h-\varphi ))\theta _\varphi +\chi '(h-\varphi )\theta _ h\geq 0. \]

For unbounded potentials we apply this bounded assertion to $\varphi _ k=\max (\varphi ,-k)$ and $h_ k=\max (h,-k)\leq \varphi _ k$. Since $G$ is nondecreasing in both variables, $G(\varphi _ k,h_ k)$ decreases as $k$ increases. Its limit is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and lies between $\varphi $ and $\varphi +1$; hence it is not identically $-\infty $, and it equals $\psi $. Locality on common finite levels and positivity of bounded products give

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi ) \geq (\varphi -h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h) \geq c(\varphi -h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma \varphi _\varepsilon } \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where the second inequality uses $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h)\geq ((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\geq cg\, \mathrm{d}V$. These inequalities are first obtained off the pole sets; since pole sets have zero volume, they extend by exhaustion to all Borel sets.

Write $T=-\varphi _\varepsilon \geq 0$. If $C_1\geq 1+\| v\| _\infty $, then $\varphi -h+1\leq T+C_1$. Since

\[ A:=\inf _{T\geq 0}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\gamma T}}{(T+C_1)^{2n}}>0, \]

we obtain, with a uniform $d>0$,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi ) \geq d \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \]

Choose $K=2+\gamma ^{-1}|\log d|$. On $\{ u<\psi -K\} $ we have $u<1-K$, because $\psi \leq \varphi +1\leq 1$; hence

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq d^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma (1-K)} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi -K) \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi -K). \]

The potentials $u$ and $\psi -K$ have the same ordinary singularity type. Moreover, $u\geq \varphi -C_ u$ for some constant $C_ u$, so $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(u)\geq \varphi _\varepsilon -C_ u$ is not identically $-\infty $. Theorem 14.1.2 therefore yields $u\geq \psi -K\geq \varphi -K$. All constants used to choose $K$ are uniform over the family in the theorem; the possibly nonuniform constant $C_ u$ serves only to show that the smaller-background envelope of $u$ is not identically $-\infty $, and it enters no estimate. $\square$

The existence proof starts from a supersolution in the prescribed type. It is obtained from a bounded solution by a rooftop envelope with $\varphi $.

Lemma 14.11.4. For nonzero $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\lambda >0$, there exists $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w\sim \varphi $ such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda w}f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Proof. Let $v$ be the bounded solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\, \mathrm{d}V$ given by theorem 14.2.2, and write $M=\sup _ Xv$ and $a=v-M\leq 0$. The rooftop envelope $p=P_\theta (a,\varphi )$ satisfies $\varphi -\| a\| _\infty \leq p\leq \varphi $, so $p\sim \varphi $. By the rooftop inequality of proposition 13.1.5,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p) \leq \mathbf1_{\{ p=a\leq \varphi \} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a) +\mathbf1_{\{ p=\varphi <a\} }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\varphi ). \]

The second term vanishes: by proposition 13.1.6, the measure of $\varphi $ is carried by $\{ \varphi =0\} $, whereas $\varphi <a\leq 0$ on the set in question. On the first contact set, $a=p$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (a+M)}f\, \mathrm{d}V$. Thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda (p+M)}f\, \mathrm{d}V$, and $w=p+M$ has the required properties. $\square$
