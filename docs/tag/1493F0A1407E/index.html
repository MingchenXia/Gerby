Lemma 10.4.11

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Lemma 10.4.11

Lemma 10.4.11. Let $T\in \mathcal{Z}_+(X,\alpha )$ be a Kähler current and $\omega $ be a Kähler form on $X$. Then

10.88
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DeltaTincreaseomegatemp1} \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T+\omega ). \end{equation}

Moreover,

10.89
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DeltaTincreaseomegatemp2} \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)=\bigcap _{\epsilon >0}\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T+\epsilon \omega ). \end{equation}

Proof. We first prove (10.88). Taking quasi-equisingular approximations, we reduce immediately to the case where $T$ has analytic singularities. By 10.4.5, we may assume that $T$ has log singularities. Take $D$ and $R$ as in (10.84). By definition again, it suffices to show that

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\{ R\} )\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\{ R\} +\{ \omega \} ), \]

which is clear by definition.

Next we prove (10.89). Thanks to (10.88), it remains to prove that both sides have the same volume:

\[ \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}\operatorname{vol}(T+\epsilon \omega )=\operatorname{vol}T. \]

This is proved in proposition 7.3.4. $\square$
