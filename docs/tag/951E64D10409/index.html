11.31.9 Continuous potentials and pluriharmonic traces951E64D10409

The Gauduchon trace pairs $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with the smooth form $\gamma ^{n-1}$, which is positive and pluriharmonic. In this section $n\geq 2$, $\omega $ is a Hermitian form on $X$, and $G$ is a positive pluriharmonic $(n-1,n-1)$-current, that is, a positive current with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=0$ (see section 11.22). Such currents include $\gamma ^{n-1}$, but in general their coefficients are only measures, and the product $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge G$ is not defined a priori. Its integration-by-parts formula contains gradient terms, since $G$ need not be $d$-closed. The equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=0$ converts uniform control of a potential difference into a bound for its gradient energy against $G$. This estimate defines the gradient currents for a continuous potential and makes the product stable under uniform approximation.

Lemma 11.31.10.BD07AFC2CACB Let $(X,\omega )$ be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$, and let $G$ be a positive $(n-1,n-1)$-current with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=0$. Put $\mu =\omega \wedge G$ and $M=\mu (X)$. If $a,b$ are smooth real functions and $C\geq 0$ is a constant with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a,\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\geq -C\omega $, then

\[ 0\leq \int _ X\mathrm{d}(a-b)\wedge \mathrm{d}^ c(a-b)\wedge G \leq 2C M\| a-b\| _\infty . \]

For every smooth real function $v$, the total variation norms of the order-zero currents $\mathrm{d}v\wedge G$ and $\mathrm{d}^ cv\wedge G$ satisfy

\[ \| \mathrm{d}v\wedge G\| _{\mathrm{TV}}+ \| \mathrm{d}^ cv\wedge G\| _{\mathrm{TV}} \leq C_\omega M^{1/2} \left(\int _ X\mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm{d}^ cv\wedge G\right)^{1/2}. \]

Here the total variation norms are computed with respect to $\omega $, and $C_\omega $ depends only on $(X,\omega )$.

Proof. Set $v=a-b$. The identity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v^2)=2\mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm{d}^ cv+2v\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$, integrated against $G$, and the equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=0$ give

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm{d}^ cv\wedge G =-\int _ Xv\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge G. \]

The measures $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a+C\omega )\wedge G$ and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b+C\omega )\wedge G$ are positive, and each has mass $CM$, because the terms $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge G$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge G$ integrate to zero. Their difference $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge G$ therefore has total variation at most $2CM$, which proves the upper bound. The lower bound holds because $\mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm{d}^ cv=(\mathrm{i}/2\pi )\partial v\wedge \bar\partial v$ is a positive $(1,1)$-form.

For the second estimate, work in a local unitary frame for $\omega $. Positivity of $G$ means that its coefficients form a positive semidefinite matrix of measures. These entries are absolutely continuous with respect to the trace measure $\mu $: the diagonal entries are dominated by the trace, and the off-diagonal entries by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for positive semidefinite matrices. The resulting matrix of densities has trace bounded by a constant depending only on the normalization. Pairing $\mathrm{d}v\wedge G$ or $\mathrm{d}^ cv\wedge G$ with a test one-form $\eta $, and applying the matrix Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and then the integral Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\[ |\langle \mathrm{d}v\wedge G,\eta \rangle | +|\langle \mathrm{d}^ cv\wedge G,\eta \rangle | \leq C_\omega \| \eta \| _\infty M^{1/2} \left(\int _ X\mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm{d}^ cv\wedge G\right)^{1/2}. \]

A finite cover by such frames and a partition of unity give the global estimate. Taking the supremum over smooth test forms of norm at most one yields the total variation bound. When $M=0$, positivity gives $G=0$, and both estimates hold trivially. $\square$

Theorem 11.31.11.E0C21CC2C1A9 Let $(X,\omega )$ be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$, let $\alpha $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\alpha )\cap C^0(X)$. For every positive $(n-1,n-1)$-current $G$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=0$, the product

\[ (\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge G \]

has a canonical definition as a positive measure of mass $\int _ X\alpha \wedge G$, which agrees with the usual product if $u$ or $G$ is smooth. If $u_ j,u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\alpha )\cap C^0(X)$ and $u_ j\to u$ uniformly, and if positive pluriharmonic currents $G_ j$ converge weakly to $G$, then

\[ (\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)\wedge G_ j \rightharpoonup (\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge G. \]

Proof. We first define the gradient currents $\mathrm{d}u\wedge G$ and $\mathrm{d}^ cu\wedge G$ as limits, then define the product by an integration-by-parts formula, and finally prove joint continuity.

Step 1: gradient currents. Choose smooth approximants $a_ k\to u$ as in lemma 5.42.5. There is a common constant $C>0$ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ k\geq -C\omega $. Write $M=\int _ X\omega \wedge G$. Since $a_ k$ converges uniformly, lemma 11.31.10 shows that $\mathrm{d}a_ k\wedge G$ and $\mathrm{d}^ ca_ k\wedge G$ are Cauchy sequences in total variation. Denote their limits, which are currents of order zero, by $\mathrm{d}u\wedge G$ and $\mathrm{d}^ cu\wedge G$. Applying the same lemma to two uniformly convergent smooth sequences with a common lower Hessian bound shows that the limits do not depend on the sequence. Uniform convergence also gives $a_ kG\to uG$ in total variation.

Step 2: the product. Define

39820CDDAFC2 11.31.12
\begin{equation} (\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge G =\alpha \wedge G+\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}^ cu\wedge G) -\mathrm{d}^ c(\mathrm{d}u\wedge G)-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(uG) \end{equation}

in the sense of distributions. For smooth $u$, the Leibniz rule and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=0$ show that this agrees with the usual product. Consequently $(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ k)\wedge G$ converges in distributions to the right-hand side of (11.31.12). With $\varepsilon _ k\to 0$ as in lemma 5.42.5, the measures $(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ k+\varepsilon _ k\omega )\wedge G$ are positive, with masses $\int _ X\alpha \wedge G+\varepsilon _ kM$. Their distributional limit is therefore a positive measure, and testing against the constant function one gives its mass. The uniform mass bound upgrades distributional convergence to weak convergence of measures. If $G$ is smooth, the uniform convergence of $a_ k$ also identifies the limit with the product of the current $\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and the smooth form $G$.

Step 3: independence of the decomposition. Adding a smooth function $h$ to $u$ adds $\mathrm{d}h\wedge G$ and $\mathrm{d}^ ch\wedge G$ to the gradient currents, so replacing $(\alpha ,u)$ by $(\alpha -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h,u+h)$ leaves the product unchanged. If two continuous decompositions define the same $(1,1)$-current, the difference of their potentials has smooth $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$; taking its trace in a coordinate chart and applying elliptic regularity shows that this difference is smooth. Thus the product depends only on the current $\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and on $G$.

Step 4: joint continuity. For $u$ and for each $u_ j$, keep only those smooth approximants from lemma 5.42.5 whose curvature errors are at most one. Choosing $C$ with $\alpha \leq (C-1)\omega $, all these approximants satisfy the same lower Hessian bound $-C\omega $. The numbers $M_ j=\int _ X\omega \wedge G_ j$ are bounded, by weak convergence. Fix a smooth approximant $a_ k$ of $u$, and put $\delta _{j,k}=\| u_ j-a_ k\| _\infty $. Applying lemma 11.31.10 to $a_ k$ and to smooth approximants of $u_ j$, and passing to the gradient limits, we find that the total variations of the differences $(\mathrm{d}u_ j-\mathrm{d}a_ k)\wedge G_ j$ and $(\mathrm{d}^ cu_ j-\mathrm{d}^ ca_ k)\wedge G_ j$ are at most $C'M_ j\sqrt{\delta _{j,k}}$, where $C'$ is independent of $j$ and $k$.

For a smooth real test function $\phi $, formula (11.31.12) now gives

\[ \left|\left\langle (\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)\wedge G_ j -(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ k)\wedge G_ j,\phi \right\rangle \right| \leq C_\phi M_ j(\sqrt{\delta _{j,k}}+\delta _{j,k}), \]

where $C_\phi $ depends on the first two derivatives of $\phi $, on $\omega $ and on $C$. The same estimate holds with $u_ j,G_ j$ replaced by $u,G$. For fixed $k$, the products $(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ k)\wedge G_ j$ of a fixed smooth form with $G_ j$ converge weakly to $(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ k)\wedge G$. Let first $j\to \infty $ and then $k\to \infty $; since $u_ j\to u$ uniformly, the error tends to zero. This proves convergence in distributions, and positivity together with the bounded masses $\int _ X\alpha \wedge G_ j$ upgrades it to weak convergence of measures. $\square$

For $G=\gamma ^{n-1}$ and $\alpha =\theta $, the product in theorem 11.31.11 is the Gauduchon trace of a continuous potential. Its conserved mass controls a linear pairing of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$; the nonlinear powers of this current, constructed for unbounded potentials in section 13.14, require a separate mass analysis.
