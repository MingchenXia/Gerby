32.1 Equations for finite measures71BC8ED98CA7

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 32.1 Equations for finite measures

A positive exponent determines a unique solution in the relative class. At exponent zero, the equation determines its positive scalar, but normalized potentials may still differ, as in corollary 31.7.2.

Theorem 32.1.1. For every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu $ on $X$:

  1. There are $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and a unique $c>0$ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $.

  2. For each $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $.

If $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$ with $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for some $p>1$, the solutions in both parts may be taken with $u\sim \varphi $.

Part (1) is theorem 32.7.2, part (2) is theorem 32.7.1, and the assertion for $L^ p$ densities is corollary 32.8.3.

The standing assumptions also give $v_-(\omega )>0$. Indeed, choose $C>0$ with $\theta \leq C\omega $. Since $\varphi \preceq 0$, theorem 30.1.1 gives

\[ 0<\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi ) \leq \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{C\omega }(0) =C^ n v_-(\omega ). \]
