Zero volume nonanalytic product

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Example 16.6.22

Example 16.6.22. Take $C=\mathbb P^1$ and $Z=\mathbb P^ k$, $k\geq 1$, with their standard Fubini–Study forms, and let $D$ be a hyperplane. In this product setting there is a potential $u$ without an analytic ordinary type such that

\[ P_\Theta [u]=P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi , \qquad u\not\sim \Psi , \]

and the two envelopes of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle. Their common model for the zero obstacle has zero singularity volume.

Proof. Use the unbounded curve potential $w$ constructed in the proof of proposition 16.6.4, whose two envelopes are zero. A logarithmic potential of a hyperplane on $Z$ gives $\psi $ with $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]$ and supremum zero. Set $u=\Psi +w\circ p$ and apply proposition 16.6.21, corollary 16.6.15. Fixing $z\notin D$ shows that $u-\Psi =w\circ p$ is unbounded, so $u\not\sim \Psi $. If a potential $a$ with analytic singularities were ordinarily equivalent to $u$, proposition 16.2.7 would give $u\sim a\sim P_\Theta [a]=P_\Theta [u]=\Psi $, a contradiction. $\square$
