Current cones and endpoint equations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Current cones and endpoint equations

We keep the product $X=B\times C$, the backgrounds $\theta =b\kappa +(ca+e)w$ with real parameters $b,c,e$, and the probability $\mu =\sigma \wedge w$ of section 26.5.3, and write $h=\kappa +w$ for the product Hermitian form. Pullbacks of nonnegative top-degree forms on $B$ are closed, and testing against them determines which backgrounds have positive current representatives, not only which have smooth positive ones. At a finite end of the unit-volume hypersurface, the reciprocal of the fiber coefficient is integrable with an exponent greater than one, so the equation on the base still has a continuous solution.

Proposition 26.5.14. In this real span, the pseudoeffective and nef cones both equal

\[ \{ b\geq 0,\ e\geq 0,\ e+cM\geq 0\} . \]

A background in this span is big, that is, admits a representative $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \delta h$ with $\delta >0$, if and only if $b>0$, $e>0$ and $e+cM>0$. Thus the big backgrounds in this span form exactly the cone $\mathscr C$ of theorem 26.5.10.

Proof. Put $L=ca+e$, and suppose that a quasi-plurisubharmonic $u$ satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \delta h$ for some $\delta \geq 0$. Pairing with the closed semipositive form $\kappa ^{m-1}\wedge w$ and using Stokes' theorem for currents gives $b\geq \delta $. For every smooth nonnegative top-degree form $\eta $ on $B$, its pullback to $X$ is closed for degree reasons, and

\[ \int _ X(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-\delta h)\wedge \eta =\int _ B(L-\delta )\eta \geq 0. \]

If $L<\delta $ at some point, a nonzero form $\eta $ supported in the open set where $L<\delta $ contradicts this inequality. Hence $L\geq \delta $ everywhere. For $\delta =0$ these conditions are exactly $b\geq 0$, $e\geq 0$ and $e+cM\geq 0$; conversely, they make $\theta $ semipositive, and such forms are nef because $\theta +\varepsilon h>0$ for every $\varepsilon >0$. Conversely, if $\theta $ is nef, applying the same tests to smooth positive representatives of $\theta +\varepsilon h$ and letting $\varepsilon $ tend to zero gives these inequalities. For $\delta >0$ the conditions force the three strict inequalities, and conversely these make $\theta $ itself strictly positive. $\square$

Proposition 26.5.15. Under the hypotheses of lemma 26.5.12 on the extreme sets, let $\theta =b\kappa +L_0w$, with $b>0$, be one of the boundary forms $\theta _+(b)$ or $\theta _-(b)$ of theorem 26.5.13 at a finite end. There is a continuous $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function $u$, pulled back from $B$, such that

26.37
\begin{equation} \label{eq:warped-continuous-endpoint-equation} (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=\mu . \end{equation}

Here the product is the usual product of bounded potentials. No solution of this equation is of class $C^{1,1}$ on $X$.

Proof. Let $f=a$ for $\theta _+(b)$ and $f=1-a/M$ for $\theta _-(b)$, and let $r>1$ be the corresponding ratio $r_0$ or $r_ M$. The coefficient $L_0$ is a positive constant multiple of $f$. In each tube around an extreme component, the integral of $f^{-p}$ converges exactly when

\[ \int _0^{s_0}s^{\operatorname {codim}_{\mathbb R}Z_ i-1-k_ ip}\, \mathrm{d}s \]

converges, where $s$ is the normal radius and $s_0>0$ is small. Thus $1/L_0\in L^ p(B,\sigma )$ for every $1<p<r$. The zero set of $L_0$ has volume zero, so the value assigned to the density there is irrelevant. The formulas for $\theta _\pm (b)$ give

\[ H_0:=\int _ B\frac{\sigma }{L_0}=nb^ m. \]

We apply theorem 10.1.1 on $B$ to the positive closed background $b\kappa $ and obtain a continuous $\phi $ with $A=b\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi \geq 0$ and $A^ m=c_0\sigma /L_0$ for a constant $c_0>0$. Since $b\kappa $ is closed, bounded potentials do not change its mass, by theorem 2.2.2; hence $\int _ BA^ m=b^ m$ and $c_0=b^ m/H_0$. Set $u=\phi \circ p_ B$, where $p_ B:X\to B$ is the projection. Then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=A+L_0w\geq 0$.

The pullback identity of lemma 1.7.3 gives $A^{m+1}=0$ on $X$, and $(L_0w)^2=0$. The multilinear expansion of proposition 1.7.1 therefore gives

\[ (A+L_0w)^ n=nL_0 A^ m\wedge w =\frac{nb^ m}{H_0}\sigma \wedge w=\mu . \]

The cancellation $L_0(\sigma /L_0)=\sigma $ holds as measures, since the zero set of $L_0$ has volume zero and $A^ m$ is absolutely continuous with density $c_0/L_0$ relative to $\sigma $.

Suppose that some solution $q$, not necessarily pulled back from $B$, were of class $C^{1,1}$ on $X$. Since $\mu /h^ n$ is smooth and strictly positive on compact $X$, proposition 1.7.5 would give $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \delta h$ for some $\delta >0$. This contradicts proposition 26.5.14, because $L_0$ vanishes somewhere. $\square$
