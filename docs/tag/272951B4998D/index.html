From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 14.4 Minimal multiplicities and positivity loci

Let $\alpha $ be a class in the pseudo-effective cone $\mathcal E$ and $\theta \in \alpha $ a smooth representative. For every $\varepsilon >0$ the class $\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ is big, since it contains $T+\varepsilon \omega $ for every positive current $T\in \alpha $, and its potential $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }$ with minimal singularities measures the positivity of $\alpha $ that remains after the curvature is allowed to drop by $\varepsilon \omega $. The loci where positivity fails are described in theorem 14.4.4, and at the end of the section we compare positivity on $X$ and on a modification of $X$.

Definition 14.4.1. For $\alpha \in \mathcal E$, $x\in X$ and a prime divisor $D\subset X$ put

\[ \nu (\alpha ,x)=\sup _{\varepsilon >0}\nu (V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega },x), \qquad \nu (\alpha ,D)=\sup _{\varepsilon >0}\nu (V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega },D). \]

These are the minimal multiplicities of $\alpha $. The non-nef locus of $\alpha $ is

\[ E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )=\{ x\in X:\nu (\alpha ,x)>0\} . \]

If $\alpha $ is big, its non-Kähler locus is

\[ E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\bigcap _ T\{ \varphi _ T=-\infty \} , \]

where $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ T$ ranges over the Kähler currents in $\alpha $ for which $\varphi _ T$ has analytic singularities. For a class $\alpha \in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ that is not big we put $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=X$, and for a class that is not pseudo-effective we put $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )=X$.

If $0<\varepsilon <\varepsilon '$, then $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\varepsilon \omega )\subset \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\varepsilon '\omega )$, so $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }\leq V_{\theta +\varepsilon '\omega }$. Hence the minimal potentials decrease as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, their Lelong numbers increase, and the suprema in the definition may be taken along $\varepsilon =1/j$. Replacing $\theta $ by another smooth representative $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ changes $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }$ only by a bounded function, because $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }-g$ and $V_{\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g+\varepsilon \omega }$ both have minimal singularities in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g+\varepsilon \omega )$; and replacing $\omega $ by another Kähler form $\omega '$ with $C^{-1}\omega \leq \omega '\leq C\omega $ places $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega '}$ between $V_{\theta +C^{-1}\varepsilon \omega }$ and $V_{\theta +C\varepsilon \omega }$. Thus the minimal multiplicities and the two loci depend only on $\alpha $.

If $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ is a positive current in $\alpha $, then $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\varepsilon \omega )$ for every $\varepsilon >0$, so $\varphi \leq V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }+\sup _ X\varphi $; hence

\[ \nu (\alpha ,x)\leq \nu (T,x),\qquad \nu (\alpha ,D)\leq \nu (T,D), \]

and in particular both multiplicities are finite. For each $j$, the Lelong number $\nu (V_{\theta +\omega /j},x)$ equals its generic value $\nu (V_{\theta +\omega /j},D)$ at every point $x\in D$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $D$ (proposition 12.3.5 and the remarks before proposition 14.2.8). At points of $D$ outside the union of these countably many exceptional sets, which form a dense subset of $D$ by proposition 12.3.5, we therefore have $\nu (\alpha ,x)=\nu (\alpha ,D)$, while $\nu (\alpha ,x)\geq \nu (\alpha ,D)$ at every point of $D$. Consequently

\[ \nu (\alpha ,D)=\inf _{x\in D}\nu (\alpha ,x), \]

and the infimum is attained at every point of $D$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets.

Proposition 14.4.2. Let $\alpha \in \mathcal E$. Then $\nu (\alpha ,x)\leq \nu (V_\theta ,x)$ and $\nu (\alpha ,D)\leq \nu (V_\theta ,D)$ for every $x\in X$ and every prime divisor $D$, with equality if $\alpha $ is big. Moreover, $\alpha $ is modified nef if and only if $\nu (\alpha ,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$.

Proof. The positive current $T_0=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\theta \in \alpha $ gives the inequalities, by the bound preceding the proposition. Suppose that $\alpha $ is big, and choose $S\in \alpha $ with $S\geq \delta \omega $, $\delta >0$. For $\varepsilon >0$ put $T_\varepsilon =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }\geq -\varepsilon \omega $ and $t=\varepsilon /(\delta +\varepsilon )$. Then $(1-t)T_\varepsilon +tS\geq \bigl (-(1-t)\varepsilon +t\delta \bigr )\omega =0$ is a positive current in $\alpha $, whose potential $(1-t)V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }+t\varphi _ S$, with $S=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ S$, is more singular than $V_\theta $. Since Lelong numbers are additive and positively homogeneous (proposition 12.1.4) and do not decrease when a function is replaced by a more singular one, we obtain

\[ \nu (V_\theta ,x)\leq (1-t)\nu (V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega },x)+t\nu (S,x)\leq (1-t)\nu (\alpha ,x)+t\nu (S,x). \]

As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ we have $t\to 0$, and since $\nu (S,x)$ is finite we obtain $\nu (V_\theta ,x)\leq \nu (\alpha ,x)$. For a prime divisor $D$, evaluate the same inequality at a point $x\in D$ at which the Lelong numbers of $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }$ and of $S$ attain their generic values along $D$ (proposition 12.3.5); it gives $\nu (V_\theta ,D)\leq \nu (V_\theta ,x)\leq (1-t)\nu (\alpha ,D)+t\nu (S,D)$, and letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ proves the equality for $D$.

If $\alpha $ is modified nef and $T_\varepsilon $ is as in definition 14.3.8, then $T_\varepsilon =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _\varepsilon $ with $\varphi _\varepsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\varepsilon \omega )$, so $\nu (V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega },D)\leq \nu (\varphi _\varepsilon ,D)=0$ for every $\varepsilon >0$; hence $\nu (\alpha ,D)=0$. Conversely, if $\nu (\alpha ,D)=0$ for every $D$, then the currents $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }$ satisfy the conditions of definition 14.3.8. $\square$

Proposition 14.4.3. For a fixed point $x\in X$ or a fixed prime divisor $D$, the minimal multiplicity $\alpha \mapsto \nu (\alpha ,x)$, respectively $\alpha \mapsto \nu (\alpha ,D)$, is positively homogeneous, convex and lower semicontinuous on the pseudo-effective cone $\mathcal E$, and continuous on the big cone $\mathcal B$.

Proof. We treat $\nu (\cdot ,x)$. The proof for $D$ is the same, except that the additivity of Lelong numbers along $D$ is applied at a point of $D$ at which all the functions involved attain their generic Lelong numbers along $D$ (proposition 12.3.5). For $t>0$ we have $\mathrm{PSH}(X,t\theta +\varepsilon \omega )=t\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +(\varepsilon /t)\omega )$, hence $V_{t\theta +\varepsilon \omega }=tV_{\theta +(\varepsilon /t)\omega }$, and taking suprema over $\varepsilon $ gives $\nu (t\alpha ,x)=t\nu (\alpha ,x)$. If $\alpha _1,\alpha _2\in \mathcal E$ have representatives $\theta _1,\theta _2$, the sum of the minimal currents with allowance $\varepsilon $ in the two classes is a current $\theta _1+\theta _2+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(V_{\theta _1+\varepsilon \omega }+V_{\theta _2+\varepsilon \omega })\geq -2\varepsilon \omega $ in $\alpha _1+\alpha _2$. By minimality and additivity of Lelong numbers,

\[ \nu (V_{\theta _1+\theta _2+2\varepsilon \omega },x)\leq \nu (V_{\theta _1+\varepsilon \omega },x)+\nu (V_{\theta _2+\varepsilon \omega },x)\leq \nu (\alpha _1,x)+\nu (\alpha _2,x), \]

and taking the supremum over $\varepsilon $ gives subadditivity. Together with homogeneity this proves convexity.

Let $\alpha _ j\to \alpha $ in $\mathcal E$, with representatives $\theta _ j$, and fix $\varepsilon >0$. For large $j$, by (14.16), the class $\alpha -\alpha _ j$ has a smooth representative $\eta _ j$ with $-\varepsilon \omega \leq \eta _ j\leq \varepsilon \omega $. The current $\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_{\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega }+\eta _ j\in \alpha $ is at least $-2\varepsilon \omega $, and its potential relative to $\theta $ differs from $V_{\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega }$ by a smooth function. By minimality,

\[ \nu (V_{\theta +2\varepsilon \omega },x)\leq \nu (V_{\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega },x)\leq \nu (\alpha _ j,x) \]

for large $j$, so $\nu (V_{\theta +2\varepsilon \omega },x)\leq \varliminf _ j\nu (\alpha _ j,x)$. Taking the supremum over $\varepsilon $ proves lower semicontinuity.

Finally, a convex function $f$ with finite values on an open convex subset $U$ of a finite-dimensional real vector space is continuous. Indeed, let $Q\subset U$ be a small closed cube; by convexity the function is bounded above on $Q$ by the maximum $M$ of its values at the vertices. If $c$ is the center of $Q$ and $c\pm v\in Q$, then $f(c+v)\geq 2f(c)-f(c-v)\geq 2f(c)-M$, so $f$ is also bounded below on $Q$. If $y,z$ lie in the concentric cube of half the size, extend the segment from $z$ to $y$ beyond $y$ to the point $w$ at distance $s$ from $y$, where $s$ is half the side length of the smaller cube, so that $w\in Q$. The slope inequality for convex functions gives $f(y)-f(z)\leq |y-z|(f(w)-f(y))/|w-y|\leq |y-z|(M-\inf _ Qf)/s$, and by symmetry $f$ is Lipschitz on the smaller cube. Applied to the open convex cone $\mathcal B$, this proves continuity there. $\square$

Theorem 14.4.4. Let $\alpha \in \mathcal E$. Then the non-nef locus $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )$ is a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $X$, and it is empty if and only if $\alpha $ is nef. If $\alpha $ is big, then $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$ is the pole set of the potential of a single Kähler current in $\alpha $ with analytic singularities; it is a proper analytic subset of $X$, it contains $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )$, and it is empty if and only if $\alpha $ is Kähler. Finally, for every class $\alpha \in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$,

14.21
\begin{equation} E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )= \bigcap _{\varepsilon >0}E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} ). \end{equation}

Proof. The non-nef locus. By the monotonicity in $\varepsilon $, $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )$ is the union over positive integers $j,k$ of the sets $\{ x:\nu (V_{\theta +\omega /j},x)\geq 1/k\} $. Each of them is analytic by theorem 12.3.4, and proper, because it is contained in the pole set of $V_{\theta +\omega /j}$. If $\alpha $ is nef, then $\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ is Kähler for every $\varepsilon >0$, so $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }$ is bounded by corollary 14.3.6 and all these Lelong numbers vanish. Conversely, suppose that $\nu (V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega },x)=0$ for all $x\in X$ and $\varepsilon >0$. By corollary 12.3.2, $\mathcal I(mV_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega })$ is the unit ideal at every point, so the approximations $G_ m$ of lemma 13.4.1 for the background $\theta +\varepsilon \omega $ are smooth and satisfy $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m\geq -(\varepsilon +c/m)\omega $. Hence $\alpha $ is nef.

Realization of the non-Kähler locus. Let $\alpha $ be big. Kähler currents with analytic singularities exist in $\alpha $ by proposition 14.3.5, and their pole sets are closed analytic subsets of $X$. Since $X$ has a countable basis, countably many of the open complements of these pole sets already have the same union as all of them: for every element of a countable basis that is contained in one of these complements, choose one such complement. The intersection of the corresponding countably many pole sets $Z_1,Z_2,\ldots $ is therefore $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. Let $\mathcal I_{Z_ k}$ be the ideal sheaf of $Z_ k$, which is coherent by theorem A.2.8. The increasing sequence of coherent ideals $\mathcal I_{Z_1}+\cdots +\mathcal I_{Z_ k}$, whose zero sets are $Z_1\cap \cdots \cap Z_ k$, is stationary on the compact manifold $X$ by lemma 12.2.4. Hence $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=Z_1\cap \cdots \cap Z_ N$ for some $N$.

Let $T_ k=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ k$, $k\leq N$, be the corresponding Kähler currents, and choose $\delta >0$ with $T_ k\geq \delta \omega $ for all $k\leq N$. Then $\varphi =\max _ k\varphi _ k$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta -\delta \omega )$, it has analytic singularities by proposition 13.1.7, and its pole set is exactly $Z_1\cap \cdots \cap Z_ N=E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. Thus $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ is a Kähler current realizing $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$, which is a proper analytic subset because $\varphi $ is finite almost everywhere. If $\nu (\alpha ,x)>0$, then $\nu (\varphi ,x)\geq \nu (V_\theta ,x)=\nu (\alpha ,x)>0$ by minimality of $V_\theta $ and proposition 14.4.2, so $\varphi (x)=-\infty $; hence $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$.

If $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\varnothing $, the potential $\varphi $ just constructed has analytic singularities and no poles, so it is bounded, and proposition 14.3.5 shows that $\alpha $ is Kähler. Conversely, if $\alpha $ contains a Kähler form $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$, then $u$ is smooth and has analytic singularities without poles, so $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\varnothing $.

The identity (14.21). Suppose first that $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. Then $\alpha $ is big, and there is a Kähler current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq \delta \omega $ with analytic singularities and $\varphi (x)>-\infty $; in particular $\varphi $ is bounded near $x$ and $\nu (\varphi ,x)=0$. For $0<\varepsilon <\delta $, the current $\theta -\varepsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ is positive and lies in $\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $, which is therefore pseudo-effective, and the bound $\nu (\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} ,x)\leq \nu (\varphi ,x)=0$ shows that $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )$.

Conversely, suppose that $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )$ for some $\varepsilon >0$. By the convention in definition 14.4.1, the class $\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ is pseudo-effective. For $0<\eta <\varepsilon /2$ put $u=V_{\theta -(\varepsilon -\eta )\omega }$; then $\nu (u,x)\leq \nu (\alpha -\varepsilon \{ \omega \} ,x)=0$. By corollary 12.3.2, $\mathcal I(mu)$ is the unit ideal near $x$ for every $m$, and the approximation $G_ m$ of lemma 13.4.1 for $u$ and the background $\theta -(\varepsilon -\eta )\omega $ is smooth near $x$ and satisfies

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m\geq (\varepsilon -\eta -c/m)\omega . \]

For large $m$ this is a Kähler current in $\alpha $ with analytic singularities whose potential is finite at $x$. Hence $\alpha $ is big and $x\notin E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. This proves (14.21); the argument also shows that $\alpha $ is big as soon as the right-hand side of (14.21) is not all of $X$, so the formula is consistent with the convention $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=X$ for classes that are not big. $\square$

We now turn to modifications. Let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map from a Kähler manifold $Y$, with Kähler form $\omega _ Y$. By proposition 14.2.8, $Y$ is compact and connected, so the results above apply on $Y$. Pullback of smooth forms induces a linear map $\pi ^*\colon H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)\to H^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb R)$. Pushforward of currents, $(\pi _*S)(\chi )=S(\pi ^*\chi )$, maps closed real $(1,1)$-currents on $Y$ to closed real $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, preserves positivity, and satisfies $\pi _*\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\pi _*f$ for distributions $f$ (proposition 7.5.1); hence it induces a linear map $\pi _*\colon H^{1,1}(Y,\mathbb R)\to H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ with $\pi _*\{ S\} =\{ \pi _*S\} $. If $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ is a closed positive current on $X$ with $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the pullbacks $(\varphi +h)\circ \pi $ of its local potentials, where $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $, are not identically $-\infty $, because $\varphi \circ \pi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$ by proposition 14.2.8. Hence the pullback $\pi ^*T$ of proposition 7.5.4 is defined, and

\[ \pi ^*T=\pi ^*\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi \circ \pi ),\qquad \{ \pi ^*T\} =\pi ^*\{ T\} . \]

For a prime divisor $E\subset Y$, the image $\pi (E)$ is an analytic subset of $X$ by Remmert's theorem (theorem A.6.2), and it is irreducible: if $\pi (E)=A_1\cup A_2$ with analytic subsets $A_1,A_2$, then $E=(E\cap \pi ^{-1}(A_1))\cup (E\cap \pi ^{-1}(A_2))$, so $E\subset \pi ^{-1}(A_ i)$ for some $i$ and $\pi (E)=A_ i$. The divisor $E$ is exceptional for $\pi $ if $\pi (E)$ has codimension at least two in $X$.

Fix an analytic subset $A\subset X$ of codimension at least two outside which $\pi $ is an isomorphism (proposition A.7.3). We show that a prime divisor $E$ of $Y$ is exceptional if and only if $E\subset \pi ^{-1}(A)$. If $E\subset \pi ^{-1}(A)$, then $\pi (E)\subset A$ and $E$ is exceptional. Otherwise $E\cap \pi ^{-1}(A)$ is an analytic subset of $E$ different from $E$; it contains no nonempty open subset of the connected manifold $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, by the identity theorem, so it is nowhere dense in $E$. Then $\pi $ maps the dense open subset $E\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$ of $E$, a complex hypersurface of $Y\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$, isomorphically onto $\pi (E)\setminus A$, which is therefore a complex hypersurface of $X\setminus A$ and is dense in $\pi (E)$, because $\pi (E)$ is the image of the closure of $E\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$ under the closed map $\pi $. Hence $\pi (E)$ is an irreducible analytic subset of codimension one, that is, a prime divisor $D$, and $E$ is not exceptional; this proves the claim. Moreover, $E$ is the strict transform of $D$, since the closure of $\pi ^{-1}(D\setminus A)=E\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$ is $E$. By the claim and (14.20), applied on $Y$, every exceptional prime divisor is an irreducible component of $\pi ^{-1}(A)$, while every other prime divisor $E$ of $Y$ is the strict transform of the prime divisor $\pi (E)$ of $X$, and $\pi $ is an isomorphism near the points of $E$ outside $\pi ^{-1}(A)$.

Proposition 14.4.5. Let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be as above. Then $\pi ^*$ maps nef classes to nef classes, and $\pi _*$ maps modified nef classes to modified nef classes. The pushforward of a non-divisorial closed positive current on $Y$ is non-divisorial.

Proof. By lemma 13.1.1 on the compact manifold $Y$, $\pi ^*\omega \leq C\omega _ Y$ for a constant $C$. If $\theta _\varepsilon \geq -\varepsilon \omega $ are nef representatives of a class on $X$, then $\pi ^*\theta _\varepsilon \geq -C\varepsilon \omega _ Y$, so the pullback class is nef. Let $S$ be a non-divisorial closed positive current on $Y$ and $D\subset X$ a prime divisor, with strict transform $D'$. Over $X\setminus A$ the map $\pi $ is a biholomorphism, which identifies $\pi _*S$ with $S$ and $D\setminus A$ with $D'\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$, and Lelong numbers are invariant under biholomorphisms (proposition 12.1.1). The generic value of the Lelong numbers of $S$ along $D'$ is attained on a dense subset of $D'$ (proposition 12.3.5), hence at some point $y$ of the nonempty open subset $D'\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$. Therefore $\nu (\pi _*S,D)\leq \nu (\pi _*S,\pi (y))=\nu (S,y)=\nu (S,D')=0$. Hence $\pi _*S$ is non-divisorial.

Let $\beta $ be a modified nef class on $Y$ and $t>0$. The class $\beta +t\{ \omega _ Y\} $ is big, and it is modified nef because $\mathcal{MN}$ is a convex cone containing the Kähler classes. By proposition 14.4.2, a positive current with minimal singularities in this class is non-divisorial. Its pushforward is a non-divisorial positive current in $\pi _*\beta +t\pi _*\{ \omega _ Y\} $, so this class is modified nef: the definition is satisfied with $T_\varepsilon $ equal to this current for every $\varepsilon $. These classes tend to $\pi _*\beta $ as $t\downarrow 0$, and $\mathcal{MN}$ is closed, so $\pi _*\beta $ is modified nef. $\square$

Lemma 14.4.6. Let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be as above, and let $S$ be a non-divisorial closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $Y$. Then

\[ \pi ^*\pi _*S=S+\sum _ E c_ E[E],\qquad c_ E\geq 0, \]

where $E$ ranges over the finitely many exceptional prime divisors of $\pi $. For every closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$, $\pi _*\pi ^*T=T$. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, $\pi _*[E]=0$ if $E$ is exceptional, and $\pi _*[E]=[\pi (E)]$ otherwise.

Proof. Let $A$ be as above. The positive current $\pi ^*\pi _*S$, defined through local potentials as above, agrees with $S$ on $Y\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$. Their difference is a closed real current with measure coefficients supported on the proper analytic subset $\pi ^{-1}(A)$ of $Y$. By theorem A.2.17, it is a finite real linear combination of the currents of integration along the irreducible components of codimension one of the compact analytic set $\pi ^{-1}(A)$, which are exactly the exceptional prime divisors, as shown above; there are finitely many of them by corollary A.2.13.

It remains to show that the coefficients $c_ E$ are nonnegative. Near a regular point of an exceptional prime divisor $E$ that lies on no other component, the difference is $c_ E[E]=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(c_ E\log |s_ E|^2)$, where $s_ E$ is a local equation of $E$ (lemma A.2.16), so local potentials $u'$ of $\pi ^*\pi _*S$ and $u$ of $S$ satisfy $u'=u+c_ E\log |s_ E|^2+p$ with $p$ pluriharmonic. The additivity of Lelong numbers (proposition 12.1.4), applied to this identity if $c_ E\geq 0$ and to $u=u'+|c_ E|\log |s_ E|^2-p$ otherwise, together with $\nu (\log |s_ E|^2,y)=1$, gives $\nu (u',y)=\nu (u,y)+c_ E$ at such points $y$. At points that are moreover generic for both currents, which exist because the points of $E$ excluded by these conditions form a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $E$ (proposition 12.3.5), this reads $\nu (\pi ^*\pi _*S,E)=\nu (S,E)+c_ E$; hence $c_ E=\nu (\pi ^*\pi _*S,E)\geq 0$, since $S$ is non-divisorial.

Since $\pi $ is a biholomorphism over $X\setminus A$, the positive current $\pi _*\pi ^*T$ agrees with $T$ on $X\setminus A$. Hence $\pi _*\pi ^*T-T$ is a closed real current with measure coefficients (theorem 7.2.5) supported on $A$, which has codimension at least two, so it vanishes by theorem A.2.17. For a prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the current $\pi _*[E]$ is positive, closed and supported on the analytic set $\pi (E)$. If $E$ is exceptional, $\pi (E)$ has codimension at least two and $\pi _*[E]=0$ by the same theorem. Otherwise $\pi $ maps $E\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$ isomorphically onto $\pi (E)\setminus A$, so $\pi _*[E]=[\pi (E)]$ on $X\setminus A$, and the closed current $\pi _*[E]-[\pi (E)]$ of order zero is supported on $\pi (E)\cap A$, of codimension at least two in $X$; it vanishes by the same theorem. $\square$

Proposition 14.4.7. Let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be as above. Then $\pi ^*$ and $\pi _*$ map big classes to big classes.

Proof. Choose $C$ with $\pi ^*\omega \leq C\omega _ Y$. Since $\pi _*$ preserves positivity, lemma 14.4.6 gives $\pi _*\omega _ Y\geq C^{-1}\pi _*\pi ^*\omega =C^{-1}\omega $. Hence a current $S\geq \delta \omega _ Y$ on $Y$ has $\pi _*S\geq \delta C^{-1}\omega $, which proves the assertion for $\pi _*$.

Let $\alpha $ be big on $X$ and $T\in \alpha $ with $T\geq \delta \omega $. Choose a smooth closed representative $\eta $ of the class $\{ \pi _*\omega _ Y\} $, a constant $B>0$ with $\eta \leq B\omega $ (lemma 13.1.1), and $t>0$ with $tB\leq \delta $, so that $T-t\eta \geq 0$. The positive current $T-t\eta +t\pi _*\omega _ Y$ lies in $\alpha $. By lemma 14.4.6, applied to the smooth, hence non-divisorial, current $\omega _ Y$, its pullback is

\[ \pi ^*(T-t\eta )+t\omega _ Y+t\sum _ E c_ E[E]\geq t\omega _ Y. \]

This is a Kähler current in $\pi ^*\alpha $, so $\pi ^*\alpha $ is big. $\square$

Example 14.4.8. The zero class $\alpha =0$ is nef, so $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(0)=\varnothing $ by theorem 14.4.4. It is not big: a Kähler current $T\geq \delta \omega $ would have $\int _ XT\wedge \omega ^{n-1}\geq \delta \int _ X\omega ^ n>0$, whereas this integral vanishes for every closed current in the zero class. Hence $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(0)=X$. More generally, $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=X$ for every class that is not big, while $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )=\varnothing $ for every nef class; on the boundary of the pseudo-effective cone the two loci therefore detect different failures of positivity. This is also why the minimal multiplicities are defined with the allowances $\varepsilon \omega $ rather than through $V_\theta $: for a class that is not big, proposition 14.4.2 gives only the inequality $\nu (\alpha ,x)\leq \nu (V_\theta ,x)$, since the proof of equality uses a Kähler current in $\alpha $.
