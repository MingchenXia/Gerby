12.11 Locality and maxima of bounded potentials927D22F5110F

Where one bounded admissible potential is strictly larger than another, the maximum has the same positive Hessian current as the larger potential. These strict comparison sets need not be open. Quasicontinuity gives cutoffs whose products with the approximating currents converge, allowing smooth locality to pass to these sets.

Proposition 12.11.1. Let $u,v$ be bounded admissible, put $w=\max \{ u,v\} $, and fix $1\leq p\leq m$. For any bounded admissible $t_2,\ldots ,t_ p$,

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u>v\} }Q_ p(w,t_2,\ldots ,t_ p) =\mathbf1_{\{ u>v\} }Q_ p(u,t_2,\ldots ,t_ p). \]

The equality is coefficientwise as currents with measure coefficients. The corresponding equality on $\{ v>u\} $ follows by exchanging $u,v$.

Proof. Choose smooth uniformly bounded decreasing approximations of all the potentials by 12.5.5. Construct $w_ j$ by 12.1.7. Work on one compact coordinate test set. Fix $\delta >0$ and a smooth $\chi _\delta :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$ that vanishes for $s\leq \delta /2$ and equals one for $s\geq \delta $. For $j$ large enough that $\epsilon _ j<\delta /2$, ordinary smooth locality gives

\[ \chi _\delta (u_ j-v_ j)Q_ p(w_ j,t_2^ j,\ldots ,t_ p^ j) =\chi _\delta (u_ j-v_ j)Q_ p(u_ j,t_2^ j,\ldots ,t_ p^ j). \]

Indeed, wherever the weight is nonzero, $w_ j=u_ j$ on an open neighborhood. The equality elsewhere follows from its zero weight.

Both sequences of positive currents converge by 12.10.8; every coefficient variation is uniformly dominated by $C c_ m^*$ by 12.10.9. The potentials and their limits are quasicontinuous by 12.10.6. Apply 12.10.7 with $s=m$ and $F(x,y)=\chi _\delta (x-y)$ to the real and imaginary parts of each coefficient. Passing to the limit gives the same identity with $u,v,w,t_2,\ldots ,t_ p$. Choose the cutoffs so that $\chi _\delta (s)\to \mathbf1_{\{ s>0\} }$ pointwise as $\delta \downarrow 0$. Dominated convergence against the fixed finite coefficient variations on the test set gives the asserted restriction. A partition of unity proves it globally. $\square$

On the contact set, a positive constant shift gives a strict comparison before the limit is taken. Decreasing the shift then recovers that set by dominated convergence for a fixed measure.

Theorem 12.11.2. Let $u,v$ be bounded admissible. Fix $1\leq r\leq m$ and bounded admissible $z_1,\ldots ,z_{m-r}$. Define the positive Radon measure

\[ \mu _ r(t)=Q_ m(\underbrace{t,\ldots ,t}_{r},z_1,\ldots ,z_{m-r}). \]

For $w=\max \{ u,v\} $,

\[ \mu _ r(w)\geq \mathbf1_{\{ u\geq v\} }\mu _ r(u) +\mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\mu _ r(v). \]

The inequality with $u,v$ exchanged also holds.

Proof. Symmetry and 12.11.1, applied to the $r$ slots, give $\mu _ r(w)=\mu _ r(v)$ on $\{ u<v\} $ and $\mu _ r(w)=\mu _ r(u)$ on $\{ u>v\} $. To include the contact set, put $w_\epsilon =\max \{ u+\epsilon ,v\} $. Strict locality and invariance under addition of constants give

\[ \mu _ r(w_\epsilon )\geq \mathbf1_{\{ u+\epsilon >v\} }\mu _ r(u). \]

The invariance follows from the smooth products and their joint decreasing limits. As $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, $w_\epsilon \downarrow w$, so the left side converges weakly to $\mu _ r(w)$. Against any nonnegative continuous compactly supported test function, the right side converges by dominated convergence for the fixed measure $\mu _ r(u)$ to $\mathbf1_{\{ u\geq v\} }\mu _ r(u)$. This proves the measure inequality on the contact set as well as on $\{ u>v\} $. On its disjoint complement $\{ u<v\} $, the already proved equality supplies $\mu _ r(v)$. Restricting the positive measures to these two Borel sets and adding gives the assertion. Exchanging $u,v$ gives its other form. $\square$

Convex rescaling also supplies a positive background term in the measure.

Lemma 12.11.3. For bounded admissible $v$, $0\leq \alpha \leq 1$ and a real constant $b$, the function $v_\alpha =\alpha v+b$ is bounded admissible and

\[ H_ m(v_\alpha )\geq \alpha ^ m H_ m(v)+(1-\alpha )^ m\omega ^ n. \]

Proof. Take smooth decreasing uniformly bounded admissible $v_ j\downarrow v$. Cone convexity makes $\alpha v_ j+b$ admissible because

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha v_ j+b) =\alpha (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)+(1-\alpha )\omega . \]

For $\alpha >0$, an upper test $q$ for $\alpha v+b$ gives the upper test $(q-b)/\alpha $ for $v$. The same convex combination of forms therefore proves admissibility of the limit. For $\alpha =0$ the potential is constant. The pointwise decreasing limit has the displayed canonical affine values. Expand the smooth top product. Every mixed term is positive by polarized Gårding positivity. Keeping the two extreme terms gives

\[ H_ m(\alpha v_ j+b)\geq \alpha ^ m H_ m(v_ j) +(1-\alpha )^ m\omega ^ n. \]

Joint decreasing convergence from 12.10.8 passes this inequality against each nonnegative continuous test to the asserted inequality of positive Radon measures. $\square$

The weighted limits also pass integration by parts against metric derivatives. A truncation just above the infimum then excludes zero Hessian mass for a bounded potential.

Lemma 12.11.4. Put $k=m-1$ and $\ell =n-m$. For bounded admissible $t_1,\ldots ,t_ k$, let $T=R_ k(t_1,\ldots ,t_ k)\wedge \omega ^\ell $. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T$ is a signed Radon measure. If bounded admissible $t_ i^ j\downarrow t_ i$ have a common bound, these measures converge weakly, also after multiplication by any continuous function of finitely many uniformly bounded decreasing admissible potentials.

Proof. The subset definition and the torsion formulas in 12.8.4 give, in distributions,

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T={}& \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \left(\sum _{a=1}^ kR_{k-1}^{\widehat a}\omega ^\ell +\ell R_ k\omega ^{\ell -1}\right)\\ & +2\, \mathrm d\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \sum _{a<b}R_{k-2}^{\widehat a\widehat b}\omega ^\ell \\ & +2\ell \, \mathrm d\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \sum _{a=1}^ kR_{k-1}^{\widehat a}\omega ^{\ell -1}\\ & +\ell (\ell -1)\, \mathrm d\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge R_ k\omega ^{\ell -2}. \end{aligned} \]

The sum with two hats is absent when $k=1$, and the last term is absent when $\ell =1$. Every factor $R_ r$ has degree $r\leq k<m$, so its coefficients are signed measures. Expanding each $R_ r$ over subsets expresses the displayed measure as fixed smooth forms wedged with raw currents $D_ p$ of degrees $p\leq m-1$. 12.10.6 therefore bounds its variation locally by $C c_{m-1}^*$, uniformly under common bounds on the potentials. Joint convergence follows from 12.10.8 applied to this expansion. All the canonical weights are quasicontinuous for $c_{m-1}^*$. 12.10.7, with $s=m-1$, thus proves the weighted assertion coefficientwise on a finite coordinate cover. A partition of unity gives it on $X$. The term involving $R_ k\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^\ell )$ retains degree $m-1$; the argument requires no coefficient estimate in degree $m$. $\square$

Proposition 12.11.5. Every bounded admissible potential $u$ has

\[ \int _ X H_ m(u)>0. \]

No differential identities for powers of $\omega $ are required.

Proof. Suppose instead that $H_ m(u)=0$. Fix $s>0$, set $c=\inf _ Xu+s$, and put $w=\max \{ u,c\} $. The upper semicontinuity of $u$ makes $U=\{ u<c\} $ open, and the definition of the infimum makes it nonempty. Moreover $0\leq w-u\leq s$ and this difference is zero outside $U$. The maximum is bounded admissible with its canonical pointwise values by 12.1.7.

For $0\leq q\leq m$, define the positive Radon measures

\[ \mu _ q=Q_ m(\underbrace{u,\ldots ,u}_{q}, \underbrace{w,\ldots ,w}_{m-q}). \]

Thus $\mu _ m=H_ m(u)=0$. For $q<m$, let $T_ q$ be the sum of $m-q$ currents with $q+r$ slots equal to $u$ and $m-q-1-r$ slots equal to $w$, for $0\leq r<m-q$, each of degree $m-1$ and wedged with $\omega ^{n-m}$. In smooth notation this is

\[ T_ q=\sum _{r=0}^{m-q-1} (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{q+r} (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^{m-q-1-r}\omega ^{n-m}. \]

Here each displayed product denotes the symmetric shifted current $R_{m-1}$ of (12.8), wedged with $\omega ^{n-m}$.

Choose smooth bounded admissible $u_ j\downarrow u$ and the regularized maxima $w_ j=M_{\epsilon _ j}(u_ j,c)\downarrow w$, where $\epsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, as in 12.1.7. Both lists have a common bound. The smooth polynomial identity and Stokes' theorem on the compact manifold give

\[ \int _ X(\mu _ q^ j-\mu _ m^ j) =\int _ X(w_ j-u_ j)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ q^ j. \]

Joint decreasing convergence passes the left side to its limit. The preceding lemma, with $F(x,y)=y-x$ and the decreasing lists $u_ j,w_ j$, passes the right side to its limit. Hence

\[ \int _ X\mu _ q=\int _ X(w-u)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ q. \]

The difference of the two decreasing sequences need not itself decrease; the continuous two-variable weight is essential here.

On the open set $U$, the potential $w$ is constant. Locality of the raw recursion therefore sets every factor $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ to zero there, so that its shifted factors become $\omega $. The formula in the preceding lemma expresses $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ q|_ U$ as finitely many smooth metric-derivative forms wedged with powers of $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ of degrees at most $m-1$. Use smooth admissible approximations to $u$ and the constant $c$ in these local expressions; their limits are the same restricted currents. For those smooth approximations, the absolute mixed-form estimate 12.1.5 bounds each such term by a constant times the corresponding positive trace, and therefore by $C\sum _{r=0}^{m-1}\mu _ r$ on $U$ after taking joint limits. More explicitly, a term with $r$ factors is bounded by $C(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ r\omega ^{n-r}$; this is $C\mu _ r$ on $U$. Against compactly supported nonnegative tests in $U$, weak convergence and lower semicontinuity of total variation preserve the estimate. Exhaustion gives the measure inequality

\[ \mathbf1_ U|\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ q| \leq C\sum _{r=0}^{m-1}\mathbf1_ U\mu _ r. \]

Here $C$ depends only on $\omega ,m,n$, not on $u$ or $s$; a fixed finite coordinate cover bounds all the smooth metric coefficients.

Since $w-u$ vanishes outside $U$, positivity gives

\[ \int _ U\mu _ q\leq \int _ X\mu _ q \leq Cs\sum _{r=0}^{m-1}\int _ U\mu _ r \qquad (0\leq q<m). \]

Summing these $m$ inequalities and choosing $s>0$ with $mCs<1$ forces $\sum _{r=0}^{m-1}\int _ U\mu _ r=0$. But locality gives $\mu _0=\omega ^ n$ on the nonempty open set $U$, whose volume is positive. This contradiction proves the result. $\square$
