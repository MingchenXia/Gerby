Proposition 10.2.7

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Proposition 10.2.7

Proposition 10.2.7. Let $T$, $S$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, $\lambda \in \mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}$. Then

  1. if $T\preceq _{\mathcal{I}} S$, we have

    10.8
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:nuTS} \nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)\geq _{\mathrm{lex}}\nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(S). \end{equation}

  2. We have the following additivity property:

    10.9
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:nuvaluationlinear} \nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(T+S)=\nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)+\nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(S),\quad \nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(\lambda T)=\lambda \nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(T). \end{equation}

Proof. 1 We make an induction on $n\geq 0$. The case $n=0,1$ is trivial. Assume that $n\geq 2$ and the case $n-1$ is known. Observe that $\nu (T,Y_1)\geq \nu (S,Y_1)$, if the inequality is strict, we are done. So let us assume that $\nu (T,Y_1)=\nu (S,Y_1)$. By proposition 8.2.1, we find that

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_{Y_1}\Bigl (T-\nu (T,Y_1)[Y_1]\Bigr )\preceq _ P\operatorname{Tr}_{Y_1}\Bigl (S-\nu (T,Y_1)[Y_1]\Bigr ). \]

Hence the same comparison holds for $\preceq _{\mathcal{I}}$ by corollary 6.1.9. By the inductive hypothesis, we conclude (10.8).

2 We make an induction on $n\geq 0$. The cases $n=0,1$ are trivial. Assume that $n\geq 2$ and the case $n-1$ is known. By proposition 1.4.5, we have

\[ \nu (T+S,Y_1)=\nu (T,Y_1)+\nu (S,Y_1),\quad \nu (\lambda T,Y_1)=\lambda \nu (T,Y_1). \]

By proposition 8.2.1, we have

\[ \begin{split} \operatorname{Tr}_{Y_1}\Bigl (T+S-\nu (T+S,Y_1)[Y_1]\Bigr )\sim _ P& \operatorname{Tr}_{Y_1}\Bigl (T-\nu (T,Y_1)[Y_1]\Bigr )\\ & +\operatorname{Tr}_{Y_1}\Bigl (S-\nu (S,Y_1)[Y_1]\Bigr ),\\ \operatorname{Tr}_{Y_1}\Bigl (\lambda T-\nu (\lambda T,Y_1)[Y_1]\Bigr )\sim _ P& \lambda \operatorname{Tr}_{Y_1}\Bigl (T-\nu (T,Y_1)[Y_1]\Bigr ). \end{split} \]

Since $\sim _ P$ implies $\sim _{\mathcal{I}}$ by corollary 6.1.9, the inductive hypothesis and the independence of the valuation from the trace representative conclude (10.9). $\square$
