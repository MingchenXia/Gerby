1.8 Hermitian capacityDD310AD8B258

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.8 Hermitian capacity

After a fixed rescaling, a local capacity test can be glued to a constant function and so becomes a global potential on $X$. This compares the local Bedford–Taylor capacity with a capacity defined by global potentials, and it transfers to $X$ both the volume–capacity estimate and an estimate for the sublevel sets of normalized potentials.

For a Borel set $E\subset X$, the Hermitian capacity of $E$ is

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E) =\sup _{v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega ),\ 0\leq v\leq 1} \int _ E(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n, \]

where the products are those of proposition 1.7.1. Their local expansion and (1.4) show that $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (X)<+\infty $. On relatively compact coordinate balls, a fixed smooth correction bounds the measures of global tests by a constant times the corresponding local capacity. Conversely, the gluing construction in the proof of lemma 1.8.1 below bounds local capacity by global capacity. Consequently, the quasi-continuity of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions and the characterization of sets of capacity zero recalled in section 1.6 hold for $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. A sequence $u_ j$ converges to $u$ in capacity if

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ |u_ j-u|>\delta \} )\longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{for every }\delta >0, \]

where pole sets may be omitted. To compare with another Hermitian form $\omega '$, choose $C\geq 1$ with $\omega '\leq C\omega $. Every test $0\leq v\leq 1$ for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\omega '}$ gives a test $v/C$ for $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $, and the positivity of mixed products gives

\[ (\omega '+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\leq C^ n(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v/C))^ n. \]

Thus $\operatorname {Cap}_{\omega '}\leq C^ n\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. Interchanging the two forms shows that the capacities are equivalent up to fixed multiplicative constants, so convergence in capacity does not depend on the choice of $\omega $. Finally, the continuity statements for bounded products in section 1.6 pass to the products with smooth backgrounds, by their local expansion and a finite cover.
