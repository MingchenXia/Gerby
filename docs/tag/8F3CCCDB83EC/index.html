From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Theorem 12.2.9

Theorem 12.2.9. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}_{\mathrm{tor}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

12.29
\begin{equation} \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\Bigr )=\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi ). \end{equation}

In particular,

12.30
\begin{equation} n!\operatorname{vol}\Delta (\theta ,\varphi )=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \end{equation}

Proof. We first reduce to the case where $D|_{U_{\sigma }}=0$. In fact, changing $D$ to $D+\operatorname{div}\chi ^{m_{\sigma }}$ would result in changing $F_{\theta }$ to $F_{\theta }-m_{\sigma }$. Hence, $F_{\varphi }$ changes to $F_{\varphi }-m_{\sigma }$. Therefore, $\Delta (\theta ,\varphi )$ becomes $\Delta (\theta ,\varphi )-m_{\sigma }$. Taking (12.5) into consideration, we may assume that $m_{\sigma }=0$.

Step 1. We first reduce to the case where $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current.

By 2.4.19, we can find $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\psi \leq \varphi $ and $\theta _{\psi }$ is a Kähler current. Taking the average along $T_ c$, we may assume that $\psi $ is $T_ c$-invariant.

For each $t\in (0,1)$, we let

\[ \varphi _ t=(1-t)\psi +t\varphi . \]

Suppose that Kähler current case is known. Then we get

\[ \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi _ t)\Bigr )=\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi _ t) \]

for any $t\in (0,1)$. It follows from theorem A.4.5 that

\[ \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\Bigr )\supseteq \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi _ t)\Bigr )= \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi _ t) \]

for any $t\in (0,1)$. Thanks to theorem 10.3.18, we have

\[ \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\Bigr )\supseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi ). \]

Comparing the volumes of both sides using proposition 12.2.4, (10.40), and the $\mathcal{I}$-goodness of toric singularities example 7.4.5, we find that

\[ n!\operatorname{vol}\Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\Bigr )=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }=n!\operatorname{vol}\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi ). \]

In particular, we conclude (12.29).

Step 2. We handle the case where $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current.

Let $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ be the quasi-equisingular approximation given by 12.2.8.

Now assume that the result is known for each $\varphi _ j$. Then

\[ \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi _ j)\Bigr )=\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi _ j). \]

In particular, thanks to proposition 10.3.17,

\[ \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\Bigr )\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi _ j) \]

for each $j\geq 1$. It follows from theorem 10.3.18 that

\[ \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl ( \Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\Bigr )\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi ). \]

Comparing the volumes of both sides using proposition 12.2.4, (10.40), and example 7.4.5, we conclude (12.29).

Step 3. It remains to prove the assertion for the analytic approximants appearing in Step 2.

By 12.2.8, these approximants may be chosen in the form

\[ \varphi =\frac{1}{q}\log \sum _{i=1}^ a |s_ i|_{h^ q}^2+\mathcal O(1), \]

where $q\in \mathbb Z_{>0}$ and $s_1,\ldots ,s_ a\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ q)$ are toric eigen-sections.

Let $m_1,\ldots ,m_ a\in qP_ D\cap M$ be the lattice points corresponding to $s_1,\ldots ,s_ a$.

Observe that

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta (\theta ,\varphi ) =& \overline{\nabla F_{\varphi }(N_{\mathbb {R}})}\\ =& \left\{ m\in M_{\mathbb {R}} : F_{\varphi }(n)-\langle m,n \rangle \textup{ is bounded from below} \right\} \\ =& \left\{ m\in M_{\mathbb {R}}: q^{-1}\log \sum _{i=1}^ a \mathrm{e}^{\langle m_ i,n\rangle }-\langle m,n\rangle \textup{ is bounded below in } n \right\} \\ =& q^{-1}\mathrm{Conv}\{ m_1,\ldots ,m_ a\} , \end{aligned} \]

where we have applied (12.22) on the second line and A.5.2 on the third line. Since $m_1,\ldots ,m_ a\in qP_ D$, the preceding formula also shows that

\[ \Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\subseteq P_ D. \]

Thus, if $m\in k\Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\cap M$, then $m\in kP_ D\cap M$, and $\chi ^ m$ indeed determines a global section of $L^ k$.

In particular, by the same separation argument as in A.5.1, for any $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ and any $m\in k\Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\cap M$, we have

\[ |\chi ^ m|^2 \mathrm{e}^{-k\varphi } \]

is bounded from above on $T(\mathbb {C})$. In other words, the section $s$ of $L^ k$ corresponding to $m$ satisfies

\[ s\in \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}_{\infty }(k\varphi )\right). \]

Therefore,

\[ \nu _{Y_{\bullet }}(s)=\Phi (m)\in k\Delta _ k(\theta ,\varphi ), \]

where $\Delta _ k$ is defined in section 10.3. Hence,

\[ \Phi \Bigl (k\Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\cap M\Bigr )\subseteq k\Delta _ k(\theta ,\varphi ). \]

Letting $k\to \infty $ and applying theorem 10.3.24, we find that

\[ \Phi _{\mathbb {R}}\Bigl (\Delta (\theta ,\varphi )\Bigr ) \subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\varphi ). \]

Comparing the volumes of both sides using proposition 12.2.4 and (10.40), we conclude that the equality holds and (12.29) follows. $\square$
