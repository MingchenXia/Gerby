11.4 Bounded uniqueness and connectednessE0DB623401C5

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 11.4 Bounded uniqueness and connectedness

For a continuous density whose positive set is connected and dense, bounded solutions are unique up to a constant. An energy measure of $w=u-v$ recovers the information supplied by differentiability. Its pushforward under $w$ has a Lipschitz density vanishing at the extreme values. If $w$ were nonconstant, these properties would force both extremal sets to have zero mass for the common Monge–Ampère measure. A smooth test potential with a larger density near one of these sets then contradicts modified comparison.

Theorem 11.4.1. Let $F\in C^0(X)$ satisfy $F\geq 0$, $F\not\equiv 0$, with $U=\{ F>0\} $ connected and dense. If bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$ satisfy

\[ \omega _ u^ n=\omega _ v^ n=F\omega ^ n, \]

then $u-v$ is constant. In particular, $\sup _ Xu=\sup _ Xv$ implies $u=v$.

Continuity of $F$ supplies the local positive lower bounds and the neighborhoods of the extremal set used below. The auxiliary test densities are chosen smooth independently of $F$. Bounded solutions are continuous by corollary 10.4.3, so we may use pointwise extrema throughout the proof. We shall also use modified comparison in the following form. If $b$ is continuous and $\omega _ b\geq \varepsilon \omega $ for some $0<\varepsilon <1$, then $b/(1-\varepsilon )$ is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, so theorem 10.2.1 applies with $(1-\varepsilon )v=b$. Thus its thin-sublevel estimate applies directly to such a strictly positive comparison potential $b$, and the correction factor tends to one as the height of the sublevel set tends to zero, with $\varepsilon $ fixed.

Proposition 11.4.2. Let $u,v$ be continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions with $\omega _ u^ n=\omega _ v^ n=F\omega ^ n$, where $F\geq 0$ is bounded and measurable. Let $q$ be a smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function, and write $\omega _ q^ n=G\omega ^ n$. Then for every $0<\kappa <1$ and $\eta >0$,

11.26
\begin{equation} F\omega ^ n\bigl (\{ u-v<\min _ X(u-v)+\eta \} \cap \{ F>\kappa G\} \bigr )>0. \end{equation}

The analogous assertion holds for the set $\{ u-v>\max _ X(u-v)-\eta \} $.

Proof. Choose $\delta \in (0,1]$ with $\omega _ q\geq \delta \omega $. For $0<t<1$, put $b_ t=(1-t)v+tq$, $m_ t=\min _ X(u-b_ t)$, and $E_ s=\{ u<b_ t+m_ t+s\} $. Then $\omega _{b_ t}\geq t\delta \omega $. Write $\mu =F\omega ^ n$, $d=\kappa ^{-1/n}-1$, and $\gamma =nd$. By (1.23),

\[ \omega _{b_ t}^ n\geq \bigl ((1-t)F^{1/n}+tG^{1/n}\bigr )^ n \omega ^ n. \]

On $L=\{ F\leq \kappa G\} $ this is at least $(1+\gamma t)\mu $, since $G^{1/n}\geq (1+d)F^{1/n}$ there, and on $R=\{ F>\kappa G\} $ it is at least $(1-nt)\mu $. Choose $s>0$ so small that the reverse comparison factor in (10.2), with $\varepsilon =t\delta $, is at most $1+\gamma t/2$. Modified comparison on $E_ s$, with $x=\mu (E_ s\cap L)$ and $y=\mu (E_ s\cap R)$, gives

\[ (1+\gamma t)x+(1-nt)y\leq (1+\gamma t/2)(x+y), \qquad \text{and hence}\qquad y\geq \frac{1-\kappa ^{1/n}}2\, \mu (E_ s). \]

The open set $E_ s$ is nonempty by the definition of $m_ t$. As $\omega _{b_ t}^ n\geq (t\delta )^ n\omega ^ n$, (10.2) forces $\mu (E_ s)>0$, hence $y>0$.

Let $a=\min _ X(u-v)$ and $\Lambda =\| v-q\| _\infty $. Since $u-b_ t=u-v+t(v-q)$, we have $|m_ t-a|\leq t\Lambda $ and $E_ s\subset \{ u-v<a+2t\Lambda +s\} $. Given $\eta $, choose $t$ and then $s$ with $2t\Lambda +s<\eta $. Since $y=\mu (E_ s\cap R)>0$, this proves (11.26). Interchanging $u$ and $v$ gives the assertion for the near-maximum set. $\square$
