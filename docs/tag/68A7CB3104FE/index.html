33.3.8 Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds68A7CB3104FE

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 33.3.8 Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds

Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds are not Kähler, yet they carry a closed semipositive form $\theta $ whose potentials are all continuous (theorem 28.19.2). On a threefold of type $(2,1)$, the natural Gauduchon metric $\omega =\theta +y_1y_2\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ has an exactly quadratic mass functional, indefinite in sign, and its masses on fiber-invariant potentials are bounded above and below by positive constants (proposition 28.19.5). The known curvature criteria for finite upper and positive lower mass in dimension three require a Hermitian metric whose $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ has a sign, and these threefolds admit no such metric (proposition 28.19.6; see section 34.16 for the criteria). By (28.71), the remaining difficulty lies entirely in the fluctuation of a potential around its fiber average.

Problem 33.3.16. Let $X$ be an Oeljeklaus–Toma threefold of type $(2,1)$, with the Hermitian form $\omega $ of proposition 28.19.5. Does $X$ have the bounded mass property, that is, is $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $? Is the lower mass $v_-(\omega )$ positive? Equivalently, writing $F(u)$ for the fluctuation term in (28.71), is $F$ bounded above on smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u$, and is $\inf _ u\bigl (\mathrm M_\omega (Au)+F(u)\bigr )>0$? The same questions can be asked for Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds of any type $(s,t)$ with $s\geq 2$.

By proposition 6.5.2, the answer to the first question does not depend on the choice of Hermitian form. A negative answer would require potentials whose fluctuations make $\operatorname {Re}(\partial _{w_1}v\, \overline{\partial _{w_2}v})$ large, while keeping $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$. By lemma 28.19.1, every nonzero Fourier mode on the fiber tori depends on $z$, so for a single mode $c(y)\cos (\cdots )$ the positivity of the $z$-coefficient of $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ bounds $|c|$ by a multiple of $y_1y_2$ divided by the square of the $z$-frequency; the frequencies in the $x$-directions are not controlled pointwise. Whether compactness and the automorphy under the units bound the fluctuation term is the content of the problem. Unlike the Hopf threefold of chapter 8, the manifold contains no curves along which heat potentials could be concentrated, by the theorem of Ornea and Verbitsky recalled before corollary 28.19.4.

The rigidity in theorem 28.19.2 comes from two features of the null foliation of $\theta $: its leaves carry no nonconstant plurisubharmonic functions bounded above, and their closures are real tori on which translations act transitively. For the pullback of a Kähler form under a holomorphic fibration, the null leaves are the compact fibers, and $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions may have poles.

Problem 33.3.17. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed semipositive real $(1,1)$-form on a compact complex manifold $X$ whose kernel has constant rank. Suppose that every leaf of the null foliation of $\theta $ is Liouville, in the sense that plurisubharmonic functions bounded above on it are constant, and that the closure of every leaf is a compact real submanifold whose real codimension equals the complex rank of $\theta $. Is every $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function continuous? If not, describe the possible singularity types in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in terms of the closures of the leaves.
