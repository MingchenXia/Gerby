Mixed volumes

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 7.3 Mixed volumes

We first extend the notion of volume in definition 3.2.9 to the mixed case. Let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ be smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ representing pseudo-effective classes.

Definition 7.3.1. Let $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ for $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Assume that $\operatorname{vol}T_ i>0$ for all $i=1,\ldots ,n$. We define the mixed volume $\operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)$ as follows:

7.8
\begin{equation} \label{eq:volmixed} \operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)=\int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ n}[\varphi _ n]_{\mathcal{I}}\right). \end{equation}
When $\operatorname{vol}T_ i>0$ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$, the definition (7.8) does not depend on how we represent $T_ i$ as $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$, this is a consequence of theorem 2.4.8 and proposition 3.2.6.

Lemma 7.3.2.

  1. The mixed volume is symmetric in its $n$-variables.

  2. For any Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

    7.9
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:mix_vol_comp1} \operatorname{vol}\left(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n\right)=\lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} \operatorname{vol}\left(T_1+\epsilon \omega ,\ldots ,T_ n+\epsilon \omega \right). \end{equation}

  3. Consider closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n,T_1'$ on $X$ with positive volumes, then

    7.10
    \begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}(T_1+T_1',T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)=\operatorname{vol}(T_1,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)+\operatorname{vol}(T_1',T_2,\ldots ,T_ n). \end{equation}

  4. Consider closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ on $X$ with positive volumes and $\lambda > 0$, then

    \[ \operatorname{vol}(\lambda T_1,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)=\lambda \operatorname{vol}(T_1,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n). \]

  5. Suppose that $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n,S_1,\ldots ,S_ n$ are closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$ with positive volumes such that $T_ i\preceq _{\mathcal{I}} S_ i$ and $\{ T_ i\} =\{ S_ i\} $ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Then

    7.11
    \begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)\leq \operatorname{vol}(S_1,\ldots ,S_ n). \end{equation}
The notation $\operatorname{Reg}$ is defined in (1.44).

Proof. 1 This follows from the symmetry of the mixed non-pluripolar product in (7.8), cf. proposition 2.2.2 2.

2 For each $i$ and each $\epsilon >0$, we have

\[ P_{\theta _ i}[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\sim _ P P_{\theta _ i+\epsilon \omega }\left[P_{\theta _ i}[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta _ i+\epsilon \omega }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}} \]

as a consequence of example 7.1.7. Hence using proposition 6.1.16 and the multi-linearity of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 6,

\[ \begin{aligned} & \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}\operatorname{vol}(T_1+\epsilon \omega ,\ldots ,T_ n+\epsilon \omega )\\ =& \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}\int _ X \left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\wedge \cdots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ n}[\varphi _ n]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\\ =& \operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n). \end{aligned} \]

3 We write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$ as before for each $i$ and $T_1'=\theta _1'+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1'$. The potentials $P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal I}$ and $P_{\theta _1'}[\varphi _1']_{\mathcal I}$ are $\mathcal I$-model with positive masses, hence $\mathcal I$-good. By proposition 7.2.1, their sum is $\mathcal I$-good. Moreover, it is $\mathcal I$-equivalent to $\varphi _1+\varphi _1'$. Then thanks to proposition 7.2.1,

\[ P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}+P_{\theta _1'}[\varphi _1']_{\mathcal{I}}\sim _ P P_{\theta _1+\theta _1'}\left[P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}+P_{\theta _1'}[\varphi _1']_{\mathcal{I}} \right]_{\mathcal{I}} = P_{\theta _1+\theta _1'}\left[\varphi _1+\varphi _1'\right]_{\mathcal{I}} \]

Thus by proposition 6.1.16, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} & \operatorname{vol}(T_1+T_1',T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)\\ =& \int _ X \left( \theta _1+\theta _1'+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _1+\theta _1'}[\varphi _1+\varphi _1']_{\mathcal{I}} \right)\wedge \left( \theta _2+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _2}[\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\wedge \cdots \\ & \wedge \left( \theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ n}[\varphi _ n]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\\ =& \int _ X \left( \theta _1+\theta _1'+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _1'}[\varphi _1']_{\mathcal{I}} \right)\wedge \left( \theta _2+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _2}[\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\wedge \cdots \\ & \wedge \left( \theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ n}[\varphi _ n]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\\ =& \operatorname{vol}(T_1,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)+\operatorname{vol}(T_1',T_2,\ldots ,T_ n). \end{aligned} \]

4 This follows from (7.8), proposition 3.2.16 1 and proposition 2.2.2 6.

5 Our assertion follows from the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. $\square$

In view of 7.3.2 2, we may extend the definition of mixed volumes to the more general setting as follows:

Definition 7.3.3. Let $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ for $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Take a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. We define the mixed volume $\operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)$ as

7.21
\begin{equation} \label{eq:volmixedgeneral} \operatorname{vol}\left(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n\right)=\lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}\operatorname{vol}\left(T_1+\epsilon \omega ,\ldots ,T_ n+\epsilon \omega \right). \end{equation}

Note that $\operatorname{vol}(T_1+\epsilon \omega ,\ldots ,T_ n+\epsilon \omega )$ is decreasing as $\epsilon \to 0+$ due to 7.3.2 3, so the limit exists.

Furthermore, $\operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)$ does not depend on the choice of $\omega $, as a consequence of 7.3.2 3.

Proposition 7.3.4.

  1. The mixed volume is symmetric in its $n$-variables.

  2. Consider closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n,T_1'$ on $X$, then

    7.22
    \begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}(T_1+T_1',T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)=\operatorname{vol}(T_1,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)+\operatorname{vol}(T_1',T_2,\ldots ,T_ n). \end{equation}

  3. Consider closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ on $X$ and $\lambda \geq 0$, then

    \[ \operatorname{vol}(\lambda T_1,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)=\lambda \operatorname{vol}(T_1,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n). \]

  4. Suppose that $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n,S_1,\ldots ,S_ n$ are closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$ such that $T_ i\preceq _{\mathcal{I}} S_ i$ and $\{ T_ i\} =\{ S_ i\} $ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Then

    7.23
    \begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)\leq \operatorname{vol}(S_1,\ldots ,S_ n). \end{equation}

  5. Suppose that $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ are closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then

    7.24
    \begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}\left(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n \right)=\operatorname{vol}\left(\operatorname{Reg}T_1,\ldots ,\operatorname{Reg}T_ n \right). \end{equation}

  6. For any closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$, we have

    7.25
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:mix_vol_ext_pure} \operatorname{vol}(T,\ldots ,T)=\operatorname{vol}T. \end{equation}

Proof. 1, 2, 3, 4 are immediate consequences of the corresponding properties proved in 7.3.2.

5 Using 3 and 4, it suffices to establish the following: Suppose that

7.26
\begin{equation} \label{eq:T_count_sum_temp1} T=\sum _ i c_ i[E_ i] \end{equation}

is a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$, where $\{ E_ i\} $ is a countable collection of prime divisors on $X$ and $c_ i>0$. Then

7.27
\begin{equation} \label{eq:volTT2Tn_temp1} \operatorname{vol}\left(T,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n \right)=0 \end{equation}

for any closed positive $(1,1)$-currents $T_2,\ldots ,T_ n$ on $X$.

Step 1. We first assume that (7.26) is a finite sum. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$.

In this case, thanks to 3 and 4 again, we may assume that $T=[E]$ for some prime divisor $E$ on $X$. Write $[E]=\theta _{\varphi }$. Then $\varphi $ has analytic singularities thanks to proposition 1.8.4. Therefore, $P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\sim \varphi $ for any $\epsilon >0$ due to proposition 3.2.15. Write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$ for $i=2,\ldots ,n$, and choose $C>0$ so that $C\omega +\theta _ i$ are Kähler forms for each $i=2,\ldots ,n$. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} & \operatorname{vol}(T,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n)\\ =& \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} \int _ X \left([E]+\epsilon \omega \right)\wedge \left( \theta _2+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _2+\epsilon \omega }[\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\wedge \cdots \\ & \wedge \left( \theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega }[\varphi _ n]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\\ =& \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} \epsilon \int _ X \omega \wedge \left( \theta _2+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _2+\epsilon \omega }[\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\wedge \cdots \\ & \wedge \left( \theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega }[\varphi _ n]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\\ \leq & \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} \epsilon \int _ X \omega \wedge \left( \theta _2+C\omega \right)\wedge \cdots \wedge \left( \theta _ n+C\omega \right)\\ =& 0, \end{aligned} \]

where the inequality step follows from the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 and the fact that $P_{\theta _ i+\epsilon \omega }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq 0$ for all $i=2,\ldots ,n$.

Step 2. We prove the case where $\{ E_ i\} $ is infinite. We may assume that $i$ runs over $\mathbb {Z}_{>0}$.

Write $T_ i=\theta _{i}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$ for $i=2,\ldots ,n$ as before. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$ so that $\theta _ i+\omega $ is a Kähler form for each $i=2,\ldots ,n$. Fix $\epsilon >0$.

We can find $N_0>0$ so that for any $N\geq N_0$, the class of

\[ \epsilon \omega + \sum _{i=N+1}^{\infty }c_ i[E_ i] \]

is Kähler. Take a Kähler form $\omega _ N$ in this class. Then the currents

\[ \sum _{i=1}^ N c_ i [E_ i]+\omega _ N,\quad \sum _{i=1}^{\infty } c_ i [E_ i]+\epsilon \omega \]

all lie in the same cohomology class. Moreover,

\[ \sum _{i=1}^{\infty } c_ i [E_ i]+\epsilon \omega \preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\sum _{i=1}^ N c_ i [E_ i]+\omega _ N. \]

Therefore, by the monotonicity in 5, followed by the finite divisorial case of Step 1,

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{vol}\left(T,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n\right)\leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(T+\epsilon \omega ,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n\right)\\ \leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(\sum _{i=1}^ N c_ i [E_ i]+\omega _ N,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n\right)\\ =& \operatorname{vol}\left(\omega _ N,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n\right)\\ \leq & \int _ X \omega _ N\wedge (\theta _2+\omega )\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\omega ). \end{aligned} \]

Letting $N\to \infty $, and using $\{ \omega _ N\} \to \{ \epsilon \omega \} $, we get

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left(T,T_2,\ldots ,T_ n\right)\leq \epsilon \int _ X \omega \wedge (\theta _2+\omega )\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\omega ). \]

Since $\epsilon >0$ is arbitrary, we conclude (7.27).

6 Write $T=\theta _{\varphi }$. In more concrete terms, we need to show that

\[ \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}\int _ X (\theta +\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}})^ n=\int _ X (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}})^ n. \]

We may replace $\varphi $ by $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ and assume that $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-model in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then we claim that

\[ \varphi =\inf _{\epsilon >0}P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

From this, our assertion follows from proposition 3.1.26.

The $\leq $ direction is clear. For the converse, since $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-model, it suffices to show that for each prime divisor $E$ over $X$, we have

\[ \nu (\varphi ,E)\leq \nu \left(\inf _{\epsilon >0}P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}},E\right). \]

We simply compute

\[ \nu \left(\inf _{\epsilon >0}P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}},E\right)\geq \sup _{\epsilon >0}\nu \left(P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}},E\right)=\nu (\varphi ,E). \]
$\square$

Lemma 7.3.5. Let $\omega $ be a Kähler form on $X$. Then there is a constant $C>0$ depending only on $X,\omega ,\{ T_1\} ,\ldots ,\{ T_ n\} $ such that

\[ 0\leq \operatorname{vol}(T_1+\epsilon \omega ,\ldots , T_ n+\epsilon \omega )-\operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)\leq C\epsilon \]

for any $\epsilon \in [0,1]$.

Proof. By the multi-linearity established in proposition 7.3.4, we can write

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left(T_1+\epsilon \omega ,\ldots , T_ n+\epsilon \omega \right)-\operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n) \]

as a linear combination of the $2^ n-1$-terms:

\[ \epsilon ^ m\operatorname{vol}\left(\omega ^ m, T_{i_1},\ldots ,T_{i_{n-m}} \right) \]

where $J\subseteq \{ 1,\ldots ,n\} $ is a non-empty subset of cardinality $m$, and $\{ i_1,\ldots ,i_{n-m}\} $ is the complement of $J$. It suffices to show that the coefficients are bounded by a constant as above. Take $D\geq 0$ as the minimal non-negative number so that $D\{ \omega \} +\{ T_ i\} $ is nef for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Then by the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left(\omega ^ m, T_{i_1},\ldots ,T_{i_{n-m}} \right)\leq \{ \omega \} ^ m\cap \left( \{ T_{i_1}\} +D\{ \omega \} \right) \cap \cdots \cap \left( \{ T_{i_{n-m}}\} +D\{ \omega \} \right). \]

Our assertion follows. $\square$

Next we show that theorem 6.2.30 continues to hold even when $\varphi $ has vanishing mass. We prove a slightly more general result:

Theorem 7.3.6. Let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ be smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms representing big cohomology classes. Suppose that $(\varphi _ j^ k)_{k\in I}$ are nets in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ and $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ for $j=1,\ldots ,n$. We assume that $\varphi _ j^ k\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi _ j$ for each $j=1,\ldots ,n$. Then

7.39
\begin{equation} \label{eq:convmixedvolds} \lim _{k\in I}\operatorname{vol}\left( \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k},\ldots , \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\right)=\operatorname{vol}\left( \theta _{1,\varphi _1},\ldots , \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}\right). \end{equation}

Proof. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $. Then for any $\epsilon >0$, we have

\[ \lim _{k\in I}\operatorname{vol}\left( (\theta _1+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _1^ k},\ldots , (\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _ n^ k}\right)=\operatorname{vol}\left( (\theta _1+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _1},\ldots , (\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _ n}\right) \]

as a consequence of corollary 6.2.19, theorem 6.2.17 and theorem 6.2.27.

Now thanks to 7.3.5, there is $C>0$ so that for each $k\in I$,

\[ \begin{aligned} 0\leq & \operatorname{vol}\left( (\theta _1+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _1^ k},\ldots , (\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _ n^ k}\right)-\operatorname{vol}\left( \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k},\ldots , \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\right)\leq C\epsilon ,\\ 0\leq & \operatorname{vol}\left( (\theta _1+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _1},\ldots , (\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega )_{\varphi _ n}\right)-\operatorname{vol}\left( \theta _{1,\varphi _1},\ldots , \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}\right)\leq C\epsilon . \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, (7.39) follows. $\square$

Corollary 7.3.7. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form representing a big cohomology class. Let $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Then

7.42
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Ivolcont2} \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ j}\to \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi },\quad \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\to \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. The first part of (7.42) is a special case of theorem 7.3.6, while the second part of (7.42) is a special case of theorem 6.2.17. $\square$

The mixed volume has a log-concavity property:

Proposition 7.3.8. Let $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)\geq \prod _{i=1}^ n(\operatorname{vol}T_ i)^{1/n}. \]

Proof. We may assume that $\operatorname{vol}T_ i>0$ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$ since there is nothing to prove otherwise. In this case, we need to show that

\[ \begin{split} & \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\wedge \cdots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ n}[\varphi _ n]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\\ \geq & \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\int _ X \left(\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta _ i}[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}} \right)^ n \right)^{1/n}. \end{split} \]

This is a special case of theorem 2.4.4. $\square$

Next, we also study how the mixed volume behaves under bimeromorphic transforms.

Proposition 7.3.9. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold $Y$ to $X$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left(\pi ^*T_1,\ldots ,\pi ^*T_ n\right)=\operatorname{vol}\left(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n\right). \]

Proof. Using the definition of the mixed volume, we may easily reduce to the case where $\operatorname{vol}T_ i>0$ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. By proposition 3.2.7, we know that if we write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i$, then

\[ \pi ^*P_{\theta _ i}[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\pi ^*\theta _ i}[\pi ^*\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}} \]

for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. In particular,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\pi ^*T_ i=\operatorname{vol}T_ i>0. \]

Our assertion follows from the bimeromorphic invariance of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.4.3. $\square$

As for pushforward, we also have a similar result. We need a preliminary result:

Lemma 7.3.10. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold $Y$. Then for any non-divisorial closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $Y$, we have

\[ \pi ^* \pi _* T=T+\sum _{i=1}^ N c_ i [E_ i] \]

for finitely many $\pi $-exceptional divisors $E_ i$ and $c_ i>0$.

Proof. Let $E$ be the exceptional locus of $\pi $. Then

\[ T=\mathds {1}_{Y\setminus E}\pi ^* \pi _* T. \]

Therefore,

\[ \pi ^* \pi _* T-T=\mathds {1}_ E\pi ^* \pi _* T, \]

which has the stated form, due to the support theorems, see [Section 8, DemBook]. $\square$

Corollary 7.3.11. Let $\pi \colon X\rightarrow Z$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from $X$ to a Kähler manifold $Z$. Then

7.45
\begin{equation} \label{eq:volpushinc} \operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)=\operatorname{vol}(\pi _*T_1,\ldots ,\pi _*T_ n). \end{equation}

Proof. Observe that we may assume that $T_ i=\operatorname{Reg}T_ i$ for all $i=1,\ldots ,n$. In fact, since the pushforward of the divisorial part of $T_ i$ is divisorial as well, hence by proposition 7.3.4 5, they do not contribute to the volumes.

Now by proposition 7.3.9, it remains to show that

\[ \operatorname{vol}(T_1,\ldots ,T_ n)=\operatorname{vol}(\pi ^*\pi _*T_1,\ldots ,\pi ^*\pi _*T_ n). \]

By 7.3.10, the difference $\pi ^*\pi _*T_ i-T_ i$ is divisorial, hence our desired equality follows from proposition 7.3.4 5. $\square$

A particular corollary of corollary 7.3.11 will be useful later.

Corollary 7.3.12. Let $\pi \colon X\rightarrow Z$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from $X$ to a Kähler manifold $Z$. Assume that $T$ is an $\mathcal{I}$-good closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$. Then so is $\pi _*T$.

Proof. We may assume that $\int _ X T^ n>0$. Then by corollary 7.3.11,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\pi _*T=\operatorname{vol}T>0 \]

as well. Since $T$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good, we have

\[ \operatorname{vol}T=\int _ X T^ n. \]

But $\int _ X T^ n=\int _ Z (\pi _*T)^ n$, so

\[ \operatorname{vol}\pi _*T=\int _ Z (\pi _*T)^ n>0. \]

It follows that $\pi _*T$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good. $\square$

Finally we establish a semicontinuity property of the mixed volumes.

Theorem 7.3.13. Let $(\varphi _ i^ j)_{j\in J}$ ($i=1,\ldots ,n$) be nets in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$, and $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ ($i=1,\ldots ,n$). Assume that for each prime divisor $E$ over $X$, we have

\[ \lim _{j\in J} \nu \left(\varphi _ i^ j,E\right)=\nu \left(\varphi _ i,E\right),\quad i=1,\ldots ,n. \]

Then

\[ \varlimsup _{j\in J} \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j, \dots ,\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right) \leq \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1, \dots ,\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right). \]

Proof. Step 1. We first assume that $\operatorname{vol}(\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i^ j)>0$ and $\operatorname{vol}(\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i)>0$ for all $i=1,\ldots ,n$ and $j\in J$.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the $\varphi _ i^ j$'s and the $\varphi _ i$'s are $\mathcal{I}$-model for all $i=1,\ldots ,n$ and $j\in J$. Our assertion becomes

7.46
\begin{equation} \label{eq:volineq_1} \varlimsup _{j\in J} \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right) \leq \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1\right) \wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right). \end{equation}

For each $j\in J$, define

\[ \psi _ i^ j\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{k\geq j}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi _ i^ k,\quad i=1,\ldots ,n. \]

Observe that $\psi _ i^ j$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good thanks to proposition 7.2.3, since after the preceding reduction the tail family is bounded above by $0$. It follows from corollary 1.4.6 and our assumption that

\[ \lim _{j\in J}\nu \left(\psi _ i^ j,E\right)=\nu \left(\varphi _ i,E\right),\quad i=1,\ldots ,n. \]

Moreover, for each $j\in J$, the same formula gives $\varphi _ i\preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\psi _ i^ j$. Since $\varphi _ i$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model and $\psi _ i^ j$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good, proposition 6.1.12 gives

\[ \varphi _ i\leq P_{\theta _ i}\left[\psi _ i^ j\right]. \]

For each $i=1,\ldots ,n$, we define

\[ \psi _ i=\inf _{j\in J}P_{\theta _ i}\left[\psi _ i^ j\right]. \]

In particular, $\psi _ i\geq \varphi _ i$, so $\int _ X\theta _{i,\psi _ i}^ n>0$. Since $\psi _ i^ j$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good, $P_{\theta _ i}[\psi _ i^ j]$ is both model and $\mathcal{I}$-model. Due to proposition 3.2.18, $\psi _ i$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model. Thanks to proposition 3.1.26, we know

\[ \nu (\psi _ i,E)=\nu (\varphi _ i,E) \]

for any $i=1,\ldots ,n$ and any prime divisor $E$ over $X$. In other words, $\psi _ i\sim _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi _ i$ for $i=1,\ldots ,n$. But both $\varphi _ i$ and $\psi _ i$ are $\mathcal{I}$-good, therefore,

\[ \psi _ i\sim _{P} \varphi _ i,\quad i=1,\ldots ,n. \]

By proposition 6.1.16, we have

\[ \int _ X (\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n)=\int _ X (\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n). \]

Next by proposition 6.1.16 again,

\[ \begin{split} & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right)\\ \leq & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1^ j\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n^ j \right). \end{split} \]

On the other hand, due to proposition 3.1.26 and corollary 6.2.11, for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$, we have

\[ \psi _ i^ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi _ i. \]

We conclude from theorem 6.2.17 that

\[ \varlimsup _{j\in J} \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1^ j\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n^ j \right)=\int _ X (\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n). \]

Putting these equations together, (7.46) follows.

Step 2. Next we handle the general case.

Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. For any $\epsilon \in (0,1]$, from Step 1, we know that

\[ \begin{split} & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right) \\ \leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right). \end{split} \]

Using 7.3.5, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j, \dots ,\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right)\\ \leq & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right)\\ \leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right)\\ \leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1, \dots ,\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right)+C\epsilon . \end{aligned} \]

But since $\epsilon $ is arbitrary, our assertion follows. $\square$
