Lemma 9.4.45BE2CC04AAD0

Lemma 9.4.4. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma ' \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$. Then we have $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$. In particular, for any $\tau <(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\max }$,

\[ \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}} \left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta '). \]

This potential is $\mathcal{I}$-good if $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma ' \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$.

In particular, (9.67) in definition 9.4.3 makes sense.

Proof. Let

\[ \eta _{\tau }=\sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}} \left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right)=\sup _{\tau -\Gamma '_{\max }<\delta <\Gamma _{\max }}\left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right) \]

for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. Set

\[ Z=\left\{ x\in X: \Gamma _{\upsilon }(x)=-\infty \, \forall \upsilon \in \mathbb {R}\right\} \cup \left\{ x\in X: \Gamma '_{\upsilon }(x)=-\infty \, \forall \upsilon \in \mathbb {R}\right\} . \]

It follows from proposition A.2.11 that for any $x\in X\setminus Z$, we have

\[ \eta ^*_ t(x)=\Gamma ^*_ t(x)+\Gamma '^*_ t(x) \]

for all $t>0$. The same trivially holds when $x\in Z$, so the equation holds everywhere. In particular, by corollary A.2.4 and proposition 1.2.11, we have

\[ \eta _{\tau }=\left(\Gamma ^*+\Gamma '^*\right)^*_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ') \]

when $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$.

We now check that $\Gamma +\Gamma '$ is a test curve. The family $\tau \mapsto \eta _\tau $ is decreasing, and the monotonicity of the $P$-envelope proposition 3.1.24 3 gives the decreasingness of $\Gamma +\Gamma '$. If $\tau =(1-s)\tau _0+s\tau _1$, then for arbitrary $\delta _0,\delta _1\in \mathbb {R}$ and $\delta =(1-s)\delta _0+s\delta _1$,

\[ \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\geq (1-s)(\Gamma _{\delta _0}+\Gamma '_{\tau _0-\delta _0})+s(\Gamma _{\delta _1}+\Gamma '_{\tau _1-\delta _1}). \]

Taking suprema and using proposition 3.1.24 gives the concavity of $\Gamma +\Gamma '$.

Fix $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$. Choose $\delta \in \mathbb {R}$ with $\delta <\Gamma _{\max }$ and $\tau -\delta <\Gamma '_{\max }$. Then

\[ \eta _\tau \geq \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }. \]

By 9.1.3, $\Gamma _\delta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$. Hence $\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }$ has positive $(\theta +\theta ')$-mass by the multi-linearity of non-pluripolar products proposition 2.2.2 6. It follows from the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 that $\eta _\tau \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$. By corollary 3.1.12,

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_\tau =P_{\theta +\theta '}[\eta _\tau ] \]

is a model potential. Finally, $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{-\infty }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ')$ by proposition 1.2.2; since it dominates $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_\tau $ for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$, it has positive mass by another application of the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. Thus $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$.

Next, assume that $\Gamma $ and $\Gamma '$ are $\mathcal{I}$-model. Fix $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$. Then for each $\delta \in \mathbb {R}$ such that $\delta <\Gamma _{\max }$ and $\tau -\delta <\Gamma '_{\max }$, we know that $\Gamma _\delta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$ by 9.1.3. It follows from example 7.1.7 that $\Gamma _\delta $ and $\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }$ are both $\mathcal{I}$-good, hence so is $\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$ by proposition 7.2.1. These sums are bounded above by $0$, so $\eta _{\tau }$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good by proposition 7.2.3. Since $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_\tau =P_{\theta +\theta '}[\eta _\tau ]$, we conclude that $\Gamma +\Gamma '$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model. $\square$
