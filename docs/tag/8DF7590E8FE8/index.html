16.2.6 A minimum with a varying upper radius8DF7590E8FE8

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 16.2.6 A minimum with a varying upper radius

Near the pole set of the potential of a Kähler current with analytic singularities the smoothing radius must be taken smaller, so the minimum over radii will be taken over an interval whose upper end depends on the base point. Kiselman's minimum principle (theorem 8.5.1) concerns functions that are plurisubharmonic on a domain with open fibers. Here the minimum is taken over a closed interval and may be attained at its end point, and the joint Levi form is controlled only where the slope in the radius is small; the curvature of the end point function then enters. The following lemma records this curvature error explicitly.

Lemma 16.2.28. Let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a domain, let $\widehat\omega $ be a Hermitian form on $D$, and let $b$ be a smooth real function on $D$ with $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial b\leq C_ b\widehat\omega $ for a constant $C_ b\geq 0$. Let $H(z,s)$ be a smooth function, defined for $z\in D$ and $s$ in a neighborhood of $(-\infty ,b(z)]$, such that $H_ s\geq 0$ and $H_{ss}>0$, and such that $H$ is bounded below, locally uniformly in $z$, as $s\to -\infty $. Regard $H$ as a function of $(z,\zeta )\in D\times \mathbb C$ with $s=\operatorname{Re}\zeta $.

Fix $c>0$ and a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\Lambda $ on $D$, and let $\pi _ D$ denote the projection to $D$. Suppose that at every point with $s\leq b(z)$ and $H_ s\leq c$ the joint Levi form satisfies

\[ \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial _{z,\zeta }H\geq -\pi _ D^*\Lambda . \]

Then the constrained minimum

\[ v(z)=\inf _{s\leq b(z)}\bigl (H(z,s)-cs\bigr ) \]

is finite and continuous, and it satisfies, in the sense of distributions,

\[ \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial v\geq -\Lambda -cC_ b\widehat\omega . \]

In particular, if $b$ is constant, or more generally plurisuperharmonic, we may take $C_ b=0$, and the upper end point produces no additional curvature error.

Proof. For $\varepsilon >0$ we minimize instead the penalized function

\[ H_\varepsilon (z,s)=H(z,s)-cs-\varepsilon \log (b(z)-s),\qquad s<b(z). \]

For fixed $z$ it tends to $+\infty $ at both ends of the interval: as $s\to -\infty $ the linear term $-cs$ dominates the logarithm, because $H$ is bounded below. Its second derivative in $s$ is $H_{ss}+\varepsilon /(b-s)^2>0$, so it has a unique minimizer $s_\varepsilon (z)$, a nondegenerate critical point. By the implicit function theorem, $s_\varepsilon $ and the minimum value $v_\varepsilon (z)=H_\varepsilon (z,s_\varepsilon (z))$ are smooth. The critical equation is

\[ H_ s-c+\frac\varepsilon {b-s}=0, \]

so at the minimizer $0\leq H_ s\leq c$ and $0\leq \sigma :=\varepsilon /(b-s)\leq c$. Since $\operatorname{Re}\zeta $ is pluriharmonic, the joint Levi form of the barrier $-\varepsilon \log (b(z)-\operatorname{Re}\zeta )$ is $\varepsilon \, \mathrm i\partial \phi \wedge \bar\partial \phi /\phi ^2-\sigma \, \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial b$, where $\phi =b(z)-\operatorname{Re}\zeta $; the first term is nonnegative, and the second is at least $-\sigma C_ b\widehat\omega \geq -cC_ b\widehat\omega $. Hence, at the minimizer, the joint Levi form of $H_\varepsilon $ is bounded below by $-\pi _ D^*(\Lambda +cC_ b\widehat\omega )$.

We now use the Schur complement argument of Kiselman's minimum principle (theorem 8.5.1); it needs the Levi bound only at the minimizer, not elsewhere in the fiber. At the minimizer write the joint complex Hessian of $H_\varepsilon $ in block form $\bigl (\begin{smallmatrix}P& y/2\\ y^*/2& a/4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr )$, where $P=(\partial ^2H_\varepsilon /\partial z_ j\partial \bar z_ k)$, $y=(\partial ^2H_\varepsilon /\partial z_ j\partial s)$ and $a=\partial ^2H_\varepsilon /\partial s^2>0$; here we used that $\partial _\zeta =\partial _{\bar\zeta }=\frac12\partial _ s$ on functions of $z$ and $s=\operatorname{Re}\zeta $. Since $\partial _ sH_\varepsilon (z,s_\varepsilon (z))=0$, the chain rule gives $\partial _{z_ j}v_\varepsilon (z)=(\partial _{z_ j}H_\varepsilon )(z,s_\varepsilon (z))$, and differentiating the critical equation in $\bar z_ k$ gives $\bar y_ k+a\, \partial _{\bar z_ k}s_\varepsilon =0$, because $H_\varepsilon $ is real. Hence $\partial _{z_ j}\partial _{\bar z_ k}v_\varepsilon =P_{jk}+y_ j\partial _{\bar z_ k}s_\varepsilon $, that is, the complex Hessian of $v_\varepsilon $ is $P-a^{-1}yy^*$.

To bound it from below, let $M$ be the coefficient matrix of $\Lambda +cC_ b\widehat\omega =\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}M_{jk}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$. By the Levi bound at the minimizer, the block matrix $\bigl (\begin{smallmatrix}P+M& y/2\\ y^*/2& a/4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr )$ is positive semidefinite, and evaluating its Hermitian form at the vector $(\xi ,-2a^{-1}y^*\xi )$, $\xi \in \mathbb C^ n$, gives $\xi ^*(P+M-a^{-1}yy^*)\xi \geq 0$. Thus

\[ \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial v_\varepsilon \geq -\Lambda -cC_ b\widehat\omega . \]

It remains to pass to the limit $\varepsilon \to 0$. On a compact neighborhood in the base, $b$ is bounded, and evaluating at $s=b-1$ gives a uniform upper bound for $v_\varepsilon $ when $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$. Since $H$ is bounded below and $-cs-\log (b-s)$ grows linearly as $s\to -\infty $, the minimizers $s_\varepsilon $ are bounded below uniformly; the minimizers of the unpenalized problem have the same lower bound. Both problems may therefore be restricted to a common compact interval below their varying upper end point. The unpenalized minimum $v$ is finite, and it is continuous: after the substitution $s=b(z)-t$ the upper end point becomes fixed, the range of $t$ is a fixed compact interval, and a minimum of a continuous function over a fixed compact interval depends continuously on the parameter.

On this range the penalty is bounded below by a quantity that tends uniformly to zero, so $v_\varepsilon \geq v-o(1)$. For an upper bound, evaluate $H_\varepsilon $ at an unpenalized minimizer if it lies at least $\sqrt\varepsilon $ below $b$, and otherwise at $s=b-\sqrt\varepsilon $. In the first case the penalty is at most $\varepsilon |\log \sqrt\varepsilon |$; in the second, the unpenalized function changes by $o(1)$, uniformly, by its uniform continuity on the compact range. Consequently $v_\varepsilon \to v$ locally uniformly, and the inequalities for $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial v_\varepsilon $ pass to the distributional limit. $\square$
