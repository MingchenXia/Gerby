Divisorial constraints and residual volume

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.21 Divisorial constraints and residual volume

Suppose we require a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function to have generic Lelong numbers at least $a_ j$ along prescribed prime divisors $D_ j$. Removing the divisor $D=\sum _ ja_ jD_ j$ leaves the residual background $\theta -\eta _ D$, where $\eta _ D$ is a curvature form of $D$, and in each Gauduchon quotient this removal subtracts the Gauduchon degree of $D$ from the numerator. Lamari's duality therefore decides when such functions exist, and the volume of the residual background measures what remains. By the correspondence of theorem 15.9.1, equations in the ordinary singularity type of the logarithmic potential of $D$ reduce to equations in the residual background. When the residual background has a smooth positive representative, the smooth maximizer of the dual problem forces every subsolution with the prescribed Lelong numbers and the residual volume as scalar to be the logarithmic potential of $D$ plus a smooth function, up to a constant.

Fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$. Let $D=\sum _{j=1}^ N a_ jD_ j$ be an effective real divisor, with distinct prime divisors $D_ j$ and coefficients $a_ j>0$. Choose a smooth Hermitian metric on each line bundle $\mathcal O_ X(D_ j)$, write $s_ j$ for its canonical section, and let $\eta _ j$ be its Chern curvature form. Set

\[ \sigma _ D=\sum _ j a_ j\log |s_ j|^2, \qquad \eta _ D=\sum _ j a_ j\eta _ j, \qquad \beta _ D=\theta -\eta _ D. \]

The Poincaré–Lelong formula reads

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\sigma _ D=[D]-\eta _ D. \]

For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $ on $X$, define the Gauduchon degree

\[ \deg _\gamma D=\int _ X[D]\wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =\sum _ j a_ j\int _{D_ j}\gamma ^{n-1}, \]

where integration over a singular divisor means integration over its regular locus, or equivalently against its current of integration. This degree is finite, positive for $D\ne 0$, and equal to $\int _ X\eta _ D\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ by distributional Stokes. We write $\nu (u,D_ j)$ for the generic Lelong number of $u$ along $D_ j$, as in section 1.13.

Proposition 26.21.1. The following are equivalent:

  1. there is $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\nu (u,D_ j)\geq a_ j$ for every $j$;

  2. the residual background $\beta _ D$ is pseudoeffective;

  3. for every Gauduchon metric $\gamma $,

    \[ \int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}\geq \deg _\gamma D. \]

The first condition does not require $u-\sigma _ D$ to be bounded, nor the Lelong numbers of $u$ to equal the prescribed ones.

Proof. Suppose first that $u$ satisfies the first condition. By lemma 1.17.1, these Lelong numbers can be subtracted: $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-[D]\geq 0$. Consequently

\[ \beta _ D+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-\sigma _ D)=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-[D]\geq 0, \]

where the difference $u-\sigma _ D$ is first taken in $L^1$ and then replaced by its quasi-plurisubharmonic representative. This proves the second condition. Conversely, if $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta _ D)$, then $u=\sigma _ D+v$ satisfies

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=[D]+\beta _ D+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq 0, \]

so its generic Lelong numbers along the $D_ j$ are at least $a_ j$. For $n\geq 2$, Lamari's duality, theorem 4.3.1, says that a smooth background is pseudoeffective exactly when all its Gauduchon integrals are nonnegative; applied to $\beta _ D$, together with the degree identity, it gives the equivalence of the second and third conditions. For $n=1$ the same criterion reads $\int _ X\beta _ D\geq 0$: necessity follows from Stokes' theorem, and for sufficiency one solves $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=(\int _ X\beta _ D)\mu -\beta _ D$ for a smooth real function $f$ with the scalar Green operator. $\square$

For a nonzero effective real divisor $D$ and a pseudoeffective $\theta $, the exact threshold is therefore

26.171
\begin{equation} \label{eq:divisorial-gaud-threshold} \tau _\theta (D)= \inf _{\gamma \text{ Gauduchon}} \frac{\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}}{\deg _\gamma D}. \end{equation}

It is finite and nonnegative, and a function $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\nu (u,D_ j)\geq ta_ j$ for all $j$ exists if and only if $0\leq t\leq \tau _\theta (D)$. The endpoint is included, because the non-strict Gauduchon inequalities still hold there and duality gives a positive current. This is a threshold for the existence of a positive current, not for the existence of a bounded residual potential.

Whenever $\beta _ D$ is pseudoeffective, define the residual volume

\[ \mathcal V_\mu (\theta ;D)=\mathcal V_\mu (\beta _ D). \]

The definition and the degree identity give the exact formula

26.172
\begin{equation} \label{eq:divisorial-gaud-volume} \mathcal V_\mu (\theta ;D)^{1/n} =\inf _{\gamma \text{ Gauduchon}} \frac{\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}-\deg _\gamma D}{D_\mu (\gamma )}, \end{equation}

and the residual volume is finite and nonnegative. Changing the auxiliary metrics on the line bundles changes $\eta _ D$ by a smooth $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact term, so the residual volume does not depend on these metrics, nor on a smooth $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ change of $\theta $. It depends on the prescribed divisor and on the smooth probability measure $\mu $.

Here $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta $ denotes the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 6.1.2.

Theorem 26.21.2. Suppose that $\beta _ D$ has a smooth positive representative, and put $c=\mathcal V_\mu (\theta ;D)$. There is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function $u_0=\sigma _ D+v$, with $v$ smooth, satisfying

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_0)=c\mu . \]

Every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

\[ \nu (u,D_ j)\geq a_ j\quad (1\leq j\leq N),\qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)\geq c\mu \]

differs from $u_0$ by a constant. In particular, a solution of a normalized equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=c'\mu $ in the ordinary singularity type of $\sigma _ D$ has scalar $c'=c$, has a smooth remainder $u-\sigma _ D$, and is unique up to constants.

Proof. Existence. The smooth equation and theorem 26.1.3 give a smooth $v$ with

\[ \Omega =\beta _ D+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v>0,\qquad \Omega ^ n=c\mu . \]

Then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\sigma _ D+v)=[D]+\Omega \geq 0$. Off $D$, the extended measure of $u_0=\sigma _ D+v$ is $\Omega ^ n$, and both measures give zero mass to $D$. This proves existence.

Reduction to the residual background. Let $u$ satisfy the two inequalities. Removing the divisor as in the proof of proposition 26.21.1, we obtain

\[ w=u-\sigma _ D-v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\Omega ),\qquad \Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-[D]\geq 0, \]

where the difference is first taken in $L^1$ and then replaced by its canonical representative. On $X\setminus D$, the two currents differ only by smooth changes in the decomposition of their local potentials, so their extended measures agree there, and both measures vanish on $D$. Hence

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Omega (w) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)\geq c\mu . \]

Rigidity. Take $\mathrm{d}V=\mu $. The zero function solves the smooth equation $\Omega ^ n=c\mu $ in the background $\Omega $, so its subsolution threshold is $b_\Omega (1)=c$, by proposition 14.27.4. For $n\geq 2$, the normalized $(n-1)$st power of the Gauduchon metric conformal to $\Omega $ is a smooth strictly positive maximizer of the dual problem. By corollary 26.9.6, any two threshold subsolutions differ by a constant; comparing $w$ with zero shows that $w$ is constant. For $n=1$, the positive current $\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ dominates its extended measure, hence dominates $c\mu =\Omega $, while both have total mass $\int _ X\Omega =c$ by Stokes' theorem. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=0$, and $w$ is again constant.

Solutions in the ordinary type. Finally, let $u$ be in the ordinary singularity type of $\sigma _ D$ with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=c'\mu $ for some $c'\geq 0$. Then $\nu (u,D_ j)=a_ j$, and the same reduction gives a bounded $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\Omega )$ with $\mathrm{MA}_\Omega (w)=c'\mu $. Suppose $c'<c$. For every constant $C$, the bounded functions $w$ and $C$ satisfy $\mathrm{MA}_\Omega (w)=(c'/c)\mathrm{MA}_\Omega (C)$, so theorem 10.2.2 gives $w\geq C$, which is impossible for a bounded $w$. The case $c'>c$ is symmetric, with the roles of $w$ and the constants exchanged. Therefore $c'=c$, and the rigidity just proved gives $u=u_0+\mathrm{constant}$. $\square$

When $\theta $ is Hermitian, write $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$ relative to a smooth strictly positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$. For every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq [D]$ and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)\geq cf\, \mathrm{d}V$, in particular for the potential $u_0$ of theorem 26.21.2 with $c=\mathcal V_\mu (\theta ;D)$, proposition 28.8.1 bounds the coefficient $c$ in terms of the subsolution threshold $b_\theta (f)$ of theorem 14.27.2 and the linear threshold of the class of the divisor. The residual problem prescribes the divisor, whereas the threshold $b_\theta (f)$ allows all $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions.

Along a family $D_ t=tD$, $t\geq 0$, in the feasible interval, the root of (26.172) is concave and nonincreasing in $t$: every test quotient is affine in $t$ with nonpositive slope, and both properties pass to the infimum. On a subinterval where $\theta -t\eta _ D$ has a smooth positive representative, choose a minimizing Gauduchon metric $\gamma _ t$ for that background. Proposition 26.2.2 then gives the sharper formula

26.173
\begin{equation} \label{eq:divisorial-volume-derivative} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal V_\mu (\theta ;tD)^{1/n} =-\frac{\deg _{\gamma _ t}D}{D_\mu (\gamma _ t)}<0. \end{equation}

In dimension one the derivative is $-\deg D$, since the denominator is one. If the residual background at an endpoint of the interval is nef, theorem 26.10.1 also expresses its volume as the limit of the scalars of the smooth normalized equations for the perturbations by $\epsilon h$, with $h$ a fixed Hermitian form and $\epsilon \downarrow 0$.
