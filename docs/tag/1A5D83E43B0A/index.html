14.16 Local Hölder continuity in a big background1A5D83E43B0A

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 14.16 Local Hölder continuity in a big background

We now combine the stability estimate above $\rho $ with the regularization of lemma 1.21.7, whose curvature error is linear in the regularization parameter. As in the proof of theorem 14.4.1, a small multiple of the strict barrier absorbs the curvature error of the transforms, and a lower bound for the minimizing radius converts the estimate for the transforms into an estimate for the smoothing on compact subsets of $\Omega $. This gives a local Hölder modulus for solutions with minimal singularities.

Theorem 14.16.1. Fix $C_0>0$. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy

\[ \sup _ Xu=0,\qquad V_\theta -C_0\leq u\leq V_\theta , \qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$ and $p>1$. Then $u$ is locally Hölder continuous on $\Omega $, with every exponent

\[ 0<\beta <\frac{2(p-1)}{np+3(p-1)}. \]

For fixed bounds on $C_0$ and $\| f\| _ p$, the Hölder constants are uniform on each fixed compact subset of $\Omega $.

Proof. Since $u\geq V_\theta -C_0\geq \rho -C_0$, we may subtract a constant from the strict barrier so that $\rho \leq u-1$; it still satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \kappa \omega $ for a fixed $\kappa >0$. Choose $0<\gamma <(p-1)/(np+p-1)$ and set $\alpha =2\gamma <1$. Apply lemma 1.21.7 with $a=u$, and let $A,K,C$ and $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ be the constants and transforms given there. For small $\delta >0$ set

\[ c=\delta ^\alpha ,\qquad e=Ac+C\delta ,\qquad s=e/\kappa , \qquad v_\delta =\frac{\Phi _{c,\delta }+s\rho }{1+s}. \]

By the curvature bound of lemma 1.21.7, $v_\delta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The lower bound for $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ in that lemma implies $\rho \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }$ when $\delta $ is small. Thus

\[ v_\delta \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }\leq R_\delta u\leq 0. \]

In particular $v_\delta \leq V_\theta $, so theorem 14.14.1 applies with the reference $V_\theta $ and the comparison potential $v_\delta $. Together with the $L^1$ smoothing estimate, it gives

\[ \sup _\Omega (v_\delta -u)_+ \leq C\| (R_\delta u-u)_+\| _1^\gamma \leq C\delta ^\alpha . \]

Fix a compact subset of $\Omega $ on which $\rho \geq -M$. Since $s\leq C\delta ^\alpha $ and $u\leq 0$, the identity

\[ \Phi _{c,\delta }-u=(1+s)(v_\delta -u)+s(u-\rho ) \]

gives $\Phi _{c,\delta }-u\leq C_ M\delta ^\alpha $ there.

At a radius $t$ where the infimum defining $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ is attained, the monotone lower bound yields

\[ -c\log (t/\delta )\leq \Phi _{c,\delta }-u+K(\delta ^2+\delta ) \leq C_ Mc. \]

Hence $t\geq a_ M\delta $ for a fixed $a_ M>0$. As in the proof of theorem 14.4.1, monotonicity of the smoothing now gives

\[ R_{a_ M\delta }u-u\leq C_ M\delta ^\alpha , \qquad R_{a_ M\delta }u-u\geq -K(a_ M\delta )^2 \]

on the chosen compact set. Since every small radius has the form $a_ M\delta $, we obtain $|R_ tu-u|\leq C_ Mt^\alpha $ there for all small $t>0$, with constants uniform on that compact set. We apply this to a slightly larger compact neighborhood, on which also $\| \nabla R_ tu\| \leq C_ M/t$. For nearby points $x,y$ at distance $d>0$, the smooth function $R_ tu$ and a short path in that neighborhood give

\[ |u(x)-u(y)|\leq C_ M(t^\alpha +d/t). \]

The choice $t=d^{1/(1+\alpha )}$ gives the exponent $\alpha /(1+\alpha )$, and letting $\gamma $ approach its upper endpoint gives the asserted range. Pairs of points farther apart are handled by the boundedness of $u$ on the compact neighborhood. $\square$

It suffices that the equation hold on $\Omega $. Suppose that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$ on $\Omega $, where $c>0$ and $0\leq f\in L^ p(X,\mathrm{d}V)$, and that $u$ satisfies the same bounds as in the theorem. The lower bound $u\geq V_\theta -C_0\geq \rho -C_0$ shows that $u$ is locally bounded on $\Omega $, so there $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$ is the local bounded product. Since $Z$ has zero volume and is not charged by $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$, the equation holds on $X$, and the theorem applies with $cf$ in place of $f$. The uniform estimates then depend on a bound for $\| cf\| _ p$, which includes the constant $c$.
