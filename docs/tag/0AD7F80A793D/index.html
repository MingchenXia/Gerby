Theorem 13.3.20AD7F80A793D

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Theorem 13.3.2

Theorem 13.3.2. Assume that $X$ admits a smooth flag $Y_{\bullet }$. The Duistermaat–Heckman measure $\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )$ of $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ in definition 13.3.1 is independent of the choice of the smooth flag $Y_{\bullet }$. Furthermore, for any $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, the following moment identity holds:

13.28
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb {R}}x^ m\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )(x)=\Gamma _{\max }^ m\operatorname{vol}\Gamma +m\int _{-\infty }^{\Gamma _{\max }}\tau ^{m-1}\Bigl ( \operatorname{vol}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{\tau })-\operatorname{vol}\Gamma \Bigr )\, \mathrm{d}\tau , \end{equation}

and

13.29
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb {R}}\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )=\operatorname{vol}\Gamma . \end{equation}

Proof. We observe that (13.28) holds by the computations in proposition 10.5.10 and theorem 10.4.13 1. It is independent of the choice of the flag. Furthermore, (13.29) follows immediately from theorem 10.4.13 1.

Assume first that $\Gamma $ is bounded. Since the Duistermaat–Heckman measure has bounded support in this case (cf. theorem 10.5.6), we conclude that $\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )$ is uniquely determined.

By translating the parameter, it suffices to treat the case $\Gamma _{\max }=0$. Indeed, if $a=\Gamma _{\max }$ and

\[ \widetilde\Gamma _\tau \coloneqq \Gamma _{\tau +a}, \]

then $\widetilde\Gamma _{\max }=0$, and the associated test function is translated by $a$. Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )=(x\mapsto x+a)_*\operatorname{DH}(\widetilde\Gamma ). \]

Thus independence of the flag and the moment identity for $\widetilde\Gamma $ imply the corresponding assertions for $\Gamma $.

For each $\epsilon >0$, we define $\Gamma ^{\epsilon }\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ as follows:

  1. Let $\Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\max }=0$, and

  2. we set

    \[ \Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\tau }= \begin{cases} \Gamma _{-\infty },& \text{ if }\tau \leq -\epsilon ^{-1},\\ P_{\theta }\left[ (1+\epsilon \tau )\Gamma _{\tau }-\epsilon \tau \Gamma _{-\infty } \right],& \text{ if }\tau \in \left(-\epsilon ^{-1},0\right). \end{cases} \]

Let us check that $\Gamma ^\epsilon $ is indeed an element of $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. The concavity and monotonicity in $\tau $ follow from proposition 3.1.24. Since the $\Gamma _\tau $'s are $\mathcal I$-model, they are $\mathcal I$-good by example 7.1.7; hence

\[ (1+\epsilon \tau )\Gamma _\tau -\epsilon \tau \Gamma _{-\infty } \]

is $\mathcal I$-good by proposition 7.2.1. Therefore its $P$-envelope is also its $\mathcal I$-envelope, and the corresponding level is $\mathcal I$-model. The value at $-\infty $ is $\Gamma _{-\infty }$. Thus $\Gamma ^\epsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

For each fixed $\tau <0$, the argument of theorem 9.2.9 Step 3.3 gives

\[ \Gamma ^\epsilon _\tau \searrow \Gamma _\tau \quad \textup{as }\epsilon \to 0+. \]

By proposition 3.1.26 and corollary 6.2.11, this convergence is in $d_ S$. Hence theorem 10.4.13 4 gives

\[ \Delta _{Y_\bullet }(\theta ,\Gamma ^\epsilon _\tau )\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}\Delta _{Y_\bullet }(\theta ,\Gamma _\tau ) \]

for every $\tau <0$. Since

\[ \Gamma ^\epsilon _{-\infty }=\Gamma _{-\infty } \]

for all $\epsilon $, the underlying convex body is fixed. Therefore 10.5.11 applies and gives

\[ \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma ^\epsilon )\rightharpoonup \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma ). \]

It follows that $\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )$ is independent of the choice of the flag. $\square$
