Chern-connection regularization

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.21 Chern-connection regularization

Convolution in local coordinates does not directly produce global approximants with a controlled lower curvature bound. Demailly's regularization instead averages over the fibers of the tangent bundle, using the Chern connection of $\omega $, and a Kiselman-type minimum of these averages controls the loss of positivity. The classical curvature estimate for this minimum holds in a Hermitian background, while the $L^1$ and gradient bounds for the averages are elementary.

Fix $C_0>0$ and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $-C_0\leq u\leq 0$. Distances, gradients and norms of tensors are taken with respect to the Riemannian metric associated with $\omega $.

For small $t>0$, let $R_ tu$ be Demailly's regularization of $u$ formed with the Chern connection of $\omega $. It averages $u$ over the image of the ball of radius $t$ in each tangent space, with a fixed smooth nonnegative radial kernel of integral one. The map used for this purpose is a smooth realization of the holomorphic part of the formal fiberwise expansion of the exponential map; it need not be holomorphic in the fiber. The following classical estimate controls the Kiselman-type transform of these averages, including its mixed base–fiber terms; see section 34.1 for the sources.

Lemma 1.21.1. There are constants $A,K>0$ and $t_*>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega )$ and $C_0$, such that $R_ tu$ is smooth for $0<t<t_*$,

\[ -C_0\leq R_ tu\leq 0,\qquad R_ tu+Kt^2\downarrow u\quad \text{as }t\downarrow 0. \]

For $0<\delta <t_*$ and $c>0$, the bounded function

\[ \Phi _{c,\delta } =\inf _{0<t\leq \delta } \left\{ R_ tu+K(t^2-\delta ^2)+K(t-\delta ) -c\log (t/\delta )\right\} \]

is quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _{c,\delta }\geq -(Ac+2K\delta )\omega . \]

At every point, the infimum is attained at some $t>0$.

We use only this curvature estimate from the classical theory. The linear term $K(t-\delta )$ is needed in the Hermitian case, where it absorbs the mixed base–fiber terms in the complex Hessian of the regularization. Since $-c\log (t/\delta )$ tends to $+\infty $ as $t\downarrow 0$ while $u$ is bounded, the infimum is attained away from zero. We need neither smoothness of the minimizing radius nor smoothness of the transform across the region where this radius equals $\delta $.

Next we prove two elementary estimates for the averaging operators. They do not rely on any volume identity from Kähler geometry.

Lemma 1.21.2. For all sufficiently small $t>0$,

\[ \| R_ tu-u\| _{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq Ct^2, \qquad \| \nabla R_ tu\| _\infty \leq C/t. \]

The constants are uniform for $-C_0\leq u\leq 0$.

Proof. Let $z$ be local coordinates on $X$ and $\xi \in \mathbb C^ n$ the fiber variable with respect to a smooth orthonormal frame of the tangent bundle. Write $\rho (|\xi |^2)$ for the fixed radial averaging kernel, normalized with respect to Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{d}\xi $ and supported in $|\xi |\leq 1$. In these coordinates the averaging map $F$ gives

\[ R_ tu(z)=\int u(F(z,t\xi ))\rho (|\xi |^2)\, \mathrm{d}\xi , \qquad F(z,0)=z. \]

The map $F$ is smooth in these variables, and its fiber differential at zero is an isomorphism. Let $R_ t^*$ be the formal adjoint of $R_ t$ with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$, so that $\int _ X(R_ ta)b\, \mathrm{d}V=\int _ Xa\, R_ t^*b\, \mathrm{d}V$. We first claim that

\[ R_ t^*1=1+O(t^2) \]

uniformly. To see this, insert a smooth partition of unity in the variable $z$, each term being compactly supported in a chart on which the frame is defined. For fixed $\xi $, change variables $x=F(z,t\xi )$ on a neighborhood of the support of a term. The inverse map and its Jacobian have uniform Taylor expansions in $t$. The term of order zero in the transformed density is the chosen partition function at $x$, and the term of order one is linear in $\xi $, so it integrates to zero against the even radial kernel. The remaining density is bounded by $Ct^2\mathrm{d}V(x)$, since the second derivatives of all maps, partition functions and volume coefficients are bounded on these finitely many compact charts. Summing over the partition functions proves the claim.

Consequently,

\[ \left|\int _ X(R_ tu-u)\, \mathrm{d}V\right| =\left|\int _ Xu(R_ t^*1-1)\, \mathrm{d}V\right|\leq Ct^2. \]

By lemma 1.21.1, $R_ tu-u\geq -Kt^2$. A real function $a\geq -Kt^2$ satisfies $|a|\leq a+2Kt^2$; applying this to $a=R_ tu-u$ and integrating gives the first estimate.

For the derivative bound, change variables in the fiber to write $R_ tu(z)=\int _ Xu(x)k_ t(z,x)\, \mathrm{d}V(x)$. In local coordinates the smooth kernel $k_ t$ is supported where $|x-z|\leq Ct$ and satisfies

\[ |\nabla _ z k_ t(z,x)|\leq Ct^{-2n-1}. \]

Indeed, the inverse fiber map and its Jacobian have uniformly bounded derivatives, and differentiating the rescaled radial argument introduces at most one factor $t^{-1}$ in addition to $t^{-2n}$. Since the kernel is smooth with compact support, there are no boundary terms. Integration over a ball of volume at most $Ct^{2n}$ gives $\int |\nabla _ zk_ t|\, \mathrm{d}V\leq C/t$, and multiplication by the uniform bound for $u$ proves the assertion. $\square$

The first estimate relies on the cancellation of the odd first-order term against the even kernel, and not on conservation of Monge–Ampère mass.
