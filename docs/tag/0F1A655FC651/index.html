Theorem 14.19.450F1A655FC651

Theorem 14.19.45.0F1A655FC651 There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed data, such that any two normalized solutions $u_1,u_2$ with right-hand sides $h_1,h_2$ in the family satisfy

\[ \| u_1-u_2\| _\infty \leq C\bigl (\| h_1-h_2\| _\infty +\| u_1-u_2\| _{L^1}\bigr ). \]

In particular, $C$ does not depend on a positive lower bound for the $h_ i$ or on bounds for their derivatives.

Proof. Set $a=u_1-\underline u$, $b=u_2-\underline u$ and $\theta =\chi +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\underline u$. By proposition 14.19.44, $|a|,|b|\leq C_0$ for a fixed $C_0\geq 1$. It suffices to bound $s=\max _ X(b-a)$ when it is positive; the opposite difference is handled by interchanging the solutions.

Fix $r,R,\delta $ from lemma 14.19.43 and put $\alpha =r\omega $. For every smooth positive $\rho $ with

\[ \rho \geq \tfrac 12,\qquad \langle \rho \rangle _\alpha =1,\qquad \langle \rho ^2\rangle _\alpha \leq 4, \]

theorem 14.2.1 gives a smooth solution

\[ (\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )^ n=c\rho \alpha ^ n,\qquad \alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi >0,\qquad \sup _ X\psi =0. \]

At a maximum of $\psi $, $c\rho \leq 1$, so $c\leq 2$. Apply theorem 14.19.19 with $p=2$ and background $\alpha $. Its potential and lower-density bounds give fixed $c_0,C_\psi >0$ with

\[ c\geq c_0,\qquad -C_\psi \leq \psi \leq 0. \]

These constants depend only on the three bounds for $\rho $, and not on the cutoff function chosen below.

Choose $\kappa >0$ so that $2C_0\kappa \leq 1/4$, $\kappa C_0\leq 1/16$ and $\kappa C_\psi /2\leq 1/16$. Next choose $M\geq 1$ with $2^{-n}c_0Mr^ n>R^ n$, and then $\varepsilon _0>0$ with

\[ \tfrac {16}{7}\varepsilon _0\leq \min \{ (2M)^{-1},M^{-2}\} . \]

If one of the inequalities

\[ \| h_1-h_2\| _\infty \leq \kappa \delta s, \qquad \langle (b-a)_+\rangle _\omega \leq \varepsilon _0s. \]

fails, then $s\leq C(\| h_1-h_2\| _\infty +\| u_1-u_2\| _{L^1})$, with $C$ depending only on $\kappa ,\delta ,\varepsilon _0$ and the volume of $X$. We assume both and derive a contradiction. Put $t=\kappa s$; since $s\leq 2C_0$, we have $t\leq 1/4$. On $\Omega =\{ (1-t)b-a>s/2\} $, we have $b-a>7s/16$, so by Chebyshev's inequality the relative volume of $\Omega $ is at most $16\varepsilon _0/7$.

Take a smooth $\zeta :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$, zero on $(-\infty ,1/2]$ and one on $[3/4,\infty )$, and set

\[ \rho =b_0+M\zeta \bigl (((1-t)b-a)/s\bigr ),\qquad b_0=1-M\bigl \langle \zeta \bigl (((1-t)b-a)/s\bigr )\bigr \rangle _\omega . \]

The second term is supported in $\Omega $, so $1/2\leq b_0\leq 1$ and $\langle \rho ^2\rangle _\alpha \leq 2+2M^2(16\varepsilon _0/7)\leq 4$; here we use that averages with respect to $\alpha =r\omega $ and $\omega $ agree. Thus the auxiliary solution for this $\rho $ satisfies the bounds with the same constants $c_0,C_\psi $.

At a point where $b-a=s$, the function $\Phi =(1-t)b-a+t\psi /2$ is at least $7s/8$, by the choice of $\kappa $. At a maximum point $x_0$ of $\Phi $, therefore, $(1-t)b-a\geq 7s/8$ because $\psi \leq 0$, and so $\rho (x_0)\geq M$. The second derivative test gives, at $x_0$,

\[ P=\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl ((a-(1-t)b)/t\bigr ) \geq \tfrac 12(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )+\tfrac 12\alpha >0. \]

The identity

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a =t\bigl ((\theta -\alpha )+P\bigr )+(1-t)(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b) \]

and concavity imply

\[ F\bigl (\omega ^{-1}((\theta -\alpha )+P)\bigr ) \leq h_2+(h_1-h_2)/t\leq H+\delta . \]

Both forms in the convex combination are admissible, since $\mu -r\mathbf1\in \Gamma $ and $P>0$. By lemma 14.19.43, $P\leq R\omega $. On the other hand, $P\geq \frac12(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )$, so the auxiliary equation gives

\[ R^ n\geq P^ n/\omega ^ n \geq 2^{-n}c\rho r^ n\geq 2^{-n}c_0Mr^ n>R^ n, \]

a contradiction. Hence one of the two smallness inequalities fails, which gives the required bound for $s$. Interchanging the solutions proves the theorem. $\square$
