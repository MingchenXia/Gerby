Chapter 16 · Sections, graded series, and Okounkov bodies · Tag 1E6D5AA611CC

Curve weighted section growth

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 20.5.1

Theorem 20.5.1. In this situation $0\leq d\leq \ell $, and

\[ \begin{split} \mathcal H_ m(u)& =H^0\left(X,L^ m\Bigl (-\sum _ i\lfloor mc_ i\rfloor p_ i\Bigr )\right),\\ \mathcal W_ m(u)& =H^0\left(X,L^ m\Bigl (-\sum _ i\lceil mc_ i\rceil p_ i\Bigr )\right). \end{split} \]

Moreover,

\[ \lim _{m\to \infty }\frac{\dim \mathcal H_ m(u)}m =\lim _{m\to \infty }\frac{\dim \mathcal W_ m(u)}m =\ell -d =\int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta (u). \]

Proof. Step 1: the section spaces. A germ of a section of $L^ m$ vanishing to order $k$ at $p_ i$ has weighted squared norm comparable to $|z_ i|^{2(k-mc_ i)}$. As in example 1.12.5, it is locally integrable if and only if $k-mc_ i>-1$, that is, $k\geq \lfloor mc_ i\rfloor $; it is bounded if and only if $k\geq mc_ i$, that is, $k\geq \lceil mc_ i\rceil $. Away from the points $p_ i$ the weight is locally bounded and imposes no condition. Since $X$ is compact, this gives the displayed global identifications.

Step 2: the mass. The positive measure $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ has total mass $\ell $, and it has an atom of mass $c_ i$ at $p_ i$. Subtracting these atoms leaves a positive measure, which proves $d\leq \ell $. This remaining measure does not charge polar sets: after subtraction of the logarithmic singularity, its local potential has a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic representative, and by proposition 1.7.1 its curvature gives polar sets zero mass. Thus the remaining measure is exactly the extended measure, and its mass is $\ell -d$.

Step 3: growth. If $\ell >d$, the degrees of both line bundles in the display equal $m(\ell -d)+O(1)$ and tend to infinity. Let $g$ be the genus of $X$. By Riemann–Roch and Serre duality, $h^0=\deg +1-g$ once the degree exceeds $2g-2$. Dividing by $m$ proves both limits.

If $\ell =d$, both degrees remain bounded. A line bundle of negative degree has no nonzero section, and one of nonnegative degree $k$ has at most $k+1$ independent sections: the sections vanishing at $k+1$ given distinct points are sections of a line bundle of negative degree, hence zero. Therefore both dimensions are uniformly bounded, and both limits vanish. These arguments include the case $r=0$. $\square$
