16.6.6 Relative divisors and fiber obstaclesF77ABCE68FED

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.6.6 Relative divisors and fiber obstacles

To test equality of the envelopes below all obstacles beyond curves, we need potentials whose envelopes can be computed. Consider a holomorphic submersion with compact connected fibers, and a potential that is the sum of a logarithmic potential $\Psi $ with fixed weights along some divisors and the pullback of a potential $w$ on the base. Every competitor has at least the poles of $\Psi $, and removing them leaves a current pulled back from the base. Since the fibers are compact and connected, the remaining potential descends to the base, and minimizing the obstacle over the fibers reduces both envelopes to envelopes on the base. Over a fiber contained in the divisor the induced obstacle is infinite, but normalized compactness still bounds the competitors. Because the remaining current comes from a base of smaller dimension, all these potentials have zero top-degree mass. The reduction gives criteria on the base for membership in relative envelope classes and for equality of the envelopes below all obstacles. The divisors may be singular or ramified over the base; only the explicit product formula for the multiplier ideals requires relative normal crossings.

Let $p:M\to Y$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion between connected compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers, and put $d=\dim Y$ and $n=\dim M$, where $1\leq d<n$. Let $H_1,\ldots ,H_ s$ be a nonempty finite family of distinct prime divisors on $M$ and put $H=\bigcup _ jH_ j$. For positive real weights $\lambda _ j$, choose smooth Hermitian metrics $h_ j$ on $\mathcal O_ M(H_ j)$, with canonical sections $s_ j$ and Chern curvatures $\kappa _ j$. Set

\[ A=\sum _{j=1}^ s\lambda _ jH_ j,\qquad \Psi =\sum _{j=1}^ s\lambda _ j\log |s_ j|_{h_ j}^2,\qquad \kappa _ A=\sum _{j=1}^ s\lambda _ j\kappa _ j. \]

Thus $\kappa _ A+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi =[A]=\sum _ j\lambda _ j[H_ j]$. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta $ on $Y$ and $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$, put

\[ \Theta =p^*\beta +\kappa _ A,\qquad u=\Psi +w\circ p. \]

The form $\beta $ need not be closed or positive. Envelopes below continuous real obstacles are defined by the same competitor families as for smooth obstacles. Let

\[ Z=\{ y\in Y:p^{-1}(y)\subset H\} \]

be the set of points over which the whole fiber lies in $H$.

Proposition 16.6.13. For every $F\in C(M,\mathbb R)$, the function

\[ \widehat F(y)=\inf _{x\in p^{-1}(y)\setminus H}\{ F(x)-\Psi (x)\} , \qquad \inf \varnothing =+\infty , \]

is continuous in the extended real sense, finite on $Y\setminus Z$ and equal to $+\infty $ on $Z$. Defining the base envelopes below this extended-real obstacle by the usual competitor families, one has

16.11
\begin{equation} \begin{split} P_{\Theta ,F}[u]& =\Psi +P_{\beta ,\widehat F}[w]\circ p,\\ P_{\Theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} & =\Psi +P_{\beta ,\widehat F}[w]_{\mathcal I}\circ p. \end{split} \end{equation}

Every representative of the ordinary type of $u$ or of $P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ has zero extended mass. Consequently both singularity volumes of these types are zero.

If the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $w$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y$, then those of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$.

Proof. We first study the induced obstacle and bound the base competitors, then describe all competitors upstairs by divisorial removal and descent, and finally prove the mass assertion.

Step 1: the induced obstacle. Since $p$ is open, $Y\setminus Z=p(M\setminus H)$ is open and nonempty. The function $F-\Psi $ is bounded below by $\min _ MF-\sup _ M\Psi $ and tends to $+\infty $ near $H$, because $\Psi \to -\infty $ there. On every fiber that meets $M\setminus H$, its infimum is therefore attained outside $H$. A local holomorphic section through a minimizer gives upper semicontinuity of $\widehat F$. Conversely, let $y_ j\to y$ with bounded fiber minima. By compactness, a subsequence of the minimizers converges, and its limit lies outside $H$ because $F-\Psi $ remains bounded along it; this gives lower semicontinuity. If $y\in Z$, the same limit would lie in $H$, a contradiction. Thus $\widehat F\to +\infty $ at $Z$, and $\widehat F$ is continuous in the extended sense.

Step 2: a uniform bound for the base competitors. Choose a relatively compact nonempty ball $U$ in the base and a holomorphic section $s$ of $p$ on a neighborhood of $\overline U$ with image outside $H$. There is a finite $C_ U$ with $\widehat F\leq F\circ s-\Psi \circ s\leq C_ U$ on $U$. Fix a smooth probability volume form $\mathrm dV$ on $Y$. For every $b\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$ below $\widehat F$, normalized compactness in theorem 1.4.2 gives

\[ -C_\beta \leq \int _ U(b-\sup _ Yb)\, \mathrm dV \leq (C_ U-\sup _ Yb)\int _ U\mathrm dV. \]

Hence every such $b$ satisfies $\sup _ Yb\leq K_ F=C_ U+C_\beta /\int _ U\mathrm dV$. A sufficiently low translate of $w$ belongs to both base competitor families. Their regularized suprema are therefore $\beta $-plurisubharmonic functions, not identically $-\infty $, and by the extended continuity of $\widehat F$ they still lie below $\widehat F$.

Step 3: the competitors upstairs. The pullback of $w$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, so its generic Lelong numbers are nonnegative, and $\nu (u,H_ j)=\lambda _ j+\nu (w\circ p,H_ j)\geq \lambda _ j$. Let $v$ be an ordinary competitor for $u$, or an ideal competitor with $v\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. By the ordinary order or by theorem 1.16.3, its generic Lelong number along $H_ j$ is at least $\lambda _ j$. Divisorial removal in lemma 1.17.1 gives

\[ \Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v-[A]\geq 0. \]

Hence $v-\Psi $ has a quasi-plurisubharmonic representative $a$ satisfying $p^*\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\geq 0$. Descent along the compact connected fibers, proposition 1.22.3, gives $a=b\circ p$ for a unique $b\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$, and $v=\Psi +b\circ p$ for the canonical representatives. Conversely, every potential of this form is $\Theta $-plurisubharmonic.

For such $v$, one has $v\preceq u$ if and only if $b\preceq w$. Indeed, canceling $\Psi $ outside $H$ gives the base inequality almost everywhere, by Fubini's theorem in product charts, hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3; the reverse implication follows by pullback and addition. Similarly, $v\sim _{\mathcal I}u$ if and only if $b\sim _{\mathcal I}w$: lemma 1.16.4 permits both addition and cancellation of $\Psi $, the pullback formula in lemma 1.16.6 then gives one direction, and restriction of the extended ideals along any local section gives the other. This comparison of ideals does not need a section outside $H$.

If the divisors have relative simple normal crossings, the ideals have an explicit form. Here this means that near every point there are product coordinates $(y,z)$ with $p(y,z)=y$ in which the components through the point are distinct coordinate hyperplanes in the fiber variables. Then

16.14
\begin{equation} \mathcal I\bigl (t(\Psi +b\circ p)\bigr ) =\mathcal O_ M\! \left(-\sum _ j\lfloor t\lambda _ j\rfloor H_ j\right) \bigl (\mathcal I(tb)\mathcal O_ M\bigr ) \qquad (t>0). \end{equation}

To see this, work in such coordinates. Up to a smooth correction, $\Psi $ is the sum of the weights $\lambda _ j\log |z_ j|^2$ over the components through the point. Since $b$ is locally bounded above, integrability forces vanishing to order at least $\lfloor t\lambda _ j\rfloor $ along each hyperplane. After this product of coordinate powers is factored out, the remaining fiber weight is

\[ \prod _ j|z_ j|^{-2(t\lambda _ j-\lfloor t\lambda _ j\rfloor )}. \]

It is integrable and bounded below by a positive constant on a small polydisk. Integrability of the quotient against this weight therefore implies integrability against the pullback of $\mathrm e^{-tb}$ alone, and lemma 1.16.6 puts the quotient in the extension of $\mathcal I(tb)$. Conversely, local generators of that ideal are weighted-integrable against the integrable product weight, by Fubini's theorem. Outside $H$ the formula is the same pullback lemma. For one smooth divisor of weight one transverse to the fibers, the first factor is the ideal sheaf of the divisor raised to the power $\lfloor t\rfloor $.

Step 4: the envelope identities. The bound $v\leq F$ is equivalent to $b\leq \widehat F$, with no constraint over $Z$. The competitor families upstairs and on the base therefore correspond to each other, which gives (16.11) outside $H$, since regularization commutes with pullback in product coordinates there. On $H$ both sides equal $-\infty $: every competitor is at most $\Psi +K_ F$, by the bound $b\leq K_ F$, and this upper semicontinuous function equals $-\infty $ on $H$, so regularization does not change the values there.

Step 5: equality below all obstacles. Suppose that the two base envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle. Uniform obstacle comparison extends this equality to continuous real obstacles. The functions $G_ j=\min (\widehat F,j)$ are continuous and real-valued. Every base competitor below $\widehat F$ has finite supremum and hence lies below $G_ j$ for every sufficiently large integer $j$. Each full competitor family is thus the union of its subfamilies below the $G_ j$, and equality for all $G_ j$ gives equality below $\widehat F$ after taking regularized suprema. By the reduction, the envelopes upstairs agree below every smooth obstacle.

Step 6: zero mass. Take any $b\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$ and write $a=\Psi +b\circ p$. On a relatively compact chart outside $H$, the functions $b_ j=\max (b,-j)$ are locally bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic, and there

\[ \Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\Psi +b_ j\circ p)=p^*(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ j). \]

Since $n>d$, the $n$th bounded product of this current is zero by lemma 1.7.3. This identity does not require $b_ j$ to be $\beta $-plurisubharmonic: proposition 1.7.1 defines the products for locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials. By plurifine locality, this zero product agrees with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Theta (a)$ on $\{ b\circ p>-j\} $ in the chart. These sets cover the locus where $b\circ p$ is finite, while the remaining pole set and $H$ receive no extended mass. Thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Theta (a)=0$.

By divisorial removal and descent, every representative ordinarily equivalent to $u$ has this form. By the identity already proved, the ideal model is also of this form, with base potential $P_{\beta ,\widehat0}[w]_{\mathcal I}$, where $\widehat0$ is the induced obstacle for $F=0$. The same removal and descent apply to every representative of its ordinary type. All these masses are zero, which proves both volume assertions. $\square$

Proposition 16.6.14. For $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$ put $v=\Psi +\varphi \circ p$. Then

\[ u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,v) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad w\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta ,\varphi ). \]

In particular, for $q_ M=P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_ Y=P_\beta [w]_{\mathcal I}$,

\[ u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,q_ M) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad w\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta ,q_ Y). \]

Every ordinary representative of $u$ or $q_ M$ has zero extended mass, so both singularity volumes of these types vanish.

Proof. The ordinary order $u\preceq v$ is equivalent to $w\preceq \varphi $. One implication follows by addition and pullback. Conversely, cancel $\Psi $ outside $H$, which has smooth volume zero; Fubini's theorem in product charts gives the base inequality almost everywhere, and lemma 1.3.3 makes it hold everywhere. Membership in the relative envelope class requires, for every $a\in (0,1)$, a stretching competitor $h\in \mathrm{PSH}(M,\Theta )$ with

\[ av+(1-a)h\leq u. \]

A base competitor $c\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$ with $a\varphi +(1-a)c\leq w$ gives such an $h$, namely $h=\Psi +c\circ p$.

Conversely, put $\rho _ j=\nu (w\circ p,H_ j)$ and $\sigma _ j=\nu (\varphi \circ p,H_ j)$. The ordinary order $w\preceq \varphi $ gives $\rho _ j\geq \sigma _ j\geq 0$. Comparing generic Lelong numbers along $H_ j$ in the inequality above gives

\[ a(\lambda _ j+\sigma _ j)+(1-a)\nu (h,H_ j) \geq \lambda _ j+\rho _ j. \]

Consequently $\nu (h,H_ j)\geq \lambda _ j+(\rho _ j-a\sigma _ j)/(1-a)\geq \lambda _ j$. Divisorial removal and descent along the fibers give $h=\Psi +c\circ p$ with $c\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$. Canceling $\Psi $ outside $H$ and the pole sets, and applying Fubini's theorem, we obtain $a\varphi +(1-a)c\leq w$ almost everywhere on the base, and hence on all of $Y$ by lemma 1.3.3. This proves the first equivalence.

For the ideal reference, theorem 16.2.2 gives $q_ M\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. The generic divisorial coefficients of $q_ M$ are therefore at least $\lambda _ j$, and removal and descent give

\[ q_ M=\Psi +b\circ p,\qquad b\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta ). \]

Cancellation in lemma 1.16.4 gives $b\circ p\sim _{\mathcal I}w\circ p$. By lemma 1.16.6, these ideals are extensions of the base ideals, so restriction along any local section gives $b\sim _{\mathcal I}w$, even over a fiber contained in $H$.

Put $C=\max \{ 0,\sup _ M\Psi \} $ and $K=\max \{ 0,\sup _ Yb\} $. The potential $\Psi +q_ Y\circ p-C$ is nonpositive and multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u$, so it is a competitor for $q_ M$. Likewise, $b-K$ is a competitor for $q_ Y$. Canceling $\Psi $ in the first inequality almost everywhere and extending as above gives

\[ q_ Y-C\leq b\leq q_ Y+K. \]

Thus $b\sim q_ Y$. The first equivalence with reference $b$, together with the invariance of the class under ordinarily equivalent references in proposition 13.4.2, proves the second equivalence. This argument works with the ideal model itself and does not require the induced fiber obstacle to be finite. If $w$ belongs to the base relative class, proposition 16.6.8 gives equality of the two envelopes upstairs below every smooth obstacle.

For any ordinary representative of $u$ or $q_ M$, removal and descent give the form $\Psi +c\circ p$ with $c\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$. Outside $H$, replace $c$ locally by $\max (c,-k)$ and keep $\Psi $. The resulting bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials have currents pulled back from the $d$-dimensional base, so their $n$th products vanish by lemma 1.7.3, since $n>d$. By plurifine locality, this product agrees with the original extended measure on $\{ c\circ p>-k\} $. These sets cover the locus where $c\circ p$ is finite, and the pole sets and $H$ receive no mass. This proves the mass and volume assertions. $\square$

Corollary 16.6.15. The ordinary and ideal envelopes of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$ if and only if those of $w$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y$.

Put $q=P_\beta [w]_{\mathcal I}$. If $w\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta ,q)$, then

\[ P_{\Theta ,F}[u]=P_{\Theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} \quad \text{for every smooth real }F. \]

If $Y$ is a curve, write

\[ T_ w=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w,\qquad \mu _ w=\mathbf1_{\{ w=-\infty \} }T_ w,\qquad D_ w=\sum _{y\in Y}\nu (w,y)\delta _ y \]

for the curvature measure of $w$, its polar part and its atomic part. Then equality of the two envelopes of $u$ below zero, the equality $\mu _ w=D_ w$, and equality of the two envelopes of $u$ below every smooth obstacle are equivalent. They are also equivalent to

\[ u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}). \]

These equivalences also hold when $\int _ Y\beta =0$.

Proof. The forward implication is part of proposition 16.6.13. For the converse, we approximate a smooth obstacle on the base by the induced obstacles of continuous obstacles upstairs. Suppose that the envelopes upstairs agree below every smooth obstacle. Uniform obstacle comparison extends this equality to continuous real obstacles. Fix a smooth real function $B$ on $Y$ and put $G=\widehat0$. The continuous obstacles

\[ F_ j=B\circ p+\max (\Psi ,-j)\qquad (j\geq 1) \]

have induced base obstacles

\[ G_ j=B+\max (G-j,0), \]

with value $+\infty $ on $Z$. By the reduction and the assumed equality,

\[ q_ j=P_{\beta ,G_ j}[w]_{\mathcal I}=P_{\beta ,G_ j}[w]. \]

Write $q_ B=P_{\beta ,B}[w]_{\mathcal I}$. Then $q_ B\leq q_ j\leq q_1$, and the $q_ j$ decrease to a $\beta $-plurisubharmonic potential $r$, not identically $-\infty $, with convergence in $L^1$; indeed, their negative parts are bounded by that of $q_ B$, and corollary 1.4.3 applies. The set $Z$ has smooth volume zero: in each product chart of the submersion, the preimage of $Z$ lies in $H$, which has smooth volume zero, and Fubini's theorem gives the assertion on the base. Off $Z$, the bounds $q_ j\leq G_ j\downarrow B$ give $r\leq B$, and almost-everywhere order in lemma 1.3.3 extends the inequality to all of $Y$.

Each $q_ j$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $w$. To see this without assuming $G_ j$ finite, put $Q_0=P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $Q_ j=P_{\Theta ,F_ j}[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Translation of the competitor families gives

\[ Q_0+\min _ MF_ j\leq Q_ j\leq Q_0+\max _ MF_ j. \]

Thus $Q_ j\sim Q_0\sim _{\mathcal I}u$ by theorem 16.2.2. Since $Q_ j=\Psi +q_ j\circ p$, cancellation and reflection of multiplier ideals under submersions, lemma 1.16.4, lemma 1.16.6, give $q_ j\sim _{\mathcal I}w$. The same bounded comparison with $P_\beta [w]_{\mathcal I}$ gives $q_ B\sim _{\mathcal I}w$.

Let $\pi $ be any smooth local modification of a coordinate neighborhood in the base. The bounds $q_ B\circ \pi \leq q_ j\circ \pi \leq q_1\circ \pi $ give decreasing local $L^1$ convergence to $r\circ \pi $. At every point $x$ of this modification, lemma 1.17.6 and the ordinary order give

\[ \nu (w\circ \pi ,x) \leq \nu (r\circ \pi ,x) \leq \nu (q_ B\circ \pi ,x)=\nu (w\circ \pi ,x). \]

All these Lelong numbers are therefore equal, and theorem 1.16.3 gives $r\sim _{\mathcal I}w$. Together with $r\leq B$, this makes $r$ a competitor for $q_ B$. Hence $r=q_ B$.

Put $\epsilon _ j=\max \{ 0,\sup _ Y(q_ j-B)\} $. The moving-point estimate in lemma 1.4.1 gives $\epsilon _ j\to 0$: otherwise a subsequence of maximizers of $q_ j-B$ converges to some $y$, where that estimate contradicts $q_ B(y)\leq B(y)$. Every ordinary competitor $b$ below $G_ j$ satisfies $b\leq q_ j$, so $b-\epsilon _ j\leq B$ and $b-\epsilon _ j\preceq w$. Taking regularized suprema yields

\[ q_ j-\epsilon _ j\leq P_{\beta ,B}[w]. \]

Since $q_ B\leq q_ j$, letting $j\to \infty $ proves $q_ B\leq P_{\beta ,B}[w]$. The reverse inequality always holds, so the two envelopes agree below $B$. Since $B$ was arbitrary, this proves the converse.

Under the relative-class hypothesis, proposition 16.6.8 gives equality of the two base envelopes below every smooth obstacle, and the equivalence just proved gives the stated equality upstairs.

In the curve case, put $G=\widehat0$ and $L=-\sup _ M\Psi $. The uniform bound in Step 2 of the proof of proposition 16.6.13 gives a finite $K$ with $\sup _ Yb\leq K$ for every $b\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$ below $G$. Since $G\geq L$, translation of the two competitor families gives

\[ \begin{split} P_\beta [w]+L& \leq P_{\beta ,G}[w] \leq P_\beta [w]+K,\\ q+L& \leq P_{\beta ,G}[w]_{\mathcal I} \leq q+K. \end{split} \]

By proposition 30.13.1, corollary 1.6.8, the polar curvatures of these two base envelopes are $\mu _ w$ and $D_ w$, respectively. Thus equality of the envelopes upstairs below zero forces $\mu _ w=D_ w$. Conversely, under this equality of measures, corollary 30.13.2 gives equality below every smooth obstacle on the base. As in Step 5 of the proof of proposition 16.6.13, this extends to every induced obstacle $\widehat F$, and the reduction gives equality upstairs below every smooth obstacle. Taking $F=0$ proves the remaining implication. The same curve criterion identifies the equality of measures with membership of $w$ in the base relative envelope class of $q$, and the equivalence for ideal references in proposition 16.6.14 gives the last assertion upstairs. $\square$

The normal-crossing hypothesis is needed only for the explicit formula (16.14). The reduction of the envelopes holds without it.

Example 16.6.16. On $M=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, let $p,r$ be the projections and let $\sigma $ be the probability Fubini–Study form. With homogeneous coordinates $[y_0:y_1]$ and $[z_0:z_1]$, the divisor

\[ H=\{ y_0z_0^2+y_1z_1^2=0\} \]

is smooth, meets every fiber properly and ramifies over the first factor. Give $\mathcal O_ M(H)=\mathcal O_ M(1,2)$ its product metric, with logarithmic divisor potential $\Psi $ and curvature $\kappa _ H=p^*\sigma +2r^*\sigma $. Put $\Theta =2p^*\sigma +2r^*\sigma $ and choose $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^1,\sigma )$ with $\sigma +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=\delta _{[0:1]}$. Both obstacle envelopes of $u=\Psi +w\circ p$ reduce to the base, but the product formula for its multiplier ideals fails at a ramification point.

Proof. For each $[z_0:z_1]$ there is a unique point $[y_0:y_1]=[-z_1^2:z_0^2]$ on $H$, so $H$ is a smooth graph over the second factor, and its projection to the base has degree two. In particular no fiber of $p$ lies in $H$. Since $\Theta =p^*\sigma +\kappa _ H$, proposition 16.6.13 gives both envelope identities.

Near $([0:1],[1:0])$, use $y=y_0/y_1$ and $z=z_1/z_0$. Up to smooth additions,

\[ \Psi =\log |y+z^2|^2,\qquad w=\log |y|^2, \qquad u=\log |y(y+z^2)|^2. \]

At $t=3/4$, each of the two weights separately has trivial multiplier ideal, so the right side of (16.14) would be $\mathcal O_ M$. However, $1\notin \mathcal I(3u/4)$ at this point. Indeed, consider the region where $y=cz^2$ with $|c-1|<1/4$ and $0<|z|<\epsilon $; for fixed $z$, it has area comparable to $|z|^4$ in the $y$ variable. The weighted integral of $1$ is therefore bounded below by a positive constant times

\[ \int _{0<|z|<\epsilon }|z|^{-6}|z|^4\, \mathrm d\lambda _ z =2\pi \int _0^\epsilon \frac{\mathrm dr}{r}=+\infty , \]

where $\mathrm d\lambda _ z$ is planar area. Thus the multiplier ideal is proper. The envelope identities follow instead from the behavior of multiplier-ideal equivalence under addition, and do not require the product formula. $\square$

If the divisor contains an entire fiber, the induced obstacle is infinite over that fiber. The next example still admits an explicit model for the zero obstacle.

Example 16.6.17. Let $M=\mathbb P^2\times \mathbb P^1$, with projections $p,r$, and let $\sigma _ j$ be the standard Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ j$. In the positive closed background

\[ \Theta =2p^*\sigma _2+r^*\sigma _1, \]

there is a logarithmic potential $\Psi $ of a smooth prime divisor containing the fiber over $E=[0:0:1]$. Its fiber infimum for the zero obstacle is finite and continuous off $E$ and tends to $+\infty $ at $E$. Nevertheless,

\[ P_\Theta [\Psi ]=P_\Theta [\Psi ]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi +q\circ p, \]

where $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^2,\sigma _2)$ is bounded, $0\leq q\leq \log 4$ and $q(E)=\log 4$. Every ordinary representative of this model has zero extended mass, so both singularity volumes vanish. The two envelopes also agree below every smooth obstacle.

Proof. Use homogeneous coordinates $[y_0:y_1:y_2]$ and $[z_0:z_1]$, and take

\[ H=\{ y_0z_0+y_1z_1=0\} ,\qquad \Psi =\log \frac{|y_0z_0+y_1z_1|^2}{(|y_0|^2+|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2)(|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2)}. \]

Outside $E$, the divisor is the graph $[z_0:z_1]=[-y_1:y_0]$. Its closure contains the whole fiber over $E$, so $H$ is prime. It is smooth outside that fiber by the graph description, and smooth along it because the derivatives in the $y_0$ and $y_1$ directions are $z_0$ and $z_1$, which cannot both vanish. Its product metric has curvature $p^*\sigma _2+r^*\sigma _1$, hence

\[ \Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi =p^*\sigma _2+[H]. \]

Writing $S=|y_0|^2+|y_1|^2$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives the fiber obstacle

\[ G(y)=-\max _{z\in \mathbb P^1}\Psi (y,z) =\log \frac{S+|y_2|^2}{S},\qquad G(E)=+\infty . \]

It has no finite continuous extension at $E$.

We claim that the largest $\sigma _2$-plurisubharmonic potential below $G$ is

\[ q(y)=\begin{cases} \displaystyle \log \frac{4|y_2|^2}{S+|y_2|^2},& S\leq |y_2|^2,\\[6pt] \displaystyle \log \frac{S+|y_2|^2}{S},& S\geq |y_2|^2. \end{cases} \]

To see that $q$ is $\sigma _2$-plurisubharmonic, work on $y_2\ne 0$ with $x=(y_0/y_2,y_1/y_2)$ and $t=\log |x|^2$. Adding the Fubini–Study potential $L(x)=\log (1+|x|^2)$ gives

\[ q+L=\begin{cases} \log 4,& t\leq 0,\\ 2\log (1+\mathrm e^ t)-t,& t\geq 0. \end{cases} \]

This function and its first derivative are continuous at $t=0$. For $t>0$, its first two derivatives are $(\mathrm e^ t-1)/(\mathrm e^ t+1)\geq 0$ and $2\mathrm e^ t/(1+\mathrm e^ t)^2>0$. It is therefore a convex nondecreasing function of $t$, hence plurisubharmonic in $x$, across the unit sphere as well. The expression is constant near $x=0$, and $q=G$ is smooth near $y_2=0$, so $q$ is globally $\sigma _2$-plurisubharmonic and bounded.

If $b\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^2,\sigma _2)$ lies below $G$, then $b+L\leq \log 4$ on $|x|=1$, and the maximum principle on the unit ball gives $b\leq q$ inside it. Outside the ball, the obstacle already gives $b\leq G=q$. For $0<R=|x|^2\leq 1$, the inequality $q\leq G$ reads $4R\leq (1+R)^2$, and at $E$ it is automatic. The formula for $q$ also gives the asserted bounds, which proves the claim.

For every ordinary or ideal competitor $v$ for $\Psi $, the ordinary order or theorem 1.16.3 gives $\nu (v,H)\geq 1$. Divisorial removal and descent along the fibers, lemma 1.17.1, proposition 1.22.3, give $v=\Psi +b\circ p$ with $b\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^2,\sigma _2)$. The bound $v\leq 0$ gives $b\leq G$ off $E$, hence everywhere with the extended value at $E$. Thus $v\leq \Psi +q\circ p$. Conversely, this upper bound is a competitor for both envelopes, since it is nonpositive and differs boundedly from $\Psi $. This proves the envelope identities directly.

On $M\setminus H$, bounded local truncations of $b$ have products pulled back from a two-dimensional base, so their third products vanish by lemma 1.7.3, and plurifine locality gives zero extended mass. Divisorial removal puts every ordinary representative of the model in this form, which proves both volume assertions. Finally, since the model is ordinarily equivalent to $\Psi $, proposition 13.4.2 gives $\Psi \in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,P_\Theta [\Psi ]_{\mathcal I})$, and proposition 16.6.8 gives equality below every smooth obstacle. $\square$

Example 16.6.18. There is a Hermitian background $\Theta $ on a compact fourfold that fails the bounded mass property and admits a potential $u$ without an analytic ordinary type such that

\[ P_\Theta [u]=P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi , \qquad u\not\sim \Psi . \]

Both envelopes agree below every smooth obstacle, and their common model for the zero obstacle has both singularity volumes zero.

Proof. Let $(Y,\omega )$ be the Hopf threefold of theorem 8.0.1. On $\mathbb P^1$, let $\sigma $ be the probability Fubini–Study form, choose a point $D$ and a logarithmic potential $\psi $ with $\sigma +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]$ and $\sup \psi =0$. On $M=Y\times \mathbb P^1$, with projections $p,r$, put

\[ \Theta =p^*\omega +r^*\sigma ,\qquad H=Y\times D,\qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r. \]

Take the potential $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\omega ,0)$ of infinite mass from proposition 13.6.1, which satisfies $P_\omega [U]=0$. Its multiplier ideals are trivial, so also $P_\omega [U]_{\mathcal I}=0$. Set $u=\Psi +U\circ p$. Here $\widehat0=0$, so proposition 16.6.13, corollary 16.6.15 give the envelope identities, the equality below all obstacles and the vanishing of the singularity volumes. Moreover $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,\Psi )$ by proposition 16.6.14. Since $U$ has infinite extended mass, it is unbounded; restriction to a section outside $H$ therefore proves $u\not\sim \Psi $. If a potential $a$ with analytic singularities were ordinarily equivalent to $u$, proposition 16.2.7 would give $u\sim a\sim P_\Theta [a]=P_\Theta [u]=\Psi $, a contradiction.

Finally, let $V_ j$ be the smooth potentials of theorem 8.0.1. Their pullbacks are bounded and $\Theta $-plurisubharmonic, and proposition 19.9.1 gives

\[ \int _ M\mathrm{MA}_\Theta (V_ j\circ p) =4\int _ Y\mathrm{MA}_\omega (V_ j)\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

Thus $\Theta $ itself fails the bounded mass property. $\square$

The descent of potentials uses that the fibers are connected. Otherwise the residual potential can take independent values on different components of a fiber, as a finite isogeny shows.

Example 16.6.19. There is a surjective holomorphic submersion $p:M\to Y$ from a connected compact Kähler surface to an elliptic curve, with four components in every fiber, for which both identities in (16.11) fail. The pole divisor is smooth and maps onto $Y$, and the obstacle upstairs is smooth.

Proof. Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$, let $\beta $ be its flat probability form, and let $q:E\to E$ be multiplication by two. On $M=E\times \mathbb P^1$, denote the projections by $a,r$ and put $p=q\circ a$. Then $q$ is an unramified covering of degree four and $q^*\beta =4\beta $. The manifold $M$ is connected, whereas every fiber of $p$ is the disjoint union of four projective lines.

On $\mathbb P^1$ take the probability Fubini–Study form $\sigma $, a point $D$ and a logarithmic potential $\psi $ with $\sigma +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]$ and $\sup \psi =0$. Put

\[ H=E\times D,\qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r,\qquad \Theta =p^*\beta +r^*\sigma =4a^*\beta +r^*\sigma . \]

Thus $\Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi =p^*\beta +[H]$, which corresponds to the base potential $w=0$. Choose $\epsilon >0$ so small that

\[ f(\zeta )=\epsilon \cos (2\pi \operatorname {Re}\zeta ) \in \mathrm{PSH}(E,4\beta ), \]

and set $F=f\circ a$. The function $f$ is well defined and smooth on $E$. Since it changes sign under translation by $1/2$, it is not constant on the fibers of $q$.

Apply proposition 16.6.13 to the projection $a$, which has connected fibers, with base background $4\beta $ and base potential zero. Its induced obstacle is $f$, since $\sup \psi =0$. The bounded potential $f$ is a competitor for both base envelopes and equals their obstacle, so

\[ P_{\Theta ,F}[\Psi ]=P_{\Theta ,F}[\Psi ]_{\mathcal I} =\Psi +f\circ a. \]

For the map $p$, the fiber obstacle of proposition 16.6.13 would instead be

\[ \widehat F(y)=\min _{q(\zeta )=y}f(\zeta ). \]

Every expression $\Psi +b\circ p$ is invariant under translation of the first factor by points of the kernel of $q$. The computed envelope is not invariant under translation by $1/2$, because $\Psi $ is unchanged and $f$ changes sign. It therefore cannot equal either right side of (16.11) for $p$. $\square$

The correct formula takes the fiber minimum over each connected component of a fiber separately. These components are the points of the finite covering of the base in lemma 1.22.2.

Corollary 16.6.20. Let $p:M\to Y$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion between connected compact complex manifolds, with $1\leq \dim Y<\dim M$; its fibers may be disconnected. Use the weighted divisor data $A,H,\Psi ,\kappa _ A$ above, let $\beta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$, and let $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$. Put

\[ \Theta =p^*\beta +\kappa _ A,\qquad u=\Psi +w\circ p. \]

For the factorization $p=r\circ \pi $ of lemma 1.22.2, set $\beta '=r^*\beta $ and $w'=w\circ r$. For $F\in C(M,\mathbb R)$ define

\[ \widehat F_{\mathrm{comp}}(y') =\inf _{x\in \pi ^{-1}(y')\setminus H}\{ F(x)-\Psi (x)\} , \qquad \inf \varnothing =+\infty . \]

This obstacle is continuous in the extended real sense on $Y'$, and

\[ \begin{split} P_{\Theta ,F}[u] & =\Psi +P_{\beta ',\widehat F_{\mathrm{comp}}}[w']\circ \pi ,\\ P_{\Theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} & =\Psi +P_{\beta ',\widehat F_{\mathrm{comp}}}[w']_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi . \end{split} \]

Every representative of the ordinary type of $u$ or of $P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ has zero extended mass, and both singularity volumes of these types are zero. The ordinary and ideal envelopes of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $M$ if and only if those of $w'$ agree below every smooth obstacle on $Y'$.

For $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$, put $v=\Psi +\varphi \circ p$. Then

\[ u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,v) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad w\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta ,\varphi ). \]

For $q_ M=P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_ Y=P_\beta [w]_{\mathcal I}$, one also has

\[ u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,q_ M) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad w\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\beta ,q_ Y). \]

If $Y$ is a curve, let $\mu _ w,D_ w$ be the polar and atomic measures of corollary 16.6.15. Then equality of the envelopes of $u$ below zero, the equality $\mu _ w=D_ w$, and equality of the envelopes of $u$ below every smooth obstacle are equivalent. They are also equivalent to $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta ,q_ M)$.

Proof. The covering map $r$ preserves the curvature inequality, so $w'\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y',\beta ')$. Moreover $\dim Y'=\dim Y<\dim M$, and

\[ \Theta =\pi ^*\beta '+\kappa _ A,\qquad u=\Psi +w'\circ \pi . \]

Applying proposition 16.6.13 to the submersion $\pi $, which has connected fibers, gives the obstacle identities and the mass assertions, and corollary 16.6.15 for the same submersion gives the equivalence for all obstacles. In example 16.6.19, this factorization has $Y'=E$, $r=q$ and $\pi =a$, so it recovers the computed envelope.

For the assertions on relative classes, apply proposition 16.6.14 to $\pi $, with reference $\varphi \circ r$ on $Y'$, and then transfer membership through $r$ by proposition 16.6.10. That proposition, applied with the zero obstacle, also gives

\[ P_{\beta '}[w']_{\mathcal I}=q_ Y\circ r. \]

Applying the same two equivalences with these ideal references proves the assertion for $q_ M$ and $q_ Y$.

In the curve case, $Y'$ is a curve. The covering is locally biholomorphic, so curvature measures and point Lelong numbers pull back without multiplicities. The pole set of $w'$ is $r^{-1}\{ w=-\infty \} $, and therefore

\[ \mu _{w'}=r^*\mu _ w,\qquad D_{w'}=r^*D_ w, \]

where the pullback of a measure is defined on each sheet through the local inverse of $r$. Such pullbacks reflect equality: over every sufficiently small ball in $Y$, the restriction of a measure is recovered from any sheet over that ball. Hence $\mu _{w'}=D_{w'}$ if and only if $\mu _ w=D_ w$. The curve case of corollary 16.6.15, applied to $\pi $, identifies the former equality with the equality of the envelopes upstairs below zero, with their equality below every smooth obstacle and with membership in the relative envelope class upstairs. This proves the last assertion. $\square$
