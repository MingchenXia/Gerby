15.18.7 Smooth exact approximantsE9933083FDD7

We now construct the approximants of the first step. The smooth Dirichlet theorem, lemma 15.7.1, applies to every smooth positive density $F$. Approximating the Hölder boundary values of $\varphi $ by smooth functions therefore gives smooth solutions, and the point is to control them quantitatively. The estimates below bound the trace of a smooth solution by a power of the $C^4$ norm of its boundary value. The $C^4$ norms of the boundary approximations grow polynomially while their uniform errors decay polynomially, so both the convergence and the trace growth have polynomial rates.

In this subsection balls are centered at the origin. On the boundary sphere, $\nu $ is the outward Euclidean unit normal and $\nabla _{\tan }$ denotes tangential differentiation. For a positive form $T$, inner products $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle _ T$ and norms $|\cdot |_ T$ of $(1,0)$-covectors are computed with the inverse of the coefficient matrix of $T$.

Lemma 15.18.8.3EBB652C42D7 Let $B$ be a coordinate ball, let $\vartheta $ and $F>0$ be smooth near $\overline B$, and let $q$ be smooth with $R=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \varepsilon \beta $ for a fixed $\varepsilon >0$. Fix a finite $L\geq 0$. If $u\in C^\infty (B)$ satisfies

\[ T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0, \qquad T^ n=F\beta ^ n, \qquad |u-q|\leq L, \]

put $S=\operatorname {tr}_\beta T$ and $v=u-q$. For $A>0$, define

\[ Q_ A=\log S-Av+\mathrm{e}^{-v}. \]

There are $A_0,C$, depending only on the fixed data and $L$, such that at every interior local maximum of $Q_ A$, with $A\geq A_0$,

59253C0B8B5A 15.18.9
\begin{equation} S\leq C\sqrt A. \end{equation}

The constants do not depend on any bound for the gradient of $u$.

Proof. This is the maximum-principle computation behind the classical second-order estimate. The non-closedness of $\vartheta $ produces a first-order error term, which is absorbed by the convex term $\mathrm{e}^{-v}$.

At the point in question make a constant unitary change of coordinates so that the matrix of $T$ is diagonal, with eigenvalues $\lambda _ i$. Write

\[ \Delta _ T=\sum _ i\lambda _ i^{-1}\partial _ i\partial _{\bar i}, \qquad H=\operatorname {tr}_ T\beta . \]

Differentiating $\log \det T=\log F$ and commuting derivatives gives

8A24C1B85BE1 15.18.10
\begin{equation} \Delta _ TS=\Delta _\beta \log F+P+E_0, \quad P=\sum _{k,i,j}\frac{|\partial _ kT_{i\bar j}|^2}{\lambda _ i\lambda _ j}, \quad |E_0|\leq C_0H. \end{equation}

The non-closedness of $\vartheta $ enters through the bounded vector

\[ E_ p=\sum _ k(\partial _ p\vartheta _{k\bar k}-\partial _ k\vartheta _{p\bar k}). \]

Since $\partial _ pS-E_ p=\sum _ k\partial _ kT_{p\bar k}$, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives $|\partial S-E|_ T^2\leq SP$. From (15.18.10),

1894AFF4178E 15.18.11
\begin{equation} \Delta _ T\log S \geq -\frac{C_0(1+H)}S- \frac2S\operatorname {Re}\langle \partial \log S,E\rangle _ T. \end{equation}

Set $g_ A(s)=-As+\mathrm{e}^{-s}$. At a local maximum of $Q_ A$, $\partial \log S=-g_ A'(v)\partial v$ and $\Delta _ Tv=n-\operatorname {tr}_ TR$. Combining these identities with (15.18.11), then completing the square in $\partial v$, yields

9C906B419E9F 15.18.12
\begin{equation} 0\geq \left[\varepsilon A-\frac{C_2}{S}-\frac{C_2(A+1)^2}{S^2}\right]H -C_3(A+1)-\frac{C_2}{S}. \end{equation}

Here $|E|_ T^2\leq CH$ and the bounds for $v$ have been used. If $S\geq K\sqrt A$ and $K$ is large, the bracket is at least $\varepsilon A/2$. Thus $H\leq C$ independently of $A$ and $u$. The determinant upper bound then gives $S\leq C'$. Enlarging $K$ makes this incompatible with $S\geq K\sqrt A$, proving (15.18.9). $\square$

We shall also use the following variant, in which a gradient bound replaces the convex term. If $|\nabla (u-q)|_\beta $ is bounded, then at an interior maximum of $\log S-A(u-q)$ the trace is bounded independently of $A$ for all large $A$. Indeed, at the maximum $\partial \log S=A\partial (u-q)$, and (15.18.11) gives

\[ 0\geq \left[A\varepsilon -\frac CS-\frac{CA}{S}\right]H-nA-\frac CS. \]

For $S$ larger than a fixed constant the bracket is at least $A\varepsilon /2$; the determinant again controls $S$.

15.18.13 A polynomial boundary trace estimate94BAFACA36D1

Proposition 15.18.14.6586F9B3B128 Let $B$ be the unit ball. Fix smooth $\vartheta $, smooth $F>0$ near $\overline B$, and $M<\infty $. There are $C,N<\infty $, depending only on these fixed data, such that every smooth solution $u$ with boundary value $g\in C^\infty (\partial B)$ of

\[ T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0, \qquad T^ n=F\beta ^ n, \qquad \lVert u\rVert _{L^\infty (B)}\leq M, \qquad u|_{\partial B}=g, \]

satisfies

F89A683ED708 15.18.15
\begin{equation} \sup _{\overline B}\operatorname {tr}_\beta T \leq C\bigl (1+\lVert g\rVert _{C^4(\partial B)}\bigr )^ N. \end{equation}

Proof. We follow the classical boundary estimates for the Dirichlet problem on a ball and keep track of the dependence on $L=1+\lVert g\rVert _{C^4}$. Barriers bound the first derivatives, and differentiation along rotations bounds the mixed normal–tangential second derivatives on the boundary. A lower bound for the tangential block of $T$ then bounds the double normal derivative through the equation, and lemma 15.18.8 reduces the interior bound to the boundary bound.

Step 1: first derivatives. Fix a sufficiently large constant $K>0$, depending only on the fixed data. Extend $g$ to $G$ on $\overline B$ with $\lVert G\rVert _{C^4}\leq CL$. For $A=CL$ large enough,

\[ \underline u=G+A(|z|^2-1), \qquad \underline T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\underline u\geq K\beta , \qquad \underline T^ n\geq F\beta ^ n. \]

Proposition 15.18.3 gives $\underline u\leq u$. If $h$ solves $\Delta _\beta h=-C_\vartheta $ with $h=g$ on $\partial B$ and $C_\vartheta \geq \sup \operatorname {tr}_\beta \vartheta $, then $u\leq h$. Boundary differentiation of these barriers gives

A09942AD8934 15.18.16
\begin{equation} |\partial _\nu u|+|\nabla _{\tan }u|\leq CL \quad \text{on }\partial B. \end{equation}

Let $H=\operatorname {tr}_ T\beta $, $\mathcal L=\operatorname {tr}_ T\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$, and $w=u-\underline u\geq 0$, the linearized operator and the barrier function used below. Taking $K$ fixed and sufficiently large gives

F834489897B3 15.18.17
\begin{equation} \mathcal Lw=n-\operatorname {tr}_ T\underline T\leq -(1+H). \end{equation}

Let $E=\sum _ k(z_ k\partial _{z_ k}+\bar z_ k\partial _{\bar z_ k})$ be the real radial field. Write $\Theta $ for the coefficient matrix of $\vartheta $. Differentiating $\log \det T=\log F$ and commuting $E$ with complex second derivatives yields

\[ \mathcal L(Eu)=E(\log F)+2n-\operatorname {tr}_ T(E\Theta +2\Theta ). \]

Hence $|\mathcal L(Eu)|\leq C(1+H)$. The same estimate holds for $Zu$ when $Z$ is an infinitesimal unitary rotation. Applying the minimum principle to $CL+C'w\pm Yu$, with $Y=E$ or $Z$, gives

13E61CA163BF 15.18.18
\begin{equation} |Eu|+|Zu|\leq CL\quad \text{on }B. \end{equation}

Step 2: mixed second derivatives on the boundary. For a rotation $Z$, the function $R_ Z=Z(u-G)$ vanishes on $\partial B$ and satisfies $|\mathcal LR_ Z|\leq CL(1+H)$. The barriers $CLw\pm R_ Z$ and (15.18.17) imply $|\partial _\nu R_ Z|\leq CL^2$ on the boundary. Rotations span the real tangent space of $\partial B$, so all real mixed normal–tangential second derivatives are $O(L^2)$. Pure tangential second derivatives are $O(L)$ by differentiating $u=g$ twice and using (15.18.16). Thus all complex Hessian entries except the complex normal–normal entry are $O(L^2)$.

Step 3: the double normal derivative. It remains to bound the tangential block of $T$ from below. For $n\geq 2$, let

\[ \lambda _0=\min \{ T_ p(\xi ,\bar\xi ):p\in \partial B, \ \xi \in T_ p^{1,0}(\partial B),\ |\xi |_\beta =1\} . \]

Choose a minimizing pair $(p,\xi )$ and extend $\xi $ to a smooth unit tangential field near $p$, with uniformly bounded derivatives. On the sphere, any smooth function vanishing there satisfies

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-G)(\xi ,\bar\xi )=\frac12E(u-G). \]

Define

\[ a(z)=(\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G)_ z(\xi (z),\overline{\xi (z)})-\frac12EG(z), \qquad P=a+\frac12Eu-\lambda _0. \]

Then $P\geq 0$ on the nearby spherical boundary and $P(p)=0$. The boundary first-derivative bound also gives $\lambda _0\leq CL$ by the tangential identity above. Consequently

\[ |P|\leq CL, \qquad |\mathcal LP|\leq CL(1+H). \]

On a fixed cap $D=B\cap B_ r(p)$ consider

\[ \Psi =P+C_1Lw+C_2L|z-p|^2. \]

Choose $C_2$ so that $\Psi \geq 0$ on the interior spherical part of $\partial D$, and then $C_1$ so that $\mathcal L\Psi \leq 0$ by (15.18.17). The minimum principle yields $\Psi \geq 0$. At $p$ the normal derivative of $|z-p|^2$ vanishes, and therefore

\[ \partial _\nu P+C_1L\partial _\nu w\leq 0. \]

Equations (15.18.16)–(15.18.18) now give $D^2u(\nu ,\nu )(p)\leq CL^2$, hence $\operatorname {tr}_\beta T(p)\leq CL^2$. Positivity and $\det _\beta T=F$ imply

4327E0F53426 15.18.19
\begin{equation} \lambda _0\geq cL^{-2(n-1)}. \end{equation}

At any boundary point write

\[ T=\begin{pmatrix} B_0 & b \\ b^* & d \end{pmatrix} \]

in tangential–normal blocks, with $B_0$ an $(n-1)\times (n-1)$ Hermitian matrix, $b\in \mathbb C^{n-1}$ and $d\in \mathbb R$. We have $|b|\leq CL^2$ and $B_0\geq \lambda _0I$. The determinant equation and the Schur complement give

\[ d=\frac{F}{\det B_0}+b^*B_0^{-1}b \leq (\sup F)\lambda _0^{-(n-1)}+CL^4\lambda _0^{-1}, \]

which is polynomial in $L$. For $n=1$, the trace is $F$ and this argument is unnecessary.

Step 4: the interior. Choose a fixed quadratic $q$ with $\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \varepsilon \beta $. Apply lemma 15.18.8 with one fixed $A$. At an interior maximum of its auxiliary function the trace is uniformly bounded, while at a boundary maximum the preceding estimate applies. Since the remaining terms are uniformly bounded, (15.18.15) follows. $\square$

15.18.20 Exact approximationDF626CDAD5D9

We apply these estimates to smooth approximations of the boundary values of $\varphi $ on a ball in $\Omega $. Recall that $\theta $, $\omega $, $c$ and $f$ are the data of theorem 15.18.1.

Proposition 15.18.21.59A5B60FAB74 Let $B\Subset \Omega $ be a coordinate ball, identified with $\{ |z|<r_0\} $, with closure in a larger coordinate chart, and put

\[ F=\frac{cf\, \omega ^ n}{\beta ^ n}. \]

There are $a>0$, $b,C<\infty $, and $u_ j\in C^\infty (\overline B)$ such that

9F54A2578D16 15.18.22
\begin{equation} \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j>0, \qquad (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^ n=F\beta ^ n, \end{equation}

and

082B0A6218F2 15.18.23
\begin{equation} \lVert u_ j-\varphi \rVert _{L^\infty (B)}\leq Cj^{-a}, \qquad \sup _{\overline B}\operatorname {tr}_\beta (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)\leq Cj^ b. \end{equation}

Proof. Since $\varphi $ is Hölder continuous near $\overline B$, mollification at scale $1/j$ gives smooth boundary approximations $g_ j$ with

\[ \lVert g_ j-\varphi \rVert _{L^\infty (\partial B)}\leq Cj^{-a}, \qquad \lVert g_ j\rVert _{C^4(\partial B)}\leq Cj^4. \]

Choose a fixed quadratic $q$ with $\eta =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q>0$ near $\overline B$. Extend $g_ j$ smoothly to $G_ j$. For a sufficiently large, polynomially bounded $A_ j$, the function

\[ \underline w_ j=G_ j-q+A_ j(|z|^2-r_0^2) \]

has boundary value $g_ j-q$ and satisfies

\[ \eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\underline w_ j =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ j+A_ j\beta >0, \qquad (\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\underline w_ j)^ n\geq F\beta ^ n. \]

Apply the smooth Dirichlet theorem recalled at the beginning of this subsection, with density $H=F\beta ^ n/\eta ^ n$ to obtain a solution $w_ j$ for the Hermitian background $\eta $; put $u_ j=w_ j+q$. Comparing $u_ j$ with $\varphi \pm \lVert g_ j-\varphi \rVert _\infty $ by proposition 15.18.3 proves the first bound in (15.18.23). The functions $u_ j$ are therefore uniformly bounded. Proposition 15.18.14 and the polynomial $C^4$ boundary norm give the second bound. $\square$
