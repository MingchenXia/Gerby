Selection by a vanishing exponential parameter

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 15.12 Selection by a vanishing exponential parameter

The equation with exponent zero can have several normalized solutions. Nevertheless, the solutions of the equations with positive exponent select one of them in the limit: the scalar functional provides a family of integral normalizations, and the selected potential is the greatest subsolution of the critical equation satisfying all of them. To prove that it is the greatest, we use supporting probabilities of a second functional, obtained by varying the forcing term in the subsolution inequality.

In this section, $\theta $ is an arbitrary smooth big real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, not necessarily semipositive. Fix a strict analytic barrier $\rho \leq 0$ as in section 14.12, and put $\Omega =X\setminus \{ \rho =-\infty \} $. A potential has minimal type if it has the ordinary singularity type of the envelope $V_\theta =P_\theta (0)$, which has minimal singularities (section 1.19). Fix a nonzero $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$, with $1<p<\infty $. For a nonzero nonnegative density $g$ in some $L^ q$, $q>1$, let $c(g)>0$ be the unique constant of theorem 14.15.4 for which the equation $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=c(g)g\, \mathrm{d}V$ has a solution $v$ of minimal type. We call $c(g)$ the critical scalar of $g$ and such solutions critical potentials for $g$; they are continuous on $\Omega $ by theorem 14.16.1. Here $\mathrm{MA}_\theta $ is the extended measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta $. On $\Omega $ it is the locally bounded product for every potential of minimal type, and it gives zero mass to the analytic set $X\setminus \Omega $.

Put $c=c(f)$ and define

15.46
\begin{equation} \label{eq:selection-probabilities} \begin{aligned} \kappa (r)& =\log c-\log c(\mathrm{e}^ r f),\\ \mathcal P_ f& =\left\{ \nu \in \mathcal P(X): \int _ X r\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq \kappa (r) \text{ for every }r\in C(X)\right\} . \end{aligned} \end{equation}

When $\theta $ is semipositive and $f=\mathrm{e}^ F$ with $F\in C(X)$, we have $\kappa (r)=B_0(F)-B_0(F+r)$, and $\mathcal P_ f$ is the set $\mathcal A_0(F)$ of supporting probabilities. The definition also applies when $f$ vanishes on a set of positive measure and when $\theta $ is not semipositive.

Theorem 15.12.1. For $\lambda >0$, let $u_\lambda $ be the solution of minimal type of

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_\lambda ) =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f\, \mathrm{d}V \]

given by theorem 14.15.2 and unique by corollary 14.12.3. The set $\mathcal P_ f$ is nonempty and weakly compact, and every potential of minimal type is integrable against all its measures. As $\lambda \downarrow 0$,

\[ w_\lambda =u_\lambda -\lambda ^{-1}\log c \]

converges locally uniformly on $\Omega $ and in $L^1(X)$ to a potential $w$ of minimal type, continuous on $\Omega $. It is the greatest among the potentials $s$ of minimal type, continuous on $\Omega $, satisfying

15.49
\begin{equation} \label{eq:selection-class} \mathrm{MA}_\theta (s)\geq cf\, \mathrm{d}V,\qquad \int _ Xs\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq 0\quad (\nu \in \mathcal P_ f). \end{equation}

Moreover,

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (w)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V,\qquad \max _{\nu \in \mathcal P_ f}\int _ Xw\, \mathrm{d}\nu =0. \]

Consequently, $u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda $ converges to $w-\sup _ Xw$ in the same two topologies. For a Hermitian $\theta $, one may take $\Omega =X$, and both convergences of potentials are uniform.
