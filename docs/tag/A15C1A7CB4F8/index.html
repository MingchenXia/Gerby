Chapter 12 · Monge–Ampère equations and stability · Tag A15C1A7CB4F8

Common barrier criterion

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 14.8.1

Theorem 14.8.1. Let $\theta \geq 0$ be big. Suppose $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies $P_{\delta \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty $ for some $0<\delta <1$. Let $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy, for a fixed $C$,

\[ \varphi -C\leq u_ j\leq \varphi +C,\qquad u_ j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1(X). \]

If

14.10
\begin{equation} \label{eq:common-barrier-self-error} I_ j:=\int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)\longrightarrow 0, \end{equation}

then $u_ j\to u$ in capacity.

Proof. The canonical representative of the limit also lies between $\varphi -C$ and $\varphi +C$. It suffices to show that every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in capacity. We may therefore assume that $I_ j\leq 2^{-j}$ and that $u_ j\to u$ almost everywhere. For $N\geq j$ define

\[ p_{j,N}=P_\theta (\min \{ u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N\} ),\qquad p_ j=P_\theta \left(\inf _{\ell \geq j}u_\ell \right). \]

All these envelopes lie between $\varphi -C$ and $\varphi +C$. The contact theorem and ordered contact give an inequality for the finite rooftops. Partition the contact set of $p_{j,N}$ according to the first index $\ell $ for which $p_{j,N}=u_\ell $. On the corresponding piece, the measure of $p_{j,N}$ is bounded by that of $u_\ell $, and $\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}=\mathrm{e}^{u_\ell }$. Therefore

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p_{j,N}) \leq \sum _{\ell =j}^ N I_\ell \leq 2^{1-j}. \]

Only positive measures enter this argument, so it applies also when their total masses are infinite.

As $N\to \infty $, $p_{j,N}$ decreases to $p_ j$; the limit is the envelope of the infimum because every competitor lies below every finite rooftop. The sequence $p_ j$ increases, and its canonical limit $p$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. By lemma 1.8.12, both monotone limits hold in capacity. Applying lemma 6.3.1 to the bounded nonnegative weights $|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|$, first in $N$ and then in $j$, we obtain

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^ p-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p)=0. \]

The inequalities $p_ j\leq u_\ell $ for all $\ell \geq j$ imply $p\leq u$, first almost everywhere and then for the canonical representatives. Also $p\sim u\sim \varphi $. Off the pole sets, $\mathrm{e}^ p<\mathrm{e}^ u$ exactly on $\{ p<u\} $, so $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p)(\{ p<u\} )=0$. Since $P_{\delta \theta }(\varphi )-C$ is a competitor for $P_{\delta \theta }(p)$, the latter is not identically $-\infty $. Theorem 14.1.2, applied with coefficient zero, gives $p\geq u$, and hence $p=u$.

Finally, by Hartogs' lemma and the almost-everywhere convergence, $U_ j=(\sup _{\ell \geq j}u_\ell )^*$ decreases to $u$. The inequalities $p_ j\leq u_ j\leq U_ j$, together with the convergence in capacity of both monotone sequences, prove the claim. $\square$
