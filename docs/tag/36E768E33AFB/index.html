Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag 36E768E33AFB

Corollary 7.1.11

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Corollary 7.1.11

Corollary 7.1.11. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a compact Kähler manifold $Y$. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and let $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ be a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $. Then $(\pi ^*\varphi _ j)_ j$ is a quasi-equisingular approximation of the $\pi ^*\theta $-psh function $\pi ^*\varphi $.

Proof. Let $(\epsilon _ j)_ j$ and $\omega $ be as in definition 1.6.8, so that $\epsilon _ j\searrow 0$ and $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _ j\omega )$ for all $j$. Choose a Kähler form $\tilde\omega $ on $Y$ and a constant $C>0$ such that $\pi ^*\omega \leq C\tilde\omega $. Then

\[ \pi ^*\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta +C\epsilon _ j\tilde\omega ). \]

Moreover, the sequence $(\pi ^*\varphi _ j)_ j$ is decreasing, has analytic singularities, and decreases to $\pi ^*\varphi $.

It remains to verify the quasi-equisingular condition definition 1.6.8 4. Fix a constant $A>0$. Then $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is also a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ with respect to $\theta +A\omega $. Since $\theta _{\varphi }+A\omega $ is a Kähler current, corollary 7.1.10 gives

\[ \varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta +A\omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

Viewing the convergence in a fixed enlarged background class, pulling back and using corollary 6.2.8, proposition 3.2.7, we get

\[ \pi ^*\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\pi ^*P_{\theta +A\omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I} =P_{\pi ^*(\theta +A\omega )}[\pi ^*\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

The domination $\pi ^*\omega \leq C\tilde\omega $ also gives

\[ \pi ^*\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}\bigl (Y,\pi ^*(\theta +A\omega )+C\epsilon _ j\tilde\omega \bigr ), \]

so the preceding convergence is in the setting of corollary 7.1.10 on $Y$. The potential $\pi ^*\varphi $ has positive mass with respect to $\pi ^*(\theta +A\omega )$ by the bimeromorphic invariance of non-pluripolar products proposition 2.4.3. Applying corollary 7.1.10 again on $Y$ shows that $(\pi ^*\varphi _ j)_ j$ satisfies the quasi-equisingular condition definition 1.6.8 4 for $\pi ^*\varphi $. Combining this with the first paragraph proves the assertion with respect to $\pi ^*\theta $. $\square$
