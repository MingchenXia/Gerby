16.5 Limits of analytic envelopesE69A0F80536C

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.5 Limits of analytic envelopes

By the curve construction of theorem 16.4.2, convergence of potentials alone does not determine the limit of their ideal envelopes. Two further conditions do: a common lower bound in the multiplier-ideal order, and convergence of all divisorial Lelong numbers. Together they force every cluster value of the envelopes to have the multiplier ideals of the limiting potential, and maximality of the envelope then identifies the cluster value. The dyadic analytic approximants satisfy both conditions.

In this section, $\theta $ is again an arbitrary smooth real $(1,1)$-form. We fix $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, which will absorb the curvature errors of the approximants. Recall from section 1.17 that a local divisor $E$ over $X$ is a prime divisor on a smooth modification $\pi $ of a coordinate neighborhood in $X$, and that $\nu _ E(u)=\nu (u\circ \pi ,E)$ is the generic Lelong number of the pullback of $u$ along $E$.

Proposition 16.5.1. Let $\epsilon _ j\geq 0$ tend to zero, and let $a_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _ jh)$ satisfy

\[ u\preceq _{\mathcal I}a_ j,\qquad \nu _ E(a_ j)\longrightarrow \nu _ E(u) \quad \text{for every local divisor }E\text{ over }X. \]

Then

\[ P_{\theta +\epsilon _ jh}[a_ j]_{\mathcal I} \longrightarrow P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I} \quad \text{in }L^1. \]

If the $a_ j$ have analytic singularities, their ordinary envelopes converge to $P_\theta [u]$ in $L^1$ if and only if $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$.

Proof. Set $p=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_ j=P_{\theta +\epsilon _ jh}[a_ j]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection and the ideal order give

\[ p\leq P_{\theta +\epsilon _ jh}[p]_{\mathcal I}\leq q_ j\leq 0. \]

The first inequality holds because $p$ is a competitor in the larger background, and the second follows from proposition 16.2.6, since $p\sim _{\mathcal I}u\preceq _{\mathcal I}a_ j$. All $q_ j$ have supremum zero and belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +Ch)$ for a fixed $C\geq \sup _ j\epsilon _ j$. By normalized compactness, every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence converging in $L^1$. Let $q$ be such a limit. Passing to the limit in the current inequalities gives $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and lemma 1.3.3 gives $p\leq q\leq 0$.

Fix a local modification $\pi :Z\to U$ and a prime divisor $E\subset Z$. By lemma 1.22.13, the chosen subsequence still converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ after pullback. Since $q_ j\sim _{\mathcal I}a_ j$, the two functions have the same divisorial Lelong numbers, and the pointwise semicontinuity in lemma 1.17.6 yields

\[ \nu _ E(q)\geq \limsup _ j\nu _ E(q_ j) =\lim _ j\nu _ E(a_ j)=\nu _ E(u). \]

Indeed, at every point of $E$ the pullback of each $q_ j$ has Lelong number at least $\nu _ E(q_ j)$; it remains to apply the pointwise inequality at a point of $E$ where the pullback of $q$ has Lelong number $\nu _ E(q)$. On the other hand, $p\leq q$ gives $\nu _ E(q)\leq \nu _ E(p)=\nu _ E(u)$. Thus $q$ and $u$ have the same Lelong numbers along all local divisors. Blowing up a point of any local modification recovers the pointwise Lelong number there as a generic divisorial number; on a curve the point is already a divisor. By theorem 1.16.3, $q\sim _{\mathcal I}u$.

Consequently $q$ is a competitor for $p$, so $q\leq p$. Every cluster value is therefore $p$, which proves convergence of the whole sequence. For analytic $a_ j$, proposition 16.2.7 identifies the ordinary and ideal envelopes, and the last assertion follows from uniqueness of $L^1$ limits and lemma 1.3.3. $\square$

The divisorial convergence follows from inclusions of multiplier ideals at fixed weights. The local Bergman estimate bounds the error in terms of the log discrepancy, and this error tends to zero after division by the weight.

Corollary 16.5.2. Let $\epsilon _ j\geq 0$ tend to zero, and let $a_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _ jh)$ satisfy $u\preceq _{\mathcal I}a_ j$. Suppose that, for every integer $m\geq 1$ and every $\delta >0$,

\[ \mathcal I((1+\delta )m a_ j)\subseteq \mathcal I(mu) \quad \text{for all sufficiently large }j. \]

Then the conclusions of proposition 16.5.1 hold.

Proof. Fix a local divisor $E$ over a coordinate neighborhood $U$, and write $A=A_ U(E)$ for its log discrepancy. By lemma 1.17.8, the assumed inclusion implies

\[ (1+\delta )m\nu _ E(a_ j) \geq \operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I((1+\delta )m a_ j) \geq \operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I(mu) \geq m\nu _ E(u)-A \]

for all large $j$. Taking the lower limit in $j$ first, and then letting $m\to \infty $ and $\delta \downarrow 0$, we obtain $\liminf _ j\nu _ E(a_ j)\geq \nu _ E(u)$. The opposite inequality $\nu _ E(a_ j)\leq \nu _ E(u)$ holds for every $j$ because $u\preceq _{\mathcal I}a_ j$. Hence proposition 16.5.1 applies. $\square$

The common ideal bound cannot be omitted, even when all divisorial Lelong numbers converge. In the atomic approximation of theorem 16.4.2, every individual atom can tend to zero while the limiting potential is not an ideal model.

Example 16.5.3. On a compact connected curve $Y$ with a positive form $\theta $ of total mass one, there are normalized $a_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$ with analytic singularities and a smooth $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$ such that

\[ a_ j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1,\qquad \nu _ E(a_ j)\longrightarrow \nu _ E(u)\text{ for every local divisor }E, \]

but $P_\theta [a_ j]=P_\theta [a_ j]_{\mathcal I}\to u\ne P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ in $L^1$.

Proof. Choose $u$ smooth, nonconstant and normalized, with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$, and apply the construction in theorem 16.4.2 to the smooth probability measure $\nu =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. Since $\nu $ has no atoms, the largest mass of a cell tends to zero along partitions of vanishing diameter. Choose the rational approximations of the weights so that their total absolute error tends to zero. The resulting atomic probability measures $\nu _ j$ converge weakly to $\nu $, and their largest atoms tend to zero.

By the same theorem, their normalized Green potentials $a_ j$ have analytic singularities, satisfy $P_\theta [a_ j]=P_\theta [a_ j]_{\mathcal I}=a_ j$, and converge to $u$ in $L^1$. At every point $y\in Y$,

\[ 0\leq \nu (a_ j,y)=\nu _ j(\{ y\} ) \leq \max _{z\in Y}\nu _ j(\{ z\} )\longrightarrow 0=\nu (u,y). \]

Since local modifications of a smooth curve are isomorphisms, this gives all the required divisorial limits. As $u$ is bounded and $\theta >0$, both of its envelopes are zero, and since $u$ is not constant, the envelopes do not converge to them. Each $a_ j$ has a logarithmic pole, so $u\preceq _{\mathcal I}a_ j$ fails. $\square$
