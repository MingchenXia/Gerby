Chapter 11 · Non-pluripolar measures and mass · Tag 017BC9CAC896

Curve relative envelope criterion

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Corollary 30.12.11

Corollary 30.12.11. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$ put $q=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$. The following are equivalent:

\[ \begin{gathered} u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta ,q),\qquad \mu _ u=D_ u,\\ P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I},\\ P_{\theta ,F}[u]=P_{\theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} \quad \text{for every smooth real }F. \end{gathered} \]

This also holds when $D_ u(Y)=A$, that is, when $q$ has zero mass.

Proof. The classifications of ordinary and ideal models in lemma 30.12.1, lemma 19.5.2 show that $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ if and only if $\mu _ u=D_ u$. Step 1 of the proof of proposition 30.12.10 shows that this is equivalent to equality of the envelopes at every smooth obstacle. Membership of $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta ,q)$ implies the latter by proposition 16.6.8.

Conversely, assume that $\mu _ u=D_ u$. By lemma 19.5.2, $\mu _ q=D_ u$. Ordinary equivalence preserves polar curvature by corollary 1.6.8, so every potential equivalent to $q$ has non-pluripolar mass $A-D_ u(Y)$, by Stokes' theorem. Hence

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)=A-D_ u(Y). \]

If $D_ u(Y)<A$, then $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$, and proposition 30.8.2 puts $u$ in $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta ,q)$.

If $D_ u(Y)=A$, the positive curvature measures $T_ u$ and $T_ q$ both dominate $D_ u$ and both have total mass $A$. Hence $T_ u=T_ q=D_ u$. The distribution $u-q$ is harmonic, and therefore constant on the compact connected curve. Thus $u=q+C$ for a constant $C$, and proposition 13.4.2 gives $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta ,q)$ also in this case. $\square$
