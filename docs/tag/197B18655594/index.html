Balanced metrics and the Hopf cone

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 29.6.2 Balanced metrics and the Hopf cone

If zero belongs to the Lee–Gauduchon cone, then the image of $j_ X$ contains no nonzero pseudoeffective class; this is the case when $X$ carries a balanced metric. On the Hopf threefold, by contrast, the Lee form spans a ray of classes with pseudoeffective image under $j_ X$, and the Lee–Gauduchon cone is an open half-line.

Corollary 29.6.8. The following conditions are equivalent:

\[ 0\in \operatorname {LG}(X),\qquad \operatorname {LG}(X)=W_ X,\qquad \mathcal C_ X=K_ X. \]

A balanced metric implies these conditions. On a manifold in Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$, one has $W_ X=\{ 0\} $ and $\operatorname {LG}(X)=\{ 0\} $.

Proof. An open cone in a finite-dimensional vector space that contains zero is the entire space, since scaling a neighborhood of zero reaches every point. The two dual descriptions in theorem 29.6.4 now prove the equivalences, including the case $W_ X=\{ 0\} $. If $b$ is a balanced metric, the form $b^{n-1}$ is closed and of pure type, hence also $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$-closed. Thus $b$ is Gauduchon and gives the element $[\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(b^{n-1})]=0$ of $\operatorname {LG}(X)$.

On a compact Kähler manifold the $\partial \bar\partial $-lemma gives $j_ X=0$, since every $\kappa _\lambda $ is closed and de Rham exact. Thus $K_ X=V_ X$ and $W_ X=\{ 0\} $, and since $\operatorname {LG}(X)$ is nonempty, $\operatorname {LG}(X)=\{ 0\} $. By theorem 29.6.7, this conclusion extends to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$. $\square$

Consequently, a nonzero pseudoeffective class in the image of $j_ X$ obstructs balanced metrics on every compact complex manifold bimeromorphic to $X$. For LCK manifolds, 29.2.4 would provide such a class.

For the Hopf threefold of chapter 8, with the Lee form $\lambda $ of proposition 29.3.2, the covering description identifies the underlying manifold with $S^1\times S^5$, so $V_ X=\mathbb R[\lambda ]$. The class $j_ X([\lambda ])=\{ \alpha \} $ is nonzero by (29.4), and it is pseudoeffective because $\alpha \geq 0$. Its negative multiples are not pseudoeffective, since their pairing with the Gauduchon metric $\omega $ is negative. Thus

\[ K_ X=0,\qquad \mathcal C_ X=\mathbb R_{\geq 0}[\lambda ],\qquad \operatorname {LG}(X)=\{ a\in H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R): \langle [\lambda ],a\rangle >0\} . \]

The same manifold has unbounded Monge–Ampère masses by theorem 8.0.1. The positive ray detected here controls pairings with cohomology classes, which are linear and do not bound those nonlinear masses.
