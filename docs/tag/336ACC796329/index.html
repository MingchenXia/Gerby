Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag 336ACC796329

Partial fubini study upper

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 27.4.1

Theorem 27.4.1. The potentials $U_ m$ with $N_ m>0$ form a relatively compact subset of $L^1(X)$. Let $U$ be the $L^1$ limit of a sequence $U_{m_ j}$ with $N_{m_ j}>0$ and $m_ j\to \infty $. Then $U\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$,

\[ U\preceq _{\mathcal I}\varphi , \]

and $U\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Consequently

\[ \left(\limsup _{m\to \infty }U_ m\right)^* \leq E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w). \]

If only finitely many selected spaces are nonzero, the left-hand side is identically $-\infty $.

Proof. Step 1: compactness. The extremal formula for the kernel and the Bernstein–Markov inequality give, for every $\epsilon >0$ and with $C_\epsilon \geq 1$,

\[ U_ m\leq w+\delta _{m,\epsilon }\quad \hbox{on }K, \qquad \delta _{m,\epsilon }=2\epsilon +\frac{2\log C_\epsilon }{m}. \]

No factor depending on $N_ m$ occurs, because the extremal formula is a supremum over single sections of unit norm. Fix one $\epsilon >0$. Since $\delta _{m,\epsilon }\leq 2\epsilon +2\log C_\epsilon $, lemma 1.19.1 gives an upper bound for $\sup _ XU_ m$ that is uniform over all $m$ with $N_ m>0$. On the other hand, the total mass identity gives

\[ \int _ K \mathrm{e}^{m(U_ m-w)}\, \mathrm{d}\mu =N_ m\geq 1. \]

Since $\mu $ is a probability measure, $U_ m-w\geq 0$ somewhere on $K$, and therefore $\sup _ XU_ m\geq \min _ Kw$. The normalized compactness theorem (theorem 1.4.2) now gives relative compactness in $L^1(X)$, with limits in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Step 2: the multiplier-ideal order. Let $U_{m_ j}\to U$ in $L^1(X)$, with $m_ j\to \infty $. Fix a local smooth modification $\pi :Y\to W$ of an open set $W\subseteq X$, and a point $y\in Y$. By lemma 1.22.13, $U_{m_ j}\circ \pi \to U\circ \pi $ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Upper semicontinuity of Lelong numbers (lemma 1.17.6) and the order bound before the theorem give

\[ \nu (U\circ \pi ,y) \geq \limsup _ j\nu (U_{m_ j}\circ \pi ,y) \geq \nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,y). \]

Since $\pi $ and $y$ are arbitrary, the local valuative characterization in theorem 1.16.3 gives $U\preceq _{\mathcal I}\varphi $. Note that the modification and the point are fixed before passing to the limit; the same convergent sequence serves for all of them.

Step 3: the obstacle. Since $K$ may have zero smooth volume, the inequality on $K$ in Step 1 does not pass directly to $L^1$ limits. We therefore use the envelope of the obstacle alone. Set

\[ G=E_{\theta ,K}[V_\theta ]_{\mathcal I}(w). \]

Every $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies $a\leq V_\theta +\sup _ Xa$, and hence $a\preceq _{\mathcal I}V_\theta $. Thus the multiplier-ideal constraint in the definition of $G$ is no restriction, and $G$ is the largest $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function lying below $w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Since $U_ m-\delta _{m,\epsilon }$ is such a function, Step 1 gives, on all of $X$,

\[ U_ m\leq G+\delta _{m,\epsilon }. \]

Passing to the $L^1$ limit gives $U\leq G+2\epsilon $ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $U\leq G$. Since $G\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$ by theorem 27.3.1, the same holds for $U$. Thus $U$ satisfies both defining conditions of the partial envelope, and $U\leq E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)$.

Step 4: the upper limit. By the local Hartogs lemma, if $U_{m_ j}\to U$ in $L^1$ and $x_ j\to x$, then $\limsup _ jU_{m_ j}(x_ j)\leq U(x)$. Given a point $x$, a sequence of indices $m_ j\to \infty $ with $N_{m_ j}>0$ and points $x_ j\to x$, pass to a subsequence along which $U_{m_ j}(x_ j)$ converges to $\limsup _ jU_{m_ j}(x_ j)$ and $U_{m_ j}$ converges in $L^1$. By Steps 2 and 3 the $L^1$ limit is at most the partial envelope, so $\limsup _ jU_{m_ j}(x_ j)\leq E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)(x)$. This proves the inequality for the regularized upper limit, also at the poles of the envelope. Indices with $N_ m=0$ contribute only $-\infty $. $\square$
