14.20.3 Compact gluingAABABE424D5C

We now glue the local example into a compact torus by a regularized maximum with a quadratic function. The result is a bounded solution with minimal singularities whose density is constant on a ball, where the solution is discontinuous; in the transition region the density acquires singularities that destroy every $L^ p$ bound with $p>1$.

We work on the flat complex torus $X=(\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm{i}\mathbb Z))^2$, identifying a small coordinate ball with a ball in $U\times W$. For a smooth function $v$ on the ball write $H_ v=(v_{j\bar k})$ for its complex Hessian and $I$ for the two-dimensional identity matrix.

Theorem 14.20.4. There are a positive flat Kähler form $\theta $ on $X$, a bounded $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with minimal ordinary singularities, and a density $F>0$ almost everywhere such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=F\theta ^2,\qquad F\in L^1(X,\theta ^2),\qquad F\notin L^ p(X,\theta ^2)\quad (p>1). \]

On an open coordinate ball $\Omega $, $F$ is a positive constant and $u$ is discontinuous. The constant function $\rho =0$ is a smooth strict barrier on $X$.

Proof. Set $R=1/8$ and choose $M$ so that $P_0=P+M$ satisfies $0\leq P_0\leq L$ on $B_ R\Subset U\times W$ for some finite $L$. Choose $C>0$ with

14.76
\begin{equation} \frac{3CR^2}{16}\geq 2,\qquad \frac{5CR^2}{16}>L+2, \end{equation}

and put $Q(Z)=C(|Z|^2-R^2/4)$ for $Z=(z,w)$. Let $\chi $ be the convolution of $|t|$ with an even nonnegative smooth probability kernel supported in $(-1,1)$. Then $\chi $ is smooth and convex, $|\chi '|\leq 1$, $\chi '(0)=0$, and $\chi (t)=|t|$ for $|t|\geq 1$. The function

\[ m(s,t)=\frac{s+t+\chi (s-t)}2,\qquad v=m(P_0,Q) \]

is bounded and plurisubharmonic, because $m$ is convex and nondecreasing in each variable. The collar inequalities imply

\[ v=P_0\quad \text{on }B_{R/4},\qquad v=Q\quad \text{when }3R/4\leq |Z|<R. \]

Indeed, $Q\leq -2\leq P_0-2$ on the first ball and $Q>L+2\geq P_0+2$ on the collar. Define

\[ \theta =\frac{\mathrm{i}C}{2\pi } (\mathrm{d}z\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z+\mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar w), \qquad u=\begin{cases} v-Q& \text{on }B_ R,\\ 0& \text{outside }B_ R.\end{cases} \]

The collar identity makes $u$ identically zero near $\partial B_ R$ and gives a global bounded potential with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ on the ball and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\theta $ outside. Thus $u$ is admissible and has minimal ordinary singularities.

Outside the pluripolar set $(S\times W)\cap B_ R$, set $\lambda =(1+\chi '(P_0-Q))/2$. Differentiating the regularized maximum gives

14.79
\begin{equation} H_ v=\lambda H_ P+(1-\lambda )CI +\frac{\chi ''(P_0-Q)}2 \partial (P_0-Q)\otimes \overline{\partial (P_0-Q)}. \end{equation}

Both $H_ P$ and $CI$ are positive definite there; the final term is positive semidefinite. Hence the classical density is positive. Bounded products charge no pluripolar sets, so there is no further measure on the exceptional set. Their mass on compact $X$ is finite by proposition 1.7.1. This proves $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=F\theta ^2$ for an almost-everywhere positive $F\in L^1$. On $\Omega =B_{R/4}$, (14.74) gives the representative $F=C^{-2}$. There $u=P+M-Q$ is discontinuous at $(0,w_0)$ for $0<|w_0|<R/4$.

We prove failure of every global $L^ p$ directly in the transition region. For large $k$, set

\[ m_ k=a(a_ k)+M\in [0,L],\qquad w_ k=\bigl (R^2/4+m_ k/C-|a_ k|^2\bigr )^{1/2}, \qquad Z_ k=(a_ k,w_ k). \]

Then $w_ k>0$, $P_0(Z_ k)=Q(Z_ k)=m_ k$, and (14.76) gives $|Z_ k|^2<9R^2/16$. Near the isolated pole $a_ k$,

\[ h(z)=\alpha _ k\log |z-a_ k|^2+H_ k(z), \]

where $H_ k$ is smooth and harmonic. Consequently $b$ and $P_0$ are continuous near $Z_ k$. There is a product neighborhood $V_ k\Subset B_ R$ of $Z_ k$, with $|w|$ bounded below by a positive number, on which $1/4\leq \lambda \leq 3/4$.

At smooth points of $V_ k$, positivity and the two-dimensional determinant expansion give

\[ \begin{split} \det H_ v& \geq \det \bigl (\lambda H_ P+(1-\lambda )CI\bigr )\\ & \geq \lambda (1-\lambda )C\operatorname {tr}H_ P \geq c_ k b_{z\bar z}, \end{split} \]

where $c_ k>0$ may depend on $k$ and $C$. Here $P_{z\bar z}=b^{-1}+|w|^2b_{z\bar z}$. With $\zeta =z-a_ k$, shrink the first factor of $V_ k$ so that

\[ b_{z\bar z}=|\zeta |^{2\alpha _ k}\mathrm{e}^{H_ k} \left|\frac{\alpha _ k}{\zeta }+\partial _ zH_ k\right|^2 \geq c'_ k|\zeta |^{2\alpha _ k-2} \]

for some $c'_ k>0$. Since $F=C^{-2}\det H_ v$, this lower bound implies $F\notin L^ p$ whenever $p(1-\alpha _ k)\geq 1$: the $w$-disk has positive area and the radial integral in $z$ contains

\[ \int _0^{\delta _ k}r^{1-2p(1-\alpha _ k)}\, \mathrm{d}r=+\infty . \]

For each $p>1$ such a $k$ exists because $\alpha _ k\downarrow 0$. $\square$

The bounded-product capacity estimate in section 1.6 also holds for the discontinuous potential $P$. Thus a first-order capacity bound, even together with a constant positive density, cannot supply the missing boundary control.

Example 14.20.5. On the bidisk in $\mathbb C^2$ with coordinates $(z,w)$, the function

\[ H(z,w)=2|z|(1+|w|^2) \]

is locally Lipschitz, plurisubharmonic and not $C^1$ along $\{ z=0\} $. Plurisubharmonicity follows by writing its two summands as convex exponential compositions of $\log |z|$ and $\log |z|+\log |w|^2$. At $z\ne 0$,

\[ H_{z\bar z}=\frac{1+|w|^2}{2|z|},\qquad H_{w\bar w}=2|z|,\qquad |H_{z\bar w}|^2=|w|^2. \]

Hence $\det (H_{j\bar k})=1$, and pluripolar nullity gives $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^2=2\pi ^{-2}\, \mathrm{d}\Lambda $ on the entire bidisk.

The last example rules out a purely local implication from Hölder continuity and a smooth positive density to smoothness; being local, it says nothing about the global hypotheses of theorem 14.17.1. Nor does theorem 14.20.4 contradict that theorem: there the solution is not locally Hölder continuous on the ball $\Omega $, and its density is not smooth on the rest of $X$, which is the regular locus of the barrier $\rho =0$. The trace criterion of theorem 14.20.1 avoids both global hypotheses by identifying the bounded solution with a smooth Dirichlet solution on each ball.
