26.20 Current tests for smooth positivity509A5021C77D

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.20 Current tests for smooth positivity

Lamari's duality, theorem 4.3.1, characterizes pseudoeffective backgrounds by their pairings with Gauduchon metrics. Smooth positivity, and hence nefness, is characterized in the same way by pairing with all positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-currents. By the Hahn–Banach theorem, the minimum of these pairings over normalized currents is exactly the largest $a$ such that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>ah$ for a smooth function $u$, and the nef boundary is where this minimum vanishes. The full set of currents is needed here: the closure of the smooth tests used in Lamari's duality detects pseudoeffectivity and bigness instead. The minimizing currents at the nef boundary appear again at the maximal time of the Chern–Ricci flow, and positive variational volume forces their determinant-weighted measures to vanish.

Let $X$ again be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. Fix a Hermitian metric $h$, and let $\mathscr G_ h$ be the set of normalized Gauduchon currents of section 4.4, that is, positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-currents $T$ with $\int _ Xh\wedge T=1$. This set is weakly compact for $n\geq 2$. For $n=1$, elliptic regularity and the maximum principle give $\mathscr G_ h=\{ 1/\int _ Xh\} $, so compactness holds in this case as well. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $, define

\[ m_ h(\theta )=\min _{T\in \mathscr G_ h}\int _ X\theta \wedge T. \]

The minimum exists by weak compactness, and it is finite since $-Ch\leq \theta \leq Ch$ for some constant $C$.

Theorem 26.20.1. The background $\theta $ has a smooth positive representative if and only if $m_ h(\theta )>0$. Moreover, $\theta $ is nef if and only if $m_ h(\theta )\geq 0$, and

\[ m_ h(\theta )=\sup \{ a\in \mathbb R:\ \exists u\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R), \ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>ah\} . \]

Proof. If $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq ah$ with $a>0$ and $u$ smooth, then integration by parts gives $\int _ X\theta \wedge T\geq a$ for every $T\in \mathscr G_ h$. Since a smooth positive representative satisfies such a bound for some $a>0$ by compactness, this proves one implication.

For the converse, work in the real Fréchet space of smooth real $(1,1)$-forms. Let $\mathcal K$ be its open convex cone of strictly positive forms, and let $\mathcal L=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. If the affine subspace $\theta +\mathcal L$ is disjoint from $\mathcal K$, the Hahn–Banach separation theorem for an open convex set and a disjoint affine subspace gives a nonzero continuous real linear functional $T$ with

\[ T(\theta +\ell )\leq T(\eta )\qquad (\ell \in \mathcal L,\ \eta \in \mathcal K). \]

Replacing $\ell $ by arbitrary real multiples shows that $T(\mathcal L)=0$. Scaling a fixed $\eta \in \mathcal K$ to zero shows that $T(\theta )\leq 0$, while scaling it to infinity shows that $T(\eta )\geq 0$. Since every smooth semipositive form is a limit of strictly positive forms, $T$ is a positive current, and positivity makes it a current of order zero. The equality $T(\mathcal L)=0$ says exactly that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$. Since $T\ne 0$ and $h>0$, the trace $T(h)$ is positive, and normalizing $T$ gives an element of $\mathscr G_ h$ with nonpositive pairing against $\theta $. This contradicts $m_ h(\theta )>0$ and proves the converse.

Applying the equivalence just proved to $\theta -ah$, and using $m_ h(\theta -ah)=m_ h(\theta )-a$, shows that the set in the supremum is exactly $(-\infty ,m_ h(\theta ))$. Finally, $\theta $ is nef if and only if $\theta +\varepsilon h$ has a smooth positive representative for every $\varepsilon >0$, that is, if and only if $m_ h(\theta )+\varepsilon >0$ for all such $\varepsilon $, or equivalently $m_ h(\theta )\geq 0$. $\square$

For $n\geq 2$, let $\mathscr C_ h\subseteq \mathscr G_ h$ be the weak closure of the smooth strictly positive normalized Gauduchon forms, and let $\kappa _ h(\theta )$ be the linear positivity threshold of corollary 4.3.2, which is the minimum of $\int _ X\theta \wedge T$ over $T\in \mathscr C_ h$, by section 4.4. The inclusion $\mathscr C_ h\subseteq \mathscr G_ h$ gives $m_ h(\theta )\leq \kappa _ h(\theta )$. The larger test set detects smooth positivity and nefness, whereas the weak closure of the smooth tests detects the existence of positive and of strictly positive currents in the class, that is, pseudoeffectivity and bigness. The inequality can be strict, as example 28.8.2 shows.

Proposition 26.20.2. Let $\theta $ be nef with $\lambda _\mu (\theta )>0$. If $T\in \mathscr G_ h$ satisfies $\int _ X\theta \wedge T=0$, then the determinant-weighted measure of $T$, computed as in (26.39) with reference metric $h$ and with $\mu $ in place of $\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV$, vanishes.

Proof. In dimension one the hypotheses cannot hold, since $\int _ X\theta \wedge T=\lambda _\mu (\theta )/\int _ Xh>0$. Suppose $n\geq 2$, and write $D_\mu (T)$ for the total mass of this determinant-weighted measure; for $T=\gamma ^{n-1}$ it is the denominator $D_\mu (\gamma )$ of definition 26.1.1. If $\eta \in \mathscr P$, its solution form $\Omega $ satisfies $\Omega ^ n=\lambda _\mu (\eta )^ n\mu $, so $\Omega /\lambda _\mu (\eta )$ is an admissible test in the infimum of lemma 26.6.1. Together with Stokes' theorem, this gives

\[ \int _ X\eta \wedge T=\int _ X\Omega \wedge T \geq \lambda _\mu (\eta )D_\mu (T). \]

Apply this to $\eta =\theta +\varepsilon h$. The left side equals $\varepsilon $, while theorem 26.10.1 gives $\lambda _\mu (\eta )\to \lambda _\mu (\theta )>0$. Hence $D_\mu (T)=0$. The determinant-weighted measure is positive and has total mass zero, so it vanishes. $\square$

Thus, at a nef background of positive volume, every annihilating current in $\mathscr G_ h$ has a coefficient matrix of deficient rank almost everywhere with respect to its trace measure. This alone does not force the trace measure to be supported on an analytic subset.
