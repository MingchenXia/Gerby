Trace inequalities at the subsolution threshold

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.9.3 Trace inequalities at the subsolution threshold

Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero with $b=b_\omega (f)>0$. Recall from section 14.27 that a threshold subsolution is a function $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V$, and a threshold solution is one for which equality holds; threshold subsolutions exist by theorem 14.27.2. A smooth Gauduchon test can be paired with the full current $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ of a threshold subsolution, and by lemma 6.4.2 the resulting trace measure is bounded below by the determinant-weighted density. The nonnegative difference has a mass that depends only on the test. Along a maximizing sequence this mass tends to zero, uniformly over all threshold subsolutions. We write $\| \cdot \| _{\mathrm{TV}}$ for the total variation of a finite signed measure.

Proposition 26.9.5. Let $0\leq f\in L^1$ be nonzero and $b=b_\omega (f)>0$, and write $p=f^{1/n}$. Suppose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V$. For every smooth strictly positive $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, the measure

\[ R_{u,T}=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T-b^{1/n}p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \]

is positive and has mass

\[ R_{u,T}(X)=1-b^{1/n}D_ p(T). \]

In particular, if $(T_ j)$ is a sequence of smooth strictly positive members of $\mathscr G_\omega $ with $D_ p(T_ j)\to \rho _\omega (p)$, then $\| R_{u,T_ j}\| _{\mathrm{TV}}\to 0$, uniformly over all threshold subsolutions $u$.

If also $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$ and $T_ j\to T$ as currents, then $T$ maximizes $D_ p$ and

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T_ j \rightharpoonup b^{1/n}p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

In particular, for a fixed sequence $(T_ j)$ with limit $T$, this limiting probability is the same for all threshold subsolutions $u$.

Proof. A smooth strictly positive $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ is $\gamma ^{n-1}$ for a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $ normalized by $\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=1$. Lemma 6.4.2 proves positivity of $R_{u,T}$, and Stokes' theorem for currents gives $((\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T)(X)=\int _ X\omega \wedge T=1$. This proves the mass formula. Since $\rho _\omega (p)=b^{-1/n}$, the nonnegative mass tends to zero along a maximizing sequence; it equals the total variation norm and does not depend on $u$.

Under the tail hypothesis, theorem 26.9.1 gives uniform integrability of the densities $p k_{T_ j}$. Thus proposition 26.9.3 makes $T$ a maximizer and gives $p k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V\rightharpoonup p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$. Adding the error measures $R_{u,T_ j}$, which tend to zero in total variation, proves the limit formula. $\square$

The limit formula concerns smooth maximizing sequences. It does not define the product $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T$ for a singular current $T$, nor does it show that the limit is the same for different maximizing currents.

If a maximizing test is itself a smooth strictly positive form, the trace inequality becomes an equality of currents, and since $n\geq 2$ this forces an exact smooth equation. The local regularity needed for this comes from lemma 1.1.4.

Corollary 26.9.6. Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero with $b=b_\omega (f)>0$. If $D_{f^{1/n}}$ has a smooth strictly positive maximizer $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, then $f^{1/n}$ agrees almost everywhere with a smooth nonnegative function, and every threshold subsolution $u$ is smooth and satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\omega (u)=bf\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

In particular a smooth threshold solution exists, and any two threshold subsolutions differ by a constant.

Proof. Let $u$ be any threshold subsolution; one exists by theorem 14.27.2. Write $T=\gamma ^{n-1}$, where $\gamma $ is the smooth Gauduchon metric given by the positive $(n-1)$st root of $T$, and set

\[ a=\left(\frac{bf\, \mathrm{d}V}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}. \]

Put $A=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$. Since $T$ maximizes, the positive measure $R_{u,T}$ of proposition 26.9.5 has mass zero, which gives the equality

\[ A\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=a\gamma ^ n. \]

The coefficient measures of the positive current $A$ are controlled by this trace measure, which is absolutely continuous. Thus $A$ has a matrix density that is positive semidefinite almost everywhere, with coefficients in $L^ n$ because $a\in L^ n$.

For every smooth Hermitian form $\beta $, lemma 6.4.2 also gives

\[ A\wedge \beta ^{n-1} \geq \left(\frac{bf\, \mathrm{d}V}{\beta ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\beta ^ n. \]

The resulting pointwise matrix inequalities hold almost everywhere, simultaneously for all positive definite matrices: on each coordinate chart, first use a countable dense family of constant positive matrices, realized on smaller charts by global smooth Hermitian forms, and then use continuity in the matrix. The variational formula for $(\det P)^{1/n}$ in the proof of lemma 26.6.1 therefore yields

\[ \left(\frac{A^ n}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\geq a \quad \text{almost everywhere}. \]

Here $A^ n$ denotes only the determinant of the measurable coefficient matrix of $A$; no nonlinear product of currents is used. The trace equality gives the reverse inequality, by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality. Equality then forces all eigenvalues of $A$ relative to $\gamma $ to equal $a$, also where $a=0$. Consequently $A=a\gamma $ as currents.

By lemma 1.1.4, applied with $\theta =\omega $, both $a$ and $u$ are smooth, and $f^{1/n}=b^{-1/n}a(\gamma ^ n/\mathrm{d}V)^{1/n}$ has a smooth nonnegative representative. Since $u$ is smooth, we may now take the top power: $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=a^ n\gamma ^ n=bf\, \mathrm{d}V$. Finally, the function $a$ was defined from $f$, $b$ and $\gamma $ alone, so two threshold subsolutions $u_1,u_2$ satisfy $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1=a\gamma =\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2$. Their smooth difference is therefore pluriharmonic, hence constant on the connected compact manifold $X$. $\square$

If $f$ is smooth and strictly positive, theorem 26.1.3 provides a smooth strictly positive maximizing Gauduchon form, obtained from the smooth equation. The corollary therefore applies, and shows in particular that all threshold subsolutions, bounded or not, differ from the smooth solution by constants.

Corollary 26.9.7. Under the hypotheses of corollary 26.9.6, let $u$ be the threshold solution with $\sup _ Xu=0$. If $u_\lambda $ is the unique bounded solution of

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\omega (u_\lambda )=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad \lambda >0, \]

then $u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda \to u$ uniformly as $\lambda \downarrow 0$.

Proof. By corollary 26.9.6, $f$ agrees almost everywhere with a smooth function, so it belongs to $L^ p$ for every finite $p$. By proposition 14.27.4, the constant $c$ for which $\mathrm{MA}_\omega (v)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$ has a bounded solution is $b_\omega (f)$. Thus proposition 14.19.1, with the trivial symmetry group, shows that the normalized family $u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda $ is uniformly precompact as $\lambda \downarrow 0$ and that every cluster limit is a normalized solution with this constant. Each cluster limit is therefore a normalized threshold subsolution and equals $u$ by corollary 26.9.6. If the whole family did not converge uniformly, a sequence staying at a fixed positive uniform distance from $u$ would have a uniformly convergent subsequence, a contradiction. $\square$

Corollary 26.9.8. Suppose $\omega $ is Kähler. For a nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, the functional $D_{f^{1/n}}$ has a smooth strictly positive maximizer in $\mathscr G_\omega $ if and only if $f$ agrees almost everywhere with a smooth strictly positive function.

Proof. The Kähler formula in proposition 14.27.4 gives $b_\omega (f)>0$. If a smooth strictly positive maximizer exists, the proof of corollary 26.9.6 gives

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=a\gamma , \qquad a\geq 0, \]

with $a,u$ smooth and $a\not\equiv 0$. Since $\mathrm{d}\omega =0$, the first-order equation in lemma 1.1.4 becomes $\mathrm{d}a=-aC$ for a smooth one-form $C$. Along any smooth path $\sigma $, this is the linear scalar equation

\[ (a\circ \sigma )'=-(a\circ \sigma )C(\dot\sigma ). \]

whose solution is the initial value multiplied by a positive exponential. Since $X$ is connected and $a\not\equiv 0$, it follows that $a>0$ everywhere, and $f=b_\omega (f)^{-1}a^ n\gamma ^ n/\mathrm{d}V$ is smooth and strictly positive. Conversely, if $f$ is smooth and strictly positive, the smooth equation and theorem 26.1.3 provide the maximizing Gauduchon form. $\square$

For a nonclosed background, the strict positivity of $f$ in corollary 26.9.8 fails, on every compact complex manifold of dimension at least two, including Kähler manifolds.

Example 26.9.9. On every connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n\geq 2$, with a fixed smooth strictly positive probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$, there is a nonclosed Hermitian form $\omega $ and a nonzero smooth nonnegative density $f$ vanishing on an open set such that $b_\omega (f)=1$ and $D_{f^{1/n}}$ has a smooth strictly positive maximizer.

Proof. Fix a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $ and a point $x$. Choose a smooth real function $\psi $ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi \leq -\gamma $ on a coordinate neighborhood $U$ of $x$; in holomorphic coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots ,z_ n)$ centered at $x$, such a function is obtained by multiplying $-M|z|^2$, with $M$ large enough, by a cutoff that equals one near $x$, and then shrinking $U$.

Choose a smooth function $\chi $ with $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ that vanishes on a smaller neighborhood of $x$ and equals one outside $U$. Put

\[ a=L\chi ,\qquad \omega =a\gamma -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi . \]

Since $X$ is compact, $\omega $ is strictly positive for sufficiently large $L>0$: on $U$ the term $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi $ is at least $\gamma $, and outside $U$ the term $L\gamma $ dominates $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi $. Set $f=a^ n\gamma ^ n/\mathrm{d}V$. The smooth function $\psi $ satisfies $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =a\gamma \geq 0$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\omega (\psi )=f\, \mathrm{d}V$, so $b_\omega (f)\geq 1$.

Stokes' theorem and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\gamma ^{n-1}=0$ give

\[ \int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =\int _ Xa\gamma ^ n =\int _ X(f\, \mathrm{d}V/\gamma ^ n)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n. \]

Testing (14.104) with $\gamma $ therefore gives the opposite inequality $b_\omega (f)\leq 1$. Hence $b_\omega (f)=1$, the metric $\gamma $ attains the infimum, and the normalized current $\gamma ^{n-1}/\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ is a smooth strictly positive maximizer of $D_{f^{1/n}}$. If $\omega $ were closed, corollary 26.9.8 would force $f$ to be strictly positive, contrary to its vanishing on an open set. Thus $\omega $ is nonclosed. $\square$

In example 26.9.9, corollary 26.9.6, corollary 26.9.7 show that every threshold subsolution is a constant translate of $\psi $ and that the normalized solutions of the exponential equations converge uniformly to $\psi -\sup _ X\psi $. Thus vanishing of the density on an open set does not by itself force nonuniqueness or the failure of this approximation.

The restriction $n\geq 2$ in corollary 26.9.6 is necessary. On a compact connected Riemann surface $X$ with a Hermitian form $\omega $, a normalized Gauduchon current has bidegree $(0,0)$, and the equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$ makes it a harmonic function, hence the constant $(\int _ X\omega )^{-1}$. This single smooth strictly positive current maximizes for every nonzero nonnegative $L^1$ density, which need not have a smooth representative.
