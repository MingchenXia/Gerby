Corollary 1.22.65B3D7BB20499

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Corollary 1.22.6

Corollary 1.22.6. For $v,w\in \mathrm{QPSH}(C)$, the inclusions $\mathcal I(tv)\subseteq \mathcal I(tw)$ for every $t>0$ hold if and only if

\[ \mathcal I(t(v\circ p))\subseteq \mathcal I(t(w\circ p)) \quad \text{for every }t>0. \]

In particular pullback preserves and reflects all-weight multiplier-ideal equivalence.

Proof. On a smooth curve, local modifications are isomorphisms. By theorem 1.16.3, the base inclusions are therefore equivalent to $\nu (v,s)\geq \nu (w,s)$ for every $s\in C$. Let $\pi $ be any local modification of a neighborhood in $X$ with smooth source, and let $y$ be a point of its source. In a coordinate at $s=p(\pi (y))$, the germ $p\circ \pi -s$ is nonconstant. Indeed, a modification is an isomorphism on a dense open set, and a nonconstant holomorphic map cannot be constant on an open set. Its order $e_ y$ is positive and finite. The preceding lemma gives

\[ \nu (v\circ p\circ \pi ,y)=e_ y\nu (v,s),\qquad \nu (w\circ p\circ \pi ,y)=e_ y\nu (w,s). \]

The base inequalities thus imply all inequalities upstairs, and the same valuative theorem gives the ideal inclusions. Conversely the upstairs inclusions give the inequalities for the identity modification at any $x\in p^{-1}(s)$. Dividing by $\operatorname {ord}_ x(p-s)>0$ recovers the base inequalities. Apply this argument in both directions for equality. $\square$
