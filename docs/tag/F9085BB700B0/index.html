Theorem 7.1.1F9085BB700B0

Theorem 7.1.1. Let $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. There exists a sequence $(\varphi _ j)_{j>0}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with analytic singularities such that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $;

  2. we have

    7.1
    \begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }; \end{equation}

  3. we have

    7.2
    \begin{equation} P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \end{equation}

In 1, we can in addition require that each $\theta _{\varphi _ j}$ is a Kähler current.

Moreover, if $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current, one may take the sequence in 1 to be any quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. 1 $\implies $ 2. By theorem 6.2.17, we have

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0. \]

We may therefore assume that $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n>0$ for all $j \geq 1$. It follows from proposition 3.2.15 that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ j} \]

for any $j\geq 1$. Using theorem 6.2.30, we conclude (7.1).

2 $\iff $ 3. By definition,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }= \int _ X\theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}}^ n. \]

Moreover, $\varphi -\sup _ X\varphi \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$. Thus, if 2 holds, theorem 3.1.9 gives $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$; the reverse inequality follows from proposition 3.2.13. Conversely, if 3 holds, then 2 follows from proposition 3.1.6.

3 $\implies $ 1. Note that the condition in 1 characterizes the closure of analytic singularities in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Step 1. We first assume that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. We will prove the following more general result in this case: Without assuming 3, $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ always lies in the closure of analytic singularities.

Let $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ be a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We will show that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$. Let

\[ \psi =\inf _{j\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]. \]

Since $\varphi \leq \varphi _ j$ for all $j>0$, we have

\[ \varphi -\sup _ X\varphi \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j] \]

for all $j$, and hence

\[ \varphi -\sup _ X\varphi \leq \psi . \]

In particular, $\int _ X\theta _\psi ^ n>0$.

Now proposition 6.2.4, proposition 3.1.26 and corollary 6.2.11 give

\[ \varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi . \]

Moreover, observe that $\psi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-model by proposition 3.2.18 and proposition 3.2.15. Hence, if $\varphi \sim _{\mathcal I}\psi $, then

\[ P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta [\psi ]_{\mathcal I}=\psi . \]

So it suffices to show that $\varphi \sim _{\mathcal I}\psi $. It only remains to argue that $\psi \preceq _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi $. For this purpose, take $\lambda >0$. We need to show that

\[ \mathcal{I}(\lambda \psi )\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi ). \]

By the strong openness theorem 1.4.16, we may take $\lambda '>\lambda $ such that $\mathcal{I}(\lambda \psi )=\mathcal{I}(\lambda ' \psi )$. It follows from the definition of the quasi-equisingular approximation that

\[ \mathcal{I}(\lambda '\psi )\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda '\varphi _ j)\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi ) \]

for large enough $j$, where the first inclusion uses $\psi \leq P_\theta [\varphi _ j]$ and $P_\theta [\varphi _ j]\sim _{\mathcal{I}}\varphi _ j$. Our assertion follows.

It follows from the proof that we may take $\varphi _ j$ so that $\theta _{\varphi _ j}$ is a Kähler current for all $j\geq 1$.

Step 2. We handle the general case.

Assume 3 holds. By 2.4.19, we can find $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ so that $\theta _{\psi }$ is a Kähler current and $\psi \leq \varphi $. We let

\[ \psi _ j=(1-j^{-1})\varphi +j^{-1}\psi \]

for each $j\in \mathbb {Z}_{>1}$. Then $(\psi _ j)_ j$ is an increasing sequence converging almost everywhere to $\varphi $. Then

\[ P_{\theta }[\psi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\varphi ] \]

by proposition 3.2.19, corollary 6.2.9. From Step 1, we know that each $P_{\theta }[\psi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}$ lies in the closure of analytic singularities, hence so is $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\sim _ P \varphi $. Therefore, 1 follows. $\square$
