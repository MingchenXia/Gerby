The critical exponent on a compact torus

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 28.15.1 The critical exponent on a compact torus

The exponent $2$ in corollary 28.15.2 is sharp. On a product torus, two explicit logarithmic profiles give a complete metric with $q+a+b=2$ and incomplete metrics with $q+a+b=2+\beta $ for every $\beta >0$. Both profiles have full mass, and the second one is bounded.

Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm{i}\mathbb Z)$, let $\omega _ E=(\mathrm{i}/2\pi )\, \mathrm{d}z\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z$, and put $X=E^ n$, $D=\{ 0\} \times E^{n-1}$ and $M=X\setminus D$. Write $\pi _ j:X\to E$ for the coordinate projections and $\omega _0=\sum _{j=1}^ n\pi _ j^*\omega _ E$. Choose radii $0<r_1<r_2<\mathrm{e}^{-1}$ and a smooth radial cutoff $\chi $ on the coordinate disk $\{ |z|<r_2\} \subset E$, equal to $1$ on $\{ |z|\leq r_1\} $ and to $0$ near its outer boundary, with $0\leq \chi \leq 1$. For fixed numbers $A,\eta ,\beta >0$, set $L=-\log |z|^2$ and define on $E$

28.64
\begin{equation} \label{eq:torus-length-profiles} v_{\mathrm c}=-A\chi \log L,\qquad v_\beta =\eta \chi L^{-\beta }. \end{equation}

Both functions are extended by zero outside the disk, and at its center we set $v_{\mathrm c}(0)=-\infty $ and $v_\beta (0)=0$. The bounded profile also occurs in example 14.20.2. We write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$ for the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$ of theorem 6.1.2.

Lemma 28.15.3. For $C>0$ sufficiently large, put $\theta =C\omega _0$. For either profile $v$ in (28.64), the function $u=\pi _1^*v$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the form $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is smooth and positive on $X\setminus D$, and

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\int _ X\theta ^ n. \]

The same $C$ may be used for the two fixed profiles. The bounded profile has minimal ordinary singularities; the profile $v_{\mathrm c}$ does not.

Proof. For a twice differentiable function $G$ of $L=-\log |z|^2$, differentiation away from zero gives

\[ \frac{\partial ^2G(L)}{\partial z\partial \bar z} =\frac{G''(L)}{|z|^2}. \]

On the inner disk this yields

28.65
\begin{equation} \label{eq:torus-profile-hessians} (v_{\mathrm c})_{z\bar z}=\frac{A}{|z|^2L^2},\qquad (v_\beta )_{z\bar z}=\frac{\eta \beta (\beta +1)}{|z|^2L^{\beta +2}}. \end{equation}

Both densities are integrable at zero. Both profiles are locally integrable, and their distributional Hessians have no additional atom at zero. To see this, integrate twice by parts outside $\{ |z|=\varepsilon \} $: the boundary terms against a smooth test function are bounded by constant multiples of $\varepsilon |G(-\log \varepsilon ^2)|$ and $|G'(-\log \varepsilon ^2)|$, and both tend to zero for the two profiles. Thus (28.65) holds in the sense of distributions across the center. On the cutoff annulus all derivatives are bounded. Hence, for a sufficiently large common $C$,

\[ \alpha _ v=C\omega _ E+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v \]

is a positive measure, smooth and strictly positive away from zero, with no atom at zero. Pairing $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ with the constant test function gives $\int _ E\alpha _ v=C\int _ E\omega _ E$.

Let $Y=E^{n-1}$, let $\pi _ Y:X\to Y$ be the projection onto the remaining factors, and let $\theta _ Y$ be $C$ times the sum of the $n-1$ flat forms of $Y$. On $X\setminus D$ the smooth wedge product is

\[ T^ n=n\, \pi _1^*\alpha _ v\wedge \pi _ Y^*\theta _ Y^{n-1}; \]

for $n=1$ this means just $\alpha _ v$, with $Y$ a point. The right side defines a finite product measure on $X$ that gives $D$ zero mass. By theorem 6.1.2, the non-pluripolar measure agrees with this product off $D$ and gives the pluripolar set $D$ zero mass, so the two global measures are equal. Integrating the product proves the full-mass identity. Since $\theta >0$, its envelope is $V_\theta =0$ (section 1.19). The bounded profile therefore has minimal ordinary singularities, whereas $v_{\mathrm c}$ is not bounded below by $0$ minus a constant. $\square$

We apply the preceding results with $\omega =\theta $. Choose a smooth norm on $\mathcal O_ E(0)$, the line bundle of the point $0\in E$, for which its canonical section has squared norm $|z|^2$ near zero; such a norm is obtained from any smooth norm by multiplying by a smooth positive function prescribed on a smaller disk. Pull it back to $X$ and rescale it by a constant. For some fixed $K>0$ we may then arrange

\[ R^2=\mathrm{e}^{-K}|z_1|^2,\qquad \tau =L+K \]

near $D$, and $\tau \geq 1$ on $M$. For both profiles, $E_\tau $ is exactly the tangent space of the remaining torus factors near $D$, so $\det _\theta B_\tau =1$.

Example 28.15.4. Fix $\lambda >0$ and $0<A<1/\lambda $, and take $u_{\mathrm c}=\pi _1^*v_{\mathrm c}$ with the background of lemma 28.15.3. This gives a complete metric on $M$ solving

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_{\mathrm c}) =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_{\mathrm c}}f_{\mathrm c}\theta ^ n, \qquad f_{\mathrm c}\in C^\infty (M)\cap L^1(X,\theta ^ n),\quad f_{\mathrm c}>0, \]

where the right side is extended by zero on $D$. The three exponents in (28.63) can be taken to be

\[ q=2-\lambda A>1,\qquad a=\lambda A,\qquad b=0, \]

so their sum equals the critical exponent $2$.

Indeed, define $f_{\mathrm c}=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u_{\mathrm c}}\det _\theta (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{\mathrm c})$ on $M$. On the inner disk the product structure gives

\[ f_{\mathrm c}=L^{\lambda A} +\frac{A}{C|z_1|^2L^{2-\lambda A}}. \]

The substitution $L=-\log r^2$ in the radial integral shows that the second term is integrable precisely when $\lambda A<1$. The first term is integrable as well, and the density is smooth elsewhere. The identity of measures across $D$ follows from the preceding lemma. Since $\tau =L+K$, all three bounds hold with the stated exponents, and completeness follows from corollary 28.15.2. Along a radial normal segment the divergence of the length at the critical exponent is also visible directly:

\[ \int _0^{r_1}\frac{\sqrt A}{r(-\log r^2)}\, \mathrm{d}r=\infty . \]

For arbitrary paths, however, completeness requires the corollary, not just this family of radial paths.

Example 28.15.5. Fix $\lambda ,\eta ,\beta >0$ and take $u_\beta =\pi _1^*v_\beta $ with the background of lemma 28.15.3. This is a continuous bounded solution of full mass with minimal ordinary singularities, and its metric on $M$ is incomplete, although

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_\beta )=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\beta }f_\beta \theta ^ n, \qquad f_\beta \in C^\infty (M)\cap L^1(X,\theta ^ n),\quad f_\beta >0. \]

To see this, put $K_\beta =\eta \beta (\beta +1)$ and define the density by the equation on $M$. Near $D$ it is

\[ f_\beta =\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda \eta L^{-\beta }} \left(1+\frac{K_\beta }{C|z_1|^2L^{\beta +2}}\right). \]

It is integrable, and the global identity follows as before. Fix the other coordinates and let $z_1=r\downarrow 0$ along a radial segment starting at $r_0$, with $0<r_0<r_1$. The length of this segment is at most

\[ \sqrt C\, r_0+\sqrt{K_\beta } \int _0^{r_0}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r(-\log r^2)^{1+\beta /2}} =\sqrt C\, r_0+\frac{\sqrt{K_\beta }}{\beta } (-\log r_0^2)^{-\beta /2}<\infty . \]

Points along this segment approaching $D$ form a Cauchy sequence with no limit in $M$.

Here the smallest admissible exponents in (28.63) are $q=\beta +2$, $a=0$ and $b=0$: the density is asymptotic to a positive constant times $R^{-2}\tau ^{-(\beta +2)}$, the potential is bounded, and the tangential determinant is $1$. Their sum is $2+\beta $. Since $\beta >0$ is arbitrary, the threshold $2$ in the criterion cannot be replaced by any larger universal number, even in a Kähler class with a bounded full-mass solution.

In these examples one may write $f=F/R^2$ with $F=R^2f$ smooth and positive on $M$, but $F$ does not extend smoothly and positively across $D$: the logarithmic factors make it tend to zero there. Thus the examples concern integrable divisorial poles weakened by logarithmic factors. They do not decide completeness for a nonintegrable pole with smooth positive coefficient across the divisor, nor the question of compatibility with the flow discussed in section 34.17.
