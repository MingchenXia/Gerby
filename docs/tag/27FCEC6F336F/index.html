10.1 Pluripolar sets and relative extremal functions27FCEC6F336F

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 10.1 Pluripolar sets and relative extremal functions

We define pluripolar sets on complex manifolds and then introduce, on a ball, the relative extremal functions that connect them with the Monge–Ampère capacity of section 9.3.

Definition 10.1.1. Let $X$ be a complex manifold. A subset $E\subset X$ is pluripolar, or locally pluripolar, if every point of $X$ has a connected coordinate neighborhood $U$ carrying a function $V\in \mathrm{PSH}(U)$ with $E\cap U\subset \{ V=-\infty \} $. If $X$ is connected, a subset $E\subset X$ is globally pluripolar in $X$ if there is a single $V\in \mathrm{PSH}(X)$ with $E\subset \{ V=-\infty \} $. A property holds quasi-everywhere if it holds outside a pluripolar set.

Pluripolar sets are not required to be Borel, and subsets of pluripolar sets are pluripolar. If $E\subset X$ is pluripolar and $W\subset X$ is open, then $E\cap W$ is pluripolar in $W$: restrict the functions of the definition to small coordinate balls in $W$. Every pluripolar set $E$ has Lebesgue measure zero in every chart. Indeed, a chart domain $C$ has a countable basis, so it is covered by countably many of the sets $U\cap C$, with $U$ as in the definition, and each set $E\cap U\cap C$ lies in the pole set of the corresponding function $V$. In the coordinates of the chart, $V$ is plurisubharmonic on each connected component of $U\cap C$, hence locally integrable there (theorem 8.2.1), so its pole set in $U\cap C$ is Lebesgue null. In dimension zero every plurisubharmonic function is finite, so the only pluripolar set is the empty set. From now on the complex dimension $n$ is positive.

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be open. By theorem 8.2.1, the plurisubharmonic functions in definition 10.1.1 are nontrivial subharmonic functions when $\mathbb C^ n$ is identified with $\mathbb R^{2n}$, so every pluripolar subset of $\Omega $ is real polar in the sense of section 8.3. For $n\ge 2$ the converse fails: example 10.2.3 exhibits a real polar subset of $\mathbb C^ n$ that is not pluripolar. For $n=1$ the converse holds, so that the pluripolar subsets of $\Omega $ are exactly its polar subsets in the sense of definition 3.5.1. Indeed, let $A\subset \Omega $ be polar, and let $D$ be a disc with $\overline D\subset \Omega $. By proposition 4.2.3, applied to $A\cap D$, there is a fundamental potential on $\mathbb C$ equal to $-\infty $ on $A\cap D$; it is a nontrivial subharmonic function by proposition 3.2.1, hence plurisubharmonic on $D$. Since every point of $\Omega $, whether in $A$ or not, lies in such a disc, $A$ is pluripolar.

This changes the meaning of quasi-everywhere. In definition 5.2.1 it meant outside a polar set of $\mathbb R^ d$; from now on, throughout 2, it means outside a pluripolar set, as in definition 10.1.1. Since pluripolar sets are real polar, a property that holds quasi-everywhere on $\Omega $ in the new sense also holds outside a real polar set; for $n=1$ the two notions agree, whereas for $n\ge 2$ the new notion is strictly stronger.

For the rest of this section, $B$ denotes an open ball in $\mathbb C^ n$. Recall the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$ and the outer capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*$ of definition 9.3.2; the competitors for $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$ are the functions $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $0\le v\le 1$. For a set $E\subset B$ we define

\[ h_ E(z)=\sup \{ v(z):v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B),\ v\le 0\ \text{on }B,\ v\le -1\ \text{on }E\} , \qquad H_ E=h_ E^*. \]

The functions $v$ in this supremum are called competitors for $E$; we always name the set, to distinguish them from the competitors for $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$. The constant $-1$ is a competitor for $E$, and so is the maximum of a competitor for $E$ with $-1$; hence we may restrict the supremum to competitors with values in $[-1,0]$, and $-1\le h_ E\le H_ E\le 0$. Since every competitor for $E$ is at most $-1$ on $E$, we also have $h_ E=-1$ on $E$. If $v$ is a competitor for $E$ with values in $[-1,0]$, then $v+1$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$. By theorem 8.3.2, $H_ E$ is plurisubharmonic; we call it the relative extremal function of $E$ with respect to $B$. It is the plurisubharmonic counterpart of the relative extremal function $u_ A$ of section 4.3, which is defined by the same constraints on subharmonic functions.

We shall use the following properties of these functions repeatedly. If $E\subset F\subset B$, then every competitor for $F$ is a competitor for $E$, so $h_ F\le h_ E$ and $H_ F\le H_ E$. If $B=B(a,R)$ and $\overline E$ is a compact subset of $B$, then for a sufficiently large constant $A>0$ the function $A(|z-a|^2-R^2)$ is at most $-1$ on $E$, hence it is a competitor for $E$, and

10.1
\begin{equation} A(|z-a|^2-R^2)\le h_ E\le H_ E\le 0\quad \text{on }B; \end{equation}

in particular $H_ E(z)\to 0$ as $z\to \partial B$.

Next, Choquet's lemma (lemma 3.4.1) gives competitors $w_1,w_2,\ldots $ for $E$ with $(\sup _ kw_ k)^*=H_ E$. The finite maxima $v_ j=\max \{ -1,w_1,\ldots ,w_ j\} $ are competitors for $E$, and their limit $g=\lim _ jv_ j$ satisfies $\sup _ kw_ k\le g\le h_ E$; taking regularizations, we get $g^*=H_ E$. By theorem 8.3.2, applied to the family $(v_ j)$, we have $g=g^*$ almost everywhere. Thus there are competitors $v_ j$ for $E$ with

10.2
\begin{equation} -1\le v_1\le v_2\le \cdots \le 0,\qquad \lim _ jv_ j=H_ E\quad \text{almost everywhere on }B. \end{equation}

Finally, by corollary 9.5.2, for every compact set $L\subset B$ the set $L\cap \{ h_ E<H_ E\} $ has outer capacity zero in $B$. Exhausting $B$ by countably many compact sets and using the countable subadditivity of outer capacity (proposition 9.3.3), we obtain

10.3
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(\{ h_ E<H_ E\} )=0. \end{equation}

A set $N\subset B$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(N)=0$ has outer measure zero for $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$ whenever $v$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$, since $\int _ G(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\le \operatorname {Cap}_ B(G)$ for every open set $G\supset N$.

Proposition 10.1.2. Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be an open ball, and let $K\subset B$ be compact. Then $H_ K(z)\to 0$ as $z\to \partial B$, the Monge–Ampère measure $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$ is carried by $K\cap \{ H_ K=-1\} $, and

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)=\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n. \]

If $(K_ j)$ is a decreasing sequence of compact subsets of $B$ with intersection $K$, then the functions $H_{K_ j}$ increase and converge to $H_ K$ almost everywhere, and $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K_ j)$ decreases to $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)$. In particular $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(K)=\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)$.

Proof. The boundary behavior of $H_ K$ follows from (10.1). Since $H_ K$ is bounded, its Monge–Ampère measure is defined (definition 9.1.1).

We first show that $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$ vanishes on $B\setminus K$. Choose competitors $v_ j$ for $K$ as in (10.2), and let $D$ be a ball with $\overline D\subset B\setminus K$. Let $\widehat v_ j$ be the function obtained from $v_ j$ by corollary 9.6.4: it is plurisubharmonic on $B$, equals $v_ j$ outside $D$, and satisfies $\widehat v_ j\ge v_ j$ and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widehat v_ j)^ n=0$ on $D$. Each $\widehat v_ j$ is again a competitor for $K$. Indeed, $\widehat v_ j=v_ j\le -1$ on $K$. Moreover $\widehat v_ j\le 0$: outside $D$ because $\widehat v_ j=v_ j$, and on $D$ by the maximum principle (proposition 3.1.4), since by upper semicontinuity the upper limit of $\widehat v_ j$ at each point $x\in \partial D$ is at most $\widehat v_ j(x)=v_ j(x)\le 0$.

Since $\widehat v_ j$ is increasing in $v_ j$ by corollary 9.6.4, the functions $\widehat v_ j$ increase with $j$, and $v_ j\le \widehat v_ j\le h_ K\le H_ K$ shows that $\widehat v_ j\to H_ K$ almost everywhere. They take values in $[-1,0]$, so theorem 9.5.1 gives $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widehat v_ j)^ n\to (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$ weakly, and therefore $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n=0$ on $D$. Covering the open set $B\setminus K$ by countably many such balls $D$, we conclude that $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$ is carried by $K$.

As $h_ K=-1$ on $K$, the Borel set $K\cap \{ H_ K>-1\} $ is contained in $\{ h_ K<H_ K\} $, which has outer capacity zero by (10.3). Because $H_ K+1$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$, this set is therefore null for $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(H_ K+1))^ n=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$. Thus $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$ is carried by $K\cap \{ H_ K=-1\} $.

We now prove the capacity formula. As $H_ K+1$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$ and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$ is carried by $K$, we have $\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n=\int _ K(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(H_ K+1))^ n\le \operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)$. Conversely, let $v$ be a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_ B$, let $0<\varepsilon <1/2$, and put $w=(1-2\varepsilon )(v-1)-\varepsilon $. Then $-1+\varepsilon \le w\le -\varepsilon $. In particular $w>-1$, so $K\cap \{ H_ K\ge w\} \subset K\cap \{ H_ K>-1\} $; this set has outer capacity zero and is therefore null for $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n=(1-2\varepsilon )^ n(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$. Moreover $H_ K(z)\to 0$ as $z\to \partial B$ while $w\le -\varepsilon $, so $\varliminf _{z\to x}(H_ K(z)-w(z))\ge \varepsilon >0$ for every $x\in \partial B$, and the comparison principle (theorem 9.2.8) applies to $H_ K$ and $w$ on $B$. We obtain

\[ \begin{aligned} (1-2\varepsilon )^ n\int _ K(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n & =\int _{K\cap \{ H_ K<w\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n \le \int _{\{ H_ K<w\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n\\ & \le \int _{\{ H_ K<w\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n \le \int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and taking the supremum over $v$, we get $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)\le \int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$.

Now let $(K_ j)$ be compact subsets of $B$ decreasing to $K$. Since $K\subset K_{j+1}\subset K_ j$, the functions $H_{K_ j}$ increase and are bounded above by $H_ K$. Put $H=(\sup _ jH_{K_ j})^*$. By theorem 8.3.2, $H$ is plurisubharmonic and equals $\sup _ jH_{K_ j}$ almost everywhere, and $H\le H_ K$ because $H_ K$ is upper semicontinuous. For the reverse inequality, let $v$ be a competitor for $K$ and let $0<\varepsilon <1$. The open set $G=\{ v<-1+\varepsilon \} $ contains $K$. The compact sets $K_ j\setminus G$ decrease and have empty intersection, so one of them is empty; hence $K_ j\subset G$ for all large $j$. For such $j$ the function $v/(1-\varepsilon )$ is a competitor for $K_ j$, and hence $v\le (1-\varepsilon )H_{K_ j}\le (1-\varepsilon )H$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $v\le H$, and taking the supremum over all competitors $v$ for $K$ gives $h_ K\le H$. As $H$ is upper semicontinuous, $H_ K\le H$. Therefore $H=H_ K$, and $H_{K_ j}\to H_ K$ almost everywhere.

By theorem 9.5.1, $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_{K_ j})^ n\to (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ K)^ n$ weakly. All these measures are carried by the fixed compact set $K_1$, so testing with a function in $C_ c(B)$ equal to one on $K_1$ shows that their total masses converge. By the capacity formula, $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K_ j)\to \operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)$, and this convergence is decreasing because capacity is increasing.

Finally, $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)\le \operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(K)$ because capacity is increasing. For the converse we may assume $K\neq \varnothing $, since both capacities of the empty set vanish. Choose $\delta >0$ such that the compact sets $K_ j=\{ z:\operatorname {dist}(z,K)\le \delta /j\} $ lie in $B$. They decrease to $K$, and each contains $K$ in its interior. Hence $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(K)\le \operatorname {Cap}_ B(\operatorname{Int}K_ j)\le \operatorname {Cap}_ B(K_ j)$, and the right-hand side tends to $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K)$ by what we have just proved. $\square$

Proposition 10.1.3. Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be an open ball, and let $G$ be an open set with $G\Subset B$. Then $h_ G=H_ G$, $H_ G=-1$ on $G$, $H_ G(z)\to 0$ as $z\to \partial B$, the measure $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ G)^ n$ is carried by $\overline G$, and

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_ B(G)=\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ G)^ n. \]

Proof. Choose compact sets $K_ j$ with $K_ j\subset \operatorname{Int}K_{j+1}$ and $\bigcup _ jK_ j=G$. Since the $K_ j$ increase, the functions $H_{K_ j}$ decrease. They take values in $[-1,0]$, so their limit $H$ is plurisubharmonic by theorem 8.3.1. Since $h_{K_ j}=-1$ on $K_ j$ and the upper regularization at a point only involves the values of $h_{K_ j}$ near that point, we have $H_{K_ j}=-1$ on $\operatorname{Int}K_ j$. Every point of $G$ lies in $\operatorname{Int}K_ j$ for all large $j$, so $H=-1$ on $G$. Thus $H$ is a competitor for $G$, and $H\le h_ G$. Conversely, every competitor for $G$ is a competitor for each $K_ j$, hence bounded above by every $H_{K_ j}$ and thus by $H$; so $h_ G\le H$. We have shown $h_ G=H$. As $H$ is upper semicontinuous, $H_ G=h_ G^*=H$; in particular $H_ G=-1$ on $G$. The boundary behavior follows from (10.1).

By theorem 9.2.5, $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_{K_ j})^ n\to (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ G)^ n$ weakly. By proposition 10.1.2, these measures are carried by $K_ j\subset \overline G$. Hence the limit measure vanishes on the open set $B\setminus \overline G$, that is, it is carried by the compact set $\overline G$, and testing with a function in $C_ c(B)$ equal to one on $\overline G$ shows that the total masses converge. Since the Borel sets $K_ j$ increase to $G$, proposition 9.3.3(2) gives $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(K_ j)\to \operatorname {Cap}_ B(G)$. Together with the capacity formula of proposition 10.1.2 for the $K_ j$, this yields $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G)=\lim _ j\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_{K_ j})^ n=\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ G)^ n$. $\square$

Theorem 10.1.4. Let $E$ be a subset of an open ball $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$;

  2. $E\subset \{ V=-\infty \} $ for some $V\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $V\le 0$;

  3. $H_ E=0$ on $B$.

Every pluripolar subset of $B$ satisfies these conditions; in particular it is globally pluripolar in $B$. Consequently, on every complex manifold $X$, a countable union of pluripolar sets is pluripolar, and if $W\subset X$ is open, every subset of $W$ that is pluripolar in $W$ is pluripolar in $X$.

Proof. We prove (2)$\Rightarrow $(1), then (1)$\Rightarrow $(3) for relatively compact sets $E$ and (3)$\Rightarrow $(2), deduce (1)$\Rightarrow $(2) in general by exhaustion, and finally prove (2)$\Rightarrow $(3). The remaining assertions then follow by localization.

(2)$\Rightarrow $(1). We show more generally that every pluripolar set $E\subset B$ satisfies (1); a set as in (2) is pluripolar. By definition 10.1.1, every point of $B$ has an open neighborhood $W\subset B$ carrying a plurisubharmonic function whose pole set contains $E\cap W$. The second assertion of proposition 9.3.4, applied with $D=B$, therefore gives $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E\cap L)=0$ for every compact set $L\subset B$. Exhausting $B$ by countably many compact sets and using countable subadditivity, we obtain $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$.

(1)$\Rightarrow $(3) when $E\Subset B$. Fix an open set $O$ with $\overline E\subset O\Subset B$. Since $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$, there are open sets $G_ j'\supset E$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j')<1/j$. The open sets $G_ j=O\cap G_1'\cap \cdots \cap G_ j'$ decrease, contain $E$, lie in $O$, and satisfy $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j)\to 0$. Since the $G_ j$ decrease, the functions $H_{G_ j}$ increase; put $H=(\sup _ jH_{G_ j})^*$. By theorem 8.3.2, $H$ is plurisubharmonic, takes values in $[-1,0]$, and is the almost-everywhere limit of the $H_{G_ j}$. By proposition 10.1.3, $\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_{G_ j})^ n=\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j)\to 0$, and by theorem 9.5.1 these measures converge weakly to $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n$. Hence $\int \chi \, (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=\lim _ j\int \chi \, (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_{G_ j})^ n\le \lim _ j\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j)=0$ for every $\chi \in C_ c(B)$ with $0\le \chi \le 1$, and therefore $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=0$.

Since every $G_ j$ lies in $O$, the barrier (10.1), with a constant $A$ chosen for $O$, bounds every $H_{G_ j}$, and hence $H$, from below by one function tending to zero at $\partial B$; thus $H(z)\to 0$ as $z\to \partial B$. The last assertion of the comparison principle (theorem 9.2.8), applied to $u=H$ and $v=0$, gives $H\ge 0$, and therefore $H=0$. Finally, $E\subset G_ j$ gives $H_{G_ j}\le H_ E$ for all $j$; since $H_ E$ is upper semicontinuous, $0=H\le H_ E\le 0$.

(3)$\Rightarrow $(2). Suppose $H_ E=0$, and choose competitors $v_ j$ for $E$ as in (10.2). Fix a point $a\in B$ with $v_ j(a)\to 0$, and choose indices $j_1<j_2<\cdots $ with $v_{j_ l}(a)>-2^{-l}$. Since every term is nonpositive, the partial sums of $V=\sum _ lv_{j_ l}$ form a decreasing sequence of nonpositive plurisubharmonic functions, and $V(a)\ge -1$. By theorem 8.3.1, $V$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$; moreover $V\le 0$, and $V=-\infty $ on $E$ because every term is at most $-1$ there.

(1)$\Rightarrow $(2) in general. Let $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$, and let $(L_ l)$ be an exhaustion of $B$ by compact sets. Each set $E\cap L_ l$ has outer capacity zero and is relatively compact in $B$, so the two preceding steps give $V_ l\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $V_ l\le 0$ and $E\cap L_ l\subset \{ V_ l=-\infty \} $. The pole sets of the $V_ l$ are Lebesgue null, so there is a point $a\in B$ at which all $V_ l$ are finite. Choose constants $c_ l>0$ with $c_ l|V_ l(a)|\le 2^{-l}$. The partial sums of $\sum _ lc_ lV_ l$ decrease and are at least $-1$ at $a$, so, as in the previous step, $V=\sum _ lc_ lV_ l$ is plurisubharmonic by theorem 8.3.1, and it is nonpositive. Every point of $E$ lies in some $L_ l$, where $c_ lV_ l=-\infty $ while all other terms are nonpositive; hence $V=-\infty $ on $E$.

(2)$\Rightarrow $(3). If $V$ is as in (2), then $\varepsilon V$ is a competitor for $E$ for every $\varepsilon >0$, so $\varepsilon V\le h_ E\le 0$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, we get $h_ E=0$ on the set $\{ V>-\infty \} $. This set has full measure and is therefore dense in $B$, so every neighborhood of a point contains points where $h_ E=0$. Hence $H_ E=h_ E^*\ge 0$, and $H_ E=0$. This completes the proof of the equivalences.

By the proof of (2)$\Rightarrow $(1), every pluripolar subset of $B$ satisfies (1), and hence all three conditions; in particular, it is globally pluripolar in $B$. Now let $X$ be a complex manifold and $E=\bigcup _ mE_ m$, where each $E_ m\subset X$ is pluripolar. Let $x\in X$, and let $B$ be a coordinate ball around $x$, identified with an open ball in $\mathbb C^ n$. Each $E_ m\cap B$ is pluripolar in $B$, so $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E_ m\cap B)=0$ for every $m$, and $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E\cap B)=0$ by countable subadditivity. By (1)$\Rightarrow $(2), the set $E\cap B$ lies in the pole set of a plurisubharmonic function on the connected coordinate neighborhood $B$ of $x$. Hence $E$ is pluripolar.

Finally, let $W\subset X$ be open and let $E\subset W$ be pluripolar in $W$. That $E$ is pluripolar in $X$ is not immediate from definition 10.1.1 at the boundary points of $W$, where a single plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood must have poles at all nearby points of $E$. Let $x\in X$, and let $U$ be a coordinate ball around $x$, identified with an open ball in $\mathbb C^ n$. The open set $W\cap U$ is a countable union of balls $D_ k$ with $\overline{D_ k}\subset W\cap U$. Each set $E\cap D_ k$ is pluripolar in $U$: at points of $\overline{D_ k}\subset W$ we may use, after restriction to small balls in $U$, the functions provided by the pluripolarity of $E$ in $W$, and every point of $U\setminus \overline{D_ k}$ has a neighborhood disjoint from $D_ k$. By the previous paragraph, applied in $U$, the countable union $E\cap U=\bigcup _ k(E\cap D_ k)$ is pluripolar in $U$, and hence, by the first part of the theorem, it lies in the pole set of a plurisubharmonic function on $U$. Thus $E$ is pluripolar in $X$. $\square$
