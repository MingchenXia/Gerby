16.9.26 Convergence theorems0BF3C2E4260B

Next we establish some important convergence theorems, allowing us to effectively manipulate the $d_ S$-convergence.

Lemma 16.9.27.51EA136E15FA Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Then for any $t\in (0,1]$,

\[ (1-t)\varphi _ i+ tV_{\theta }\xrightarrow {d_ S}(1-t)\varphi + tV_{\theta }. \]
When $t=1$, the sum is understood as in 13.13.6.

Proof. Fix $t\in (0,1]$. We write

\[ \varphi _{i,t}=(1-t)\varphi _ i+ tV_{\theta },\quad \varphi _ t=(1-t)\varphi + tV_{\theta } \]

for any $i\in I$.

By corollary 16.9.9, it suffices to show that for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$,

D81A5AAFDA65 16.9.28
\begin{equation} 2\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _{i,t}\lor \varphi _ t}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _{i,t}}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\to 0. \end{equation}

Observe that

\[ \varphi _{i,t}\lor \varphi _ t=(1-t)(\varphi \lor \varphi _ i)+t V_{\theta }. \]

So after binomial expansion, (16.9.28) follows from corollary 16.9.9. $\square$

Lemma 16.9.29.2E78B4FEFFF9 Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For each $t\in (0,1)$, let $\varphi _ t=(1-t)\varphi +t V_{\theta }$. Then

\[ \varphi _ t\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \]

as $t\to 0+$.

Proof. Since $\varphi \preceq \varphi _ t$, by 16.9.7, we need to show that for each $j=1,\dots ,n$, we have

\[ \lim _{t\to 0+} \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \]

For this purpose, we compute

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}- \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\\ =& \left((1-t)^ j-1\right)\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\\ & +\sum _{i=0}^{j-1} \binom {j}{i}(1-t)^ i t^{j-i}\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ i\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-i}. \end{aligned} \]

As $t\to 0+$, the right-hand side clearly tends to $0$. $\square$

The following convergence theorem lies at the heart of the whole theory.

Theorem 16.9.30.713B9E26113B Let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ be smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ representing big cohomology classes. Suppose that $(\varphi _ j^ k)_{k\in I}$ are nets in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ and $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ for $j=1,\ldots ,n$. We assume that $\varphi _ j^ k\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi _ j$ for each $j=1,\ldots ,n$. Then

3F8CB8C5B9A5 16.9.31
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\in I}\int _ X\theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}=\int _ X\theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}. \end{equation}

Proof. Since $d_ S$ is a pseudometric, in order to establish the continuity of mixed masses, it suffices to consider sequences instead of nets. So we may assume that $I=\mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ as ordered sets.

Step 1. We reduce to the case where $\varphi _ j^ k,\varphi _ j$ all have positive masses and there is a constant $\delta >0$ such that for all $j=1,\ldots ,n$ and $k\in I$,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{j,\varphi _ j^ k}^ n>\delta . \]

Assuming in this case, the theorem has been proved, let us handle the general case. Take $t\in (0,1)$. By 16.9.27, we have

\[ (1-t)\varphi _ j^ k+tV_{\theta _ j}\xrightarrow {d_ S}(1-t)\varphi _ j+tV_{\theta _ j} \]

as $k\to \infty $ for each $j=1,\ldots ,n$. Note that for all $k\in I$,

\[ \int _ X \theta ^ n_{j,(1-t)\varphi _ j^ k+tV_{\theta _ j}}\geq t^ n\int _ X \theta _{V_{\theta _ j}}^ n>0. \]

Assume that we have proved the special case of the theorem, we have

\[ \begin{split} & \lim _{k\to \infty }\int _ X\theta _{1,(1-t)\varphi _1^ k+tV_{\theta _1}}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,(1-t)\varphi _ n^ k+tV_{\theta _ n}}\\ =& \int _ X\theta _{1,(1-t)\varphi _1+tV_{\theta _1}}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,(1-t)\varphi _ n+tV_{\theta _ n}}. \end{split} \]

More explicitly, for fixed $k$ the left-hand side is the value at $t$ of a polynomial $Q_ k(t)$ of degree at most $n$, and the right-hand side is the value of a polynomial $Q(t)$ of degree at most $n$. Choose $n+1$ distinct numbers $t_0,\ldots ,t_ n\in (0,1)$. Lagrange interpolation expresses $Q_ k(0)-Q(0)$ as a fixed linear combination of the numbers $Q_ k(t_ a)-Q(t_ a)$, which tend to $0$. Hence the same formula holds at $t=0$. From this, (16.9.31) follows.

Step 2. We now assume that the assumptions mentioned at the beginning of Step 1 are met. Note that we may assume that $\varphi _ j^ k$, $\varphi _ j$ are model potentials for all $j=1,\ldots ,n$, $k>0$ by proposition 16.9.6 and corollary 16.3.20.

We first handle the following monotone situation. Assume that, for each fixed $i$, the sequence $(\varphi _ i^ k)_ k$ is either increasing or decreasing. Let $\tilde{\varphi }_ i$ denote its pointwise limit, namely

\[ \tilde{\varphi }_ i=\inf _{k>0}\varphi _ i^ k \]

in the decreasing case and

\[ \tilde{\varphi }_ i=\operatorname *{sup}_{k>0}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i^ k \]

in the increasing case. We assume moreover that $\tilde{\varphi }_ i\sim _ P\varphi _ i$ for every $i$. Since mixed non-pluripolar masses are invariant under replacing each entry by a $P$-equivalent potential, it suffices in this monotone situation to prove convergence to the mixed mass of the $\tilde{\varphi }_ i$'s.

After reordering the indices, we may assume that there is $i_0\in \{ 0,\ldots ,n\} $ such that $(\varphi _ i^ k)_ k$ is decreasing for $i\leq i_0$ and increasing for $i>i_0$. Therefore, for each $k>0$, using theorem 13.13.11, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\geq \int _ X \theta _{1,\tilde{\varphi }_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{i_0,\tilde{\varphi }_{i_0}}\wedge \theta _{i_0+1,\varphi ^ k_{i_0+1}}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}. \]

Using corollary 13.13.12, we therefore conclude that

\[ \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\geq \int _ X \theta _{1,\tilde{\varphi }_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\tilde{\varphi }_ n}. \]

It remains to prove

DDD39F87B553 16.9.32
\begin{equation} \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\leq \int _ X \theta _{1,\tilde{\varphi }_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\tilde{\varphi }_ n}. \end{equation}

By theorem 13.13.11, for each $k>0$, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\leq \int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{i_0,\varphi _{i_0}^ k}\wedge \theta _{i_0+1,\tilde{\varphi }_{i_0+1}}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\tilde{\varphi }_{n}}. \]

When proving (16.9.32), we may replace $\varphi _ j^ k$ by $\tilde{\varphi }_ j$ whenever $j>i_0$, $k>0$. Thus, we are reduced to the case where for all $i$, $(\varphi _ i^ k)_ k$ is decreasing.

By proposition 16.4.4, the masses $\int _ X\theta _{i,\varphi _ i^ k}^ n$ decrease to $\int _ X\theta _{i,\tilde{\varphi }_ i}^ n$. Hence, thanks to 13.13.30, for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$, we may take an increasing sequence $(b^ k_ i)_ k$ tending to $\infty $ satisfying

\[ b_ i^ k\in \left(1, \left( \frac{\int _ X \theta _{i,\varphi _ i^ k}^ n}{\int _ X \theta _{i,\varphi _ i^ k}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{i,\tilde{\varphi }_ i}^ n} \right)^{1/n} \right) \]

where the upper endpoint is understood as $\infty $ if the denominator is $0$. For each such $b_ i^ k$, 13.13.30 gives $\rho _ i^ k\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ with

\[ (b^ k_ i)^{-1}\rho _ i^ k+\left(1-(b^ k_ i)^{-1}\right)\varphi _ i^ k\leq \tilde{\varphi }_ i. \]

Then by theorem 13.13.11 again,

\[ \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(1-(b^ k_ i)^{-1}\right)\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k}\leq \int _ X \theta _{1,\tilde{\varphi }_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\tilde{\varphi }_ n}. \]

Letting $k\to \infty $, we conclude (16.9.32). This proves the monotone situation.

We now return to the general case. It suffices to prove that any subsequence of

\[ \int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k} \]

has a further subsequence converging to the desired limit. Starting with an arbitrary subsequence, we pass to a further subsequence, still indexed by $k$, so that the construction in proposition 16.9.17 applies simultaneously to the finitely many sequences $(\varphi _ i^ k)_ k$, $i=1,\ldots ,n$.

Thus, after discarding finitely many terms and relabeling, for each $i$ there are model potentials $\eta _ i^ k,\psi _ i^ k\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ such that $(\eta _ i^ k)_ k$ is increasing, $(\psi _ i^ k)_ k$ is decreasing, and

\[ \eta _ i^ k\leq \varphi _ i^ k\leq \psi _ i^ k,\quad \eta _ i^ k\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi _ i,\quad \psi _ i^ k\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi _ i. \]

For the upper sequence, we take $\psi _ i^ k=P_{\theta _ i}[\operatorname *{sup}_{\ell \geq k}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i^\ell ]$; as in (16.9.21), this gives $\inf _ k\psi _ i^ k=\varphi _ i$. For the lower sequence, the construction in proposition 16.9.17 together with proposition 16.4.3 shows that the $\eta _ i^ k$'s are model after discarding finitely many terms, and the proof of proposition 16.9.17 shows that their increasing limit is $P$-equivalent to $\varphi _ i$.

By theorem 13.13.11, for every $k$,

\[ \int _ X\theta _{1,\eta _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\eta _ n^ k} \leq \int _ X\theta _{1,\varphi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n^ k} \leq \int _ X\theta _{1,\psi _1^ k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\psi _ n^ k}. \]

The monotone situation just proved applies to both the lower and upper sequences, and both limiting mixed masses are equal to

\[ \int _ X\theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}. \]

The squeeze theorem therefore gives the desired convergence along this further subsequence. This concludes the proof. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.33.6C532715D3CD Suppose that $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. 28F7794ED0EE

    $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $;

  2. CE229E338F57

    $\varphi _ i\lor \varphi \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $ and

    F98DF8D2E20D 16.9.34
    \begin{equation} \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j} \end{equation}

    for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$;

  3. 90CB70DBE452

    for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$, (16.9.34) holds and

    217CFC062068 16.9.35
    \begin{equation} \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \end{equation}
The corollary allows us to reduce a number of convergence problems related to $d_ S$ to the case $\varphi _ i\geq \varphi $. This is the most handy way of establishing $d_ S$-convergence in practice.

Proof. The equivalence between 2 and 3 follows directly from 16.9.7.

1 $\implies $ 2. That $\varphi _ i\lor \varphi \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $ follows from corollary 16.9.9. While (16.9.34) follows from theorem 16.9.30.

2 $\implies $ 1. By (16.9.10), we need to show that for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$, we have

\[ 2\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\to 0. \]

This follows from theorem 16.9.30 and (16.9.34). $\square$

Corollary 16.9.36.3EB846B21817 Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let $\omega $ be a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. 9F20E828AA4A

    $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_{S,\theta }}\varphi $;

  2. 952942BD613A

    $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_{S,\theta +\omega }}\varphi $.

In particular, there is no risk when we simply write $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Similarly, consider two nets $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$, $(\psi _ i)_{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. If both admit $d_ S$-limits in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, then we can write $d_ S(\varphi _ i,\psi _ i)\to 0$ without ambiguity.

Proof. 1 $\implies $ 2. It suffices to show that for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$, we have

\[ \begin{split} 2\int _ X (\theta +\omega )_{\varphi _ i\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge (\theta +\omega )_{V_{\theta +\omega }}^{n-j}-\int _ X (\theta +\omega )_{\varphi _ i}^ j\wedge (\theta +\omega )_{V_{\theta +\omega }}^{n-j}\\ -\int _ X (\theta +\omega )_{\varphi }^ j\wedge (\theta +\omega )_{V_{\theta +\omega }}^{n-j}\to 0. \end{split} \]

Note that this quantity is a linear combination of terms of the following form:

\[ \begin{split} 2\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i\lor \varphi }^ r\wedge \omega ^{j-r}\wedge (\theta +\omega )_{V_{\theta +\omega }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ r\wedge \omega ^{j-r}\wedge (\theta +\omega )_{V_{\theta +\omega }}^{n-j}\\ -\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ r\wedge \omega ^{j-r}\wedge (\theta +\omega )_{V_{\theta +\omega }}^{n-j}, \end{split} \]

where $r=0,\ldots ,j$. By theorem 16.9.30, it suffices to show that $\varphi \lor \varphi _ i \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. But this follows from corollary 16.9.33.

2 $\implies $ 1. From the direction we already proved, for each $C\geq 1$, we have

\[ \varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_{S,\theta +C\omega }}\varphi . \]

By theorem 16.9.30, it follows that

\[ \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X (\theta +C\omega )_{\varphi _ i}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X (\theta +C\omega )_{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j} \]

for all $j=0,\ldots ,n$. It follows that

B509AA3C2576 16.9.37
\begin{equation} \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \end{equation}

By corollary 16.9.33, it remains to show that $\varphi _ i\lor \varphi \xrightarrow {d_{S,\theta }}\varphi $. By corollary 16.9.33 again, we know that $\varphi _ i\lor \varphi \xrightarrow {d_{S,\theta +\omega }}\varphi $. So it suffices to apply (16.9.37) to $\varphi _ i\lor \varphi $ instead of $\varphi _ i$, and we conclude by 16.9.7. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.38.B1559025EC82 Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Define $\varphi _ t=t\varphi _1+(1-t)\varphi _0$ for $t\in (0,1)$. Then

\[ \varphi _ t\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi _0 \]

as $t\to 0+$.

Proof. First note that for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$,

\[ \lim _{t\to 0+}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _0}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \]

So thanks to corollary 16.9.33, it remains to argue that for all $j=0,\ldots ,n$,

\[ \lim _{t\to 0+}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ t\lor \varphi _0}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _0}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \]

Observe that for $t\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ \varphi _ t\lor \varphi _0=t(\varphi _1\lor \varphi _0)+(1-t)\varphi _0, \]

so the desired limit follows by the same binomial expansion. $\square$

We have the following sandwich criterion:

Corollary 16.9.39.300ED38851D0 Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}, (\psi _ i)_{i\in I}, (\eta _ i)_{i\in I}$ be three nets in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that

  1. FB107A3F1389

    $\psi _ i\preceq _ P \varphi _ i\preceq _ P \eta _ i$ for each $i\in I$;

  2. C027488E50E6

    $\eta _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S} \varphi $, $\psi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S} \varphi $.

Then $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $.

Proof. By corollary 16.9.36, we may replace $\theta $ by $\theta +\omega $, where $\omega $ is a Kähler form on $X$. In particular, we may assume that $\varphi _ i,\psi _ i,\eta _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ for all $i\in I$. By proposition 16.9.6, we may assume that $\varphi _ i,\psi _ i,\eta _ i$ are model potentials for all $i\in I$ and hence $\psi _ i \leq \varphi _ i\leq \eta _ i$ for all $i\in I$.

It follows from theorem 13.13.11 that for each $k=0,\ldots ,n$, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\psi _ i}^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}\leq \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ i}^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k} \]

for all $i\in I$. By theorem 16.9.30, the limits, with respect to $i\in I$, of the two outer terms are $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}$. It follows that

EDB32DFBA6A5 16.9.40
\begin{equation} \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}. \end{equation}

By corollary 16.9.33, it remains to prove that $\varphi _ i \lor \varphi \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. By corollary 16.9.33 and proposition 16.8.28, up to replacing $\psi _ i$ (resp. $\varphi _ i$, $\eta _ i$) by $\psi _ i\lor \varphi $ (resp. $\varphi _ i\lor \varphi $, $\eta _ i\lor \varphi $), we may assume from the beginning that $\psi _ i,\varphi _ i,\eta _ i\geq \varphi $. Now $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $ by (16.9.40) and 16.9.7. $\square$

Lemma 16.9.41.F0C01F4F75EB Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Fix $\lambda >0$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. 9CF34F7033CA

    $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_{S,\theta }}\varphi $;

  2. 1FA7990ED285

    $\lambda \varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_{S,\lambda \theta }}\lambda \varphi $.

Proof. We have $V_{\lambda \theta }=\lambda V_{\theta }$. The middle expression in (16.9.10) for the pair $(\lambda \varphi _ i,\lambda \varphi )$ in the class $\lambda \theta $ is therefore $\lambda ^ n$ times the corresponding expression for $(\varphi _ i,\varphi )$ in the class $\theta $. The equivalence follows from corollary 16.9.9. $\square$

Proposition 16.9.42.FDEBDB1E8A22 Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ (resp. $(\psi _ i)_{i\in I}$) be a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\varphi _ i \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ (resp. $\psi _ i \xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$). Then

\[ \varphi _ i\lor \psi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \lor \psi . \]

Proof. Since $d_ S$ is a pseudometric, we may assume that both nets are actually sequences and $I=\mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. By corollary 16.9.36, we may assume that the masses $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$, $\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n>0$.

By proposition 16.9.6 and proposition 16.8.28, replacing all potentials by their $P$-envelopes changes neither the hypotheses nor the conclusion. Thus we may assume that the $\varphi _ j$'s, the $\psi _ j$'s, $\varphi $ and $\psi $ are all model. After discarding finitely many terms, the masses of the two sequences are bounded below by a positive constant. Applying proposition 16.9.17 to both sequences and using the sandwich criterion corollary 16.9.39, it is enough to treat the case where each of $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ and $(\psi _ j)_ j$ is monotone. In this reduced situation, $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ (resp. $(\psi _ j)_ j$) converges to $\varphi $ (resp. $\psi $) almost everywhere due to proposition 16.9.47, corollary 16.9.12 and proposition 16.4.9.

We handle three cases separately.

Step 1. Assume that both sequences are increasing.

In this case, we have $\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j\nearrow \varphi \lor \psi $ almost everywhere. Therefore, $\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S} \varphi \lor \psi $ by corollary 16.9.12.

Step 2. Assume that one sequence, say $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is increasing while the other is decreasing. Then we have

\[ \varphi _ j \lor \psi \leq \varphi _ j \lor \psi _ j \leq \varphi \lor \psi _ j. \]

Thanks to corollary 16.9.39, it suffices to show that both sides converge to $\varphi \lor \psi $ with respect to $d_ S$. So we reduce to the case where both sequences are decreasing.

Step 3. Assume that both sequences are decreasing.

In this case, due to corollary 16.9.14, it suffices to show that

76B405D47467 16.9.43
\begin{equation} \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n. \end{equation}

The $\geq $ direction follows from theorem 13.13.11; it remains to argue the $\leq $ direction.

Thanks to 13.13.30, we may find a sequence $(\epsilon _ j)_ j$ in $(0,1)$ with limit $0$ and a sequence $(\eta _ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ such that

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)\varphi _ j+\epsilon _ j \eta _ j \leq \varphi ,\quad \eta _ j \leq \varphi _ j. \]

It follows that for each $j\geq 1$, we have

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)(\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j)+\epsilon _ j \eta _ j \leq \varphi \lor \psi _ j. \]

Therefore by theorem 13.13.11,

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)^ n \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j}^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi _ j}^ n. \]

Letting $j\to \infty $, we find that

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j}^ n\leq \lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi _ j}^ n. \]

Therefore, in order to prove (16.9.43), we may assume that one of the sequences is constant; let us say $\psi _ j=\psi $ for all $j$. Repeating the same argument as before and constructing $(\epsilon _ j)_ j$, $(\eta _ j)_ j$ as above, we get

\[ (1-\epsilon _ j)^ n \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j\lor \psi }^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n. \]

Letting $j\to \infty $, we conclude (16.9.43). $\square$

By proposition 16.9.6 again, $\varphi \lor \psi \sim _ P\psi $ and hence $\varphi \preceq _ P \psi $ by 16.8.13. $\square$

Lemma 16.9.50.CC25BA40D9FE Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. As $\epsilon \to 0$ (from both sides), we have

11D8415D35F8 16.9.51
\begin{equation} V_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }\xlongrightarrow {d_ S}V_{\theta }. \end{equation}

Proof. There are two assertions to prove, as detailed in the two steps.

Step 1. We first handle the case where $\epsilon \to 0+$.

In this case (16.9.51) means

\[ V_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }\xlongrightarrow {d_{S,\theta +\omega }}V_{\theta } \]

as $\epsilon \to 0+$. So thanks to corollary 16.9.14 and proposition 16.4.4, it suffices to prove the following:

\begin{equation} \inf _{\epsilon >0} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ V_{\theta +\epsilon \omega } \right]=P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ V_{\theta } \right]. \end{equation}

First observe that

\[ V_{\theta }=\inf _{\epsilon >0} V_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }. \]

In fact, the $\leq $ direction is trivial. As for the reverse inequality, it suffices to observe that the right-hand side lies in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Therefore, due to proposition 16.4.4,

\[ \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} \int _ X \left( \theta +\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_{\theta +\epsilon \omega } \right)^ n=\int _ X \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n. \]

Therefore, for all $\epsilon >0$ small enough, we can find $\eta _{\epsilon }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon \omega )$ and $a_{\epsilon }>0$ decreasing to $0$ so that

\[ (1-a_\epsilon )V_{\theta +\epsilon \omega } + a_\epsilon \eta _\epsilon \leq V_{\theta }. \]

Therefore, thanks to proposition 16.4.2,

\[ (1-a_\epsilon )P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ V_{\theta +\epsilon \omega } \right] + a_\epsilon P_{\theta +\omega }\left[\eta _\epsilon \right] \leq P_{\theta +\omega }\left[V_{\theta }\right]. \]

Since the envelopes $P_{\theta +\omega }[\eta _\epsilon ]$ have supremum $0$, they are uniformly bounded in $L^1$ by proposition 5.17.3; hence $a_\epsilon P_{\theta +\omega }[\eta _\epsilon ]\to 0$ in $L^1$. Letting $\epsilon \to 0$ and using proposition 5.14.6, we conclude (16.9.52).

Step 2. We then handle the case where $\epsilon \to 0-$.

In this case, (16.9.51) simply means

\[ V_{\theta -\epsilon \omega }\xrightarrow {d_{S,\theta }}V_{\theta } \]

as $\epsilon \to 0+$. But this follows from corollary 16.9.12 if we can prove

4D05EB795FC2 16.9.53
\begin{equation} \operatorname *{sup}_{\epsilon >0}{}\! {}^{*} V_{\theta -\epsilon \omega }=V_{\theta }, \end{equation}

where we understand that $\epsilon $ is small enough so that $\theta -\epsilon \omega $ represents a big cohomology class. Since $\{ \theta \} $ is big, we can find $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ so that

\[ \varphi \leq 0,\quad \theta _{\varphi }\geq \delta \omega \]

for some $\delta >0$. For a small $\epsilon >0$, we then have

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\left( \left(1-\frac{\epsilon }{\delta }\right)V_{\theta }+ \frac{\epsilon }{\delta }\varphi \right)\geq \epsilon \omega . \]

Therefore,

\[ \left(1-\frac{\epsilon }{\delta }\right)V_{\theta }+ \frac{\epsilon }{\delta }\varphi \leq V_{\theta -\epsilon \omega }. \]

\[ V_{\theta }\leq \operatorname *{sup}_{\epsilon >0}{}\! {}^{*} V_{\theta -\epsilon \omega }. \]

The reverse inequality is trivial, hence (16.9.53) is established. $\square$
