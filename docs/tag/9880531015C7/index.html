8.8.1 Positive fiber flux9880531015C7

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 8.8.1 Positive fiber flux

Let $V$ be a smooth invariant $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function with $V\leq 0$. In the affine chart it has the form $V(x,w)$. The matrix calculation in the proof of lemma 8.2.1 applies at every finite $x$, without assuming the special endpoint behavior of the heat family. Put

\begin{gather*} A_ V=\mu +V_ x,\qquad B_ V=1+V_{w\bar w},\qquad R_ V=\mu +V_{xx},\\ C_ V=\mu +V_{x\bar w},\qquad \Delta _ V=B_ VR_ V-|C_ V|^2. \end{gather*}

Let $\mathrm d\Omega $ be probability angular area in $\mathbb C^2$. The radial Fubini–Study measure is $2\mu ^2(1-\mu )\, \mathrm dx\, \mathrm d\Omega $, and $\omega ^3=6\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2\mathrm dA_ E$. Semipositivity gives $A_ V,B_ V,R_ V,\Delta _ V\geq 0$ and, without dividing by $B_ V$,

8.26
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}_\omega (V)=12 A_ V\Delta _ V\, \mathrm dx\, \mathrm d\Omega \, \mathrm dA_ E. \end{equation}

The complement of these radial coordinates has zero mass for a smooth top form.

Lemma 8.8.1. For every smooth invariant $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function,

\[ \int _{\mathbb R\times E}\Delta _ V\, \mathrm dx\, \mathrm dA_ E=1. \]

If in addition $V\leq 0$, then

\[ |V_ x|^2\leq -2V,\qquad 0\leq A_ V\leq 1+\sqrt{-2V}. \]

Proof. Set $m(x)=\int _ EV\, \mathrm dA_ E$ and $e(x)=\int _ E|V_ w|^2\, \mathrm dA_ E$. Periodic integration by parts gives

\[ \begin{split} \int _ E V_{w\bar w}V_{xx}\, \mathrm dA_ E & =-\operatorname {Re}\int _ EV_{\bar w}V_{xxw}\, \mathrm dA_ E,\\ e''& =2\int _ E|V_{xw}|^2\, \mathrm dA_ E +2\operatorname {Re}\int _ EV_{\bar w}V_{xxw}\, \mathrm dA_ E. \end{split} \]

The mean of every fiber derivative vanishes. Expanding $\Delta _ V$ yields

\[ \int _ E\Delta _ V\, \mathrm dA_ E =m''+\mu (1-\mu )-\tfrac 12e''. \]

Thus $f(x)=\log (1+\mathrm e^ x)+m(x)-e(x)/2$ is convex.

Both $m$ and $e$ are smooth global functions on the projective base: local fiber coordinates change by holomorphic translations, preserving fiber Haar area, the vertical derivative and the Dirichlet integral. They are $U(2)$-invariant. Smoothness and radial invariance at $\xi =0$ give $m',e'\to 0$ at the negative end. At the positive end use a normal coordinate $z_0/z_1$ to the hyperplane $\{ z_0=0\} $ on the base. The invariant base functions are constant along this hyperplane and smooth across it; their $x$ derivatives tend to zero. This does not require that the pointwise potential be constant on its elliptic fibers at that end. Consequently $f'(-\infty )=0$ and $f'(+\infty )=1$. Integrating the nonnegative $f''$ proves the flux identity.

For each fixed $w$, $R_ V\geq 0$ implies $V_{xx}\geq -1$ on all of $\mathbb R$. Hence for every real $h$,

\[ V(x+h,w)\geq V(x,w)+hV_ x(x,w)-h^2/2. \]

Taking $h=V_ x(x,w)$ and using $V\leq 0$ proves the derivative bound. Finally $\mu \leq 1$ proves the bound for $A_ V$. $\square$

Combining the positive density with lemma 8.8.1 proves

8.29
\begin{equation} \int _ XW(-V)\mathrm{MA}_\omega (V)\leq 12Q(W),\qquad \int _{\{ -V>T\} }W(-V)\mathrm{MA}_\omega (V)\leq 12Q_ T(W). \end{equation}

The positive flux supplies the reference integral even though the unweighted Monge–Ampère masses are unbounded.

Lemma 8.8.2. Every bounded invariant $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function $u$, with $-L\leq u\leq 0$, admits smooth invariant $\omega $-plurisubharmonic approximants with the same bounds converging to $u$ in capacity.

Proof. The compact group $G=U(3)\times (\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)$ acts transitively and holomorphically on $X$ by unitary transformations and positive scalar multiplication. It preserves $\omega $. Choose a nonnegative smooth central approximate identity $\rho _\epsilon $ on $G$, supported near its identity, and put

\[ u_\epsilon (p)=\int _ Gu(gp)\rho _\epsilon (g)\, \mathrm dg. \]

Convolution on a compact homogeneous space is smooth. Averaging preserves the current inequality and the bounds, and centrality makes convolution commute with the $U(2)$ action. Transporting the bounded tests defining capacity shows that $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ is $G$-invariant.

Suppose $L>0$. By quasi-continuity, choose an open set $O$ of arbitrarily small capacity such that $u$ is continuous on $X\setminus O$. When $g$ is sufficiently close to the identity, $u(gp)$ and $u(p)$ are uniformly close whenever both points lie outside $O$. Thus, outside $O$ and for small kernel support,

\[ \{ |u_\epsilon -u|>\delta \} \subset \{ P_\epsilon \mathbf1_ O>\delta /(4L)\} ,\qquad P_\epsilon \mathbf1_ O(p)=\int _ G\mathbf1_ O(gp)\rho _\epsilon (g)\, \mathrm dg. \]

Markov's inequality, followed by capacity invariance, gives

\[ \begin{split} \operatorname {Cap}_\omega \{ P_\epsilon \mathbf1_ O>\delta /(4L)\} & \leq \frac{4L}{\delta }\sup _{0\leq v\leq 1} \int _ XP_\epsilon \mathbf1_ O\, (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^3\\ & \leq \frac{4L}{\delta }\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (O), \end{split} \]

where the supremum runs over $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. Letting the capacity of $O$ tend to zero proves the claim. If $L=0$, $u=0$ and no approximation is needed. $\square$

Proposition 8.8.3. For every $u\in \mathcal R$ and $T\geq 0$,

8.32
\begin{equation} \nu _{W,u}(X)\leq 12Q(W),\qquad \nu _{W,u}(\{ -u>T\} )\leq 12Q_ T(W). \end{equation}

If $\sqrt t\, W(t)\to 0$, the family $\{ \nu _{W,u}:u\in \mathcal R\} $ is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to capacity, and $u\mapsto \nu _{W,u}$ is continuous from convergence in capacity to weak convergence of finite measures.

Proof. For bounded $u\leq 0$, apply lemma 8.8.2. The bounded capacity continuity of products and quasi-continuous coefficients in section 1.6, section 1.8 passes the total estimate (8.29) to $u$. For the tail at $T>0$, use continuous depth cutoffs majorizing $\mathbf1_{\{ -u>T\} }$ and supported in $\{ -u>T-\delta \} $, where $0<\delta <T$. The same product continuity gives a bound by $12Q_{T-\delta }(W)$. Continuity of $(1+\sqrt{2t})W(t)$ gives $Q_{T-\delta }(W)\to Q_ T(W)$. For $T=0$, use the total estimate.

For unbounded $u$, the canonical truncations $u^ K=\max (u,-K)$ remain bounded and invariant. On $\{ u>-K\} $ their measures agree with the finite-level restrictions defining $\mu _ u$. These restrictions increase to $\mu _ u$, so monotone convergence proves (8.32).

Splitting at depth $T$ and rescaling $u^ T$ into a capacity test gives, for every Borel set $B$,

8.33
\begin{equation} \nu _{W,u}(B)\leq W(0)(1+T)^3\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (B)+12Q_ T(W). \end{equation}

Indeed $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u^ T\leq (1+T)(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u^ T/(1+T)))$, and the latter potential has range of length at most one. If $\sqrt t\, W(t)\to 0$, then $Q_ T(W)\to 0$. Taking first $T$ large and then the capacity small proves uniform absolute continuity.

Suppose $u_ j\to u$ in capacity in $\mathcal R$. For fixed $K$, the truncations $u_ j^ K$ converge in capacity to $u^ K$, and their weighted bounded products converge weakly. Outside $\{ u\leq -K\} $, locality identifies $\nu _{W,u^ K}$ with $\nu _{W,u}$. Both measures give this exceptional set mass at most $12Q_{K-1}(W)$ when $K>1$, by their tail estimates; this also includes any truncation mass on $\{ u=-K\} $. Hence

\[ \| \nu _{W,u^ K}-\nu _{W,u}\| _{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 24Q_{K-1}(W) \]

uniformly over $u\in \mathcal R$. Letting $K\to +\infty $ removes the truncations and proves the claimed continuity. $\square$
