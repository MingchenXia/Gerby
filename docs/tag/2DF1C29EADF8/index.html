From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Conventions

Unless otherwise stated, $X$ denotes a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. A Hermitian form is a smooth real strictly positive $(1,1)$-form. The letter $\omega $ is reserved for such a form, while $\theta $ denotes an arbitrary smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Whenever $\theta $ or $\omega $ is assumed to be closed, this is stated explicitly.

We follow the normalizations of [SGPT]:

\[ \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi }(\bar\partial -\partial ), \qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial . \]

Thus, on a disk in $\mathbb C$ with coordinate $z$, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^2$ is the unit Dirac measure at the origin. Multiplier ideals of a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ are defined with the weight $\exp (-u)$, and Monge–Ampère masses carry no factor $1/n!$.
