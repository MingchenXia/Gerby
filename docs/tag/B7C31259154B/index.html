Chapter 12 · Monge–Ampère equations and stability · Tag B7C31259154B

Big minimal normalized

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 14.15.4

Theorem 14.15.4. Under the assumptions of theorem 14.15.2, there are $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, a constant $C>0$ and a uniquely determined $c>0$ such that

\[ \sup _ Xv=0, \qquad V_\theta -C\leq v\leq V_\theta , \qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Proof. Apply lemma 14.15.3 with $\lambda =1/j$, and write $v_ j=v_{1/j}$ and $c_ j=c_{1/j}$. Extract a subsequence with $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1$ and almost everywhere, and with $c_ j\to c\in [m,C]$. The limit is normalized, $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and has minimal singularities. The densities $c_ j\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}f$ have a common $L^ p$ bound. Hence corollary 14.14.2, applied with the reference $V_\theta $, upgrades the convergence to uniform convergence of differences on $\Omega $ and identifies the weak $L^ p$ limit of these densities with the density of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)$.

At almost every point, $v_ j$ converges to a finite value, so $v_ j/j\to 0$. Since $v_ j\leq 0$ and $c_ j\leq C$, dominated convergence gives $c_ j\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}f\to cf$ in $L^ p$. Therefore $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$. The uniqueness of the constant follows from corollary 14.12.3. $\square$
