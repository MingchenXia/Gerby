From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 14.31 Stability of the subsolution threshold

We now study how the threshold $b_ h(f)$ depends on $f$. By (14.104), $b_ h^{1/n}$ is an infimum of functions that are continuous on $L^1$, and is therefore upper semicontinuous. It is not continuous in general: when $v_-(h)=0$, every density with positive threshold is an $L^1$ limit of densities with zero threshold. For density roots, (14.103) expresses $\rho _ h$ as a supremum of positive linear functionals. Hence $\rho _ h$ is a norm on the space where it is finite, this space is complete for a suitable norm, and the threshold depends continuously on the root in that norm.

Theorem 14.31.1. Suppose $f_ j,f$ are nonzero nonnegative $L^1$ densities and $f_ j\to f$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. Then

\[ \limsup _{j\to \infty }b_ h(f_ j)\leq b_ h(f). \]

Moreover,

14.113
\begin{equation} b_ h(f)=\lim _{\delta \downarrow 0}\sup \{ c_ h(q):q\in L^\infty ,\ q\geq 0,\ q\not\equiv 0, \ \| q-f\| _{L^1}<\delta \} , \end{equation}

and the suprema are finite for sufficiently small $\delta $.

Proof. For a Gauduchon form $\gamma $ on $X$, write $A_\gamma (f)=\int _ X(f\, \mathrm{d}V/\gamma ^ n)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n$. Since $|s^{1/n}-t^{1/n}|\leq |s-t|^{1/n}$ for $s,t\geq 0$, Hölder's inequality shows that $A_\gamma $ is continuous on $L^1$; it is positive at every nonzero nonnegative density. By theorem 14.28.1, $b_ h^{1/n}$ is the infimum over $\gamma $ of the continuous positive functions $f\mapsto \int _ Xh\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}/A_\gamma (f)$. It is therefore upper semicontinuous, and testing a single $\gamma $ bounds it above on an $L^1$ neighborhood of $f$.

For the second assertion, recall that $b_ h(q)=c_ h(q)$ for bounded $q$ (proposition 14.27.4). By the first assertion, $\limsup _ jc_ h(q_ j)\leq b_ h(f)$ for every sequence of nonzero nonnegative bounded densities $q_ j\to f$ in $L^1$. Hence the limit in (14.113) is at most $b_ h(f)$, and the local upper bound makes the suprema finite for small $\delta $. Conversely, the truncations $f_ k$ converge to $f$ in $L^1$ and satisfy $c_ h(f_ k)\geq b_ h(f)$, so every supremum in (14.113) is at least $b_ h(f)$. $\square$

Since $b_ h\geq 0$, upper semicontinuity makes $b_ h$ continuous at every density where it vanishes. If $v_-(h)=0$, these are its only points of continuity. Indeed, let $b_ h(f)>0$ and $g=f/\int _ Xf\, \mathrm{d}V$. By corollary 14.26.5 and theorem 14.27.2, the densities $(1-t)g+tf_0$, where $f_0$ is the density of theorem 14.26.3, have zero threshold and converge to $g$ in $L^1$ as $t\downarrow 0$; multiplying them by $\int _ Xf\, \mathrm{d}V$ gives densities with zero threshold converging to $f$. These statements concern only the scalar $b_ h$; they say nothing about capacity convergence of the corresponding potentials or about the limits of their Monge–Ampère measures.
