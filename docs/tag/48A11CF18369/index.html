Global finiteness and bounded cohomological mass

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 15.4 Global finiteness and bounded cohomological mass

From now on, $X$ is a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$ with a fixed Kähler form $\omega $, and $\theta $ and $\theta _ j$ always denote smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. (We exclude dimension zero, where the only product is the empty one.) If $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ (definition 13.1.3) is locally bounded, then near each point $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(h+\varphi )$, where $h$ is a smooth local potential of $\theta $ and $h+\varphi $ is bounded and plurisubharmonic. By proposition 15.1.5, the Bedford–Taylor products of such local potentials do not depend on the choice of $h$; hence they glue to closed positive currents $\bigwedge _ j(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)$ on $X$, which can be multiplied by smooth forms. We first prove Stokes' formula for these products and then show that non-pluripolar products on $X$ are always finite.

Proposition 15.4.1. Let $1\leq p\leq n$, let $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ be locally bounded for $1\leq j\leq p$, and let $\eta $ be a smooth closed $(n-p,n-p)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ \int _ X\bigwedge _{j=1}^ p(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\wedge \eta =\int _ X\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _ p\wedge \eta . \]

Proof. Fix $j$, let $R$ be the closed positive current $\bigwedge _{i>j}(\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ i)$, and let $\eta '$ be a smooth closed form of pure complementary bidegree. Locally $\theta _ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ j$ with $h_ j$ smooth, and since $R$ is closed, the recursion gives

\[ (\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\wedge R=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}((h_ j+\varphi _ j)R)=\theta _ j\wedge R+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi _ jR). \]

Here $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(h_ jR)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ j\wedge R$ because $h_ j$ is smooth and $R$ is closed of pure bidegree, so that $\partial R=\bar\partial R=0$. Both sides are globally defined, because $\varphi _ j$ is a global bounded function. Moreover $\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi _ jR)\wedge \eta '=\int _ X\varphi _ jR\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta '=0$, since $\eta '$ is closed of pure bidegree, so that $\partial \eta '=\bar\partial \eta '=0$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta '=0$. Hence $\int _ X(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\wedge R\wedge \eta '=\int _ X\theta _ j\wedge R\wedge \eta '$. Apply this successively for $j=1,2,\ldots ,p$, with $\eta '=\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j-1}\wedge \eta $ at the $j$th step, which is smooth and closed. After $p$ steps the integral becomes $\int _ X\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _ p\wedge \eta $. $\square$

Lemma 15.4.2. Let $R$ be a closed positive current of bidimension $(1,1)$ on $X$, and let $a,b$ be bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$, or differences of such functions. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge R=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(bR)$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(aR)$ are finite signed measures, and

\[ \int _ X a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge R =\int _ X b\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R =-\int _ X\mathrm da\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R. \]

Here the mixed gradient

\[ \mathrm da\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R=\tfrac 12\bigl (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(abR)-a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge R-b\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R\bigr ) \]

is a finite signed measure on $X$, symmetric in $a$ and $b$. It is the exterior product of $\mathrm da\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b$ with $R$ when $a$ and $b$ are smooth, and on an open set where $a=b$ is plurisubharmonic it is the gradient measure $E_ R(a)$ of (9.84).

Proof. The measures. Near each point of $X$, a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic function $b$ is the difference $b_1-b_2$ of two bounded plurisubharmonic functions: if $b+g$ is plurisubharmonic on a coordinate ball with $g$ smooth, and $q=C|z|^2$ with $C$ so large that $g+q$ is plurisubharmonic on a smaller ball, take $b_1=b+g+q$ and $b_2=g+q$. Hence $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(bR)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_1\wedge R-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_2\wedge R$ is locally the difference of two Bedford–Taylor products, which are positive measures. Covering the compact manifold $X$ by finitely many such neighborhoods, we see that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(bR)$ is a finite signed measure; the same holds for $a$.

For bounded plurisubharmonic functions $A$ and $B$ on an open set, additivity of Bedford–Taylor products (proposition 9.1.2) and the formula (9.84) give, after expanding $(A+B)^2$,

\[ E_ R(A+B)=E_ R(A)+E_ R(B)+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(ABR)-A\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}B\wedge R-B\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A\wedge R, \]

so that $\frac12(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(ABR)-A\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}B\wedge R-B\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A\wedge R)=\frac12(E_ R(A+B)-E_ R(A)-E_ R(B))$ is a signed measure, by lemma 9.13.1. Since the expression defining the mixed gradient is bilinear in $(a,b)$, writing $a=a_1-a_2$ and $b=b_1-b_2$ locally shows that the mixed gradient is a signed measure near each point, hence a finite signed measure on $X$. It is symmetric by definition. For $a=b$ plurisubharmonic it equals $\frac12\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a^2R)-a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R=E_ R(a)$. For smooth $a$ and $b$ we have $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(ab)=a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b+b\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a+\mathrm da\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b+\mathrm db\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}a$, and the $(1,1)$-parts of $\mathrm da\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b$ and $\mathrm db\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}a$ both equal $\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi }(\partial a\wedge \bar\partial b+\partial b\wedge \bar\partial a)$; since only $(1,1)$-parts contribute in products with $R$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(abR)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(ab)\wedge R$, the mixed gradient is $\mathrm da\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R$.

A local identity. Let $\chi $ be a test function with support in a coordinate ball $W$, and let $A$ and $B$ be bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline W$. We claim that

15.13
\begin{equation} \label{eq:local-symmetry-defect} \int \chi A\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(BR)-\int \chi B\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(AR)=-\int AB\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \wedge R-2\, \mathrm d(BR)(A\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\chi ), \end{equation}

where $\mathrm d(BR)$ is the current of order zero of lemma 9.13.1 and its value on the bounded Borel one-form $A\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\chi $ with compact support is computed with its coefficient measures. Suppose first that $A$ is smooth. Then $\int \chi A\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(BR)=(BR)(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi A))$ and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi A)=\chi \, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A+A\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi +\mathrm d\chi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}A+\mathrm dA\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\chi . \]

The first term contributes $\int \chi B\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A\wedge R$. Since $BR$ has bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$, it sees only the $(1,1)$-parts of the last two terms, which agree as above; so these terms contribute $2(BR)(\mathrm dA\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\chi )$. Finally $\mathrm dA\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\chi =\mathrm d(A\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\chi )-A\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $, and $(BR)(\mathrm d\psi )=-\mathrm d(BR)(\psi )$ for one-forms $\psi $, because $BR$ has even degree. Collecting terms gives (15.13).

In general, let $A_ l$ be smooth plurisubharmonic functions decreasing to $A$ on a neighborhood of $\overline W$, for instance mollifications (proposition 8.2.2(2)). Then (15.13) holds for $A_ l$, and every term converges to the corresponding term for $A$. For the first, third and last terms this follows by dominated convergence against the fixed signed measures $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(BR)$, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \wedge R$ and the coefficient measures of $\mathrm d(BR)$, since the $A_ l$ are uniformly bounded on $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $ and converge pointwise to $A$. For the second term it follows from the weighted form of theorem 9.2.5, with the constant sequence of weights $B$. This proves the claim. Since both sides of (15.13) are bilinear in $(A,B)$, the identity also holds for differences of bounded plurisubharmonic functions, and in particular for $A=a$ and $B=b$ near $\overline W$.

Conclusion. Let $(\chi _ i)$ be a finite smooth partition of unity on $X$ subordinate to coordinate balls as above (proposition 7.1.3). Summing (15.13) for $\chi =\chi _ i$, $A=a$ and $B=b$ over $i$, and using that $abR$, $\mathrm d(bR)$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(aR)$, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(bR)$ are global currents, we obtain

\[ \int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge R-\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R=-\int _ Xab\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Bigl (\sum _ i\chi _ i\Bigr )\wedge R-2\, \mathrm d(bR)\Bigl (a\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\sum _ i\chi _ i\Bigr )=0, \]

because $\sum _ i\chi _ i=1$. This is the first equality. For the second, note that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(abR)$ is a finite signed measure, being a combination of the mixed gradient and the measures $a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge R$ and $b\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R$, and that its total integral is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(abR)(1)=(abR)(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}1)=0$, because the constant function $1$ is a test function on the compact manifold $X$. Integrating the definition of the mixed gradient over $X$ and using the first equality, we get $\int _ X\mathrm da\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge R=-\int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge R$. $\square$

Theorem 15.4.3. Let $T_1,\ldots ,T_ p$, $1\leq p\leq n$, be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on the compact Kähler manifold $X$. Then their non-pluripolar product is finite. More precisely, write $T_ j=\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j$ with $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$, and choose $C\geq 0$ such that the forms $\tau _ j=\theta _ j+C\omega $ are semipositive. Then

15.14
\begin{equation} \label{eq:shifted-cohomological-mass-bound} \int _ X\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ p\rangle \wedge \omega ^{n-p} \leq \int _ X\tau _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \tau _ p\wedge \omega ^{n-p}. \end{equation}

In particular, if the forms $\theta _ j$ are semipositive, we may take $C=0$.

Proof. The decomposition $T_ j=\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j$ exists by theorem 14.1.7. Since $\tau _ j\geq \theta _ j$ and $\tau _ j\geq 0$, $\varphi _ j$ is $\tau _ j$-plurisubharmonic, and so is every constant; hence $\max \{ \varphi _ j,-k\} $ is a bounded $\tau _ j$-plurisubharmonic function, and

\[ R_{j,k}=\tau _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max \{ \varphi _ j,-k\} \]

is a closed positive current. By proposition 15.4.1, the total mass $\int _ XR_{1,k}\wedge \cdots \wedge R_{p,k}\wedge \omega ^{n-p}$ equals the right side of (15.14) for every $k$.

Local finiteness. Let $W$ be a relatively compact coordinate chart, and choose smooth functions $h_ j$ and $h$ near $\overline W$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ j=\theta _ j$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\omega $. Then $u_ j=\varphi _ j+h_ j$ is a plurisubharmonic potential of $T_ j$ on $W$. Let $B$ be an integer with $h_ j\leq B$ on $W$ for all $j$, fix an integer $l\geq 1$, and put $O_ l(u)=\bigcap _ j\{ u_ j>-l\} $. On $W\cap O_ l(u)$ we have $\varphi _ j>-l-B$, so for every integer $k\geq l+B$ the bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\max \{ u_ j,-l\} +Ch$ and $\max \{ \varphi _ j,-k\} +h_ j+Ch$ both equal $u_ j+Ch$ there; the second is a local potential of $R_{j,k}$. By plurifine locality,

\[ \mathbf1_{O_ l(u)}\bigwedge _ j(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max \{ u_ j,-l\} +C\omega )=\mathbf1_{O_ l(u)}\bigwedge _ jR_{j,k}\quad \text{on }W. \]

Expanding the left side into positive terms gives

\[ \mathbf1_{O_ l(u)}\bigwedge _ j\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max \{ u_ j,-l\} \leq \mathbf1_{O_ l(u)}\bigwedge _ j R_{j,k}\leq \bigwedge _ jR_{j,k}. \]

Let $K\subset W$ be compact. On a neighborhood of $K$ in $W$ we have $\beta \leq c\, \omega $ for some $c>0$ (lemma 13.1.1), so by the last display and (15.7), the trace masses on $K$ of the truncated local products are at most $c^{n-p}$ times the right side of (15.14), uniformly in $l$. Hence the non-pluripolar product is locally finite everywhere, and $\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ p\rangle $ is a closed positive current by theorem 15.2.1.

Mass bound. By the same argument, the currents $T_ j+C\omega =\tau _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j$, with local potentials $u_ j+Ch$, have a locally finite non-pluripolar product. The sets $G_ k=\bigcap _ j\{ \varphi _ j>-k\} $ increase and exhaust the complement of the union of the pole sets of the $\varphi _ j$. On $G_ k$ the global truncations $\max \{ \varphi _ j,-k\} $ coincide with $\varphi _ j$. If $B'$ is an integer with $h_ j+Ch\geq -B'$ on $W$ for all $j$, then on $G_ k\cap W$ all local potentials satisfy $u_ j+Ch>-k-B'$, so they coincide there with their truncations at level $k+B'$, and also with the local potentials $\max \{ \varphi _ j,-k\} +h_ j+Ch$ of $R_{j,k}$. By plurifine locality and lemma 15.1.2, $\mathbf1_{G_ k}\bigwedge _ jR_{j,k}=\mathbf1_{G_ k}\langle \bigwedge _ j(T_ j+C\omega )\rangle $, and these restrictions increase to $\langle \bigwedge _ j(T_ j+C\omega )\rangle $. Their total masses against $\omega ^{n-p}$ are at most the right side of (15.14), hence so is that of the limit. Finally, expanding $\langle \bigwedge _ j(T_ j+C\omega )\rangle $ by multilinearity (proposition 15.1.5) into a sum of positive currents, one of which is $\langle \bigwedge _ jT_ j\rangle $, we obtain $\langle \bigwedge _ j T_ j\rangle \leq \langle \bigwedge _ j(T_ j+C\omega )\rangle $, which proves (15.14). $\square$

Example 15.4.4. On $\mathbb P^1$, the current of integration $[0]$ at the point $0$ satisfies $\langle [0]\rangle =0$. Indeed, near $0$ it has the potential $\log |z|^2$ (theorem 7.4.5), whose pole set is $\{ 0\} $, so $\langle [0]\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ z\neq 0\} }[0]=0$ there by proposition 15.3.6; away from $0$ the current vanishes. Thus $[0]$ has mass one while $\langle [0]\rangle $ has mass zero: discarding the poles can change the cohomology class, even in degree one.
