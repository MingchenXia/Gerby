28.18 Semipositive metrics in a four-point blowup family058F9CA104B3

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 28.18 Semipositive metrics in a four-point blowup family

The preceding constructions keep the manifold fixed. For a family of compact manifolds and a line bundle on the total space, one may ask whether a semipositive singular metric on the restriction of the bundle to a special fiber is the restriction of a semipositive metric over a neighborhood of that fiber. In a family of blowups of the projective plane at four points, the answer is negative. The obstruction comes from compact curves on the nearby fibers on which the bundle has degree zero: the curvature of a semipositive metric vanishes on such a curve, and this persists on a component of the limiting curve in the central fiber. It forces the restricted metric to have a logarithmic singularity of coefficient one at a point of that component, whereas the prescribed metric has coefficient one half there. Varying these curves also identifies exactly which central metrics extend.

Let $\Delta $ be a disk with coordinate $t$, and let $[x:y:z]$ be homogeneous coordinates on $\mathbb P^2$. Blow up $\mathbb P^2\times \Delta $ along the four disjoint sections

\[ \begin{aligned} p_1(t)& =[1:0:0],& \qquad p_2(t)& =[0:1:0],\\ p_3(t)& =[1:1:t],& \qquad p_4(t)& =[0:0:1], \end{aligned} \]

write $\pi :\mathcal X\to \Delta $ for the resulting family and $X_ t=\pi ^{-1}(t)$ for its fibers. Let $H$ be the pullback of the hyperplane line bundle, let $E_ i$ be the exceptional divisor over $p_ i$, and put

\[ L=2H-E_1-E_2-E_3-E_4,\qquad L_ t=L|_{X_ t}. \]

We use additive notation for tensor products of line bundles and also for their divisor classes, and we write $K_{X_ t}$ for the canonical line bundle. A singular Hermitian metric $h$ has squared norm $|e|_ h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi }$ in a local holomorphic frame $e$; the function $\varphi $ is its local weight, and its curvature is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $. The metric is semipositive if its local weights are plurisubharmonic. For an effective divisor with local equation $w$, the divisor metric on the associated line bundle is the singular metric for which the canonical section has norm one; its weight is $\log |w|^2$ and its curvature is the integration current along the divisor.

Lemma 28.18.1. The family is smooth and projective with rational surface fibers, and $L$ is effective on $\mathcal X$. For every $t\ne 0$, $L_ t$ is generated by global sections, has square zero and is nef. For every integer $m\geq 1$,

\[ H^0(X_ t,K_{X_ t}+mL_ t)=0\qquad (t\ne 0). \]

Proof. Near one of the sections, coordinates centered on it identify the blowup with $\operatorname {Bl}_0(\mathbb C^2)\times \Delta $. Since the four centers are disjoint, these charts prove smoothness and the submersion property. The blowup of the projective morphism $\mathbb P^2\times \Delta \to \Delta $ is projective and proper, and each fiber $X_ t$ is the blowup of $\mathbb P^2$ at the four points $p_ i(t)$.

The quadratic forms

\[ F_1=x(z-ty),\qquad F_2=y(z-tx) \]

vanish along all four sections and therefore lift to sections of $L$. They are nonzero, which proves that $L$ is effective. For $t\ne 0$, their common zeros in $\mathbb P^2$ are exactly the four points. In suitable affine coordinates $(u,v)$ centered at $p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4$, their linear initial terms are

\[ (v-tu,-tu),\quad (-tu,v-tu),\quad (v-tu,v),\quad (u,v). \]

Each pair is linearly independent. Hence, after the exceptional divisors are subtracted, the transformed sections have no common zero on an exceptional curve. The two transformed sections therefore generate $L_ t$, and $L_ t$ has nonnegative degree on every irreducible curve.

On a fiber, $H^2=1$, $H\cdot E_ i=0$, and $E_ i\cdot E_ j=-\delta _{ij}$. Hence $L_ t^2=0$, and $K_{X_ t}=-3H+\sum _ iE_ i$ gives $K_{X_ t}\cdot L_ t=-2$. A nonzero section of $K_{X_ t}+mL_ t$ would have an effective zero divisor whose intersection with the nef line bundle $L_ t$ is $-2<0$, a contradiction. This argument also excludes a nowhere-vanishing section, whose zero divisor would have intersection zero. $\square$

On $X_0$, let $\ell $ be the strict transform of the line $\{ z=0\} $, which passes through $p_1(0)$, $p_2(0)$ and $p_3(0)$. In additive notation,

\[ \ell =H-E_1-E_2-E_3,\qquad A=2L_0-\ell =3H-E_1-E_2-E_3-2E_4. \]

Lemma 28.18.2. The line bundle $A$ is generated by global sections. There is a semipositive metric $h_0$ on $L_0$ whose weight along $\ell $ is

\[ \varphi _0=\tfrac 12\log |w|^2+\text{a smooth function}, \]

where $w$ is a local equation of $\ell $. It is smooth off $\ell $ and has analytic singularities.

Proof. Consider the cubic forms

\[ x^2z,\qquad xyz,\qquad y^2z,\qquad xy(x-y). \]

Their common zero set in $\mathbb P^2$ consists of the four central points. In affine coordinates at $p_1$, $p_2$ and $p_3$, their linear initial terms include $(z,y)$, $(z,x)$ and $(z,x-y)$, respectively, while at $p_4$ their quadratic initial terms include $x^2,xy,y^2$. These have no common zero on the corresponding exceptional projective lines. After the simple exceptional factors at $p_1,p_2,p_3$ and the double factor at $p_4$ are removed, the transformed cubics therefore generate $A$.

Their Fubini–Study metric $h_ A$ is smooth and semipositive. Let $h_\ell $ be the divisor metric on $\mathcal O(\ell )$. Since $2L_0=A+\ell $, the square root of $h_ A\otimes h_\ell $ defines a metric $h_0$ on $L_0$, with

\[ \Theta _{h_0}=\tfrac 12\Theta _{h_ A}+\tfrac 12[\ell ]\geq 0. \]

The stated form of the weight and of its singularities follows directly. $\square$

Lemma 28.18.3. The strict transforms of the conics

\[ F_1-2F_2=z(x-2y)+txy=0 \]

form a surface $\Gamma \subset \mathcal X$. For $t\ne 0$, its fiber $C_ t$ is a smooth compact rational curve with $L_ t\cdot C_ t=0$. The central fiber of $\Gamma $ is $\ell +q$, where $q$ is the strict transform of the line $\{ x=2y\} $. The curves $\ell $ and $q$ meet transversely at one point $p$, and $L_0\cdot q=1$. Near every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, $\Gamma $ is smooth and $\pi |_\Gamma $ is a submersion.

Proof. The symmetric matrix of the quadratic form has determinant $-t/2$, so the conic is smooth for $t\ne 0$. It passes simply through all four centers, so its strict transform is a smooth rational curve of class $L_ t$, and its degree against $L_ t$ is $L_ t^2=0$.

At $t=0$ the conic is the union of the lines $\{ z=0\} $ and $\{ x=2y\} $. The second line avoids $p_1,p_2,p_3$ and passes through $p_4$. Each component has multiplicity one at every center it contains, so the central fiber of $\Gamma $ contains no additional exceptional component. The two lines meet at $[2:1:0]$, transversely and away from every center. Since $q=H-E_4$, we have $L_0\cdot q=1$.

Away from the centers, the defining equation at $t=0$ has nonzero derivative in the directions of $\mathbb P^2$ at every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, which proves the assertion there. Near $p_4$, use the blowup chart $z=1$, $x=r$, $y=rs$. After the exceptional factor is removed, the equation becomes

\[ 1-2s+trs=0. \]

At the central point of $q\cap E_4$ we have $r=0$ and $s=1/2$, and the derivative in $s$ is $-2$. Thus $(r,t)$ are local coordinates on $\Gamma $ there. These two cases cover $q\setminus \{ p\} $. $\square$

Theorem 28.18.4. There is no semipositive metric on $L$ over an open neighborhood of $X_0$ whose local weights, restricted to $X_0$, satisfy

\[ \psi \geq \varphi _0-C \]

in compatible local frames, for a constant $C$. In particular, $h_0$ is neither the restriction of a semipositive metric on such a neighborhood nor of the same singularity type as such a restriction.

Proof. Suppose that such a metric exists. Since $\pi $ is proper, we may shrink $\Delta $ so that the whole family lies in its domain: otherwise points outside the neighborhood over $t_ j\to 0$ would have a limit point on $X_0$ outside it. The lower bound makes the restricted weight finite at every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, because $\varphi _0$ is smooth there. Hence the restrictions of the weight to $\Gamma $ and to $q$ are not identically $-\infty $.

Near a point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, choose a product chart $(\zeta ,t)$ on $\Gamma $, as given by lemma 28.18.3, and a holomorphic frame of $L$. The restricted weight $u(\zeta ,t)$ is plurisubharmonic and locally integrable. By Fubini's theorem, for almost every small $t\ne 0$ its restriction to $C_ t$ is not identically $-\infty $, so the metric restricts to $C_ t$. The curvature of this restriction is a positive measure on the compact curve $C_ t$ of total mass $L_ t\cdot C_ t=0$, so it vanishes. Thus $u(\cdot ,t)$ is harmonic for almost every $t$. By lemma 28.18.5 below, whose proof does not use this theorem, the central slice $u(\cdot ,0)$ is harmonic or identically $-\infty $, and it is finite. Hence the restricted weight is harmonic near every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $.

The restricted metric on $L_0|_ q$ therefore has curvature supported at $p$. This curvature is a positive measure of total mass $L_0\cdot q=1$, so it is the Dirac measure $[p]$. In a coordinate $w$ on $q$ centered at $p$, the restricted weight is therefore $\log |w|^2$ plus a harmonic function, because their difference has zero distributional Laplacian. Since $\ell $ and $q$ meet transversely, $\varphi _0|_ q=\tfrac 12\log |w|^2+O(1)$. The difference $\psi |_ q-\varphi _0|_ q$ thus tends to $-\infty $ at $p$, contradicting the assumed lower bound. $\square$

The obstruction concerns the prescribed singularity type. Since $L$ is effective on the total space, the divisor metric of the zero divisor of a nonzero section of $L$ is a semipositive metric on $\mathcal X$, with a different restriction to $X_0$.
