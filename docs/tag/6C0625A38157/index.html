-equivalence

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 3.2.1 -equivalence

Proposition 3.2.1. Given $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$, the following are equivalent:

  1. For any $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we have

    \[ \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )=\mathcal{I}(k\psi ); \]

  2. for any $\lambda \in \mathbb {R}_{>0}$, we have

    \[ \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi )=\mathcal{I}(\lambda \psi ); \]

  3. for any modification $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ and any $y\in Y$, we have

    \[ \nu (\pi ^*\varphi ,y)=\nu (\pi ^*\psi ,y); \]

  4. for any proper bimeromorphic morphism $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ from a Kähler manifold and any $y\in Y$, we have

    \[ \nu (\pi ^*\varphi ,y)=\nu (\pi ^*\psi ,y); \]

  5. for any prime divisor $E$ over $X$, we have

    \[ \nu (\varphi ,E)=\nu (\psi ,E). \]
See definition B.1.1 for the definition of prime divisors over $X$. Recall that in the whole book, a modification of a compact complex space means a finite composition of blow-ups with smooth centers. This terminology is highly non-standard.

For simplicity, we usually write

\[ \nu (\varphi ,y)=\nu (\pi ^*\varphi ,y) \]

when $\pi $ is obvious from the context.

Proof. 4 $\iff $ 5. This follows from 1.4.14.

3 $\iff $ 5. This follows from corollary B.1.8.

1 $\implies $ 5. This follows from proposition 1.4.13.

5 $\implies $ 2. This follows from theorem 1.4.15.

2 $\implies $ 1. This is the restriction to positive integral values of $\lambda $. $\square$

Definition 3.2.2. Given $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$, we say they are $\mathcal{I}$-equivalent and write $\varphi \sim _{\mathcal{I}}\psi $ if the equivalent conditions in proposition 3.2.1 are satisfied.

Clearly, $\sim _{\mathcal{I}}$ is an equivalence relation on $\mathrm{QPSH}(X)$.

Proposition 3.2.3. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold $Y$ to $X$. For $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$, the following are equivalent:

  1. $\varphi \sim _{\mathcal{I}}\psi $;

  2. $\pi ^*\varphi \sim _{\mathcal{I}}\pi ^*\psi $.

Proof. 1 $\implies $ 2. This follows from proposition 3.2.1 4.

2 $\implies $ 1. This follows from the simple fact that

\[ \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )=\pi _* \left( \omega _{Y/X}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\pi ^*\varphi ) \right),\quad \mathcal{I}(k\psi )=\pi _* \left( \omega _{Y/X}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\pi ^*\psi ) \right) \]

for any $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, where $\omega _{Y/X}$ is the relative dualizing sheaf. See [Proposition 5.8, Dem12]. $\square$

Proposition 3.2.4. Let $\varphi ,\varphi ',\psi ,\psi '\in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$ and $\lambda >0$. Assume that $\varphi \sim _{\mathcal{I}}\psi $ and $\varphi '\sim _{\mathcal{I}}\psi '$. Then

\[ \varphi \lor \varphi '\sim _{\mathcal{I}} \psi \lor \psi ',\quad \varphi +\varphi '\sim _{\mathcal{I}} \psi +\psi ',\quad \lambda \varphi \sim _{\mathcal{I}}\lambda \psi . \]

Similarly, if $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$, $(\psi _ i)_{i\in I}$ are two non-empty uniformly bounded from above families in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for some closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$ such that $\varphi _ i\sim _{\mathcal{I}}\psi _ i$ for all $i\in I$, then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi _ i\sim _{\mathcal{I}}\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \psi _ i. \]

Proof. This follows from proposition 1.4.5 and corollary 1.4.6. $\square$
