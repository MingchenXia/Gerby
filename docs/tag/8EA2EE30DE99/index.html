1.19 Obstacle envelopes8EA2EE30DE99

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.19 Obstacle envelopes

Given an obstacle function, one looks for the largest $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function lying below it. The local upper-envelope theorem constructs this envelope as soon as the competitors have a common upper bound, and normalized compactness provides such a bound for an obstacle that is finite almost everywhere or prescribed on a non-pluripolar compact set. Letting the obstacles $\min (u+C,0)$ increase with $C$ produces the envelope $P_\theta [u]$ of the ordinary singularity type of $u$.

A smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ is called big if there are $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\delta >0$ such that

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \omega . \]

Here $\omega $ is any Hermitian form; changing it changes $\delta $ but not the condition. The definition does not require $\theta $ to be closed.

Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. For an extended-real function $f$ on $X$, defined at least outside a pluripolar set (the obstacle), write

\[ P_\theta (f)=\left(\sup \{ v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):v\leq f \ \hbox{q.e.}\} \right)^*. \]

As in section 1.6, q.e. (quasi-everywhere) means outside a pluripolar set. The functions $v$ in the supremum are called competitors; the empty supremum is $-\infty $. For obstacles $f_1,\ldots ,f_ N$, we write $P_\theta (f_1,\ldots ,f_ N)=P_\theta (\min _ if_ i)$; when the $f_ i$ belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, this is their rooftop envelope. For an upper semicontinuous obstacle, the pointwise constraint $v\leq f$ gives the same competitors: by the local submean inequality and upper semicontinuity of $f$, a quasi-plurisubharmonic function lying below $f$ almost everywhere lies below it everywhere.

If the family of competitors is nonempty and uniformly bounded above, then $P_\theta (f)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. To apply the local upper-envelope theorem while keeping the exact lower bound $-\theta $, fix a constant positive form $\kappa $ in local coordinates. For each $\epsilon >0$, shrink the coordinate neighborhood and choose a constant form $\theta '$ with $-\epsilon \kappa \leq \theta -\theta '\leq \epsilon \kappa $. A smooth function $r$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r=\theta '+\epsilon \kappa $ makes every competitor plus $r$ plurisubharmonic, so the local envelope theorem gives $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_\theta (f)\geq -2\epsilon \kappa $ there. Letting $\epsilon $ tend to zero proves the exact bound. The same local theorem shows that the regularization changes the supremum only on a pluripolar set.

The following observation gives the upper bound for an unbounded obstacle. Suppose that $f\leq A$, for a real constant $A$, on a Borel set $E$ with $\operatorname {Vol}(E)=\int _ E\mathrm{d}V>0$, where $\mathrm{d}V$ is the volume form fixed in theorem 1.4.2. If $h$ is a competitor, put $\widetilde h=h-\sup _ Xh$. By theorem 1.4.2,

\[ (\sup _ Xh-A)\operatorname {Vol}(E) \leq \int _ E|\widetilde h|\, \mathrm{d}V\leq C_\theta \quad \hbox{whenever }\sup _ Xh>A. \]

Thus $\sup _ Xh\leq A+C_\theta /\operatorname {Vol}(E)$ for every competitor. In particular, the competitors for a measurable obstacle that is finite almost everywhere have a common upper bound: apply this to a sublevel set $\{ f\leq A\} $ of positive volume.

An obstacle bound on a non-pluripolar compact set also bounds every competitor globally, even when that set has zero smooth volume.

Lemma 1.19.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $w\in C(K,\mathbb R)$. There is a constant $C=C(\theta ,K,w)$ such that every $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfying $a\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$ has $\sup _ X a\leq C$.

Proof. Otherwise there are such functions $a_ j$ with

\[ M_ j:=\sup _ Xa_ j\geq 2^ j+\max _ Kw. \]

The normalized functions $v_ j=a_ j-M_ j\leq 0$ have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms by theorem 1.4.2. Choose $A>0$ with $\theta \leq A\omega $. The partial sums

\[ s_ N=\sum _{j=1}^ N2^{-j}v_ j \]

decrease, have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms, and satisfy $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s_ N\geq -A\omega $. By corollary 1.4.3, their limit $s$ is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function; in particular, $s$ is not identically $-\infty $.

Let $N_ j\subseteq K$ be a pluripolar set outside which $a_ j\leq w$ on $K$. On $K\setminus \bigcup _ jN_ j$ one has $v_ j\leq -2^ j$ for every $j$, hence $s=-\infty $. The pole set $\{ s=-\infty \} $ of the quasi-plurisubharmonic function $s$ is pluripolar, and so is the countable union of the $N_ j$. Their union would contain all of $K$, contradicting the hypothesis on $K$. $\square$

Suppose that $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is nonempty. Then the envelope of the zero obstacle,

\[ V_\theta =P_\theta (0), \]

belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$: the competitors are bounded above by zero, and they include $u-\sup _ Xu$ for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. In particular,

\[ u-\sup _ Xu\leq V_\theta \qquad (u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )). \]

A potential $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has minimal singularities if $u\preceq v$ for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. By the last inequality, $V_\theta $ has minimal singularities, and every potential $v$ with minimal singularities satisfies $v\sim V_\theta $. If $\theta \geq 0$, then $0$ is a competitor, and hence $V_\theta =0$.

Lemma 1.19.2. Let $\eta $ be a smooth semipositive real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. If an obstacle $f$ is finite almost everywhere and bounded below quasi-everywhere by a constant, then $P_\eta (f)$ is a bounded $\eta $-plurisubharmonic function.

Proof. Since $\eta \geq 0$, constants are $\eta $-plurisubharmonic, so the constant lower bound of $f$ is a competitor. The finite-sublevel argument above gives a common upper bound for all competitors. Hence $P_\eta (f)$ is an $\eta $-plurisubharmonic function lying between these two constants. $\square$

Proposition 1.19.3. If $f\in C^0(X)$, then $P_\omega (f)$ is continuous. Moreover,

\[ \| P_\omega (f)-P_\omega (g)\| _\infty \leq \| f-g\| _\infty \quad (f,g\in C^0(X)). \]

Proof. The constant $\min _ Xf$ is a competitor, so $p=P_\omega (f)$ is bounded. The local envelope theorem gives $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere, and continuity of $f$ makes this inequality hold everywhere. By theorem 1.11.1, there are smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $v_ j\downarrow p$. Fix $\epsilon >0$. The continuous functions $v_ j-f$ decrease to a function that is at most zero, so $v_ j\leq f+\epsilon $ everywhere for all large $j$; otherwise the decreasing compact sets $\{ v_ j-f\geq \epsilon \} $ would have nonempty intersection. Hence $v_ j-\epsilon $ is a competitor, and

\[ p\leq v_ j\leq p+\epsilon . \]

Thus $v_ j\to p$ uniformly, which proves continuity. The last inequality follows from the monotonicity of $P_\omega $ in the obstacle and the identity $P_\omega (g+c)=P_\omega (g)+c$ for constants $c$, applied with $c=\| f-g\| _\infty $. $\square$

Definition 1.19.4. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, set

\[ P_\theta [u] =\left(\lim _{C\to +\infty }P_\theta (u+C,0)\right)^* =\left(\sup \{ h\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):h\preceq u,\ h\leq 0\} \right)^*. \]

We call $u$ an envelope fixed point, or an ordinary model, if $P_\theta [u]=u$; in the Kähler literature such potentials are called model potentials. For $F\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$, replacing the constraint $h\leq 0$ by $h\leq F$ defines $P_{\theta ,F}[u]$.

The two expressions agree, because the family in the second one is the increasing union over $C$ of the families defining $P_\theta (u+C,0)$. They do not change if zero is replaced by $V_\theta $, since every $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function below zero lies below $V_\theta $. The competitors include $u-\sup _ Xu$ and are bounded above by zero, so $P_\theta [u]\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $u-\sup _ Xu\leq P_\theta [u]\leq 0$. In particular, $u\preceq P_\theta [u]$; this does not assert that $P_\theta [u]-u$ is bounded.

Directly from the definition, $P_\theta [u]$ depends only on the ordinary singularity type of $u$, and

\[ u\preceq v\quad \Longrightarrow \quad P_\theta [u]\leq P_\theta [v]. \]

Replacing the condition $h\preceq u$ by $h\sim _{\mathcal I}u$ gives the multiplier-ideal envelope $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ of definition 16.2.1. This is a different operator; its relation to $P_\theta [u]$ is studied in chapter 16.

For a bounded obstacle in a big background, exceptional sets in the competitor inequality can be removed by a small singular perturbation.

Lemma 1.19.5. Let $\theta $ be big, let $f$ be a bounded function on $X$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Then there are $u_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $t>0$, such that $u_ t\leq f$ everywhere and $u_ t\to u$ almost everywhere as $t\downarrow 0$.

Proof. Choose $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \kappa \omega $ for some $\kappa >0$. The exceptional set $E=\{ u>f\} $ is pluripolar. The global form of Josefson's theorem on compact complex manifolds gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\chi $ with $\chi =-\infty $ on $E$; see [Theorem 1.1.5, SGPT]. After rescaling and adding a constant, we may assume that $\chi \leq 0$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \geq -\omega $. Subtract a constant from $\rho $ so that $\rho \leq \inf _ X f$. With $s=t/\kappa $, put

\[ u_ t=\frac{u+t\chi +s\rho }{1+s}. \]

All three functions in the numerator are finite almost everywhere, so $u_ t$ is not identically $-\infty $. Moreover,

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t =\frac{\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u+t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi +s(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )}{1+s} \geq 0. \]

Outside $E$, both $u$ and $\rho $ are at most $f$, while $\chi \leq 0$, so $u_ t\leq f$. On $E$ the numerator is $-\infty $. Finally, $u_ t\to u$ wherever all three functions are finite, which is a set of full measure. $\square$

For an obstacle prescribed only on a compact set, the same perturbation shows that pluripolar exceptions in the constraint do not change the regularized envelope.

Proposition 1.19.6. Let $\theta $ be big, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $\psi $ be a bounded real function on $K$. Set

\[ \begin{aligned} R& =\sup \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\leq \psi \text{ on }K\} ,\\ Q& =\sup \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\leq \psi \text{ q.e. on }K\} . \end{aligned} \]

Then $R^*=Q^*$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and has minimal singularities; it is bounded if $\theta \geq 0$. Moreover, this regularized envelope does not change if the pointwise constraint is imposed only on $K\setminus E$, for any pluripolar subset $E\subseteq K$.

Proof. Put $m=\inf _ K\psi $ and $M=\sup _ K\psi $. Since $\theta $ is big, $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is nonempty, and $V_\theta \leq 0$; hence $V_\theta +m$ belongs to the family defining $R$. Applying lemma 1.19.1 with the constant obstacle $M$ gives a common upper bound $C$ for the larger family defining $Q$. The upper-envelope argument at the beginning of section 1.19 therefore shows that $R^*$ and $Q^*$ belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Now fix a competitor $u$ for $Q$ and define a bounded function on $X$ by

\[ f(x)= \begin{cases} \psi (x),& x\in K,\\ \max \{ \sup _ Xu,M\} ,& x\notin K. \end{cases} \]

Then $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. By lemma 1.19.5, there are $u_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfying $u_ t\leq f$ everywhere and $u_ t\to u$ almost everywhere. Each $u_ t$ is a competitor for $R$, so $R^*\geq u$ almost everywhere, and lemma 1.3.3 gives this inequality everywhere. Taking the supremum over $u$ and regularizing yields $R^*\geq Q^*$; the inclusion of the competitor families gives the reverse inequality.

Write $F=R^*=Q^*$. Since $F\leq C$, the potential $F-C$ is a competitor for $V_\theta $. Thus

\[ V_\theta +m\leq F\leq V_\theta +C, \]

which proves minimal singularities and, when $\theta \geq 0$, boundedness.

Finally, if $E\subseteq K$ is pluripolar, the family constrained on $K\setminus E$ lies between the families defining $R$ and $Q$, so its regularized supremum also equals $F$. Note that this argument uses the upper bound obtained from the compact set $K$, even when $K\setminus E$ is not compact. $\square$

A potential with minimal singularities stays away from the poles of a strictly positive potential with analytic singularities, in the following sense.

Lemma 1.19.7. Let $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ have analytic singularities with smooth remainder and satisfy $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \varepsilon \omega $ for some $\varepsilon >0$. Set $\Omega =\{ \rho >-\infty \} $. If $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has minimal singularities and is continuous on $\Omega $, then $\varphi -\rho $ is continuous, bounded below, and proper on $\Omega $: all its sublevel sets are compact in $\Omega $.

Proof. Adding constants to $\rho $ and $\varphi $ does not affect the assertions, so we may assume that $\rho \leq 0$ and $\sup _ X\varphi =0$. Since $\varphi $ has minimal singularities, there is a constant $C$ with $\varphi \geq V_\theta -C$. Choose $M\geq 0$ with $\theta \geq -M\omega $. If $a\in (0,1)$ is sufficiently close to one, then

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a\rho ) =a(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )+(1-a)\theta \geq \bigl (a\varepsilon -(1-a)M\bigr )\omega >0. \]

Thus $a\rho $ is a competitor for $V_\theta $, and therefore

\[ 0\geq \varphi \geq V_\theta -C\geq a\rho -C, \]

and hence

1.66
\begin{equation} \varphi -\rho \geq (1-a)(-\rho )-C. \end{equation}

By hypothesis $\varphi $ is continuous on $\Omega $, and $\rho $ is smooth there because it has analytic singularities with smooth remainder. Hence $\varphi -\rho $ is continuous on $\Omega $, and by (1.66) it is bounded below. Since $\rho \to -\infty $ at $X\setminus \Omega $, the sets $\{ \rho \geq -c\} $ are compact subsets of $\Omega $, and (1.66) places every sublevel set of $\varphi -\rho $ in one of them. Being closed in $\Omega $, each sublevel set is therefore compact. $\square$
