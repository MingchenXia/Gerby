Weighted energy

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 11.2.1 Weighted energy

Testing the weighted cofactor against $w^2$ produces a nonnegative gradient integral. Only distributional $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closedness of $hT$ is needed: for a continuous $h>0$, the condition is

11.13
\begin{equation} \label{uni-eq:weightedclosed} \int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \wedge hT=0\qquad (\varphi \in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)). \end{equation}

of 11.2.1(1). The product rule gives

11.14
\begin{equation} \label{uni-eq:w2} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w^2)=2w\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w+2\mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w =2w\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi }\partial w\wedge \bar\partial w. \end{equation}

Approximate $w^2$ in $C^2$ by smooth functions $\varphi $ in (11.13). Since $hT$ is continuous and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$ by (11.1), this gives

11.15
\begin{equation} \label{uni-eq:energy} 0=\frac1\pi \int _ X h\, \mathrm{i}\partial w\wedge \bar\partial w\wedge T. \end{equation}

The continuous integrand is nonnegative, so it vanishes identically, which is (11.12).

By (11.5), (11.12) says that $Q(\mathrm{d}w)=0$. For the nonnegative quadratic form $Q$, this implies $Q(\mathrm{d}w,\eta )=0$ for every $\eta $. Therefore

11.16
\begin{equation} \label{uni-eq:annihilate} \mathrm{d}w_ x\bigl (Q_ x^\sharp \eta \bigr )=0\qquad (x\in X,\ \eta \in T_ x^*X). \end{equation}

By the chain rule and (11.16), $w$ is constant along every absolutely continuous curve whose velocity lies in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $ almost everywhere. Thus $w$ is constant on each reachable set, and by continuity one dense reachable set suffices. If instead $Q$ is positive definite on a dense set, (11.15) gives $\mathrm{d}w=0$ there, and hence everywhere. $\square$

The argument uses only the test function $w^2$. For general test functions, integration by parts introduces derivatives of $hT$; the condition $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(hT)=0$ alone does not make $L\varphi =(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \wedge T)/\mathrm{d}V$ a symmetric operator in divergence form.
