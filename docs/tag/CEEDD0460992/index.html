The critical coefficient and separated order

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 15.13.3 The critical coefficient and separated order

Corollary 15.13.4. For the fixed data, $c_0$ is the largest coefficient $c>0$ for which $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi _{a,d}$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)\geq c f\, \mathrm{d}W$. Among separated potentials $u=\psi +r(x)\preceq \phi _{a,d}$, the critical subsolutions are exactly the translates of $\psi +w_0$. Thus $v_0$ is their unique supremum-zero member, and $s_0$ is their greatest member with $\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{d}\nu _0\leq 0$.

Proof. For a separated critical subsolution, (15.71) and the product expansion give $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{a\omega _1}(r)\geq A_0\omega _1$. Closed curve truncations have mass $a$, so the non-pluripolar measure has mass at most $a$. Since $\int A_0\omega _1=a$, it must equal $A_0\omega _1$. The positive closed current $a\omega _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r$ also has mass $a$, so it has no remaining measure on the pole set. Consequently $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r=(A_0-a)\omega _1$ as distributions, and $r=w_0+C$ because harmonic distributions on $\mathbb P^1$ are constant. Supremum zero gives $C=-S_0$, while the mean condition gives $C\leq -J$.

For a general $u\preceq \phi _{a,d}$ with coefficient $c>0$, write $u=\psi +r$ by (15.71) and put $B_ r=\sup _ Xr$. Since $\psi \leq 0$, the potential $u+\sigma ^{-1}\log c-B_ r$ is an exponential subsolution for every $\sigma >0$. Greatestness gives

\[ u+\sigma ^{-1}\log c-B_ r\leq s_\sigma . \]

At a point off $D$ where $r$ is finite, subtract $\sigma ^{-1}\log c_0$, multiply by $\sigma $ and use the fixed-data case of theorem 15.13.3. This yields $\log c\leq \log c_0$. The potential $v_0$ attains $c_0$. $\square$

The scalar in the centering follows the varying data. Replacing $c_{0,j}$ by $c_0$ adds $\sigma _ j^{-1}\log (c_{0,j}/c_0)$ to the centered potential, and this term need not vanish. For example, take $a=1$, $d=1/4$, $H=1$ and $b=2+t$, where $t$ is the sphere height. Then $w_0=0$, $S_0=0$, $J=-1/4$ and $c_0=1$, so $s_0=\psi +1/4$ and the scalar-error limit is $1/4$. If $a_ j=1+\sqrt{\sigma _ j}$ and the other data are fixed, the appropriate centering uses $c_{0,j}=a_ j$. Centering by $1$ instead adds

\[ \frac{\log (1+\sqrt{\sigma _ j})}{\sigma _ j}\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

These examples can be taken on a non-Kähler manifold by choosing $Y$ to be the scalar Hopf surface with its Hermitian form rescaled to unit volume. The product cannot be Kähler, since a Kähler form would restrict to one on the Hopf fiber. The pole divisor remains present in the selected ordinary type.
