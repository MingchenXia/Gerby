16.6.4 All smooth obstaclesB61E129D450F

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.6.4 All smooth obstacles

We return to the Hermitian background $\omega $ on $X$. By proposition 16.6.6, a smooth change of background need not preserve convergence when the obstacle is kept at zero. Requiring convergence below every smooth obstacle gives a topology that is stable under such changes. The dyadic approximation still determines its analytic closure, which is now described by equality of the two envelopes below every obstacle.

Let $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $ be the topology of the uniformity generated by all the uniformities $\tau _{\omega ,F}$ with $F\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. Thus an entourage involves finitely many distances between obstacle envelopes, together with finitely many of the controlled ideal relations and divisorial distances defined above.

Proposition 16.6.7. A countable family $\{ F_ k\} _{k\geq 1}\subset C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$ that is dense for the uniform norm suffices for the obstacle coordinates of $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $. For smooth $\chi $ with $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi >0$, the map $[u]\mapsto [u-\chi ]$ is a uniform isomorphism between $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $ and $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_{\omega '}$. For $s>0$, positive scaling gives the corresponding uniform isomorphism onto $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_{s\omega }$. This topology is not $T_0$ on ordinary classes.

Proof. For either envelope operator, the obstacle comparison gives

\[ \| P_{\omega ,F}[u]-P_{\omega ,G}[u]\| _{L^1(\mathrm dV)} \leq \| F-G\| _\infty . \]

Hence the two envelope distances for a pair $[u],[v]$ differ by at most $2\| F-G\| _\infty $, uniformly in the pair. The space $C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$ contains a countable subset that is dense for the uniform norm: approximate each member of a countable dense subset of $C(X)$ by smooth functions with errors $1/m$, $m\geq 1$. Given an entourage, we approximate each of its finitely many obstacles by a member of the family and shrink the radii accordingly; this proves that the family generates the same uniformity. The divisorial coordinates still range over all fixed compact modifications.

The transport in proposition 16.6.5 relabels the entire family of smooth obstacles by $F\mapsto F-\chi $, or by $F\mapsto sF$. These are bijections of the family, which proves both isomorphisms.

For nonseparation, use the unbounded function $u=1-\sqrt{1-w}$ of proposition 16.6.4. It has trivial multiplier ideals and zero divisorial Lelong numbers, and $\delta w\preceq u$ for $0<\delta \leq 1$. Fix $F$ and put $q=P_\omega (F)$, the envelope of the obstacle $F$ in the sense of section 1.19. Since $\omega >0$, the constant $\min _ XF$ is a competitor, so $q$ is bounded and $q\leq F$. The function

\[ h_\delta =(1-\delta )q+\delta w-\delta \| F\| _\infty \]

is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, lies below $F$, and satisfies $h_\delta \preceq u$. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ off the pole of $w$ gives $P_{\omega ,F}[u]=q=P_{\omega ,F}[0]$. Since $u$ and $0$ have the same multiplier ideals, their ideal envelopes below $F$ also agree. Every generating entourage therefore has identical sections at $[u]$ and $[0]$, although these ordinary classes differ. $\square$

Below a smooth obstacle, the ideal envelope is the ordinary envelope of the ideal model obtained with the zero obstacle. Equality of the two envelopes below every obstacle therefore reduces to a question about ordinary envelopes alone.

Proposition 16.6.8. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and put $q=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$. For every smooth real $F$,

\[ P_{\theta ,F}[q]=P_{\theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}\sim q. \]

If $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,q)$, then also

\[ P_{\theta ,F}[u]=P_{\theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} \quad \text{for every smooth real }F. \]

Proof. Set $q_ F=P_{\theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$. Translation of competitors gives

\[ q+\min _ XF\leq q_ F\leq q+\max _ XF, \]

so $q_ F\sim q$. The identity $q_ F-F=P_{\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F}[u-F]_{\mathcal I}$ and theorem 16.2.2 give $q_ F\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. Since $q_ F\leq F$ and $q_ F\sim q$, it is a competitor for $P_{\theta ,F}[q]$. Conversely, the maximum of a competitor for $P_{\theta ,F}[q]$ with a sufficiently low translate of $q$ is a competitor for $P_{\theta ,F}[q]_{\mathcal I}$, and this ideal envelope equals $q_ F$ because $q\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. These bounds give

\[ q_ F\leq P_{\theta ,F}[q]\leq P_{\theta ,F}[q]_{\mathcal I}=q_ F. \]

The last assertion follows from proposition 13.4.5, which gives $P_{\theta ,F}[u]=P_{\theta ,F}[q]$. $\square$

Theorem 16.6.9. The closure and the sequential closure of $\mathcal A_\omega $ in $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $ are both equal to

\[ \mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega =\{ [u]:P_{\omega ,F}[u]=P_{\omega ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} \text{ for every smooth }F\} . \]

It suffices to test equality on the countable dense family above. The dyadic sequence $b_ j$ of corollary 16.5.6 converges to $[u]$ in this topology if and only if $[u]\in \mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $.

Proof. Fix a smooth obstacle $F$. As in the preceding proof, transport of the obstacle and theorem 16.2.2 give $P_{\omega ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. For $a$ with analytic singularities, lemma 1.17.10 therefore makes this ideal envelope a competitor for the ordinary envelope, so $P_{\omega ,F}[a]=P_{\omega ,F}[a]_{\mathcal I}$.

Write $p=P_{\omega ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$ and $q_ j=P_{\omega ,F}[b_ j]_{\mathcal I}$. The dyadic construction gives $u\preceq _{\mathcal I}b_ j$, decreasing ordinary types, and convergence of every local divisorial Lelong number of $b_ j$ to that of $u$. The ideal envelope preserves the ideal order, as one sees by taking maxima with a translate of $b_ j$ below $F$, so $p\leq q_ j\leq F$. Since $q_ j=P_{\omega ,F}[b_ j]$ and the ordinary types decrease, the sequence $q_ j$ decreases. Its limit $q$ is bounded below by $p$, so it is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, and $p\leq q\leq F$; the same bounds give convergence in $L^1$.

Convergence after pullback and semicontinuity of Lelong numbers, as in proposition 16.5.1, give $\nu _ E(q)\geq \nu _ E(u)$ for every local divisor $E$, and $p\leq q$ gives the reverse inequality. The Lelong-number criterion of theorem 1.16.3 yields $q\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. Since $q\leq F$, it is a competitor for $p$, which proves

\[ P_{\omega ,F}[b_ j]=P_{\omega ,F}[b_ j]_{\mathcal I} \searrow P_{\omega ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}. \]

The controlled ideal relations and all divisorial distances on fixed modifications converge by the proof of theorem 16.6.3; they do not depend on $F$. Consequently this one dyadic sequence converges in $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $ exactly when the stated envelope equalities hold.

For each $F$, equality of the two $L^1$ envelope coordinates is a closed condition. Analytic classes satisfy all these equalities, so the closure of the analytic classes under nets lies in $\mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $, and the dyadic sequence proves the converse. Finally, the uniform obstacle bound in the proof of proposition 16.6.7 extends equality from a dense family to every smooth obstacle. $\square$

Before turning to examples, we record how the envelopes and the relative envelope classes behave under finite unramified coverings; this will be used for fibrations with disconnected fibers. Recall from definition 13.4.1 that $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi )$ means that $u\preceq \varphi $ and that, for every $a\in (0,1)$, there is a stretching competitor $h\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $a\varphi +(1-a)h\leq u$; here $a=1-b^{-1}$ in the notation of that definition. Pullback of competitors and the maximum over the fibers of the covering identify the envelopes upstairs and downstairs, and the same fiber maximum transfers stretching competitors from the covering space to the base.

Proposition 16.6.10. Let $r:Y\to X$ be a finite unramified holomorphic covering between connected compact complex manifolds. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $u,\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and put $\theta '=r^*\theta $ and $u'=u\circ r$. For every $F\in C(X,\mathbb R)$,

\[ \begin{split} P_{\theta ',F\circ r}[u']& =P_{\theta ,F}[u]\circ r,\\ P_{\theta ',F\circ r}[u']_{\mathcal I} & =P_{\theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}\circ r. \end{split} \]

Moreover,

\[ u'\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(Y,\theta ',\varphi \circ r) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ). \]

Proof. For $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta ')$, define

\[ v_{\max }(x)=\max _{y\in r^{-1}(x)}v(y). \]

Over a sufficiently small ball in $X$, this is the finite maximum of the functions obtained by composing $v$ with the local inverse branches of $r$. Each of them is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, so $v_{\max }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by lemma 1.3.1. If $v\preceq u'$, then $v_{\max }\preceq u$. If $v\sim _{\mathcal I}u'$, a local biholomorphic change of variables makes each branch multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u$, and the maximum rule in lemma 1.16.4 gives $v_{\max }\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. The obstacle bound $v\leq F\circ r$ gives $v_{\max }\leq F$.

Thus every competitor upstairs is dominated by the pullback of a corresponding competitor downstairs. Conversely, pullback of a competitor downstairs preserves the background inequality, the ordinary order or multiplier-ideal equivalence, and the obstacle bound. These two correspondences between the competitor families give the envelope identities; upper regularization commutes with $r$, since $r$ is locally biholomorphic.

Since $r$ is surjective, it reflects the ordinary order: $u'\preceq \varphi \circ r$ if and only if $u\preceq \varphi $. Fix $a\in (0,1)$. Pullback takes any stretching competitor $h$ with $a\varphi +(1-a)h\leq u$ to a stretching competitor upstairs. Conversely, if $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta ')$ satisfies

\[ a\varphi \circ r+(1-a)v\leq u', \]

then taking the maximum over the fibers gives $a\varphi +(1-a)v_{\max }\leq u$; this also holds at poles, because the convex sums are defined there. Applying the definition of the relative class for every $a$ proves the last assertion. $\square$

Taking $F=0$ gives $\mathcal G^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega \subseteq \mathcal G_\omega $. For the reverse inclusion, let $q=P_\omega [u]_{\mathcal I}$. By proposition 16.6.8, it suffices to show that $P_{\omega ,F}[u]=P_{\omega ,F}[q]$ for every $F$, and membership $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega ,q)$ is sufficient for this. Under bounded mass and positive lower volume of $q$, this membership is equivalent to equality of the envelopes below zero, by proposition 30.8.2. On curves the two closures coincide even at zero mass, by proposition 30.12.10. We do not know whether the reverse inclusion holds in general. The following subsections prove equality for explicit classes of potentials in higher dimension, including types of zero volume. The reduction along a divisor in a fibration gives such classes over curves, and one example of this kind has a base potential in the required relative envelope class and an ambient background with unbounded mass (example 16.6.18). Although $\tau ^{\mathrm{all}}_\omega $ removes the dependence on a fixed obstacle, it still has incomplete curve families and is strictly weaker than polar total variation on ordinary curve models, by proposition 30.12.12. On the product and fibration model loci constructed below, complete metrics come instead from polar curvature.
