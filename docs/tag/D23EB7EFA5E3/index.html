From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 31.5.1

Theorem 31.5.1. Let $\mu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure with $\mu (X)>0$. For every $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathcal E_0$ satisfying

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta ^{\mathrm{ext}}(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu . \]

There also exist $v\in \mathcal E_0$, normalized by $\sup _ Xv=0$, and a unique positive scalar $c$ such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta ^{\mathrm{ext}}(v)=c\mu . \]

Proof. For the reference zero, $P_{\epsilon \theta }(0)=0$ for every $\epsilon >0$. Letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ in theorem 13.5.3 therefore gives

31.11
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w)(X)\geq v_-(\theta ) \qquad (w\in \mathcal E_0). \end{equation}

Positive exponent. By lemma 14.23.2, write $\mu =f\nu $ with $\nu =\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )$, where $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is bounded and $0\leq f\in L^1(\nu )$. For positive integers $j$, let $f_ j=\min (f,j)$. These densities are bounded, and the measures $f_ j\nu $ are nonzero. By theorem 14.9.1 there are bounded $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j\nu . \]

These bounded potentials belong to $\mathcal E_0$ by proposition 13.4.2. Since $f_ j$ increases with $j$, exponential order (corollary 31.2.4) shows that $u_ j$ decreases; in particular $u_ j\leq u_1$. On the other hand, since $f_ j\leq f$,

\[ v_-(\theta )\leq \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)(X) \leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_ j}\mu (X), \]

so the suprema of the $u_ j$ have a common lower bound. Hence the decreasing limit $u$ is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function, not identically $-\infty $, and $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$. Moreover

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_1}\mu . \]

Applying theorem 31.3.3 with reference zero, we obtain $u\in \mathcal E_0$ and weak convergence of the Monge–Ampère measures to $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$. The right-hand sides converge in total variation to $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\nu $, since the functions $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j$ converge $\nu $-almost everywhere and are bounded by $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_1}f\in L^1(\nu )$. This proves the equation, and corollary 31.2.4 gives uniqueness.

Zero exponent. For each positive integer $j$, let $u_ j\in \mathcal E_0$ be the solution just constructed with exponent $1/j$. To bound the normalization, we compare with the solution $s\in \mathcal E_0$ for exponent one, so that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (s)=\mathrm{e}^ s\mu $. Set $H=\max (0,\sup _ Xs)$ and $w_ j=s+(j-1)H$. Then $w_ j/j\geq s$, and

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-w_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w_ j) \leq \mathrm{e}^{-s}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (s) =\mu =\mathrm{e}^{-u_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j). \]

Exponential order, now with exponent $1/j$, gives $u_ j\leq w_ j$. Put

\[ M_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j,\qquad v_ j=u_ j-M_ j, \qquad c_ j=\mathrm{e}^{M_ j/j}. \]

Then $\sup _ Xv_ j=0$ and $0<c_ j\leq \mathrm{e}^ H$, because $M_ j\leq \sup _ Xw_ j\leq jH$. By (31.11),

\[ v_-(\theta )\leq c_ j\int _ X \mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}\, \mathrm{d}\mu \leq c_ j\mu (X), \]

so $c_ j\geq v_-(\theta )/\mu (X)>0$. After extraction, theorem 1.4.2 gives $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$, where $v\not\equiv -\infty $ and $\sup _ Xv=0$, and $c_ j\to c>0$.

By lemma 1.23.3, $a_ j=\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}\to 1$ in $L^1(\mu )$. Consequently

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j)=c_ ja_ j\mu \longrightarrow c\mu \quad \hbox{in total variation},\qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j)\leq \mathrm{e}^ H\mu . \]

Applying theorem 31.3.3 again, we obtain $v\in \mathcal E_0$ and identify the limit as $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v)$; thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v)=c\mu $. The scalar assertion of corollary 31.2.4, applied in both directions to two solutions, proves that $c$ is unique. $\square$
