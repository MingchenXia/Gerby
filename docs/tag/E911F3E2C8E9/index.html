Lelong level sets

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.13.1 Lelong level sets

Siu's analyticity theorem states that, for every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ and every $c>0$, the upper level set $\{ x:\nu (u,x)\geq c\} $ is an analytic subset. After a smooth local correction, this is the theorem for closed positive currents, which we use in the form recalled in [SGPT]; see section 34.1 for the original source.

For a closed positive current, the Lelong monotonicity formula also bounds the trace mass of small balls. The resulting Hausdorff estimate shows that a closed positive $(j,j)$-current puts no mass on sets of zero Hausdorff $2(n-j)$-measure.

Corollary 1.13.2. Let $T$ be a closed positive $(j,j)$-current on a complex manifold of dimension $n$, with $1\leq j<n$. Its coefficient measures vanish on every Borel set of zero Hausdorff $2(n-j)$-measure.

Proof. In a coordinate chart put $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and $\mu =T\wedge \kappa ^{n-j}$. By the Lelong monotonicity formula in bidimension $(n-j,n-j)$, the function $r\mapsto r^{-2(n-j)}\mu (B(x,r))$ is nondecreasing; see [Chapter III, formulas (5.5)–(5.8), pp. 158–159, DemCADG]. Fix a relatively compact inner chart and a radius $r_0>0$ such that all balls of radius $r_0$ centered in it lie in a fixed compact subset of the chart. Their masses are bounded by the mass of that compact set. Hence

\[ \mu (B(x,r))\leq Cr^{2(n-j)},\qquad 0<r\leq r_0, \]

uniformly for centers in the inner chart. A Borel set in the inner chart with zero Hausdorff $2(n-j)$-measure can be covered by balls $B(x_ i,r_ i)$ with $\sum _ ir_ i^{2(n-j)}$ arbitrarily small, so it has zero $\mu $-mass. Since $T$ is positive, the total variation of each of its coefficient measures is bounded by a constant multiple of $\mu $. A countable cover by charts proves the assertion. $\square$

The non-strict inequality in Siu's theorem cannot be replaced by a strict one, even on a compact Kähler manifold.

Example 1.13.3. Let $X=\mathbb P^2$, with homogeneous coordinates $[x:y:z]$, and let $\sigma $ be the Fubini–Study form normalized by $\int _ X\sigma ^2=1$. Put $\| Z\| ^2=|x|^2+|y|^2+|z|^2$, $a_ j=2^{-j}$ and $\lambda _ j=2^{-j-1}$ for $j\geq 1$. The global function

\[ u([Z])=\log \frac{|y|^2}{\| Z\| ^2} +\sum _{j\geq 1}\lambda _ j \log \frac{|x-a_ jz|^2}{(1+a_ j^2)\| Z\| ^2} \]

belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\frac32\sigma )$. For the line $D=\{ y=0\} $ and the points $p_ j=[a_ j:0:1]$, it satisfies

\[ \{ \nu (u,\cdot )\geq 1\} =D, \qquad \{ \nu (u,\cdot )>1\} =\{ p_ j:j\geq 1\} . \]

The second set is not analytic.

Proof. For a linear form $\ell $ with coefficient vector of norm one, the function $v_\ell =\log (|\ell (Z)|^2/\| Z\| ^2)$ is nonpositive and satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\ell =[\ell =0]-\sigma $. Its $L^1(X,\sigma ^2)$ norm is finite and independent of $\ell $, because the unitary group $U(3)$ acts on homogeneous coordinates, preserves $\sigma $, and acts transitively on such forms. Since $\sum _ j\lambda _ j=1/2$, the partial sums of the series defining $u$ decrease to an $L^1$ function. They have the common curvature lower bound $-\frac32\sigma $, so their limit is quasi-plurisubharmonic and

\[ T:=\tfrac 32\sigma +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=[D]+\sum _{j\geq 1}\lambda _ j[L_ j], \qquad L_ j=\{ x-a_ jz=0\} . \]

The series of positive closed currents converges, since every line has $\sigma $-trace mass one and the coefficients are summable.

We compute the Lelong numbers from the local mass formula. In affine coordinates $w=(w_1,w_2)$ centered at a point $q$, put $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|w|^2$. An affine complex line $H$ at distance $d$ from $q$ satisfies

\[ \int _{H\cap B(q,r)}\kappa =(r^2-d^2)_+. \]

Consequently $r^{-2}\int _{H\cap B(q,r)}\kappa $ is at most one and tends to $\mathbf1_ H(q)$ as $r\downarrow 0$; a line that does not meet the chart contributes zero. Tonelli's theorem and dominated convergence for the summable coefficients therefore give

\[ \nu (u,q)=\nu (T,q) =\mathbf1_ D(q)+\sum _{j\geq 1}\lambda _ j\mathbf1_{L_ j}(q). \]

Here we used that a smooth local correction does not change the Lelong number. The computation also covers points at which infinitely many lines accumulate.

The lines $L_ j$ meet $D$ at the distinct points $p_ j$ and meet one another only at $p_\infty =[0:1:0]\notin D$. Thus $\nu (u,p_ j)=1+\lambda _ j$, whereas $\nu (u,q)=1$ for $q\in D\setminus \{ p_ j:j\geq 1\} $. Outside $D$, the Lelong number is at most $\sum _ j\lambda _ j=1/2$. This proves the two level-set identities. Finally, $p_ j\to p_*=[0:0:1]$ and $\nu (u,p_*)=1$, so the strict level set is not closed and hence not analytic. $\square$
