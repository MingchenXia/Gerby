Hopf fibrations and toric potentials

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 22 Hopf fibrations and toric potentials

Let $n\geq 2$ and let $X_ n$ be the scalar Hopf manifold of section 20.6, which fibers over $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ with compact elliptic fibers. A potential in a pulled-back closed class is constant on each fiber and therefore descends to the base. For such a weight, the weighted section integrands also descend, and fiber integration identifies the section norms and Bergman kernels on the two spaces.

A potential of the standard Gauduchon form may vary along the fibers. For one that descends, the vertical part of this form supplies a single factor in the Hermitian Monge–Ampère measure, and fiber integration reduces the equation to the projective base. For bounded descended potentials, closedness on the base determines the normalizing constant and gives uniqueness up to additive constants. On the Hopf threefold, the descended masses are at most six, so unbounded masses require variation along the fibers.

On the base, invariance under separate rotations of the homogeneous coordinates gives a real convex description in logarithmic coordinates. Strict positivity of the curvature makes the gradient a parametrization of the relative interior of a simplex.

Write $\pi :X_ n\to \mathbb P^{n-1}$ for the projection. The forms $\alpha ,\zeta ,\omega $ and the probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V=\omega ^ n/(2n)$ are as in section 20.6. Write $\sigma $ for the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ normalized by $\int _{\mathbb P^{n-1}}\sigma ^{n-1}=1$, and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}$ for the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 6.1.2. The fiber calculations use the following identities, where $y\in \mathbb P^{n-1}$:

\[ \alpha =\pi ^*\sigma ,\qquad \omega =\alpha +\zeta ,\qquad \zeta ^2=0,\qquad \mathrm{d}V=\tfrac 12\alpha ^{n-1}\wedge \zeta ,\qquad \int _{\pi ^{-1}(y)}\zeta =2. \]
