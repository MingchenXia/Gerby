13.24.6 A countable divisorial positivity criterion1D7E7321D20C

A prescribed divisorial part can determine an ideal envelope's ordinary type even when it has infinitely many components. On the projective plane with a pluriclosed background, the smooth residual determines the mass of every representative of that type. This gives a positivity test directly from divisorial orders.

Let $X=\mathbb P^2$, and let $\kappa $ be the Fubini–Study form with $\int _ X\kappa ^2=1$. Choose distinct hyperplanes $H_ j$ and coefficients $a_ j>0$ with $A=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j<\infty $. For unit defining sections $s_ j$, put

\[ \ell _ j(Z)=\log \frac{|s_ j(Z)|^2}{\| Z\| ^2}\leq 0, \qquad u_0=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j\ell _ j, \qquad u=u_0-\sup _ Xu_0. \]

Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form satisfying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta =0$ and $R=\theta -A\kappa >0$.

Lemma 13.24.7.487BF774470F The series defines a quasi-plurisubharmonic function, and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=S-A\kappa ,\qquad S=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j[H_ j],\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=R+S>0. \]

For every prime divisor $E$ on every smooth compact modification $\pi :Z\to X$, the convergent order profile is

\[ b(E):=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j\operatorname {ord}_ E(\pi ^*H_ j) =\nu (u\circ \pi ,E). \]

Proof. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ell _ j=[H_ j]-\kappa $. Unitary invariance reduces its integral to the distribution of $x=|Z_0|^2/\| Z\| ^2$, whose density on $[0,1]$ is $2(1-x)$. Thus

\[ \int _ X\ell _ j\kappa ^2=\int _0^1 2(1-x)\log x\, \mathrm dx=-\frac32. \]

The decreasing series has finite $L^1$ norm and a common lower curvature bound $-A\kappa $. Its canonical limit is quasi-plurisubharmonic. Distributional convergence gives the current identities and strict positivity.

Fix $\pi $ and $E$, and choose a Gauduchon form $\gamma $ on the compact surface $Z$. For the tail $r_ N=\sum _{j>N}a_ j\ell _ j$ of total coefficient $A_ N=\sum _{j>N}a_ j$, the closed positive current

\[ T_ N=A_ N\pi ^*\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(r_ N\circ \pi ) \]

has trace mass $A_ N\int _ Z\pi ^*\kappa \wedge \gamma $, by distributional Stokes. The pullback of $r_ N$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, since $\pi $ is an isomorphism on a dense open set where $r_ N$ is finite almost everywhere. Divisorial removal gives

\[ 0\leq \nu (r_ N\circ \pi ,E) \leq A_ N\frac{\int _ Z\pi ^*\kappa \wedge \gamma }{\int _ E\gamma }. \]

The denominator is positive and the ratio is finite. Finite additivity of Lelong numbers now identifies the partial profile with $\nu (u\circ \pi ,E)$ up to an error tending to zero. This proves convergence and the asserted equality. $\square$

Proposition 13.24.8.20027E32C14E Let $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The conditions

\[ \nu (v\circ \pi ,E)=b(E)\text{ for every }\pi ,E, \qquad v\sim _{\mathcal I}u \]

are equivalent. Under either condition,

\[ q=P_\theta [v]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}\sim u, \]

and every $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w\sim q$ satisfies

027648EDCB33 13.24.9
\begin{equation} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q) =\int _ XR^2>0. \end{equation}

The background is big and $X$ has the bounded mass property.

Proof. Ideal profile and envelope. Choose an integer $C>A$ with $C\kappa \geq \theta $. Both $u$ and $v$ are $C\kappa $-plurisubharmonic. The projective valuative characterization of all-weight ideal types applies in this closed background: generic orders on all smooth projective modifications recover point Lelong numbers by further point blowups, and equality of those numbers is equivalent to ideal equivalence. The stated profile therefore implies $v\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. Conversely, ideal equivalence pulls back under every compact modification by lemma 5.43.23; the local valuative criterion theorem 5.37.3 then gives equality of generic coefficients on every such modification. The common factor between growth against $\log |z|$ and the book's $\log |z|^2$ convention does not affect these equalities.

The potential $u$ has complex Hessian $S-A\kappa $. By corollary 18.4.9, its ordinary and ideal envelopes agree and are ordinarily equivalent to $u$. Ideal equivalence identifies the defining families for the two ideal envelopes in the statement, proving the assertion about $q$. The inequality $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq R>0$ proves bigness. For any Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, choose $D$ with $D\kappa \geq h$. Bounded $h$-plurisubharmonic potentials are $D\kappa $-plurisubharmonic, and positivity of bounded mixed products bounds their masses by $\int _ X(D\kappa )^2=D^2$. This proves the bounded mass property.

The bounded residual. Fix $w\sim u$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and put $T=C\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$. Its base divisorial coefficients are $a_ j$, so divisorial removal and passage through increasing finite sums give

\[ K=T-S=(C-A)\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\geq 0,\qquad h=w-u. \]

The distributional difference has a canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representative, which is bounded by ordinary equivalence and almost-everywhere order. Thus $K$ has bounded local potentials, and its trace charges no pluripolar set. The current $S$ is carried by the actual pole union $\bigcup _ jH_ j\subset \{ w=-\infty \} $. Thus $\langle T\rangle =K$. For every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $L$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}L=0$, distributional Stokes gives

\[ \int _ X L\wedge \langle T\rangle =(C-A)\int _ XL\wedge \kappa . \]

Closed top mass and smooth correction. Put $T_ u=C\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=S+(C-A)\kappa $. For the finite partial sums $u_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j\ell _ j$, the closed non-pluripolar mass is $(C-\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j)^2$: outside the finite divisor union the current is the smooth residual, and the measure gives that union zero mass. Since $u_0\leq u_ N$, closed-background mass monotonicity, the closed case of theorem 13.17.1, gives

\[ \int _ X\langle T_ u^2\rangle \leq \left(C-\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j\right)^2. \]

For the reverse inequality, locally write $S=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b$ with $b$ plurisubharmonic. The currents $(C-A)\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (b,-k)$ have bounded local potentials and dominate $(C-A)\kappa $. Their squared products, restricted to $\{ b>-k\} $, therefore dominate $(C-A)^2\kappa ^2$ on that set. Letting $k$ increase and using the local truncation definition gives $\langle T_ u^2\rangle \geq (C-A)^2\kappa ^2$. The two bounds prove equality of total masses. Closed mass invariance under bounded changes then gives

\[ \int _ X\langle T^2\rangle =(C-A)^2. \]

Set $L=C\kappa -\theta \geq 0$, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}L=0$. Expanding the bounded products of $\max (w,-k)$ in the two backgrounds and restricting to $\{ w>-k\} $ gives, in the limit,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) =\langle T^2\rangle -2L\wedge \langle T\rangle +L^2. \]

All correction measures are finite: the closed top measure has the mass just computed, the degree-one limit is $K$, and the smooth measure $L^2$ gives the pole set zero mass. The same truncations are used in both backgrounds, so this identity is an equality of Borel measures. Integrating yields

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) =(C-A)^2-2(C-A)\int _ XL\wedge \kappa +\int _ XL^2 =\int _ X(\theta -A\kappa )^2. \]

This holds throughout the ordinary type, proving both singularity-volume identities and their strict positivity. $\square$

Example 13.24.10.E76D9C1B44B6 There is a nonclosed pluriclosed background on $\mathbb P^2$ with a nonanalytic ideal type satisfying 13.24.8. Its prescribed divisors are not contained in a single pencil, and both singularity volumes of its ideal model are at least $1/16$.

Proof. In a coordinate ball choose a smooth real cutoff $\chi $ equal to one near the origin, and extend $\alpha =\chi |z_2|^2\mathrm dz_1$ by zero. Put

\[ \tau =\partial \overline\alpha +\bar\partial \alpha . \]

It is real and smooth, with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0$. Where $\chi =1$,

\[ \mathrm d\tau =\mathrm dz_2\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_2 \wedge (\mathrm dz_1-\mathrm d\bar z_1)\ne 0. \]

Choose $\epsilon >0$ small enough that $\theta =\kappa +\epsilon \tau \geq \kappa /2$. Set $A=1/4$, $a_ j=A2^{-j}$, $t_ j=2^{-j}$ and

\[ H_ j=\{ Z_0+t_ jZ_1+t_ j^2Z_2=0\} . \]

The Vandermonde determinant shows that any three defining linear forms are independent; hence the hyperplanes are not in one pencil. Here $R=\theta -A\kappa \geq \kappa /4$, so $\int _ XR^2\geq 1/16$. The preceding proposition gives the mass throughout the ordinary type and both singularity volumes. There are infinitely many positive base divisor coefficients. An analytic singularity on a compact manifold has only finitely many prime divisorial components, so no analytic potential has this ideal type. $\square$

Problem 13.24.11.D0837305A3CF For a big nonclosed background $\theta $ on a manifold with the bounded mass property, find criteria for $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I})>0$ in terms of restricted multiplier ideals or divisorial orders. When this lower volume vanishes, which implications between envelope equality and upper-volume equality, conditions (i) and (ii) of proposition 13.24.2, remain valid?

One implication fails: when the ideal envelope has zero lower volume, equality of the upper volumes of a potential and of its ideal envelope does not force equality of the envelopes. In example 18.15.5, the background $\Theta $ is positive and nonclosed on a projective manifold with the bounded mass property, and $q=P_\Theta [a]_{\mathcal I}$ satisfies $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (a)=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (q)=0$ but $P_\Theta [a]\ne q$. The example leaves the other implications in 13.24.11 undecided.
