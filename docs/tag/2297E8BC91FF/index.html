Proposition 5.47.152297E8BC91FF

Proposition 5.47.15.2297E8BC91FF Let $X$ be a complex manifold and $a\in X$.

  1. A set $U\subset X$ containing $a$ is a plurifine neighborhood of $a$ if and only if $X\setminus U$ is plurithin at $a$.

  2. In a chart around $a$, the sets $B\cap \{ \psi >0\} $ containing $a$, where $B$ is a sufficiently small coordinate ball centered at $a$ and $\psi $ is a bounded plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $\overline B$, form a base of plurifine neighborhoods of $a$.

  3. The plurifine topology of an open subset of $X$ is the topology induced by the plurifine topology of $X$. Biholomorphic maps are homeomorphisms for the plurifine topologies, and holomorphic maps between complex manifolds are continuous for the plurifine topologies.

Proof. We begin with the first two assertions of (3). Let $W\subset X$ be open. Every subbasic set of the plurifine topology of $W$ is a subbasic set of the plurifine topology of $X$. Conversely, the intersection of a subbasic set of $X$ with $W$ is subbasic for $W$, because the restriction of a function $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(V)$ to $V\cap W$ is plurisubharmonic. Since the sets $S\cap W$, with $S$ subbasic for $X$, form a subbasis of the induced topology, the plurifine topology of $W$ is the induced topology.

Next, if $F$ is a biholomorphism, then $F^{-1}(\{ u>t\} )=\{ u\circ F>t\} $, and $u\circ F$ is plurisubharmonic whenever $u$ is. Indeed, by proposition 5.9.3, in connected charts $u\circ F$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $, and the second alternative would make $u$ identically $-\infty $ on a nonempty open set, which is impossible because $u$ is locally integrable (theorem 5.9.1). Applying this to $F$ and to $F^{-1}$, we see that both map subbasic sets to subbasic sets. In particular, we may compute plurifine neighborhoods of $a$ in a chart, as if $X$ were an open subset of $\mathbb C^ n$.

(2) Each set $B\cap \{ \psi >0\} $ as in (2) is plurifine open, as the intersection of a Euclidean open set with a subbasic set. Now let $N$ be a plurifine neighborhood of $a$. By the definition of the topology, $N$ contains a set

\[ N_0=W_0\cap \{ u_1>c_1\} \cap \cdots \cap \{ u_ k>c_ k\} \]

containing $a$, where $W_0$ is a Euclidean open neighborhood of $a$, $k\ge 0$, each $u_ i$ is plurisubharmonic on a Euclidean open neighborhood $V_ i$ of $a$, and $c_ i\in \mathbb R$. If $k=0$, every coordinate ball $B\subset W_0$ centered at $a$ works, with $\psi =1$. Let $k\ge 1$. Replacing $u_ i$ by $\max \{ u_ i,c_ i-1\} $, which does not change $\{ u_ i>c_ i\} $, we may assume that the $u_ i$ are locally bounded. Put $\delta _ i=u_ i(a)-c_ i>0$. By upper semicontinuity there is a coordinate ball $B'$ centered at $a$, contained in $W_0$ and in every $V_ i$, on which every $u_ i$ is bounded and satisfies $u_ i<u_ i(a)+\delta _ i/(2k)$. Let $B$ be any coordinate ball centered at $a$ with $\overline B\subset B'$, and put

\[ \psi =\frac14+\sum _{i=1}^ k\frac{u_ i-u_ i(a)}{\delta _ i}. \]

This is a bounded plurisubharmonic function on $B'$ with $\psi (a)=1/4>0$. If $z\in B$ and $u_ i(z)\le c_ i$ for some $i$, then the $i$-th term of the sum is at most $-1$ and each of the other $k-1$ terms is less than $1/(2k)$, so $\psi (z)\le 1/4-1+(k-1)/(2k)<0$. Hence $B\cap \{ \psi >0\} \subset N_0\subset N$, which proves (2).

(1) Let $U$ be a plurifine neighborhood of $a$, and put $E=X\setminus U$; note that $a\notin E$. By (2), $U$ contains a set $B\cap \{ \psi >0\} $ with $\psi (a)>0$, so $\psi \le 0$ on $E\cap B$. Hence either $a$ is not in the Euclidean closure of $E$, or $\varlimsup _{E\ni z\to a}\psi (z)\le 0<\psi (a)$; in both cases $E$ is plurithin at $a$. Conversely, suppose that $E=X\setminus U$ is plurithin at $a\in U$. If $a$ is not in the Euclidean closure of $E$, then $U$ contains a Euclidean neighborhood of $a$. Otherwise let $\psi $ be as in definition 5.47.14, and choose $c$ with $\varlimsup _{E\ni z\to a}\psi (z)<c<\psi (a)$. Then $\psi <c$ on $E\cap B$ for a small ball $B$ centered at $a$, so the plurifine open set $B\cap \{ \psi >c\} $ contains $a$ and is contained in $U$.

(3) It remains to prove that a holomorphic map $F\colon Y\to X$ is continuous for the plurifine topologies. Preimages of Euclidean open sets are Euclidean open. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(V)$ with $V\subset X$ open, let $t\in \mathbb R$, and let $y\in F^{-1}(\{ u>t\} )$. Choose a connected coordinate neighborhood $Y'$ of $y$ such that $F(Y')$ lies in a connected coordinate neighborhood contained in $V$. By proposition 5.9.3, $u\circ F$ is either identically $-\infty $ or plurisubharmonic on $Y'$; the first alternative is excluded because $u(F(y))>t$. Thus $Y'\cap \{ u\circ F>t\} $ is a plurifine open neighborhood of $y$ contained in $F^{-1}(\{ u>t\} )$. Hence preimages of subbasic sets are plurifine open. $\square$
