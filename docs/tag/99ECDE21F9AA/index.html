The case of analytic singularities

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · The case of analytic singularities

Definition 10.4.3. When $T$ is a Kähler current with analytic singularities, we take a modification $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ so that

  1. 10.84
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:resolveanalytic} \pi ^*T=[D]+R, \end{equation}

    where $D$ is an effective $\mathbb {Q}$-divisor on $Y$ and $R$ is a closed positive $(1,1)$-current with bounded potential, and

  2. the lifting $(Z_{\bullet },g)$ of $Y_{\bullet }$ to $Y$ exists.

Define

10.85
\begin{equation} \label{eq:POBanalsing} \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)\coloneqq \Delta _{Z_{\bullet }}(\{ R\} )g^{-1}+\nu _{Z_{\bullet }}([D])g^{-1}. \end{equation}
The existence of $\pi $ is guaranteed by theorem 1.6.7 and theorem 10.2.11.

Lemma 10.4.4. The convex body $\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)$ defined in definition 10.4.3 is independent of the choice of $\pi $.

Proof. Take another map $\pi '\colon Y'\rightarrow X$ with the same properties. We want to show that $\pi $ and $\pi '$ define the same $\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)$. We may assume that $\pi '$ dominates $\pi $ through $p\colon Y'\rightarrow Y$, so that we have a commutative diagram

We take $D$ and $R$ as in (10.84). Then

\[ \pi '^*T=[p^*D]+p^*R. \]

Write $(Z_{\bullet },g)$ and $(Z_{\bullet }',g')$ for the liftings of $Y_{\bullet }$ to $Y$ and $Y'$ respectively. We need to prove that

\[ \Delta _{Z_{\bullet }}(\{ R\} )g^{-1}+\nu _{Z_{\bullet }}([D])g^{-1}=\Delta _{Z_{\bullet }'}(\{ p^*R\} )g'^{-1}+\nu _{Z_{\bullet }'}([p^*D])g'^{-1}. \]

By theorem 10.4.2, applied to the class $\{ R\} $, we have

\[ \Delta _{Z'_\bullet }(\{ p^*R\} )=\Delta _{Z_\bullet }(\{ R\} )\, \operatorname{cor}(Z_\bullet ,p). \]

By proposition 10.2.9, applied to the divisor current $[D]$, we also have

\[ \nu _{Z'_\bullet }([p^*D])=\nu _{Z_\bullet }([D])\, \operatorname{cor}(Z_\bullet ,p). \]

Finally, proposition 10.2.10 gives

\[ g'=g\, \operatorname{cor}(Z_\bullet ,p). \]

Multiplying by $(g')^{-1}$ gives the desired equality. $\square$

The above proof also describes the bimeromorphic behavior of $\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)$:

Lemma 10.4.5. Let $T\in \mathcal{Z}_{+}(X,\alpha )$ be a Kähler current with analytic singularities. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism and $(W_{\bullet },g)$ be the lifting of $Y_{\bullet }$ to $Y$. Then

\[ \Delta _{W_{\bullet }}(\pi ^*T)=\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)g. \]

Lemma 10.4.6. Assume that $T,S\in \mathcal{Z}_+(X,\alpha )$ are two Kähler currents with analytic singularities and $T\preceq S$. Then

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(S)\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\alpha ). \]

Moreover,

10.86
\begin{equation} \label{eq:volpobanaly} \operatorname{vol}\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)=\frac{1}{n!} \int _ X T^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. We first show that

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(S). \]

Using 10.4.5, we may assume that $T$ and $S$ have log singularities along effective $\mathbb {Q}$-divisors $E$ and $F$ respectively. By assumption, $E\geq F$. Replacing $T$ and $S$ by $T-[F]$ and $S-[F]$ respectively, we may assume that $F=0$, cf. (10.85).

In this case, we need to show that

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\alpha )\supseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\alpha -\{ E\} )+\nu _{Y_{\bullet }}([E]), \]

which is obvious.

Next we prove that

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(T)\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\alpha ). \]

By 10.4.5 and theorem 10.4.2 again, we may assume that $T$ has log singularities. We take $D$ and $R$ as in (10.84). We need to show that

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\alpha -\{ D\} )+\nu _{Y_{\bullet }}([D])\subseteq \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\alpha ), \]

which is again obvious.

Finally, (10.86) follows immediately from theorem 10.4.2. $\square$
