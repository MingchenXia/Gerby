15.37.27 General measures in the minimal envelope class0E0ED0471018

We first take the reference zero. It has minimal singularities because $\theta \geq 0$, and it satisfies (16.28.67) for every $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$; we call $\mathcal E_0$ the minimal envelope class. By lemma 15.24.4, a finite non-pluripolar measure has an integrable density with respect to the Monge–Ampère measure of a bounded potential. Truncating that density gives bounded solutions whose measures are dominated by one fixed finite measure, and dominated continuity passes to the full density. Letting the exponent tend to zero then produces the normalized equation.

Theorem 15.37.28.D23EB7EFA5E3 Let $\mu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure with $\mu (X)>0$. For every $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathcal E_0$ satisfying

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta ^{\mathrm{ext}}(u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu . \]

There also exist $v\in \mathcal E_0$, normalized by $\sup _ Xv=0$, and a unique positive scalar $c$ such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta ^{\mathrm{ext}}(v)=c\mu . \]

Proof. For the reference zero, $P_{\epsilon \theta }(0)=0$ for every $\epsilon >0$. Letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ in theorem 16.28.70 therefore gives

AFE1DD54089D 15.37.29
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w)(X)\geq v_-(\theta ) \qquad (w\in \mathcal E_0). \end{equation}

Positive exponent. By lemma 15.24.4, write $\mu =f\nu $ with $\nu =\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )$, where $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is bounded and $0\leq f\in L^1(\nu )$. For positive integers $j$, let $f_ j=\min (f,j)$. These densities are bounded, and the measures $f_ j\nu $ are nonzero. By theorem 15.10.1 there are bounded $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j\nu . \]

These bounded potentials belong to $\mathcal E_0$ by proposition 16.28.62. Since $f_ j$ increases with $j$, exponential order (corollary 15.37.11) shows that $u_ j$ decreases; in particular $u_ j\leq u_1$. On the other hand, since $f_ j\leq f$,

\[ v_-(\theta )\leq \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)(X) \leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_ j}\mu (X), \]

so the suprema of the $u_ j$ have a common lower bound. Hence the decreasing limit $u$ is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function, not identically $-\infty $, and $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$. Moreover

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_1}\mu . \]

Applying theorem 15.37.19 with reference zero, we obtain $u\in \mathcal E_0$ and weak convergence of the Monge–Ampère measures to $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$. The right-hand sides converge in total variation to $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\nu $, since the functions $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j$ converge $\nu $-almost everywhere and are bounded by $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_1}f\in L^1(\nu )$. This proves the equation, and corollary 15.37.11 gives uniqueness.

Zero exponent. For each positive integer $j$, let $u_ j\in \mathcal E_0$ be the solution just constructed with exponent $1/j$. To bound the normalization, we compare with the solution $s\in \mathcal E_0$ for exponent one, so that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (s)=\mathrm{e}^ s\mu $. Set $H=\max (0,\sup _ Xs)$ and $w_ j=s+(j-1)H$. Then $w_ j/j\geq s$, and

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-w_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w_ j) \leq \mathrm{e}^{-s}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (s) =\mu =\mathrm{e}^{-u_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j). \]

Exponential order, now with exponent $1/j$, gives $u_ j\leq w_ j$. Put

\[ M_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j,\qquad v_ j=u_ j-M_ j, \qquad c_ j=\mathrm{e}^{M_ j/j}. \]

Then $\sup _ Xv_ j=0$ and $0<c_ j\leq \mathrm{e}^ H$, because $M_ j\leq \sup _ Xw_ j\leq jH$. By (15.37.29),

\[ v_-(\theta )\leq c_ j\int _ X \mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}\, \mathrm{d}\mu \leq c_ j\mu (X), \]

so $c_ j\geq v_-(\theta )/\mu (X)>0$. After extraction, theorem 5.25.2 gives $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$, where $v\not\equiv -\infty $ and $\sup _ Xv=0$, and $c_ j\to c>0$.

By lemma 5.44.5, $a_ j=\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}\to 1$ in $L^1(\mu )$. Consequently

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j)=c_ ja_ j\mu \longrightarrow c\mu \quad \hbox{in total variation},\qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j)\leq \mathrm{e}^ H\mu . \]

Applying theorem 15.37.19 again, we obtain $v\in \mathcal E_0$ and identify the limit as $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v)$; thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v)=c\mu $. The scalar assertion of corollary 15.37.11, applied in both directions to two solutions, proves that $c$ is unique. $\square$

The solutions for the reference zero also bound from above the approximating solutions for the reference $\varphi $. Indeed, every $\theta $-plurisubharmonic potential is $\preceq 0$, so exponential order with reference zero compares them with arbitrary $\theta $-plurisubharmonic potentials. This comparison controls the normalization in subsection 15.37.30.
