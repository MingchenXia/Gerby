16.4.1 Completion on a curveEFFAC36297D5

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.4.1 Completion on a curve

On a curve, a normalized potential is determined by its curvature measure. Replacing that measure by finite atomic approximations produces ideal models converging to the potential, even when the potential itself is not an ideal model.

Theorem 16.4.2. Let $Y$ be a compact connected Riemann surface and $\theta $ a smooth positive form of total mass one. Fix a smooth positive probability volume form on $Y$, again denoted by $\mathrm{d}V$, and use $\mathcal K_\theta $, the envelopes and the ideal metric on $Y$ in place of $X$. Then the ideal models with finitely many logarithmic poles and zero non-pluripolar mass are dense in $\mathcal K_\theta $ for the $L^1$ topology. The logarithmic coefficients can be chosen rational, so these approximants have analytic singularities in the convention of [SGPT]. Consequently the completion in proposition 16.4.1 is all of $\mathcal K_\theta $, and the metric on ideal classes is not complete.

Proof. We first show that the normalized Green potentials of finite atomic probability measures are ideal models, and then approximate the curvature measure of an arbitrary $u\in \mathcal K_\theta $ by such measures.

Let $\delta _ p$ denote the Dirac mass at $p$. For distinct points $p_1,\ldots ,p_ N\in Y$, consider a finitely supported probability measure

\[ \nu =\sum _{j=1}^ N a_ j\delta _{p_ j},\qquad a_ j>0,\quad \sum _ j a_ j=1. \]

The Green-kernel construction gives a potential $q_\nu $ with

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_\nu =\nu ,\qquad \sup _ Yq_\nu =0. \]

Indeed, choose a kernel with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_ xG(x,p)=\delta _ p-\theta $. The sum $\sum _ j a_ jG(x,p_ j)$ has curvature $\nu $, and subtracting its finite supremum gives $q_\nu $. In a coordinate $z$ centered at $p_ j$, the function $q_\nu $ is $a_ j\log |z|^2$ plus a smooth function, and it is smooth away from the finite support of $\nu $. Since all of its curvature lies on its pole set, its non-pluripolar measure is zero.

We claim that $P_\theta [q_\nu ]_{\mathcal I}=q_\nu $. Let $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$ satisfy $w\sim _{\mathcal I}q_\nu $. The Lelong-number criterion of theorem 1.16.3 gives $\nu (w,p_ j)=a_ j$, where $\nu (w,p_ j)$ is the Lelong number of $w$ at $p_ j$. On a curve, this Lelong number is exactly the mass of the atom at $p_ j$ of the positive measure $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$. By distributional Stokes, that measure has total mass one, so the prescribed atoms already exhaust its mass and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=\nu $. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w-q_\nu )=0$, and the difference is a harmonic distribution, hence a smooth function that is constant on the compact connected curve. Among these translates, the greatest nonpositive one is $q_\nu $, which proves the claim. By proposition 16.2.4, every such $q_\nu $ is also an ordinary model.

Now let $u\in \mathcal K_\theta $. Its curvature $\nu =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is a positive probability measure. Choose finitely supported probability measures $\nu _ j$ converging weakly to $\nu $. For instance, partition the compact metric space $Y$ into finitely many Borel sets of diameter tending to zero, place the mass of each set at a chosen point of it, and omit the sets of mass zero; uniform continuity of each continuous test function gives weak convergence.

The weights can be made rational. For a partition with weights $a_1,\ldots ,a_ N$, choose a large integer $M$, replace $a_ i$ by $b_ i=\lfloor Ma_ i\rfloor /M$ for $i<N$, put $b_ N=1-\sum _{i<N}b_ i$, and discard zero weights. The total absolute change is at most $2(N-1)/M$, which can be made to tend to zero along the partitions, so weak convergence is preserved. Writing $b_ i=m_ i/M$, the corresponding Green potential has the analytic type

\[ \left(\frac1M,\mathcal O_ Y\left(-\sum _ i m_ i p_ i\right)\right) \]

with smooth local remainders.

Let $q_ j=q_{\nu _ j}$. By normalized compactness, every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in $L^1$ to some $q\in \mathcal K_\theta $. Passing to the limit in the curvature identity in the sense of distributions gives

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q=\nu =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u. \]

By the same harmonic argument, $q-u$ is constant, and the constant is zero because both suprema vanish. Every cluster value is therefore $u$, so the whole sequence converges to $u$ in $L^1$. This proves the density assertion and identifies the completion.

To see incompleteness concretely, choose a nonconstant smooth real function $f$. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$, one has $\theta +\varepsilon \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f>0$, so $u=\varepsilon f-\sup _ Y(\varepsilon f)$ is a smooth, normalized and nonconstant element of $\mathcal K_\theta $. Its multiplier ideals are all trivial, so zero is a competitor and $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}=0$. Thus $u\notin \mathfrak M_{\mathcal I}(Y,\theta )$, although $u$ lies in the completion. The set of ideal models is therefore not closed in its completion, and the metric is not complete. $\square$
