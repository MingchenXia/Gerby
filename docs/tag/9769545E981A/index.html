Polarized cofactors and propagation

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 11.2 Polarized cofactors and propagation

Uniform ellipticity is sufficient for the propagation of an extremum, but it is not necessary: the directions in which the cofactor is positive may already connect enough points to force constancy. For $C^2$ potentials, the quadratic form $Q$ in (11.5) is continuous, and its polarization defines $Q^\sharp :T^*X\to TX$ by $\eta (Q^\sharp \xi )=Q(\xi ,\eta )$. The reachable set of a point consists of the endpoints of absolutely continuous curves starting there whose velocity lies in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $ almost everywhere. The control orbit of a point consists of the endpoints of finite concatenations of flow lines, starting there, of locally Lipschitz vector fields with values in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $.

A positive weight $h$ satisfying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(hT)=0$ gives an energy identity that controls $\mathrm{d}w$ everywhere. Without such a weight, a degenerate maximum principle gives propagation only from the extremal sets. This distinction accounts for the different density assumptions on reachable sets and on orbits in the following theorem.

Theorem 11.2.1. Let $u,v\in C^2(X)$ satisfy $A,B\geq 0$ and $A^ n=B^ n$, and put $w=u-v$.

  1. If there is a continuous $h>0$ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(hT)=0$ in distributions, then

    11.12
    \begin{equation} \mathrm{i}\, \partial w\wedge \bar\partial w\wedge T\equiv 0. \label{uni-eq:energyzero} \end{equation}

    It follows that $w$ is constant if the set where $T$ is strictly positive is dense, or if the reachable set of one point is dense.

  2. Without the weight hypothesis, the sets where $w$ attains its maximum and its minimum are invariant under the flow of every locally Lipschitz vector field with values in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $. If every control orbit is dense, then $w$ is constant. For smooth data it is enough that smooth vector fields with values in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $, for example the columns of the symbol $a$ in (11.18) below, together with their iterated Lie brackets, span $TX$ at every point.
