Potentials, energy and capacity

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 4 Potentials, energy and capacity

Polar sets were introduced in section 3.5 as the sets that lie locally in the pole set of a nontrivial subharmonic function. Every polar set has Lebesgue measure zero, but the converse fails: a sphere is a Lebesgue null set, yet its normalized surface measure has a bounded potential (example 4.3.6), and we shall see that this prevents the sphere from being polar. In this chapter we introduce two quantities adapted to the Laplacian, the energy of a measure and the capacity of a set. They characterize the polar sets, and they show that the exceptional sets of the limit theorems of chapter 3 are polar.

The energy of a positive measure is the integral of its potential against the measure itself. The Newtonian kernel, and the logarithmic kernel on measures of total mass zero, define positive definite energies, and minimizing the energy over the probability measures on a compact set gives its equilibrium measure. Measures with finite continuous potentials give no mass to polar sets, and by the continuity principle (theorem 4.2.2) every compactly supported measure whose potential is finite almost everywhere with respect to the measure itself is a countable sum of such measures. We also construct a polar Cantor set carrying an atom-free probability measure (proposition 4.2.4); this measure reappears in example 15.3.8.

Capacity is defined by an extremal problem for subharmonic functions. For a compact set $K$ in a ball $B$, let $u_ K$ be the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of all nonpositive subharmonic functions on $B$ that are at most $-1$ on $K$. The relative capacity $C_ B(K)$ is the total mass of the Riesz measure of $u_ K$. A compact set is nonpolar if and only if it carries a nonzero measure of finite energy, if and only if its relative capacity is positive (theorem 4.3.3). With Choquet's capacitability theorem this extends to arbitrary sets with compact closure in $B$: such a set is polar if and only if its outer capacity vanishes (corollary 4.4.2). The main application, theorem 4.4.4, has two parts. If subharmonic functions $v_ j$ converge to $v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, then the inequality $\varlimsup _ jv_ j\leq v$ of Hartogs' lemma (theorem 3.3.3) is an equality outside a polar set. Moreover, the upper envelope of a family of subharmonic functions that is locally uniformly bounded above agrees with its upper semicontinuous regularization outside a polar set, which sharpens theorem 3.4.2. The chapter closes with the Dirichlet energy of Green potentials and the quasi-continuity of subharmonic functions.

Throughout this chapter $d\geq 2$; in dimension one the empty set is the only polar set (section 3.5).

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in section B.4.
