Operations on non-Archimedean metrics

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 13.2 Operations on non-Archimedean metrics

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta ,\theta ',\theta ''$ be closed real smooth $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ representing big cohomology classes.

This section relies heavily on section 9.4. We shall use the notions introduced in that section without further explanations.

Definition 13.2.1. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$. We say $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$ if for some $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega } \leq \Gamma '{}^{\theta '+\omega }. \]
This notion is independent of the choice of $\omega $ thanks to 9.4.2. Moreover, we have the following:

Proposition 13.2.2. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\omega $ be a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$;

  2. $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}$.

Proof. This follows immediately from 9.4.2. $\square$

Observe that this definition coincides with the corresponding definition in definition 9.4.1 when $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Proposition 13.2.3. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma \leq \operatorname{vol}\Gamma '. \]

Proof. It suffices to show that for any Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\leq \operatorname{vol}\Gamma '{}^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }, \]

which is an immediate consequence of theorem 2.4.8. $\square$

Definition 13.2.4. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$. Then we define $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\theta ')$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \Gamma +\Gamma ' \right)^{\theta +\theta '+2\omega }=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }+\Gamma '^{\theta '+\omega }. \]
This definition yields an element in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\theta ')$ by 9.4.6 and it extends the definition in definition 9.4.3 by 9.4.6 as well.

Proposition 13.2.5. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$. Suppose that $\omega ,\omega '$ are two smooth closed positive $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega +\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma +\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}+P_{\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of 9.4.6. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.6. The operation $+$ is commutative and associative: For any $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$ and $\Gamma ''\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta '')$, we have

\[ \Gamma +\Gamma '=\Gamma '+\Gamma ,\quad (\Gamma +\Gamma ')+\Gamma ''=\Gamma +(\Gamma '+\Gamma ''). \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of proposition 9.4.5. $\square$

Definition 13.2.7. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $C\in \mathbb {R}$. We define $\Gamma +C\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \Gamma +C \right)^{\theta +\omega }=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }+C. \]
It is obvious from definition 9.4.7 that $\Gamma +C\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. It is also obvious that this definition extends definition 9.4.7.

Proposition 13.2.8. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Suppose that $\omega $ is a smooth closed positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}+C=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma +C]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is clear by definition. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.9. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$ and $C,C'\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

  1. $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')+C=\Gamma +(\Gamma '+C)=(\Gamma +C)+\Gamma '$;

  2. $\Gamma +(C+C')=(\Gamma +C)+C'$.

Proof. This is a direct consequence of proposition 9.4.8. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.10. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma =\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma +C). \]

Proof. It suffices to show that for each Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }=\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma +C)^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }, \]

which is obvious. $\square$

Definition 13.2.11. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, we define $\Gamma \lor \Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \Gamma \lor \Gamma ' \right)^{\theta +\omega }=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }\lor \Gamma '^{\theta +\omega }. \]
It follows from 9.4.12 that $\Gamma \lor \Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and this definition extends the corresponding definition in definition 9.4.9.

Proposition 13.2.12. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\omega $ be a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma \lor \Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}\lor P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of 9.4.12. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.13. The operation $\lor $ is commutative and associative.

In particular, given a finite non-empty family $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, we then define $\bigvee _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i$ in the obvious way.

Proof. This is a direct consequence of corollary 9.4.11. $\square$

Definition 13.2.14. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Assume that

13.10
\begin{equation} \label{eq:supPSHNAmaxfinite} \sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }<\infty . \end{equation}

Then we define $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i \right)^{\theta +\omega }=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }. \]
It follows immediately from 9.4.18 that $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and this definition extends definition 9.4.16. Moreover, this definition clearly extends definition 13.2.11 as well.

Proposition 13.2.15. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.10). Assume that $\omega $ is a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}= \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of 9.4.18. $\square$

We also have a non-Archimedean version of Choquet's lemma.

Proposition 13.2.16. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.10). Then there exists a countable subfamily $I'\subseteq I$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i. \]

Proof. Fix a Kähler form $\omega _0$ on $X$. For each $m\geq 1$, proposition 9.4.19 gives a countable subfamily $I_ m\subseteq I$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}P_{\theta +m^{-1}\omega _0}[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}}=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I_ m}{}\! {}^{*}P_{\theta +m^{-1}\omega _0}[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Set $I'=\bigcup _{m\geq 1}I_ m$. This is countable. We claim that it works.

Let $\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)$. Choose $m$ so large that $\omega '\geq m^{-1}\omega _0$. By proposition 13.2.15 and the compatibility of the transition maps,

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}P_{\theta +\omega '}[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}} =& P_{\theta +\omega '}\left[\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}P_{\theta +m^{-1}\omega _0}[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}\\ =& P_{\theta +\omega '}\left[\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}P_{\theta +m^{-1}\omega _0}[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}\\ =& \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}P_{\theta +\omega '}[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}}. \end{aligned} \]

Thus the two non-Archimedean metrics have the same component in every class $\theta +\omega '$, and the assertion follows. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.17. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.10). Let $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} (\Gamma ^ i+C)=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^ i+C. \]

Suppose that $(\Gamma '^ i)_{i\in I}$ is another family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$ satisfying (13.10). Suppose that $\Gamma ^ i\leq \Gamma '^ i$ for all $i\in I$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\leq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma '^ i. \]

Proof. This is an immediate consequence of proposition 9.4.20. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.18. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be an increasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.10). Then

13.14
\begin{equation} \label{eq:prop:vol_NA_incnet_cont} \operatorname{vol}\left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^ i \right)=\lim _{i\in I} \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^ i. \end{equation}

Proof. The $\geq $ direction in (13.14) is a direct consequence of proposition 13.2.3. It remains to prove the reverse inequality.

Note that (13.14) holds when $\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^ i>0$ for each $i\in I$, as a consequence of proposition 9.4.14, corollary 6.2.9 and theorem 6.2.30.

In particular, for each Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega } \right)=\lim _{i\in I} \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }. \]

For our purpose, we need to show that for any $\epsilon >0$, we can find $\omega $ so that

\[ \sup _{i\in I} \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }<\sup _{i\in I} \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i}+\epsilon . \]

We shall show that it is possible to choose $\omega $ so that the stronger statement holds:

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }<\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i}+\epsilon ,\quad \forall i\in I. \]

Equivalently, we need to choose $\omega $ so that for any Kähler form $\omega '$ on $X$ dominated by $\omega $, we have

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }<\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega '}_{-\infty }+\epsilon /2,\quad \forall i\in I. \]

Fix a Kähler form $\omega _0$ on $X$ and a Kähler form $\Omega $ such that $\Omega \geq \theta +\omega _0$. It is enough to take $\omega =\delta \omega _0$ for some $\delta \in (0,1)$. If $\omega '$ is dominated by $\omega $, then $\theta +\omega '\leq \Omega $. Set

\[ T_ i\coloneqq \theta +\omega '+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega '}_{-\infty }. \]

By the transition compatibility and proposition 9.1.11, the potential $\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }$ has the same $P$-singularity type in the class $\theta +\omega $ as $\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega '}_{-\infty }$. Hence its mass can be computed as

\[ \int _ X\left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega '}_{-\infty }\right)^ n=\int _ X \left(T_ i+\omega -\omega '\right)^ n. \]

Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }-\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega '}_{-\infty } =& \int _ X \left(T_ i+\omega -\omega '\right)^ n-\int _ X T_ i^ n\\ =& \sum _{a=0}^{n-1} \binom {n}{a} \int _ X T_ i^ a\wedge \left( \omega -\omega '\right)^{n-a}\\ \leq & \sum _{a=0}^{n-1} \binom {n}{a} \int _ X \Omega ^ a\wedge \omega ^{n-a}. \end{aligned} \]

In the last inequality we used the facts that $0\leq \omega -\omega '\leq \omega $ and $\theta +\omega '\leq \Omega $, and the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. We can choose $\omega $ so that the right-hand side is less than $\epsilon /2$. Our assertion then follows. $\square$

Definition 13.2.19. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Assume that

13.18
\begin{equation} \label{eq:decnetcontition} \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }>-\infty , \end{equation}

then we define $\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ as the unique element such that for each $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

13.19
\begin{equation} \label{eq:decnettestcurdef} \left( \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i \right)^{\theta +\omega }=\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }. \end{equation}
We observe that
\[ \left( \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i \right)^{\theta +\omega }\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega )_{>0}. \]

This follows from proposition 9.4.26. Moreover, by 9.4.27, we have $\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, and this definition extends definition 9.4.25.

In general,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left( \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i \right)\leq \lim _{i\in I} \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^ i \]

as a consequence of proposition 13.2.3. But the reverse inequality fails in general.

Proposition 13.2.20. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.18). Assume that $\omega $ is a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}= \inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This follows from 9.4.27. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.21. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.18). Let $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

\[ \inf _{i\in I} (\Gamma ^ i+C)=\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^ i+C. \]

Suppose that $(\Gamma '^ i)_{i\in I}$ is another decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$ satisfying (13.18). Suppose that $\Gamma ^ i\leq \Gamma '^ i$ for all $i\in I$. Then

\[ \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i\leq \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma '^ i. \]

Proof. This is clear by definition. $\square$

Definition 13.2.22. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb {R}_{>0}$. Then we define $\lambda \Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\lambda \theta )$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \lambda \Gamma \right)^{\lambda \theta +\omega }=\lambda \Gamma ^{\theta +\lambda ^{-1}\omega }. \]
It follows immediately from 9.4.24 that $\lambda \Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\lambda \theta )$ and this definition extends definition 9.4.21.

Proposition 13.2.23. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb {R}_{>0}$. Then for any closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

\[ P_{\lambda \theta +\omega }[\lambda \Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}=\lambda P_{\theta +\lambda ^{-1}\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This follows immediately from 9.4.24. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.24. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$, $C\in \mathbb {R}$ and $\lambda ,\lambda '>0$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda (\Gamma +\Gamma ')=& \lambda \Gamma +\lambda \Gamma ',\\ (\lambda \lambda ')\Gamma =& \lambda (\lambda '\Gamma ),\\ \lambda (\Gamma +C)=& \lambda \Gamma +\lambda C. \end{aligned} \]

Suppose that $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ is a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.10). Then

\[ \lambda \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i \right)=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}(\lambda \Gamma ^ i). \]

If $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ is a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.18), then

\[ \lambda \left( \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i \right)=\inf _{i\in I}(\lambda \Gamma ^ i). \]

Proof. Everything except the last assertion follows from proposition 9.4.23. The last assertion follows directly from the definition. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.25. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb {R}_{>0}$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left( \lambda \Gamma \right)=\lambda ^ n \operatorname{vol}\Gamma . \]

Proof. This is clear by definition. $\square$

Definition 13.2.26. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Let $Y\subseteq X$ be an irreducible analytic subset. We say that the trace operator of $\Gamma $ along $Y$ is well-defined if

\[ \nu \left(\Gamma _{\tau }^{\theta +\omega },Y\right)=0 \]

for all sufficiently negative $\tau $ and every $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$. We define

\[ \left(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )\right)_{\max }\coloneqq \sup \left\{ \tau <\Gamma _{\max }: \nu \left(\Gamma _{\tau }^{\theta +\omega },Y\right)=0\right\} . \]

Here $\omega $ is any Kähler form on $X$; this number is independent of the choice of $\omega $ by the compatibility of the transition maps.

In this case, we define $\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\widetilde{Y},\theta |_{\widetilde{Y}})$

as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(\widetilde{Y})$, the component
13.23
\begin{equation} \label{eq:TrYinPSHNAg0} \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\theta |_{\widetilde{Y}}+\omega }\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\widetilde{Y},\theta |_{\widetilde{Y}}+\omega )_{>0} \end{equation}

is defined as follows:

  1. We let

    13.24

  2. for each $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ less than $\left(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )\right)_{\max }$, we define

    \[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\theta |_{\widetilde{Y}}+\omega }_{\tau }\coloneqq P_{\theta |_{\widetilde{Y}}+\omega }\left[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y^{\theta +\tilde{\omega }}\left(\Gamma ^{\theta +\tilde{\omega }}_{\tau }\right) \right]_{\mathcal{I}}, \]

    where $\tilde{\omega }$ is an arbitrary Kähler form on $X$ such that $\omega \geq \tilde{\omega }|_{\widetilde{Y}}$.

It follows from [Proposition 3.6, GK20] that $\widetilde{Y}$ is a normal Kähler space and hence $\tilde{\omega }$ exists. We observe that the choice of the trace operator $\operatorname{Tr}_ Y^{\theta +\tilde{\omega }}\left(\Gamma ^{\theta +\tilde{\omega }}_{\tau }\right)$ is irrelevant since two different choices are $\mathcal{I}$-equivalent. Moreover, (13.23) holds as a consequence of proposition 8.1.5 and proposition 8.2.1. It is therefore clear that $\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\widetilde{Y},\theta |_{\widetilde{Y}})$. Here when $\widetilde{Y}$ is not smooth, we understand $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\widetilde{Y},\theta |_{\widetilde{Y}})$ as the set of non-Archimedean metrics on a resolution of $\widetilde{Y}$, which is independent of the choice of the resolution by proposition 8.2.1. See chapter B for more details.

Proposition 13.2.27. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a compact Kähler manifold $Y$. Then all definitions in this section are invariant under pullback to $Y$.

The meaning is clear in most cases. In the case of the trace operator, this means the following: Suppose that $Z\subseteq X$ is an analytic subset and $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ has non-trivial restriction to $Z$. Suppose that $Z$ is not contained in the non-isomorphism locus of $\pi $ so that the strict transform $W$ of $Z$ is defined. If we write $\Pi \colon W\rightarrow Z$ for the restriction of $\pi $ and $\widetilde{\Pi }\colon \widetilde{W}\rightarrow \widetilde{Z}$ the strict transform of $\Pi $, then we have
\[ \widetilde{\Pi }^* \operatorname{Tr}_ Z(\Gamma )=\operatorname{Tr}_ W(\pi ^*\Gamma ). \]

The relevant notations are summarized in the following diagram:

Proof. We only prove the assertion for the trace operator, as the other proofs are similar.

We shall use the notations above. Observe that for any closed positive smooth $(1,1)$-form $\omega $ on $X$ with positive mass, we have

\[ \left(\widetilde{\Pi }^* \operatorname{Tr}_ Z(\Gamma )\right)_{\max }=\left( \operatorname{Tr}_ Z(\Gamma )\right)_{\max }=\sup \left\{ \tau <\Gamma _{\max }: \nu \left(\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau },Z\right)=0 \right\} , \]

and

\[ \begin{aligned} \left(\operatorname{Tr}_ W(\pi ^*\Gamma )\right)_{\max }=& \sup \left\{ \tau <\Gamma _{\max }: \nu \left((\pi ^*\Gamma )^{\pi ^*\theta +\pi ^*\omega }_{\tau },W\right)=0 \right\} \\ =& \sup \left\{ \tau <\Gamma _{\max }: \nu \left(\pi ^*\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau },W\right)=0 \right\} \\ =& \sup \left\{ \tau <\Gamma _{\max }: \nu \left(\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau },Z\right)=0 \right\} . \end{aligned} \]

Here we implicitly used proposition 13.1.12. Therefore,

\[ \left(\widetilde{\Pi }^* \operatorname{Tr}_ Z(\Gamma )\right)_{\max }=\left(\operatorname{Tr}_ W(\pi ^*\Gamma )\right)_{\max }. \]

Let $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ be less than this common value. Take a Kähler form $\omega _{\widetilde{Z}}$ (resp. $\omega _{\widetilde{W}}$) on $\widetilde{Z}$ (resp. $\widetilde{W}$). Take a Kähler form $\omega _ Y$ on $Y$ (resp. $\omega _ X$ on $X$) such that

\[ \omega _{\widetilde{Z}}\geq \omega _ X|_{\widetilde{Z}},\quad \omega _{\widetilde{W}} \geq \omega _ Y|_{\widetilde{W}},\quad \omega _ Y \geq \pi ^*\omega _ X. \]

We want to show that, after projection to the common background $(\pi ^*\theta )|_{\widetilde{W}}+\omega _{\widetilde{W}}$,

\[ P_{(\pi ^*\theta )|_{\widetilde{W}}+\omega _{\widetilde{W}}}\left[\widetilde{\Pi }^*\left(\operatorname{Tr}_ Z(\Gamma )^{\theta |_{\widetilde{Z}}+\omega _{\widetilde{Z}}}_\tau \right)\right]_{\mathcal I} \sim _ P \left(\operatorname{Tr}_ W(\pi ^*\Gamma )\right)^{(\pi ^*\theta )|_{\widetilde{W}}+\omega _{\widetilde{W}}}_\tau . \]

Unfolding the definitions, we reduce to

\[ \widetilde{\Pi }^* \operatorname{Tr}_ Z^{\theta +\omega _ X}\left( \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega _ X}_{\tau } \right)\sim _ P \operatorname{Tr}_{W}^{\pi ^*\theta +\omega _ Y}\left( \left( \pi ^*\Gamma \right)^{\pi ^*\theta +\omega _ Y}_{\tau }\right). \]

Using proposition 8.2.1, this is equivalent to

\[ \widetilde{\Pi }^* \operatorname{Tr}_ Z\left( \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega _ X}_{\tau } \right)\sim _ P \operatorname{Tr}_{W}\left( \left( \pi ^*\Gamma \right)^{\pi ^*\theta +\pi ^*\omega _ X}_{\tau }\right). \]

This is a consequence of 8.2.4. $\square$
