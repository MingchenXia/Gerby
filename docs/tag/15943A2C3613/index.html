5 Gauduchon traces and the topology of potentials15943A2C3613

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 5 Gauduchon traces and the topology of potentials

Throughout this chapter, $\theta $ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive probability volume form used for $L^1$ norms, and $\gamma $ is a Gauduchon metric, that is, a Hermitian form with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\gamma ^{n-1})=0$ (definition 4.1.1). In dimension one every Hermitian form is Gauduchon, since $\gamma ^{n-1}=1$.

The current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ has a conserved linear trace even when its non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure has infinite mass, as we shall see in chapter 6. By proposition 4.2.1, the positive measure $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ has the same total mass for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The ellipticity of $f\mapsto \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ makes the trace more informative than this mass alone: its kernel consists of the constants, so the trace measure determines the potential up to an additive constant.

On mean-normalized potentials, this correspondence also identifies the topologies: weak convergence of trace measures is equivalent to $L^1$ convergence of potentials. Lipschitz tests give a complete metric for this topology, and the inverse elliptic operator compares it with the $L^1$ norm by sharp power bounds. Extending the conserved pairing to a positive pluriharmonic current requires more than this elliptic inversion. For continuous potentials, a gradient-energy estimate defines the derivative terms in the product and gives continuity under uniform approximation.
