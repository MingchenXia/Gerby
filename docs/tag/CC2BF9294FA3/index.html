From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 21.3.1

Theorem 21.3.1. The affine dimension of $\Delta (W_\bullet )$ is $d$. Its lattice volume is finite and strictly positive, and

\[ \lim _{q\to \infty }\frac{\dim W_{eq}}{(eq)^ d} =\operatorname {vol}_\Lambda \Delta (W_\bullet ). \]

If $e$ does not divide $m$, then $W_ m=0$. In particular, $W_ m\neq 0$ for all sufficiently large multiples $m$ of $e$.

Proof. Choose $g_0=(b,e)\in G$ and an integral basis $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ d$ of $\Lambda $. Every element of $G$ has a unique expression

\[ \sum _{i=1}^ d a_ i\lambda _ i+qg_0, \qquad (a,q)\in \mathbb Z^ d\times \mathbb Z. \]

These coordinates identify $G$ with $\mathbb Z^{d+1}$. Let $S'$ be the image of $S$ under this identification. It generates $\mathbb Z^{d+1}$, and the height of the image of $(x,m)\in S$ is $q=m/e\geq 0$.

The points $(m^{-1}x,1)$, for $(x,m)\in S$ with $m>0$, span the $(d+1)$-dimensional real vector space $\operatorname {span}_{\mathbb R}G$. Since they all have height one, their affine span has dimension $d$. Taking the convex hull and the closure does not change this affine span, which proves the dimension assertion.

Intersect the cone containing $S$ with $\operatorname {span}_{\mathbb R}G$. The linear change of coordinates carries this intersection to a closed cone with compact height-one slice whose only point at height zero is the origin. Indeed, a vector $(x,m)$ with $|x|\leq Cm$ has horizontal coordinates bounded by a constant times $m$, hence by a constant times $q$. The height-one body of $S'$ is

\[ \Delta '=e\bigl (\Delta (W_\bullet )-b/e\bigr ), \]

where the right-hand side is expressed in the basis $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ d$. It is full-dimensional in $\mathbb R^ d$, and therefore

\[ \operatorname {vol}_{\mathbb R^ d}\Delta ' =e^ d\operatorname {vol}_\Lambda \Delta (W_\bullet )>0. \]

Apply the semigroup counting theorem of section 21.2 to $S'$, which generates its full lattice. The points of $S'$ at height $q$ correspond bijectively to the valuations of the nonzero elements of $W_{eq}$, and by lemma 21.1.1 there are $\dim W_{eq}$ of them. Hence

\[ \lim _{q\to \infty }\frac{\dim W_{eq}}{q^ d} =\operatorname {vol}_{\mathbb R^ d}\Delta '. \]

Dividing by $e^ d$ gives the asserted limit, and its positivity gives eventual nonvanishing. For $d=0$ the body is a point of volume one, so the integer dimensions converge to one and are eventually equal to one. Finally, every degree with a nonzero section lies in the projection $e\mathbb Z$ of $G$, which proves the vanishing outside these multiples. $\square$
