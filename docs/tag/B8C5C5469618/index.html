14.32.1 A logarithmic density with critical coefficient oneB8C5C5469618

The power capacity condition can hold for a density outside every $L^ p$ class with $p>1$. A bounded continuous potential with logarithmic modulus at one point gives such a datum on each fixed Hermitian background.

Theorem 14.32.2. In the setting of theorem 14.32.1, fix $\alpha >0$. There is $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)\cap C(X)$ with $h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq h/2$ whose measure $\mathrm{MA}_ h(u)=f\, \mathrm dV$ satisfies

\[ f\in L^1(\mathrm dV),\qquad f\notin L^ p(\mathrm dV)\quad \text{for every }p>1. \]

For some $A>0$ it satisfies (14.115), and $b_ h(f)=1$. For all sufficiently large $K$,

14.118
\begin{equation} \rho _ h\bigl (f^{1/n}\mathbf1_{\{ f>K\} }\bigr ) \leq C(\log K)^{-\alpha }. \end{equation}

Proof. Choose a holomorphic ball with coordinates centred at $x_0$ and $R=|z|<1$, and a smooth cutoff $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ supported in it and equal to one on a smaller ball. Put

\[ q(s)=(1-s)^{-\alpha },\qquad s=\log R^2, \qquad \psi =\chi q(\log R^2)+1-\chi . \]

Set $\psi (x_0)=0$ and extend $\psi =1$ outside the ball. In the smaller ball, $q'=\alpha (1-s)^{-\alpha -1}>0$ and $q''=\alpha (\alpha +1)(1-s)^{-\alpha -2}>0$, so $\psi $ is plurisubharmonic there, including its continuous extension at $x_0$. The transition is smooth and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi \geq -K_0h$ globally for a fixed $K_0$. For sufficiently small $\delta >0$, the normalized potential $u=\delta \psi -\sup _ X\delta \psi $ satisfies $h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq h/2$.

The bounded product gives zero mass to $x_0$. Indeed, after a smooth local correction, its expansion is dominated in total variation by a constant times the top product of a bounded plurisubharmonic function plus a strictly plurisubharmonic quadratic function. Rescaling this sum into $[0,1]$ bounds that top product by local capacity, and a pluripolar point has capacity zero. Off $x_0$ the potential is smooth. Thus the measure is absolutely continuous; write it as $f\, \mathrm dV$. The strict lower bound implies $f\geq c>0$ almost everywhere.

In the smaller ball the tangential and radial eigenvalues of the Hessian of $q(\log R^2)$ are $q'/R^2$ and $q''/R^2$. In the operator convention of the book this gives

14.119
\begin{equation} (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n =\frac{\delta ^ nn!\alpha ^ n(\alpha +1)}{\pi ^ n} R^{-2n}(1-\log R^2)^{-n(\alpha +1)-1}\, \mathrm d\lambda , \end{equation}

where $\mathrm d\lambda $ is Euclidean volume.

Integration over the ball gives

\[ \int _{B_ r}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n =\delta ^ n\alpha ^ n(1-\log r^2)^{-n(\alpha +1)}. \]

For $k<n$, the degree-$k$ mixed terms with the smooth positive form $h$ have density bounded by $CR^{-2k}(1-\log R^2)^{-k(\alpha +1)}$ and mass $O(r^2)$ on $B_ r$. The expansion has positive terms in this ball. Hence $f\in L^1$. For $p>1$, the pure top term gives a positive multiple of the divergent integral

\[ \int _0^ r R^{2n(1-p)-1} (1-\log R^2)^{-p(n(\alpha +1)+1)}\, \mathrm dR \]

as a lower bound for $\int _{B_ r}f^ p\, \mathrm dV$, since smooth volume densities are comparable. Thus $f\notin L^ p$. For all sufficiently small $r$,

14.120
\begin{equation} \int _{B_ r}f\, \mathrm dV \leq C\bigl [(1-\log r^2)^{-n(\alpha +1)}+r^2\bigr ], \qquad \mathop{\rm ess\, sup}_{X\setminus B_ r}f\leq Cr^{-2n}. \end{equation}

By (1.25),

\[ \mathrm dV(E)\leq C\exp \bigl (-a\operatorname {Cap}_ h(E)^{-1/n}\bigr ). \]

Let $s=\operatorname {Cap}_ h(E)>0$ be small and choose $r=\exp [-as^{-1/n}/(4n)]$. Splitting $E$ inside and outside $B_ r$ and applying (14.120), we obtain

\[ \int _ Ef\, \mathrm dV \leq Cs^{1+\alpha } +C\exp [-as^{-1/n}/(2n)] +C\exp [-as^{-1/n}/2] \leq C's^{1+\alpha }. \]

For larger $s$ use the finite total mass; for $s=0$ the volume estimate gives $\mathrm dV(E)=0$. This proves (14.115) for every Borel set.

By theorem 14.32.1, there is a normalized continuous $\phi $ with $\mathrm{MA}_ h(\phi )=b_ h(f)f\, \mathrm dV$. The explicit potential $u$ is a subsolution with coefficient one, so theorem 14.27.2 gives $b_ h(f)\geq 1$. Suppose $b_ h(f)>1$, and choose $0<\varepsilon <1$ with $(1-\varepsilon )^ nb_ h(f)>1$. Positive multilinearity gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_ h((1-\varepsilon )\phi ) \geq (1-\varepsilon )^ nb_ h(f)f\, \mathrm dV. \]

Apply theorem 10.2.1 to $u,\phi $ on

\[ U_ s=\{ u<(1-\varepsilon )\phi + \inf _ X(u-(1-\varepsilon )\phi )+s\} . \]

For sufficiently small $s>0$ its reverse factor $Q_\varepsilon (s)$ is defined, tends to one as $s\downarrow 0$, and satisfies

\[ (1-\varepsilon )^ nb_ h(f)\int _{U_ s}f\, \mathrm dV \leq Q_\varepsilon (s)\int _{U_ s}f\, \mathrm dV. \]

Choose $s$ so that $Q_\varepsilon (s)<(1-\varepsilon )^ nb_ h(f)$. The set $U_ s$ is open and nonempty, and $f\geq c>0$ almost everywhere, so its $f\, \mathrm dV$ measure is positive. This contradiction proves $b_ h(f)=1$.

Finally take $r_ K=K^{-1/(4n)}$. The outside bound in (14.120) gives $f\leq CK^{1/2}<K$ almost everywhere on $X\setminus B_{r_ K}$ for large $K$. Therefore

\[ \int _{\{ f>K\} }f\, \mathrm dV \leq C(\log K)^{-n(\alpha +1)}+CK^{-1/(2n)} \leq C'(\log K)^{-n(\alpha +1)}. \]

Apply (14.116) to $g=f\mathbf1_{\{ f>K\} }$ to get (14.118). $\square$

The potential has logarithmic modulus at $x_0$ and is not Hölder there. Its root-tail estimate follows from the capacity bound despite the failure of higher $L^ p$ integrability. The construction applies to each fixed background, including one with $v_-(h)=0$.
