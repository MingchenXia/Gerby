Proposition 16.2.22E43C0935B545

Proposition 16.2.22. Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$, and for $\beta >0$ let $a_\beta $ be the solution of (16.14) given by theorem 16.2.21. Put

\[ B=\sup _ X\operatorname {tr}_ hk+\sup _ XD_ hf,\qquad d_0=\inf _ X\frac{\det k}{\det h},\qquad K_0=\frac{B^ n}{n^ nd_0}. \]

Then $K_0\geq 1$, $a_\beta \leq P_\kappa (f)+\beta ^{-1}\log K_0$ for every $\beta >0$, and for every $\beta \geq 1$

\[ P_\kappa (f)-\frac{\operatorname {osc}_ Xf+n\log \beta }{\beta } \leq a_\beta \leq P_\kappa (f)+\frac{\log K_0}{\beta }. \]

In particular $a_\beta \to P_\kappa (f)$ uniformly on $X$ as $\beta \to \infty $. Moreover $P_\kappa (f)$ has bounded metric Laplacian: with the constant $A$ of lemma 16.2.15,

16.23
\begin{equation} -\sup _ X\operatorname {tr}_ hk\leq D_ hP_\kappa (f)\leq 2B\, \mathrm e^{2A\| f\| _\infty } \end{equation}

in the sense of distributions, that is, (16.11) holds for $v=P_\kappa (f)$ with $c_1=\sup _ X\operatorname {tr}_ hk$ and $c_2=2B\mathrm e^{2A\| f\| _\infty }$. Finally, $P_\kappa (f)\in C^{1,\alpha }(X)$ for every $0<\alpha <1$.

Proof. At each point, the eigenvalues of $k$ relative to $h$ are positive, and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives $\operatorname {tr}_ hk\geq n(\det k/\det h)^{1/n}\geq nd_0^{1/n}$. Since $\sup _ XD_ hf\geq 0$ by (16.10), it follows that $B\geq nd_0^{1/n}$, that is, $K_0\geq 1$.

The upper bound. Let $\beta >0$, write $a=a_\beta $ and $g=k+(a_{i\bar j})$, and let $x_0$ be a maximum point of $a-f$. There the complex Hessian of $a-f$ is negative semidefinite, so $0<g\leq k+(f_{i\bar j})$. In particular $k+(f_{i\bar j})$ is positive definite at $x_0$, and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality for its eigenvalues relative to $h$ gives

\[ \mathrm e^{\beta (a-f)}=\frac{\det g}{\det k} \leq \frac{\det (k+(f_{i\bar j}))}{\det h}\cdot \frac{\det h}{\det k} \leq \Bigl (\frac{\operatorname {tr}_ hk+D_ hf}{n}\Bigr )^ n\frac1{d_0} \leq K_0 \]

at $x_0$. Hence $a-f\leq \log K_0/\beta $ on $X$. The function $a-\log K_0/\beta $ is therefore a smooth $\kappa $-psh function below $f$, that is, a competitor for $P_\kappa (f)$, and so $a\leq P_\kappa (f)+\log K_0/\beta $.

The lower bound. Let $\beta \geq 1$ and put $v=P_\kappa (f)$; as noted at the beginning of this subsection, $\min _ Xf\leq v\leq f$. For $0<t\leq 1$ put

\[ w=(1-t)v+t\min _ Xf-\frac{n\log (1/t)}{\beta }. \]

This is a bounded $\kappa $-psh function, and we show that $w\leq a$.

For a bounded $\kappa $-psh function $b$ write $\mu _ b=\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^ n\rangle $, the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure, which is the Bedford–Taylor measure since $b$ is bounded (proposition 15.3.3, applied with $q=p=n$ to the sums of $b$ with smooth local potentials of $\kappa $), and equals the smooth form $(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^ n$ if $b$ is smooth (proposition 9.1.2(1)). Locally $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ with $\rho $ smooth, and $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl ((1-t)(\rho +v)+t\rho \bigr )$. Expanding the $n$th power by multilinearity and positive homogeneity (proposition 15.1.5), and discarding the mixed terms, which are positive measures, we get $\mu _ w\geq t^ n\kappa ^ n$. On the other hand $w\leq f-n\log (1/t)/\beta $, so $\mathrm e^{\beta (w-f)}\leq t^ n$.

Let $E=\{ a<w\} $, a Borel set. Since $a$ and $w$ are bounded, $a\geq w-O(1)$, so proposition 15.8.5 applies without the correction terms; together with the two inequalities just proved and the equation for $a$ it gives

\[ \int _ E\mathrm e^{\beta (w-f)}\kappa ^ n \leq \int _ E\mu _ w \leq \int _ E\mu _ a =\int _ E\mathrm e^{\beta (a-f)}\kappa ^ n. \]

On $E$ the first integrand is strictly larger than the last one, so $E$ has volume zero. Thus $w\leq a$ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere by lemma 13.1.4(1), since both functions are quasi-plurisubharmonic.

Now take $t=1/\beta $, which is allowed since $\beta \geq 1$. Since $\min _ Xf-v\geq \min _ Xf-\max _ Xf=-\operatorname {osc}_ Xf$, we obtain

\[ a\geq w=v+\frac{\min _ Xf-v}{\beta }-\frac{n\log \beta }{\beta } \geq v-\frac{\operatorname {osc}_ Xf+n\log \beta }{\beta }. \]

The Laplacian bound. Let $\beta _0$ be the constant of lemma 16.2.15. For $\beta >\beta _0$ that lemma, together with $D_ ha_\beta =H-\operatorname {tr}_ hk$, $H>0$ and $\operatorname {osc}_ Xf\leq 2\| f\| _\infty $, gives

\[ -\sup _ X\operatorname {tr}_ hk\leq D_ ha_\beta \leq 2B\, \mathrm e^{2A\| f\| _\infty }. \]

Since $a_\beta \to v$ uniformly, $\langle D_ hv,\chi \rangle =\int _ Xv\, D_ h\chi \, \mathrm dV=\lim _{\beta \to \infty }\int _ X(D_ ha_\beta )\chi \, \mathrm dV$ for smooth $\chi $, which proves (16.23). By the remark following (16.11), $Lv=-4\pi D_ hv$ is therefore a bounded function, with a bound depending only on $X$, $\omega $, $\kappa $ and upper bounds for $\| f\| _\infty $ and $\sup _ XD_ hf$. Finally $v$ is continuous, as a uniform limit of continuous functions, so by proposition 16.2.11 it belongs to $C^{1,\alpha }(X)$ for every $0<\alpha <1$. $\square$
