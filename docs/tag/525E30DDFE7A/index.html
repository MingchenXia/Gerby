8.4 Variation of the heat time525E30DDFE7A

The heat time will remain small on a fixed base ball and then increase with the radial variable until the fiber potential can be cut off. We choose its rate of increase so that the negative part of the Schur complement has small integral in the radial variable. Fix $\kappa $ as in lemma 8.3.3.

All estimates below concern the limit $\eta \downarrow 0$, with $\kappa $ fixed. Write

\[ L=\log (1/\eta ),\quad \varepsilon =\eta ^{1/\eta ^2},\quad q_0=\sqrt{\log \frac1{\eta \varepsilon }},\quad a=\sqrt{\frac\kappa {2\eta }}. \]

Fix a smooth nondecreasing $\chi :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$, independent of $\eta $, equal to zero on $(-\infty ,0]$ and one on $[1,+\infty )$, with $\int _0^1\chi =1/2$. Define

8.14
\begin{equation} q(x)=q_0-\int _0^{ax}\chi (s)\, \mathrm{d}s, \qquad t(x)=\eta ^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-q(x)^2}. \end{equation}

Thus $t=\varepsilon $ on $x\leq 0$, and $q=q_0+1/2-ax$ on $x\geq a^{-1}$. Put

\[ b=\frac{q_0-1/2}{a},\qquad x_* =\frac{q_0+1/2}{a}. \]

For small $\eta $, we have $0<a^{-1}<b<x_*$, with

8.15
\begin{equation} q(b)=1,\quad q(x_*)=0,\quad b,x_*=O\bigl (\sqrt{L/\eta }\bigr ),\quad \eta b=o(1). \end{equation}

Fix also a smooth cutoff $\vartheta :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$, independent of $\eta $, zero on $(-\infty ,0]$ and one on $[1,+\infty )$, and define

\[ W(x,w)=\vartheta (q(x))F_\eta (t(x),w). \]

This function equals $F_\eta (\varepsilon ,w)$ for $x\leq 0$ and vanishes for $x\geq x_*$. Moreover, by (8.8),

8.16
\begin{equation} 1+W_{w\bar w}=1+\vartheta (q)\eta (k_ t-1)>1-\eta . \end{equation}

Lemma 8.4.1. For the functions just constructed, let

\[ d_\eta (x)=\max _{w\in E}\max \{ -Q[W](x,w),0\} . \]

For all sufficiently small $\eta $, the function $d_\eta $ is continuous, supported in $[0,b]$, and

8.17
\begin{equation} \int _\mathbb Rd_\eta (x)\, \mathrm{d}x=o(1),\qquad \sup _{\mathbb R\times E}\max \{ -W_ x,0\} =o(1). \end{equation}

On $[-2,-1]\times E$, one also has $Q[W]\geq 2/27$.

Proof. We first derive an exact formula for $Q[W]$ on $[0,b]$, then bound its negative part separately on $[0,a^{-1}]$ and on $[a^{-1},b]$, integrate these bounds, and finally examine the ends of the radial line.

Step 1: the Schur complement on $[0,b]$. Write $F=F_\eta $ for the heat potential. On $[0,b]$ we have $q\geq 1$, hence $\vartheta (q)=1$ and $W=F(t(x),w)$. Set

\[ h=\eta k_ t,\qquad \sigma =(\log t)_ x,\qquad \zeta =\frac{\sigma t}{\mu }\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t. \]

The heat equation gives $F_ t=h-\eta $, and the chain rule gives

\[ W_ x=\sigma tF_ t,\quad W_{xx}=(\sigma _ x+\sigma ^2)tF_ t+\sigma ^2t^2F_{tt}, \quad W_{x\bar w}=\sigma tF_{t\bar w}. \]

Substituting these in $Q[W]$, using $h_ t=h((\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}+|\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t|^2)$, and completing the square, we obtain the exact identity

8.18
\begin{equation} \begin{split} Q[W]={}& \sigma ^2t\{ h[1+t(\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}]-\eta \} +\sigma _ xtF_ t+\mu (1-\mu )-\frac{\mu ^2\eta }{1-\eta }\\ & +\frac{\mu ^2(1-\eta )h}{1-\eta +h} \left|\zeta -\frac1{1-\eta }\right|^2. \end{split} \end{equation}

By (8.9), the part $\sigma ^2th[1+t(\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}]$ is nonnegative.

Step 2: the initial interval $[0,a^{-1}]$. Here

\[ \sigma =2aq\chi (ax),\qquad \sigma _ x=-\frac\kappa \eta \chi (ax)^2 +\frac\kappa \eta q\chi '(ax). \]

Moreover $q_0-1/2\leq q\leq q_0$ and $t\leq \varepsilon \mathrm{e}^{q_0}$, and the latter tends to zero faster than any power of $\eta $. In particular,

\[ \kappa q\chi '(ax)t\leq \eta ,\qquad \sigma ^2t\leq \eta ^2. \]

At each point where $\chi (ax)>0$, apply lemma 8.3.3 with $\kappa \chi (ax)^2$ in place of $\kappa $; its constant is uniform in $x$. The positive square in (8.18) then absorbs the negative term $-\kappa \chi ^2tk_ t$ coming from $\sigma _ xtF_ t$. The remaining part of $\sigma _ xtF_ t$, which involves $\chi '$, is at least $-\eta $, because $F_ t\geq -\eta $. The other negative terms in (8.18) are $O(\eta )$, and so is the exponential error of lemma 8.3.3, since $\sigma ^2t\leq \eta ^2$. Thus $d_\eta \leq C\eta $ on this interval. At points where $\chi (ax)=0$, the smooth nonnegative function $\chi $ has a minimum, so $\chi '(ax)=0$ and directly

\[ Q[W]=\mu -\frac{\mu ^2}{1-\eta +h}>0, \]

since $\mu \leq \mu (a^{-1})<1-\eta $ for small $\eta $.

Step 3: the interval $[a^{-1},b]$. Here the formulas simplify to

8.21
\begin{equation} \sigma =2aq,\qquad \sigma _ x=-\kappa /\eta ,\qquad \mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{a}=\sqrt{\frac\eta {2\kappa }}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{tq}. \end{equation}

For $t\leq 1$, lemma 8.3.3 and (8.18) give

8.22
\begin{equation} d_\eta (x)\leq 2\eta +2\kappa q^2t +C\kappa \exp \left(-\frac\eta {C\kappa q^2t}\right). \end{equation}

Indeed, $-\eta \sigma ^2t=-2\kappa q^2t$ and $\sigma _ xtF_ t=-\kappa tk_ t+\kappa t$; these are the two terms for which the scale was chosen. When $t\geq 1$, we insert the exponential derivative estimates (8.11) directly in the definition of $Q$. The denominator stays above $1-\eta $, and the chain rule gives

8.23
\begin{equation} d_\eta (x)\leq C\kappa q^2t^2\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}. \end{equation}

The perturbing derivatives are bounded by constants times $\eta \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$, $\eta \sigma t \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ and $\eta [(|\sigma _ x|+\sigma ^2)t+\sigma ^2t^2]\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$. Since $q,t\geq 1$ here, each is bounded by a constant times the right side of (8.23). Since $1+W_{w\bar w}\geq 1-\eta $, subtracting the two quotients gives

\[ |Q[W]-Q[0]|\leq |W_{xx}|+ C\bigl (|W_{x\bar w}|+|W_{x\bar w}|^2+|W_{w\bar w}|\bigr ). \]

The square of the mixed derivative is at most $C\kappa \eta q^2t^2\mathrm{e}^{-t/2}$, so it satisfies the same bound. Since $Q[0]>0$, this proves (8.23).

Step 4: integration of the negative part. The constant $2\eta $ and the initial interval contribute $O(\eta b)=o(1)$, by (8.15). In the small-time region, $q\geq \sqrt L$ and $t=\eta ^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-q^2}$. Therefore

\[ \int 2\kappa q^2t\, \mathrm{d}x \leq \frac{2\kappa }{a\eta } \int _{\sqrt L}^{\infty }q^2\mathrm{e}^{-q^2}\, \mathrm{d}q \leq C\sqrt{\eta L}=o(1). \]

For the exponential term the integral is at most

\[ \frac{C\kappa }{a} \int _{\sqrt L}^{q_0} \exp \left(-\frac{\eta ^2\mathrm{e}^{q^2}}{C\kappa q^2}\right)\, \mathrm{d}q. \]

The interval $[\sqrt L,2\sqrt L]$ has length $O(\sqrt L)$. For $q\geq 2\sqrt L$ and sufficiently small $\eta $, $\eta ^2\mathrm{e}^{q^2}/q^2\geq \mathrm{e}^{q^2/4}$. The integrand on this remaining interval is bounded by $\exp (-\mathrm{e}^{q^2/4}/(C\kappa ))$, which is integrable on $[1,+\infty )$. The prefactor is $O(\sqrt\eta )$, and the initial interval contributes $O(\sqrt{\eta L})$, so the full expression is $o(1)$. In the large-time region, (8.21) gives

\[ \int d_\eta (x)\, \mathrm{d}x \leq C\sqrt\eta \int _1^{1/(\mathrm{e}\eta )}qt \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\, \mathrm{d}t \leq C\sqrt{\eta L}\int _1^\infty t \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\, \mathrm{d}t=o(1). \]

This proves the integral assertion on $[0,b]$.

Step 5: the radial slope. The negative part of $W_ x$ is uniformly small as well. For $t\leq 1$, we have $F_ t\geq -\eta $ and $0\leq \sigma \leq 2aq$, so

\[ \max \{ -W_ x,0\} \leq \sqrt{2\kappa \eta }\, t\sqrt{\log \frac1{\eta t}}\leq C\sqrt{\eta L}. \]

The last inequality follows because $t\sqrt{L+\log (1/t)}$ is increasing on $(0,1]$ when $L>1$. For $t\geq 1$ and $q\geq 1$, use $|W_ x|\leq C\sqrt\eta \, qt \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ and $q\leq \sqrt L$.

Step 6: the ends. It remains to consider the two ends, including the cutoff region. If $b\leq x\leq x_*$, then $0\leq q\leq 1$ and $1/(\mathrm{e}\eta )\leq t\leq 1/\eta $. The derivatives of the cutoffs and scale have at most polynomial growth in $\eta ^{-1}$. Thus (8.11) implies

\[ \sup _ w\bigl (|W_ x|+|Q[W]-Q[0]|\bigr ) \leq C\mathrm{e}^{-1/(5\mathrm{e}\eta )}. \]

But $Q[0]=\mu (1-\mu )\geq \mathrm{e}^{-x_*}/4$ on this interval, and (8.15) shows that the error is $o(\mathrm{e}^{-x_*})$. Hence $Q[W]>0$ there. For $x\geq x_*$, $W=0$ and positivity is immediate. For $x\leq 0$, $W_ x=0$ and

\[ Q[W]\geq \mu \left(1-\frac\mu {1-\eta }\right)>0, \]

since $\mu \leq 1/2$. On $[-2,-1]$, the bounds $1/9\leq \mu \leq 3/10$ and $1-\eta \geq 9/10$ give $Q[W]\geq 2/27$. Finally, $Q[W]$ is continuous, with positive denominator, so the maximum of its negative part over the compact fiber $E$ is continuous in $x$. This proves the assertions about support and continuity, and completes the proof. $\square$
