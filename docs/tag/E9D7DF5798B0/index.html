Chapter 11 · Non-pluripolar measures and mass · Tag E9D7DF5798B0

Diffuse polar zero nonpluripolar mass

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Example 15.3.8

Example 15.3.8. Let $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^1$, as in proposition 15.3.7. There is a nonpositive $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic function $q$ on $\mathbb P^1$ such that

\[ \nu (q,x)=0\quad \text{for every }x,\qquad \mathcal I(tq)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}\quad (t>0),\qquad \langle \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\rangle =0. \]

Let $\mu $ be the atom-free probability measure on the compact polar set $K$ of proposition 4.2.4, and let $q$ be its potential from proposition 15.3.7. Since $\mu $ has no atoms, all Lelong numbers of $q$ vanish, and corollary 12.3.2 gives the assertion on multiplier ideals. On the affine chart, $q$ differs from the function $p(z)=\int _ K\log |z-a|^2\, \mathrm d\mu (a)$ of proposition 4.2.4 by the smooth function $-\log (1+|z|^2)$ and a constant, and $p=-\infty $ on $K$; hence $q=-\infty $ on $K$. Applying proposition 15.3.6 to local potentials of $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$, we obtain

\[ \langle \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ q>-\infty \} }\mu =0, \]

because $\mu $ is carried by $K$. By contrast, the potential $0$ has the same multiplier ideals and non-pluripolar mass one. Thus exponential integrability at every scale does not determine the non-pluripolar mass.
