Local bergman approximation

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Theorem 13.2.3

Theorem 13.2.3. Let $\Omega $ have diameter at most $L$, and put $u_ m=m^{-1}\log K_ m$. Then $u_ m$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega $ and has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$. There are constants $C_1,C_2>0$, depending only on $n$ and $L$, such that

13.3
\begin{equation} \label{eq:local-bergman-bounds} u(z)-\frac{\log C_1}{m}\leq u_ m(z) \leq \sup _{B(z,r)}u+\frac1m\log \frac{C_2}{r^{2n}} \end{equation}

for all $z\in \Omega $ and $r>0$ with $B(z,r)\Subset \Omega $. As $m\to \infty $, $u_ m\to u$ pointwise and in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega )$. For every $z\in \Omega $,

13.4
\begin{equation} \label{eq:local-bergman-lelong} \nu (u,z)-\frac nm\leq \nu (u_ m,z)\leq \nu (u,z). \end{equation}

Finally, for each fixed $m$, the function $\exp (m(u_ m-u))$ is locally integrable on $\Omega $. All these assertions remain true if Lebesgue measure is replaced by $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ in the definition of $H_ m$, provided $C_1$ and $C_2$ are replaced by $C_\varrho C_1$ and $C_2/c_\varrho $.

Proof. Let $\Omega _0$ be a connected component of $\Omega $, and choose $a\in \Omega _0$ with $u(a)>-\infty $. By corollary 11.4.5, applied on $\Omega $ to the weight $mu$, some element of $H_ m$ takes the value $1$ at $a$, so $H_ m$ contains a function that does not vanish identically on $\Omega _0$. For every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_ m$ that is not identically zero on $\Omega _0$, $\log |F|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega _0$ by proposition 8.3.3, and $\log K_ m$ is the supremum of these functions on $\Omega _0$. Since $K_ m$ is continuous by lemma 13.2.2, this family is locally uniformly bounded above, and $\log K_ m$ is upper semicontinuous with values in $[-\infty ,\infty )$, so it equals its upper semicontinuous regularization. Hence theorem 8.3.2 shows that $u_ m$ is plurisubharmonic. The comparison in lemma 13.2.2 gives $u_ m=m^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ locally, with generators $g_ i$ of $\mathcal I(mu)$; this is the asserted type.

For the lower bound in (13.3), let $u(z)>-\infty $; at points of the pole set of $u$ there is nothing to prove. By corollary 11.4.5, applied on $\Omega $ to the weight $mu$, there is $F\in H_ m$ with $F(z)=1$ and $\| F\| _ m^2\leq C_1\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}$, where $C_1$ depends only on $n$ and $L$. Hence $K_ m(z)\geq |F(z)|^2/\| F\| _ m^2\geq C_1^{-1}\mathrm e^{mu(z)}$. For the upper bound, let $\| F\| _ m\leq 1$. The mean-value inequality (12.9) of lemma 12.2.6 for $|F|^2$ on $B(z,r)$, where $|B(z,r)|=\pi ^ nr^{2n}/n!$, gives

\begin{align*} |F(z)|^2& \leq C_2r^{-2n}\int _{B(z,r)}|F|^2\\ & \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp \Bigl (m\sup _{B(z,r)}u\Bigr )\int _{B(z,r)}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu} \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp \Bigl (m\sup _{B(z,r)}u\Bigr ), \end{align*}

with $C_2=n!/\pi ^ n$, and taking the supremum over $F$ proves the upper bound.

Now fix $z$ and $r$, and let $m\to \infty $ in (13.3): we obtain $u(z)\leq \varliminf _ mu_ m(z)\leq \varlimsup _ mu_ m(z)\leq \sup _{B(z,r)}u$. Letting $r\downarrow 0$ and using the upper semicontinuity of $u$ gives $u_ m(z)\to u(z)$, also at points of the pole set. On a compact set $K\subset \Omega $, a fixed $r<\operatorname {dist}(K,\partial \Omega )$ in the upper bound gives a uniform upper bound for all $u_ m$, while $u_ m\geq u-|\log C_1|$. Since $u\in L^1(K)$, dominated convergence gives $u_ m\to u$ in $L^1(K)$.

For the Lelong numbers, let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s$ generate $\mathcal I(mu)$ near $z$. The stalk $\mathcal I(mu)_ z$ is not zero: by the first paragraph, applied to the component of $\Omega $ containing $z$, it contains the germ of an element of $H_ m$ that does not vanish identically on that component, and this germ is not zero by the identity theorem. Since $u_ m=m^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ near $z$, lemma 13.1.6 gives $\nu (u_ m,z)=m^{-1}\operatorname {ord}_ z\mathcal I(mu)$, and (13.4) follows from lemma 12.3.1.

Finally, near each point $K_ m\leq C\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$ with $g_ i\in H_ m$, by lemma 13.2.2, and each $|g_ i|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is integrable on $\Omega $. Hence $\exp (m(u_ m-u))=K_ m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable.

Now replace Lebesgue measure by $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$, and let $K^\varrho _ m$ be the corresponding kernel. The space of holomorphic functions is the same, and the two norms satisfy $c_\varrho \| F\| _ m^2\leq \| F\| _{m,\varrho }^2\leq C_\varrho \| F\| _ m^2$. If $\| F\| _ m\leq 1$, then $F/C_\varrho ^{1/2}$ lies in the unit ball for $\varrho $, and if $\| F\| _{m,\varrho }\leq 1$, then $c_\varrho ^{1/2}F$ lies in the unit ball for Lebesgue measure; hence

\[ C_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m\leq K^\varrho _ m\leq c_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m. \]

The proof of plurisubharmonicity applies verbatim to $m^{-1}\log K^\varrho _ m$, with the unit ball taken for $\varrho $ and the continuity of $K^\varrho _ m$ given by lemma 13.2.2. The lower and upper bounds follow from the last display, with the constants $C_\varrho C_1$ and $C_2/c_\varrho $. The convergence and the Lelong numbers then follow as before, since lemma 13.2.2 applies to $K^\varrho _ m$. Finally, $K^\varrho _ m\mathrm e^{-mu}\leq c_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable. $\square$
