Chapter 12 · Monge–Ampère equations and stability · Tag 9E30269224CD

Positive exponent endpoint attainment

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 14.34.10

Proposition 14.34.10. Suppose $\Lambda _ h(f)\ne \varnothing $ and $a=\inf \Lambda _ h(f)>0$. Then $a\in \Lambda _ h(f)$ if and only if there are exponents $\lambda _ j>a$ decreasing to $a$ and solutions $u_ j$ at exponent $\lambda _ j$ with $\inf _ j\sup _ Xu_ j> -\infty $.

If $a\notin \Lambda _ h(f)$, then every sequence of solutions $u_ j$ at exponents $\lambda _ j>a$ decreasing to $a$ satisfies

\begin{gather*} \sup _ Xu_ j\longrightarrow -\infty ,\qquad \mathrm e^{\lambda _ ju_ j}\longrightarrow 0\ \text{uniformly on }X,\\ \| \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_ j)\| _{\mathrm{TV}} \longrightarrow 0, \end{gather*}

where the exponential is zero on the pole sets.

Proof. Suppose first that $a\in \Lambda _ h(f)$, let $u_ a$ be a solution at $a$, and put $s=u_ a-\max (0,\sup _ Xu_ a)$. As in the proof of proposition 14.34.7, $s$ is an exponential subsolution at every $\lambda \geq a$, so $U_\lambda \geq s$. Hence the greatest solutions at any exponents $\lambda _ j\downarrow a$ have suprema at least $\sup _ Xs>-\infty $.

Conversely, let $\lambda _ j$ and $u_ j$ be as in the statement. The lemma with $\tau =a$ gives the common upper bound $u_ j\leq K_ a$. Write $M_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j$. After extraction, $M_ j\to M\in \mathbb R$ and, by theorem 1.4.2, $u_ j-M_ j\to v$ in $L^1$ for some $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$. Then $u_ j\to u=v+M$ in $L^1$ and, after a further extraction, almost everywhere. Since $a\leq \lambda _ j\leq \lambda _1$, $u_ j\leq K_ a$ and $K_ a\geq 0$, the densities $\mathrm e^{\lambda _ ju_ j}f$ are bounded by $\mathrm e^{\lambda _1K_ a}f$, and dominated convergence gives

\[ \mathrm e^{\lambda _ ju_ j}f\longrightarrow \mathrm e^{au}f \quad \text{in }L^1(\mathrm dV). \]

Lemma 14.27.1 now gives $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u)\geq \mathrm e^{au}f\, \mathrm dV$. Thus $u$ is an exponential subsolution at $a$, and theorem 14.34.1 gives a solution at $a$.

Finally, suppose $a\notin \Lambda _ h(f)$, and let $u_ j$ be solutions at exponents $\lambda _ j\downarrow a$. If $\sup _ Xu_ j$ did not tend to $-\infty $, a subsequence would satisfy the condition just shown to imply $a\in \Lambda _ h(f)$. Hence $M_ j\to -\infty $. Once $M_ j<0$, since $\lambda _ j\geq a>0$,

\[ 0\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda _ ju_ j}\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda _ jM_ j}\leq \mathrm e^{aM_ j} \longrightarrow 0, \]

and the total mass $\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda _ ju_ j}f\, \mathrm dV$ is at most $\mathrm e^{aM_ j}\| f\| _{L^1}$. $\square$
