Example 10.7.3F8EB82A5AD2B

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Example 10.7.3

Example 10.7.3. Choose $0<a<1/2$, and use the function $s=\log |z|^2$ on the covering space $\mathbb C^3\setminus \{ 0\} $ from chapter 8. Since $s$ changes by $-2$ under the defining contraction, every $2$-periodic function of $s$ descends to $X$. For an integer $m\geq 1$, define the smooth normalized potential

\[ u_ m=\frac{a}{\pi ^2m^2}\bigl (\cos (\pi m s)-1\bigr ), \qquad \| u_ m-Au_ m\| _{C^0}=\frac{a}{\pi ^2m^2}\longrightarrow 0. \]

Primes below denote derivatives with respect to $s$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s=\alpha $ and $\mathrm{d}s\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s=\beta $, the chain rule gives

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ m=(1+u_ m')\alpha +(1+u_ m'')\beta , \quad u_ m'=-\frac{a}{\pi m}\sin (\pi m s),\quad u_ m''=-a\cos (\pi m s). \]

Both coefficients are bounded below by positive constants independent of $m$. The rank identities $\alpha ^3=\beta ^2=0$ imply

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ m)^3=H_ m\omega ^3,\qquad H_ m=\left(1-\frac{a}{\pi m}\sin (\pi m s)\right)^2 \bigl (1-a\cos (\pi m s)\bigr ). \]

On each fiber, $s$ is twice the real Haar coordinate plus a constant depending on the base point, so its distribution modulo $2$ is uniform. When the periodic expression $H_ m=(1+u_ m')^2(1+u_ m'')$ is averaged, the terms $u_ m'$, $u_ m''$, $u_ m'u_ m''$ and $(u_ m')^2u_ m''$ contribute zero, since they are derivatives of periodic functions. Hence

\[ I_ m:=\int _ XH_ m\, \mathrm{d}\nu =AH_ m =1+\frac{a^2}{2\pi ^2m^2}. \]

Set $f_ m=H_ m/I_ m$. These smooth densities have unit integral and common strictly positive lower and finite upper bounds. Uniqueness of the constant gives $c_{f_ m}=I_ m\to 1$, and $Af_ m=1$. Nevertheless,

\[ \| f_ m-1+a\cos (\pi m s)\| _{C^0(X)}=O(m^{-1}), \qquad \lim _{m\to \infty }\| f_ m-Af_ m\| _{L^1(X,\nu )}=\frac{2a}{\pi }>0. \]

Here the constant in $O(m^{-1})$ depends only on $a$, and the last identity uses the fact that the average of $|\cos |$ over a period is $2/\pi $. Thus near equality does not imply strong $L^1$ convergence to the fiber average, even for uniformly positive bounded smooth densities. For any pullback $g$ of a function on the base, the contraction property gives $\| f_ m-Af_ m\| _1\leq 2\| f_ m-g\| _1$, so approximation by such pullbacks also fails in $L^1$.
