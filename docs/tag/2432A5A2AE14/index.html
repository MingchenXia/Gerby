Curve atomic source metric

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 19.5.3

Theorem 19.5.3. For positive atomic measures $D,E$ with masses less than $A$,

\[ \frac1{96}\| D-E\| _{\mathrm{TV}} \leq d_{S,\beta }(q_ D,q_ E) \leq \frac12\| D-E\| _{\mathrm{TV}}. \]

Thus the singularity distance is a metric on these ideal envelopes, and convergence for it is exactly convergence of the atomic measures in total variation.

Proof. Write $d_ S=d_{S,\beta }$ and $a\lor b=\max (a,b)$. For potentials $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$ of positive mass, besides the formula for ordered potentials we use the inequality [Lemma 6.2.1, SGPT]

\[ d_ S(u,v)\leq d_ S(u,u\lor v)+d_ S(v,u\lor v) \leq 48d_ S(u,v) \]

on a curve; here $48=3(1+1)2^{1+2}$ is the constant of that lemma in dimension one.

Put $z=\max (w_ D,w_ E)$. By lemma 19.5.2, the polar parts of the curvature measures of $w_ D$ and $w_ E$ are $D$ and $E$. By (1.14), the polar part of the curvature measure of $z$ is $D\wedge E$, the atomic measure with the smaller coefficient at each point. Stokes' theorem therefore gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\beta (z)(Y)=A-(D\wedge E)(Y). \]

The formula for ordered potentials yields

\[ \begin{aligned} d_ S(w_ D,z)+d_ S(w_ E,z) & =\frac12\bigl (D(Y)+E(Y)-2(D\wedge E)(Y)\bigr )\\ & =\frac12\| D-E\| _{\mathrm{TV}}. \end{aligned} \]

Combining this equality with the two inequalities above proves both bounds, since replacing $w_ D,w_ E$ by the ordinarily equivalent $q_ D,q_ E$ does not change the singularity distance. The lower bound and the injectivity in lemma 19.5.2 make the pseudometric a metric on these envelopes. $\square$
