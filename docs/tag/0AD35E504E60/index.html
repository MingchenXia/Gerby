32.4 General measures in the minimal class0AD35E504E60

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 32.4 General measures in the minimal class

We now solve the equations in $\mathcal E_ V$. For a measure $\nu $ with $A_\theta (\nu )<\infty $, the coordinate convolution of 1.23.4 gives approximating equations with smooth densities. The integral estimates for this smoothing, combined with the higher-moment bound, give convergence of the solutions against the original measure. The resulting self-measure error yields convergence in capacity, and a first-moment tail estimate identifies the limit of the Monge–Ampère measures.

Proposition 32.4.1. Let $\nu $ be a non-pluripolar probability measure with $A_\theta (\nu )<\infty $, and let $\nu _ j$ be its smooth approximations defined by (1.76). Fix a constant $C>0$ and let $v_ j,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy

\[ v_ j\sim V,\qquad \sup _ Xv_ j=0,\qquad \mu _{v_ j}\leq C\nu _ j, \qquad v_ j\longrightarrow v\text{ in }L^1(X). \]

Then $v\in \mathcal E_ V$, the convergence also holds in $L^1(\nu )$ and in capacity, and

\[ \mu _{v_ j}\rightharpoonup \mu _ v. \]

Proof. The limit $v$ is not identically $-\infty $ and satisfies $\sup _ Xv=0$, and $v_ j,v\leq V\leq 0$. By lemma 1.23.6, $\sup _ jA_\theta (\nu _ j)<\infty $, which gives the uniform estimate

32.12
\begin{equation} \int _ X(-v_ j)\, \mu _{v_ j} \leq C\int _ X(-v_ j)\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j\leq C'. \end{equation}

In particular the relative first moments $\int _ X(V-v_ j)\mu _{v_ j}$ are bounded, and proposition 32.3.1, with fixed reference $w=V$, puts $v$ in $\mathcal E_ V$.

By proposition 32.3.4, applied to the fixed measure $\nu $, the $v_ j$ are uniformly integrable with respect to $\nu $. For each fixed $T$, the bounded functions $\max (v_ j,-T)$ have a common lower curvature bound in a Hermitian background $\Omega \geq \theta $ and converge in $L^1$ for smooth volume to $\max (v,-T)$; hence lemma 1.23.1 gives their convergence in $L^1(\nu )$. These truncations need not be $\theta $-plurisubharmonic; they are used only in the larger Hermitian background. Uniform integrability, followed by $T\to \infty $, gives $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1(\nu )$, and the limit is integrable because $A_\theta (\nu )<\infty $.

The smoothing lemma, in the form (1.77), now yields

\[ \int _ X|v_ j-v|\, \mu _{v_ j} \leq C\int _ X|v_ j-v|\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j\longrightarrow 0. \]

Therefore the self-measure convergence theorem theorem 32.3.2 gives convergence in capacity. Finally, (32.12) gives the sublevel estimate

\[ \sup _ j\mu _{v_ j}(\{ v_ j\leq -T\} )\leq C'/T, \]

and the masses of the $\mu _{v_ j}$ are bounded by $M_ V$. The tail criterion theorem 7.6.1 therefore gives the asserted weak convergence. $\square$
