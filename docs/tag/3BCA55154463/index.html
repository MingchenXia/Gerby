The global curvature bound

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.15 The global curvature bound

We return to the compact manifold $X$ with its Hermitian form $\omega $. Demailly's global regularization theorem glues the local Bergman constructions while controlling the curvature error. We use it as a classical input in the following form.

Theorem 1.15.1 (Demailly regularization). Let $u$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on $X$ and let $\gamma $ be a continuous real $(1,1)$-form with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \gamma $. There are quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u_ m$, defined for all sufficiently large positive integers $m$, and numbers $\epsilon _ m\downarrow 0$ such that

  1. $u_ m$ has analytic type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$;

  2. $u_ m\geq u$ and $u_ m\to u$ pointwise and in $L^1(X)$;

  3. $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ m\geq \gamma -\epsilon _ m\omega $;

  4. $\max \{ \nu (u,x)-n/m,0\} \leq \nu (u_ m,x)\leq \nu (u,x)$ for all $x$.

The sequence $(u_ m)$ need not be monotone.

This is the form of [Theorem 14.12, DemAM11] used in this book. The local generators in the construction are the weighted Bergman generators of $\mathcal I(mu)$; smooth local corrections do not change this ideal. On overlaps the local Bergman sums are comparable, so the glued logarithm keeps the analytic type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$. The construction freezes the continuous lower bound $\gamma $ on sufficiently small coordinate balls, and its curvature error combines the modulus of continuity of $\gamma $ with an overlap term that tends to zero as $m$ increases. It is this global curvature estimate, and not merely the local convergence of proposition 1.14.1, that we take from the theorem.

Corollary 1.15.2. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The approximants $u_ m$ of theorem 1.15.1 for $\gamma =-\theta $ satisfy

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ m\geq -\epsilon _ m\omega . \]

In particular, they have analytic type $(1/m,\mathcal I(mu))$.

Proof. Apply theorem 1.15.1 to $u$ with the lower bound $\gamma =-\theta $; the theorem does not require $\gamma $ to be closed. Adding $\theta $ to the curvature inequality gives the assertion. In the normalization of [DemAM11], the theorem is applied to the potential $u/2$, since $\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi }\partial \bar\partial (u/2)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. $\square$

Thus the approximants keep the curvature lower bound up to an error that tends to zero. Their multiplier ideals determine the limit of their ordinary envelopes; see theorem 16.5.4.
