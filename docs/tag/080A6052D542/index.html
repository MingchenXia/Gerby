10 Continuous solutions and normalization080A6052D542

Throughout this chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, $\omega $ is a Hermitian form and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form on $X$. As in chapter 9, we write $\omega _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$; for a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function $u$, the power $\omega _ u^ n$ is the Bedford–Taylor product of proposition 1.7.1. We fix $p>1$ and a nonnegative $f\in L^ p(X,\mathrm{d}V)$ with $\int _ Xf\, \mathrm{d}V>0$. Norms and almost-everywhere statements refer to $\mathrm{d}V$ unless another measure is specified.

We seek continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic solutions of $\omega _ u^ n=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$, with an unknown constant $c>0$, and of $\omega _ u^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\, \mathrm{d}V$, with $\lambda >0$. Smooth positive approximations of $f$ give solutions by chapter 9. To pass to a limit, we need uniform control of both their constants and their potentials. Integration against a Gauduchon metric bounds the constants and hence the $L^ p$ norms of the resulting densities. Modified comparison then turns normalized $L^1$ compactness into uniform compactness, which identifies the limiting Monge–Ampère measure.

For the exponential equation, a weighted Gauduchon mean also controls the additive constants. Letting $\lambda $ decrease to zero determines the normalization constant $c$ from $f$, even when the normalized potential is not unique. When a bounded solution has a smooth positive density depending on the point and the potential, proper exponential equations with a frozen smooth approximation of that potential give a trace estimate and prove that the solution is smooth.

On the Hopf threefold, the constant has a sharp lower bound, with equality exactly for densities pulled back from the projective plane. For densities of unit integral with a common $L^ p$ bound, $1<p<\infty $, near equality forces normalized potentials to approach their fiber averages uniformly and gives weak convergence in $L^ p$ for the density differences. Smooth oscillating examples show why strong $L^1$ convergence of the densities need not follow.

For $n\geq 2$, the final two sections fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\chi $ and study concave functions of the eigenvalues of $\omega ^{-1}(\chi +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)$. An admissible reference with a strict asymptotic gap controls an auxiliary Monge–Ampère comparison, giving stability as positive right-hand sides tend to zero. Equicontinuous data then give uniform compactness. Nearby smooth admissible functions yield a gradient bound if the first derivatives of the data are uniformly bounded.
