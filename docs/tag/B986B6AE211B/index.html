Chapter 5 · Plurisubharmonic functions and positive currents · Tag B986B6AE211B

Ball divisor green

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 1.19.8

Proposition 1.19.8. The envelope $G_ m$ is given by

1.67
\begin{equation} \label{eq:ball-divisor-green} G_ m(\zeta ,w)=m\log \frac{|\zeta |^2}{1-|w|^2}. \end{equation}

It is plurisubharmonic and continuous on $B$ with values in $[-\infty ,0)$. The finite part $G_ m-m\log |\zeta |^2$, first defined off $D$, extends smoothly across $D$ as the plurisubharmonic function

\[ H_ m(w)=-m\log (1-|w|^2). \]

Proof. The function $-\log (1-|w|^2)$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic on $|w|<1$: its complex Hessian is

\[ \left(\frac{\delta _{jk}}{1-|w|^2} +\frac{\overline w_ jw_ k}{(1-|w|^2)^2}\right)_{j,k}. \]

Thus the right-hand side of (1.67) is plurisubharmonic; it is negative on $B$ and satisfies the pole condition, so it is a competitor. For the reverse inequality, fix a competitor $u$ and a point $w$ with $|w|<1$. The restriction $u(\cdot ,w)$ is either identically $-\infty $ or subharmonic on the disk of radius $R=(1-|w|^2)^{1/2}$. In the latter case, $u(\zeta ,w)-m\log |\zeta |^2$ is subharmonic on the punctured disk and, by the pole condition, bounded above near zero, so it extends as a subharmonic function across zero.

Since $u\leq 0$, this extension is at most $-m\log r^2$ on every circle $|\zeta |=r<R$. The maximum principle and then $r\uparrow R$ give

\[ u(\zeta ,w)\leq m\log \frac{|\zeta |^2}{R^2}. \]

On $D$ both sides are $-\infty $. Taking the supremum over $u$ proves the formula. Near a point of $D$, the function $H_ m$ is bounded and the logarithmic term tends to $-\infty $, which proves continuity there as an extended-real function. $\square$
