Comparison with scalar normalization

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.4.3 Comparison with scalar normalization

We return to $X$ of arbitrary dimension, with the fixed volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. The scalar pseudometric $\Delta $ of (15.28) compares the normalization scalars $B_\varphi $ of section 15.1 uniformly over all continuous logarithmic densities. When an analytic singularity is shrunk to zero, the estimate in a smaller background makes this scalar distance tend to zero. At a fixed mass, however, the normalization scalar in a closed background does not see the location of the poles.

Example 16.4.4. Let $\theta $ be semipositive and big, and let $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ have analytic singularities with $a\leq 0$. Use the normalization scalars $B_\varphi $ and the sets $\mathcal A_\varphi (G)$ of supporting probabilities from section 15.1, section 15.2, defined with the fixed volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. Set

\[ \varphi _ t=P_\theta [ta],\qquad t\in \mathbb Q\cap (0,1). \]

By proposition 16.2.7, $\varphi _ t\sim ta$; since the ordinary envelope depends only on the ordinary type, $P_\theta [\varphi _ t]=\varphi _ t$. Moreover $ta\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,t\theta )$ and $ta\leq \varphi _ t$, so the envelope $P_{t\theta }(\varphi _ t)$ in the smaller background is not identically $-\infty $. By proposition 15.7.1,

\[ \sup _{G\in C(X)}|B_{\varphi _ t}(G)-B_0(G)| \leq -n\log (1-t)\longrightarrow 0 \qquad (t\downarrow 0). \]

When $\theta $ is Hermitian and $G$ is fixed and smooth, every choice in $\mathcal A_{\varphi _ t}(G)$ converges weakly in $L^1$ to the smooth Gauduchon adjoint probability, by corollary 15.7.4, theorem 15.2.5.

By contrast, the projective references in example 15.10.2 have equal mass and identical normalization functionals, although their poles and multiplier ideals differ.
