Singularities along a section over a divisor

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.10 Singularities along a section over a divisor

We now replace the isolated poles by a pole along the image of a holomorphic section of the fibration over a divisor of the base. The selected sections must then vanish to a certain order along that divisor. As for point poles, the normal variables in the fiber shift the integrability threshold only by a bounded amount, so this order differs from the order imposed by a divisorial pole on the base by a bounded number. Restriction sequences along the divisor show that each unit of vanishing order costs $O(m^{q-1})$ sections, and kernel convergence again reduces to the equilibrium theorem on the base.

For the restriction sequences we need a bound for the dimensions of section spaces that is uniform over a range of twists growing linearly with $m$.

Lemma 27.10.1. Let $M$ be a compact complex manifold of dimension $d\geq 0$, and let $E,F$ be holomorphic line bundles on $M$. For each $C_0>0$ there is a constant $C_1$ such that

\[ h^0(M,E^ m\otimes F^ j)\leq C_1m^ d \qquad (m\geq 1,\ j\in \mathbb Z,\ |j|\leq C_0m). \]

Proof. For $d=0$, the manifold $M$ is a finite set and the dimension is at most its number of points. Let $d\geq 1$. Fix smooth Hermitian metrics on $E$ and $F$, a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$, and finitely many coordinate charts with holomorphic frames, together with smaller subcharts that still cover $M$. For $|j|\leq C_0m$, the local weight of the metric on $E^ m\otimes F^ j$ has second derivatives bounded by $Cm$. At a point $p$ of a smaller subchart, multiply the frame by the exponential of a holomorphic affine function to remove the constant and real-linear terms of this weight at $p$. The new weight vanishes at $p$ and is bounded by $Cm|z-p|^2$ on a fixed neighborhood. A ball of radius $\varepsilon m^{-1/2}$ about $p$ lies in the chart, with $\varepsilon >0$ independent of $p$, $m$ and $j$, and on this ball the weight is bounded. The submean inequality for the holomorphic coefficient of a section $s$ on this ball gives

\[ |s(p)|^2\leq Cm^ d\int _ M|s|^2\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where all constants depend only on the fixed charts, metrics, volume and $C_0$. Evaluation at $p$ has a one-dimensional target, so for an $L^2$-orthonormal basis $s_1,\ldots ,s_ N$ of $H^0(M,E^ m\otimes F^ j)$ its squared norm is $\sum _{\ell =1}^ N|s_\ell (p)|^2\leq Cm^ d$. Integrating over $M$ gives $N\leq C\operatorname {Vol}(M)m^ d$. $\square$

Let $\pi :X\to B$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion of connected compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers of dimension $r\geq 1$, and suppose that $B$ is Kähler of dimension $q\geq 1$. Fix a holomorphic line bundle $A$ on $B$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h_ A$ whose curvature $\theta =c_1(A,h_ A)$ is positive. Let $Z\subset B$ be a nonempty smooth divisor, and suppose that $\pi $ has a holomorphic section $\sigma :Z\to X$ over $Z$; put $Y=\sigma (Z)$. Fix $c>0$ and a smooth metric $h_ Z$ on $\mathcal O_ B(Z)$, with curvature $\Theta _ Z$, such that $\alpha :=\theta -c\Theta _ Z>0$. Let $s_ Z$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal O_ B(Z)$, and set $\psi =c\log |s_ Z|_{h_ Z}^2$.

Fix a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$, chosen independently of $h_ A$. Suppose that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus Y$ and that, in submersion coordinates $(x,z',w_1,\ldots ,w_ r)$ near each point of $Y$,

27.33
\begin{equation} \label{eq:horizontal-center-singularity} \begin{gathered} \varphi =c\log \left(|x|^2+\sum _{j=1}^ r|w_ j|^2\right)+O(1),\\ Z=\{ x=0\} ,\qquad Y=\{ x=w_1=\cdots =w_ r=0\} , \end{gathered} \end{equation}

with a locally bounded remainder. Here $x$ and $z'$ are coordinates on the base, and the vector $z'$ of the $q-1$ tangential coordinates is absent when $q=1$.

Theorem 27.10.2. The function $\psi $ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, with positive non-pluripolar mass

\[ \int _ B\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=\int _ B\alpha ^ q>0. \]

For every integer $m\geq 1$ put $\ell _ m=\lfloor mc\rfloor $ and $k_ m=\max \{ \ell _ m-r,0\} $. Under holomorphic descent, the spaces selected by $\varphi $ on $X$ and by $\psi $ on $B$ are

\[ \begin{split} V_ m& :=H^0\bigl (X,\pi ^*A^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\varphi )\bigr ) =H^0\bigl (B,A^ m(-k_ m Z)\bigr ),\\ W_ m& :=H^0\bigl (B,A^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\psi )\bigr ) =H^0\bigl (B,A^ m(-\ell _ m Z)\bigr )\subseteq V_ m. \end{split} \]

There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed data, such that

\[ 0\leq \dim V_ m-\dim W_ m\leq Cm^{q-1}. \]

For $q=1$ the difference is at most $r\, \# Z$ for every $m$.

Proof. By the Poincaré–Lelong formula, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\alpha +c[Z]\geq 0$. The divisorial measure identity (15.31), applied with the residual form $\alpha >0$ and the bounded part zero, gives $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=\alpha ^ q$, which proves the mass formula.

Every section of $\pi ^*A^ m$ descends by proposition 27.7.1. In the $r+1$ normal variables $(x,w)$, lemma 1.16.1 gives

\[ \mathcal I(m\varphi )=(x,w_1,\ldots ,w_ r)^{k_ m}. \]

The local coefficient $g(x,z')$ of a pulled-back section does not depend on $w$. Expanding $g$ in powers of $x$, we see that $g$ lies in this ideal exactly when the coefficients of $x^ j$ vanish for $j<k_ m$, that is, when $x^{k_ m}$ divides $g$. This is an identity of germs in all tangential variables, so it holds at every point of $Z$. Outside $Y$ the function $\varphi $ is locally bounded. On the base, the same lemma in the single normal variable $x$ gives $\mathcal I(m\psi )=\mathcal O_ B(-\ell _ mZ)$. These facts prove the two identities and the inclusion.

For $k_ m\leq j<\ell _ m$, the restriction sequence along $Z$,

\[ \begin{split} 0& \longrightarrow A^ m(-(j+1)Z)\longrightarrow A^ m(-jZ)\\ & \longrightarrow \bigl (A^ m(-jZ)\bigr )|_ Z\longrightarrow 0, \end{split} \]

shows that passing from vanishing order $j$ to order $j+1$ along $Z$ loses at most $h^0(Z,(A^ m(-jZ))|_ Z)$ dimensions. Apply lemma 27.10.1 on $Z$ with $E=A|_ Z$ and $F=\mathcal O_ B(-Z)|_ Z$; since $0\leq j\leq cm$, each loss is at most $Cm^{q-1}$, uniformly in $j$. There are at most $r$ steps, which proves the estimate. When $q=1$, the divisor $Z$ is a finite set and each restriction space has dimension $\# Z$, which gives the last assertion directly. $\square$

We call $\psi $ the reference potential, $W_ m$ the reference spaces, and $\dim V_ m-\dim W_ m$ the defect; the same terms are used for the analogous comparisons below. The following example shows that the order $m^{q-1}$ in theorem 27.10.2 is sharp.

Example 27.10.3. For all integers $q,r,k\geq 1$ and every real $0<c<k$, the hypotheses above are satisfied on $X=\mathbb P^ q\times \mathbb P^ r$ with $B=\mathbb P^ q$ and $A=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^ q}(k)$, for suitable choices made in the proof. For $q\geq 2$ the defect is

\[ \dim V_ m-\dim W_ m =\frac{r(k-c)^{q-1}}{(q-1)!}m^{q-1}+O(m^{q-2}), \]

and for $q=1$ it equals $\min \{ r,\lfloor mc\rfloor \} $ for every $m$.

Proof. Use homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots :Z_ q]$ on the base and $[W_0:\cdots :W_ r]$ on the fiber. Let $Z=\{ Z_0=0\} $ and $Y=Z\times \{ [1:0:\cdots :0]\} $, and let $\omega _ B,\omega _ F$ be the Fubini–Study forms normalized to have top integrals one. Define

\[ R_ B=\frac{|Z_0|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ q|Z_ i|^2},\qquad R_ F=\frac{\sum _{j=1}^ r|W_ j|^2}{\sum _{j=0}^ r|W_ j|^2},\qquad \varphi =c\log (R_ B+R_ F),\quad \psi =c\log R_ B. \]

The function $\varphi $ is smooth off $Y$ and satisfies (27.33). Over a common denominator, the numerator of $R_ B+R_ F$ is

\[ \sum _{j=0}^ r|Z_0W_ j|^2+\sum _{i=0}^ q\sum _{j=1}^ r|Z_ iW_ j|^2, \]

whose logarithm is plurisubharmonic in each affine chart. Hence $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -c(\omega _ B+\omega _ F)$. Choose an integer $b\geq c$ and set $\omega =k\omega _ B+b\omega _ F$. This is a Hermitian form, and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq (k-c)\omega _ B+(b-c)\omega _ F\geq 0$. On the base, $\theta =k\omega _ B$, $\Theta _ Z=\omega _ B$ and $\alpha =(k-c)\omega _ B>0$.

Put $\ell _ m=\lfloor mc\rfloor $. Divisibility of homogeneous polynomials by powers of $Z_0$ gives the exact dimensions

\[ \dim W_ m=\binom {km-\ell _ m+q}{q},\qquad \dim V_ m=\binom {km-\max \{ \ell _ m-r,0\} +q}{q}. \]

For large $m$, the two residual degrees differ by $r$ and equal $(k-c)m+O(1)$. Subtracting the two binomial coefficients, which are polynomials of degree $q$ in the residual degrees, gives the asserted leading coefficient and remainder. For $q=1$ the difference is $\ell _ m-\max \{ \ell _ m-r,0\} =\min \{ r,\ell _ m\} $ for every $m$. $\square$

The bounded shift of divisorial order thus has negligible normalized codimension. To compare the kernels under the hypotheses of theorem 27.10.2, fix a smooth positive probability volume $\nu _ B$ on $B$, smooth fiber probabilities, and the lifting map $\mathcal L$ of section 27.7, and put $\nu _ X=\mathcal L\nu _ B$. Use the pullback metric on $\pi ^*A$ and, for a real function $v\in C(B)$, the weight $v\circ \pi $ in the norm on $X$. Write $\beta _{X,m,v\circ \pi ,\varphi }^{(q)}$ and $\mathcal F_{X,m}^{(q)}$ for the kernel measure and the determinant functional on $X$, as in section 27.8, and

\[ E(v)=E_{\theta ,B}[\psi ]_{\mathcal I}(v). \]

Corollary 27.10.4. The total variation distance between the kernel measure $\beta _{X,m,v\circ \pi ,\varphi }^{(q)}$ selected by $\varphi $ on $X$ and the lift of the kernel measure selected by $\psi $ on $B$ is $O(m^{-1})$. In particular,

\[ \beta _{X,m,v\circ \pi ,\varphi }^{(q)} \rightharpoonup \mathcal L\bigl (\mathrm{MA}_\theta (E(v))\bigr ). \]

The determinant functionals, normalized to vanish at the zero weight, satisfy

\[ \mathcal F_{X,m}^{(q)}(v\circ \pi )\longrightarrow \int _0^1\left(\int _ Bv\, \mathrm{d}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (E(tv))\right)\mathrm{d}t. \]

With the normalization $(q+r)!/m^{q+r}$ given by the dimension of $X$, the kernel measures tend to zero in total variation.

Proof. As in proposition 27.7.1, fiber integration identifies the norm on $X$ with the norm on $V_ m\subseteq H^0(B,A^ m)$, so the kernel measure on $X$ is the lift of the kernel measure of $V_ m$ on $B$. By lemma 27.8.1, theorem 27.10.2, the latter differs from the kernel measure of $W_ m$ by a positive measure of total mass $q!(\dim V_ m-\dim W_ m)/m^ q=O(m^{-1})$, and so do the lifts. Since $B$ is Kähler and $\psi $ has positive non-pluripolar mass, the equilibrium theorem used in the proof of theorem 27.7.2 applies to $W_ m$ and gives the weak limit. By lemma 27.8.1, the determinant functionals of $V_ m$ and $W_ m$ differ by at most $O(m^{-1})\| v\| _\infty $, and corollary 27.6.4, applied on $B$ with the identity modification, gives the limit of the functionals; its dominated convergence bound is $\| v\| _\infty $ times the bounded sequence of $q$-normalized dimensions. Finally, the $q$-normalized masses are bounded, so multiplying them by $(q+r)!m^{-r}/q!$ proves the last assertion. $\square$

Thus the selecting potentials of (27.33) admit the approximation asked for in 33.9.2, whenever the curvature margin $\theta -c\Theta _ Z$ is positive.
