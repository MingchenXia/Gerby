10.6 The vanishing exponent and scalar uniqueness27FAAF143258

To show that the constant in theorem 10.1.1 is unique, we compare the solution $u_\lambda $ of (10.1) with constant translates of a solution $u$ of the normalized equation. Translating $u$ by its maximum and by its minimum gives two barriers for the exponential equation, whose separation is the oscillation of $u$. After multiplication by $\lambda $, both barriers converge to the logarithm of the constant as $\lambda \downarrow 0$. Since $u_\lambda $ depends only on $f$ and $\lambda $, the constant is determined by $f$.

Proposition 10.6.1. Suppose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap C(X)$ and $c>0$ satisfy $\omega _ u^ n=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$. Set $m=\inf _ Xu$ and $M=\sup _ Xu$. Then, for every $\lambda >0$, the solution of (10.1) satisfies

10.19
\begin{equation} u+\frac{\log c}{\lambda }-M \leq u_\lambda \leq u+\frac{\log c}{\lambda }-m. \end{equation}

In particular,

\[ \| \lambda u_\lambda -\log c\| _\infty \leq \lambda (M-m), \qquad \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }\longrightarrow c \quad \hbox{uniformly on }X\hbox{ as }\lambda \downarrow 0. \]

Consequently, $c$ is the only constant for which the equation $\omega _ v^ n=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$ has a solution $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap C(X)$.

Proof. Set $a=u+\lambda ^{-1}\log c-M$ and $b=u+\lambda ^{-1}\log c-m$. These translates of $u$ have the same Monge–Ampère measure as $u$, and since $m\leq u\leq M$,

\[ \mathrm{e}^{\lambda a}f\, \mathrm{d}V \leq cf\, \mathrm{d}V=\omega _ a^ n=\omega _ b^ n \leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda b}f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Thus $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a}\omega _ a^ n\geq f\, \mathrm{d}V=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u_\lambda }\omega _{u_\lambda }^ n\geq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda b}\omega _ b^ n$. Applying corollary 10.2.4 first to the pair $(u_\lambda ,a)$ and then to the pair $(b,u_\lambda )$ gives $a\leq u_\lambda \leq b$, which is (10.19). Multiplying these inequalities by $\lambda $ and using $m\leq u\leq M$ gives the norm bound, and the uniform convergence follows.

If $(v,c')$ is another such pair, the same argument applied to $v$ gives $\lambda u_\lambda \to \log c'$, because $u_\lambda $ depends only on $f$ and $\lambda $. Hence $c'=c$. $\square$

The barriers also give $\operatorname {osc}_ Xu_\lambda \leq 2(M-m)$, and the densities $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f$ converge to $cf$ in $L^ p$. By theorem 10.3.4, the normalized potentials $u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda $ are therefore precompact in $C^0(X)$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$, and every cluster point solves the equation with constant $c$. Uniqueness of the constant does not, however, single out one cluster point; proposition 14.19.1 describes the symmetries that these limits inherit.

For $C^2$ potentials, uniqueness of the constant also follows directly from the maximum principle: replacing one potential $v$ by $(1+\varepsilon )v$ leaves a strictly positive multiple of $\omega $ in reserve at an extremum.

Proposition 10.6.2. Let $u,v\in C^2(X,\mathbb R)$, and set $A=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and $B=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$. If $A,B\geq 0$ and $A^ n=cB^ n$ for a constant $c>0$, then $c=1$.

Proof. Fix $\varepsilon >0$. At a maximum point of $u-(1+\varepsilon )v$, the complex Hessian of this function is nonpositive, so that

\[ A+\varepsilon \omega \leq (1+\varepsilon )B. \]

At this point $B>0$, because $A+\varepsilon \omega >0$. Monotonicity of the determinant and $A^ n=cB^ n$ give $c\leq (1+\varepsilon )^ n$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $c\leq 1$, and interchanging the two potentials gives $c^{-1}\leq 1$. $\square$
