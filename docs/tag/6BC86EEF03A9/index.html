Theorem 5.1 (Half-space representation)6BC86EEF03A9

Theorem 5.1 (Half-space representation). Every nonnegative harmonic function $h$ on $H$ has a representation

4
\begin{equation} h(x',t)=ct+\int _{\mathbb R^{d-1}}P_ H((x',t),z)\, d\nu (z), \end{equation}

where $c\geq 0$ and $\nu $ is a positive Radon measure satisfying

5
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb R^{d-1}}(1+|z|^2)^{-d/2}\, d\nu (z)<\infty . \end{equation}

Conversely, any such pair defines a nonnegative harmonic function on $H$. The measure need not have finite total mass.

Proof. We first derive the ball representation needed here from the ball Poisson formula and compactness of bounded positive measures on a compact set. Let $v\geq 0$ be harmonic on $B=B(b,R)$ and let $0<r<R$. On $\partial B$ define the measure

\[ d\mu _ r(\zeta )=v\bigl (b+(r/R)(\zeta -b)\bigr )\, dS(\zeta ). \]

The mean-value property gives $\mu _ r(\partial B)=s_{d-1}R^{d-1}v(b)$. For a sequence $r_ j\uparrow R$, compactness therefore gives a subsequence converging weakly to a finite positive measure $\mu $ on $\partial B$. To apply the Euclidean measure-selection theorem, extend $\mu _{r_ j}$ by zero to $\mathbb R^ d$; their common compact support gives uniform tightness, and their weak limit is supported on that same closed sphere. For $|x-b|<r$, the ball Poisson formula, followed by the dilation from $\partial B$ to $\partial B(b,r)$, reads

\[ v(x)=\int _{\partial B}\left(\frac rR\right)^{d-1} \frac{r^2-|x-b|^2}{s_{d-1}r\, |x-b-(r/R)(\zeta -b)|^ d}\, d\mu _ r(\zeta ). \]

For fixed $x\in B$ the integrands converge uniformly on $\partial B$ as $r\uparrow R$. The masses are uniformly bounded, so weak convergence gives

6
\begin{equation} v(x)=\int _{\partial B}\frac{R^2-|x-b|^2}{s_{d-1}R|x-\zeta |^ d}\, d\mu (\zeta ). \end{equation}

This argument also covers $v(b)=0$, in which case all measures and $v$ vanish.

Take $a=-e_ d$, $b=-e_ d/2$, $R=1/2$, and $T(x)=a+(x-a)/|x-a|^2$. Write $q=T(x)$ and $L=|x-a|$. Direct expansion gives

7
\begin{equation} |q-a|=L^{-1},\qquad \frac14-|q-b|^2=\frac{t}{L^2},\qquad |T(x)-T(y)|=\frac{|x-y|}{|x-a||y-a|}. \end{equation}

For the last identity, square both sides and expand the inner products; the numerator on the left becomes $|x-a|^2+|y-a|^2-2(x-a)\cdot (y-a)=|x-y|^2$ after multiplying by $|x-a|^2|y-a|^2$. The middle identity and $T^2=1$ show that $T$ maps $H$ onto $B(b,1/2)$ and maps $\partial H$ onto $\partial B\setminus \{ a\} $.

By Theorem 4.1, $v(q)=|q-a|^{2-d}h(T(q))$ is nonnegative and harmonic on $B$. Apply (6) and split $\mu =m\delta _ a+\mu _0$, where $\mu _0(\{ a\} )=0$. Since $h(x)=L^{2-d}v(T(x))$, the atom contributes $2mt/s_{d-1}$. Let $\sigma =T_*\mu _0$ on $\partial H$, and put

\[ c=2m/s_{d-1},\qquad d\nu (z)=|(z,0)-a|^ d\, d\sigma (z). \]

The measure $\sigma $ is finite and positive, and its continuous positive weight is bounded on compact sets. Hence $\nu $ is a positive Radon measure. The distance identity in (7) transforms the remaining integral into

\[ L^{2-d}\frac{2}{s_{d-1}} \int _{\partial B\setminus \{ a\} } \frac{t/L^2}{|T(x)-\zeta |^ d}\, d\mu _0(\zeta ) =\frac{2t}{s_{d-1}}\int _{\mathbb R^{d-1}} \frac{d\nu (z)}{|x-(z,0)|^ d}. \]

Also $|(z,0)-a|^ d=(1+|z|^2)^{d/2}$, so the integral in (5) equals $\sigma (\mathbb R^{d-1})<\infty $. This proves the required representation.

For the converse, each kernel is smooth and harmonic as a function of $x\in H$. Indeed, with $r=|x-(z,0)|$, the radial differentiation formula used above gives $\Delta r^{-d}=2d r^{-d-2}$ and $\partial _ t r^{-d}=-dt r^{-d-2}$. Consequently $\Delta (t r^{-d})=2dt r^{-d-2}-2dt r^{-d-2}=0$. On a compact subset $K\subset H$, $t$ is bounded above and away from $0$, and $|x'|$ is bounded. Writing $v=x-(z,0)$, differentiation gives

\begin{align*} \partial _ i(t r^{-d})& =\delta _{id}r^{-d}-dtv_ i r^{-d-2},\\ \partial _ j\partial _ i(t r^{-d}) & =-d(\delta _{id}v_ j+\delta _{jd}v_ i+t\delta _{ij})r^{-d-2} +d(d+2)tv_ iv_ j r^{-d-4}. \end{align*}

For $|z|$ large, $r\geq |z|/2$ and $|v_ i|\leq r$, so these formulas bound the kernel and all its $x$ derivatives of orders at most two by $C_ K(1+|z|^2)^{-d/2}$. For bounded $z$, the lower bound on $t$ supplies the same estimate after increasing $C_ K$. These estimates also hold on a compact neighborhood of $K$ inside $H$, so dominated convergence justifies two differentiations under the integral. Condition (5) gives finiteness and harmonicity; positivity is immediate. $\square$
