Limits of exponential equations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 14.33 Limits of exponential equations

In this section $\theta $ is an arbitrary smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $\mu $ a finite non-pluripolar measure. We consider solutions $u_\lambda \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ of

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_\lambda )=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }\mu \qquad (\lambda >0). \]

As $\lambda \downarrow 0$, one expects suitably normalized solutions to converge to a solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $ with a scalar $c>0$. For an $L^ p$ density with $p>1$ and a Hermitian background, the continuous solutions satisfy $\lambda u_\lambda \to \log c$ uniformly (proposition 10.6.1). For solutions in a fixed unbounded ordinary type, no such uniform convergence is possible: by proposition 1.23.2, the $L^\infty $ distance from $\lambda u_\lambda $ to every finite constant is infinite for each $\lambda >0$. The limit must instead be formulated through normalized potentials and their measures. Write

\[ v_\lambda =u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda ,\qquad c_\lambda =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_\lambda }, \]

so that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v_\lambda )=c_\lambda \mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }\mu $. By lemma 1.23.3, $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }\to 1$ in $L^1(\mu )$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$; hence, if $c_\lambda \to c<+\infty $, the right-hand sides converge in total variation to $c\mu $. If $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$ with $f\in L^ p$ for some $p>1$, lemma 1.23.3 gives an error linear in $\lambda $.

To identify the Monge–Ampère measure of an $L^1$ limit of the $v_\lambda $, one needs in addition a continuity theorem for the Monge–Ampère operator. For potentials of a fixed ordinary type, corollary 14.8.3 identifies the limiting measure under a common barrier and a domination hypothesis. In a relative envelope class, theorem 31.3.3 instead constructs a common lower envelope under positive lower mass: for a semipositive big $\theta $ and a reference satisfying (13.26), an $L^1$-convergent sequence in the class whose measures are dominated by one finite non-pluripolar measure converges in capacity, and its measures converge weakly. That argument, with its mass hypothesis, is developed in chapter 31.
