Chapter 5 · Plurisubharmonic functions and positive currents · Tag 4388B85DDD67

Theorem 1.3.9

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Theorem 1.3.9

Theorem 1.3.9. A base of the plurifine topology on $\Omega $ is given by sets of the following form:

1.21
\begin{equation} \label{eq:basis_fine} \left\{ x\in U: \varphi (x)>0\right\} , \end{equation}

where $U\subseteq \Omega $ is an open subset and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(U)$.

Proof. Observe that sets of the form (1.21) are $\mathcal{F}$-open.
Note. This is not entirely obvious by definition, as $\varphi $ is not defined on the whole $\Omega $.
By theorem 1.3.8, it suffices to show its complement in $\Omega $ is thin at each point of (1.21), which is obvious.

Now consider $x\in \Omega $ and an $\mathcal{F}$-open neighborhood $V\subseteq \Omega $ of $x$. We want to find a set of the form (1.21) contained in $V$ and containing $x$.

Write $E=\Omega \setminus V$. In case $x\in \operatorname{Int}V$, there is nothing to prove. So we may assume that $x\in \overline{E}$. By theorem 1.3.8, $E$ is thin at $x$. By definition, there is an open neighborhood $U\subseteq \Omega $ of $x$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(U)$ such that

\[ \varlimsup _{y\to x,y\in U\cap (E\setminus \{ x\} )}\varphi (y)<\varphi (x). \]

We may assume that $\varphi |_{E\cap U}\leq 0<\varphi (x)$. Then the set $\{ y\in U:\varphi (y)>0\} $ suffices for our purpose. $\square$
