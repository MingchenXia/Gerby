Analytic and divisorial singularities

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.2.1 Analytic and divisorial singularities

For a potential $a$ with analytic singularities, the multiplier-ideal order controls the ordinary order: by lemma 1.17.10, every quasi-plurisubharmonic $v$ with $v\preceq _{\mathcal I}a$ satisfies $v\preceq a$. The ideal envelope of $a$ is then a competitor for its ordinary envelope, and the two envelopes coincide. This extends [Proposition 3.2.10, SGPT] to smooth backgrounds.

Proposition 16.2.7. Let $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ have analytic singularities. Then

\[ a\sim P_\theta [a]=P_\theta [a]_{\mathcal I}. \]

In particular the common envelope has analytic singularities with the same coefficient and ideal as $a$, up to a bounded remainder.

Proof. Set $q=P_\theta [a]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection gives $q\sim _{\mathcal I}a$, so lemma 1.17.10 gives $q\preceq a$. Since $q\leq 0$, it is a competitor for $P_\theta [a]$. Together with proposition 16.2.4, this yields

\[ a-\sup _ Xa\leq P_\theta [a]\leq q\leq P_\theta [a]. \]

Hence the two envelopes are equal and ordinarily equivalent to $a$. Since they differ from $a$ by a bounded function, they inherit its analytic presentation. $\square$

The same argument applies to the potential of a countable divisorial current of finite mass, with lemma 1.17.4 in place of lemma 1.17.10.

Corollary 16.2.8. Let $S$ be a closed positive divisorial current of finite mass on a connected compact complex manifold $Z$, and choose a smooth residual decomposition $S=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ as in corollary 1.17.5. For every Hermitian form $\Omega $ with $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(Z,\Omega )$,

\[ P_\Omega [v]=P_\Omega [v]_{\mathcal I}\sim v. \]

Proof. Set $q=P_\Omega [v]_{\mathcal I}$. Projection gives $q\sim _{\mathcal I}v$, and lemma 1.17.4 gives $q\preceq v$. Thus $q$ is a competitor for $P_\Omega [v]$. Together with proposition 16.2.4, this yields $v-\sup _ Zv\leq P_\Omega [v]=q\preceq v$. $\square$
