Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag AD8335312F45

Pencil three band space

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 27.18.4

Proposition 27.18.4. For every $m\geq 1$ the selected space is

\[ V_ m=\operatorname {span}\{ Z_1^ aT_1^{m-a}Z_2^ bT_2^{m-b}: 0\leq a,b\leq m,\ |a-b|\leq 1\} , \qquad N_ m=3m+1. \]

In particular, $N_ m$ grows linearly in $m$. These spaces are not multiplicative. If the sections were instead required to satisfy $|\sigma |_{h^ m}^2\leq C\mathrm e^{m\psi }$, only the monomials with $a=b$ would remain. The measure $\nu $ is Bernstein–Markov for $(K,w)$.

Proof. At $p_+$ the monomial with exponents $(a,b)$ has local exponents $(a,m-b)$, and at $p_-$ it has local exponents $(m-a,b)$. By lemma 27.18.1, both total orders must be at least $m-1$. Distinct exponent pairs cannot cancel, so these conditions select exactly the monomials with $|a-b|\leq 1$. The three bands $a=b$, $a=b+1$ and $a=b-1$ have $m+1$, $m$ and $m$ elements. The bound $|\sigma |_{h^ m}^2\leq C\mathrm e^{m\psi }$ would require total order $m$ at both poles and leave only the diagonal. The product of two sections with offset $a-b=1$ has offset two and lies outside the selected space of the sum degree; such products fail for every pair of positive degrees. Thus the linear growth of $N_ m$ is not the growth of a graded section ring.

Integration over the angles makes all global monomials orthogonal, including those outside the selected space. For $t\in [0,1]$ set

\[ I_ m(t)=\int _{-1}^2\mathrm e^{m(tx-x^2/2)}\, \mathrm dx =\mathrm e^{mt^2/2}J_ m(t),\qquad J_ m(t)=\int _{-1}^2\mathrm e^{-m(x-t)^2/2}\, \mathrm dx. \]

The squared norm of the monomial with exponents $(a,b)$ is

27.133
\begin{equation} \label{eq:pencil-gaussian-norm} A_ m(a,b)=\frac19 I_ m(a/m)I_ m(b/m). \end{equation}

With $c=\int _{-1}^1\mathrm e^{-y^2/2}\, \mathrm dy$, uniformly in $t\in [0,1]$,

\[ \frac c{\sqrt m}\leq J_ m(t)\leq \sqrt{\frac{2\pi }m},\qquad J_ m(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }m} \bigl (1+O(m^{-1/2}\mathrm e^{-m/2})\bigr ). \]

For the lower bound, integrate over the interval of radius $m^{-1/2}$ centered at $t$; for the last assertion, note that each of the two omitted Gaussian tails is at most $\mathrm e^{-m/2}/m$. Hence

27.134
\begin{equation} \label{eq:pencil-gaussian-norm-bounds} \frac{c^2}{9m}\mathrm e^{(a^2+b^2)/(2m)} \leq A_ m(a,b)\leq \frac{2\pi }{9m}\mathrm e^{(a^2+b^2)/(2m)}. \end{equation}

For $x\in [-1,2]^2$, completing squares bounds the weighted contribution of each normalized monomial by $9m/c^2$. Summing over all $(m+1)^2$ global monomials and using the evaluation inequality gives

\[ \sup _ K|\sigma |_{h^ m}\mathrm e^{-mw/2} \leq C m^{3/2}\| \sigma \| _ m \quad (\sigma \in H^0(X,L^ m)). \]

This polynomial bound proves the Bernstein–Markov property. On the selected space the same argument gives the factor $Cm$. $\square$
