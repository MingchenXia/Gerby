Strong openness

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Corollary 12.5.4 (Strong openness)

Corollary 12.5.4 (Strong openness). Let $\varphi $ and $\chi $ be weights on $X$, and let $x\in X$. Then

12.19
\begin{equation} \label{eq:strong-openness} \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x=\bigcup _{\varepsilon >0} \mathcal I((1+\varepsilon )\varphi )_ x \end{equation}

and, more generally,

\[ \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x=\bigcup _{\varepsilon >0} \mathcal I(\varphi +\varepsilon \chi )_ x. \]

Moreover, there is a single $\varepsilon >0$ with $\mathcal I((1+\varepsilon )\varphi )_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$, and similarly a single $\varepsilon >0$ with $\mathcal I(\varphi +\varepsilon \chi )_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$.

Proof. The assertions are local. On a relatively compact neighborhood of $x$ the weights are bounded above, and subtracting constants we may assume $\varphi \leq 0$ and $\chi \leq 0$ there; this changes $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi $ and $\varphi +\varepsilon \chi $ only by bounded amounts and hence changes none of the ideals. Then $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi $ and $\varphi +\varepsilon \chi $ decrease as $\varepsilon $ increases, so the corresponding ideals decrease with $\varepsilon $ and are contained in $\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$. The sequences $(1+1/j)\varphi $ and $\varphi +\chi /j$ increase with $j$ and converge to $\varphi $ wherever $\varphi $ and $\chi $ are finite, hence almost everywhere. By theorem 12.5.3, $\mathcal I((1+1/j)\varphi )_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ and $\mathcal I(\varphi +\chi /j)_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ for all large $j$, which proves both assertions. $\square$
