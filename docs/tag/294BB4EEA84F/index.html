Chapter 5 · Plurisubharmonic functions and positive currents · Tag 294BB4EEA84F

Compact obstacle upper bound

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Lemma 1.19.1

Lemma 1.19.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $w\in C(K,\mathbb R)$. There is a constant $C=C(\theta ,K,w)$ such that every $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfying $a\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$ has $\sup _ X a\leq C$.

Proof. Otherwise there are such functions $a_ j$ with

\[ M_ j:=\sup _ Xa_ j\geq 2^ j+\max _ Kw. \]

The normalized functions $v_ j=a_ j-M_ j\leq 0$ have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms by theorem 1.4.2. Choose $A>0$ with $\theta \leq A\omega $. The partial sums

\[ s_ N=\sum _{j=1}^ N2^{-j}v_ j \]

decrease, have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms, and satisfy $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s_ N\geq -A\omega $. By corollary 1.4.3, their limit $s$ is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function; in particular, $s$ is not identically $-\infty $.

Let $N_ j\subseteq K$ be a pluripolar set outside which $a_ j\leq w$ on $K$. On $K\setminus \bigcup _ jN_ j$ one has $v_ j\leq -2^ j$ for every $j$, hence $s=-\infty $. The pole set $\{ s=-\infty \} $ of the quasi-plurisubharmonic function $s$ is pluripolar, and so is the countable union of the $N_ j$. Their union would contain all of $K$, contradicting the hypothesis on $K$. $\square$
