Chapter 12 · Monge–Ampère equations and stability · Tag 9016C2906071

Individual exponential subsolution

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 14.34.1

Theorem 14.34.1. Fix $\lambda >0$, and suppose that (14.123) has an exponential subsolution $s\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$. For $k\geq 1$, let $u_ k$ be the continuous solution of

\[ \mathrm{MA}_ h(u_ k)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ k}f_ k\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad f_ k=\min (f,k), \]

given by theorem 10.1.2. Then $u_ k$ decreases to a solution $u_\lambda $ of (14.123), and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_\lambda )$ has finite mass. Moreover, $u_\lambda $ is the greatest exponential subsolution: every exponential subsolution $v$ at exponent $\lambda $ satisfies $v\leq u_\lambda $. In particular, $u_\lambda $ is the greatest solution of (14.123).

Proof. Since $f_ k\leq f_{k+1}$, lemma 10.4.2 gives $u_{k+1}\leq u_ k$.

We first show that every exponential subsolution $v$ lies below each $u_ k$. Let $t>0$. On the set $\{ u_ k<v-t\} $,

\[ \mathrm{MA}_ h(u_ k)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ k}f_ k\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v-t). \]

Since $u_ k$ is bounded, $P_{h/2}(u_ k)\geq \inf _ Xu_ k$ is not identically $-\infty $, and $v-t\preceq u_ k$. Strict domination, theorem 14.1.2, therefore gives $u_ k\geq v-t$. Letting $t\downarrow 0$ gives $u_ k\geq v$.

In particular $s\leq u_ k\leq u_1$ for every $k$, so the decreasing limit $u_\lambda \geq s$ is not identically $-\infty $ and belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$. By lemma 1.8.12, $u_ k\to u_\lambda $ in capacity. Choose a constant $A\geq \sup _ Xu_1$. The nonpositive functions $u_ k-A$ satisfy

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_ k-A)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (u_ k-A)}\, \mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f_ k\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

and $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f_ k\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda A}f$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. Theorem 14.33.1, applied with $\mu =\mathrm{d}V$ and constant exponents, shows that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u_\lambda )$ is finite and that $u_\lambda $ solves (14.123). Finally, every exponential subsolution lies below all $u_ k$, hence below $u_\lambda $, which is itself a subsolution. $\square$
