Theorem 5.47.89421BA4DC329

Theorem 5.47.8.9421BA4DC329 Let $E$ be a subset of an open ball $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$;

  2. $E\subset \{ V=-\infty \} $ for some $V\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $V\le 0$;

  3. $H_ E=0$ on $B$.

Every pluripolar subset of $B$ satisfies these conditions; in particular it is globally pluripolar in $B$. Consequently, on every complex manifold $X$, a countable union of pluripolar sets is pluripolar, and if $W\subset X$ is open, every subset of $W$ that is pluripolar in $W$ is pluripolar in $X$.

Proof. We prove (2)$\Rightarrow $(1), then (1)$\Rightarrow $(3) for relatively compact sets $E$ and (3)$\Rightarrow $(2), deduce (1)$\Rightarrow $(2) in general by exhaustion, and finally prove (2)$\Rightarrow $(3). The remaining assertions then follow by localization.

(2)$\Rightarrow $(1). We show more generally that every pluripolar set $E\subset B$ satisfies (1); a set as in (2) is pluripolar. By definition 5.47.2, every point of $B$ has an open neighborhood $W\subset B$ carrying a plurisubharmonic function whose pole set contains $E\cap W$. The second assertion of proposition 7.4.4, applied with $D=B$, therefore gives $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E\cap L)=0$ for every compact set $L\subset B$. Exhausting $B$ by countably many compact sets and using countable subadditivity, we obtain $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$.

(1)$\Rightarrow $(3) when $E\Subset B$. Fix an open set $O$ with $\overline E\subset O\Subset B$. Since $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$, there are open sets $G_ j'\supset E$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j')<1/j$. The open sets $G_ j=O\cap G_1'\cap \cdots \cap G_ j'$ decrease, contain $E$, lie in $O$, and satisfy $\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j)\to 0$. Since the $G_ j$ decrease, the functions $H_{G_ j}$ increase; put $H=(\sup _ jH_{G_ j})^*$. By theorem 5.10.2, $H$ is plurisubharmonic, takes values in $[-1,0]$, and is the almost-everywhere limit of the $H_{G_ j}$. By proposition 5.47.7, $\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_{G_ j})^ n=\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j)\to 0$, and by theorem 7.6.1 these measures converge weakly to $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n$. Hence $\int \chi \, (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=\lim _ j\int \chi \, (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_{G_ j})^ n\le \lim _ j\operatorname {Cap}_ B(G_ j)=0$ for every $\chi \in C_ c(B)$ with $0\le \chi \le 1$, and therefore $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=0$.

Since every $G_ j$ lies in $O$, the barrier (5.47.3), with a constant $A$ chosen for $O$, bounds every $H_{G_ j}$, and hence $H$, from below by one function tending to zero at $\partial B$; thus $H(z)\to 0$ as $z\to \partial B$. The last assertion of the comparison principle (theorem 7.3.11), applied to $u=H$ and $v=0$, gives $H\ge 0$, and therefore $H=0$. Finally, $E\subset G_ j$ gives $H_{G_ j}\le H_ E$ for all $j$; since $H_ E$ is upper semicontinuous, $0=H\le H_ E\le 0$.

(3)$\Rightarrow $(2). Suppose $H_ E=0$, and choose competitors $v_ j$ for $E$ as in (5.47.4). Fix a point $a\in B$ with $v_ j(a)\to 0$, and choose indices $j_1<j_2<\cdots $ with $v_{j_ l}(a)>-2^{-l}$. Since every term is nonpositive, the partial sums of $V=\sum _ lv_{j_ l}$ form a decreasing sequence of nonpositive plurisubharmonic functions, and $V(a)\ge -1$. By theorem 5.10.1, $V$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$; moreover $V\le 0$, and $V=-\infty $ on $E$ because every term is at most $-1$ there.

(1)$\Rightarrow $(2) in general. Let $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E)=0$, and let $(L_ l)$ be an exhaustion of $B$ by compact sets. Each set $E\cap L_ l$ has outer capacity zero and is relatively compact in $B$, so the two preceding steps give $V_ l\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $V_ l\le 0$ and $E\cap L_ l\subset \{ V_ l=-\infty \} $. The pole sets of the $V_ l$ are Lebesgue null, so there is a point $a\in B$ at which all $V_ l$ are finite. Choose constants $c_ l>0$ with $c_ l|V_ l(a)|\le 2^{-l}$. The partial sums of $\sum _ lc_ lV_ l$ decrease and are at least $-1$ at $a$, so, as in the previous step, $V=\sum _ lc_ lV_ l$ is plurisubharmonic by theorem 5.10.1, and it is nonpositive. Every point of $E$ lies in some $L_ l$, where $c_ lV_ l=-\infty $ while all other terms are nonpositive; hence $V=-\infty $ on $E$.

(2)$\Rightarrow $(3). If $V$ is as in (2), then $\varepsilon V$ is a competitor for $E$ for every $\varepsilon >0$, so $\varepsilon V\le h_ E\le 0$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, we get $h_ E=0$ on the set $\{ V>-\infty \} $. This set has full measure and is therefore dense in $B$, so every neighborhood of a point contains points where $h_ E=0$. Hence $H_ E=h_ E^*\ge 0$, and $H_ E=0$. This completes the proof of the equivalences.

By the proof of (2)$\Rightarrow $(1), every pluripolar subset of $B$ satisfies (1), and hence all three conditions; in particular, it is globally pluripolar in $B$. Now let $X$ be a complex manifold and $E=\bigcup _ mE_ m$, where each $E_ m\subset X$ is pluripolar. Let $x\in X$, and let $B$ be a coordinate ball around $x$, identified with an open ball in $\mathbb C^ n$. Each $E_ m\cap B$ is pluripolar in $B$, so $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E_ m\cap B)=0$ for every $m$, and $\operatorname {Cap}_ B^*(E\cap B)=0$ by countable subadditivity. By (1)$\Rightarrow $(2), the set $E\cap B$ lies in the pole set of a plurisubharmonic function on the connected coordinate neighborhood $B$ of $x$. Hence $E$ is pluripolar.

Finally, let $W\subset X$ be open and let $E\subset W$ be pluripolar in $W$. That $E$ is pluripolar in $X$ is not immediate from definition 5.47.2 at the boundary points of $W$, where a single plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood must have poles at all nearby points of $E$. Let $x\in X$, and let $U$ be a coordinate ball around $x$, identified with an open ball in $\mathbb C^ n$. The open set $W\cap U$ is a countable union of balls $D_ k$ with $\overline{D_ k}\subset W\cap U$. Each set $E\cap D_ k$ is pluripolar in $U$: at points of $\overline{D_ k}\subset W$ we may use, after restriction to small balls in $U$, the functions provided by the pluripolarity of $E$ in $W$, and every point of $U\setminus \overline{D_ k}$ has a neighborhood disjoint from $D_ k$. By the previous paragraph, applied in $U$, the countable union $E\cap U=\bigcup _ k(E\cap D_ k)$ is pluripolar in $U$, and hence, by the first part of the theorem, it lies in the pole set of a plurisubharmonic function on $U$. Thus $E$ is pluripolar in $X$. $\square$
