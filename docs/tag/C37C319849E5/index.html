5.27.20 Sobolev regularity at divisorial polesC37C319849E5

For a potential that is a convex function of $\log |f|$, with $f$ holomorphic, the growth of the convex function at $-\infty $ determines which derivatives are integrable near the divisor of $f$. The gradient and the classical Laplacian have different thresholds. Moreover, the distributional Laplacian keeps a measure on the divisor unless the slope of the convex function tends to zero.

Let $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a sufficiently small neighborhood of $0$, and let $f\not\equiv 0$ be a holomorphic function on $U$ with $f(0)=0$. Write

\[ f=g\prod _{j=1}^ N h_ j^{m_ j},\qquad m_0=\min _ jm_ j, \]

where $g$ is a holomorphic unit and the $h_ j$ are distinct irreducible germs represented on $U$. Fix a convex nondecreasing function $\chi \in C^2(({-\infty },A])$ and shrink $U$ so that $\log |f|<A-1$. Set $v=\chi (\log |f|)$ off $Z=\{ f=0\} $; this function is plurisubharmonic and bounded above, and we extend it across $Z$ by lemma 5.24.7. In this subsection, gradients, Laplacians, Sobolev spaces and the volume form $\mathrm dV$ are the real Euclidean ones. Write $|\partial f|^2=\sum _ j|\partial f/\partial z_ j|^2$, so that $|\nabla |f||=|\partial f|$ off $Z$.

Lemma 5.27.21.C3F45D193092 After shrinking $U$, there is a constant $C>0$ such that

\[ |\partial f|\leq C|f|^{1-1/m_0}\quad \text{on }U\setminus Z. \]

For each compact set $K\subset U$, there are $C_ K,r_ K>0$ with

\[ \int _{K\cap \{ |f|=r\} }|\partial f|\, \mathrm dS\leq C_ Kr \qquad (0<r<r_ K), \]

where $\mathrm dS$ is the Euclidean surface measure on the real hypersurface $\{ |f|=r\} $. In particular, for every nonnegative measurable function $\Psi $,

\[ \int _{K\cap \{ 0<|f|<r_ K\} }\Psi (|f|)|\partial f|^2\, \mathrm dV \leq C_ K\int _0^{r_ K}\Psi (r)r\, \mathrm dr. \]

Proof. Shrink $U$ so that $|h_ j|\leq 1$ and the unit $g$, its inverse and the first derivatives of $g$ and of all $h_ j$ are bounded. For each $j$, $|f|^{1/m_0}\leq C|h_ j|^{m_ j/m_0}\leq C|h_ j|$. Logarithmic differentiation of the factorization therefore gives

\[ \frac{|\partial f|}{|f|} \leq C\left(1+\sum _ j|h_ j|^{-1}\right) \leq C|f|^{-1/m_0}, \]

which proves the first bound.

For small $\zeta \in \mathbb C$, let $\mu _\zeta $ be the Euclidean volume measure of the fiber divisor $\{ f=\zeta \} $, counted with multiplicity. As $\zeta \to 0$, we have $\log |f-\zeta |\to \log |f|$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$: by the local compactness theorem recalled at the beginning of section 5.25, it suffices to identify subsequential limits, and the convergence is locally uniform off $Z$, which identifies every such limit and excludes convergence to $-\infty $. The Poincaré–Lelong formula therefore gives weak convergence of the fiber currents, and hence of their Euclidean trace measures. Pairing with a fixed nonnegative cutoff function equal to one near $K$ shows that $\sup _{|\zeta |<r_ K}\mu _\zeta (K)<\infty $ for some $r_ K>0$.

The critical locus of $f$ has finitely many local components, and $f$ is constant on each of them. After shrinking $U$, every remaining critical component passes through $0$, where $f$ vanishes. Thus the small nonzero fibers are smooth. On $\{ |f|=r\} $, the coarea formula for the map $f$ to the circle of radius $r$ gives

\[ \int _{K\cap \{ |f|=r\} }|\partial f|\, \mathrm dS =\int _{|\zeta |=r}\mu _\zeta (K)\, \mathrm dS(\zeta ) \leq C_ Kr. \]

The coarea formula for the function $|f|$, together with $|\nabla |f||=|\partial f|$, proves the last inequality. The same formulas hold in dimension one, where the fiber volume is the counting measure. $\square$

Proposition 5.27.22.4BF5A61AFC51 For each $2\leq p<\infty $,

\[ v\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \int _{-\infty }^ A|\chi '(s)|^ p \mathrm e^{(2-p)s/m_0}\, \mathrm ds<\infty . \]

In this case, the weak gradient of $v$ is the zero extension of $\chi '(\log |f|)\nabla \log |f|$ from $U\setminus Z$.

Proof. At a smooth point of a component of multiplicity $m_0$, outside the other components, multiplying the defining function of the component by a holomorphic $m_0$-th root of the remaining unit gives a normal coordinate $w_1$ with $f=w_1^{m_0}$. Coordinate changes with bounded derivatives and Jacobians preserve local first-order Sobolev integrability. With $r=|w_1|$ and $s=m_0\log r$, polar integration of the classical gradient gives, up to fixed positive factors,

\[ \int _0^{r_0}|\chi '(m_0\log r)|^ p r^{1-p}\, \mathrm dr =\frac1{m_0}\int _{-\infty }^{m_0\log r_0} |\chi '(s)|^ p\mathrm e^{(2-p)s/m_0}\, \mathrm ds. \]

Such smooth points occur in every neighborhood of $0$. This proves necessity, since changing the finite upper endpoint does not affect convergence.

For sufficiency, put

\[ s_\varepsilon =\tfrac 12\log (|f|^2+\varepsilon ^2), \qquad v_\varepsilon =\chi (s_\varepsilon ). \]

For small $\varepsilon >0$, these are $C^2$ plurisubharmonic functions decreasing to $v$. By convexity, $|\chi (s)|\leq C(1+|s|)$ for $s\leq A$, so the local exponential integrability of $\log |f|$, recalled in [SGPT], gives convergence in every local $L^ p$ space with $p<\infty $. By the chain rule,

\[ |\nabla v_\varepsilon | =\chi '(s_\varepsilon ) \frac{|f|}{|f|^2+\varepsilon ^2}|\partial f|. \]

Apply lemma 5.27.21 with the coarea formula, keeping two powers of $|\partial f|$ for the level estimate and bounding the other $p-2$ powers by the first inequality of the lemma. The local integral of $|\nabla v_\varepsilon |^ p$ over $K\cap \{ 0<|f|<r_ K\} $ is then bounded by

\[ C\int _0^{r_ K} |\chi '(\tfrac 12\log (r^2+\varepsilon ^2))|^ p \frac{r^{p+(p-2)(1-1/m_0)+1}}{(r^2+\varepsilon ^2)^ p} \, \mathrm dr. \]

With $s=\tfrac 12\log (r^2+\varepsilon ^2)$, we have $\mathrm ds=r\, \mathrm dr/(r^2+\varepsilon ^2)$ and $r\leq (r^2+\varepsilon ^2)^{1/2}$, so this is at most

\[ C\int _{-\infty }^ A|\chi '(s)|^ p \mathrm e^{(2-p)s/m_0}\, \mathrm ds. \]

The integral over the part where $|f|\geq r_ K$ is uniformly bounded as well. Weak compactness in Sobolev spaces and the local $L^ p$ convergence therefore show that $v\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Its weak gradient agrees with the classical one off $Z$, and $Z$ has Lebesgue measure zero, which proves the final assertion. $\square$

Proposition 5.27.23.5B874925BCA1 Let $G$ be the zero extension of $\chi ''(\log |f|)|\nabla \log |f||^2$ from $U\setminus Z$, and put $\ell =\lim _{s\to -\infty }\chi '(s)$. For each $1\leq p<\infty $,

\[ G\in L^ p_{\mathrm{loc}}(U) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \int _{-\infty }^ A|\chi ''(s)|^ p \mathrm e^{2(1-p)s/m_0}\, \mathrm ds<\infty . \]

In the sense of distributions,

\[ \Delta v=G+\ell \, \Delta \log |f|, \]

where $\Delta \log |f|$ is a positive measure carried by the divisor of $f$. In particular, $\Delta v$ is represented by a locally $L^ p$ function exactly when $\ell =0$ and the integral above is finite.

Proof. The limit $\ell \geq 0$ exists because $\chi '$ is nonnegative and nondecreasing. Near a smooth point of a component of multiplicity $m_0$, outside the other components, the coordinate reduction to $f=w_1^{m_0}$ makes the positive complex Hessian of $v$ off $Z$ a single factor in the normal direction. Its Euclidean traces in the two coordinate systems are uniformly comparable, so polar integration gives necessity. In general, $G=\chi ''(\log |f|)|\partial f|^2/|f|^2$ off $Z$. The coarea formula and lemma 5.27.21, now bounding the remaining $2p-2$ powers of $|\partial f|$ by the first inequality of the lemma, give

\[ \int _{K\cap \{ 0<|f|<r_ K\} }G^ p\, \mathrm dV \leq C\int _0^{r_ K}|\chi ''(\log r)|^ p r^{-1-2(p-1)/m_0}\, \mathrm dr. \]

The substitution $s=\log r$ proves sufficiency. For $p=1$ the integral is always finite, since $\int _{-\infty }^ A\chi ''=\chi '(A)-\ell $.

For the distributional identity, use $s_\varepsilon $ and $v_\varepsilon $ from the preceding proof. Direct differentiation gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta v_\varepsilon & =\chi ''(s_\varepsilon ) \frac{|f|^2|\partial f|^2}{(|f|^2+\varepsilon ^2)^2} +\chi '(s_\varepsilon )\Delta s_\varepsilon ,\\ \Delta s_\varepsilon & =\frac{2\varepsilon ^2|\partial f|^2}{(|f|^2+\varepsilon ^2)^2}. \end{aligned} \]

Off $Z$, the first term tends locally uniformly to $G$. By the level estimate of lemma 5.27.21 and the same substitution, its integral over $K\cap \{ |f|<r_0\} $ is at most

\[ C\int _{-\infty }^{\frac12\log (r_0^2+\varepsilon ^2)} \chi ''(s)\, \mathrm ds. \]

This bound tends to zero if we first let $\varepsilon \to 0$ and then $r_0\to 0$, so the first term converges to $G$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

The positive measures $\Delta s_\varepsilon $ converge weakly to $\Delta \log |f|$, with locally bounded masses, and the Poincaré–Lelong formula shows that this limit is a measure supported on $Z$. On $\{ |f|<r_0\} $,

\[ 0\leq \chi '(s_\varepsilon )-\ell \leq \chi '(\tfrac 12\log (r_0^2+\varepsilon ^2))-\ell , \]

while $\Delta s_\varepsilon \to 0$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\{ |f|\geq r_0\} $. Taking the same successive limits shows that $(\chi '(s_\varepsilon )-\ell )\Delta s_\varepsilon \to 0$ weakly. Since $v_\varepsilon \to v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, passing to the limit in the formula for $\Delta v_\varepsilon $ gives the identity. If $\ell >0$, the measure $\ell \, \Delta \log |f|$ is nonzero and singular with respect to Lebesgue measure; uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition therefore proves the final criterion. $\square$

Example 5.27.24.4B607387D3F1 Shrink $U$ so that $\log |f|<-2$, and let $0<a<1$. Then the plurisubharmonic function

\[ v_ a=-(-\log |f|)^ a \]

has $\Delta v_ a\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, while

\[ v_ a\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad a<\tfrac 12. \]

It belongs to no $W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ with $p>2$ and to no $W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

Proof. Here $\chi (s)=-(-s)^ a$ is of class $C^2$ on $(-\infty ,-1]$, so the preceding propositions apply with $A=-1$. Moreover, $\chi '(s)=a(-s)^{a-1}$ and $\chi ''(s)=a(1-a)(-s)^{a-2}$, so $\ell =0$. The two propositions give the assertion about the Laplacian and the first-order thresholds. At $a=1/2$, the first-order integral diverges logarithmically; for $p>2$, its exponential factor forces divergence.

For second derivatives, take a smooth point of a component of multiplicity $m$, outside the other components, with a normal coordinate $w_1$ such that $f=w_1^ m$. Write $F(r)=\chi (m\log r)$ with $r=|w_1|$. The real second derivative of $v_ a$ in the unit direction tangent to the circles $|w_1|=r$ equals $F'(r)/r=m\chi '(m\log r)/r^2$. Thus the integral of its absolute value over the normal disk is bounded below, up to a fixed positive factor, by

\[ \int _0^{r_0}\chi '(m\log r)\frac{\mathrm dr}{r} =\frac1m\int _{-\infty }^{m\log r_0}\chi '(s)\, \mathrm ds =+\infty . \]

Hence the classical Hessian of $v_ a$ off the divisor is not locally integrable, whereas local integrability is necessary for $W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Since smooth coordinate changes preserve this Sobolev class, the argument is complete. $\square$
