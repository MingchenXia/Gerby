Chapter 14 · Energy, geodesics, rays, and test curves · Tag 548B64628B04

Testcurve to envelope ray

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 18.2.1

Theorem 18.2.1. The forward transform of an envelope test curve with cutoff $c$ is an envelope ray with the same initial potential and slope $c$. Moreover, $U_ t-ct$ decreases as $t$ increases.

Proof. By theorem 1.20.6, $U_ t$ is a subgeodesic. The inequalities

\[ \Gamma _\tau +t\tau \leq U_ t\leq \varphi +ct \]

give $\sup _ XU_ t\geq t\tau $ for each $\tau <c$, since $\sup _ X\Gamma _\tau =0$, and $\sup _ XU_ t\leq ct$. Letting $\tau \uparrow c$ proves $\sup _ XU_ t=ct$. The formula

\[ U_ t-ct=\sup _{\tau <c}\bigl (\Gamma _\tau -t(c-\tau )\bigr ) \]

shows that $U_ t-ct$ is decreasing in $t$.

Since $\Gamma _\tau \preceq U_ t\preceq \varphi $, monotonicity of the singularity envelope gives

\[ \Gamma _\tau =P_\theta [\Gamma _\tau ]\leq P_\theta [U_ t] \leq P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi . \]

Taking the regularized supremum in $\tau $ proves $P_\theta [U_ t]=\varphi $.

The local envelope theorem in section 1.19 gives $\sup _{\tau <c}\Gamma _\tau =\varphi $ almost everywhere, and at those points the bounds $\Gamma _\tau +t\tau \leq U_ t\leq \varphi +ct$ imply $U_ t\to \varphi $ as $t\downarrow 0$. For any fixed $\tau _0<c$ and $0<t\leq 1$, they also give

\[ \Gamma _{\tau _0}-|\tau _0|\leq U_ t\leq \varphi +|c|. \]

Both bounds are integrable, so dominated convergence gives $U_ t\to \varphi $ in $L^1$ as $t\downarrow 0$.

It remains to prove Perron maximality. Fix $0\leq a<b$ and replace $U_ t$ on $(a,b)$ by the Perron segment from $U_ a$ to $U_ b$, with $U_0=\varphi $ when $a=0$; it exists by proposition 17.2.1, because the restriction of $U_ t$ itself runs between these endpoints. The joint upper limits of the Perron segment at $a$ and $b$ allow gluing by lemma 1.3.2, proposition 17.1.2. The resulting subgeodesic $v_ t$ dominates $U_ t$ and equals it outside $(a,b)$, where therefore $v_ t\leq ct$. On $(a,b)$, the chord bound (17.1) together with $U_ a\leq ca$ and $U_ b\leq cb$ gives $v_ t\leq ct$ as well.

For $\tau <c$, put $\chi _\tau =\inf _{t>0}(v_ t-t\tau )$. Since $v_ t-t\tau \geq U_ t-t\tau \geq \Gamma _\tau $, proposition 1.20.3 gives $\chi _\tau \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We claim that

18.1
\begin{equation} \label{eq:finite-replacement-dual-bound} \Gamma _\tau \leq \chi _\tau \leq \Gamma _\tau +(b-a)(c-\tau ). \end{equation}

The first inequality was just noted. For the second, let $s>0$. If $s\in [a,b]$, then $v_ b=U_ b$, and since $U_ t-ct$ is decreasing,

\[ \chi _\tau \leq U_ b-b\tau \leq U_ s-s\tau +(b-s)(c-\tau ). \]

If $s\notin [a,b]$, then $\chi _\tau \leq v_ s-s\tau =U_ s-s\tau $. In both cases $\chi _\tau \leq U_ s-s\tau +(b-a)(c-\tau )$. Taking the infimum over $s>0$ and using lemma 1.20.5 proves (18.1).

Moreover, $v_ t\leq ct$ gives $\chi _\tau \leq t(c-\tau )$ for every $t>0$, hence $\chi _\tau \leq 0$. By (18.1), $\chi _\tau $ has the ordinary type of $\Gamma _\tau $, so it is a competitor for $P_\theta [\Gamma _\tau ]=\Gamma _\tau $. Thus $\chi _\tau =\Gamma _\tau $. Recovering $v_ t$ from its inverse transforms by lemma 1.20.4 gives $v_ t=U_ t$, which proves Perron maximality on every finite interval. $\square$
