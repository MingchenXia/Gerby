From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.14 Varying the probability measure

From now on $X$ is again an arbitrary connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. Changing the smooth positive probability measure $\mu $ multiplies each denominator $D_\mu (\gamma )$ by a factor bounded above and below independently of $\gamma $. The value of the volume may therefore change, but its vanishing and positivity do not. Moreover, every smooth strictly positive potential solves the normalized equation for a suitable measure. Consequently, on $\mathscr P$ the volume is independent of the measure exactly when the total Monge–Ampère mass is independent of the potential.

Proposition 26.14.1. For smooth positive probability measures $\mu ,\nu $, choose constants $m,M>0$ such that $m\mu \leq \nu \leq M\mu $. Then for every pseudoeffective $\theta $,

\[ M^{-1/n}\lambda _\mu (\theta ) \leq \lambda _\nu (\theta ) \leq m^{-1/n}\lambda _\mu (\theta ). \]

In particular, whether $\lambda _\mu (\theta )$ vanishes does not depend on the smooth positive probability measure $\mu $.

Proof. Since $D_\nu (\gamma )=\int _ X(\nu /\mu )^{1/n}(\mu /\gamma ^ n)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n$, the inequalities $m\mu \leq \nu \leq M\mu $ give

\[ m^{1/n}D_\mu (\gamma )\leq D_\nu (\gamma )\leq M^{1/n}D_\mu (\gamma ) \]

for every admissible metric $\gamma $. The numerators of the quotients are nonnegative and do not involve the measure. Dividing and taking infima proves the assertion. $\square$

Proposition 26.14.2. Let $\omega $ be a Hermitian form on $X$. The value $\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )$ is the same for all smooth positive probability measures $\mu $ if and only if

\[ \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n \]

is independent of the smooth potential $v$ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v>0$.

Proof. For a smooth potential $v$ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v>0$, put

\[ M_ v=\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n, \qquad \mu _ v=\frac{(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n}{M_ v}. \]

Then $\mu _ v$ is a smooth positive probability measure, and $v$ solves the normalized equation for $\mu _ v$ with scalar $M_ v$. Uniqueness of the scalar and theorem 26.1.3 give $\mathcal V_{\mu _ v}(\omega )=M_ v$. Hence, if the volume does not depend on the measure, neither does the mass. Conversely, suppose that all these masses equal one constant $M$. For an arbitrary smooth positive probability measure $\mu $, integrating the normalized equation gives $c_\omega (\mu )=M$, so $\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )=M$ for every $\mu $. $\square$

For instance, the nonclosed torus metric of example 2.3.1 has volumes that depend on the measure. If $n=2$ and $\omega $ is Gauduchon, Stokes' theorem shows that the smooth masses are all equal to $\int _ X\omega ^2$, so $\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )=\int _ X\omega ^2$ for every $\mu $. On the Hopf threefold of chapter 8, each $\mathcal V_\mu (\omega )$ is finite and positive, although the upper mass $v_+(\omega )$ is infinite.
