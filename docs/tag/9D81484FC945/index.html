Gaud hessian factor bounds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 26.8.8

Proposition 26.8.8. Fix $0<\delta \leq \Lambda $. For every smooth real $v$ with

\[ \delta \omega \leq \Omega _ v:=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\leq \Lambda \omega , \]

let $\rho _ v$ be its Gauduchon factor normalized by

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ v\Omega _ v^{n-1})=0, \qquad \int _ X\rho _ v\, \mathrm{d}V=1. \]

There is $C_ H\geq 1$, depending only on $X,\omega ,\mathrm{d}V,\delta ,\Lambda $, such that $C_ H^{-1}\leq \rho _ v\leq C_ H$ on $X$ for every such $v$.

Proof. Existence and uniqueness of $\rho _ v$ follow from theorem 4.1.2. Fix a real reference metric on $X$, and put $Q_ v=\Omega _ v^{n-1}$ and $J_ v=\Omega _ v^ n/\mathrm{d}V$. Since $d\Omega _ v=d\omega $,

26.50
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaud-hessian-cofactor-divergence} dQ_ v=(n-1)d\omega \wedge \Omega _ v^{n-2}. \end{equation}

Both $Q_ v$ and $dQ_ v$ have uniformly bounded coefficients in fixed charts.

Define a real tensor $A_ v$ and vector field $b_ v$ by

\[ \langle A_ vd\phi ,d\psi \rangle \, \mathrm{d}V =n\, d\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge Q_ v, \qquad (b_ v\cdot d\phi )\, \mathrm{d}V =-n\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge dQ_ v. \]

Only the $(1,1)$ part of $d\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi $ contributes to the first identity; it is

\[ \frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi } (\partial \psi \wedge \bar\partial \phi +\partial \phi \wedge \bar\partial \psi ). \]

Thus $A_ v$ is symmetric and uniformly positive definite, with a uniform upper bound. The second identity and (26.50) give a uniform bound for $b_ v$.

For $L_{\Omega _ v}\phi =n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi \wedge Q_ v/\Omega _ v^ n$, Stokes' theorem gives

\begin{align*} \int _ X\psi J_ vL_{\Omega _ v}\phi \, \mathrm{d}V & =-n\int _ Xd\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge Q_ v +n\int _ X\psi \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\phi \wedge dQ_ v\\ & =-\int _ X\langle A_ vd\phi ,d\psi \rangle \, \mathrm{d}V -\int _ X\psi b_ v\cdot d\phi \, \mathrm{d}V. \end{align*}

In particular, the adjoint equation for $\rho _ v$ is

\[ \operatorname {div}_{\mathrm{d}V}(A_ vd\rho _ v+b_ v\rho _ v)=0. \]

In each coordinate chart, multiplying the coefficients by the fixed smooth volume density converts this to Euclidean divergence form while preserving uniform ellipticity and a uniform bound for the vector coefficient. Lemma 26.8.7 applies. A fixed finite cover by inner cubes has a connected overlap graph, since $X$ is connected. Chaining its local estimates gives $\sup _ X\rho _ v\leq C_ H\inf _ X\rho _ v$. The normalization and $\mathrm{d}V(X)=1$ imply the claimed two-sided bound. $\square$
