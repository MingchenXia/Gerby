The bounded cubic identity

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.16.1 The bounded cubic identity

When the square of a Hermitian metric $g$ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed, the mass of a bounded $g$-plurisubharmonic function differs from $\int _ Xg^3$ by a term that is linear in $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$. Its coefficient is a positive current playing the role of $\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u$.

Lemma 26.16.1. Let $g$ be a Hermitian form with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g^2)=0$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,g)$ be bounded. The current

\[ E_ u=\tfrac 12\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u^2)-u\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u \]

is positive, and

26.93
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bounded-cubic-energy} \int _ X(g+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3 =\int _ Xg^3-3\int _ XE_ u\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g. \end{equation}

If $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\geq 0$, then $v_-(g)=\int _ Xg^3$.

Proof. Locally write $u=p-a$, with $p$ bounded plurisubharmonic and $a$ smooth. Expanding in $p$ and $a$ and using multilinearity, we obtain closed currents $C_ r=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ r$, not necessarily positive, with $C_0=1$. The recursive definition of bounded products, expanded in the same way, gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(uC_ r)=C_{r+1},\qquad r=0,1,2. \]

By lemma 1.6.2 and invariance under smooth corrections, the weighted product $u\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ does not depend on the local decomposition. On a smaller ball choose smooth bounded plurisubharmonic $p_ j$ decreasing to $p$, and put $u_ j=p_ j-a$. The same lemma gives $u_ j\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\to u\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$, while boundedness gives $u_ j^2\to u^2$ in $L^1$. Hence

\[ \mathrm du_ j\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u_ j =\tfrac 12\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ j^2)-u_ j\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\longrightarrow E_ u. \]

The smooth currents on the left are positive, so their weak limit $E_ u$ is positive and has order zero.

Now expand the cube. Repeated use of distributional Stokes gives

\[ \int _ XC_3=0,\qquad \int _ Xg^2\wedge C_1=\int _ Xu\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g^2)=0, \qquad \int _ Xg\wedge C_2=\int _ XuC_1\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g. \]

Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ is closed, $\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u^2)\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=0$, so the last integral equals $-\int _ XE_ u\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$. This proves (26.93). If $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\geq 0$, the correction term is nonnegative, and $u=0$ attains the lower bound $\int _ Xg^3$. $\square$

Lemma 26.16.2. Let $g,h$ be Hermitian forms with $v_-(g)=V>0$ and $h\geq c g$ for some $c>0$. Then

26.94
\begin{equation} \label{eq:determinant-background-transport} \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_ h}\| k_ T\| _{L^{3/2}(\mathrm dV)} \leq c^{-1}V^{-1/3},\qquad v_-(h)\geq c^3V. \end{equation}

Proof. We first check that the determinant measure in (26.39) does not depend on the Hermitian form used to express it. Let $T$ be a positive $(2,2)$-current, and let $\sigma _ h=h\wedge T$ and $P=P_ h$ be its trace measure and coefficient matrix relative to $h$, as in section 26.6. In an $h$-unitary frame let $G$ be the matrix of $g$, and set

\[ a=\tfrac 13\operatorname {tr}(GP),\qquad \sigma _ g=a\sigma _ h,\qquad P_ g=a^{-1}G^{1/2}PG^{1/2}. \]

Comparing the defining trace pairings shows that $\sigma _ g=g\wedge T$ and $P_ g$ are the trace measure and coefficient matrix of $T$ relative to $g$, with $\operatorname {tr}P_ g=3$. Since $G>0$ and $\operatorname {tr}P=3$, one has $a>0$ even where $P$ is singular. Moreover $\det P_ g=a^{-3}\det G\det P$ and $g^3=\det G\, h^3$, so the factors $a$ and $\det G$ cancel in $\Gamma _0(T)$. The calculation applies to the matrix density on the whole trace measure, including its singular part.

For $T\in \mathscr G_ h$ put $m=\int _ Xg\wedge T$. Then $0<m\leq c^{-1}$ and $U=T/m\in \mathscr G_ g$. By homogeneity, $k_ T=m k_ U$. Applying theorem 26.8.2 to $g$ gives the norm bound, and applying it again to $h$ gives the lower-mass bound. The supremum here is taken over all of $\mathscr G_ h$, including singular and degenerate currents. $\square$
