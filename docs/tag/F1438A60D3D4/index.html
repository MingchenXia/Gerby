The comparison theorem

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 13.4.3 The comparison theorem

Let $X$ be a connected projective manifold of dimension $n$. Let $L$ be a pseudo-effective $\mathbb {Q}$-line bundle on $X$ and $h$ be a Hermitian metric on $L$ with $\theta =c_1(L,h)$.

Thanks to corollary 13.4.20, we already have a map

13.46
\begin{equation} \label{eq:analymap} \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}}),\quad \Gamma \mapsto \Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}. \end{equation}

We observe that for $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

13.47
\begin{equation} \label{eq:proj_NA_an_invariance} \left( P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma \right]_{\mathcal{I}} \right)^{\mathrm{An}}=\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}. \end{equation}

Also observe that

13.48
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Gammamax} \Gamma _{\max }=\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}(v_{\mathrm{triv}}), \end{equation}

Lemma 13.4.21. The map (13.46) is order preserving. Moreover, suppose that $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfies that $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}\leq \Gamma '^{\mathrm{An}}$, then $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$.

In particular, the map (13.46) is injective.

Proof. The map (13.46) is order preserving by definition. Let us take $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ with $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}\leq \Gamma '^{\mathrm{An}}$. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$.

Let $v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}$ and $t \in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}$. Then, using (13.40) and the homogeneity of divisorial valuations, we notice that

13.49
\begin{equation} \label{eq:legendre_NA} t \Gamma ^\mathrm{An}\left(t^{-1} v \right) = \sup _{\tau \in \mathbb {R}}\left( \left(\Gamma _{\tau }^{\theta +\omega }\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+ t \tau \right). \end{equation}

A similar equality holds for $\Gamma '$. Therefore, by corollary A.2.4, we have

\[ \left(\Gamma _{\tau }^{\theta +\omega }\right)^{\mathrm{An}}\leq \left(\Gamma '{}_{\tau }^{\theta +\omega }\right)^{\mathrm{An}} \]

for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. It follows that

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }^{\theta +\omega }\preceq _{\mathcal{I}} \Gamma '{}_{\tau }^{\theta +\omega } \]

by proposition 6.1.5. Since both sides are $\mathcal{I}$-model, proposition 6.1.12 gives

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }^{\theta +\omega }\leq \Gamma '{}_{\tau }^{\theta +\omega } \]

for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. Hence $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$ by the definition of the order. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.22. Let $\phi \in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. Then there is a piecewise linear curve $A$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\phi =A^{\mathrm{An}}$. In particular, $\phi $ is in the image of (13.46).

Note that from the proof below, the test curve $\Gamma $ corresponding to $\phi $ satisfies the following: For any $\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }$, $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is elementary. See definition 6.1.15 for the definition of elementary metrics.

Proof. Let us write

13.50
\begin{equation} \label{eq:phiintominversemax} \phi =\frac{1}{m}\max _{i=1,\ldots ,M} \left(\log |s_ i|+\lambda _ i\right), \end{equation}

where $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, $s_{1},\ldots , s_ M$ are a finite number of sections of $L^ m$ and $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ M\in \mathbb {Q}$.

Write $I_{\tau }$ for the set of $i$ such that $m^{-1}\lambda _ i=\tau $. We denote the finitely many $\tau $ so that $I_{\tau }$ is non-empty as $\tau _0> \cdots > \tau _ N$. For each $i=0,\ldots ,N$, we write

\[ A_{\tau _ i}=\frac{1}{m}\max _{j\in I_{\tau _ i}} \log |s_ j|^2_{h^ m}. \]

We define $A$ as the piecewise linear curve associated with the $A_{\tau _ i}$'s and the $\tau _ i$'s. For every $v\in X^{\mathrm{An}}$,

\[ A^{\mathrm{An}}(v)=\max _{i=0,\ldots ,N}\left(A_{\tau _ i}^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau _ i\right) =\frac{1}{m}\max _{j=1,\ldots ,M}\left(\log |s_ j|(v)+\lambda _ j\right)=\phi (v), \]

where we used the convention $\log |s_ j|(v)=-v(s_ j)$.

The final assertion follows from 13.4.13 and theorem 13.4.3. $\square$

Proposition 13.4.23. Let $(\Gamma _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Assume that (13.18) is satisfied. Then

\[ \left( \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma _{i} \right)^{\mathrm{An}}=\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma _{i}^{\mathrm{An}}. \]

Proof. Take a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. We need to show that

\[ \left( \inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma _ i]_{\mathcal{I}} \right)^{\mathrm{An}}=\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma _{i}^{\mathrm{An}}. \]

Therefore, after replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\omega $, we may assume that $\Gamma _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ for all $i\in I$ and $\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Fix $v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}$. By theorem 13.4.3, it suffices to prove that

13.51
\begin{equation} \label{eq:anpreinf1} \sup _{\tau \in \mathbb {R}} \left( \left(\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _{i,\tau }\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right)= \adjustlimits \inf _{i\in I}\sup _{\tau \in \mathbb {R}} \left( \Gamma _{i,\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right). \end{equation}

Write $\Gamma =\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _ i$. We first compare the two sides inside the Legendre-type supremum. If $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, then by the definition of the infimum test curve,

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _{i,\tau }. \]

The net $(\Gamma _{i,\tau })_ i$ decreases to $\Gamma _{\tau }$. By 9.4.27, $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is stable under $P_{\theta }[\bullet ]$. Hence proposition 3.1.26 gives convergence of the masses, and corollary 6.2.11 yields

\[ \Gamma _{i,\tau }\xrightarrow {d_ S}\Gamma _{\tau }. \]

Hence theorem 6.2.29 gives

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _{i,\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v),\quad \tau <\Gamma _{\max }. \]

If $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$, then $\Gamma _{\tau }\equiv -\infty $, and also $\inf _ i\Gamma _{i,\tau }\equiv -\infty $, since $\Gamma _{\max }=\inf _ i(\Gamma _ i)_{\max }$. Thus the same equality holds for $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$ as well, with value $-\infty $. The possible endpoint $\tau =\Gamma _{\max }$ does not affect the supremum: for any test curve $\Lambda $ and any $v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}$,

\[ \sup _{\tau \leq \Lambda _{\max }}\left(\Lambda _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right) =\sup _{\tau < \Lambda _{\max }}\left(\Lambda _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right), \]

because $\Lambda _{\Lambda _{\max }}\leq \Lambda _{\tau }$ for $\tau <\Lambda _{\max }$, hence

\[ \Lambda _{\Lambda _{\max }}^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\Lambda _{\max } \leq \Lambda _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\Lambda _{\max } \]

and we let $\tau \nearrow \Lambda _{\max }$.

Consequently, in (13.51) we may replace $\left(\inf _ i\Gamma _{i,\tau }\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(v)$ by $\inf _ i\Gamma _{i,\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)$ inside the supremum. The equality then follows from proposition A.2.10 applied to the closed convex functions $-\Gamma _{i,\bullet }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)$. $\square$

Theorem 13.4.24. The map (13.46) is an order preserving bijection.

Proof. The map (13.46) is an order preserving injection by 13.4.21. It remains to prove that it is surjective. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. We want to construct $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ with $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi $.

Let $H$ be an ample line bundle and $(\epsilon _ i)_ i$ be a decreasing sequence of rational numbers with limit $0$, $\phi _ i\in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+\epsilon _ i H)^{\mathrm{An}})$ such that

\[ \phi =\inf _{i>0}\phi _ i. \]

The existence of these data is guaranteed by theorem 13.4.6. Fix a Kähler form $\omega \in c_1(H)$.

Thanks to 13.4.22, we can find $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\epsilon _ i \omega )$ with $(\Gamma ^ i)^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi _ i$. It follows from 13.4.21 that

\[ \Gamma ^ i\geq P_{\theta +\epsilon _ i\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i+1} \right]_{\mathcal{I}} \geq \Gamma ^{i+1}. \]

Therefore, for any $\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)$, after discarding finitely many $i$ so that $\omega '\geq \epsilon _ i\omega $, the sequence $(P_{\theta +\omega '}\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}})_ i$ is decreasing. This does not change its infimum. We let

\[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}=\inf _{i\gg 1}P_{\theta +\omega '}\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega '). \]

Note that the infimum is defined thanks to (13.48). It follows from proposition 13.4.23 that

\[ \left(\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '} \right)^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi . \]

From this, we conclude that for $\omega ',\omega ''\in \textup{Käh}(X)$ with $\omega '\leq \omega ''$, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\omega ''}\left[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega ''}. \]

Indeed, both sides have analytification $\phi $, by (13.47) and (13.46). The injectivity part of 13.4.21 gives our assertion.

It follows that $(\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '})_{\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)}$ defines an element $\Gamma $ in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi $. $\square$

Theorem 13.4.25. Under the bijection theorem 13.4.24, the operations on the space $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ defined in section 13.2 all correspond to the corresponding operations on $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ in Boucksom–Jonsson's theory.

The meaning is immediate for all operations except for the trace operator, and the proofs are elementary, as we have seen in proposition 13.4.23 for the infimum operator. We shall only restate and prove the case of trace operators; the remaining arguments are recorded in [Xia23Operations].

Theorem 13.4.26. Let $Y\subseteq X$ be an irreducible analytic subset. Consider an element $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ with well-defined restriction to $Y$. Then

13.52
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Tracena} \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\mathrm{An}}|_{Y^{\operatorname{div}}}=\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}|_{Y^{\operatorname{div}}}. \end{equation}
Observe that there is a canonical identification $Y^{\operatorname{div}}=\widetilde{Y}^{\operatorname{div}}$. Recall that a generalized Fubini–Study metric is defined in definition 1.8.11.

Proof. We first reduce to the positive mass case. Choose a Kähler form $\omega _0$ on $X$ and set $\widehat{\Gamma }=P_{\theta +\omega _0}[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$. Replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\omega _0$ and $\Gamma $ by $\widehat{\Gamma }$ does not change $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}$, by (13.47); it also only replaces $\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )$ by the corresponding $\mathcal{I}$-projection on $\widetilde{Y}$, whose analytification is unchanged. Hence we may assume that $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Let $\phi =\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. By 13.4.27, $\phi (v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}})\neq -\infty $.

Take an ample line bundle $S$ on $X$ and a Kähler form $\omega $ in $c_1(S)$. Write $\phi $ as the decreasing limit of a sequence $(\phi ^{i})_ i$ of elements in $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+i^{-1}S)^{\mathrm{An}})$ as in theorem 13.4.6.

Take $\Gamma ^{i}\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +i^{-1}\omega )$ such that $\Gamma ^{i,\mathrm{An}}=\phi ^{i}$. Note that by 13.1.8, $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +i^{-1}\omega )_{>0}$.

Write $\widehat{\Gamma }^ i\coloneqq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}}$ and $\widehat{\Gamma }\coloneqq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$. Applying proposition 13.4.23 to the decreasing sequence $(\widehat{\Gamma }^ i)_ i$ and then using the injectivity in 13.4.21, we get $\inf _ i\widehat{\Gamma }^ i=\widehat{\Gamma }$. Hence, by the componentwise definition of this infimum, for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \inf _{i\in \mathbb {N}}\widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau }=\widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau }. \]

Now proposition 3.1.26 and corollary 6.2.11 give

\[ \widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau }\xrightarrow {d_ S} \widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau }. \]

for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$.

By 13.4.27 again, each $\widehat{\Gamma }^{i}$ has non-trivial restriction to $Y$. By proposition 8.2.2, for any Kähler form $\omega '$ on $\widetilde{Y}$ satisfying $\omega '\geq \omega |_{\widetilde{Y}}$ we have

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau } \right)\xrightarrow {d_ S} \operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau }\right) \]

for any $\tau <(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma ))_{\max }$. Thanks to theorem 6.2.29, for every $c\operatorname{ord}_ F\in Y^{\operatorname{div}}$ and every such $\tau $,

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau } \right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=\inf _{i\geq 1}\operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau } \right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \]

Applying proposition A.2.10 to the Legendre transforms in $\tau $, and using again that $\mathcal{I}$-projection does not change analytification, we get

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=\inf _{i\geq 1}\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma ^ i)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \]

Since also $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\inf _ i \Gamma ^{i,\mathrm{An}}$, it suffices to prove (13.52) with $\Gamma ^ i$ in place of $\Gamma $.

In other words, we have reduced to the case where $\phi \in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ and $L$ is big.

Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ with $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi $. By 13.4.22, we can find a concave curve $(\Gamma '_{\tau })_{\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }}$ such that $\Gamma '_{\tau }$ is a generalized Fubini–Study metric for each $\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }$ and such that

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=P_{\theta }[\Gamma '_{\tau }]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\Gamma '_{\tau }]. \]

Here the second equality follows from example 7.1.6, since generalized Fubini–Study metrics have analytic singularities. With the convention that $\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(-\infty )=-\infty $ and $(-\infty )^{\mathrm{An}}\equiv -\infty $, it follows that for any $c\operatorname{ord}_ F\in \widetilde{Y}^{\operatorname{div}}$,

\[ \begin{aligned} \phi |_{Y^{\mathrm{An}}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\Gamma '_{\tau }{}^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\left(\Gamma '_{\tau }|_{\widetilde{Y}}\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma '_{\tau })^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma _{\tau })^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \end{aligned} \]

The third equality follows from example 8.1.10, since $\Gamma '_{\tau }$ has analytic singularities. The fourth equality follows from $\Gamma _{\tau }=P_{\theta }[\Gamma '_{\tau }]$, proposition 8.2.1 and corollary 6.1.9. It remains to justify the second line. Namely, we want to show that for any generalized Fubini–Study metric $\varphi $ whose restriction to $\widetilde{Y}$ is not identically $-\infty $, we have

13.58
\begin{equation} \label{eq:varphiAncordF} \varphi ^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=\left(\varphi |_{\widetilde{Y}}\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \end{equation}

We can immediately reduce to the case where $\varphi $ is a Fubini–Study metric, and then to the case

\[ \varphi =\log |s|_{h_0}^2, \]

where $s$ is a holomorphic section of $L$, not vanishing identically on $Y$, in which case (13.58) is obvious. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.27. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $Y\subseteq X$ be an irreducible analytic subset. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}(v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}})\neq -\infty $;

  2. $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}|_{Y^{\mathrm{An}}}\not\equiv -\infty $;

  3. $\Gamma $ has a well-defined restriction to $Y$.

Here $v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}}$ denotes the trivial valuation of $\mathbb {C}(Y)$.

Proof. The equivalence between 1 and 2 is a simple consequence of the maximum principle [Lemma 1.4(i), BJ22].

To see the equivalence between 1 and 3, it suffices to observe that for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$,

\[ \varphi ^{\mathrm{An}}(v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}})= \begin{cases} -\infty ,& \textup{if }\nu (\varphi ,Y)>0;\\ 0,& \textup{if }\nu (\varphi ,Y)=0. \end{cases} \]

This completes the proof. $\square$
