Weighted sections on the projective base

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 22.1 Weighted sections on the projective base

For an integer $k\geq 0$, the line bundle $L_ k$ of section 20.6, with its metric $h_ k$, is the pullback of $\mathcal O(k)$ with its Fubini–Study metric. By the proof of proposition 20.6.1, sections of $L_ k^ m$ are represented by homogeneous polynomials $f$ of degree $km$ in the covering coordinates $z\in \mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $, exactly as sections of $\mathcal O(km)$ on $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, and the pointwise squared norm $|f(z)|^2|z|^{-2km}$ is invariant under every nonzero complex scalar. If the weight also descends, the weighted integrand is a function on the base, and fiber integration identifies the norms and Bergman kernels of section 20.1 on the two spaces.

Theorem 22.1.1. Let $k\geq 0$ be an integer and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X_ n,k\alpha )$. Then $u=\pi ^*v$ for a unique $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^{n-1},k\sigma )$. For every positive integer $m$, pullback identifies the weighted section spaces isometrically: for a section $s$ in the space on the base,

\[ \begin{split} H^0\bigl (\mathbb P^{n-1},\mathcal O(km)\otimes \mathcal I(mv)\bigr ) & \simeq H^0\bigl (X_ n,L_ k^ m\otimes \mathcal I(mu)\bigr ),\\ \| \pi ^*s\| _{X_ n,m,u}^2& =\| s\| _{\mathbb P^{n-1},m,v}^2. \end{split} \]

Here the norm on the base is defined with the Fubini–Study metric and the volume form $\sigma ^{n-1}$. The kernels $K$ and, off their pole sets, $B$ on $X_ n$ are the pullbacks of the corresponding kernels on the base. Pullback also identifies the bounded weighted section algebras $\mathcal W_\bullet (v)$ and $\mathcal W_\bullet (u)$.

Proof. The descent of the potential follows from proposition 1.22.3. By the proof of proposition 20.6.1, the unweighted section spaces on both manifolds consist of the same homogeneous polynomials of degree $km$, and the pointwise norms of corresponding sections agree because the metrics correspond. For every nonnegative Borel function $F$ on the base, fiber integration gives

\[ \int _{X_ n}(F\circ \pi )\, \mathrm{d}V =\frac12\int _{\mathbb P^{n-1}}F\sigma ^{n-1} \int _{\pi ^{-1}(y)}\zeta =\int _{\mathbb P^{n-1}}F\sigma ^{n-1}. \]

Applied to $F=|s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-mv}$, with arbitrary values on the pole set, this formula gives equality of the two possibly infinite integrals. Since finiteness of the weighted norm characterizes the multiplier-ideal section spaces, this proves the asserted isometry. An orthonormal basis therefore pulls back to an orthonormal basis, which proves the kernel identities. Finally, a nonnegative function on the base is bounded if and only if its pullback is bounded, so pullback also identifies the bounded weighted sections, compatibly with multiplication. $\square$
