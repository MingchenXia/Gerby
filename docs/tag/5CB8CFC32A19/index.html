Integrable densities in the compact Gauduchon problem

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.9 Integrable densities in the compact Gauduchon problem

We keep the Hermitian background $\omega $ and replace the continuous logarithmic density by an integrable density. For an unbounded density, weak compactness of the test currents alone does not pass the determinant integral to the limit; uniform control of the weighted tails provides the missing upper semicontinuity.

We use the following notation from section 14.27, section 14.28, with $\omega $ in place of $h$. For a nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, the subsolution threshold $b_\omega (f)\geq 0$ is, by theorem 14.27.2, the largest $b\geq 0$ for which some $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V$. For $p\in L^ n$, the root norm is $\rho _\omega (p)=b_\omega (|p|^ n)^{-1/n}$ if $p\ne 0$, and $\rho _\omega (0)=0$. The space $\mathcal D_\omega $ of proposition 14.31.3 consists of the $p\in L^ n$ with $\rho _\omega (p)<+\infty $, and $\mathcal D_\omega ^0$ is the closure of the bounded functions in $\mathcal D_\omega $, characterized by vanishing tails in proposition 14.31.5. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, $L_{\omega ,\gamma }(p)$ denotes the test quotient of section 14.28, and theorem 14.28.1 gives $\rho _\omega (p)=\sup _\gamma L_{\omega ,\gamma }(|p|)$. Finally, $k_ T$ is the determinant density of $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ from theorem 26.8.1.

If $p\geq 0$ is measurable, define

\[ D_ p(T)=\int _ Xp k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V\qquad (T\in \mathscr G_\omega ), \]

allowing the value $+\infty $. If $T=\gamma ^{n-1}/\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ for a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, then $D_ p(T)=L_{\omega ,\gamma }(p)$.

Theorem 26.9.1. For every nonnegative $p\in L^ n$,

26.54
\begin{equation} \label{eq:integrable-current-supremum} \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_\omega }D_ p(T)=\rho _\omega (p), \end{equation}

with the value $+\infty $ allowed. If $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$, the functional $D_ p$ is finite, concave and weakly upper semicontinuous on $\mathscr G_\omega $, its maximum is attained, and the densities $p k_ T$, for $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, are uniformly integrable.

Proof. For a smooth strictly positive $p$, we have $D_ p=\mathfrak D_{n\log p}$ and $\mathrm{e}^{-B_0(n\log p)/n}=\rho _\omega (p)$, so theorem 26.6.2, applied to $F=n\log p$, proves (26.54). By Hölder's inequality and theorem 26.8.1, for every real $r>n$ there is a constant $C_ r$ such that, for every $q\in L^ r$,

26.55
\begin{equation} \label{eq:current-root-test-bound} \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_\omega }\int _ X|q| k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \leq C_ r\| q\| _{L^ r}. \end{equation}

Next, approximate a bounded nonnegative $p$ in $L^{2n}$ by smooth strictly positive functions. This estimate and (14.114) give uniform convergence on $\mathscr G_\omega $ of the corresponding functionals $D$ and convergence of the root norms, so the supremum formula holds for bounded $p$. For such $p$, the functional $D_ p$ is also upper semicontinuous and concave, by lemma 26.6.1 and uniform convergence. For general $p\in L^ n$, we use the truncations $\min (p,K)$ at positive integer levels $K$. Monotone convergence and the definition of $b_\omega $ through truncations give

\[ \sup _ T D_ p(T)=\sup _ T\sup _ K D_{\min (p,K)}(T) =\sup _ K\rho _\omega (\min (p,K))=\rho _\omega (p). \]

This step only interchanges two suprema and gives no attainment.

Now suppose $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$. The supremum formula applied to the tail gives

\[ 0\leq D_ p(T)-D_{\min (p,K)}(T) \leq \rho _\omega (p\mathbf1_{\{ p>K\} })\longrightarrow 0 \]

uniformly in $T$, by proposition 14.31.5. Hence $D_ p$ is finite and is a uniform limit of upper semicontinuous concave functionals, and compactness of $\mathscr G_\omega $ gives a maximizer. Finally, fix $1<q<n/(n-1)$. By Hölder's inequality and theorem 26.8.1, for every measurable set $E$,

\[ \sup _ T\int _ Ep k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \rho _\omega (p\mathbf1_{\{ p>K\} }) +K C_ q\, \mathrm{d}V(E)^{1-1/q}. \]

Choosing first $K$ large and then the volume of $E$ small proves uniform absolute continuity. Since the total masses are bounded by $\rho _\omega (p)$, the densities are uniformly integrable. $\square$

For a nonzero density, a maximizing current gives a supporting probability for the threshold, as we show below. Whether the threshold is attained by an exact equation depends on the regularity of the maximizing current, which enters through the trace inequalities of 26.9.3.
