From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Theorem 5.2.14 (Yao)

Theorem 5.2.14 (Yao). Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}_{\mathrm{tor}}(X,\omega )$. Let $m\in M$.

  1. Suppose that $m\in \Delta (\omega ,\varphi )$. Then $\chi ^ m\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L\otimes \mathcal{I}(\varphi ))$.

  2. Let $\sigma \in \Sigma $ be a cone of dimension $p$, with rays $\rho _1,\ldots ,\rho _ p$. Let $c_1,\ldots ,c_ p>0$ satisfy $\sum _{i=1}^ p c_ i=1$, and set $u=\sum _{i=1}^ p c_ i u_{\rho _ i}$. If

    5.56
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:mawayC1} \langle m,-u\rangle -\operatorname{Supp}_{\Delta (\omega ,\varphi )}(-u)>1, \end{equation}

    then $\chi ^ m\not\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L\otimes \mathcal{I}(\varphi ))$.

Proof. Put $\Delta =\Delta (\omega ,\varphi )$. By proposition 3.2.13, proposition 5.2.13, we may replace $\varphi $ by $P_{\omega }[\varphi ]$ without changing its multiplier ideal or Newton body. We may therefore assume that $F_{\varphi }-\operatorname{Supp}_{\Delta }$ is bounded on $N_{\mathbb {R}}$. Set

\[ H(\nu )=\langle m,\nu \rangle -\operatorname{Supp}_{\Delta }(\nu ),\quad \nu \in N_{\mathbb {R}}. \]

Since $\Delta $ is compact, $H$ is Lipschitz and positively homogeneous.

Observe also that $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L\otimes \mathcal{I}(\varphi ))$ is a $T_ c$-invariant subspace of $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L)$. Since the $T_ c$-weight spaces in $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L)$ are the lines $\mathbb {C}\chi ^ m$, it is the direct sum of suitable $\mathbb {C}\chi ^ m$'s.

If $m\notin P$, then $\chi ^ m$ is not a section of $L$. Suppose henceforth that $m\in M\cap P$. Membership in $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L\otimes \mathcal{I}(\varphi ))$ is equivalent to the finiteness of

5.57
\begin{equation} \label{eq:torictobefinite1} \int _{T(\mathbb {C})}|\chi ^ m|^2\exp (-\operatorname{Trop}^*F_0-\varphi )\, \omega ^ n. \end{equation}

Since $\operatorname{Trop}^*F_0+\varphi =\operatorname{Trop}^*F_{\varphi }$, the boundedness of $F_{\varphi }-\operatorname{Supp}_{\Delta }$ and proposition 5.2.10 show that this is equivalent to the finiteness of

\[ \int _{N_{\mathbb {R}}}\exp (H(\nu ))\, \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}(F_0)(\nu ). \]

Changing variables by $\nu =\nabla G_0(m')$, we obtain the equivalent integral

5.58
\begin{equation} \label{eq:torictobefinite2} \int _ P\exp \left(\langle m,\nabla G_0(m')\rangle -\operatorname{Supp}_{\Delta }(\nabla G_0(m'))\right)\, \mathrm{d}m'. \end{equation}

If $m\in \Delta $, its integrand is at most $1$, proving 1.

To prove 2, extend $\sigma $ to a maximal cone $\tau \in \Sigma $. Its primitive ray generators $u_1,\ldots ,u_ n$ form a basis of $N$, and we may arrange that $u_ i=u_{\rho _ i}$ for $1\leq i\leq p$. If $e_1,\ldots ,e_ n$ is the dual basis of $M$, then $z_ i=\chi ^{e_ i}$ are coordinates on the affine chart $U_{\tau }\cong \mathbb {C}^ n$.

Let $v\in P\cap M$ be the vertex corresponding to $\tau $. The character $\chi ^ v$ is a local frame of $L$ on $U_{\tau }$. Writing

\[ \nu =\operatorname{Trop}(z)=\sum _{i=1}^ n\log |z_ i|^2\, u_ i, \]

the weight of the singular metric in this frame is $F_{\varphi }(\nu )-\langle v,\nu \rangle $. The local representative of $\chi ^ m$ is $\chi ^{m-v}$, so its squared norm is

\[ \exp \left(\langle m,\nu \rangle -F_{\varphi }(\nu )\right). \]

On the unit polydisc, $\omega ^ n$ is comparable to Euclidean volume. Set $t_ i=-\log |z_ i|^2$. Integrating out the angular variables and using the boundedness of $F_{\varphi }-\operatorname{Supp}_{\Delta }$, the weighted integral over this polydisc is finite if and only if the following integral is finite:

\[ \int _{[0,\infty )^ n}\exp \left(H\left(-\sum _{i=1}^ n t_ i u_ i\right)-\sum _{i=1}^ n t_ i\right)\, \mathrm{d}t. \]

Restrict this integral to the tube parametrized by $s>1$ and $0<y_ i<1$ for $2\leq i\leq n$, with

\[ t_1=c_1s,\qquad t_ i=c_ is+y_ i\quad (2\leq i\leq p),\qquad t_ i=y_ i\quad (p<i\leq n). \]

The Jacobian is $c_1>0$. Since $H$ is Lipschitz and positively homogeneous and $\sum _{i=1}^ p c_ i=1$, the exponent on this tube is

\[ H\left(-\sum _{i=1}^ n t_ i u_ i\right)-\sum _{i=1}^ n t_ i =s\bigl (H(-u)-1\bigr )+\mathcal{O}(1), \]

with a bounded error uniform in $s$ and the $y_ i$'s. The local integral is therefore bounded from below by a positive multiple of

\[ \int _1^{\infty }\exp \left(s\bigl (H(-u)-1\bigr )\right)\, \mathrm{d}s, \]

which diverges under (5.56). This proves 2. $\square$
