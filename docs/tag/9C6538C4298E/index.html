Lemma 9.2.79C6538C4298E

Lemma 9.2.7. Given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we have $\Gamma ^*\in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Proof. It follows from 9.2.4, (9.9) and proposition 1.2.2 that the restriction of $\Gamma ^*$ to each compact subinterval of $(0,\infty )$ is a subgeodesic. By corollary 9.2.5, for any $t>0$, $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

First observe that as $t\to 0+$, we have

9.13
\begin{equation} \Gamma _ t^*\xrightarrow {L^1}\phi . \end{equation}

By 9.2.6 and proposition 1.5.4, it suffices to show each $L^1$-cluster point $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ of $\Gamma _ t^*$ as $t\to 0$ is equal to $\phi $.

To see this, first observe that by (9.9), for any fixed $t>0$,

\[ \Gamma _ t^*\leq t\Gamma _{\max }+\phi . \]

Therefore, $\psi \leq \phi $. On the other hand, for any fixed $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, by (9.9), we have

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t\geq \Gamma _{\tau }+t\tau \]

for any $t>0$. So $\psi \geq \Gamma _{\tau }$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. It follows that $\psi \geq \phi $. Therefore, $\psi =\phi $.

Assume that $\Gamma ^*$ is not a geodesic ray. Then we can find $0\leq a<b$ such that $(\Gamma ^*_ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$ differs from the geodesic $(\eta _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$ from $\Gamma ^*_ a$ to $\Gamma ^*_ b$. The existence of $(\eta _ t)_ t$ is guaranteed by proposition 4.2.4. We consider the subgeodesic $(\ell _ t)_{t>0}$ given by $\ell _ t=\eta _ t$ for $t\in (a,b)$ and $\ell _ t=\Gamma ^*_ t$ otherwise. Note that $\ell $ is a subgeodesic due to the gluing lemma 4.1.11.

Consider the Legendre transform

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }=\inf _{t>0} (\ell _ t-t\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \]

Then $\Gamma '_{\tau }\geq \Gamma _{\tau }$ and $\Gamma '_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $ by proposition 1.2.11 for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$.

We claim that

9.14
\begin{equation} \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _{\tau }+(b-a)(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \end{equation}

Observe that $\Gamma '_{\tau }\equiv -\infty $ when $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$ by 9.2.6. Thus it suffices to consider $\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }$. Put

\[ C_{\tau }\coloneqq (b-a)(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau )\geq 0. \]

For any $s\in [a,b]$, the monotonicity of $t\mapsto \Gamma _ t^*-t\Gamma _{\max }$ from 9.2.6 gives

\[ \Gamma _ b^*-b\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau }. \]

Since $\ell _ b=\Gamma _ b^*$, we get

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _ b^*-b\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau },\quad s\in [a,b]. \]

For $s\notin [a,b]$, we have $\ell _ s=\Gamma _ s^*$, hence

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau }. \]

Taking the infimum over all $s>0$ gives

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _{\tau }+C_{\tau }. \]

Therefore, (9.14) follows. In particular, for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have $\Gamma '_{\tau }\sim \Gamma _{\tau }$. Moreover, $\sup _ X\ell _ t\leq t\Gamma _{\max }$ for all $t>0$: this is clear outside $(a,b)$ from 9.2.6, and on $(a,b)$ it follows from the convexity of subgeodesics applied to the geodesic $(\eta _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$. Hence, for $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$,

\[ \sup _ X(\ell _ t-t\tau )\leq t(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau ). \]

Letting $t\to 0+$ gives $\Gamma '_{\tau }\leq 0$. It follows from the fact that $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is a model potential that $\Gamma _{\tau }=\Gamma '_{\tau }$ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. Therefore, by theorem A.2.2, we have $\Gamma ^*_ t=\ell _ t$ for all $t>0$, which is a contradiction. $\square$
