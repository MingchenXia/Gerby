From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 14.11.1

Theorem 14.11.1. Let $\theta $ and $\varphi $ be as above, and let $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$, where $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\mu (X)>0$. Then:

  1. For every $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ such that

    \[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu . \]

  2. There are $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ and a uniquely determined constant $c>0$ such that

    \[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu . \]

of theorem 14.11.1, part (1). Step 1: a maximizing sequence. Fix $\lambda >0$. By lemma 14.11.4, there is $w\sim \varphi $ such that

\[ \nu _0:=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda w}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) \leq \mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Consider all potentials $z\sim \varphi $ for which

\[ \nu _0\leq \nu _ z:=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda z} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (z)\leq \mu , \]

and let $B$ be the supremum of their weighted masses $\nu _ z(X)$. This family is nonempty, since it contains $w$, and $B\leq \mu (X)<+\infty $. Choose $u_ j$ in the family with $\nu _{u_ j}(X)\to B$. Since $\nu _0\leq \nu _{u_ j}$, corollary 14.1.3 gives $u_ j\leq w$.

Step 2: uniform bounds. We need a lower bound for the suprema of the $u_ j$. Put $\varphi _\varepsilon =P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\leq 0$, and let $\psi $ be the bounded solution of

\[ ((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \psi }f\, \mathrm{d}V \]

given by theorem 14.2.2. For $h=\psi +\varphi _\varepsilon $, positivity of the splitting $\theta _ h=(\varepsilon \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _\varepsilon )+((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )$ gives

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda h}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h) \geq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda \varphi _\varepsilon }f\, \mathrm{d}V \geq \mu \geq \nu _{u_ j}. \]

Here $h\preceq u_ j$, because $h\preceq \varphi $ and $u_ j\sim \varphi $, and each $u_ j$ admits a smaller-background envelope because $u_ j\sim \varphi $. Corollary 14.1.3 therefore gives $u_ j\geq h$. Consequently

\[ \sup _ Xh\leq M_ j:=\sup _ Xu_ j\leq \sup _ Xw, \]

and the lower bound is finite.

We now apply theorem 14.11.3 to $v_ j=u_ j-M_ j$. Their measures satisfy

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j) \leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xw}f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

so $\varphi -C\leq v_ j\leq \varphi $. Together with the bounds for $M_ j$, this shows that all $u_ j$ lie within a fixed bounded distance of $\varphi $.

Step 3: the limit. Extract an $L^1$ limit $u$. By corollary 14.8.3, applied with the dominating measure $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xw}\mu $, the convergence holds in capacity, $u\sim \varphi $, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j) \rightharpoonup \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \]

After a further extraction we may also assume $\nu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \nu $. Since $\nu _0\leq \nu _{u_ j}\leq \mu $, the limit satisfies the same inequalities. We claim that

\[ \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\nu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\nu . \]

Indeed, $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ in $L^1(\mu )$ by lemma 1.23.1, and all the measures $\nu _{u_ j}$ are at most $\mu $; this controls the error caused by changing the weight. The fixed bounded weight $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ can be approximated in $L^1(\mu )$ by continuous functions, and since both $\nu _{u_ j}$ and $\nu $ are dominated by $\mu $, this approximation is uniform in the integrals. The weak convergence $\nu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \nu $ then gives the claim, and the same argument applies after multiplication by any continuous test function.

Since $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\nu _{u_ j}=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)$, it follows that

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\nu , \qquad \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=\nu , \qquad \nu (X)=B. \]

The second identity holds off the pole set of $u$, which has zero $\mu $-measure. Hence $u$ belongs to the family and attains the supremum $B$. Write $\nu =g\, \mathrm{d}V$, with $0\leq g\leq f$.

Step 4: no deficit. Suppose that $g\neq f$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. Applying lemma 14.11.4 to $f-g$, we find $v\sim \varphi $ with

\[ \nu _ v:=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda v}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v) \leq (f-g)\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

This measure is nonzero. In fact, no potential $v$ admitting a smaller-background envelope has zero Monge–Ampère measure, since theorem 14.1.2, applied with coefficient zero to $v$ and $v+1$, would give $v\geq v+1$. Thus $\nu _ v(X)>0$.

The sum $\nu +\nu _ v$ is finite, so theorem 14.10.1 gives $a\sim \varphi $ with

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (a)=\nu +\nu _ v. \]

This measure still lies between $\nu _0$ and $\mu $, but its mass is $B+\nu _ v(X)>B$, a contradiction. Therefore $g=f$, and $u$ solves the required equation. Uniqueness in the prescribed ordinary type follows from corollary 14.1.3. $\square$

of theorem 14.11.1, part (2). For each $j\geq 1$, part (1) gives $u_ j\sim \varphi $ with

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)=\mathrm{e}^{u_ j/j}f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Write $M_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j$, $v_ j=u_ j-M_ j$ and $c_ j=\mathrm{e}^{M_ j/j}$. Let $h$ be a bounded normalized solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (h)=\alpha f\, \mathrm{d}V$, with $\alpha >0$, given by theorem 14.2.3.

For $H_ j=h+\| h\| _\infty +j\log \alpha $ we have

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-H_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (H_ j) =\mathrm{e}^{-(h+\| h\| _\infty )/j}f\, \mathrm{d}V \leq f\, \mathrm{d}V =\mathrm{e}^{-u_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j). \]

The bounded function $H_ j$ admits a smaller-background envelope, and $u_ j\preceq H_ j$, so corollary 14.1.3 gives $u_ j\leq H_ j$. In particular,

\[ 0<c_ j\leq \alpha \exp (2\| h\| _\infty /j). \]

The lower density bound of lemma 14.11.2, applied to $v_ j$ with $q=p$, gives

\[ b_ p\leq \| c_ j\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}f\| _ p\leq c_ j\| f\| _ p, \]

so the $c_ j$ are also bounded away from zero.

Since $\sup _ Xv_ j=0$ and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j)=c_ j\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}f\, \mathrm{d}V\leq C f\, \mathrm{d}V$, theorem 14.11.3 gives $\varphi -C'\leq v_ j\leq \varphi $. Extract a subsequence with $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$ and $c_ j\to c>0$. By corollary 14.8.3, the convergence holds in capacity, $v\sim \varphi $, and the Monge–Ampère measures converge weakly. Meanwhile, lemma 1.23.3 gives

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}-1|f\, \mathrm{d}V\longrightarrow 0. \]

Thus the right-hand sides converge in total variation to $cf\, \mathrm{d}V$, and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$.

For the uniqueness of the constant, suppose that $a,b\sim \varphi $ solve the normalized equation with constants $c_ a<c_ b$. For every real $t$,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (a) =\frac{c_ a}{c_ b}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (b+t), \]

so theorem 14.1.2 gives $a\geq b+t$. This is impossible as $t\to +\infty $, at any point where both potentials are finite. Interchanging the roles of the two constants proves that $c$ is unique. $\square$
