Chapter 12 · Monge–Ampère equations and stability · Tag 2C3AA01F259B

Weak extremum localization

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 11.4.2

Proposition 11.4.2. Let $u,v$ be continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions with $\omega _ u^ n=\omega _ v^ n=F\omega ^ n$, where $F\geq 0$ is bounded and measurable. Let $q$ be a smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function, and write $\omega _ q^ n=G\omega ^ n$. Then for every $0<\kappa <1$ and $\eta >0$,

11.26
\begin{equation} \label{eq:weak-localization-min} F\omega ^ n\bigl (\{ u-v<\min _ X(u-v)+\eta \} \cap \{ F>\kappa G\} \bigr )>0. \end{equation}

The analogous assertion holds for the set $\{ u-v>\max _ X(u-v)-\eta \} $.

Proof. Choose $\delta \in (0,1]$ with $\omega _ q\geq \delta \omega $. For $0<t<1$, put $b_ t=(1-t)v+tq$, $m_ t=\min _ X(u-b_ t)$, and $E_ s=\{ u<b_ t+m_ t+s\} $. Then $\omega _{b_ t}\geq t\delta \omega $. Write $\mu =F\omega ^ n$, $d=\kappa ^{-1/n}-1$, and $\gamma =nd$. By (1.23),

\[ \omega _{b_ t}^ n\geq \bigl ((1-t)F^{1/n}+tG^{1/n}\bigr )^ n \omega ^ n. \]

On $L=\{ F\leq \kappa G\} $ this is at least $(1+\gamma t)\mu $, since $G^{1/n}\geq (1+d)F^{1/n}$ there, and on $R=\{ F>\kappa G\} $ it is at least $(1-nt)\mu $. Choose $s>0$ so small that the reverse comparison factor in (10.2), with $\varepsilon =t\delta $, is at most $1+\gamma t/2$. Modified comparison on $E_ s$, with $x=\mu (E_ s\cap L)$ and $y=\mu (E_ s\cap R)$, gives

\[ (1+\gamma t)x+(1-nt)y\leq (1+\gamma t/2)(x+y), \qquad \text{and hence}\qquad y\geq \frac{1-\kappa ^{1/n}}2\, \mu (E_ s). \]

The open set $E_ s$ is nonempty by the definition of $m_ t$. As $\omega _{b_ t}^ n\geq (t\delta )^ n\omega ^ n$, (10.2) forces $\mu (E_ s)>0$, hence $y>0$.

Let $a=\min _ X(u-v)$ and $\Lambda =\| v-q\| _\infty $. Since $u-b_ t=u-v+t(v-q)$, we have $|m_ t-a|\leq t\Lambda $ and $E_ s\subset \{ u-v<a+2t\Lambda +s\} $. Given $\eta $, choose $t$ and then $s$ with $2t\Lambda +s<\eta $. Since $y=\mu (E_ s\cap R)>0$, this proves (11.26). Interchanging $u$ and $v$ gives the assertion for the near-maximum set. $\square$
