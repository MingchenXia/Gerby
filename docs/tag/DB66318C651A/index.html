17.17.7 Recovering the singularity envelopesDB66318C651A

The minimum principle shows that the inverse transforms are $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, but their envelope property uses the whole ray. For an inverse transform $g$ and $C>0$, the envelope $P_\theta (g+C,0)$ of the obstacle $\min (g+C,0)$ gives an admissible affine path on every interval $[0,M]$, lying below the ray translated by an affine function of $t$. Letting the length $M$ vary, and then letting the relative time $t/M$ tend to zero, removes the additive constant $C$.

Theorem 17.17.8.062DEC0E6527 If $u_ t$ is an envelope ray of slope $c$ with initial potential $\varphi $, its inverse transform is an envelope test curve with cutoff $c$ and initial potential $\varphi $. The forward and inverse transforms are mutually inverse bijections between the envelope test curves of definition 17.17.2 and the envelope rays of definition 17.17.3 with initial potential $\varphi $.

Proof. By lemma 5.41.1, for each $x$ the function $t\mapsto u_ t(x)-ct$ is either finite and convex or identically $-\infty $. It is bounded above by zero, so it is decreasing. Since $\sup _ X(u_ t-ct)=0$, theorem 5.25.2, corollary 5.25.3 give

\[ \widehat u_ c=\inf _{t>0}(u_ t-ct)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

For $\tau <c$, we have $\widehat u_ c\leq \widehat u_\tau \leq u_1-\tau $, so proposition 5.41.3 gives $\widehat u_\tau \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Also

\[ \widehat u_\tau \leq \inf _{t>0}t(c-\tau )=0 \quad (\tau \leq c), \]

while the bound $\widehat u_\tau \leq t(c-\tau )$ with $t\to \infty $ shows $\widehat u_\tau \equiv -\infty $ for $\tau >c$. Concavity and monotonicity in $\tau $ hold because $\widehat u_\tau $ is an infimum of affine functions of $\tau $ with negative slopes $-t$.

Fix $\tau <c$ and write $g=\widehat u_\tau $. The inequality $g\leq u_ M-M\tau $, valid for every $M>0$, gives

\[ P_\theta [g]\leq P_\theta [u_ M]=\varphi . \]

For $C>0$ put $q_ C=P_\theta (g+C,0)$. Since $g\leq 0$, the function $g$ is a competitor, so $q_ C\not\equiv -\infty $, and

\[ g\leq q_ C\leq P_\theta [g]\leq \varphi , \qquad q_ C-C\leq g\leq u_ M-M\tau . \]

The affine path $q_ C-Ct/M$ is admissible for the endpoints $\varphi $ and $u_ M-M\tau $ on $[0,M]$. Adding $-t\tau $ gives a bijection between the competitor families for the endpoints $\varphi ,u_ M$ and $\varphi ,u_ M-M\tau $, by proposition 17.16.2, so the Perron segment for the latter endpoints is $u_ t-t\tau $. For $0<s\leq 1$ this yields

\[ q_ C-Cs\leq u_{sM}-sM\tau . \]

Taking the infimum over $M>0$ gives $q_ C-Cs\leq g$, and letting $s\downarrow 0$ gives $q_ C\leq g$. Thus $q_ C=g$ for every $C>0$, and letting $C\to \infty $ in the definition of $P_\theta [g]$ proves $P_\theta [g]=g$.

Let $\psi =(\sup _{\tau <c}\widehat u_\tau )^*$; since $\widehat u_\tau =P_\theta [\widehat u_\tau ]\leq \varphi $, we have $\psi \leq \varphi $. Because $u_ t-ct$ is decreasing in $t$, the limit of $u_ t(x)$ as $t\downarrow 0$ exists at every $x$, and an almost everywhere convergent sequence given by the $L^1$ convergence $u_ t\to \varphi $ identifies it with $\varphi $ almost everywhere. At these points, the endpoint identity in lemma 5.41.5 gives

\[ \sup _{\tau <c}\inf _{t>0}(u_ t(x)-t\tau )=\varphi (x). \]

Therefore $\psi =\varphi $ by lemma 5.24.3.

As $\tau \uparrow c$, the transforms decrease to $\widehat u_ c$, since the infima over $t>0$ and over $\tau <c$ commute; this is the convention for the value at the cutoff. Finally, lemma 5.41.5 recovers $u_ t$ from its inverse transforms, also at points where $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ is identically $-\infty $, and lemma 5.41.8 recovers each test curve from its forward transform. Hence the correspondence is a bijection. $\square$
