13.14.20 Integrable Hessians and infinite mass25C7A8032994

In the construction of theorem 13.14.18, the Gauduchon identity of proposition 11.5.1 fixes the trace integral of every positive summand $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$. The series defining $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$ therefore converges in the coefficient $L^1$ norm, although its determinant is not integrable. The trace measures converge in total variation, yet the Monge–Ampère mass is infinite. In section 13.15, weights that decay on deep sublevel sets will control these masses.

Fix a Gauduchon form $\gamma $ and set $M=\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$. For a real $(1,1)$-form $T$ with measurable coefficients, let $A_ T$ be its Hermitian matrix relative to $\gamma $, and let $|A_ T|=(A_ T^2)^{1/2}$ be its positive matrix absolute value. We use the norm

\[ \| T\| _{L^1(\gamma )} =\frac1n\int _ X\operatorname {tr}|A_ T|\, \gamma ^ n. \]

For $T\geq 0$ this equals $\int _ XT\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$. On compact $X$, any other smooth Hermitian form gives an equivalent norm and hence the same space of integrable coefficients. When the coefficients of $T$ are integrable, we write $\det _\gamma T=\det A_ T$; this function is defined almost everywhere, and $T^ n=(\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n$ when $T$ is smooth.

Proposition 13.14.21.D889AEAF2866 Suppose $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $. For the potential $U$ and its smooth decreasing approximants $U_ N$ constructed in the proof of theorem 13.14.18, the current $T=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$ has integrable coefficients and

\[ \| \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N-T\| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq 2^{1-N}M. \]

Its pointwise determinant satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U) \geq (\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n, \qquad \int _ X(\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n=+\infty . \]

Moreover,

\[ \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N)^ n\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

Proof. Write $T_ j=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$ for the smooth positive forms in that construction, and put

\[ S_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ jT_ j,\qquad \delta _ N=\sum _{j>N}a_ j=2^{-N}. \]

The smooth positive forms $S_ N$ increase pointwise, and the Gauduchon identity gives

\[ \int _ XS_ N\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=(1-\delta _ N)M. \]

By monotone convergence of their traces, the series of matrices has a finite positive limit $S$ almost everywhere. The coefficients of $S$ are integrable, because in a $\gamma $-unitary frame the absolute value of every coefficient of a positive matrix is bounded by its trace. Positivity and the same trace identity give

\[ \| S-S_ N\| _{L^1(\gamma )}=\delta _ N M. \]

In particular, $S_ N\to S$ in the sense of distributions. Since

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N=S_ N+\delta _ N\omega \longrightarrow \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U \]

in the sense of distributions, by $L^1$ convergence of the potentials, we obtain $T=S$. The triangle inequality now gives

\[ \| \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N-T\| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq \| S_ N-S\| _{L^1(\gamma )} +\delta _ N\| \omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )} =2\delta _ N M. \]

To compare $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)$ with the determinant, fix $N$ and set $V_ N=U-U_ N$. The remaining partial sums give $\delta _ N\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_ N\geq 0$ and $V_ N\leq 0$. For $t>0$, the function $W_{N,t}=U_ N+\max (V_ N,-t)$ is bounded, and its current is the sum of two positive terms:

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W_{N,t} =S_ N+\bigl (\delta _ N\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (V_ N,-t)\bigr ). \]

On $D_ t=\{ U>-t\} $, the inequality $U_ N\leq 0$ gives $V_ N\geq U>-t$, and consequently $W_{N,t}=U=\max (U,-t)$ there. Locality and positivity of bounded products imply

\[ \mathbf1_{D_ t}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U) \geq \mathbf1_{D_ t}S_ N^ n. \]

The smooth measure $S_ N^ n$ gives no mass to the pluripolar pole set of $U$, so letting $t\to \infty $ proves $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)\geq S_ N^ n$. The positive matrices $S_ N$ increase to $T$ almost everywhere, so their determinants increase to $\det _\gamma T$. Monotone convergence on every Borel set gives the asserted inequality between $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)$ and the determinant measure.

Finally, for every $N\geq j$, positivity gives $S_ N\geq a_ jT_ j$. Hence both the measurable limit and the smooth approximating forms satisfy

\[ \int _ X(\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n \geq a_ j^ n\int _ XT_ j^ n\geq 2^ j, \qquad \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N)^ n \geq a_ j^ n\int _ XT_ j^ n\geq 2^ j. \]

The first bound holds for every $j$, and the second for every $N\geq j$; together they prove the two assertions about infinite mass. $\square$

Corollary 13.14.22.1D7A78F6BD02 If the bounded mass property fails, there are nonpositive potentials $w_\varepsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, $0<\varepsilon <1$, such that

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon \geq (1-\varepsilon )\omega , \qquad \mathcal I(pw_\varepsilon )=\mathcal O_ X\quad (p>0), \]

and such that both the integral of the pointwise determinant $\det _\gamma (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon )$ against $\gamma ^ n$ and the extended Monge–Ampère mass of $w_\varepsilon $ are infinite. As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, the potentials converge to zero in $L^1$, their currents converge to $\omega $ in the coefficient $L^1$ norm, and their full trace measures $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ converge to $\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ in total variation.

Proof. Take $w_\varepsilon =\varepsilon U$, with $U$ and $T=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$ as in proposition 13.14.21. Its current is

\[ T_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )\omega +\varepsilon T. \]

The lower bound and the two $L^1$ convergence assertions follow immediately. The full trace measures have integrable densities, and their distance from $\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ in total variation is at most

\[ \varepsilon \| T-\omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )}. \]

Every multiplier ideal is trivial by the exponential integrability of $U$ proved in theorem 13.14.18. Since $T_\varepsilon \geq \varepsilon T$ almost everywhere, the determinant integral of $T_\varepsilon $ is at least $\varepsilon ^ n$ times that of $T$, hence infinite. The extended mass of $w_\varepsilon $ is infinite by the proof of corollary 13.14.19. $\square$

For smooth potentials, the total masses can be prescribed in advance while both the currents and the exponential weights converge.

Corollary 13.14.23.BED3510B9193 Suppose $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $. Let $(\varepsilon _ j)_{j\geq 1}$ and $(b_ j)_{j\geq 1}$ be real sequences with $0<\varepsilon _ j<1$, $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$, and $b_ j>\int _ X\omega ^ n$. There are smooth potentials $w_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\sup _ Xw_ j=0$ such that

\[ \begin{gathered} \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\geq (1-\varepsilon _ j)\omega , \qquad \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^ n=b_ j, \\ \| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq 2M\varepsilon _ j. \end{gathered} \]

Moreover, $w_ j\to 0$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ and $\mathrm{e}^{-pw_ j}\to 1$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ for every fixed $p>0$. The full Gauduchon trace measures converge to $\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ in total variation.

Proof. By proposition 13.14.15, we can choose a smooth $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$ and

\[ \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^ n>\varepsilon _ j^{-n}b_ j. \]

Put $T_ j=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$. The polynomial

\[ F_ j(s)=\int _ X\bigl ((1-s)\omega +sT_ j\bigr )^ n, \qquad 0\leq s\leq \varepsilon _ j, \]

satisfies $F_ j(0)<b_ j$, and also $F_ j(\varepsilon _ j)\geq \varepsilon _ j^ n\int _ XT_ j^ n>b_ j$ by positivity of the mixed terms. The intermediate value theorem gives $s_ j\in (0,\varepsilon _ j)$ with $F_ j(s_ j)=b_ j$. Set $w_ j=s_ ju_ j$. Then $\sup _ Xw_ j=0$, $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j=(1-s_ j)\omega +s_ jT_ j\geq (1-\varepsilon _ j)\omega $, and $w_ j$ has the prescribed mass $b_ j$.

The Gauduchon identity gives $\| T_ j\| _{L^1(\gamma )}=\| \omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )}=M$. Hence

\[ \| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\| _{L^1(\gamma )} =s_ j\| T_ j-\omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq 2M\varepsilon _ j. \]

Since the absolute value of the trace of a Hermitian matrix is bounded by the trace of its absolute value, the same bound holds for the total variation of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$. With $K_\omega $ as in (5.26.1), we also have $\| w_ j\| _{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq K_\omega \varepsilon _ j$.

Finally, let $\alpha ,C$ be the constants of lemma 5.26.2 for $(X,\omega ,\mathrm{d}V)$, and fix $p>0$. For all sufficiently large $j$, one has $ps_ j\leq \alpha $. Convexity of the exponential function, applied with weight $ps_ j/\alpha $, gives

\[ 0\leq \mathrm{e}^{-pw_ j}-1 \leq \frac{ps_ j}{\alpha }\bigl (\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha u_ j}-1\bigr ). \]

Integrating, we bound the $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ norm of $\mathrm{e}^{-pw_ j}-1$ by $pC\varepsilon _ j/\alpha $, which proves the last convergence. $\square$
