10.9 Gradient bounds for concave equationsF77131C8889A

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 10.9 Gradient bounds for concave equations

We keep the fixed data, the family of right-hand sides and the normalization of section 10.8, and assume in addition that $\| \partial h\| _{L^\infty (\omega )}\leq H_1$; throughout the section, $u$ denotes a solution with such a right-hand side $h$. The gradient bound is obtained by comparison with a nearby smooth admissible function $V$. A maximum principle for a weighted gradient quantity bounds the gradient of $u$ in terms of that of $V$, provided $\| u-V\| _\infty $ is below a threshold that does not depend on the derivatives of $V$. The $C^0$ compactness of corollary 10.8.4 then shows that finitely many such comparison functions suffice for all solutions.

For the derivative calculation, write $U=u/(2\pi )$ and $\underline U=\underline u/(2\pi )$. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial U$. For any smooth real $V$, put $\mathcal A_ V=\omega ^{-1}(\chi +\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial V)$ and call $V$ admissible if $\lambda (\mathcal A_ V)\in \Gamma $. We use the Chern connection of $\omega $, and define the endomorphism $B=DF(\mathcal A_ U)$ by $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}F(\mathcal A_ U+tM)|_{t=0}=\operatorname {tr}(BM)$. Write $\lambda =(\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ n)=\lambda (\mathcal A_ U)$. At a point where $\mathcal A_ U$ is diagonal in a unitary frame, set

\[ B_ i=f_ i(\lambda ),\qquad \mathcal Lq=\sum _ iB_ iq_{i\bar i},\qquad T=\sum _ iB_ i, \qquad |\partial q|_ B^2=\sum _ iB_ i|q_ i|^2. \]

Here subscripts denote frame derivatives, $U_{ki}=\nabla _ iU_ k$ and $U_{i\bar j}=\partial _ i\partial _{\bar j}U$; the second derivatives $U_{ki}$ need not be symmetric. The trace expressions below do not depend on the unitary frame.

Lemma 10.9.1. There are fixed $\tau ,\kappa >0$ such that

\begin{gather*} T\geq \tau ,\qquad 0\leq \operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_ U)\leq h,\\ \mathcal L(U-V)\leq h\quad (V\text{ admissible}),\qquad \mathcal L(\underline U-U)\geq -h. \end{gather*}

Moreover, at every point at least one of the alternatives

\[ \mathcal L(\underline U-U)\geq \kappa T, \qquad B\geq \kappa T\operatorname {Id} \]

holds. The constants depend only on the fixed data; in particular they are uniform for all right-hand sides in the family.

Proof. If $\lambda (P)\in \Gamma $, then $\mathcal A_ U+tP$ is admissible for $t\geq 0$. The function $t\mapsto F(\mathcal A_ U+tP)$ is positive and concave on $[0,\infty )$, so its derivative at $t=0$ cannot be negative; thus $\operatorname {tr}(BP)\geq 0$. Since $F$ is concave and $F(0)=0$, the tangent inequality at $\mathcal A_ U$, evaluated at the origin, gives $\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_ U)\leq h$. Choose $a_*>0$ with $F(a_*\operatorname {Id})>H_0+1$. Concavity gives

\[ H_0+1<F(a_*\operatorname {Id}) \leq h+a_*T-\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_ U)\leq H_0+a_*T. \]

Thus $T\geq a_*^{-1}$. Since $\mathcal L(U-V)=\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_ U)-\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_ V)$ and $\mathcal L(\underline U-U)=\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_{\underline U})-\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_ U)$, the remaining two bounds follow from the nonnegativity of $\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_ V)$ and $\operatorname {tr}(B\mathcal A_{\underline U})$.

We derive the alternative from the strict gap. Fix $r,H,\delta $ from lemma 10.8.1, and put $\mu =\lambda (\mathcal A_{\underline U})$. Compactness and the gap give one $S>r$ such that

\[ f(\mu -2r\mathbf1+Se_ i)>H+2\delta \qquad (1\leq i\leq n) \]

everywhere. We first prove a scalar alternative. At a point of $X$, let $\lambda \in \Gamma $ satisfy $f(\lambda )=\sigma $, where $\sigma =h$ at that point, and consider the set

\[ K=\{ v\geq \mu -r\mathbf1:f(v)\leq \sigma \} . \]

It is contained in $\Gamma $ because $\mu -r\mathbf1\in \Gamma $, it is closed, and the matrix bound of lemma 10.8.1, applied to diagonal matrices, makes it bounded; thus $K$ is compact. When nonempty, it contains $\mu -r\mathbf1$ and is star-shaped about that point: moving along a segment toward it decreases each coordinate, hence decreases $f$. Thus $K$ is connected. Let $N=\nabla f(\lambda )$, whose entries are positive, and let $\Pi $ be the tangent hyperplane $N\cdot (v-\lambda )=0$.

If $f(\mu -r\mathbf1)>\sigma $, concavity gives $N\cdot (\mu -\lambda )>r\sum _ iN_ i$. Otherwise $\mu -r\mathbf1\in K$. The diagonal ray $\mu -r\mathbf1+t\mathbf1$, $t\geq 0$, meets $\{ f=\sigma \} $, since $f$ diverges along it; at the meeting point $v$, the tangent inequality gives $N\cdot (v-\lambda )\geq 0$. If $\Pi $ misses $K$, connectedness makes $N\cdot (v-\lambda )$ strictly positive throughout $K$, and in particular at $\mu -r\mathbf1$; this again gives $N\cdot (\mu -\lambda )>r\sum _ iN_ i$.

If $v\in \Pi \cap K$, put $z_ i=v-r\mathbf1+Se_ i$. Then $z_ i\geq \mu -2r\mathbf1+Se_ i$, so $f(z_ i)>\sigma $. The tangent inequality at $\lambda $ gives

\[ 0<N\cdot (z_ i-v)=SN_ i-r\sum _ jN_ j. \]

Thus $N_ i>(r/S)\sum _ jN_ j$ for every $i$. We have proved that $N\cdot (\mu -\lambda )>r\sum _ iN_ i$ or $N_ i>(r/S)\sum _ jN_ j$ for all $i$, at every positive level $\sigma $, with constants independent of $\sigma $.

To pass to matrices, take $\lambda =\lambda (\mathcal A_ U)$, and order $\lambda $ and $\mu $ decreasingly. By concavity and symmetry, the numbers $f_ i(\lambda )$ are then ordered increasingly. In the eigenframe of $\mathcal A_ U$, the diagonal of $\mathcal A_{\underline U}$ is a doubly stochastic average of permutations of $\mu $, so the rearrangement inequality gives

\[ \operatorname {tr}\bigl (B(\mathcal A_{\underline U}-\mathcal A_ U)\bigr ) \geq \sum _ if_ i(\lambda )(\mu _ i-\lambda _ i). \]

Since $N=(f_ i(\lambda ))_ i=(B_ i)_ i$, taking $\kappa $ smaller than $\min \{ r,r/S\} $ proves the matrix alternative. $\square$

Lemma 10.9.2. Put $Q=1+|\partial U|_\omega ^2$. For any smooth real $\xi $, one has

\begin{align*} \mathrm e^{-\xi }\mathcal L(\mathrm e^\xi Q) & \geq \sum _{i,k}B_ i|U_{ki}+U_ k\xi _ i|^2 +\frac14\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ i^2+Q\mathcal L\xi +|\partial \xi |_ B^2\\ & \quad +2\operatorname{Re}\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ iU_ i\xi _{\bar i} -2\operatorname{Re}\sum _{i,k}B_ i\chi _{i\bar k}U_ k\xi _{\bar i} -C_ gTQ-2H_1|\partial U|_\omega , \end{align*}

where $C_ g$ depends only on $\omega $ and $\chi $.

Proof. Write $\mathscr T^ p_{ik}$ and $R^ p_{i\bar jk}$ for the torsion and curvature of the Chern connection. The commutation formula and the differentiated equation read

\begin{gather*} \nabla _{\bar j}\nabla _ iU_ k =\nabla _ kU_{i\bar j}+\sum _ p\bigl (R^ p_{i\bar jk}U_ p-\mathscr T^ p_{ik}U_{p\bar j}\bigr ),\\ \sum _ iB_ i\nabla _ kU_{i\bar i}=h_ k-\sum _ iB_ i\nabla _ k\chi _{i\bar i}. \end{gather*}

The product rule for $|\partial U|^2$ gives both squares $\sum B_ i|U_{ki}|^2$ and $\sum B_ i|U_{k\bar i}|^2$, the term $2\operatorname{Re}\sum _ kh_ kU_{\bar k}$, a curvature error bounded by $CT|\partial U|^2$, and the errors from $\nabla \chi $ and torsion. The latter are bounded by

\[ CT|\partial U|+CT|\partial U|^2 +C|\partial U|T^{1/2}\Bigl (\sum _{i,k}B_ i|U_{k\bar i}|^2\Bigr )^{1/2}. \]

Absorb the last term into half of the mixed square. Since $U_{i\bar j}=\lambda _ i\delta _{ij}-\chi _{i\bar j}$, the remaining half is at least $\frac14\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ i^2-CT$. Hence

\[ \mathcal L|\partial U|^2\geq \sum _{i,k}B_ i|U_{ki}|^2 +\frac14\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ i^2-C_ gTQ-2H_1|\partial U|. \]

Finally,

\[ \partial _ i|\partial U|^2 =\sum _ kU_{ki}U_{\bar k}+\lambda _ iU_ i-\sum _ k\chi _{i\bar k}U_ k. \]

In the product rule for $\mathrm e^\xi Q$, combine the cross term coming from the first sum with $|\partial U|^2|\partial \xi |_ B^2$ and with the square $\sum B_ i|U_{ki}|^2$. This gives $\sum B_ i|U_{ki}+U_ k\xi _ i|^2$, while the remaining weight term is $|\partial \xi |_ B^2$. $\square$

Proposition 10.9.3. There are constants $\eta ,C>0$, depending only on the fixed data and $H_1$, such that every smooth admissible $V$ with $\| U-V\| _\infty \leq \eta $ satisfies

\[ \| \partial U\| _\infty \leq C\bigl (1+\| \partial V\| _\infty ^2\bigr ). \]

The comparison radius $\eta $ is independent of the derivatives of $V$.

Proof. Let $a\geq 1$ and $\beta >1$ be constants, to be chosen below. Assuming $\| U-V\| _\infty \leq \beta ^{-1}$, put

\[ w=U-V-\beta ^{-1},\qquad \xi =a(\underline U-U)+\beta w^2,\qquad K_ V=1+\| \partial \underline U\| _\infty +\| \partial V\| _\infty . \]

Then $-2\beta ^{-1}\leq w\leq 0$ and

\[ \xi _ i=a(\underline U_ i-V_ i)+(-a+2\beta w)(U_ i-V_ i), \qquad |-a+2\beta w|\leq a+4. \]

Writing $D=|\partial (U-V)|_ B^2$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\begin{align*} \left|2\operatorname{Re}\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ iU_ i\xi _{\bar i}\right| & \leq \frac18\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ i^2+C_ aK_ V^4T+C_ a(Q+K_ V^2)D,\\ \left|2\operatorname{Re}\sum _{i,k}B_ i\chi _{i\bar k}U_ k\xi _{\bar i}\right| & \leq \tfrac 12QD+C_ aT+C_ aK_ VQ^{1/2}T. \end{align*}

For the first line, split $\xi _ i$ as above and use $\sum B_ i|U_ i|^2\leq 2D+2K_ V^2T$; then apply Young's inequality to the resulting three products, each absorbing at most $\frac1{24}\sum B_ i\lambda _ i^2$. For the second line, use $|\partial \xi |_ B\leq aK_ VT^{1/2}+(a+4)D^{1/2}$ and the bound for $\chi $. In both lines, $C_ a$ depends on $a$ but not on $\beta $ or on the derivatives of $V$.

By lemma 10.9.1 and $w\leq 0$,

\[ \mathcal L\xi \geq a\mathcal L(\underline U-U)-4H_0+2\beta D. \]

Choose $a$ so large that $(a\kappa -C_ g-2)\tau >4H_0+2H_1+1$, and then $\beta \geq 2C_ a+2$. Combining the preceding bounds with lemma 10.9.2, we obtain, at every point where $Q\geq C'(1+K_ V^4)$ for a suitable constant $C'$,

\begin{align*} \mathrm e^{-\xi }\mathcal L(\mathrm e^\xi Q) & \geq \frac18\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ i^2\\ & \quad +Q\bigl [a\mathcal L(\underline U-U)-(C_ g+2)T +\beta D-(4H_0+2H_1)\bigr ]. \end{align*}

Indeed, the coefficient of $D$ before absorption is $(2\beta -C_ a-1/2)Q-C_ aK_ V^2$, which is at least $\beta Q$ once $C'$ is large enough. The other errors satisfy $C_ aK_ V^4\leq Q$ and $C_ a+C_ aK_ VQ^{1/2}\leq Q$, and they account for the additional term $-2T$ in the bracket.

Consider a maximum point of $\mathrm e^\xi Q$ at which $Q\geq C'(1+K_ V^4)$. In the first alternative of lemma 10.9.1, the bracket is positive by the choice of $a$, since $T\geq \tau $. In the second, $D\geq \kappa T|\partial (U-V)|^2$. For $Q\geq 4(1+K_ V^2)$, the triangle inequality gives $|\partial (U-V)|^2\geq c_*Q$, where $c_*=(\sqrt3-1)^2/4$. Since $\mathcal L(\underline U-U)\geq -H_0$, the bracket is then at least

\[ (\beta \kappa c_*Q-C_ g-2)T-(a+4)H_0-2H_1. \]

This is positive once $C'$ is large enough, because $T\geq \tau $. In both cases $\mathcal L(\mathrm e^\xi Q)>0$ at a maximum point, which contradicts the second derivative test. Thus $Q\leq C(1+K_ V^4)$ at the maximum point. The weight has uniformly bounded oscillation:

\[ \operatorname {osc}_ X\xi \leq a(\operatorname {osc}_ X\underline U+\operatorname {osc}_ XU)+4/\beta . \]

By the potential bound in proposition 10.8.2, this oscillation is bounded independently of $V$, so the estimate at the maximum point gives $Q\leq C(1+K_ V^4)$ everywhere. Taking square roots and setting $\eta =\beta ^{-1}$ proves the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 10.9.4. There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed data and $H_1$, such that $\| \mathrm du\| _{L^\infty (\omega )}\leq C$ for every normalized solution $u$ whose right-hand side $h$ lies in the family and satisfies $\| \partial h\| _\infty \leq H_1$. In particular, the bound is uniform as the positive right-hand sides tend to zero.

Proof. The right-hand sides in the family with $\| \partial h\| _\infty \leq H_1$ form an equicontinuous set. By corollary 10.8.4, the corresponding functions $U$ form a precompact subset of $C^0(X)$. Choose a finite $\eta $-net in this set, consisting of smooth admissible functions $V_1,\ldots ,V_ N$. Each solution is within $\eta $ of some $V_ j$, so proposition 10.9.3 bounds its gradient by $C(1+\max _ j\| \partial V_ j\| _\infty ^2)$. The set of solutions, and hence the chosen finite net, depends only on the fixed data and $H_1$. Finally, $u=2\pi U$, so the same conclusion holds for $\mathrm du$. $\square$

In chapter 11 we return to the Monge–Ampère equation for $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and ask when two potentials with the same density agree up to a constant. The uniqueness of the normalization scalar does not determine the potential: where the density vanishes, the polarized linear equation for the difference can be degenerate.
