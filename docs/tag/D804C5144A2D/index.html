Chapter 10 · Hermitian geometry, torsion, and Gauduchon duality · Tag D804C5144A2D

Semipositive dual exponential limit

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 26.9.11

Proposition 26.9.11. Assume the hypotheses of theorem 26.9.10, and let $u_0$ be its unique threshold solution with $\sup _ Xu_0=0$. For real $\lambda >0$, let $u_\lambda $ be the greatest solution of the exponential equation (14.123), with $\omega $ in place of $h$, given by corollary 14.34.2, and put

\[ v_\lambda =u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda ,\qquad c_\lambda =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu_\lambda }. \]

There is a constant $C$ independent of $\lambda >0$ such that

\[ b\leq c_\lambda \leq b\, \mathrm{e}^{C\lambda },\qquad u_0-C\leq v_\lambda \leq 0. \]

Moreover $v_\lambda \to u_0$ in $L^1(X)$ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$.

Proof. Write $p=f^{1/n}$ and introduce the probability measure

\[ \mathrm{d}\nu =b^{1/n}p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

It has mass one because $T$ maximizes. Its density belongs to $L^ n$, since $p\in L^ n$ and the smooth semipositive form $T$ has bounded determinant density $k_ T$. By lemma 1.5.1, $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions with supremum zero have uniformly bounded moments of every finite order. Hölder's inequality with the exponents $n$ and $n/(n-1)$, which is finite because $n\geq 2$, therefore gives

\[ \int _ X(-v_\lambda )\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq C. \]

Since $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (v_\lambda )=c_\lambda \mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }f\, \mathrm{d}V$, the trace inequality of lemma 6.4.2, applied with the positive regularizations of $T$ and passed to the limit as in the preceding proof, gives $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\lambda )\wedge T\geq (c_\lambda /b)^{1/n}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda /n}\, \nu $. Integrating, we obtain

\[ 1\geq (c_\lambda /b)^{1/n} \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda /n}\, \mathrm{d}\nu \geq (c_\lambda /b)^{1/n}\mathrm{e}^{-C\lambda /n}, \]

where the last step is Jensen's inequality. This proves the upper bound for $c_\lambda $. The lower bound and the comparison

\[ v_\lambda \geq u_0-\lambda ^{-1}\log (c_\lambda /b) \]

follow from corollary 14.34.2 applied with $v=u_0$, and together with the upper bound they give the common lower barrier $u_0-C$.

Take any sequence $\lambda _ j\downarrow 0$. By theorem 1.4.2, a subsequence of $(v_{\lambda _ j})$ converges in $L^1$ to some $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with supremum zero, and proposition 14.27.3 makes $v$ a threshold subsolution. Uniqueness in theorem 26.9.10 therefore gives $v=u_0$. Since every sequence tending to zero has a subsequence with this same $L^1$ limit, the whole family converges. $\square$
