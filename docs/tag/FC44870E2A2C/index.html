14.29 Exact equations on products with zero lower massFC44870E2A2C

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 14.29 Exact equations on products with zero lower mass

Positive lower mass guarantees solvability for all measures, but an individual density can have a positive threshold and an exact equation even when the lower mass vanishes. We construct explicit examples on products. Let $(X_0,h_0)$ be a connected compact Hermitian manifold of dimension $m\geq 1$ with $v_-(h_0)=0$. On its product with a projective space, a singular potential pulled back from the projective factor solves an explicit equation whose density has a sublogarithmic singularity. The coefficient is computed from the normalization scalars of bounded truncations, the equation itself follows from the product structure of the Monge–Ampère measure, and the product background again has zero lower mass.

Throughout this section, $Y=\mathbb P^ k$ with $k\geq 1$, $\kappa $ is the Fubini–Study form normalized by $\int _ Y\kappa ^ k=1$, and $X=X_0\times Y$, a compact complex manifold of dimension $N=m+k$ with the Hermitian background $H=h_0+\kappa $. Forms and functions on the factors are identified with their pullbacks to $X$. Put

\[ V_0=\int _{X_0}h_0^ m,\qquad C=\binom Nm,\qquad \mu =V_0^{-1}h_0^ m\wedge \kappa ^ k,\qquad R=\frac{\mu }{\mathrm{d}V}, \]

where $\mathrm{d}V$ is any smooth positive probability volume form on $X$. Then $\mu $ is a smooth positive probability volume form, and $R$ is smooth and bounded above and below by positive constants. The normalization scalar $c_ H$ and the threshold $b_ H$ are those of section 14.26, section 14.27, now on the $N$-dimensional manifold $X$ with background $H$.

For $0<\alpha <1$ define, on $Y$,

\[ \psi ([z_0:\cdots :z_ k])=\log \frac{\sum _{i=1}^ k|z_ i|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ k|z_ i|^2}, \qquad u_\alpha =1-(1-\psi )^\alpha . \]

The function $\psi $ is smooth on $Y\setminus \{ o\} $, where $o=[1:0:\cdots :0]$, and tends to $-\infty $ at $o$; moreover $\psi \leq 0$, with equality exactly on the hyperplane $\{ z_0=0\} $. Thus $u_\alpha \leq 0$, and $u_\alpha $ has a single pole at $o$, where $u_\alpha (o)=-\infty $.

Lemma 14.29.1. The function $u_\alpha $ is $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic with supremum zero, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\kappa (u_\alpha )=q_\alpha \kappa ^ k, \qquad \int _ Yq_\alpha \kappa ^ k=1. \]

The density $q_\alpha $ is smooth and strictly positive on $Y\setminus \{ o\} $, belongs to $L^1(\kappa ^ k)$, and belongs to no $L^ p(\kappa ^ k)$ with $p>1$. If we set $q_\alpha (o)=+\infty $, then every truncation $\min (q_\alpha ,s)$, $s>0$, is strictly positive and continuous on $Y$.

Proof. In homogeneous coordinates, $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \sum _{i=1}^ k|z_ i|^2\geq 0$ on $Y\setminus \{ o\} $. The function $\chi (t)=1-(1-t)^\alpha $, $t\leq 0$, is increasing and convex, with $0<\chi '\leq \alpha $. Hence, off $o$,

\[ \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\alpha \geq (1-\chi '(\psi ))\kappa +\chi '(\psi )(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi ) \geq (1-\alpha )\kappa . \]

Since $u_\alpha $ is bounded above, it extends across $o$ as a $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic function, with value $-\infty $ at $o$. On the hyperplane $\{ z_0=0\} $ we have $\psi =0$, hence $u_\alpha =0$, so $\sup _ Yu_\alpha =0$.

To compute the measure, work in the affine chart $z_0=1$ and put $r=\sum _{i=1}^ k|z_ i|^2$. On this chart $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+r)$ and $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F$, where $s(r)=1-\log (r/(1+r))$ and

\[ F(r)=\log (1+r)+1-s(r)^\alpha ,\qquad Q(r)=rF'(r)=\frac{r+\alpha s(r)^{\alpha -1}}{1+r}. \]

For a radial function, the Levi form has the tangential eigenvalue $F'(r)=Q(r)/r$, with multiplicity $k-1$, and the radial eigenvalue

\[ Q'(r)=\frac{1-\alpha s(r)^{\alpha -1}+\alpha (1-\alpha )r^{-1}s(r)^{\alpha -2}}{(1+r)^2}>0. \]

Integrating in the radial variable gives $\int _{\{ a<r<b\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F)^ k=Q(b)^ k-Q(a)^ k$; the constant of proportionality is fixed by the case $F=\log (1+r)$, for which $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F)^ k=\kappa ^ k$ has mass one on the chart and $Q(r)=r/(1+r)$. Since again $Q(0)=0$ and $Q(\infty )=1$, the punctured chart has mass one. The complement of the chart is a hyperplane, which has zero volume, and the non-pluripolar measure does not charge $o$. Hence $\int _ Yq_\alpha \kappa ^ k=1$.

Relative to $\kappa ^ k$, whose tangential and radial eigenvalues are $1/(1+r)$ and $1/(1+r)^2$, the density on this chart is

\[ q_\alpha (r)=(1+r)^{k+1}(Q(r)/r)^{k-1}Q'(r) \asymp r^{-k}(1-\log r)^{-1-k(1-\alpha )}\quad (r\downarrow 0). \]

Near $r=0$ the smooth volume is comparable to $r^{k-1}\mathrm{d}r$ in the radial variable, so $q_\alpha $ is integrable. For $p>1$, the substitution $L=1-\log r$ turns the integral of $q_\alpha ^ p$ into the integral of $\mathrm{e}^{k(p-1)L}$ times a power of $L$, which diverges. The same asymptotics show that $q_\alpha (r)\to +\infty $ uniformly as $r\downarrow 0$, so every truncation $\min (q_\alpha ,s)$ is continuous at $o$. Finally, $q_\alpha \geq (1-\alpha )^ k>0$ by the lower bound $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\alpha \geq (1-\alpha )\kappa $. $\square$

Lemma 14.29.2. Let $g$ be a strictly positive continuous function on $Y$, and put $M_ g=\int _ Yg\kappa ^ k$. Then

\[ c_ H(Rg)=\frac{CV_0}{M_ g}. \]

Proof. Suppose first that $g$ is smooth. Since $\kappa $ is Kähler, theorem 9.1.1 on $Y$ gives a smooth $\phi _ g$ with $A_ g=\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi _ g>0$ and $A_ g^ k=(g/M_ g)\kappa ^ k$; the scalar is determined by integration, since both sides have mass one. On $X$, since $h_0^{m+1}=0$ and $A_ g^{k+1}=0$ for dimensional reasons,

\[ (H+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi _ g)^ N=C h_0^ m\wedge A_ g^ k =(CV_0/M_ g)Rg\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Uniqueness of the scalar gives $c_ H(Rg)=CV_0/M_ g$. For continuous $g>0$, choose smooth $g_ l>0$ converging uniformly to $g$. Since $g$ has a positive minimum, $(1-\varepsilon _ l)g\leq g_ l\leq (1+\varepsilon _ l)g$ for some $\varepsilon _ l\in (0,1)$ tending to zero. The order and homogeneity of the scalar in lemma 14.26.1 give

\[ (1-\varepsilon _ l)c_ H(Rg_ l)\leq c_ H(Rg) \leq (1+\varepsilon _ l)c_ H(Rg_ l). \]

Since $M_{g_ l}\to M_ g$, the smooth case proves the formula. $\square$

Theorem 14.29.3. The Hermitian form $H$ is not closed and satisfies $v_-(H)=0$. For every $0<\alpha <1$, the function $f=Rq_\alpha $ is a probability density in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, belonging to no $L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$ with $p>1$, and

\[ b_ H(f)=CV_0>0,\qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(u_\alpha )=b_ H(f)f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

The potential $u_\alpha $, pulled back to $X$, has supremum zero and finite mass $CV_0$. It belongs to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,H,0)$, and $P_{\delta H}(u_\alpha )\not\equiv -\infty $ for every $0<\delta <1$.

Proof. Step 1: the threshold. Write $a=\min _ XR>0$, $A=\max _ XR$, and $M_ s=\int _ Y\min (q_\alpha ,s)\kappa ^ k$ for $s>0$. By lemma 14.29.1, the truncations $\min (q_\alpha ,s)$ are continuous and strictly positive, and $M_ s\uparrow 1$ as $s\to \infty $. The truncations of $f$ satisfy

\[ R\min (q_\alpha ,j/A)\leq \min (Rq_\alpha ,j) \leq R\min (q_\alpha ,j/a). \]

Hence lemma 14.29.2 and the monotonicity of the scalar give

\[ \frac{CV_0}{M_{j/a}}\leq c_ H(\min (f,j)) \leq \frac{CV_0}{M_{j/A}}. \]

Both bounds tend to $CV_0$ as $j\to \infty $, so $b_ H(f)=CV_0$, whatever the reference volume $\mathrm{d}V$.

Step 2: the equation. For $u_{\alpha ,j}=\max (u_\alpha ,-j)$, the binomial expansion of bounded local products, with factor forms of degrees $m$ and $k$, gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_ H(u_{\alpha ,j}) =C h_0^ m\wedge \mathrm{MA}_\kappa (u_{\alpha ,j}). \]

Multiplying by $\mathbf1_{\{ u_\alpha >-j\} }$ and letting $j\to \infty $, as in the definition (6.1) of the extended measure, we obtain

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(u_\alpha )=Ch_0^ m\wedge q_\alpha \kappa ^ k =CV_0\, q_\alpha \mu =CV_0f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

a measure of mass $CV_0$. The bounds $a\leq R\leq A$ transfer the integrability properties of $q_\alpha $ to $f$.

Step 3: lower mass and nonclosedness. Pull back bounded $h_0$-plurisubharmonic functions whose masses on $X_0$ tend to zero. The same expansion shows that their $H$-masses are $C$ times their masses on $X_0$, so $v_-(H)=0$. If $h_0$ were closed, Stokes' theorem would give every bounded $h_0$-plurisubharmonic function the mass $V_0>0$, contradicting $v_-(h_0)=0$. Hence $h_0$, and therefore $H$, is not closed.

Step 4: the envelopes. Choose $\alpha <\beta <1$. For $b\geq 1$ and $\varepsilon >0$ with $\varepsilon \beta \leq 1$, the number

\[ K_ b=\sup _{s\geq 1}\{ b s^\alpha -\varepsilon s^\beta \} -b \]

is finite, since $\beta >\alpha $. The function $g_ b=-\varepsilon (1-\psi )^\beta -K_ b$ on $Y$ satisfies $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_ b\geq (1-\varepsilon \beta )\kappa \geq 0$, by the chain-rule computation in the proof of lemma 14.29.1, applied to the increasing convex function $t\mapsto -\varepsilon (1-t)^\beta $, whose derivative is at most $\varepsilon \beta $ for $t\leq 0$. With $L=1-\psi \geq 1$, the inequality $g_ b\leq bu_\alpha $ reads $bL^\alpha -\varepsilon L^\beta -b\leq K_ b$, so it holds on $Y\setminus \{ o\} $, and also at $o$, where both sides are $-\infty $. Thus the pullback of $g_ b$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,H)$ and lies below $bu_\alpha $. Since $b\geq 1$ is arbitrary, $u_\alpha \in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,H,0)$ by definition 13.4.1. For $0<\delta <1$, take $b=1$ and $\varepsilon \beta \leq \delta $. Then $g_1$ is $\delta H$-plurisubharmonic and lies below $u_\alpha $, so $P_{\delta H}(u_\alpha )\geq g_1$ is not identically $-\infty $. $\square$

Corollary 14.29.4. Let $X_0$ be the elliptic threefold of theorem 26.18.1, with the balanced metric $\omega $ of proposition 26.18.7. On $X_0\times \mathbb P^2$, with background $H=\omega +\kappa $, the densities $f=Rq_\alpha $ of the preceding theorem satisfy

\[ v_-(H)=0,\qquad b_ H(f)=240\pi , \qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(u_\alpha )=240\pi f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Proof. Theorem 26.18.1 gives $v_-(\omega )=0$, and proposition 26.18.7 gives $V_0=24\pi $. The product theorem applies with $m=3$, $k=2$ and $C=\binom 53=10$. $\square$

These examples show that an individual density can have a positive threshold and an exact equation even though the background has zero lower mass. The exactness comes from the product structure. For $k\geq 2$, the radial flux also identifies the normalized exponential limit in theorem 14.35.2; the first moment determines whether scalar centering has a finite limit. 33.1.1 asks for an exact equation for every integrable density with positive threshold.
