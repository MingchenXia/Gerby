Iwasawa fiber invariant density

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 26.5.9

Theorem 26.5.9. For every $A>0$ and fiber-invariant $\mu _ f$,

26.20
\begin{equation} \label{eq:iwasawa-fiber-invariant-volume} \mathcal V_{\mu _ f}(\theta _ A)=H\det A, \qquad H=\frac6{\pi ^3}. \end{equation}

Its unique minimizing Gauduchon metric normalized by $D_{\mu _ f}=1$ is

26.21
\begin{equation} \label{eq:iwasawa-fiber-invariant-minimizer} \gamma _{A,f}=\frac{\theta _ A+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p^*v_{A,f})}{(H\det A)^{1/3}}, \qquad \gamma _{A,f}^3=\mu _ f, \end{equation}

where $v_{A,f}$ is the solution of the Monge–Ampère equation (26.22) on the torus $B$, with a closed background, constructed in the proof. Conversely, for a fixed $A>0$, the probabilities $\nu \in \mathscr M$ maximizing $\mathcal V_\nu (\theta _ A)$ are exactly the fiber-invariant ones.

For each fixed real linear $W\subset \operatorname {Herm}(3)$ containing a positive matrix, the Hessian metric for $\mu _ f$ is

\[ g_ A(U,Z)=\frac13\operatorname {tr}(A^{-1}UA^{-1}Z). \]

Its unit-volume hypersurface $S_ W=\{ A\in W:A>0,\ H\det A=1\} $ is complete and proper, and the Cauchy criterion and the distance bounds of theorem 26.5.5 hold with $n=3$.

Proof. The map $p$ is well defined because multiplication by lattice elements adds Gaussian integers to the first two coordinates. It is a submersion, and fixing representatives of those coordinates identifies each fiber with the central quotient $E$. Integrating first in the third coordinate over the fundamental cube proves the pushforward identity.

The closed equation on the base. Write the positive Hermitian matrix in block form and take its Schur complement:

\[ \begin{gathered} A=\begin{pmatrix} C & b \\ b^* & a \end{pmatrix},\\ a=A_{33}>0,\qquad S=C-a^{-1}bb^*>0. \end{gathered} \]

On $B$, let

\[ \omega _ S=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{j,k=1}^2 S_{jk}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k, \qquad Q_ B=\int _ B\omega _ S^2=\frac2{\pi ^2}\det S. \]

We apply theorem 9.1.1 to this closed background with the density $Q_ Bf\mu _ B$. Since $\omega _ S$ is closed and the density has total mass $Q_ B$, Stokes' theorem forces the constant in the equation to be one. We obtain a smooth real $v=v_{A,f}$, unique up to an additive constant, such that

26.22
\begin{equation} \label{eq:iwasawa-schur-base-equation} (\omega _ S+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^2=Q_ Bf\mu _ B, \qquad \omega _ S+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v>0. \end{equation}

If $K$ is the coefficient matrix of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ in the base coframe, this says $\det (S+K)=(\det S)f$. The form

\[ \Omega =\theta _ A+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p^*v) \]

has coefficient matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}C+K& b\\ b^*& a\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ in the invariant coframe. Since $a>0$ and the Schur complement $S+K$ is positive, $\Omega >0$; its determinant is $a\det (S+K)=(\det A)(f\circ p)$. Consequently $\Omega ^3=(H\det A)\mu _ f$.

This solution form is also balanced. Put $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p^*v)$, which is closed and uses only $\alpha _1,\alpha _2$ and their conjugates. Since $\mathrm d\alpha _3=-\alpha _1\wedge \alpha _2$, every term of $\partial \theta _ A$ contains $\alpha _1\wedge \alpha _2$, and every conjugate term contains $\bar\alpha _1\wedge \bar\alpha _2$. Thus $\mathrm d\theta _ A\wedge \beta =0$. Together with $\mathrm d\theta _ A^2=0$, this proves

\[ \mathrm d\Omega ^2=\mathrm d\theta _ A^2+2\mathrm d\theta _ A\wedge \beta =0. \]

Theorem 26.1.3 now gives (26.20). Its characterization of the minimizers, the balancedness of $\Omega $ and the homogeneity of degree two of $D_{\mu _ f}$ give the unique normalized minimizer (26.21).

All maximizing densities. Let $Z_3=\partial /\partial z_3$ be dual to $\alpha _3$, and set $\tau _ j=(\mathrm i/(2\pi ))\alpha _ j\wedge \bar\alpha _ j$. Directly from (26.19),

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta _ A=A_{33}\tau _1\wedge \tau _2, \qquad h_0^3=6\tau _1\wedge \tau _2\wedge \tau _3. \]

Since $\theta _ A$ is Gauduchon, the identity (26.92) therefore becomes, for every smooth real $u$,

26.23
\begin{equation} \label{eq:iwasawa-vertical-mass-defect} \int _ X(\theta _ A+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3 =H\det A-\frac{A_{33}H}{2}\int _ X|Z_3u|^2\mu _0. \end{equation}

Here $\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u=(\mathrm i/(2\pi ))\partial u\wedge \bar\partial u$, and wedging with $\tau _1\wedge \tau _2$ selects its coefficient on $\alpha _3\wedge \bar\alpha _3$; this also accounts for the factor $1/2$.

For an arbitrary $\nu \in \mathscr M$, let $u_\nu $ be the smooth solution of the equation for $\theta _ A$ and $\nu $. Its mass equals $\mathcal V_\nu (\theta _ A)$ by theorem 26.1.3. By (26.23), this mass equals $H\det A$ exactly when $Z_3u_\nu =0$. As $u_\nu $ is real, its derivatives in both real directions of each elliptic fiber then vanish, and connectedness of the fibers gives $u_\nu =p^*v$ for a smooth $v$ on $B$. The density of $(\theta _ A+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\nu )^3$ relative to $\mu _0$ is then a function on $B$, so the equation makes $\nu $ fiber invariant. The converse was proved by the construction on the base.

Completion in the background parameters. For fixed $f$, formula (26.20) holds on the entire cone of positive matrices, and differentiating the logarithm of its cube root twice gives the stated Hessian metric. Since this is the same metric as in theorem 26.5.5, the matrix-path and coefficient-limit arguments of that proof apply unchanged on each fixed $W$. They give the distance bounds, completeness, properness and the Cauchy criterion; they do not use that the minimizing forms have constant coefficients. $\square$
