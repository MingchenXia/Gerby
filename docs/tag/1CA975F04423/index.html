Theorem 6.1.231CA975F04423

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Theorem 6.1.23

Theorem 6.1.23. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. There is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$;

  2. $\varphi _0\sim _ P\varphi _1$.

Proof. 2 $\implies $ 1. This follows from proposition 4.2.4.

1 $\implies $ 2. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$.

Step 1. We assume that $\varphi _0\geq \varphi _1$.

We first replace the given subgeodesic by the geodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Note that the geodesic is necessarily a subgeodesic as well by 4.2.2.

Observe that $t\mapsto \varphi _ t$ is decreasing due to 4.2.5.

Let $\varphi _ t=\varphi _1$ for all $t>1$. Then by the gluing lemma 4.1.11, we find that $(\varphi _ t)_{t\geq 0}$ is a subgeodesic ray.

Next, we consider the Legendre transform

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }\coloneqq \inf _{t\geq 0}(\varphi _ t-t\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \]

It follows from Kiselman's principle proposition 1.2.11 that $\Gamma _{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $. Note that for $\tau >0$, we clearly have $\Gamma _{\tau }\equiv -\infty $. On the other hand, for $\tau \leq 0$,

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=\inf _{t\in [0,1]}(\varphi _ t-t\tau )\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

By Legendre inversion (cf. corollary A.2.4), for $t>0$,

\[ \varphi _ t=\sup _{\tau <0} \left(\Gamma _{\tau }+t\tau \right). \]

Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. It follows from proposition 6.1.20 that for each $t>0$,

\[ \varphi _ t\sim _ P \operatorname *{sup}_{\tau <0}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma _{\tau }]. \]

The right-hand side is independent of $t$. Here by adding $\omega $, we no longer have to worry about the possibility where $\Gamma _{\tau }$ has vanishing mass.

Write

\[ \varphi _0=\operatorname *{sup}_{t\in (0,1)}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ t. \]

By proposition 6.1.20 again, we find that

\[ \varphi _0\sim _ P \operatorname *{sup}_{\tau <0}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma _{\tau }] \]

as well. So $\varphi _0\sim _ P \varphi _1$.

Step 2. We prove the general case.

Observe that $(\varphi _ t\lor \varphi _1)_{t\in (0,1)}$ is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0\lor \varphi _1$ to $\varphi _1$. By Step 1,

\[ \varphi _0\lor \varphi _1\sim _ P \varphi _1. \]

Hence $\varphi _0\preceq _ P \varphi _1$ by 6.1.8. The converse is proved similarly. Hence 2 follows. $\square$
