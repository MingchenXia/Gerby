Equations with prescribed data

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Equations with prescribed data

The varying mass also changes the normalization of an equation. Given a smooth real function $F$ on $X$, one seeks a smooth pair $(u,c)$ satisfying

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=c\, \mathrm e^ F\, \mathrm dV, \qquad \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0,\qquad \sup _ Xu=0. \]

In a Kähler background, integration determines $c>0$ before solving for $u$. In a Hermitian background the left-hand mass depends on the unknown potential, so the constant must be found together with it. Such a smooth pair exists and is unique. The linearization gives an adjoint Gauduchon probability measure describing the variation of $\log c$ with the density, and leads to a variational volume defined by Gauduchon test forms. This volume remains finite even on manifolds with unbounded upper Monge–Ampère mass.

Positive variational volume, however, need not imply that a background is big, meaning that it admits a potential $\rho $ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \omega $ for some $\delta >0$. A nef nonclosed background on a projective manifold has positive variational volume and fails this condition (theorem 26.13.2). Up to a smooth change of representative, the same example occurs at a finite maximal Chern–Ricci time (theorem 26.20.5). An argument for geometric convergence that needs bigness therefore cannot substitute positive variational volume for it.

For a nonnegative, nonzero $L^ p$ density, $p>1$, continuous solutions still exist and the positive constant is still unique, but uniqueness of the potential is a separate matter. A smooth density with zeros may have distinct normalized smooth solutions. For a continuous density whose positive set is connected and dense, bounded solutions are unique up to constants (theorem 11.4.1). Selection by approximation must also be distinguished from uniqueness. Moreover, when singularities are prescribed, distinct divisorial types can have the same normalization functional and supporting Gauduchon probabilities (chapter 15); the scalar response does not recover the full type.

Replacing the determinant by an intermediate elementary symmetric polynomial leads to the Hessian measure $H_ m(u)=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m}$, for $2\leq m<n$. Its admissible cone permits negative eigenvalues of the perturbed form, so bounded products require capacity estimates beyond the plurisubharmonic calculus. Derivatives of the background again obstruct the usual comparison inequality. Rescaling one potential and working on a sufficiently small sublevel set, however, allows the torsion error to be absorbed. The resulting modified comparison gives strict domination on an arbitrary Hermitian background. For example, for every $a>0$ and smooth real $F$, the equation $H_ m(u)=\mathrm e^{au+F}\omega ^ n$ has exactly one bounded admissible solution, and it is smooth (12.12.10). Thus the increasing dependence on the potential can recover uniqueness and regularity even when mass is not conserved.

To prescribe singularities as well as the measure, one fixes a reference potential and uses relative envelopes to control the solution. For general finite non-pluripolar measures, 9 proves existence in two regimes: semipositive big backgrounds of positive lower mass, with a model reference admitting a competitor in a smaller background, and arbitrary smooth big backgrounds of bounded mass, with a model reference of positive lower volume. These solutions belong to a relative envelope class. Membership in the prescribed ordinary type requires the stronger conclusion that their difference from the reference is bounded; additional estimates give this conclusion for suitable data.
