Theorem 9.2.9

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Theorem 9.2.9

Theorem 9.2.9. The Legendre transform in definition 9.2.1 is a bijection

9.21
\begin{equation} \label{eq:RWNco} \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi ). \end{equation}

Moreover, this bijection restricts to the following bijections:

9.22
\begin{equation} \label{eq:RWNcotwocases} \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi ),\quad \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi ). \end{equation}

For any $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we have

9.23
\begin{equation} \label{eq:RWNenergy} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )=\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ^*). \end{equation}

Proof. Step 1. We first establish (9.21).

It follows from 9.2.7 that the forward map is well-defined. The inverse map is given by (9.15). We show that the inverse map is also well-defined. Given $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we know from 9.2.8 that $\ell ^*\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We need to show that $\ell ^*\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

The equality $\ell ^*_{-\infty }=\phi $ was proved in 9.2.8. Therefore, $\ell ^*\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ as expected.

The two operations are inverse to each other thanks to corollary A.2.4. Hence, (9.21) is established.

Step 2. Next we consider the bounded situation. Namely, we want to establish the second half of (9.22).

Suppose that $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Take $\tau _0\in \mathbb {R}$ so that $\Gamma _{\tau }=\phi $ for all $\tau \leq \tau _0$. It follows from (9.9) that

\[ \Gamma _ t^*\geq \phi +t\tau _0 \]

for all $t>0$. Therefore, $\Gamma ^*_ t\sim \phi $ for all $t>0$ and hence $\Gamma ^*\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Conversely, suppose that $\ell \in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Thanks to proposition 4.2.7, there is a constant $C>0$ such that

\[ \ell _ t\geq \phi -Ct. \]

Therefore, according to (9.15), we have

\[ \ell ^*_{\tau }\geq \inf _{t>0} \Bigl ( \phi -(C+\tau )t \Bigr )=\phi \]

if $\tau \leq -C$. Therefore, $\ell ^*_{\tau }=\phi $ for all $\tau \leq -C$.

Step 3. We establish (9.23) and the first half of (9.22).

Step 3.1. We reduce to the case where $\Gamma _{\max }=0$.

Suppose that we define

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }=\Gamma _{\tau +\Gamma _{\max }},\quad \forall \tau <0. \]

Then $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ as well and for all $t>0$,

\[ \Gamma '^*_ t=\sup _{\tau <0} \left( t\tau +\Gamma '_{\tau } \right)=\sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left( t\tau +\Gamma _{\tau } \right)-t\Gamma _{\max }=\Gamma ^*_ t-t\Gamma _{\max }. \]

Therefore,

\[ \mathbf{E}^{\phi }\left(\Gamma '^*\right)=\mathbf{E}^{\phi }\left(\Gamma ^*\right)-\Gamma _{\max }\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n. \]

by (3.71). Using (9.5), we also have

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ')=& \int _{-\infty }^0 \left( \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma '_{\tau }}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n \right)\, \mathrm{d}\tau \\ =& \int _{-\infty }^{\Gamma _{\max }}\left( \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau }}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n \right)\, \mathrm{d}\tau \\ =& \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )-\Gamma _{\max }\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, it suffices to establish (9.23) for $\Gamma '$ in place of $\Gamma $.

Step 3.2. We assume that $\Gamma _{\max }=0$ and $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We prove (9.23).

For $N\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, $M \in \mathbb {Z}$, we introduce the following:

\[ \Gamma ^{*,N,M}_ t \coloneqq \max _{\substack {k \in \mathbb {Z} \\ k \leq M}} \left(\Gamma _{k/2^ N} + tk/2^ N\right)\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi ),\quad t>0. \]

We first claim that for all $t>0$, $N\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ and $M\in \mathbb {Z}_{<0}$,

9.27
\begin{equation} \label{eq:diff_eq_I} \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}}\leq E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}\right)-E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\leq \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{M/2^ N}}. \end{equation}

Assuming this, let us prove (9.23).

Fixing $N$, let $M=\lfloor 2^{N}\Gamma _{\min }\rfloor $. Recall that $\Gamma _{\min }$ is defined in (9.4). Then repeated applications of (9.27) yield

\[ \sum _{j=M+1}^0 \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{j/2^ N}}\leq E^{\phi }_{\theta }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,0}\right)-E^{\phi }_{\theta }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\leq \sum _{j=M}^{-1} \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{j/2^ N}}. \]

Since $M \leq 2^{N}\Gamma _{\min }$, we have

\[ \Gamma ^{*,N,M}_ t =\phi +t M/2^ N. \]

Using (3.71), we can continue to write

\[ \sum _{j=M+1}^0 \frac{t}{2^ N} \left(\int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{j/2^ N}} - \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\phi }\right)\leq E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,0}\right)\leq \sum _{j=M}^{-1} \frac{t}{2^ N} \left(\int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{j/2^ N}} - \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\phi }\right). \]

We now notice that we have Riemann sums on both the left and right of the above inequality. Moreover, as $N\to \infty $, the potentials $\Gamma _ t^{*,N,0}$ increase to $\Gamma _ t^*$, hence their energies converge to $E^\phi _\theta (\Gamma _ t^*)$ by proposition 3.1.47. Using proposition 9.1.4, it is possible to let $N \to \infty $ and obtain

9.28
\begin{equation} \label{eq:EGammastartt} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\Gamma _ t^{*})=t\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ) \end{equation}

So (9.23) follows as desired.

It remains to argue (9.27). Fix $t>0$, $N\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ and $M\in \mathbb {Z}$. By proposition 3.1.46,

9.29
\begin{equation} \label{eq:first_I_ineq} \begin{aligned} \int _ X \left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}-\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}}\leq & E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}\right)-E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\\ \leq & \int _ X \left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}-\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}}. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

Clearly $\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1} \geq \Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}$. Moreover, since $\mathbb {R}\ni \tau \mapsto \Gamma _{\tau }+t\tau $ is concave, we notice that

\[ U_ t \coloneqq \left\{ \Gamma ^{*,N,M+1}_ t>\Gamma ^{*,N,M}_ t\right\} = \left\{ \Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N} + 2^{-N}t >\Gamma _{M/2^ N}\right\} , \]

and on $U_ t$ we have

9.32
\begin{equation} \label{eq:GammstaronUntemp1} \Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1} = \Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N} + t (M+1)/2^{N},\quad \Gamma _ t^{*,N,M} = \Gamma _{M/2^ N} + t M /2^{N}. \end{equation}

We also note that $U_ t$ is $\mathcal{F}$-open by corollary 1.3.16. So from the lower bound in (9.29), we have

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}\right)-E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\geq & \int _{U_ t} \left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}-\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}}\\ = & \int _{U_ t} \left(\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}-\Gamma _{M/2^ N}+t2^{-N}\right)\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}}\\ \geq & \int _{\left\{ \Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}=0\right\} }t2^{-N}\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}}, \end{aligned} \]

where on the second line, we applied (9.32) and proposition 2.2.2, on the third line, we applied the fact that $\theta _{\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}}^ n$ is supported on the set (cf. theorem 3.1.7)

\[ \left\{ \Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}=0\right\} \subseteq U_ t\cap \left\{ \Gamma _{M/2^ N}=0\right\} . \]

When $(M+1)/2^ N=0$, we use proposition 3.1.25 to see that $P_\theta [\Gamma _0]=\Gamma _0$, since $\Gamma _0$ is the decreasing limit of the model potentials $\Gamma _\tau $ as $\tau \nearrow 0$. Thus theorem 3.1.7 applies to this endpoint as well.

We have deduced the first inequality in (9.27). Next, we apply the upper bound part in (9.29) and compute similarly

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1})-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M})\leq & \int _ X \left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}-\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}}\\ =& \int _{U_ t} \left(\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}-\Gamma _{M/2^ N}+t2^{-N}\right)\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{M/2^ N}}\\ \leq & \int _{\{ \Gamma _{M/2^ N}=0\} \cap U_ t}\left(\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}+t2^{-N}\right)\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{M/2^ N}} \\ \leq & \int _{\{ \Gamma _{M/2^ N}=0\} \cap U_ t} t2^{-N}\, \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{M/2^ N}}. \end{aligned} \]

We conclude the latter half of (9.27).

Step 3.3. We assume that $\Gamma _{\max }=0$. Now $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ only.

For each $\epsilon >0$, we introduce $\Gamma ^{\epsilon }\in \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$ as follows:

  1. Let $\Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\max }=0$, and

  2. we set

    \[ \Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\tau }= \begin{cases} \phi ,& \textup{if }\tau \leq -\epsilon ^{-1};\\ P_{\theta }\left[ (1+\epsilon \tau )\Gamma _{\tau }-\epsilon \tau \phi \right],& \textup{if }\tau \in \left(-\epsilon ^{-1},0\right). \end{cases} \]

The concavity and monotonicity in $\tau $ follow from proposition 3.1.24. The value at $-\infty $ is $\phi $. Moreover, $\Gamma ^\epsilon _\tau =\phi $ for $\tau \leq -\epsilon ^{-1}$, so $\Gamma ^\epsilon \in \mathrm{TC}^\infty (X,\theta ;\phi )$.

It follows from corollary 6.2.20 and corollary 6.2.11 that for each $\tau <0$, the potentials $\Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\tau }$ decrease to $\Gamma _{\tau }$ as $\epsilon \searrow 0$. Applying proposition 3.1.26, we have

\[ \lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\tau }\right)^ n=\int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{\tau }\right)^ n \]

for all $\tau <0$. Hence, by the monotone convergence theorem and Step 3.2, we find

9.42
\begin{equation} \label{eq:EphiGammatemp1} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )=\lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ^{\epsilon })=\lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ^{\epsilon *})=\lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\Gamma ^{\epsilon *}_1), \end{equation}

where the last equality follows from (9.28). Furthermore, according to proposition A.2.10, we have

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t=\inf _{\epsilon >0} \Gamma ^{\epsilon *}_ t \]

for all $t>0$. Note that we do not have to take the closure of the right-hand side since it is automatically upper semicontinuous in $t$.

Now suppose that $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then by (9.42), as $\epsilon \to 0+$, $(\Gamma _ t^{\epsilon *})_{\epsilon }$ is a decreasing Cauchy net in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and hence by theorem 4.3.15 for each $t>0$,

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma ^*_ t\right)=\lim _{\epsilon \to 0+}E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma ^{\epsilon ,*}_ t\right)=t\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )>-\infty , \]

where we have applied (9.28) and (9.42). Hence, $\Gamma ^*\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Moreover, (9.23) follows.

Conversely, suppose that $\Gamma ^*\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then (9.42) implies that

\[ \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )=\lim _{\epsilon \to 0+} E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma ^{\epsilon *}_1\right) \geq E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma ^*_1\right)>-\infty . \]

Hence, $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. $\square$
