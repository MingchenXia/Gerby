Cotangent extensions with zero ruling component

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.19.13 Cotangent extensions with zero ruling component

Every Kähler class has a nonzero ruling component, but the extensions with $\rho =0$, that is, with class $Kf^*\eta $, can be treated in the same way. Their section algebras are the same as for $\rho \neq 0$, but their torsors behave differently: they contain compact rational curves, and their universal covers are not Stein.

Keep the notation of the preceding subsections, with the class $\eta $ and the form $\vartheta _ E$ of lemma 26.19.35. Let $W$ be an extension of $\mathcal O_ M$ by $\Omega _ M^1$ with class $Kf^*\eta $, and put $V=W^*$. The homogeneous charts used above, now with zero ruling component, have transitions

\[ v=1/t,\qquad u_\infty =u,\qquad y_\infty =-t^2y,\qquad w_\infty =w. \]

For $\lambda =m\tau +n$, the lattice acts by

\[ (z,t,u,y,w)\longmapsto (z+\lambda ,b_\lambda t,u+m(q_0ty+Kw),y/b_\lambda ,w), \qquad q_0=2\pi \mathrm{i}d. \]

For the differences $z_ i-z_ j=m_{ij}\tau +n_{ij}$ of local lifts, the zero sections of these charts differ by $Km_{ij}f^*\vartheta _ E$ on overlaps over the base and agree on the two ruling charts, so they give the class $Kf^*\eta $. The linear parts are the cotangent transitions, and in the infinite ruling chart the lattice acts holomorphically by $u_\infty \mapsto u_\infty +m(Kw-q_0v y_\infty )$. Thus the same cocycle computation constructs $W$ also when its ruling component is zero. The fiberwise change of coordinates used for $\rho \ne 0$ is not available, but the polynomial functions on the cotangent bundle give the homogeneous algebra directly.

Lemma 26.19.46. Over $\mathbb C\times \mathbb P^1$, the graded algebra of fiber-homogeneous polynomial functions on the pullback of $W$ is

\[ \mathcal O(\mathbb C)[c_-,c_+,h,u,w]/(c_-c_+-h^2), \qquad c_-=y,\quad c_+=t^2y,\quad h=ty, \]

where all five variables have degree one. The lattice fixes $h$ and $w$, sends $c_-$ to $b_\lambda ^{-1}c_-$ and $c_+$ to $b_\lambda c_+$, and translates $u$ by $m(q_0h+Kw)$.

Proof. As in the proof of lemma 26.19.39, holomorphy in the infinite ruling chart bounds the degree in $t$ of the coefficient of $y^ j$ by $2j$; the variable $w$ does not change between the charts. Distributing the exponent $a$ among $j$ integers in $\{ 0,1,2\} $, every monomial $t^ ay^ j$ with $0\leq a\leq 2j$ is a product of $y$, $ty$ and $t^2y$. These generators satisfy $y(t^2y)=(ty)^2$.

In degree $j$, the space of functions of $y$ and $t$ has dimension $2j+1$. The polynomial algebra in three variables modulo the nonzero quadratic relation has the same dimension

\[ \binom {j+2}{2}-\binom {j}{2}=2j+1, \]

where the second term is zero for $j<2$, so the natural surjective graded map is an isomorphism. Adjoining the two trivial fiber coordinates $u,w$ gives the stated algebra, with entire coefficients in $z$. In the other chart,

\[ c_-=-v^2y_\infty ,\qquad c_+=-y_\infty , \qquad h=-v y_\infty , \]

so all generators are holomorphic there. Substitution gives their lattice transformations. $\square$

Proposition 26.19.47. There is a degree-preserving isomorphism of algebras

\[ \bigoplus _{r\geq 0}H^0(M,\operatorname {Sym}^ rV) \simeq \left(\bigoplus _{r\geq 0}H^0(M,\operatorname {Sym}^ rT_ M)\right)[w], \qquad \deg w=1. \]

Consequently the dimension formula, the bigness and volume of the tautological line bundle, and the failure of finite generation in corollary 26.19.45 hold also for this extension.

Proof. Give $c_-,c_+$ weights $-1,1$, and $h,u,w$ weight zero. Using $c_-c_+=h^2$, the blocks of negative weight have the form $c_-^ kF(z,h,u,w)$, those of positive weight the form $c_+^ kF(z,h,u,w)$, and the block of weight zero consists of the polynomials $F$. These expressions are unique: the substitution $c_+=h^2/c_-$ embeds the algebra into $\mathcal O(\mathbb C)[h,u,w,c_-,c_-^{-1}]$, and distinct weights correspond to distinct powers of $c_-$ with nonzero polynomial coefficients. In tensor degree $r$, the remaining polynomial degree is $r-k$.

The coefficient bundle $U_3$ now has the inverse translation $u\mapsto u-m(q_0h+Kw)$. Thus the block of weight $-k$ is $H^0(E,A^ k\otimes \operatorname {Sym}^{r-k}U_3)$, the block of weight $k$ has $A^{-k}$ in place of $A^ k$, and the block of weight zero is $H^0(E,\operatorname {Sym}^ rU_3)$. Put $H=h+(K/q_0)w$. Since $q_0\ne 0$, this identifies $U_3$ with the bundle $U$ defined above, with $H$ in place of its Euler variable, plus the trivial line bundle generated by $w$.

By lemma 26.19.40, all positive weights vanish, and the algebra of weight zero is $\mathbb C[H,w]$. Expanding the negative blocks in powers of $w$ identifies them with the blocks for the tangent bundle in lemma 26.19.39, and the substitutions $h\mapsto H$ and $y^ k\mapsto c_-^ k$ give bijections in every degree and surviving weight. Products multiply the coefficient polynomials and add the powers of $c_-$, so these bijections preserve multiplication. This proves the isomorphism. Summing the dimensions for the tangent bundle and taking the quotient by $w$ gives the remaining assertions, exactly as in the proof of corollary 26.19.45. $\square$

Proposition 26.19.48. The cotangent torsor $T_ W$ is not Stein. Its universal cover is

\[ \mathbb C^2\times \operatorname {Tot} (\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)), \]

where $\operatorname {Tot}$ denotes the total space of a line bundle; this cover is not Stein either.

Proof. Set $w=1$. The subset $\{ y=0\} $ of the finite ruling chart agrees with $\{ y_\infty =0\} $ in the other chart, and the lattice acts on it by $u\mapsto u+Km$. It therefore descends to a submanifold of $T_ W$ that is a $\mathbb P^1$-bundle over

\[ B_ K=\mathbb C^2_{z,u}/\Lambda ,\qquad (m\tau +n)\cdot (z,u)=(z+m\tau +n,u+Km); \]

this quotient is a complex manifold because translation in $z$ gives a free and properly discontinuous action. Every fiber of this bundle is a compact connected curve in $T_ W$, on which holomorphic functions are constant. Thus $T_ W$ is not holomorphically separable, and hence not Stein.

Before the lattice quotient, the affine charts have no affine term in the ruling direction, and they glue to $\mathbb C^2_{z,u}\times \operatorname {Tot}(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2))$, since $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)$ is the cotangent bundle of $\mathbb P^1$. This space is simply connected: contracting the fibers of the line bundle retracts the last factor onto $\mathbb P^1$, and $\mathbb C^2$ is contractible. The lattice acts freely and properly discontinuously, so this space is the universal cover. Its zero section contains a compact projective line, which again obstructs holomorphic separability and hence the Stein property. $\square$

Thus the classes with zero and with nonzero ruling component have isomorphic graded section algebras, although theorem 26.19.38 gives a Stein universal cover only when the ruling component is nonzero.
