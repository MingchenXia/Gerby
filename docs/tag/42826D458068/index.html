The energy of the difference

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 11.4.1 The energy of the difference

In this subsection, $u,v$ are continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions with $A^ n=B^ n=F\omega ^ n$ for a bounded measurable function $F\geq 0$, and $w=u-v$. Although the cofactor $T$ is now only a current, its product with $\mathrm dw\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w$ still defines a positive measure. The identity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$ determines how this energy is distributed over the level sets of $w$.

Proposition 11.4.3. There is a finite positive measure $E=\mathrm{d} w\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge T$, defined below as a limit of regularizations, with the following properties.

For every smooth real function $\chi $ on $\mathbb R$,

11.27
\begin{equation} \label{eq:weak-energy-chain} \int _ X\chi ''(w)\, \mathrm{d} E=\int _ X\chi (w)\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T. \end{equation}

The signed current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T$ is a finite measure. If $a=\min _ Xw$, $b=\max _ Xw$, and

\[ k(t)=\int _ X(w-t)_+\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T, \]

then $k$ is nonnegative, globally Lipschitz, supported on $[a,b]$, and

11.28
\begin{equation} \label{eq:weak-energy-pushforward} w_*E=k(t)\, \mathrm{d} t. \end{equation}

In particular, $E$ charges no level set of $w$.

Let $V$ be a relatively compact coordinate domain such that $F$ has a positive essential lower bound on a neighborhood of $\overline V$. Then there is $C_ V<\infty $ such that every piecewise $C^1$ Lipschitz function $p$ on $[a,b]$ satisfies

11.29
\begin{equation} \label{eq:weak-local-coercivity} \int _ V|\mathrm{d}(p(w))|\, \mathrm{d}\lambda \leq C_ V\left(\int _ V|p'(w)|^2\, \mathrm{d} E\right)^{1/2}, \end{equation}

where $\mathrm{d}\lambda $ is Euclidean coordinate volume.

Proof. Locally, expand $T$ into powers of the smooth form $\omega $ multiplied by closed Bedford–Taylor products of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$. The operator $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ then acts only on the powers of $\omega $, and the local mass bounds in section 1.6 show that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T$ is a finite signed measure.

We first construct $E$ and prove (11.27). On a coordinate ball, convolve $u$, $v$ and the coefficients of $\omega $ with a common nonnegative radial mollifier. Write the resulting smooth positive forms as $A_\rho =\omega _\rho +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\rho $ and $B_\rho =\omega _\rho +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\rho $, and let $T_\rho $ be their mixed sum. On a slightly larger ball, add the same fixed multiple of $|z|^2$ to each potential to obtain plurisubharmonic functions. Their radial regularizations decrease to the originals, so Bedford–Taylor continuity applies to all closed Hessian products. Since $u,v$ are continuous, $u_\rho ,v_\rho ,w_\rho $ converge uniformly on compact subsets.

For a closed smooth product $S_\rho $ appearing in $T_\rho $, the identity

11.30
\begin{equation} \label{eq:weak-energy-regularization} \mathrm{d} w_\rho \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w_\rho \wedge S_\rho =\tfrac 12\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w_\rho ^2S_\rho )-w_\rho \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\rho \wedge S_\rho \end{equation}

has a distributional limit: both Hessian products on the right converge weakly with uniformly bounded local variation, and their weights converge uniformly. The same holds after multiplication by the convergent smooth factors coming from $\omega _\rho $. Thus the positive measures $E_\rho =\mathrm{d} w_\rho \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w_\rho \wedge T_\rho $ converge to a finite positive measure $E$. By (11.30), this limit does not depend on the regularization, so the local limits agree on overlapping charts.

For smooth regularizations, the chain rule gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi (w_\rho )\wedge T_\rho =\chi ''(w_\rho )E_\rho +\chi '(w_\rho )\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\rho \wedge T_\rho . \]

The last current tends to zero, because $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\rho \wedge T_\rho =A_\rho ^ n-B_\rho ^ n$ tends weakly to $A^ n-B^ n=0$ with uniformly bounded variation, and $\chi '(w_\rho )$ converges uniformly. Passing to the limit and integrating by parts on the compact manifold $X$ gives (11.27). A partition of unity justifies this passage without a global regularization; the terms containing derivatives of the cutoff functions cancel after summation.

Let $\sigma =w_*(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T)$ and $\nu =w_*E$. Equation (11.27) says that $\nu ''=\sigma $ as distributions on $\mathbb R$. Stokes' theorem and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$ give

\[ \sigma (\mathbb R)=\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0,\qquad \int _{\mathbb R}s\, \mathrm{d}\sigma (s)=\int _ Xw\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0. \]

Consequently $k(t)=\int (s-t)_+\, \mathrm{d}\sigma (s)$ is globally Lipschitz and vanishes outside $[a,b]$. It satisfies $k''=\sigma $, so the compactly supported distribution $\nu -k(t)\, \mathrm{d} t$ has second derivative zero and therefore vanishes. Positivity of $\nu $ and continuity of $k$ imply $k\geq 0$. This proves (11.28), and the assertion about level sets follows because $\nu $ is absolutely continuous.

We now prove (11.29). On a coordinate neighborhood of $\overline V$, set $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and choose $c_ V>0$ with $F\omega ^ n\geq c_ V\beta ^ n$. Let $A_{\mathrm{ac}}$ denote the absolutely continuous part of the current $A$, whose coefficients are locally integrable functions. Applying lemma 1.7.7 with the constant function $g=c_ V^{1/n}$ and differentiating the coefficient measures, we obtain $\det _\beta A_{\mathrm{ac}}\geq c_ V$ almost everywhere. The local Sobolev regularity in section 1.6 gives $w_\rho \to w$ strongly in $W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, so a subsequence of the gradients converges almost everywhere. Moreover, $A_\rho \to A_{\mathrm{ac}}$ almost everywhere, by differentiation of the coefficient measures.

Since $T_\rho $ contains the term $A_\rho ^{n-1}$ and all its terms are positive, Fatou's lemma, applied to $E_\rho $, yields the measure inequality

11.31
\begin{equation} \label{eq:weak-energy-ac-lower} E\geq \mathrm{d} w\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge A_{\mathrm{ac}}^{n-1}. \end{equation}

The cofactor matrix of $A_{\mathrm{ac}}$ has smallest eigenvalue at least $\det A_{\mathrm{ac}}/\operatorname {tr}A_{\mathrm{ac}}\geq c_ V/\operatorname {tr}A_{\mathrm{ac}}$, where traces and determinants are taken with respect to $\beta $. The trace is locally integrable because $A$ has locally finite mass. The chain rule for Sobolev functions, (11.31) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give

\[ \int _ V|\mathrm{d}(p(w))|\, \mathrm{d}\lambda \leq C\left(\int _ V|p'(w)|^2\, \mathrm{d} E\right)^{1/2} \left(\int _ V\frac{\operatorname {tr}A_{\mathrm{ac}}}{c_ V}\, \mathrm{d}\lambda \right)^{1/2}. \]

The last factor is finite and is absorbed into $C_ V$. The values of $p'$ at the finitely many corners of $p$ are immaterial, because $E$ charges no level set of $w$. $\square$

Lemma 11.4.4. In the setting of proposition 11.4.3, assume in addition that $F$ is continuous and that $U=\{ F>0\} $ is connected and dense. If $w=u-v$ is nonconstant, then

\[ F\omega ^ n(\{ w=\min _ Xw\} )=F\omega ^ n(\{ w=\max _ Xw\} )=0. \]

Proof. Put $a=\min _ Xw<b=\max _ Xw$, and let $k$ be the function of proposition 11.4.3. It is Lipschitz and nonnegative, and $k(a)=0$. Choose $\epsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ with $a+\epsilon _ j<b$. For $\delta >0$, set

\[ J_ j(\delta )=\int _ a^{a+\epsilon _ j}\frac{\mathrm{d} t}{k(t)+\delta }. \]

If $L$ is a Lipschitz constant for $k$, then $k(t)\leq L(t-a)$ on this interval, so $J_ j(\delta )\to \infty $ as $\delta \downarrow 0$. Choose $\delta _ j>0$ with $J_ j=J_ j(\delta _ j)\geq j$. Let $p_ j=0$ on $(-\infty ,a]$, $p_ j=1$ on $[a+\epsilon _ j,\infty )$, and

\[ p_ j'(t)=\frac{1}{J_ j(k(t)+\delta _ j)}\quad (a<t<a+\epsilon _ j). \]

The pushforward identity (11.28) gives

\[ \int _ X|p_ j'(w)|^2\, \mathrm{d} E =\frac{1}{J_ j^2}\int _ a^{a+\epsilon _ j}\frac{k(t)}{(k(t)+\delta _ j)^2}\, \mathrm{d} t \leq \frac1{J_ j}\longrightarrow 0. \]

Every point of $U$ has a coordinate neighborhood $V$ as in proposition 11.4.3, since $F$ is continuous. By (11.29), therefore, $\mathrm{d}(p_ j(w))\to 0$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. The bounded functions $p_ j(w)$ converge pointwise and in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ to $\mathbf1_{\{ w>a\} }$, whose distributional gradient therefore vanishes on $U$. Since $U$ is connected, this indicator function is almost everywhere constant on $U$. As $w$ is nonconstant, $\{ w>a\} $ is a nonempty open set, and its intersection with the dense open set $U$ is nonempty and open; hence the constant is one. Thus $\{ w=a\} \cap U$ has zero volume, and since $F=0$ off $U$, the first assertion follows. Applying the argument to $-w$ gives the assertion for the maximum set. $\square$
