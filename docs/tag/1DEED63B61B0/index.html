From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Proposition 6.2.12

Proposition 6.2.12. Let $\varphi _ j,\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ ($j\geq 1$), $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Assume that there is $\delta >0$ such that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\geq \delta \]

for all $j$ and the $\varphi _ j$'s are all model potentials. Then up to replacing $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ by a subsequence, there is a decreasing sequence $(\psi _ j)_ j$ and an increasing sequence $(\eta _ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

  1. $\psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $, $\eta _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $;

  2. $\psi _ j\geq \varphi _ j\geq \eta _ j$ for all $j$.

In fact, for any $j\geq 1$, we will take

\[ \eta _ j=\inf _{k\in \mathbb {N}}\varphi _ j\land \varphi _{j+1}\land \cdots \land \varphi _{j+k},\quad \psi _ j=\operatorname *{sup}_{k\geq j}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ k. \]

Proof. First note that $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n\geq \delta $ as well, due to the $d_ S$-continuity of non-pluripolar masses as proved in corollary 6.2.10. Replacing $\varphi $ by $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$, we may then assume that $\varphi $ is a model potential in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

We are free to replace $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ by a subsequence. So we may assume that

6.32
\begin{equation} d_ S(\varphi _ j,\varphi _{j+1})\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-4}}{D_ n C_ n^{j+1}},\quad d_ S(\varphi ,\varphi _ j)\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-4}}{D_ n}, \end{equation}

where $C_ n$ is the constant in corollary 6.2.7, $D_ n$ is the constant in corollary 6.2.10.

In particular, by corollary 6.2.10,

6.33
\begin{equation} \left|\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n\right|\leq \delta 2^{-j-4}. \end{equation}

Step 1. We handle the $\psi _ j$'s. For each $j\geq 1$ and $k\geq 1$, by 6.2.3 we have

\[ \begin{aligned} d_ S(\varphi _ j,\varphi _ j\lor \varphi _{j+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{j+k}) & \leq C_ n\, d_ S(\varphi _ j,\varphi _{j+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{j+k})\\ & \leq C_ n\, d_ S(\varphi _ j,\varphi _{j+1})\\ & \quad + C_ n\, d_ S(\varphi _{j+1},\varphi _{j+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{j+k}). \end{aligned} \]

By iteration, we find

\[ \begin{split} d_ S(\varphi _ j,\varphi _ j\lor \varphi _{j+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{j+k})\leq \sum _{a=j}^{j+k-1}C_ n^{a+1-j}d_ S(\varphi _ a,\varphi _{a+1})\\ \leq \frac{C_ n^{-j}}{D_ n}\sum _{a=j}^{j+k-1}\delta 2^{-a-4}\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-3}}{D_ n}. \end{split} \]

Using corollary 6.2.9, we have

\[ \varphi _ j\lor \varphi _{j+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{j+k}\xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi _ j \]

as $k\to \infty $. Hence

6.39
\begin{equation} d_ S(\varphi _ j,\psi _ j)\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-3}}{D_ n}. \end{equation}

We conclude that $\psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $.

Moreover, we observe that

6.40
\begin{equation} \varphi =\inf _{j\geq 1} P_{\theta }[\psi _ j] \end{equation}

Indeed, set $\tilde{\psi }_ j=P_{\theta }[\psi _ j]$. Then $(\tilde{\psi }_ j)_ j$ is a decreasing sequence of model potentials, and $d_ S(\tilde{\psi }_ j,\psi _ j)=0$ by proposition 6.2.4. Hence $\tilde{\psi }_ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. If $\chi =\inf _ j\tilde{\psi }_ j$, then proposition 3.1.26 and corollary 6.2.11 give $\tilde{\psi }_ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\chi $. Thus $d_ S(\chi ,\varphi )=0$. Since both $\chi $ and $\varphi $ are model potentials, proposition 6.2.4 gives $P_{\theta }[\chi ]=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$, hence $\chi =\varphi $, proving (6.40).

Step 2. We consider the $\eta _ j$'s.

For each $j\geq 1$ and $k\geq 0$, we let

\[ \eta _ j^ k\coloneqq \varphi _ j\land \cdots \land \varphi _{j+k}. \]

Using (6.32), (6.39) and corollary 6.2.10, we have

\[ \left|\int _ X\theta _{\psi _ j}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n \right|\leq \delta 2^{-j-2} \]

for all $j\geq 1$. Taking (6.33) into account, we have

6.41
\begin{equation} \left|\int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\psi _{j-1}}^ n \right|\leq \delta 2^{-j} \end{equation}

for $j>1$.

Step 2.1. We claim that for a fixed $j\geq 1$, for any $k\in \mathbb {N}$, we have $\eta _ j^ k\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and

6.42
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j^ k}^ n\geq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\sum _{a=1}^{k} \delta 2^{-j-a}. \end{equation}

We argue by induction on $k\geq 0$. The case $k=0$ is trivial. When $k>0$, assume that the case $k-1$ is known. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j^{k-1}}^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _{j+k}}^ n \geq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\sum _{a=1}^{k-1}\delta 2^{-j-a}+\int _ X \theta _{\psi _{j+k-1}}^ n-\delta 2^{-j-k}\\ \geq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\delta 2^{-j}+\int _ X \theta _{\psi _{j+k-1}}^ n>\int _ X \theta _{\psi _{j+k-1}}^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where the first inequality follows from the inductive hypothesis and (6.41).

Observe that

\[ \eta _ j^{k-1}\lor \varphi _{j+k}\leq \psi _{j+k-1}, \]

it follows from proposition 3.1.15 that $\eta _ j^ k\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. By theorem 3.1.18, we deduce that

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j^ k}^ n\geq & \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _{j+k}}^ n+ \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j^{k-1}}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\psi _{j+k-1}}^ n\\ \geq & \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\sum _{a=1}^{k} \delta 2^{-j-a}, \end{aligned} \]

where the second inequality follows from the inductive hypothesis and (6.41). Therefore, (6.42) follows.

Step 2.2. It follows by induction from proposition 3.1.16 that for any $j\geq 1$, $k\geq 0$,

\[ P_{\theta }\left[\eta _ j^ k\right]=\eta _ j^ k. \]

By proposition 3.1.26, we have

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j^ k}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j}^ n \]

for any $j\geq 1$. Letting $k\to \infty $ in (6.42), we find that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j}^ n\geq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\delta 2^{-j}>0 \]

for $j\geq 1$. Observe that we also have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j}^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n \leq \int _ X \theta _{\psi _ j}^ n \]

for $j\geq 1$ by theorem 2.4.8. It follows from these inequalities and (6.33) that

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ X\theta _{\eta _ j}^ n=\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

For $k\geq 1$, we have

\[ \eta _ j^ k=\varphi _ j\land \eta _{j+1}^{k-1}\leq \eta _{j+1}^{k-1}. \]

Letting $k\to \infty $ gives $\eta _ j\leq \eta _{j+1}$. Hence $(\eta _ j)_ j$ is increasing. Hence corollary 2.4.9 gives

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n=\lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{\eta _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n, \]

where $\eta =\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq 1}{}\! {}^{*} \eta _ j$. For fixed $m$, if $j\geq m$, then $\eta _ j\leq \varphi _ j\leq \psi _ m$, whereas if $j<m$, then $\eta _ j\leq \eta _ m\leq \psi _ m$. Thus $\eta _ j$ is a candidate in the definition of $P_{\theta }[\psi _ m]$ for all $j,m$, and (6.40) gives $\eta \leq \varphi $. It follows from proposition 6.1.4 that $\eta \sim _ P \varphi $. By corollary 6.2.9 and proposition 6.2.4, we have $\eta _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. $\square$
