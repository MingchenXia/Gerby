Theorem 3.5.8977ECF0867E0

Theorem 3.5.8.977ECF0867E0 The integral $G_\Omega \mu $ is lsc and supermean, hence superharmonic on every component where it is nontrivial. Every finite measure has a potential. For any potential,

\[ -\Delta (G_\Omega \mu )=c_ d\mu \]

in distributions. Its greatest harmonic minorant is zero. It is harmonic on an open set exactly when that set has $\mu $-measure zero. Two potentials whose difference is harmonic almost everywhere on an open set have equal measures there. If $S\subset \Omega $ is open, $\mu $ is supported in $S$ and $G_\Omega \mu $ is a potential, then $G_\Omega \mu =G_ S\mu +h$ on $S$ with $h\ge 0$ harmonic. Reciprocity $\int G\mu \, d\nu =\int G\nu \, d\mu $ holds in $[0,\infty ]$.

Proof. Fatou's lemma applied to the nonnegative continuous extended kernel gives lsc; Tonelli and the supermean inequality for each pole give supermeans. If $\mu $ is finite and gives zero mass to a closed interior ball containing $x$, the bound for $G(x,\cdot )$ outside that ball makes its potential finite at $x$, hence superharmonic on that component. Split a finite measure into its part on a small interior ball and its complement. Each part misses some interior ball in the component, so both have superharmonic potentials and their sum is superharmonic. Apply this separately to every component, using that $G$ vanishes between components.

A potential $p$ is locally integrable by superharmonic local integrability. For $\varphi \in C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$,

\[ \iint G(x,y)|\Delta \varphi (x)|\, dx\, d\mu (y) =\int p(x)|\Delta \varphi (x)|\, dx<\infty . \]

Thus signed Fubini is legitimate, and the fundamental-solution identity plus harmonicity of the correction gives $\int p\Delta \varphi =-c_ d\int \varphi \, d\mu $. In particular a harmonic potential has zero measure; more generally subtracting the distributional identities proves equality of measures wherever the difference is harmonic. If $\mu (U)=0$, Weyl's lemma gives a harmonic representative on $U$; canonical representative uniqueness identifies it pointwise with $p$.

For the greatest minorant use the same sequence of harmonic lowerings for $p$ and for every kernel $G(\cdot ,y)$. Induction and nonnegative Tonelli show $p_ j(x)=\int G_ j(x,y)\, d\mu (y)$. For each $y$, $G_ j(x,y)\downarrow 0$, because the greatest harmonic minorant of the minimal Green kernel is zero. In each connected component choose $x_0$ with $p(x_0)<\infty $. Dominated convergence gives $p_\infty (x_0)=0$; its nonnegative harmonic minorant is therefore zero on that component by the minimum principle. This is a componentwise argument, even when $\Omega $ is disconnected.

On $S$, domain monotonicity gives $0\le G_ S\mu \le p$. Both potentials are locally integrable; their distributional difference is harmonic and nonnegative almost everywhere, hence a nonnegative harmonic $h$. Equality $p=G_ S\mu +h$ holds everywhere by uniqueness of superharmonic representatives. Finally symmetry of $G$ and nonnegative Tonelli give reciprocity, including infinite values. None of these arguments separates divergent fundamental and harmonic-correction integrals. $\square$
