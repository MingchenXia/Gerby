Integer orders in fixed projective cones

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 26.19.5 Integer orders in fixed projective cones

In the examples of section 26.19.1 the order is not an integer because a coefficient of the background vanishes at a point. We now consider backgrounds that are combinations, with strictly positive coefficient functions, of finitely many fixed closed semipositive generators on products of projective spaces and their linear blowups. Such backgrounds have integral orders even when they are not closed. The tool is a comparison with a closed semipositive form $B$ of constant rank. If $\theta $ is bounded above by a multiple of $B$ and, after the addition of a possibly singular potential, below by another multiple, then the Gauduchon quotients of $\theta +\epsilon h$ and of the closed form $B+\epsilon \Omega $ are comparable, and the latter have an explicit order. On a blown-up factor the pulled-back hyperplane form loses rank along the exceptional divisor, and the logarithmic potential of that divisor supplies the lower bound.

Lemma 26.19.13. Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$, let $\Omega $ be a Kähler form, and let $B$ be a smooth closed semipositive $(1,1)$-form. Suppose a smooth nef background $\theta $ satisfies

\[ \theta \leq CB,\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq cB \]

as currents, for constants $c,C>0$ and a real function $\varphi \in L^1(X)$. For every Hermitian form $h$ and smooth strictly positive probability form $\mu $, there are constants $k,K>0$ such that, for $0<\epsilon \leq 1$,

26.142
\begin{equation} \label{eq:nef-logarithmic-quotient-comparison} k\lambda _\mu (B+\epsilon \Omega ) \leq \lambda _\mu (\theta +\epsilon h) \leq K\lambda _\mu (B+\epsilon \Omega ). \end{equation}

If $B$ has constant rank $R$, there are moreover constants $L,U>0$ with

\[ L\epsilon ^{n-R}\leq \mathcal V_\mu (\theta +\epsilon h) \leq U\epsilon ^{n-R},\qquad 0<\epsilon \leq 1. \]

In particular, $\theta $ then has common order $n-R$.

Proof. Choose $c_ h,C_ h>0$ with $c_ h\Omega \leq h\leq C_ h\Omega $. For every Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, distributional Stokes gives

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =\langle \varphi ,\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\gamma ^{n-1}\rangle =0. \]

The lower bound for $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ therefore gives $\int _ X(\theta +\epsilon h)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}\geq k\int _ X(B+\epsilon \Omega )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ with $k=\min (c,c_ h)$, and the upper bound for $\theta $ gives the reverse inequality with $K=\max (C,C_ h)$. Dividing by $D_\mu (\gamma )>0$ and taking infima over all Gauduchon metrics proves (26.142); no rank hypothesis has been used.

Now suppose that $B$ has constant rank $R$. Its $R$ positive eigenvalues relative to $\Omega $ are then bounded above and away from zero, uniformly on $X$. With $A_\epsilon =B+\epsilon \Omega $, compactness gives constants $m_0,M_0>0$ with

26.143
\begin{equation} \label{eq:nef-projective-pointwise-volume} m_0\epsilon ^{n-R}\mu \leq A_\epsilon ^ n \leq M_0\epsilon ^{n-R}\mu ,\qquad 0<\epsilon \leq 1. \end{equation}

For every Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, the pointwise arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives

\[ A_\epsilon \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} \geq \left(\frac{A_\epsilon ^ n}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n, \]

and hence $\lambda _\mu (A_\epsilon )\geq m_0^{1/n}\epsilon ^{(n-R)/n}$. In the other direction, the positive closed form $\gamma =A_\epsilon $ is itself an admissible test. Writing $A_\epsilon ^ n=f_\epsilon \mu $, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} D_\mu (A_\epsilon )& =\int _ X f_\epsilon ^{(n-1)/n}\mu \geq m_0^{(n-1)/n}\epsilon ^{(n-R)(n-1)/n},\\ \int _ X A_\epsilon ^ n& \leq M_0\epsilon ^{n-R}, \end{aligned} \]

so the quotient of this test is at most $M_0m_0^{-(n-1)/n}\epsilon ^{(n-R)/n}$. Raising both bounds to the $n$th power and using (26.142) gives the two-sided power bounds. The argument includes $R=0$; when $n=1$, $D_\mu =1$. Fixed positive factors do not affect the order, since their logarithms are divided by $\log \epsilon $. $\square$
