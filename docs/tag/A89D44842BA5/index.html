14.27.2 Smooth comparison testsA89D44842BA5

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 14.27.2 Smooth comparison tests

The infimum (14.102) runs over bounded measurable densities below $f$. One may ask whether smooth densities suffice to compute the threshold. A smooth test $g$ is in general not below $f$, so we compare it with $f$ through the ratio $\| g/f\| _{L^\infty }$. Thus let $f$ be a probability density bounded below by a positive constant, and define

\[ C_ h^{\mathrm{sm}}(f)=\inf _ g c_ h(g)\| g/f\| _{L^\infty }, \]

where $g$ ranges over smooth strictly positive probability densities. For each such $g$, with $R=\| g/f\| _{L^\infty }$, a subsolution with coefficient $a$ for $f\, \mathrm{d}V$ is a subsolution with coefficient $a/R$ for $g\, \mathrm{d}V$, so $a\leq Rc_ h(g)$ by lemma 14.26.1. Hence $b_ h(f)\leq C_ h^{\mathrm{sm}}(f)$, with equality if $f$ is smooth (take $g=f$). When the lower mass vanishes, the two quantities can differ even in their positivity.

Theorem 14.27.5. If $v_-(h)=0$, there is a probability density $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ bounded below by a positive constant such that

\[ b_ h(f)=0<C_ h^{\mathrm{sm}}(f). \]

Thus $f\, \mathrm{d}V$ admits no positive scaled subsolution, although $c_ h(g)\| g/f\| _{L^\infty }$ is bounded below by a positive constant for all smooth strictly positive probability densities $g$.

Proof. We construct $f=(1+Q)/M$, where $Q\geq 0$ concentrates on closed nowhere dense sets in such a way that it has essential infimum zero on every nonempty open set.

Step 1: the pieces. Let $(U_ i)_{i\geq 1}$ be a countable basis of nonempty open subsets of $X$. We construct inductively numbers $\alpha _ j>0$, nonzero bounded densities $q_ j$, and closed nowhere dense sets $K_ j\subset X$ such that

\[ \int _ Xq_ j\, \mathrm{d}V\leq 1,\qquad q_ j=0\text{ outside }K_ j,\qquad c_ h(q_ j)\leq \alpha _ j2^{-j}. \]

Start with $\alpha _1=1/4$. Once $\alpha _ j$ has been chosen, theorem 26.14.3, theorem 26.1.3 and $v_-(h)=0$ give a smooth strictly positive probability density $g_ j$ with $c_ h(g_ j)<\alpha _ j2^{-j}$. Removing from $X$ an open dense set of small volume, we obtain a closed nowhere dense set $K_ j$ for which $q_ j=g_ j\mathbf1_{K_ j}$ is nonzero and, since $g_ j$ is bounded, arbitrarily close to $g_ j$ in $L^2$. By lemma 14.26.2 with $p=2$, we can therefore choose $K_ j$ so that $c_ h(q_ j)\leq \alpha _ j2^{-j}$.

After $K_1,\ldots ,K_ N$ have been chosen, put

\[ m_ N=\min _{1\leq i\leq N} \mathrm{d}V\left(U_ i\setminus \bigcup _{j=1}^ NK_ j\right)>0; \]

each set inside the minimum is open and nonempty, since a finite union of closed nowhere dense sets is closed and nowhere dense. Choose

\[ 0<\alpha _{N+1}\leq \min \{ \alpha _ N/2,\ 2^{-N-2}m_ N\} , \]

and continue. In particular,

\[ \sum _{j>N}\alpha _ j\leq 2\alpha _{N+1} \leq 2^{-N-1}m_ N. \]

Step 2: the concentrating part. The function $Q=\sum _ j\alpha _ jq_ j$ is nonnegative and integrable. We claim that its essential infimum on every nonempty open set is zero. Since every nonempty open set contains some $U_ i$, it suffices to consider $U_ i$. Take $N\geq i$ and remove $K_1,\ldots ,K_ N$ from $U_ i$. On the remaining set, whose volume is at least $m_ N$, the first $N$ summands of $Q$ vanish. By Markov's inequality, the set where the tail $\sum _{j>N}\alpha _ jq_ j$ exceeds $2^{-N}$ has volume at most

\[ 2^ N\sum _{j>N}\alpha _ j\int _ Xq_ j\, \mathrm{d}V \leq m_ N/2. \]

Hence $Q\leq 2^{-N}$ on a subset of $U_ i$ of positive volume. Letting $N\to \infty $ proves the claim.

Step 3: $b_ h(f)=0$. Set $M=\int _ X(1+Q)\, \mathrm{d}V$ and $f=(1+Q)/M$, a probability density with $f\geq 1/M$. If $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u)\geq af\, \mathrm{d}V$ with $a>0$, then $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u)\geq (a\alpha _ j/M)q_ j\, \mathrm{d}V$, and lemma 14.26.1 gives

\[ \frac{a\alpha _ j}{M}\leq c_ h(q_ j)\leq \alpha _ j2^{-j} \quad \text{for every }j. \]

This is impossible. Hence $b_ h(f)=0$ by theorem 14.27.2.

Step 4: $C_ h^{\mathrm{sm}}(f)>0$. Let $g$ be a smooth strictly positive probability density and put $R=\| g/f\| _{L^\infty }$. Then $g\leq R(1+Q)/M$ almost everywhere, and this forces $g\leq R/M$ everywhere. Otherwise, by continuity, $g>R(1+\varepsilon )/M$ on a nonempty open set for some $\varepsilon >0$, while $Q<\varepsilon $ on a subset of positive volume of this set by Step 2, a contradiction. The order and homogeneity of the scalar in lemma 14.26.1 now give $c_ h(g)\geq c_ h(R/M)=(M/R)c_ h(1)$, that is,

\[ R c_ h(g)\geq M c_ h(1). \]

The constant density $g=1$ attains this value, since $\operatorname *{ess\, inf}_ XQ=0$ gives $\| 1/f\| _{L^\infty }=M$. Thus $C_ h^{\mathrm{sm}}(f)=M c_ h(1)>0$, as required. $\square$

The point is that a smooth test $g$ with $\| g/f\| _{L^\infty }\leq R$ is controlled by the essential infimum of $f$ on open sets. It therefore cannot detect an increase of $f$ that leaves this essential infimum unchanged on every open set, whereas bounded measurable tests below $f$ do detect it. The theorem concerns only the existence of subsolutions. Exact solutions of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(u)=b_ h(f)f\, \mathrm{d}V$, the singularity class in which they should be sought, and compactness of their normalized solutions are further problems; see 33.1.1.
