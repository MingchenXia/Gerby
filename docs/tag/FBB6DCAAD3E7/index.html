Lemma 15.37.9FBB6DCAAD3E7

Lemma 15.37.9.FBB6DCAAD3E7 Let $u,v\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ satisfy $u\leq v$. If

\[ \mu _ u(\{ u<v\} )=0, \]

then $u=v$.

Proof. Translate both potentials by a common constant so that $u\leq v\leq \varphi \leq 0$. Fix $\delta $ with $\varepsilon <\delta <1$, put $\beta =1-\delta $, and set

\[ q=P_{\delta \theta }(v). \]

This envelope is not identically $-\infty $ by theorem 16.28.70, applied to $v$. By lemma 16.28.68,

\[ w=P_\theta (u-\varphi )\in \mathcal E_0, \qquad w+\varphi \leq u,\qquad w\leq 0. \]

The stretching lemma lemma 16.28.63, applied with reference zero, shows that $z=P_\theta (w/\beta )$ belongs to $\mathcal E_0$. Scaling the background and each defining competitor gives

\[ \beta z\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\beta \theta ,0). \]

Indeed, if $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ lies below $t z$, then $\beta a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta \theta )$ lies below $t\beta z$, for every $t\geq 1$.

Since $q\leq v\leq \varphi $, we have

\[ \beta z\leq w\leq u-\varphi \leq u-q \]

off the pole sets. Thus the envelope

\[ k=P_{\beta \theta }(u-q) \]

is not identically $-\infty $. Here the difference obstacle is taken off the pluripolar pole sets; it is quasi-continuous and finite almost everywhere. The general envelope theorem gives a finite upper bound for $k$ and the inequality $k+q\leq u$. Since $\beta z\leq k$, upward stability gives $k\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\beta \theta ,0)$.

Write $\nu _ k=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\beta \theta }(k)$. By contact support, $\nu _ k$ is carried by

\[ D=\{ k+q=u\} \]

outside the pole sets. Positivity of mixed products and ordered contact give

\[ \nu _ k\leq \mathbf1_ D\mu _ u. \]

These inequalities are first proved on common finite truncation levels and then pass to the limit by exhaustion. On $D\cap \{ k<0\} $ we have $u=k+q<q\leq v$, so the hypothesis gives $\nu _ k(\{ k<0\} )=0$.

This vanishing implies that $\nu _ k$ is finite. Indeed, $\widehat k=\max (k,-1)$ is a bounded $\beta \theta $-plurisubharmonic function, and locality gives

\[ \nu _ k(X)=\nu _ k(\{ k\geq 0\} ) =\int _{\{ k\geq 0\} }\mathrm{MA}_{\beta \theta }(\widehat k) \leq \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_{\beta \theta }(\widehat k)<\infty . \]

We now apply theorem 15.37.6 in the background $\beta \theta $, with reference zero, to the potentials $k$ and $0$ and the coefficient zero. Its hypotheses hold: $\beta \theta $ is semipositive and big, the reference zero satisfies the model condition, $\nu _ k$ is finite and vanishes on $\{ k<0\} $, and

\[ v_-(\beta \theta )=\beta ^ n v_-(\theta )>0. \]

The scaling identity follows from the bijection $a\mapsto \beta a$ between bounded potentials in the two backgrounds. The theorem yields $k\geq 0$, and hence $u\geq q$.

It remains to let $\delta \uparrow 1$. Fix $\delta _0\in (\varepsilon ,1)$ and put $r=P_{\delta _0\theta }(v)$. For $\delta _0<\delta <1$, set $t=(\delta -\delta _0)/(1-\delta _0)$. The potential $t v+(1-t)r$ is $\delta \theta $-plurisubharmonic and lies below $v$. Therefore

\[ u\geq P_{\delta \theta }(v)\geq t v+(1-t)r. \]

Letting $t\uparrow 1$ at points where $v$ and $r$ are finite gives $u\geq v$ almost everywhere, and then everywhere by lemma 5.24.3. Together with $u\leq v$, this proves equality. $\square$
