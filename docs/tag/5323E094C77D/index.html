Local regularity and strict weights5323E094C77D

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Local regularity and strict weights

Whether the regularized envelope touches the obstacle at a point $a\in K$ depends only on the parts of $K$ near $a$. Without weight, this local regularity is detected by the relative extremal function $h_{E,B}$ of definition 1.6.3, for compact pieces $E$ of $K$ near $a$ and small balls $B$ centered at $a$. If the weight extends to a global function whose complex Hessian has a strict lower bound near $a$, a failure of this regularity produces a quantitative defect of the weighted envelope.

For a non-pluripolar compact set $E\subset \mathbb C^ n$, write $V_ E$ and $V_ E^*$ for the raw and regularized envelopes of growth one with zero obstacle on $E$; thus $V_ K=V_{1,0}$ as before. For other weights and growth allowances, write $F_{\delta ,E,q}$ for the regularized envelope with growth allowance $\delta $ and obstacle $q$ imposed at every point of $E$.

Lemma 13.1.22. Let $a\in \mathbb C^ n$, $0<r<R$ and $B=B(a,R)$, and let $E\subseteq \overline B(a,r)$ be compact. If $E$ is pluripolar, then $h_{E,B}^*=0$ on $B$. If $E$ is non-pluripolar, then

\[ h_{E,B}^*(a)=-1\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad V_ E^*(a)=0. \]

Proof. In the pluripolar case, choose $b\in \mathcal L_1$ with $b=-\infty $ on $E$ by lemma 13.1.9, and a finite upper bound $C$ for $b$ on $B$. The functions

\[ u_\epsilon =\max \{ -1,\epsilon (b-C)\} \]

are competitors for $h_{E,B}$, and they tend to zero almost everywhere as $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. Hence $h_{E,B}^*\geq 0$ almost everywhere, and then everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Since $h_{E,B}^*\leq 0$, it vanishes.

Now suppose that $E$ is non-pluripolar. Proposition 13.1.8 gives a finite upper bound $M\geq 1$ for $V_ E^*$ on $B$. For each competitor $v$ for $V_ E$, the function $\max (-1,v/M-1)$ is a competitor for $h_{E,B}$. Taking suprema and regularizing gives

\[ h_{E,B}^*(a)\geq \max \{ -1,V_ E^*(a)/M-1\} . \]

As $V_ E^*\geq 0$, the equality $h_{E,B}^*(a)=-1$ forces $V_ E^*(a)=0$.

For the converse, choose $r<R_0<R$ and $k>0$ with $k\log (R_0/r)>1$. The plurisubharmonic function

\[ L(z)=k\log \bigl (\lVert z-a\rVert /r\bigr ) \]

belongs to $\mathcal L_ k$ and equals $-\infty $ at $a$. For a competitor $u$ for $h_{E,B}$, define $W=\max (u+1,L)$ on $B$ and $W=L$ outside $B$. On the collar $R_0<\lVert z-a\rVert <R$ we have $L>1\geq u+1$, so the two definitions agree there, and $W$ is plurisubharmonic by lemma 1.3.2. On $E$ one has $u=-1$ and $L\leq 0$, so $W=0$; on the ball $B(a,r)$ one has $L<0\leq u+1$, so $W=u+1$. Hence $W/k$ is a competitor for $V_ E$, with $k$ independent of $u$. Taking the supremum over $u$ and regularizing at $a$ gives

\[ V_ E^*(a)\geq (1+h_{E,B}^*(a))/k. \]

Thus $V_ E^*(a)=0$ forces $h_{E,B}^*(a)=-1$. $\square$

Let $K\subset \mathbb C^ n$ again be a non-pluripolar compact set. For $a\in K$ and $r>0$, put $K_ r=K\cap \overline B(a,r)$.

Definition 13.1.23. The compact set $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a\in K$ if every $K_ r$, $r>0$, is non-pluripolar and $V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$. For $\delta >0$ and a bounded function $q$ on $K$, it is locally $(\delta ,q)$-regular at $a$ if $F_{\delta ,K_ r,q}(a)=q(a)$ for every $r>0$.

It suffices to check arbitrarily small radii, since envelopes decrease as the compact set increases. By lemma 13.1.22, $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a$ if and only if $h_{K_ r,B(a,R)}^*(a)=-1$ for all small $0<r<R$.

Lemma 13.1.24. Let $q$ be bounded and upper semicontinuous on $K$, and suppose that raw contact holds for the parameter $\delta >0$ and the obstacle $q$ on $K$, that is, $V_{\delta ,q}=q$ on $K$. If $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a$, then $F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)=q(a)$ and $K$ is locally $(\delta ,q)$-regular at $a$. These equalities also hold with $\delta $ replaced by any larger parameter.

Proof. Put $M_ r=\sup _{K_ r}q$. Raw contact, monotonicity of envelopes with respect to the compact set, and (13.3) for $K_ r$ give

\[ q(a)\leq F_{\delta ,K,q}(a) \leq F_{\delta ,K_ r,q}(a) \leq M_ r+\delta V_{K_ r}^*(a)=M_ r. \]

Since $q$ is upper semicontinuous and $a\in K_ r$, we have $M_ r\downarrow q(a)$ as $r\downarrow 0$, which gives $F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)=q(a)$.

Every compact subset $E\subseteq K$ inherits raw contact: on $E$, the raw envelope for $E$ is at least the raw envelope for $K$, which equals $q$, and at most the obstacle $q$. For fixed $s>0$, apply the same bounds with $r<s$ and $K_ s$ in place of $K$, and let $r\downarrow 0$. This proves local $(\delta ,q)$-regularity. By theorem 13.1.11, raw contact also holds for every larger parameter, and applying what has been proved to each of them gives the last assertion. $\square$

Theorem 13.1.25. Let $a\in K$, $\tau >0$ and $\Psi \in \mathcal L_\tau \cap C(\mathbb C^ n)$, and put $q=\Psi |_ K$. Suppose that, for some $R,c>0$,

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi \geq c\omega _0\quad \text{on }B(a,R), \qquad \omega _0=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\lVert z\rVert ^2. \]

For $0<r<R$ and $\delta \geq \tau $,

13.21
\begin{equation} F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)-\Psi (a) \geq cr^2\bigl (1+h_{K_ r,B(a,R)}^*(a)\bigr ). \end{equation}

Regular contact at $a$, that is, $F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)=q(a)$, holds for some $\delta \geq \tau $ if and only if $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a$. In that case it holds for every $\delta \geq \tau $.

Proof. Set $B=B(a,R)$ and $E=K_ r$. For a competitor $u$ for $h_{E,B}$, the function

\[ H_ u(z)=\Psi (z)-c\lVert z-a\rVert ^2+cr^2(u(z)+1) \]

is plurisubharmonic on $B$, because the assumption on $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi $ makes $\Psi -c\lVert z-a\rVert ^2$ plurisubharmonic there. Since $u\leq 0$, we have $H_ u\leq \Psi $ where $\lVert z-a\rVert \geq r$. Define $W=\max (\Psi ,H_ u)$ on $B$ and $W=\Psi $ outside $B$. The two definitions agree near $\partial B$, so $W$ is plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb C^ n$, and it belongs to $\mathcal L_\tau $ because it equals $\Psi $ outside $B$. On $E$, the equality $u=-1$ gives $H_ u\leq \Psi $, and on $K\setminus E$ the distance bound gives the same inequality. Thus $W=q$ on $K$, so $W$ is a competitor for $F_{\delta ,K,q}$ for each $\delta \geq \tau $. Since $W\geq H_ u$ near $a$, taking the supremum over $u$, regularizing, and using the continuity of $\Psi $, we obtain (13.21).

The function $\Psi $ itself is a competitor, so raw contact holds for every $\delta \geq \tau $. If regular contact holds for one such $\delta $, the left-hand side of the estimate vanishes, so $h_{K_ r,B(a,R)}^*(a)=-1$ for all $0<r<R$. By lemma 13.1.22, every $K_ r$ with $r<R$ is then non-pluripolar and satisfies $V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$; for $r\geq R$ this follows by monotonicity. Conversely, local pluriregularity and lemma 13.1.24 give regular contact at $a$ for every $\delta \geq \tau $. $\square$

In this regular case, $V_ K^*(a)\leq V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$ by monotonicity, and the right derivative $D_\tau (a)$ with respect to the growth allowance, for the weight $q$, vanishes by corollary 13.1.14, since regular contact persists.

Corollary 13.1.26. Let $\tau >0$, $\Psi \in \mathcal L_\tau \cap C^2(\mathbb C^ n)$ and $a\in K$. If $F_{\tau ,K,\Psi |_ K}(a)=\Psi (a)$, but $F_{\delta ,K,\Psi |_ K}(a)>\Psi (a)$ for some $\delta >\tau $, then

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi )^ n(a)=0. \]

Proof. The complex Hessian of $\Psi $ is semipositive. If its top power did not vanish at $a$, it would be positive definite at $a$, and by continuity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi \geq c\omega _0$ on a neighborhood of $a$ for some $c>0$. Theorem 13.1.25 would then give regular contact at $a$ for every $\delta \geq \tau $, a contradiction. $\square$
