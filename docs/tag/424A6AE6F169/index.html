Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag 424A6AE6F169

Envelope pullback

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 16.6.53

Proposition 16.6.53. For $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(C,\beta )$ and every smooth real obstacle $F$ on $C$,

\[ \begin{aligned} P_{\sigma ,F\circ r}[v\circ r] & =P_{\beta ,F}[v]\circ r,\\ P_{\sigma ,F\circ r}[v\circ r]_{\mathcal I} & =P_{\beta ,F}[v]_{\mathcal I}\circ r. \end{aligned} \]

In particular, $J(v)=v\circ r$ embeds the ordinary model space $\mathcal M(\beta )$ into $\mathcal M(\sigma )$ and preserves ideal projection.

Proof. Pullback sends every base competitor to an upstairs competitor: it preserves positivity, the obstacle inequality, the ordinary order and, by the Lelong formula and the curve valuative criterion, the all-weight ideal order. Conversely, if $h$ is an upstairs competitor, its fiber maximum $m_ h$ is a base competitor by 1.22.10, since $h\leq F\circ r$ implies $m_ h\leq F$. Also $h\leq m_ h\circ r$. Thus the two unregularized suprema are equal after pullback. A finite nonconstant holomorphic map of curves is open; upper regularization commutes with its pullback, as is seen in the coordinates $t=z^ e$. This proves both identities. Take $F=0$. An ordinary model is fixed by its ordinary envelope, so its pullback is a model; surjectivity of $r$ gives injectivity. Applying the ideal identity to a model proves preservation of ideal projection. $\square$
