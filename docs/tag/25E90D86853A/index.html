23.2.55 The Lee–Gauduchon cone25E90D86853A

For the rest of the chapter, $X$ is an arbitrary connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$, not necessarily LCK. By theorem 11.6.1, a class in the image of the Lee map $j_ X$ of (23.2.9) is pseudoeffective exactly when it pairs nonnegatively with every Gauduchon metric. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, the form $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(\gamma ^{n-1})$ is closed, and these pairings depend only on its de Rham class. The classes obtained in this way lie in the annihilator of $\ker j_ X$, where they form an open convex cone, and strict positivity against this cone detects every nonzero pseudoeffective class in the image of $j_ X$.

Set $V_ X=H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)$ and

\[ K_ X=\ker j_ X,\qquad W_ X=K_ X^\perp \subset H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R), \]

where the annihilator is taken with respect to the Poincaré pairing

\[ \langle [\lambda ],a\rangle =\int _ X\lambda \wedge a \qquad \bigl (a\in H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)\bigr ). \]

Let

\[ \mathcal C_ X =\{ [\lambda ]\in V_ X:j_ X([\lambda ])\text{ is pseudoeffective}\} . \]

This is a convex cone, since pseudoeffective classes form a convex cone. The image of $j_ X$ lies in the kernel of the natural map from Bott–Chern to de Rham cohomology, and it can be strictly smaller than this kernel, as example 23.2.72 will show.

Lemma 23.2.56.70F067DD267F The space $K_ X$ consists of the de Rham classes of real parts of closed holomorphic one-forms. Moreover,

\[ \mathcal C_ X\cap (-\mathcal C_ X)=K_ X. \]

Proof. If $j_ X([\lambda ])=0$, write $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\lambda =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ for a smooth real function $f$. Since $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\mathrm{d}=-\mathrm{d}\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$, the form $\lambda +\mathrm{d}f$ is both $\mathrm{d}$-closed and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$-closed. Since the two equations separate the $\partial $- and $\bar\partial $-derivatives, its $(1,0)$-part is holomorphic and closed. Thus $\lambda +\mathrm{d}f$ is the real part of the closed holomorphic form $2(\lambda +\mathrm{d}f)^{1,0}$. Conversely, the real part of a closed holomorphic one-form is closed for both operators, so its image under $j_ X$ is zero.

By proposition 10.11.2, the pseudoeffective Bott–Chern cone contains no line. Thus $j_ X(\xi )$ and $-j_ X(\xi )$ are both pseudoeffective exactly when $j_ X(\xi )=0$, which is the identity $\mathcal C_ X\cap (-\mathcal C_ X)=K_ X$. $\square$

Definition 23.2.57.FC269BC81E64 The Lee–Gauduchon cone is

\[ \operatorname {LG}(X) =\bigl \{ [\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(\gamma ^{n-1})]: \gamma \text{ is a Gauduchon metric on }X\bigr \} . \]

For a closed real one-form $\lambda $ and any smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Gamma $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma =0$, integration by parts gives

FB0122E5265C 23.2.58
\begin{equation} \int _ X\lambda \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\Gamma =\int _ X\kappa _\lambda \wedge \Gamma . \end{equation}

The form $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\Gamma $ is closed, and both sides depend only on the cohomology classes involved. In particular, the class $[\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\Gamma ]$ annihilates $K_ X$, so $\operatorname {LG}(X)\subseteq W_ X$.

Proposition 23.2.59.EBFA309383E5 The cone $\operatorname {LG}(X)$ is nonempty, convex and open in $W_ X$. Its linear span is $W_ X$.

Proof. Let $\mathcal Z_ X$ be the real vector space of smooth $(n-1,n-1)$-forms $\Gamma $ satisfying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma =0$, and set

\[ q_ X(\Gamma )=[\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\Gamma ]. \]

We first show that $q_ X(\mathcal Z_ X)=W_ X$. The inclusion into $W_ X$ follows from (23.2.58). If $[\lambda ]$ annihilates the image of $q_ X$, then by (23.2.58) the class $\{ \kappa _\lambda \} $ has zero pairing with every Gauduchon form. The generalized Lamari criterion, theorem 11.6.1, implies that both $\{ \kappa _\lambda \} $ and $-\{ \kappa _\lambda \} $ are pseudoeffective, so $[\lambda ]\in K_ X$ by lemma 23.2.56. Thus the image of $q_ X$ and $W_ X$ have the same annihilator in $V_ X$, and since the Poincaré pairing between $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and $H^{2n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is nondegenerate, they are equal.

Let $\mathcal Z_ X^+$ be the set of strictly positive forms in $\mathcal Z_ X$. By the linear-algebra construction used in the proof of theorem 11.6.1, every such form is the $(n-1)$st power of a unique Hermitian form, which is then Gauduchon. Hence

\[ \operatorname {LG}(X)=q_ X(\mathcal Z_ X^+). \]

By Gauduchon's theorem, $\mathcal Z_ X^+$ is nonempty, and it is a convex cone; hence so is $\operatorname {LG}(X)$.

For openness, choose forms $\Gamma _1,\ldots ,\Gamma _ r\in \mathcal Z_ X$ whose images form a basis of $W_ X$. If $\Gamma \in \mathcal Z_ X^+$, then $\Gamma +\sum _ i t_ i\Gamma _ i$ remains strictly positive for all sufficiently small real $t_ i$, and the images of these forms cover a neighborhood of $q_ X(\Gamma )$ in $W_ X$. This includes the case $W_ X=\{ 0\} $, with the empty basis. Since a nonempty open subset spans the ambient vector space, the assertion about the span follows. $\square$

Theorem 23.2.60.DB27896B9A96 The cone $\mathcal C_ X$ is closed, and

\[ \mathcal C_ X =\{ \xi \in V_ X:\langle \xi ,a\rangle \geq 0 \text{ for every }a\in \operatorname {LG}(X)\} . \]

Conversely,

\[ \operatorname {LG}(X) =\{ a\in W_ X:\langle \xi ,a\rangle >0 \text{ for every }\xi \in \mathcal C_ X\setminus K_ X\} . \]

Proof. The first equality is theorem 11.6.1 applied to the smooth closed form $\kappa _\lambda $, together with (23.2.58). It expresses $\mathcal C_ X$ as an intersection of closed half-spaces, so this cone is closed.

If $a=[\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(\gamma ^{n-1})]\in \operatorname {LG}(X)$ and $\xi =[\lambda ]\in \mathcal C_ X\setminus K_ X$, choose a closed positive current $T=\kappa _\lambda +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. It is nonzero because it represents the nonzero class $j_ X(\xi )$. Therefore

\[ \langle \xi ,a\rangle =\int _ XT\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}>0. \]

Conversely, suppose that $a\in W_ X\setminus \operatorname {LG}(X)$. Separating the point $a$ from the nonempty open convex cone $\operatorname {LG}(X)$ in the finite-dimensional space $W_ X$ gives a nonzero linear functional $\ell $ on $W_ X$ such that

\[ \ell (a)\leq 0,\qquad \ell (b)\geq 0 \quad (b\in \operatorname {LG}(X)). \]

Indeed, in the usual separating inequalities the constant can be taken to be zero, by scaling the elements of the cone. By Poincaré duality, $\ell =\langle \xi ,\cdot \rangle $ for a class $\xi \in V_ X$ that is unique modulo $K_ X$, and $\xi \notin K_ X$ because $\ell \neq 0$. The first equality of the theorem gives $\xi \in \mathcal C_ X$, and then $\langle \xi ,a\rangle \leq 0$ shows that $a$ does not belong to the right side of the second equality. This proves the second equality; if $\operatorname {LG}(X)=W_ X$, there is nothing to prove. $\square$
