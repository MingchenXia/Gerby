27.11 Horizontal poles over projective space6109F3CDB146

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.11 Horizontal poles over projective space

When the base divisor is a hyperplane in projective space, the descended vanishing conditions of theorem 27.10.2 turn the kernels into binomial tail probabilities. The large-deviation rate of these tails is the domination envelope of the original singularity upstairs, and the concentration of evaluation sections identifies the limiting measure for every smooth integration volume. In this setting the weak limit of corollary 27.10.4 improves to convergence in total variation.

We specialize section 27.10 to $B=\mathbb P^ q$ with homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots :Z_ q]$, $A=\mathcal O_ B(k)$ and $Z=\{ Z_0=0\} $, where $q,r,k$ are positive integers and $0<c<k$. Let $\omega _ B$ be the Fubini–Study form on $B$, normalized by $\int _ B\omega _ B^ q=1$, and give $A$ the Fubini–Study metric, whose curvature is $\theta =k\omega _ B$. We keep the submersion $\pi :X\to B$ with connected $r$-dimensional fibers, and the selecting potential $\varphi $ with the singularity (27.33) along the section $Y$ over $Z$. Write

\[ R=\frac{|Z_0|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ q|Z_ i|^2},\qquad a=\frac ck,\qquad D_ c=\{ R\geq a\} ,\qquad C_ c=\frac{(k-c)^ q}{q!}. \]

Fix a smooth positive probability volume $\nu _ X$ on $X$. Fiber integration gives $\nu _ B=\pi _*\nu _ X=w\, \omega _ B^ q$ with a smooth function $w>0$; put $M=\max _ Bw$. Let $B_ m^ B$ be the kernel of the descended space $V_ m$ for the norm on $B$ with zero continuous weight and volume $\nu _ B$, and let $B_ m^ X$ be the kernel of the pulled-back space for the norm on $X$ with zero continuous weight and volume $\nu _ X$. We shall see that $B_ m^ X=B_ m^ B\circ \pi $.

The selecting potential $\varphi $ need not be $\pi ^*\theta $-plurisubharmonic. Its domination envelope in this curvature background is

\[ \mathcal P(\varphi )= \left(\sup \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\pi ^*\theta ):u\leq 0,\ u\preceq \varphi \} \right)^*, \]

where $u\preceq \varphi $ means that $u\leq \varphi +C$ for some constant $C$ (definition 1.12.1). The limit will be described by the function of $R$ defined by

27.50
\begin{equation} u_ c(R)= \begin{cases} -k\left(a\log \dfrac aR+(1-a)\log \dfrac {1-a}{1-R}\right),& 0<R<a,\\ 0,& a\leq R\leq 1,\\ -\infty ,& R=0. \end{cases} \end{equation}

Theorem 27.11.1. The envelope satisfies $\mathcal P(\varphi )=u_ c\circ \pi $. With $\ell _ m=\lfloor mc\rfloor $ and $t_ m=\max \{ \ell _ m-r,0\} $,

\[ N_ m=\dim V_ m=\binom {km-t_ m+q}{q} =C_ cm^ q+O(m^{q-1}). \]

Thus the section spaces grow like $m^ q$, and $q<\dim X$. For every $m\geq 1$, in the extended-value sense,

27.54
\begin{equation} \frac1m\log B_ m^ X\geq \mathcal P(\varphi )-\frac{\log M}{m}. \end{equation}

The functions $m^{-1}\log B_ m^ X$ converge to $\mathcal P(\varphi )$ in $L^1$ for every smooth positive volume on $X$, and locally uniformly on $X\setminus \pi ^{-1}(Z)$. In the normalization of section 27.1, the kernel measures converge in total variation:

27.55
\begin{equation} \begin{split} \frac{q!}{m^ q}B_ m^ B\nu _ B& \longrightarrow \mu _ c:=\mathbf1_{D_ c}\theta ^ q,\\ \frac{q!}{m^ q}B_ m^ X\nu _ X& \longrightarrow \Lambda _{\nu _ X}:=\frac{k^ q\mathbf1_{D_ c}\circ \pi }{w\circ \pi }\nu _ X. \end{split} \end{equation}

Both limits have mass $(k-c)^ q$, and $\pi _*\Lambda _{\nu _ X}=\mu _ c$. Dividing the unnormalized kernel measures by $N_ m$ instead gives the probability limits $\mu _ c/(k-c)^ q$ and $\Lambda _{\nu _ X}/(k-c)^ q$, while normalization by $m^{q+r}$ gives zero in total variation.

Let $W_ m$ be the reference spaces of theorem 27.10.2. For every sequence of intermediate spaces $W_ m\subseteq U_ m\subseteq V_ m$, equipped with the restricted norms, the lower bound and all these convergences remain valid. The dimensions of these spaces are $C_ cm^ q+O(m^{q-1})$, and the dimension normalization then uses $\dim U_ m$.
