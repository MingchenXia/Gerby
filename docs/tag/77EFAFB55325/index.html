A Potentials and equations on unibranch spaces77EFAFB55325

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · A Potentials and equations on unibranch spaces

To solve an equation on a singular complex space, we pass to a resolution and then descend the solution. The pullback background can degenerate along exceptional fibers, and removing a logarithmic pole can leave a residual that does not descend. The examples in this appendix address these two difficulties directly.

Except in the local toric calculations of A.4.1, A.4.2, $X$ denotes a reduced irreducible compact complex space of pure dimension $n\geq 1$ that is unibranch: its germ at every point is irreducible, or equivalently, its normalization is a homeomorphism. We write $X_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and $X_{\mathrm{sing}}$ for the regular and singular loci. Every point of $X$ has a neighborhood that embeds as a closed analytic subset of an open set in some $\mathbb C^ N$. Smooth functions and forms on $X$ are defined through such local embeddings: they are locally restrictions of smooth functions and forms on the ambient open sets. A Hermitian form on $X$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is locally the restriction of a smooth strictly positive ambient $(1,1)$-form. Fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$.

A resolution of $X$ is a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map $\pi :Y\to X$ from a compact complex manifold $Y$; on $Y$ the pullback $\pi ^*\theta $ is an ordinary smooth form, to which the theory of the main text applies. In the last three sections, $X$ is a specific space of this kind.

The basic tool is descent through a resolution. The pullback $\pi ^*\theta $ has no component along the fibers of $\pi $, so the maximum principle on the connected compact fibers shows that every $\pi ^*\theta $-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$ is constant on fibers and descends to $X$. This correspondence preserves envelopes and convergence. Exceptional sets carry no non-pluripolar mass, so the extended Monge–Ampère measure defined through a resolution does not depend on the resolution.

On a projective cone, a logarithmic pole at the vertex becomes a divisor on the incidence resolution, and removing it leaves a strictly positive residual background. This gives uniqueness in the prescribed ordinary class, including for certain nonclosed backgrounds. The bounded equation on the cone uses closedness and an energy identity. The full logarithmic potential descends even when its smooth residual varies along the contracted divisor.

On a curve whose normalization is a torus, a periodic Green function gives a separate construction. It allows the pulled-back background to vanish at singular points, and it treats every potential in a fixed ordinary type with finitely many logarithmic poles. A projective cusp shows that the residual can be continuous even though the density on the curve is unbounded.

On products of a cuspidal curve with elliptic curves, a smooth potential repairs the degenerate background of the normalization, and two torsion identities fix the masses of all bounded potentials. For data close to the reference density, a positive solution gives uniqueness in the whole bounded class by a $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ energy comparison, even though the background is nonclosed.

We use three facts about unibranch spaces. Resolutions and common smooth refinements of two resolutions exist, and a proper bimeromorphic map to a unibranch space has connected fibers [Theorems B.1.1–B.1.3, SGPT]. The Fornaess–Narasimhan criterion identifies plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ as the upper semicontinuous functions that are subharmonic on holomorphic disks [Theorem B.2.1, SGPT]. The ramified disk lift used below is the argument in [proof of Theorem B.2.2, SGPT].
