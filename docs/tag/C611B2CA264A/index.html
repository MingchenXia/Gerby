Theorem 9.7.1C611B2CA264A

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 9.7.1

Theorem 9.7.1. Let $A_ t=\eta _ t^{-1}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\dot u_ t$ be the endomorphism of the tangent bundle that represents the real $(1,1)$-form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\dot u_ t$ with respect to $\eta _ t$; it is Hermitian with respect to $\eta _ t$. The normalization constant satisfies

9.18
\begin{align} \dot b_ t& =-\int _ X\dot F_ t\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{u_ t},\\ \ddot b_ t& =-\int _ X\ddot F_ t\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{u_ t} -\int _ X\operatorname {tr}(A_ t^2)\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{u_ t}. \end{align}

In particular, for smooth real functions $F_0,G$ on $X$ and the affine family $F_ t=F_0+tG$, the function $b_ t$ is concave. Its second derivative vanishes at a parameter value if and only if $G$ is constant on $X$.

Proof. Differentiating the logarithm of (9.1) gives

\[ L_{u_ t}\dot u_ t=\dot F_ t+\dot b_ t. \]

Integration against $\mu _{u_ t}$ proves (9.18).

For a smooth family of positive Hermitian matrices $H_ t$, differentiation of the inverse matrix gives

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2}\log \det H_ t =\operatorname {tr}(H_ t^{-1}\ddot H_ t) -\operatorname {tr}\bigl ((H_ t^{-1}\dot H_ t)^2\bigr ). \]

Apply this pointwise to the matrices of $\eta _ t$, using $\dot\eta _ t=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\dot u_ t$ and $\ddot\eta _ t=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ddot u_ t$. The second derivative of the logarithmic equation is

\[ L_{u_ t}\ddot u_ t-\operatorname {tr}(A_ t^2) =\ddot F_ t+\ddot b_ t. \]

Integrating against $\mu _{u_ t}$ at this fixed parameter proves (9.19); there is no need to differentiate the measure.

The eigenvalues of the Hermitian endomorphism $A_ t$ are real, so $\operatorname {tr}(A_ t^2)\geq 0$. For affine $F_ t$ we have $\ddot F_ t=0$, so the second derivative of $b_ t$ is nonpositive. If it vanishes, strict positivity of the smooth density of $\mu _{u_ t}$ implies $A_ t=0$ everywhere. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\dot u_ t=0$, and the maximum principle shows that $\dot u_ t$ is constant. The first differentiated equation then gives $G=-\dot b_ t$, a constant. Conversely, if $G$ is constant, the solution metric is independent of $t$ and $b_ t=b_0-tG$. $\square$
