13.3 Duistermaat–Heckman measures3C0D12E80020

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class.

Definition 13.3.1. Assume that $X$ admits a smooth flag $Y_{\bullet }$. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. The Duistermaat–Heckman measure $\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )$ of $\Gamma $ is defined as

\[ \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )\coloneqq n!\cdot \operatorname{DH}\left(\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma )\right). \]
Recall that $\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma )\in \mathrm{TC}(\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma _{-\infty }))$ is the Okounkov test curve defined in theorem 10.5.13. See definition 10.5.9 for the definition of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of an Okounkov test curve.

Theorem 13.3.2. Assume that $X$ admits a smooth flag $Y_{\bullet }$. The Duistermaat–Heckman measure $\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )$ of $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ in definition 13.3.1 is independent of the choice of the smooth flag $Y_{\bullet }$. Furthermore, for any $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, the following moment identity holds:

13.28
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb {R}}x^ m\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )(x)=\Gamma _{\max }^ m\operatorname{vol}\Gamma +m\int _{-\infty }^{\Gamma _{\max }}\tau ^{m-1}\Bigl ( \operatorname{vol}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{\tau })-\operatorname{vol}\Gamma \Bigr )\, \mathrm{d}\tau , \end{equation}

and

13.29
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb {R}}\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )=\operatorname{vol}\Gamma . \end{equation}
For each $m\in \mathbb {N}$, $\int _{\mathbb {R}}x^ m\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )(x)$ is indeed $n!$ times the $m$-th moment of the random variable $G[\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma )]$, by definition 13.3.1, definition 10.5.9.

Proof. We observe that (13.28) holds by the computations in proposition 10.5.10 and theorem 10.4.13 1. It is independent of the choice of the flag. Furthermore, (13.29) follows immediately from theorem 10.4.13 1.

Assume first that $\Gamma $ is bounded. Since the Duistermaat–Heckman measure has bounded support in this case (cf. theorem 10.5.6), we conclude that $\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )$ is uniquely determined.

By translating the parameter, it suffices to treat the case $\Gamma _{\max }=0$. Indeed, if $a=\Gamma _{\max }$ and

\[ \widetilde\Gamma _\tau \coloneqq \Gamma _{\tau +a}, \]

then $\widetilde\Gamma _{\max }=0$, and the associated test function is translated by $a$. Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )=(x\mapsto x+a)_*\operatorname{DH}(\widetilde\Gamma ). \]

Thus independence of the flag and the moment identity for $\widetilde\Gamma $ imply the corresponding assertions for $\Gamma $.

For each $\epsilon >0$, we define $\Gamma ^{\epsilon }\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ as follows:

  1. Let $\Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\max }=0$, and

  2. we set

    \[ \Gamma ^{\epsilon }_{\tau }= \begin{cases} \Gamma _{-\infty },& \text{ if }\tau \leq -\epsilon ^{-1},\\ P_{\theta }\left[ (1+\epsilon \tau )\Gamma _{\tau }-\epsilon \tau \Gamma _{-\infty } \right],& \text{ if }\tau \in \left(-\epsilon ^{-1},0\right). \end{cases} \]

Let us check that $\Gamma ^\epsilon $ is indeed an element of $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. The concavity and monotonicity in $\tau $ follow from proposition 3.1.24. Since the $\Gamma _\tau $'s are $\mathcal I$-model, they are $\mathcal I$-good by example 7.1.7; hence

\[ (1+\epsilon \tau )\Gamma _\tau -\epsilon \tau \Gamma _{-\infty } \]

is $\mathcal I$-good by proposition 7.2.1. Therefore its $P$-envelope is also its $\mathcal I$-envelope, and the corresponding level is $\mathcal I$-model. The value at $-\infty $ is $\Gamma _{-\infty }$. Thus $\Gamma ^\epsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

For each fixed $\tau <0$, the argument of theorem 9.2.9 Step 3.3 gives

\[ \Gamma ^\epsilon _\tau \searrow \Gamma _\tau \quad \textup{as }\epsilon \to 0+. \]

By proposition 3.1.26 and corollary 6.2.11, this convergence is in $d_ S$. Hence theorem 10.4.13 4 gives

\[ \Delta _{Y_\bullet }(\theta ,\Gamma ^\epsilon _\tau )\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}\Delta _{Y_\bullet }(\theta ,\Gamma _\tau ) \]

for every $\tau <0$. Since

\[ \Gamma ^\epsilon _{-\infty }=\Gamma _{-\infty } \]

for all $\epsilon $, the underlying convex body is fixed. Therefore 10.5.11 applies and gives

\[ \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma ^\epsilon )\rightharpoonup \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma ). \]

It follows that $\operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )$ is independent of the choice of the flag. $\square$

More generally, when $X$ does not admit a smooth flag, we may choose a modification $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ so that $Y$ admits a flag. We define

13.32
\begin{equation} \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma )\coloneqq \operatorname{DH}(\pi ^*\Gamma ). \end{equation}

It follows from theorem 10.4.13 5 that this measure is independent of the choice of $\pi $.

Proposition 13.3.3. Let

\[ (\Gamma ^ i)_{i\geq 1}\subseteq \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0},\quad \Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}. \]

Assume one of the following conditions holds:

  1. The sequence $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\geq 1}$ is decreasing and $\Gamma =\inf _{i\geq 1}\Gamma ^ i$. Assume that

    13.33
    \begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}\Gamma =\lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^ i. \end{equation}

  2. The sequence $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\geq 1}$ is increasing and $\Gamma =\operatorname *{sup}_{i\geq 1}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i$.

Then

13.34
\begin{equation} \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma ^ i)\rightharpoonup \operatorname{DH}(\Gamma ). \end{equation}

Proof. We may assume that $X$ admits a smooth flag $Y_{\bullet }$.

Assume 1. Let

\[ \eta \coloneqq \inf _{i\geq 1}\Gamma ^ i_{-\infty }. \]

Since $\Gamma _\tau \leq \Gamma ^ i_{-\infty }$ for every $i$ and every $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \Gamma _{-\infty }\leq \eta . \]

By proposition 3.1.26, the decreasing limit $\eta $ is model and

\[ \int _ X\theta _\eta ^ n=\lim _{i\to \infty }\int _ X\theta _{\Gamma ^ i_{-\infty }}^ n=\lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^ i. \]

Using (13.33), this equals

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma =\int _ X\theta _{\Gamma _{-\infty }}^ n. \]

Since $\Gamma _{-\infty }\leq \eta $ and both potentials are model, proposition 6.1.4 gives

\[ \Gamma _{-\infty }=\eta . \]

Thus

\[ \Gamma _{-\infty }=\inf _{i\geq 1}\Gamma ^ i_{-\infty }. \]

We want to derive (13.34) from 10.5.11. It boils down to prove the following: For any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma ^ i_{\tau })\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}} \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma _{\tau }). \]

For fixed $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, the potentials $\Gamma ^ i_\tau $ decrease to $\Gamma _\tau $. By proposition 3.1.26, their masses converge to the mass of $\Gamma _\tau $; hence $\Gamma ^ i_\tau \xrightarrow {d_ S}\Gamma _\tau $ by corollary 6.2.11. Applying the continuity of the partial Okounkov body map, theorem 10.4.13 4, gives

\[ \Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma ^ i_{\tau })\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(\theta ,\Gamma _{\tau }). \]

The proof under the assumption 2 is similar. We only need to apply 10.5.12 instead of 10.5.11. $\square$

Remark 13.3.4. When $\{ \theta \} $ is a Hodge class and $\Gamma $ is induced by a test configuration as in example 9.3.9 and 9.3.10, our Duistermaat–Heckman measure coincides with the more traditional definition of [Section 3.2, BHJ17]. This is explained in [Remark 7.17, Xia21].
