From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 26.16.3

Theorem 26.16.3. Suppose $Q\geq 0$ and $Q\not\equiv 0$. The split form $g$ is Hermitian, and its lower mass is

26.95
\begin{equation} v_-(g)=V=3sA_ E\int _ B\omega ^2. \end{equation}

Every bounded $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,g)$ satisfies

\[ \int _ X(g+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3 =V+\frac{3s}{2\pi }\int _ XE_ u\wedge \pi ^*Q\geq V. \]

Every Hermitian form $h\geq c g$, with $c>0$, satisfies (26.94). Thus the endpoint estimate of theorem 26.8.2 holds for every Hermitian form on $X$, with a constant depending on the form. The manifold $X$ admits no pluriclosed Hermitian metric.

Proof. We suppress pullbacks in the curvature calculation. Comparing types in $\mathrm d\eta =F$ gives $\partial \eta =0$, $\bar\partial \eta =F$, $\partial \bar\eta =\bar F$ and $\bar\partial \bar\eta =0$. Hence

\[ \partial e=-\mathrm i\eta \wedge \bar F,\qquad \bar\partial e=\mathrm iF\wedge \bar\eta , \qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}e=-\frac1{2\pi }F\wedge \bar F=-\frac1{2\pi }Q. \]

Since $e^2=0$ and the base has dimension two,

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=-\frac{s}{2\pi }\pi ^*Q,\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g^2)=-\frac{s}{\pi }\pi ^*(\omega \wedge Q)=0, \qquad g^3=3s\pi ^*(\omega ^2)\wedge e. \]

The form $g$ is positive, because $\pi ^*\omega $ is positive on directions transverse to the fibers and $e$ is positive on the vertical line. Fiber integration of $g^3$ gives $V$, and lemma 26.16.1, lemma 26.16.2 give the mass identity and the endpoint bound. By compactness, every Hermitian form $h$ satisfies $h\geq cg$ for some $c>0$.

Finally, $S=-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ is smooth, semipositive and not identically zero. If a Hermitian form $b$ were pluriclosed, Stokes' theorem would give

\[ 0=\int _ Xg\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b =\int _ Xb\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=-\int _ Xb\wedge S<0, \]

where the last inequality holds because $b$ is strictly positive and $S$ is semipositive and nonzero on an open set. $\square$
