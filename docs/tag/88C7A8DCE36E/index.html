Chapter 5 · Plurisubharmonic functions and positive currents · Tag 88C7A8DCE36E

Uniform exponential

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Lemma 1.5.1

Lemma 1.5.1. There are constants $\alpha >0$ and $C<+\infty $, depending only on $(X,\omega ,\mathrm{d}V)$, such that

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha v}\, \mathrm{d}V\leq C \quad \hbox{for all }v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\hbox{ with }\sup _ Xv=0. \]

Proof. Suppose that no such pair exists. Set $a_ j=2^{-j}$ and choose normalized potentials $v_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with

\[ \int _ X\exp \! \left(-\frac{a_ j}{j}v_ j\right)\mathrm{d}V\geq 2^ j. \]

The partial sums $V_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ jv_ j$ decrease, since every $v_ j\leq 0$. They belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ by proposition 1.2.2, because $\sum _ ja_ j\leq 1$, and their $L^1$ norms are uniformly bounded by (1.1). By corollary 1.4.3, their limit $V$ is therefore $\omega $-plurisubharmonic. Since all summands are nonpositive, $V\leq a_ jv_ j$ for every $j$.

Fix $p>0$. For every $j\geq 1/p$,

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-pV}\, \mathrm{d}V \geq \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-pa_ jv_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V \geq \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-(a_ j/j)v_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V \geq 2^ j. \]

Thus $\mathrm{e}^{-pV}$ is not integrable for any $p>0$, which contradicts the integrability of $\mathrm{e}^{-a(V)V}$ obtained above from Skoda's theorem. $\square$
