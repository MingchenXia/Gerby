From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Definition 1.6.3
Definition 1.6.3. Let $D$ be an effective $\mathbb {Q}$-divisor
Note. Divisors and $\mathbb {Q}$-divisors are implicitly assumed to have locally finite coefficients as usual.
on $X$. We say $\varphi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$ has log singularities (along $D$) on $X$ if for each $x\in X$, there is an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that

  1. $D|_ U$ has finitely many irreducible components and can be written as

    \[ D|_ U=\sum _{i=1}^ N a_ i D_ i \]

    with $D_ i$ prime divisors on $U$, $a_ i\in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}$ and $s_ i$ holomorphic functions on $U$ defining $D_ i$, and

  2. we have

    1.39
    \begin{equation} \varphi |_ U=\sum _{i=1}^ N a_ i\log |s_ i|^2+R, \end{equation}

    where $R$ is a bounded function on $U$.
