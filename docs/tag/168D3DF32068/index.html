From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 13 Envelopes and volumes of singularities

Throughout this chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, with an auxiliary Hermitian metric $\omega $ and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$, and $\theta $ denotes a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ whose positivity is specified in each section.

In a closed background, the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère mass of a potential depends only on its singularity type; this is what makes mass a tool for comparing singularities. In a nonclosed background, a bounded change of the potential can change the mass, and a singularity type may contain potentials of different, even infinite, masses. This chapter opens the part of the book on relative singularities and the equations they govern, and introduces two kinds of invariants that take this into account.

The first records the variation of mass: the lower and upper volumes $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ and $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ of a potential $u$ are the infimum and the supremum of the masses of all potentials of the same singularity type. The second replaces a mass condition by a condition on envelopes. Fix a reference potential $\varphi $. Stretching a potential $u$ away from $\varphi $ by a factor $b\geq 1$ gives the obstacle $bu-(b-1)\varphi $, which is more singular than $u$ where $u$ lies below $\varphi $. The relative envelope class $\mathcal E_\varphi $ consists of the potentials $u$ at least as singular as $\varphi $ for which every such stretched obstacle lies above some $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function. This class is convex and stable under finite rooftops, its members have the same singularity envelope as $\varphi $, and its definition involves no mass.

The main tool is the contact property of envelopes: the Monge–Ampère measure of an envelope is carried by the set where it touches its obstacle. Together with ordered contact, it gives the domination principle for bounded potentials in a semipositive background and bounds the measures of rooftop envelopes. For envelopes of logarithmic growth on $\mathbb C^ n$, which correspond to envelopes on $\mathbb P^ n$, we also examine how the contact of the regularized envelope with its obstacle depends on the growth allowance.

In a semipositive big background, the contact property also produces positive mass. Let $\varphi $ be a model reference, that is, a fixed point of the singularity envelope that admits a competitor in a smaller background. Every member $u$ of $\mathcal E_\varphi $ then has a competitor in a background $a\theta $ with $0<a<1$, and an envelope in the remaining big background $(1-a)\theta $ has positive mass, which it transfers to $u$ on the contact set. The argument uses only that each bounded potential has positive mass, so it applies even when the infimum of these masses is zero. Conversely, if the Hermitian metric $\omega $ does not have the bounded mass property of definition 6.5.1, the relative envelope class of the zero potential contains potentials of infinite mass. Envelope conditions and mass bounds are therefore different requirements, and this distinction is essential for the equations with prescribed singularities in the next chapter.

We use the following notation from chapter 1. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$, we write $u\preceq v$ if $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$, and $u\sim v$ if $u\preceq v$ and $v\preceq u$; the equivalence class of $u$ is its singularity type (definition 1.12.1). For an obstacle $f$, the envelope $P_\theta (f)$ is the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of all $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $v\leq f$ quasi-everywhere; it may be identically $-\infty $, and then it does not belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the rooftop envelope is $P_\theta (u,v)=P_\theta (\min (u,v))$, and $V_\theta =P_\theta (0)$ is the potential with minimal singularities; see section 1.19. The singularity envelope $P_\theta [u]$, its variant $P_{\theta ,F}[u]$ with a smooth obstacle $F$, and envelope fixed points, that is, potentials with $P_\theta [u]=u$, are defined in definition 1.19.4. We write $\theta _ u=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and $\omega _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$ denotes the extended Monge–Ampère measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$ of theorem 6.1.2. It is the Bedford–Taylor measure $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ when $u$ is bounded, it puts no mass on pluripolar sets, and its total mass, the extended mass of $u$, may be infinite.
