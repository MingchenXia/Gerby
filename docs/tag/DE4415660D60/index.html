16.9 Comparison of relative capacitiesDE4415660D60

We now compare the relative capacities $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ with each other and with the capacity of a Kähler form. The main tool is the envelope $S$ of lemma 16.9.3, an increasing limit of envelopes of smooth penalties that vanish exactly on a compact set $K$. Its Monge–Ampère measure is carried by $K$, and its height $\sup _ XS$ controls the relative capacity of $K$ from both sides.

All backgrounds are smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms. In each statement, $\mu _ q$ denotes the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of $q$ for the background indicated there, and the mass of $q$ is $\mu _ q(X)$; when two backgrounds occur, we write the measures explicitly, as in $\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n\rangle $. For a Kähler form $\kappa $ we have $V_\kappa =0$, since the constant $0$ is $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic and $V_\kappa \leq 0$; we write $\Omega =\int _ X\kappa ^ n$ and $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa =\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,0}$.

The capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $ is the Monge–Ampère capacity $c_\kappa $ of section 14.8. Indeed, $v\mapsto v+1$ maps the competitors for $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $ onto the functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ with values in $[0,1]$, and non-pluripolar products of bounded potentials are Bedford–Taylor products (definition 15.1.3). Since $\kappa $ is closed, the form $\sigma $ in (14.49) is closed, hence locally $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a smooth function (lemma 7.3.1), and the multilinearity of Bedford–Taylor products identifies the products defined there with the Bedford–Taylor products of local potentials.

Lemma 16.9.1. Let $\kappa $ be a Kähler form, and let $p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ be bounded. Then $p$ is the pointwise limit of smooth functions $p_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ with $\sup _ j\sup _ X|p_ j|<\infty $.

Proof. Apply theorem 13.4.5 with $\kappa $ as the Kähler form of reference. It gives potentials $\Phi _ j$ decreasing to $p$ with $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ j\geq -\varepsilon _ j\kappa $, where $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, and with gentle analytic singularities of type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^ jp))$. Since $p$ is bounded, $\mathrm e^{-2^ jp}$ is bounded, so $\mathcal I(2^ jp)$ is the unit ideal. In the local presentations $\Phi _ j=2^{-j}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+b$ of definition 13.1.5, the functions $g_ i$ therefore generate the unit ideal and have no common zero, so $\Phi _ j$ is bounded. Hence the smooth function $\exp (2^ j\Phi _ j)$ has no zeros, and $\Phi _ j=2^{-j}\log \exp (2^ j\Phi _ j)$ is smooth. Put $p_ j=\Phi _ j/(1+\varepsilon _ j)$. Then

\[ \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_ j=\frac{(1+\varepsilon _ j)\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ j}{1+\varepsilon _ j}\geq 0. \]

The bounds $p\leq \Phi _ j\leq \Phi _1$ give $|p_ j|\leq |\Phi _ j|\leq \max \{ \sup _ X|p|,\sup _ X|\Phi _1|\} $, and $p_ j\to p$ pointwise because $\Phi _ j\to p$ and $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$ (unlike the $\Phi _ j$, the $p_ j$ need not decrease). $\square$

Lemma 16.9.2. Let $\kappa $ be a Kähler form, let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ satisfy $v\leq u\leq v+B$ for a constant $B\geq 0$, and let $p$ be a bounded function in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$. Then

16.77
\begin{equation} \int _ Xp\, \mu _ u\geq \int _ Xp\, \mu _ v-nB\Omega . \end{equation}

Moreover, there is a constant $C_\kappa $, depending only on $X$ and $\kappa $, such that for every model potential $\phi $ for the background $\kappa $, every $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ with $\phi -1\leq v\leq \phi $, and every $p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ with $\sup _ Xp=0$, bounded or not,

16.78
\begin{equation} \int _ X(-p)\, \mu _ v\leq C_\kappa . \end{equation}

Proof. Write $\kappa _ q=\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$. We first prove (16.77) when $u=v$ near the poles of $v$ and then reduce to this case; the second assertion follows from the first.

A special case. Assume first that there are $l_0>0$ and an open set $U\supset \{ v<-l_0\} $ on which $u=v$. For $l>l_0$ put $u_ l=\max \{ u,-l\} $ and $v_ l=\max \{ v,-l\} $; these are bounded functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ with $0\leq u_ l-v_ l\leq B$. On $U$ we have $u_ l=v_ l$ and $u=v$, so $\mu _{u_ l}=\mu _{v_ l}$ and $\mu _ u=\mu _ v$ there. The complement of $U$ lies in the plurifine open set $\{ v>-l\} $, on which $u_ l=u$ and $v_ l=v$, because $u\geq v$; by plurifine locality, $\mu _{u_ l}=\mu _ u$ and $\mu _{v_ l}=\mu _ v$ on this set. Hence $\mu _{u_ l}-\mu _{v_ l}=\mu _ u-\mu _ v$. Expanding $\kappa _{u_ l}^ n-\kappa _{v_ l}^ n$ and integrating by parts with lemma 15.4.2, we obtain

\[ \begin{split} \int _ Xp\, (\mu _ u-\mu _ v)=\int _ Xp(\mu _{u_ l}-\mu _{v_ l}) & =\sum _{j=0}^{n-1}\int _ X(u_ l-v_ l)\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p \wedge \kappa _{u_ l}^{j}\wedge \kappa _{v_ l}^{n-1-j}\\ & \geq -nB\Omega . \end{split} \]

For the last inequality write $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p=(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)-\kappa $: the first part contributes a nonnegative amount, since $u_ l-v_ l\geq 0$, and the second contributes at least $-B\int _ X\kappa \wedge \kappa _{u_ l}^ j\wedge \kappa _{v_ l}^{n-1-j}=-B\Omega $, by proposition 15.4.1.

The general case. Subtracting a common constant, we may assume $v\leq 0$. For $0<a<1$ put $v_ a=av$ and $u_ a=\max \{ u,av\} $; these belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ because $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(av)=(1-a)\kappa +a\kappa _ v$. Since $v\leq av$, we have $v_ a\leq u_ a\leq v_ a+B$. On the open set $U_ a=\{ v<-B/(1-a)\} $ we have $av-v>B\geq u-v$, so $u_ a=v_ a$ there. With $l_0=aB/(1-a)+1$, the set $\{ v_ a<-l_0\} =\{ v<-l_0/a\} $ is contained in $U_ a$, so the special case applies to $u_ a$ and $v_ a$.

As $a\uparrow 1$, $v_ a$ and $u_ a$ decrease to $v$ and $u$. The masses of $u_ a$ and $v_ a$ agree, and by multilinearity $\int _ X\langle \kappa _{v_ a}^ n\rangle $ is a polynomial in $a$ that tends to $\int _ X\langle \kappa _ v^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \kappa _ u^ n\rangle $. Decreasing sequences converge in extended capacity (lemma 15.6.3), so theorem 15.6.5 shows that $\mu _{u_ a}\to \mu _ u$ and $\mu _{v_ a}\to \mu _ v$ weakly along every sequence $a\uparrow 1$. Passing to the limit in (16.77) for $u_ a$ and $v_ a$, we obtain (16.77) for every continuous, in particular every smooth, $p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$. For a bounded $p$, take the approximations $p_ j$ of lemma 16.9.1; they converge pointwise with a common bound, and dominated convergence with respect to the finite measures $\mu _ u$ and $\mu _ v$ proves (16.77).

The second assertion. Let $\phi $ be a model potential for $\kappa $. Since $V_\kappa =0$, proposition 16.3.3 gives $\mu _\phi \leq \kappa ^ n$. If $\phi -1\leq v\leq \phi $, the first assertion, applied to $\phi \leq v+1\leq \phi +1$, gives for every bounded $p$ with $\sup _ Xp=0$

\[ \int _ X(-p)\, \mu _ v\leq \int _ X(-p)\, \mu _\phi +n\Omega \leq \int _ X(-p)\, \kappa ^ n+n\Omega . \]

By proposition 14.2.1, applied on the compact Kähler manifold $(X,\kappa )$ with background $\kappa $, the last integral is bounded by a constant depending only on $X$ and $\kappa $. For an unbounded $p$ with $\sup _ Xp=0$, apply this bound to the truncations $\max \{ p,-l\} $, which are bounded, belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$ and have supremum zero, and let $l\to \infty $ by monotone convergence. $\square$

Lemma 16.9.3. Suppose that $\{ \theta \} $ is big, let $\phi $ be a model potential of mass $m>0$, and let $K\subset X$ be a compact non-pluripolar set. There is a smooth function $g\geq 0$ on $X$ whose zero set is $K$. For every such $g$, the envelopes $q_ j=P_\theta (\phi +jg)$, $j\geq 1$, are potentials that increase with $j$, and their regularized limit $S=(\sup _ jq_ j)^*$ is a potential such that $L=\sup _ XS\geq 0$ and

\[ \begin{gathered} \phi \leq S\leq \phi +L,\qquad S=\phi \text{ quasi-everywhere on }K,\\ \int _ X\langle \theta _ S^ n\rangle =m,\qquad \mu _ S(X\setminus K)=0. \end{gathered} \]

In particular,

16.81
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(K) \geq \frac{m}{\max \{ 1,L\} ^{n}}. \end{equation}

If $\theta =\kappa $ is a Kähler form, then also

16.82
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\phi }(K) \leq \frac{C}{\max \{ 1,L\} }, \end{equation}

where $C$ depends only on $X$ and $\kappa $.

Proof. We first construct $g$. If $K=X$, take $g=0$. Otherwise fix finitely many coordinate charts $D_ i$ and compact sets $K_ i\subset D_ i$ whose interiors cover $X$, and measure derivatives on $K_ i$ in the coordinates of $D_ i$. Each point of $X\setminus K$ is the center of a coordinate ball whose closure lies in $X\setminus K$; since $X\setminus K$ has a countable basis, countably many of the concentric balls of half the radius, say $B_1',B_2',\ldots $, cover $X\setminus K$. Let $B_ l$ be the corresponding ball of full radius, and choose a smooth function $g_ l\geq 0$ with compact support in $B_ l$ that is positive on $\overline{B_ l'}$ (a smooth cutoff function, lemma 1.4.1). Multiplying $g_ l$ by a small positive constant, we may assume that all its derivatives of order at most $l$ are bounded by $2^{-l}$ on every $K_ i$. Then the series $g=\sum _ lg_ l$ converges uniformly on every $K_ i$ together with all its derivatives, so $g$ is smooth on the interior of every $K_ i$, hence on $X$; it vanishes on $K$, and it is positive on $X\setminus K$, because every point there lies in some $B_ l'$.

Now let $g$ be any smooth nonnegative function with zero set $K$, and put $q_ j=P_\theta (\phi +jg)$. Since $\phi $ is a competitor and every competitor is at most $\phi +j\sup _ Xg$, we have $\phi \leq q_ j\leq \phi +j\sup _ Xg$, so $q_ j\sim \phi $. The obstacle $\phi +jg$ is upper semicontinuous, so $q_ j\leq \phi +jg$ everywhere by proposition 16.1.4, and therefore $q_ j=\phi $ on $K$. In particular $\sup _ Kq_ j\leq 0$, and corollary 14.2.4 bounds $\sup _ Xq_ j$ uniformly in $j$. The envelopes $q_ j$ increase with $j$, since $g\geq 0$, so $S=(\sup _ jq_ j)^*$ is a potential; put $L=\sup _ XS$. Then $L\geq 0$, because $S\geq \phi $ and $\sup _ X\phi =0$. Since $q_ j\preceq \phi $ and $\sup _ Xq_ j\leq \sup _ XS=L$, lemma 16.4.2 gives $q_ j\leq \phi +L$, hence $\phi \leq S\leq \phi +L$. By corollary 10.2.2, applied after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $, the regularization changes $\sup _ jq_ j$ only on a pluripolar set, so $S=\phi $ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Since $S\sim \phi $, $\int _ X\langle \theta _ S^ n\rangle =m$.

The obstacle $\phi +jg$ is quasi-continuous, and its family of competitors is nonempty and bounded above by $j\sup _ Xg$, so theorem 16.2.5 shows that $\mu _{q_ j}$ is carried by the contact set $\{ q_ j=\phi +jg\} $. At a point of this set where $\phi $ is finite, the bound $q_ j\leq \phi +L$ gives $jg\leq L$. Thus $\mu _{q_ j}$ is carried by $\{ g\leq L/j\} $. By corollary 15.7.6, $\mu _{q_ j}\to \mu _ S$ weakly. If $\chi \geq 0$ is continuous with compact support in $X\setminus K$, then $g$ has a positive lower bound $c$ on the support of $\chi $, so $\int _ X\chi \, \mu _{q_ j}=0$ for $j>L/c$, and in the limit $\int _ X\chi \, \mu _ S=0$. By lemma 16.2.1(2), applied on $W=X\setminus K$ with the zero measure, $\mu _ S(X\setminus K)=0$.

Set $t=1/\max \{ 1,L\} $ and $h=(1-t)\phi +tS-tL$. Then $\phi -1\leq \phi -tL\leq h\leq \phi $, so $h$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$, and $\mu _ h\geq t^ n\mu _ S$. Hence $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(K)\geq t^ n\mu _ S(K)=t^ nm$, which is (16.81).

In the Kähler case apply (16.78) to the potential $p=S-L$, which has supremum zero. Quasi-everywhere on $K$ we have $-p=L-\phi \geq L$, so $L\mu _ v(K)\leq C_\kappa $ for every competitor $v$ for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\phi }$. If $L\geq 1$, this gives (16.82) with $C=C_\kappa $. If $L<1$, use $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\phi }(K)\leq \operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\phi }(X)=m\leq \Omega $; the last inequality is the monotonicity of mass, because $\phi \preceq V_\kappa =0$ and the mass of $0$ is $\int _ X\kappa ^ n=\Omega $. Thus $C=\max \{ C_\kappa ,\Omega \} $ works. $\square$

The capacity of a compact set controls the height $L$ of its penalized envelope, and the uniform exponential integrability of normalized potentials converts this height into a volume estimate. The resulting constants do not depend on the model potential.

Proposition 16.9.4. Suppose that $\{ \theta \} $ is big, and let $\phi $ be a model potential of mass $m>0$. There are $\alpha ,C>0$, depending only on $X$, $\theta $ and $\omega $, such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies

16.83
\begin{equation} \int _ E\omega ^ n\leq C\exp \left[-\alpha \left(\frac{m}{\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)}\right)^{1/n}\right], \end{equation}

where the right side is interpreted as zero when the capacity is zero.

Proof. Let $\alpha $ and $C_0$ be the constants of corollary 14.2.3. First let $K$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $S,L$ be as in lemma 16.9.3. The potential $p=S-L$ has supremum zero and satisfies $p\leq -L$ quasi-everywhere on $K$, because $S=\phi \leq 0$ there. Since pluripolar sets have zero volume, (14.17) gives

\[ \int _ K\omega ^ n\leq \int _ K\mathrm e^{-\alpha p-\alpha L}\, \omega ^ n\leq C_0\mathrm e^{-\alpha L}. \]

Write $c=\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(K)$, which is positive by (16.81). By the same inequality,

\[ \left(\frac mc\right)^{1/n}\leq \max \{ 1,L\} \leq 1+L, \]

so (16.83) holds for $K$ with $C=C_0\mathrm e^\alpha $. If $K$ is pluripolar, its volume and its capacity both vanish.

Now let $E$ be a Borel set. If $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)>0$, the estimate for compact subsets $K\subset E$, together with the monotonicity of capacity and the inner regularity of the smooth volume, gives (16.83). If $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)=0$, every compact subset of $E$ has zero capacity, hence is pluripolar by (16.81) and has zero volume; by inner regularity, $E$ has zero volume. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.5. Under the hypotheses of proposition 16.9.4, let $p>1$, $0\leq f\in L^ p(X,\omega ^ n)$ and $r>0$. There is $B_ r>0$, depending only on $X$, $\theta $, $\omega $, $p$ and $r$, such that every Borel set $E$ satisfies

16.84
\begin{equation} \int _ E f\omega ^ n\leq B_ r\| f\| _{L^ p} \left(\frac{\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)}m\right)^ r. \end{equation}

In particular, for every $r>1$ the measure $f\omega ^ n$ is dominated by a constant multiple of $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }^ r$.

Proof. If $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)=0$, then $E$ has zero volume by proposition 16.9.4, and both sides vanish. Otherwise let $q=p/(p-1)$. By Hölder's inequality and (16.83), the integral is at most

\[ \| f\| _{L^ p}C^{1/q}\exp \left[-\frac\alpha q \left(\frac{m}{\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)}\right)^{1/n}\right]. \]

Put $t=(m/\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E))^{1/n}$; then $t\geq 1$, since the capacity is at most $\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle =m$. The function $t^{nr}\mathrm e^{-\alpha t/q}$ is bounded on $[1,\infty )$: its logarithmic derivative is $nr/t-\alpha /q$, so its maximum is attained at $1$ or at $nrq/\alpha $. Hence $\mathrm e^{-\alpha t/q}\leq A\, t^{-nr}$ for a constant $A$ depending only on $n,r,q,\alpha $, and this proves (16.84). $\square$

Proposition 16.9.6. Let $\kappa $ be a Kähler form and $\phi $ a model potential for $\kappa $ of mass $m>0$. There is $A\geq 1$ such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies

16.85
\begin{equation} A^{-1}\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa (E)^ n \leq \operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\phi }(E) \leq A\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa (E)^{1/n}. \end{equation}

More generally, if $\theta \leq \kappa $, $\{ \theta \} $ is big and $\phi $ is a model potential for $\theta $ of mass $m>0$, then

16.86
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Cap}_\kappa (E)^ n \leq \frac{C^ n}{m}\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E), \end{equation}

where $C$ depends only on $X$ and $\kappa $.

Proof. All capacities involved are inner regular (proposition 16.7.6), so it suffices to treat compact sets $K$: the inequalities for a Borel set $E$ follow by applying them to the compact subsets of $E$ and taking suprema. If $K$ is pluripolar, all capacities of $K$ vanish. Otherwise fix a smooth function $g\geq 0$ with zero set $K$, and apply lemma 16.9.3 with this $g$ to $(\theta ,\phi )$ and to $(\kappa ,0)$; here $0=V_\kappa $ is a model potential for $\kappa $ of mass $\Omega $, by proposition 16.3.2(4). Denote the resulting envelopes by $S_\phi $ and $S_0$ and their suprema by $L_\phi $ and $L_0$. Every competitor for $P_\theta (\phi +jg)$ is $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic, because $\theta \leq \kappa $, and it is at most $\phi +jg\leq jg$ quasi-everywhere, so $P_\theta (\phi +jg)\leq P_\kappa (jg)$ for every $j$; hence $S_\phi \leq S_0$ and $L_\phi \leq L_0$. The two estimates of lemma 16.9.3 give

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\kappa (K)^ n \leq \frac{C^ n}{\max \{ 1,L_0\} ^ n} \leq \frac{C^ n}{\max \{ 1,L_\phi \} ^ n} \leq \frac{C^ n}{m}\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(K). \]

This proves (16.86) and, for $\theta =\kappa $, the first inequality in (16.85).

For the upper bound let $\theta =\kappa $, and choose $b>1$ with $b^ n(\Omega -m)<\Omega $. By theorem 16.4.4, applied to $\phi \preceq 0$, where the mass of $0$ is $\int _ X\kappa ^ n=\Omega $, the envelope $\eta =E_ b(\phi ,0)=P_\kappa (b\phi )$ is a potential, and it satisfies $b^{-1}\eta \leq \phi $; in particular $\eta =-\infty $ at the poles of $\phi $. Write $c=1-b^{-1}>0$. For fixed $j$ and $\delta >0$ consider

\[ v_ j=c\, P_\kappa (jg)+b^{-1}\eta -\delta , \]

a convex combination of $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic functions minus a constant. The function $P_\kappa (jg)$ is upper semicontinuous and vanishes on $K$, because $0\leq P_\kappa (jg)\leq jg$. Hence $P_\kappa (jg)<\delta /c$ on a neighborhood $U$ of $K$, and there $v_ j<b^{-1}\eta \leq \phi $. Outside the poles of $\phi $, the difference $v_ j-\phi $ is bounded above by $cj\sup _ Xg$, and $g$ has a positive minimum on $X\setminus U$. Therefore $v_ j\leq \phi +lg$ for a sufficiently large integer $l$, so $v_ j\leq P_\kappa (\phi +lg)\leq S_\phi $. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ and then $j\to \infty $, and using that the increasing limit of $P_\kappa (jg)$ equals $S_0$ almost everywhere, we obtain

\[ cS_0+b^{-1}\eta \leq S_\phi \]

almost everywhere, hence everywhere.

Choose a Borel set $G$ of positive volume on which $b^{-1}\eta \geq -D$ for a constant $D$; such a set exists because $\eta $ is finite almost everywhere. By proposition 14.2.1, there is a constant $B$, independent of $K$, with $\int _ X|S_0-L_0|\, \kappa ^ n\leq B$. Hence $S_0\geq L_0-B/\int _ G\kappa ^ n$ at some point $x\in G$, for otherwise $\int _ G(L_0-S_0)\, \kappa ^ n>B$. At this point the last display gives $L_\phi \geq S_\phi (x)\geq cL_0-D'$ with $D'=cB/\int _ G\kappa ^ n+D$, which does not depend on $K$. If $L_0\geq \max \{ 1,2D'/c\} $, then $L_\phi \geq cL_0/2$, and lemma 16.9.3 gives

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\phi }(K)\leq \frac{2C}{cL_0} \leq \frac{2C}{c\, \Omega ^{1/n}}\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa (K)^{1/n}; \]

the last inequality is the lower bound (16.81) for the construction with reference potential $0$, which gives $\Omega /L_0^ n\leq \operatorname {Cap}_\kappa (K)$. In the remaining range $L_0<\max \{ 1,2D'/c\} $, the same lower bound shows that $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa (K)$ is bounded below by a positive constant independent of $K$, and we use $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\phi }(K)\leq m$, enlarging $A$ if necessary. The constant $A$ in the upper bound depends on $\phi $. $\square$

Lemma 16.9.7. Suppose that $\{ \theta \} $ is big, let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ have positive mass, and let $\psi \in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. There is a continuous nondecreasing function $F\colon [0,\infty )\to [0,\infty )$ with $F(0)=0$, which may depend on $\psi $ and $\phi $, such that

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(E) \leq F(\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)) \]

for every Borel set $E\subset X$.

Proof. Since $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ does not change when a constant is added to $\phi $ (proposition 16.7.6), we may assume that $\psi \leq \phi $. Let $u$ be a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$, so that $\psi -1\leq u\leq \psi $, and let $t\geq 1$. We estimate $\mu _ u(E)$ separately on $\{ u>\phi -2t\} $ and on $\{ u\leq \phi -2t\} $.

Put $u_ t=\max \{ u,\phi -2t\} $, so that $\phi -2t\leq u_ t\leq \phi $. The potential $(1-(2t)^{-1})\phi +(2t)^{-1}u_ t$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$, and its measure is at least $(2t)^{-n}\mu _{u_ t}$. Since $u_ t=u$ on the plurifine open set $\{ u>\phi -2t\} $, it follows that

\[ \mu _ u(E\cap \{ u>\phi -2t\} ) \leq (2t)^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E). \]

The potentials $\psi $, $u$ and $\psi -1$ belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. So does the midpoint $q=(u+\phi )/2$: by proposition 16.4.10, $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [\phi ]$, so by proposition 16.3.2 each mixed mass $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ k\wedge \theta _\phi ^{n-k}\rangle $ equals the mass of $\phi $. Expanding $\mu _ q$ by multilinearity, we see that $q$ also has the mass of $\phi $. On the set $\{ u\leq \phi -2t\} $ we have $q-t\geq u\geq \psi -1$, and on $\{ \psi -1\leq q-t\} $ we have $\psi -1\leq (\psi +\phi )/2-t$, that is, $\psi \leq \phi -2t+2\leq \phi -t+1$. Thus

\[ \{ u\leq \phi -2t\} \subset \{ \psi -1\leq q-t\} \subset \{ \psi \leq \phi -t+1\} . \]

The non-strict comparison in proposition 16.7.4, applied with $p=n$ to $\psi -1$ and $q-t$, together with $\mu _ q\geq 2^{-n}\mu _ u$, gives

\[ \begin{split} \mu _ u(\{ u\leq \phi -2t\} )& \leq 2^ n\mu _ q(\{ \psi -1\leq q-t\} ) \leq 2^ n\mu _\psi (\{ \psi -1\leq q-t\} )\\ & \leq 2^ n\mu _\psi (\{ \psi \leq \phi -t+1\} ). \end{split} \]

Adding the two estimates and taking the supremum over $u$, we obtain

16.89
\begin{equation} \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(E) \leq (2t)^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E) +2^ n\mu _\psi (\{ \psi \leq \phi -t+1\} ) \qquad (t\geq 1). \end{equation}

The last term tends to zero as $t\to \infty $, since $\psi $ and $\phi $ are finite outside a $\mu _\psi $-null set.

It remains to replace the right side by a continuous function of the capacity. Put $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle $, and let $H(s)$ be the minimum of $m$ and of the infimum over $t\geq 1$ of the right side of (16.89) with $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)$ replaced by $s$. Since $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }\leq \int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle =m$, we have $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(E)\leq H(\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E))$. The function $H$ is nondecreasing, and $H(s)\to 0$ as $s\downarrow 0$. Choose a strictly decreasing sequence $s_ k\downarrow 0$ with $H(s_ k)\leq m2^{-k}$. Define $F(0)=0$, $F(s_ k)=m2^{1-k}$, $F$ linear on each interval $[s_{k+1},s_ k]$, and $F=m$ on $[s_1,\infty )$. On $[s_{k+1},s_ k]$ we have $H(s)\leq H(s_ k)\leq m2^{-k}=F(s_{k+1})\leq F(s)$, and $H\leq m=F$ on $[s_1,\infty )$. Thus $F$ is a continuous nondecreasing majorant of $H$ with $F(0)=0$. $\square$

Theorem 16.9.8. Suppose that $\{ \theta \} $ is big and that $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has positive mass. There are continuous nondecreasing functions $F,G\colon [0,\infty )\to [0,\infty )$, vanishing at zero, such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(E)\leq F(\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)), \qquad \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)\leq G(\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(E)). \]

Consequently, any two capacities of this kind, for possibly different big backgrounds and potentials of positive mass, are bounded by such functions of each other. In particular, they define the same notion of convergence in capacity, in which Borel functions $f_ j$ converge to $f$ if the capacities of the sets $\{ |f_ j-f|>\varepsilon \} $ tend to zero for every $\varepsilon >0$. They also define the same notion of uniform absolute continuity of a family of positive Borel measures: $\lambda (A)$ is small, uniformly over the members $\lambda $ of the family, whenever the Borel set $A$ has small capacity.

Proof. Choose $A\geq 1$ with $\theta \leq \kappa =A\omega $. Every competitor $v$ for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\psi }$, and its measure for $\kappa $ dominates its measure for $\theta $: since $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v=(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)+(\kappa -\theta )$ with a smooth closed semipositive form $\kappa -\theta $, multilinearity and positivity of non-pluripolar products give $\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\rangle \geq \langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\rangle $; hence $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }\leq \operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\psi }$, and the mass of $\psi $ for $\kappa $ is at least its mass for $\theta $, hence positive. Adding a constant to $\psi $ does not change the capacities, so we may assume $\psi \leq 0$. Let $\Phi =P_\kappa [\psi ]$. By theorem 16.4.7, $\Phi $ is a model potential for $\kappa $ with $\psi \leq \Phi $ and with the same mass for $\kappa $ as $\psi $. Thus $\psi \in \mathcal E(X,\kappa ,\Phi )$, and lemma 16.9.7 and proposition 16.9.6 bound $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\psi }$ by a continuous nondecreasing function, vanishing at zero, of $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $.

Finally, rescaling the competitors gives

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega \leq \operatorname {Cap}_\kappa \leq A^ n\operatorname {Cap}_\omega : \]

every competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ is one for $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $, with a larger measure by the same argument, and if $v$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $, then $v/A$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ with measure $A^{-n}(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$. This proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality use the model potential $\phi =P_\theta [\psi ]$ for $\theta $, which has the same positive mass $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle $ as $\psi $. By (16.86), $\operatorname {Cap}_\kappa ^ n\leq (C^ n/m)\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$. By theorem 16.8.1 with $a=2$, applied to $\psi $ and $\phi $, whose singularity envelopes agree, the potential $\eta =P_\theta (2\psi -\phi )$ has mass $m$ and satisfies $P_\theta [\eta ]=\phi $ and $(\eta +\phi )/2\leq \psi $.

It remains to bound $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ by a function of $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$. Put $u=(\eta +\phi )/2$. Since $P_\theta [\eta ]=P_\theta [\phi ]$, the preservation of mixed masses in proposition 16.3.2 shows that $u$ also has mass $m$. Since $u\leq \psi $, this means that $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\psi )$, and lemma 16.9.7 bounds $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,u}$ by a continuous nondecreasing function, vanishing at zero, of $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$. If $v$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$, then $h=(v+\eta )/2$ satisfies $u-1/2\leq h\leq u$, so it is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,u}$, and $\mu _ h\geq 2^{-n}\mu _ v$. Thus

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }\leq 2^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,u}. \]

Combining these inequalities with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega \leq \operatorname {Cap}_\kappa $ gives $G$; compositions, positive multiples and $n$th roots preserve continuity, monotonicity and the value zero at the origin. The remaining assertions follow by comparing both capacities with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. $\square$

Proposition 16.9.9. Let $B_\alpha \Subset B'_\alpha $ be finitely many pairs of concentric coordinate balls such that each $\overline{B'_\alpha }$ lies in a coordinate chart and the balls $B_\alpha $ cover $X$, and let $\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }$ be the capacities of definition 9.3.2 in these coordinates. Then there is a constant $c\geq 1$ such that every Borel set $E\subset X$ satisfies

\[ c^{-1}\sum _\alpha \operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(E\cap B_\alpha ) \leq \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E) \leq c\sum _\alpha \operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(E\cap B_\alpha ), \]

and moreover $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)\leq c\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(E)$ for every $\alpha $ and every Borel set $E\subset B'_\alpha $. Consequently, every relative capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$ as in theorem 16.9.8 has the following properties.

  1. A compact set has capacity zero if and only if it is pluripolar. Hence a Borel set has capacity zero if and only if all its compact subsets are pluripolar.

  2. If real Borel functions $f_ j$ converge to a real Borel function $f$ locally in capacity on every ball $B'_\alpha $ (definition 9.4.5), then $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(\{ |f_ j-f|>\delta \} )\to 0$ for every $\delta >0$.

  3. Every quasi-continuous function on $X$ is continuous outside open sets of arbitrarily small capacity. Moreover, let $\theta '$ be any smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. For every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$ and every $\varepsilon >0$ there is an open set $G$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(G)<\varepsilon $ which contains the pole set of $u$ and such that $u$ is finite and continuous on $X\setminus G$.

Proof. Let $v$ be a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $, so that $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ and $-1\leq v\leq 0$. Fix $\alpha $ and write $B'_\alpha =B(0,R_\alpha )$ in its coordinates. Since $\overline{B'_\alpha }$ is a compact subset of a chart, there is $A_\alpha >0$ with $\omega \leq A_\alpha \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ on $B'_\alpha $ (lemma 13.1.1). Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v+A_\alpha |z|^2)=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)+(A_\alpha \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2-\omega )\geq 0$, so $v+A_\alpha |z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $B'_\alpha $, by lemma 13.1.4(2), applied on $B'_\alpha $ to the form $A_\alpha \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(A_\alpha |z|^2)$, and the multilinearity and positivity of Bedford–Taylor products give $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v+A_\alpha |z|^2))^ n\geq (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$ on $B'_\alpha $. Put $c_\alpha =1+A_\alpha R_\alpha ^2$. The function $w=(v+A_\alpha |z|^2+1)/c_\alpha $ is plurisubharmonic on $B'_\alpha $ with values in $[0,1]$, and $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\leq c_\alpha ^ n(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ there. Hence $\mu _ v(E)\leq c_\alpha ^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(E)$ for every Borel set $E\subset B'_\alpha $, and taking the supremum over $v$ gives $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)\leq c_\alpha ^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(E)$. Applying this to the sets $E\cap B_\alpha $ and using the subadditivity of $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ (proposition 16.7.6), we obtain the upper bound.

For the lower bound fix $\alpha $ and write $B_\alpha =B(0,r)$ and $B'_\alpha =B(0,R)$ in the coordinates, with $r<R$. Choose $L>0$ with $L(R^2-r^2)>1$, and then $b>0$ so small that $\omega \geq bL\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ on $B(0,R)$ (lemma 13.1.1) and that we can choose a number $a$ with

\[ b(1+Lr^2)<a<bLR^2,\qquad a\leq 1. \]

Let $w$ be plurisubharmonic on $B(0,R)$ with $-1\leq w\leq 0$, and put $q=bw-bL|z|^2$. Then $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq b\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\geq 0$ on $B(0,R)$. At the points of $B(0,R)$ where $|z|^2>a/(bL)$, which include a neighborhood of the boundary sphere because $a<bLR^2$, we have $q\leq -bL|z|^2<-a$.

Define $v$ to be $\max \{ q,-a\} $ on $B(0,R)$ and $-a$ elsewhere. Then $v=-a$ on the open set $U$ formed by $X\setminus \overline{B(0,R)}$ and the points of the chart where $|z|^2>a/(bL)$. Since $U$ and $B(0,R)$ cover $X$, and $v$ is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic on each of them, $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. It takes values in $[-1,0]$, and on $B(0,r)$ we have $q\geq -b(1+Lr^2)>-a$, so $v=q$ there. Since $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$ is the sum of the semipositive form $\omega -bL\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and $b\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$, positivity of the mixed terms gives $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\geq b^ n(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ on $B(0,r)$. The capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_{B(0,R)}$ may equally be computed with competitors taking values in $[-1,0]$, so it follows that

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)\geq b^ n \operatorname {Cap}_{B(0,R)}(E) \qquad (E\subset B(0,r)\text{ Borel}). \]

Summing over the finitely many balls gives the lower bound.

It remains to prove (1)–(3). Let $F$ and $G$ be the functions of theorem 16.9.8 for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$; since they are continuous and vanish at zero, a sequence of Borel sets has capacities $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }$ tending to zero if and only if their capacities $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ tend to zero.

(1) Pluripolar Borel sets have capacity zero by proposition 16.7.6. Conversely, let $K$ be a compact set that is not pluripolar. Since $\omega $ is Kähler, $0=V_\omega $ is a model potential for $\omega $ of mass $\int _ X\omega ^ n>0$, by proposition 16.3.2, and (16.81) for this model potential gives $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (K)>0$. Since $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (K)\leq G(\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(K))$ and $G(0)=0$, it follows that $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(K)>0$. The assertion about Borel sets follows from the inner regularity in proposition 16.7.6.

(2) Let $\delta >0$ and $A_ j=\{ |f_ j-f|>\delta \} $. By the upper bound, $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A_ j)\leq c\sum _\alpha \operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(A_ j\cap B_\alpha )$. Each term is at most the outer capacity $\operatorname {Cap}^*_{B'_\alpha }(\overline{B_\alpha }\cap A_ j)$, which tends to zero because $\overline{B_\alpha }$ is a compact subset of $B'_\alpha $. Hence $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A_ j)\to 0$, and therefore $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(A_ j)\to 0$.

(3) Let $f$ be quasi-continuous on $X$ and $\eta >0$. For each $\alpha $ there is an open set $W_\alpha \subset B'_\alpha $ with $\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(W_\alpha )<\eta $ such that $f$ is continuous on the compact set $\overline{B_\alpha }\setminus W_\alpha $. Put $W=\bigcup _\alpha W_\alpha $. Then $X\setminus W$ is the union of the finitely many closed sets $\overline{B_\alpha }\setminus W$, on each of which $f$ is continuous, so $f$ is continuous on $X\setminus W$; and $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (W)\leq \sum _\alpha c\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(W_\alpha )<Nc\eta $, where $N$ is the number of balls. Now let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$. By lemma 16.1.2(1), $u$ is quasi-continuous. Its pole set $P$ is pluripolar, because near each point $u$ is a plurisubharmonic function plus a smooth one (definition 13.1.3). Hence, by lemma 16.1.1(2) there are open sets $W'_\alpha \subset B'_\alpha $ with $P\cap B'_\alpha \subset W'_\alpha $ and $\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(W'_\alpha )<\eta $. Replacing $W_\alpha $, chosen as above for $f=u$, by $W_\alpha \cup W'_\alpha $, we obtain an open set $W$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (W)<2Nc\eta $ which contains $P$, such that $u$ is continuous on $X\setminus W$ and finite there. In both cases $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\psi }(W)\leq F(\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (W))\leq F(2Nc\eta )$, which is arbitrarily small when $\eta $ is small. $\square$
