Valuations618C84432CDA

Let $X$ be an irreducible reduced variety over $\mathbb {C}$ of dimension $n$. We recall the Berkovich analytification $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ of $X$ with respect to the trivial valuation on $\mathbb {C}$.

Definition 13.4.1. A (real-valued) valuation on $X$ (or a valuation of $\mathbb {C}(X)$) is a map $v\colon \mathbb {C}(X)\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ satisfying the following conditions:

  1. For $f\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(f)=\infty $ if and only if $f=0$;

  2. For $f,g\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)$;

  3. For $f,g\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(f+g)\geq v(f)\land v(g)$;

  4. For $c\in \mathbb {C}^{\times }$, $v(c)=0$.

The set of valuations on $X$ is denoted by $X^{\mathrm{val}}$. The center of a valuation $v$ is the scheme-theoretic point $c=c(v)$ of $X$ such that $v\geq 0$ on $\mathcal{O}_{X,c}$ and $v>0$ on the maximal ideal $\mathfrak {m}_{X,c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_{X,c}$. The center is unique if it exists, and it exists if $X$ is proper.

In the remainder of this section, we assume that $X$ is projective.

As a set, $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ is the set of semi-valuations on $X$, in other words, real-valued valuations $v$ on irreducible reduced subvarieties $Y$ of $X$ that are trivial on $\mathbb {C}$. We call $Y$ the support of the semi-valuation $v$. In other words,

\[ X^{\mathrm{An}}=\coprod _{Y} Y^{\mathrm{val}}. \]

We will write $v_{\mathrm{triv}}\in X^{\mathrm{An}}$ for the trivial valuation on $X$: $v_{\mathrm{triv}}(f)=0$ for any $f\in \mathbb {C}(X)^{\times }$.

We endow $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ with the coarsest topology such that

  1. for any Zariski open subset $U\subseteq X$, the subset $U^{\mathrm{An}}$ of $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ consisting of semi-valuations whose supports meet $U$ is open;

  2. for each Zariski open subset $U\subseteq X$ and each $f\in \mathrm{H}^0(U,\mathcal{O}_ X)$ (here $\mathcal{O}_ X$ is the sheaf of regular functions), the map $|f|\colon U^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ sending $v$ to $\exp (-v(f))$ is continuous.

See [Berk93] for more details.

We will be most interested in divisorial valuations. Recall that a divisorial valuation on $X$ is a valuation of the form $t\operatorname{ord}_ E$, where $t\in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}$ and $E$ is a prime divisor over $X$. The set of divisorial valuations on $X$ is denoted by $X^{\operatorname{div}}$. When $\mathbb {Q}_{>0}$ is replaced by $\mathbb {R}_{>0}$, we can similarly define a space $X^{\operatorname{div}}_{\mathbb {R}}$.

Given any coherent ideal $\mathfrak {a}$ on $X$ and any $v\in X^{\mathrm{An}}$, we define

13.35
\begin{equation} v(\mathfrak {a})\coloneqq \min \{ v(f):f\in \mathfrak {a}_{c(v)}\} \in [0,\infty ], \end{equation}

where $c(v)$ is the center of the valuation $v$ on $X$.

Given any valuation $v$ on $X$, the Gauss extension of $v$ is a valuation $\sigma (v)$ on $X\times \mathbb {A}^1$:

13.36
\begin{equation} \sigma (v)\left(\sum _ i f_ i t^ i\right)\coloneqq \min _ i (v(f_ i)+i). \end{equation}

Here $t$ is the standard coordinate on $\mathbb {A}^1=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb {C}[t]$. The key property is that when $v$ is a divisorial valuation, then so is $\sigma (v)$. See [Lemma 4.2, BHJ17].
