Theorem C.2.937D86D761E81

The same theorem appears in the main chapter and the appendix. The appendix version with its proof is displayed first.

Theorem C.2.9. For each $S\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$, we have

C.6
\begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}S=\lim _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}|S_ k|=\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S)>0. \end{equation}

Moreover, as $k\to \infty $,

C.7
\begin{equation} \Delta _{k}(S)\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}\Delta (S). \end{equation}

Proof. The equalities (C.6) follow from theorem C.2.3.

It remains to prove (C.7). By the argument of [Lemma 4.8, WN14], for any compact set $K\subseteq \operatorname{Int}\Delta (S)$, there is $k_0>0$ such that for any $k\geq k_0$, $\alpha \in K\cap (k^{-1}\mathbb {Z})^ n$ implies that $\alpha \in \Delta _ k(S)$.

To conclude, fix $\epsilon >0$. Choose $\delta >0$ such that $K\coloneqq \Delta (S)_{\delta }$ has positive volume and $d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(K,\Delta (S))<\epsilon /2$. Applying C.1.6 to $K$, we find that $\Delta _ k(S)$ contains $K_{n^{1/2}k^{-1}}$ for all large $k$. After increasing $k$, we may also assume that $d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(K,K_{n^{1/2}k^{-1}})<\epsilon /2$. Since $\Delta _ k(S)\subseteq \Delta (S)$, this implies (C.7). $\square$
