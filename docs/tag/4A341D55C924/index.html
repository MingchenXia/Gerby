Varying model references

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 32.5.1 Varying model references

We now let the reference vary. Recall from definition 30.6.1 the comparison

\[ d_\theta (u,v)=2\, \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\max (u,v)) -\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)-\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (v) \]

and, for $\delta >0$, the set $\mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$ of ordinary models $u=P_\theta [u]$ with $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)\geq \delta $. For models $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ in this comparison, theorem 30.7.3 provides increasing lower models whose upper volumes approach that of $\varphi $. Projecting finite families of potentials onto these lower models makes their rooftops comparable. To keep the limit in the relative class of $\varphi $, domination of the measures is needed only where the potentials lie below one fixed member of that class; domination on all of $X$ gives, in addition, convergence in capacity and convergence of the measures.

Theorem 32.5.4. Let $\varphi _ j,\varphi \in \mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$ for some $\delta >0$, with $d_\theta (\varphi _ j,\varphi )\to 0$. Suppose $u_ j\in \mathcal E_{\varphi _ j}$, $u_ j\to u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\chi \in \mathcal E_\varphi $. If one finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu $ satisfies

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u_ j\leq \chi \} }\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu , \]

then $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Moreover, after extraction and relabeling, the potentials

\[ \psi _ j=P_\theta \left(\inf _{l\geq j}\varphi _ l\right),\qquad h_ j=P_\theta \left(\inf _{l\geq j}u_ l\right) \]

satisfy $\psi _ j\in \mathcal M_{\delta /2}(\theta )$ and $h_ j\in \mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$. If $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu $ on $X$, the original sequence converges to $u$ in capacity and $\mu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mu _ u$.

Proof. We first construct the lower models $\psi _ j$, then a common lower bound for rooftops of the $u_ l$ in the classes $\mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$, and finally treat domination on all of $X$.

By Hartogs' lemma, $u_ j$, $u$ and $\chi $ have a common upper bound; translate them to be nonpositive. The envelope identity then gives $u_ j\leq \varphi _ j$. Since $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ in $L^1$ by proposition 30.6.3, it follows that $u\leq \varphi $. Put $\Lambda =\nu +\mu _\chi $. Using lemma 1.23.1, extract a subsequence along which $u_ j\to u$ almost everywhere for smooth volume and

\[ I_ j:=\int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{d}\Lambda \leq 2^{-j}. \]

Apply theorem 30.7.3 to extract further, keeping these estimates, and obtain increasing models $w_ j\leq \varphi _ j,\varphi $ with lower volume at least $\delta /2$ and $d_\theta (w_ j,\varphi )\to 0$. For $l\geq j$ we have $w_ j\leq w_ l\leq \varphi _ l$. Hence $\psi _ j$ is not identically $-\infty $, and

\[ w_ j\leq \psi _ j\leq \varphi . \]

The upper bound follows by passing to the $L^1$ limit in $\psi _ j\leq \varphi _ l$ as $l\to \infty $. Monotonicity of the ordinary envelope gives $P_\theta [\psi _ j]\leq \varphi _ l$ for all $l\geq j$, so $\psi _ j$ is an ordinary model. It increases with $j$, has lower volume at least $\delta /2$, and

32.14
\begin{equation} \label{eq:varying-reference-volume-limit} \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\psi _ j) \longrightarrow \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi ). \end{equation}

Set $g_ j=P_\theta [\psi _ j](\chi )$. Proposition 32.5.2, with $(\varphi ,\eta )$ replaced by $(\psi _ j,\varphi )$, gives $g_ j\in \mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$, $g_ j\leq \chi $ and $\mu _{g_ j}\leq \mu _\chi $. Moreover $g_ j$ increases with $j$. For $N\geq j$, consider

\[ q_{j,N}=P_\theta (g_ j,u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N). \]

For each $l\geq j$, the projection $P_\theta [\psi _ j](u_ l)$ belongs to $\mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$ by the same proposition, now with upper reference $\varphi _ l$. The finite rooftop of these projections and $g_ j$ lies below $q_{j,N}$. Since $q_{j,N}\leq g_ j$, the finite-rooftop proposition and upward stability give $q_{j,N}\in \mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$, and hence $\mu _{q_{j,N}}(X)\geq \delta /2$.

Partition the contact set of $q_{j,N}$ into disjoint pieces, one for each obstacle. On a piece where $q_{j,N}=u_ l$, we have $u_ l\leq g_ j\leq \chi $, so the hypothesis bounds the measure there by $\nu $. On the piece where $q_{j,N}=g_ j$, the measure is bounded by $\mu _{g_ j}\leq \mu _\chi $. Consequently $\mu _{q_{j,N}}\leq \Lambda $. Put $F_ j=\min (\mathrm{e}^ u,\mathrm{e}^{g_ j})$. On the $u_ l$ piece,

\[ |\mathrm{e}^{q_{j,N}}-F_ j|\leq |\mathrm{e}^{u_ l}-\mathrm{e}^ u|. \]

On the $g_ j$ piece, $g_ j=q_{j,N}\leq u_ N$, so the error is $(\mathrm{e}^{g_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u)_+\leq |\mathrm{e}^{u_ N}-\mathrm{e}^ u|$. The $u_ N$ piece and the $g_ j$ piece are controlled by the two summands $\nu $ and $\mu _\chi $ of $\Lambda $, so together they contribute at most $I_ N$. Thus

32.15
\begin{equation} \label{eq:varying-roof-exponential-error} \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{q_{j,N}}-F_ j|\, \mu _{q_{j,N}} \leq \sum _{l=j}^ N I_ l\leq 2^{1-j}. \end{equation}

Since $F_ j\geq F_1>0$ outside a pluripolar set, we may choose $\epsilon >0$ with $\Lambda (\{ F_1\leq \epsilon \} )<\delta /4$. The lower mass bound and the domination by $\Lambda $ give

\[ \epsilon \delta /4 \leq \int _ X F_ j\, \mu _{q_{j,N}} \leq 2^{1-j}+\Lambda (X)\mathrm{e}^{\sup _ Xq_{j,N}}. \]

For every sufficiently large $j$, this bounds $\sup _ Xq_{j,N}$ from below, uniformly in $N$. Hence the decreasing limit $q_ j$ as $N\to \infty $ is not identically $-\infty $. Applying theorem 32.2.1 in the fixed class $\mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$, with dominating measure $\Lambda $, gives $q_ j\in \mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$. Now

\[ q_ j\leq h_ j\leq \psi _ j, \]

so $h_ j\in \mathcal E_{\psi _ j}$ by upward stability. We discard the finitely many earlier indices. Since $h_ j\leq u\leq \varphi $, monotonicity and (32.14) show that $u$ and $\varphi $ have the same upper volume, and theorem 30.2.4 gives $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

Suppose now that $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu $ on $X$. The finite rooftops $p_{j,N}=P_\theta (u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N)$ are not identically $-\infty $, since they lie above $h_ j$. Partitioning their contact sets gives

\[ \mu _{p_{j,N}}\leq \nu ,\qquad \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _{p_{j,N}}\leq 2^{1-j}. \]

For fixed $j$, they decrease to $h_ j$ as $N\to \infty $. These lower envelopes increase to a regularized limit $h\leq u\leq \varphi $, and their upper volumes tend to that of $\varphi $; hence $h\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Both monotone limits hold in capacity by lemma 1.8.12. Applying lemma 6.3.1 twice gives

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^ h-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _ h=0. \]

Hence $\mu _ h(\{ h<u\} )=0$, and zero domination gives $h=u$. The upper envelopes $(\sup _{k\geq j}u_ k)^*$ decrease to $u$ by Hartogs' lemma and the almost-everywhere convergence, and squeezing gives convergence in capacity. Finally,

\[ \mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j\leq -T\} )\leq \nu (\{ h_1\leq -T\} )\longrightarrow 0 \]

uniformly in $j$, and the tail criterion theorem 7.6.1 gives weak convergence of the measures. Since the extraction can start from any subsequence, both conclusions hold for the original sequence. $\square$
