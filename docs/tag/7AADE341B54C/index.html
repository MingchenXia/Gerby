Logarithmic equations on normalized curves

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · A.7 Logarithmic equations on normalized curves

On a curve the pulled-back background can be degenerate, but its total mass still determines the equation. A periodic Green function on the normalization compensates for the degeneracy. The normalization also gives direct control of descent, of the atoms at the poles and of continuity.

Let $X$ be a connected compact reduced unibranch curve whose normalization is a torus:

\[ \pi :E=\mathbb C/\Lambda \longrightarrow X, \]

a finite bijective holomorphic map, where $\Lambda \subset \mathbb C$ is a lattice. It is biholomorphic outside a finite set. Let $h$ be a Hermitian form on $X$ in the sense of the beginning of this appendix. In the flat coordinate $z=x+\mathrm iy$ on $E$, set

\[ dA=dx\, dy,\qquad V=\int _ EdA,\qquad \beta _0=dA/V,\qquad \beta =\pi ^*h=b\beta _0. \]

Here $b$ is smooth and nonnegative, positive over $X_{\rm reg}$, and may vanish at the other points. Put $H=\int _ E\beta >0$. Every two-form on a curve is closed, and this is essential for the following construction.

Lemma A.7.1. There is a real periodic distribution $G$ with mean zero, smooth off zero, such that

A.24
\begin{equation} \label{eq:torus-logarithmic-green} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=\delta _0-\beta _0,\qquad G(z)=\log |z|^2+\text{a smooth function near }0. \end{equation}

For $r\in L^ p(E,\beta _0)$, $p>1$, its convolution satisfies

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl (G*(r\beta _0)\bigr ) =\left(r-\int _ Er\beta _0\right)\beta _0. \]

The convolution is bounded and continuous, and it is smooth if $r$ is smooth.

Proof. With the normalization of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ fixed in the Conventions, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v=(\Delta v)/(4\pi )\, dA$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^2=\delta _0$, as in lemma 1.16.11. Cutting off this logarithm in a coordinate disk, with a cutoff equal to one near zero, gives a function $L$ on $E$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}L=\delta _0+q\beta _0$, where $q$ is smooth and $\int _ Eq\beta _0=-1$ by Stokes' theorem.

Let $e_\xi $ be the characters of the torus, with $-\Delta e_\xi =\lambda _\xi e_\xi $. The nonconstant characters have $\lambda _\xi >0$, growing quadratically on the dual lattice. Define the correction $S$, of mean zero, by its Fourier coefficients

\[ S_\xi =\frac{4\pi }{V}\frac{(q+1)_\xi }{\lambda _\xi } \quad (\xi \ne 0),\qquad S_0=0, \]

where $(q+1)_\xi $ is the Fourier coefficient of $q+1$. Integration by parts shows that these coefficients decay faster than any power of $\lambda _\xi $. Hence the series and all its derivatives converge uniformly, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S=-(q+1)\beta _0$. Conjugate symmetry of the coefficients makes $S$ real. Subtracting the mean of $L+S$ from $L+S$ proves (A.24). In particular, $G$ belongs to $L^ r$ for every finite $r$.

The convolution uses the group law and the Haar probability $\beta _0$. Write $K=G*(r\beta _0)$. Testing against smooth functions and using Fubini's theorem proves its distributional equation. Smooth input gives smooth output by the same Fourier argument. For $p'=p/(p-1)$, Hölder's inequality gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \| K\| _\infty & \leq \| G\| _{p'}\| r\| _ p,\\ |K(z+s)-K(z)| & \leq \| r\| _ p\| G(\cdot +s)-G\| _{p'}. \end{aligned} \]

Approximating $G$ by smooth functions shows that translation is continuous in $L^{p'}$, and hence that $K$ is continuous. $\square$

Choose finitely many distinct points $q_1,\ldots ,q_ k\in E$ and weights $a_ j>0$, possibly none, with

\[ \begin{aligned} A& =\sum _ j a_ j<H,& M& =H-A,\\ \Phi & =G*((H-b)\beta _0),& \Psi & =\Phi +\sum _ j a_ jG(\cdot -q_ j). \end{aligned} \]

The function $\Phi $ is smooth, and

A.29
\begin{equation} \label{eq:unibranch-curve-reference} \beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi =M\beta _0+\sum _ j a_ j\delta _{q_ j}. \end{equation}

Thus $\Psi $ is a $\beta $-plurisubharmonic function, not identically $-\infty $, whose logarithmic poles are exactly the points $q_ j$, with coefficients $a_ j$ measured on the normalization. The proper bijection $\pi $ is a homeomorphism, so we may define $\psi =\Psi \circ \pi ^{-1}$; its disk inequalities are proved below. The ordinary type of $\psi $ consists of the potentials $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$ with

\[ w\leq \psi +C,\qquad \psi \leq w+C \]

for a finite constant $C$, the inequalities being understood for extended real values.

Theorem A.7.2. Let $0\leq f\in L^ p(E,\beta _0)$ for some finite $p>1$, with $F=\int _ Ef\beta _0>0$, and set $\nu =\pi _*(f\beta _0)$. Every potential in the ordinary type of $\psi $ has extended non-pluripolar mass $M$. In this type there is a unique normalized solution of

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,h}(u)=c\nu , \qquad \sup _ Xu=0, \qquad c=\frac{H-A}{F}>0. \]

Every member of the type that solves $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{X,h}(w)=c'\nu $ for some constant $c'>0$, with no regularity assumed, has $c'=c$ and differs from the normalized solution by a constant.

The solution is continuous with real values off its finite pole set, and continuous at the poles as a map into $\mathbb R\cup \{ -\infty \} $ with the order topology. Its residual relative to $\psi $ extends to a continuous real function on $X$. If there are no poles, the solution itself is continuous with real values everywhere. The type and the equation do not depend on the choice of a resolution or of a common refinement. The measure $\nu $ is non-pluripolar.

Proof. Set $c=M/F$ and

\[ v=G*((cf-M)\beta _0),\qquad U=\Psi +v. \]

The lemma gives a bounded continuous $v$ and the positive current

A.30
\begin{equation} \label{eq:unibranch-curve-solution-current} \beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U=cf\beta _0+\sum _ j a_ j\delta _{q_ j}. \end{equation}

In each disk, adding a smooth local primitive of $\beta $ gives a function with positive distributional Laplacian, and hence a subharmonic representative. Continuity off the poles and the explicit logarithmic values identify this representative with $U$ as defined above. The same argument applies to $\Psi $. The supremum $S=\sup _ EU$ is finite, and we put $u=(U-S)\circ \pi ^{-1}$.

Every holomorphic disk $g:\Delta \to X$ lifts holomorphically to $\widetilde g=\pi ^{-1}\circ g$. Indeed, for a nonconstant disk the inverse image of the finite singular set is discrete, and off this set the lift is holomorphic. Since $\pi ^{-1}$ is a homeomorphism, $\widetilde g$ is continuous on the whole disk. Near an omitted point its image therefore stays in a bounded coordinate neighborhood in $E$, and the removable singularity theorem in one variable extends it holomorphically. A constant disk lifts to a constant map, and a nonconstant disk cannot have image in a finite set.

Choose a smooth local primitive $\eta $ of $\beta $ near the lifted image, that is, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta =\beta $. Then $\eta \circ \widetilde g$ is a primitive of $g^*h$, and

\[ u\circ g+\eta \circ \widetilde g=(U-S+\eta )\circ \widetilde g \]

is subharmonic or identically $-\infty $. That the composition is subharmonic follows from decreasing smooth local regularizations and the holomorphic chain rule for the Laplacian. Any other primitive on the disk differs by a harmonic function. These are exactly the disk tests of definition A.1.1, and the same argument shows that $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,h)$. A constant disk at a pole has the permitted restriction $\equiv -\infty $. A nonconstant lift meeting $q_ j$ with order $\ell $ gives the pole coefficient $\ell a_ j$.

The homeomorphism $\pi $ gives continuity off the poles. Near a pole, the smooth logarithmic remainder of $\Psi $ and the bounded continuous function $v$ give continuity with extended values. The residual is the descent of $v-S$, including at the poles, so no difference of two infinite values is involved. The solution belongs to the stated ordinary type.

For the mass and uniqueness assertions, let $w$ be any member of the type and put $W=w\circ \pi $. Pulling back along coordinate disks of the normalization shows that $W$ is $\beta $-plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty $. The two type inequalities make $W$ locally bounded away from $Q=\{ q_ j\} $, and at each pole they give

\[ W=a_ j\log |z-q_ j|^2+O(1), \]

with value $-\infty $ at $q_ j$. The positive Radon measure $T_ W=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W$ has total mass $H$ by Stokes' theorem. Its atom at $q_ j$ has weight exactly $a_ j$. This follows from the planar atom identity of lemma 1.16.11. Explicitly, choose a smooth local primitive $\eta $ of $\beta $ near $q_ j$, and let $m(r)$ be the circular mean of $W+\eta $ around $q_ j$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(W+\eta )=T_ W$, it satisfies

\[ m'(r)=\frac{2T_ W(B_ r)}r \]

for almost every $r$, by the divergence theorem and regularization. If $\tau =T_ W(\{ q_ j\} )$, integration gives $m(r)/\log r^2\to \tau $, while the bounded logarithmic remainder gives the limit $a_ j$.

Hence the non-pluripolar product of $W$, defined in dimension one by truncation, is

\[ \langle \beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W\rangle =\mathbf1_{E\setminus Q}T_ W. \]

Indeed, on each relatively compact set avoiding $Q$, a sufficiently low truncation equals $W$, and the indicator removes the poles. Its mass is $H-A=M$ for every member of the type. For (A.30), removing the atoms leaves $cf\beta _0$. The definition (A.7) gives the asserted equation on $X$. A locally pluripolar set pulls back to a set on which some quasi-subharmonic function, not identically $-\infty $, equals $-\infty $; such sets have zero flat area. Hence $\nu $ is non-pluripolar.

Suppose that $w$ solves the equation with a constant $c'>0$. Comparing total masses gives $c'F=M$, so $c'=c$. Since $\pi $ is a Borel homeomorphism and the atoms are fixed, $T_ W=T_ U$. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(W-U)=0$, where $W-U$ is an integrable function. Every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of $W-U$ vanishes, so the difference is constant almost everywhere. Subharmonic functions are determined by their averages over shrinking disks, so the values of $W$ and $U$ differ by the same constant everywhere, and the same holds on $X$. This includes members of the type with no regularity assumed.

Finally, a resolution $\rho :Y\to X$ factors through $E$ by the disk-lifting argument. The resulting proper holomorphic map $Y\to E$ has generic degree one. For smooth compact curves, the multiplicities in a fiber sum to the degree, so every fiber is a single point of multiplicity one, and the map is biholomorphic. The same holds for every common refinement, which proves the independence assertion directly. $\square$

The integrability hypothesis on $f$ refers to flat area on $E$. On $X_{\rm reg}$, the density of $\nu $ with respect to $h$ is $(f/b)\circ \pi ^{-1}$; both measures give the finite singular set zero mass. No positive lower bound for $b$ and no bound for the density on $X$ is required.
