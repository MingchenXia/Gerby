Proposition 10.3.1697A03B9F1F87

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Proposition 10.3.16

Proposition 10.3.16. The partial Okounkov body $\Delta _{\nu }(L,h)$ depends only on $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$, not on the explicit choices of $L,h_0,h$.

Proof. There are two different claims to prove, as detailed in the two steps below.

Step 1. Let $h_0'$ be another Hermitian metric on $L$. Set $\theta '=c_1(L,h_0')$. Write $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\theta -\theta '$. Let $\varphi '=\varphi +f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$. Then

10.53
\begin{equation} \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi )=\Delta _{\nu }(\theta ',\varphi '). \end{equation}

This is obvious since $\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )=\Gamma (\theta ',\varphi ')$.

Step 2. Let $L'$ be another big line bundle on $X$. By Step 1, we may assume that the reference Hermitian metric $h'_0$ on $L'$ is such that $c_1(L',h'_0)=\theta $.

Let $h'$ be a plurisubharmonic metric on $L'$ with $c_1(L,h)=c_1(L',h')$. Then

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(L,h)=\Delta _{\nu }(L',h'). \]

By the approximation step in the construction of $\Delta _\nu (L,h)$ and the continuity of the extension in theorem 10.1.6, it is enough to prove the assertion when $c_1(L,h)$ has analytic singularities. After taking a birational resolution, it suffices to deal with the case where $c_1(L,h)$ has analytic singularities along an effective $\mathbb {Q}$-divisor $D$. By rescaling, we may also assume that $D$ is a divisor. By 10.3.15, we further reduce to the case where $c_1(L,h)$ is not singular.

In this case, the assertion is proved in proposition 10.3.6. $\square$
