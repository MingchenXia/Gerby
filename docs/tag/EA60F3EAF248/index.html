Theorem 16.5.4EA60F3EAF248

Theorem 16.5.4. For every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and every choice in (16.3), put

\[ q_ j=P_{\theta +\epsilon _ jh}[a_ j]. \]

Then $q_ j$ has analytic singularities, and

\[ q_ j=P_{\theta +\epsilon _ jh}[a_ j]_{\mathcal I} \searrow P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I} \]

pointwise and in $L^1$. The limit is independent of the choice of the approximants $a_ j$, of the error bounds $\epsilon _ j$ and of the metric $h$.

Proof. By proposition 16.2.7, $q_ j=P_{\theta +\epsilon _ jh}[a_ j]_{\mathcal I}\sim a_ j$. Since the backgrounds decrease and $a_{j+1}\preceq a_ j$, the function $q_{j+1}$ is a competitor for $P_{\theta +\epsilon _ jh}[a_ j]$. Hence $q_{j+1}\leq q_ j$.

The inequality $a_ j\geq u$ gives $u\preceq _{\mathcal I}a_ j$. For every local divisor $E$, lemma 1.17.8 gives

\[ \nu _ E(a_ j)=\frac1{m_ j}\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I(m_ ju) \longrightarrow \nu _ E(u). \]

By proposition 16.5.1, $q_ j\to p:=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ in $L^1$, and $p\leq q_ j\leq 0$. The decreasing limit of the $q_ j$ is therefore bounded below by $p$; it is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function that agrees with $p$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere. This identifies the limit in terms of $\theta $ and the multiplier-ideal class of $u$ alone, which proves the asserted independence. $\square$
