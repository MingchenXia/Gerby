Integration by parts for bounded differences

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 17.1 Integration by parts for bounded differences

As in chapter 16, for a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$ and $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ we write

\[ \theta _ q=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q,\qquad \mu _ q=\langle \theta _ q^ n\rangle , \]

so that $\mu _ q(X)=\int _ X\langle \theta _ q^ n\rangle $ is the total mass of $q$, and we indicate the background when several occur. Recall from definition 14.2.6 that $v$ is more singular than $u$, written $v\preceq u$, if $v\leq u+C$ on $X$ for some constant $C$, and that $u$ and $v$ have the same singularity type, written $u\sim v$, if $u\preceq v$ and $v\preceq u$. A difference $u-v$ of two quasi-psh functions is evaluated on their common finite locus $\{ u>-\infty \} \cap \{ v>-\infty \} $, whose complement is pluripolar. Non-pluripolar products do not charge pluripolar sets (lemma 15.1.2), so the values assigned to such a difference on the pole sets never affect the integrals below.

A bounded quasi-psh difference is a bounded Borel function $a$ on $X$ such that $a=\varphi _1-\varphi _2$ quasi-everywhere, that is, outside a pluripolar set, for two quasi-psh functions $\varphi _1,\varphi _2$ on $X$; we call $(\varphi _1,\varphi _2)$ a presentation of $a$. The two functions then have the same singularity type. Indeed, if $|a|\leq C$, then $\varphi _1\leq \varphi _2+C$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, because an inequality between quasi-psh functions that holds quasi-everywhere holds everywhere (section 16.1); by symmetry, $\varphi _1\sim \varphi _2$.

Let $T_1,\ldots ,T_{n-1}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$ and put $R=\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle $, with $R=1$ if $n=1$. For a bounded quasi-psh difference $a$ with presentation $(\varphi _1,\varphi _2)$ we define the signed measure

17.1
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bounded-difference-hessian-product} \begin{aligned} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R & =\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \\ & \quad -\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _2)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle , \end{aligned} \end{equation}

where $\kappa $ is a multiple of $\omega $ so large that $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$. Both terms are non-pluripolar products of $n$ closed positive $(1,1)$-currents, hence finite measures by theorem 15.4.3; for $n=1$ they are first-degree non-pluripolar products.

The measure $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R$ does not depend on the choices made. First, let $\theta $ be any smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form with $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and let $\kappa \geq \theta $ be a multiple of $\omega $, so that $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$. Multilinearity of non-pluripolar products (proposition 15.1.5) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle & =\left\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \\ & \quad +\left\langle (\kappa -\theta )\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \end{aligned} \]

for $j=1,2$. The last term cancels in the difference, so the right side of (17.1) does not change when $\kappa $ is replaced by $\theta $. Taking for $\theta $ a smaller multiple of $\omega $ with respect to which $\varphi _1,\varphi _2$ are psh, we see that the measure does not depend on $\kappa $; hence it may be computed with any form $\theta $ as above in place of $\kappa $. Finally, the measure does not depend on the presentation of $a$. Let $(\psi _1,\psi _2)$ be another presentation and choose $\kappa $ so that all four functions are $\kappa $-psh. Then $\varphi _1+\psi _2=\varphi _2+\psi _1$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, since both sides are $2\kappa $-psh. Therefore the currents $2\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi _1+\psi _2)$ and $2\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi _2+\psi _1)$ coincide. By multilinearity,

\[ \begin{aligned} & \left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle +\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _2)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \\ & \qquad =\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _2)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle +\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle . \end{aligned} \]

All four measures are finite, so the two presentations give the same measure (17.1). We extend the notation linearly to finite sums of such products.

The truncation argument below passes to the limit in integrals whose weights converge only in capacity. We use the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega =\operatorname {Cap}_{\omega ,0}$ of definition 16.7.5, relative to $V_\omega =0$. Quasi-continuity of functions on $X$ is understood as in section 16.1; by proposition 16.9.9, the exceptional open sets may be taken of arbitrarily small $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. Recall from theorem 16.9.8 that Borel functions $f_ j$ converge in capacity to $f$ if $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ |f_ j-f|>\varepsilon \} )\to 0$ for every $\varepsilon >0$, and that a family of positive Borel measures on $X$ is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ if the supremum of $\lambda (A)$, over all members $\lambda $ of the family and all Borel sets $A$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A)\leq \delta $, tends to zero as $\delta \downarrow 0$.

Lemma 17.1.1. Let $\nu _ j$ and $\nu $ be positive Borel measures on $X$ such that $\nu _ j\to \nu $ weakly, $\sup _ j\nu _ j(X)<\infty $, and the family $\{ \nu ,\nu _1,\nu _2,\ldots \} $ is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. Let $f_ j$ and $f$ be Borel functions on $X$ with $|f_ j|,|f|\leq B$ for a constant $B$, and suppose that $f_ j\to f$ in capacity and that $f$ is continuous outside open sets of arbitrarily small capacity: for every $\eta >0$ there is an open set $G\subset X$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (G)<\eta $ such that the restriction of $f$ to $X\setminus G$ is continuous. (By proposition 16.9.9(3), the last condition holds if $f$ is quasi-continuous.) Then

\[ \int _ Xf_ j\, \nu _ j\longrightarrow \int _ Xf\, \nu . \]

Proof. We write the difference of the two integrals as

\[ \int _ X(f_ j-f)\, \nu _ j+\left(\int _ Xf\, \nu _ j-\int _ Xf\, \nu \right) \]

and show that both terms tend to zero. For the first term fix $\varepsilon >0$ and put $A_ j=\{ |f_ j-f|>\varepsilon \} $. Splitting the integral over $A_ j$ and its complement gives

\[ \left|\int _ X(f_ j-f)\, \nu _ j\right|\leq 2B\nu _ j(A_ j)+\varepsilon \sup _ k\nu _ k(X). \]

Since $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A_ j)\to 0$, uniform absolute continuity gives $\nu _ j(A_ j)\to 0$. Thus the upper limit of the first term is at most $\varepsilon \sup _ k\nu _ k(X)$, and $\varepsilon $ is arbitrary.

For the second term let $\eta >0$, and choose an open set $G$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (G)<\eta $ such that the restriction of $f$ to the compact set $X\setminus G$ is continuous. By lemma 7.1.4, there is a metric $d$ on $X$ which induces its topology, and by lemma 9.4.3, applied in the metric space $(X,d)$, the restriction of $f$ to $X\setminus G$ extends to a continuous function $g$ on $X$ with $|g|\leq B$. Since $X$ is compact, $g$ has compact support, so weak convergence of measures (see the discussion following definition 7.2.4) gives $\int _ Xg\, \nu _ j\to \int _ Xg\, \nu $. Since $f=g$ outside $G$, we have $|\int _ Xf\, \lambda -\int _ Xg\, \lambda |\leq 2B\lambda (G)$ for each $\lambda \in \{ \nu ,\nu _1,\nu _2,\ldots \} $. The upper limit of the second term is therefore at most $4B$ times the supremum of $\lambda (G)$ over the family, which tends to zero as $\eta \downarrow 0$ by uniform absolute continuity. $\square$

Lemma 17.1.2. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $\varphi _1,\varphi _2,\psi _1,\psi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $\varphi _1\sim \varphi _2$ and $\psi _1\sim \psi _2$. Put $a=\varphi _1-\varphi _2$, $b=\psi _1-\psi _2$, and

\[ S_ j=\sum _{k=0}^{n-1} \left\langle \theta _{\varphi _ j}\wedge \theta _{\psi _1}^{k} \wedge \theta _{\psi _2}^{n-1-k}\right\rangle , \qquad j=1,2. \]

Then

17.8
\begin{equation} \label{eq:singular-monge-ampere-difference-identity} \int _ Xa(\mu _{\psi _1}-\mu _{\psi _2}) =\int _ Xb(S_1-S_2). \end{equation}

Proof. If the potentials were bounded, (17.8) would follow from Stokes' formula. We reduce to that case by truncation in three steps, assuming first that $\theta $ is a Kähler form, and remove this assumption in a fourth step.

Step 1: agreement on deep sublevel sets. Let $\theta =\kappa $ be a Kähler form, and assume that $\varphi _1\leq \varphi _2$, $\psi _1\leq \psi _2$, and that there is $L>0$ such that $\varphi _1=\varphi _2$ on $U=\{ \varphi _1<-L\} $ and $\psi _1=\psi _2$ on $V=\{ \psi _1<-L\} $. These sets are open, because $\varphi _1$ and $\psi _1$ are upper semicontinuous. For an integer $l>L$ put $\varphi _ j^ l=\max \{ \varphi _ j,-l\} $ and $\psi _ j^ l=\max \{ \psi _ j,-l\} $. These are bounded $\kappa $-psh functions; let $a^ l=\varphi _1^ l-\varphi _2^ l$, $b^ l=\psi _1^ l-\psi _2^ l$, and let $S_ j^ l$ be defined as $S_ j$ with the truncated functions. The algebraic identity $x^ n-y^ n=(x-y)\sum _ kx^ ky^{n-1-k}$ for the commuting Bedford–Taylor products gives

\[ \mu _{\psi _1^ l}-\mu _{\psi _2^ l}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l,\qquad S_1^ l-S_2^ l=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l,\qquad \Sigma ^ l=\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}\kappa _{\psi _1^ l}^ k\wedge \kappa _{\psi _2^ l}^{n-1-k}. \]

Since $\Sigma ^ l$ is a closed positive current, Stokes' formula for bounded potentials (lemma 15.4.2) yields $\int _ Xa^ l\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l=\int _ Xb^ l\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l$, which is (17.8) for the truncated functions.

We show that both sides of this identity coincide with the corresponding sides of (17.8). On $U$ we have $a^ l=a=0$. On the open set $V$ we have $\psi _1^ l=\psi _2^ l$ and $\psi _1=\psi _2$, so $\mu _{\psi _1^ l}=\mu _{\psi _2^ l}$ and $\mu _{\psi _1}=\mu _{\psi _2}$ there, by locality of Bedford–Taylor and non-pluripolar products. Hence both left sides are integrals over the closed set $F=X\setminus (U\cup V)$. On $F$ we have $\varphi _2\geq \varphi _1\geq -L$ and $\psi _2\geq \psi _1\geq -L$. Thus $F$ is contained in the plurifine open set where all four potentials exceed $-l$; there every truncation coincides with its potential, so the bounded products of the truncations coincide with the corresponding non-pluripolar products, by plurifine locality (proposition 15.1.5). Consequently the two left sides are equal. The right sides are treated in the same way with the roles of $U$ and $V$ exchanged: $b^ l=b=0$ on $V$, while $S_1^ l=S_2^ l$ and $S_1=S_2$ on $U$. This proves (17.8) under the assumptions of this step.

Step 2: a Kähler background with a margin. Let $\theta =\kappa $ be Kähler, and suppose that all four potentials are $\lambda \kappa $-psh for some $0<\lambda <1$. Adding a constant to one of the four potentials changes neither side of (17.8). Indeed, the measures are unchanged, and a constant added to $a$ or $b$ contributes nothing, since $\int _ X(\mu _{\psi _1}-\mu _{\psi _2})=0$ and $\int _ X(S_1-S_2)=0$ by corollary 15.7.5. Subtracting suitable constants from $\varphi _1$ and $\psi _1$, and then a common constant from each pair, we may therefore assume that all four potentials are nonpositive and that

\[ \varphi _1\leq \varphi _2\leq \varphi _1+B, \qquad \psi _1\leq \psi _2\leq \psi _1+B \]

for a constant $B\geq 1$. Take a sequence $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ with $\lambda (1+\varepsilon )<1$ and put

\[ \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }=\max \{ \varphi _1,(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2\} , \qquad \psi _{2,\varepsilon }=\max \{ \psi _1,(1+\varepsilon )\psi _2\} . \]

Since $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2$ is $(1+\varepsilon )\lambda \kappa $-psh, the function $\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }$ is $\kappa $-psh, and $\varphi _1\leq \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }\leq \varphi _2\leq \varphi _1+B$ because $\varphi _2\leq 0$. Where $\varphi _1<-B-B/\varepsilon $,

\[ (1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2\leq (1+\varepsilon )(\varphi _1+B)=\varphi _1+B+\varepsilon (\varphi _1+B)<\varphi _1, \]

so $\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }=\varphi _1$ there. The same holds for the $\psi $'s. Step 1 therefore applies to the pairs $(\varphi _1,\varphi _{2,\varepsilon })$ and $(\psi _1,\psi _{2,\varepsilon })$ and gives (17.8) with $a_\varepsilon =\varphi _1-\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }$ and $b_\varepsilon =\psi _1-\psi _{2,\varepsilon }$ in place of $a,b$, and with the corresponding measures.

Step 3: the limit $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, the functions $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2$ increase to $\varphi _2$, so $\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }$ increases to $\varphi _{2,0}=\max \{ \varphi _1,\varphi _2\} =\varphi _2$; likewise $\psi _{2,\varepsilon }\uparrow \psi _{2,0}=\psi _2$. By corollary 15.7.6, every measure in the identity of Step 2 converges weakly to the corresponding measure in (17.8). To pass to the limit in the integrals we apply lemma 17.1.1 to each of the finitely many terms, each of which is the integral of a bounded weight against a positive measure.

We first check uniform absolute continuity. For $\varepsilon \geq 0$ put

\[ \zeta _\varepsilon =\tfrac 15 (\varphi _1+\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }+\psi _1+\psi _{2,\varepsilon }), \qquad \chi =\zeta _0-B. \]

Since $-B\leq \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }-\varphi _2\leq 0$ and $-B\leq \psi _{2,\varepsilon }-\psi _2\leq 0$, we have $\chi \leq \zeta _\varepsilon \leq \chi +B$ for all $\varepsilon \geq 0$. Each of the four potentials is $\lambda \kappa $-psh, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \geq -\frac45\lambda \kappa $, and $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $ is the sum of $(1-4\lambda /5)\kappa $ and the closed positive current $\frac45\lambda \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $. In particular $\chi $ is $\kappa $-psh, and expanding $\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi )^ n\rangle $ by multilinearity and compatibility with smooth factors (proposition 15.1.5, proposition 15.3.3) gives $\int _ X\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi )^ n\rangle \geq (1-4\lambda /5)^ n\int _ X\kappa ^ n>0$.

Now let $\mu $ refer to the background $\kappa $. By the identity

\[ 5\kappa _{\zeta _\varepsilon } =\kappa _{\varphi _1}+\kappa _{\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }} +\kappa _{\psi _1}+\kappa _{\psi _{2,\varepsilon }}+\kappa \]

and multilinearity, each measure occurring in the identity of Step 2 (for $\varepsilon >0$) or in (17.8) (for $\varepsilon =0$) is a non-pluripolar product of $n$ of the currents on the right, and hence is at most $5^ n\mu _{\zeta _\varepsilon }$. The potential $q=B^{-1}\zeta _\varepsilon +(1-B^{-1})\chi $ satisfies $\chi \leq q\leq \chi +1$, so $q-1$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }$, and $\mu _ q\geq B^{-n}\mu _{\zeta _\varepsilon }$ by multilinearity, since $B\geq 1$. Hence $\mu _{\zeta _\varepsilon }(A)\leq B^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }(A)$ for every Borel set $A$, and every measure in question, for every $\varepsilon \geq 0$, is bounded by $5^ nB^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }$; in particular its total mass is at most $5^ nB^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }(X)=5^ nB^ n\int _ X\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi )^ n\rangle \leq 5^ nB^ n\int _ X\kappa ^ n$, by proposition 16.7.6, theorem 15.4.3. Since $\chi $ has positive mass, theorem 16.9.8 bounds $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }(A)$ by a continuous nondecreasing function of $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A)$ vanishing at zero. Uniform absolute continuity follows.

Next we check the weights. Assign the value zero to $a_\varepsilon ,b_\varepsilon ,a,b$ on the pole sets; these are Borel functions with values in $[-B,0]$. Given $\eta >0$, proposition 16.9.9(3), applied to each of the four potentials with the bound $\eta /4$, and the countable subadditivity of $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ (proposition 16.7.6) give an open set $G$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (G)<\eta $ which contains the pole sets of the four potentials and such that the four potentials are finite and continuous on the compact set $X\setminus G$. Then $a$ and $b$ are continuous on $X\setminus G$, so they satisfy the continuity hypothesis of lemma 17.1.1. Moreover the four potentials are bounded in absolute value on $X\setminus G$ by a constant $C_\eta >0$. Since $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2\leq \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }\leq \varphi _2$, we have on $X\setminus G$

\[ |a_\varepsilon -a|=|\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }-\varphi _2|\leq |(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2-\varphi _2|\leq \varepsilon C_\eta , \]

and likewise $|b_\varepsilon -b|\leq \varepsilon C_\eta $. Hence, for every $\delta >0$ and $\varepsilon <\delta /C_\eta $, the sets $\{ |a_\varepsilon -a|>\delta \} $ and $\{ |b_\varepsilon -b|>\delta \} $ lie in $G$ and have capacity less than $\eta $. Since $\eta $ is arbitrary, $a_\varepsilon \to a$ and $b_\varepsilon \to b$ in capacity. Lemma 17.1.1 now passes the identity of Step 2 to the limit, which is (17.8).

Step 4: an arbitrary background. For all sufficiently large $s>0$ we have $s\omega \geq \theta $ and the form $\kappa _ s=\theta +s\omega $ is Kähler. Each $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is then $\tfrac 12\kappa _ s$-psh, because $\tfrac 12\kappa _ s+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q=\theta _ q+\tfrac 12(s\omega -\theta )\geq 0$. By Steps 2 and 3 with $\lambda =\frac12$, (17.8) holds in the background $\kappa _ s$ for all large $s$. Since $\kappa _ s+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q=\theta _ q+s\omega $, multilinearity and compatibility with smooth factors (proposition 15.1.5, proposition 15.3.3) show that every measure in the identity is a polynomial in $s$ whose coefficients are finite measures of the form $\langle \theta _{q_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{q_ k}\rangle \wedge \omega ^{n-k}$; the weights $a,b$ do not depend on $s$. Both sides of the identity are therefore polynomials in $s$, and since they agree for all large $s$, their coefficients agree. The constant coefficients are the two sides of (17.8) in the background $\theta $. $\square$

Theorem 17.1.3. Let $a,b$ be bounded quasi-psh differences on $X$, and let $T_1,\ldots ,T_{n-1}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then

17.9
\begin{equation} \label{eq:nonpluripolar-bounded-difference-integration-by-parts} \int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle =\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle , \end{equation}

where the Hessian factors are the signed measures defined in (17.1).

Proof. Step 1: a repeated factor and a common background. Let $(\varphi _1,\varphi _2)$ and $(\psi _1,\psi _2)$ be presentations of $a$ and $b$, and suppose that these four functions and a further potential $\eta $ all belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for one smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $. For $0\leq t\leq 1$ the functions $\psi _ j^ t=(1-t)\eta +t\psi _ j$ are $\theta $-psh, and $\psi _1^ t-\psi _2^ t=tb$ is bounded. Lemma 17.1.2, applied to $\varphi _1,\varphi _2,\psi _1^ t,\psi _2^ t$, gives

\[ \int _ Xa(\mu _{\psi _1^ t}-\mu _{\psi _2^ t})=t\int _ Xb(S_1^ t-S_2^ t), \qquad S_ j^ t=\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}\left\langle \theta _{\varphi _ j}\wedge \theta _{\psi _1^ t}^ k\wedge \theta _{\psi _2^ t}^{n-1-k}\right\rangle . \]

Since $\theta _{\psi _ j^ t}=(1-t)\theta _\eta +t\theta _{\psi _ j}$, multilinearity expands every measure here as a polynomial in $t$ with finite measures as coefficients, so both sides are polynomials in $t$. We compare their coefficients of $t$. On the left this coefficient is

\[ n\int _ Xa\left(\langle \theta _{\psi _1}\wedge \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle -\langle \theta _{\psi _2}\wedge \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle \right) =n\int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \langle \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle , \]

where we used that (17.1) may be computed in any smooth background with respect to which the presentation is psh. On the right it is $\int _ Xb(S_1^0-S_2^0)$, and $S_ j^0=n\langle \theta _{\varphi _ j}\wedge \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle $, so it equals $n\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \langle \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle $. This proves (17.9) when $T_1=\cdots =T_{n-1}=\theta _\eta $ and all potentials share the background $\theta $. For $n=1$ there are no currents $T_ j$, and (17.9) is (17.8) for a Kähler background with respect to which the four functions are psh.

Step 2: arbitrary currents. Let $n>1$. By theorem 14.1.7, write $T_ j=\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ j$ with $\theta _ j$ smooth closed and $\eta _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$. For positive numbers $r_1,\ldots ,r_{n-1}$ with $\sum _ jr_ j=1$ put $\theta _ r=\sum _ jr_ j\theta _ j$ and $\eta _ r=\sum _ jr_ j\eta _ j$, so that $\sum _ jr_ jT_ j=\theta _ r+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ r$. The potential $\eta _ r$ is $(\theta _ r+s\omega )$-psh for every $s\geq 0$, since $\theta _ r+s\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ r=\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega \geq 0$. Let $\kappa $ be a multiple of $\omega $ such that the presentations of $a$ and $b$ are $\kappa $-psh. Since the forms $\theta _ r$ are convex combinations of the finitely many smooth forms $\theta _ j$, there is $s_0$ with $\theta _ r+s\omega \geq \kappa $ for all $r$ and all $s\geq s_0$, and then the presentations are $(\theta _ r+s\omega )$-psh as well. Step 1 applied to the current $\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega =(\theta _ r+s\omega )+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ r$ gives

\[ \int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega \Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle =\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega \Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle , \]

where, by the independence of (17.1) from the background, the Hessian factors may be computed in one fixed Kähler background. Expanding the repeated current by multilinearity, both sides become polynomials in $s$, and comparing constant coefficients gives

\[ \int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j\Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle =\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j\Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle . \]

Both sides are homogeneous polynomials of degree $n-1$ in $(r_1,\ldots ,r_{n-1})$. Since they agree on the open simplex, homogeneity makes them agree on the open positive cone of $\mathbb R^{n-1}$. Hence their coefficients agree: a polynomial that vanishes on a nonempty open set is zero, because all its partial derivatives vanish at a point $r_0$ of that set and it equals its finite Taylor expansion at $r_0$. The coefficient of $r_1\cdots r_{n-1}$ is $(n-1)!$ times the corresponding side of (17.9). $\square$

Lemma 17.1.4. Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $u\sim v$, let $T_1,\ldots ,T_{n-1}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, and put $R=\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle $. Then

\[ \int _ X(u-v)\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-v)\wedge R\leq 0. \]

Proof. Put $\lambda _ u=\langle \theta _ u\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle $ and define $\lambda _ v$ in the same way, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-v)\wedge R=\lambda _ u-\lambda _ v$ by (17.1). Since $u-v$ is bounded, corollary 15.7.5, applied after writing $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ i$ with smooth $\theta _ i$ and $\eta _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ (theorem 14.1.7), gives $\lambda _ u(X)=\lambda _ v(X)$. Hence the integral does not change if a constant is added to $u$, and we may assume $u\leq v$. We must show that $\int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ v\leq \int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ u$.

Let $t\geq 0$ and $\varepsilon >0$, and put $q=\max \{ u,v-t-\varepsilon \} $. Then $v-t-\varepsilon \leq q\leq v$, so $\lambda _ q(X)=\lambda _ v(X)=\lambda _ u(X)$ by the same corollary. The sets $O_1=\{ u<v-t-\varepsilon \} $ and $O_2=\{ u>v-t-\varepsilon \} $ are disjoint and plurifine open, by proposition 10.5.1 applied in charts after adding the same smooth local potential of $\theta $ to $u$ and $v$; neither of them meets the common pole set of $u$ and $v$. Moreover $q=v-t-\varepsilon $ on $O_1$ and $q=u$ on $O_2$, so plurifine locality (proposition 15.1.5) gives $\lambda _ q=\lambda _ v$ on $O_1$ and $\lambda _ q=\lambda _ u$ on $O_2$, and hence

\[ \lambda _ v(O_1)+\lambda _ u(O_2)\leq \lambda _ q(X)=\lambda _ u(X). \]

Since pole sets carry no mass, it follows that

\[ \lambda _ v(\{ u<v-t-\varepsilon \} ) \leq \lambda _ u(\{ u\leq v-t-\varepsilon \} ) \leq \lambda _ u(\{ u<v-t\} ). \]

As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ the sets $\{ u<v-t-\varepsilon \} $ increase to $\{ u<v-t\} $, and therefore

\[ \lambda _ v(\{ u<v-t\} )\leq \lambda _ u(\{ u<v-t\} )\qquad \text{for every }t\geq 0. \]

On the common finite locus the bounded function $v-u\geq 0$ equals $\int _0^\infty \mathbf1_{\{ u<v-t\} }\, \mathrm dt$. Integrating the last inequality in $t$ and using Tonelli's theorem, we obtain $\int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ v\leq \int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ u$, as required. $\square$
