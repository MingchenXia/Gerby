The determinant functional

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.5 The determinant functional

As the continuous weight varies, the partial kernel measures are the derivatives of a single functional, the logarithm of a Gram determinant. Orthogonal projection expresses its second derivative as minus a sum of squared norms, which gives concavity, and the total mass identity gives a Lipschitz bound in terms of the dimension of the selected space.

We return to the norm $\| \cdot \| _{m,v,\nu }$ on the whole manifold from section 27.1, with selecting potential $\varphi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$. Fix $m$ with $N_ m>0$ and a basis of $V_ m$, and let $G_ m(v)$ be the Gram matrix of this basis for $\| \cdot \| _{m,v,\nu }$, a positive definite Hermitian matrix. The determinant functional is

\[ \mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(v) =-\frac{r!}{m^{r+1}} \log \frac{\det G_ m(v)}{\det G_ m(0)}. \]

A change of basis multiplies both determinants by the same positive factor, so $\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}$ does not depend on the basis. If $N_ m=0$, put $\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}=0$.

Proposition 27.5.1. For $v,f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, the measure $\beta _ m^{(r)}$ at the weight $v$ satisfies

\[ \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(v+tf) =\int _ X f\, \mathrm{d}\beta _ m^{(r)}. \]

The functional $\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}$ is concave on $C(X,\mathbb R)$, and for $v,w\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ and $c\in \mathbb R$,

\[ \mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(v+c)=\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(v) +\frac{r!N_ m}{m^ r}c, \qquad |\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(v)-\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(w)| \leq \frac{r!N_ m}{m^ r}\| v-w\| _\infty . \]

If $N_ m>0$, the second derivative of $t\mapsto \mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(v+tf)$ is strictly negative unless $f$ is constant.

Proof. Choose a basis $s_1,\ldots ,s_{N_ m}$ that is orthonormal for the weight $v$, so that $G_ m(v)=I$ is the identity matrix of size $N_ m$; the indices $i,j$ below range from $1$ to $N_ m$. Since the weights are bounded, we may differentiate under the integral sign. With pointwise inner products taken in $h^ m$, put

\[ A_{ij}=\int _ X f\langle s_ i,s_ j\rangle _{h^ m}\mathrm{e}^{-mv}\, \mathrm{d}\nu , \qquad C_{ij}=\int _ X f^2\langle s_ i,s_ j\rangle _{h^ m}\mathrm{e}^{-mv}\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

The first and second derivatives of $t\mapsto G_ m(v+tf)$ at $t=0$ are $-mA$ and $m^2C$. The identity $(\log \det G)'=\operatorname {tr}(G^{-1}G')$ gives the first derivative of $\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}$, and differentiating it once more yields

\[ \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2}\right|_{t=0}\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}(v+tf) =-\frac{r!}{m^{r-1}} \left(\int _ X f^2B_{m,v,\varphi ,\nu }\, \mathrm{d}\nu -\sum _{i,j}|A_{ij}|^2\right). \]

Let $P_ v$ be the orthogonal projection of the weighted $L^2$ space of measurable sections of $L^ m$ onto $V_ m$. Since $\int _ Xf^2B_{m,v,\varphi ,\nu }\, \mathrm{d}\nu =\sum _ j\| fs_ j\| _{m,v,\nu }^2$ and $P_ v(fs_ j)=\sum _ iA_{ji}s_ i$, Pythagoras' theorem shows that the quantity in parentheses equals

\[ \sum _ j\| (I-P_ v)(fs_ j)\| _{m,v,\nu }^2\geq 0. \]

This proves concavity. Multiplying every Gram entry by $\mathrm{e}^{-mc}$ shows that $\det G_ m(v+c)=\mathrm{e}^{-mcN_ m}\det G_ m(v)$, which is the translation rule. By the first-variation formula and the total mass identity, the derivative of $\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}$ along the segment from $w$ to $v$ is bounded by $(r!N_ m/m^ r)\| v-w\| _\infty $, which gives the Lipschitz estimate.

Suppose finally that the second derivative vanishes. Then $fs_ j\in V_ m$ in $L^2$ for every $j$, so there are $0\ne s\in V_ m$ and $\widetilde s\in V_ m$ with $fs=\widetilde s$ almost everywhere. Both sides are continuous and $\nu $ has a positive density, so $fs=\widetilde s$ everywhere. On a ball where $s\ne 0$, the quotient $\widetilde s/s=f$ is a real-valued holomorphic function, hence a constant $c$. The identity theorem gives $\widetilde s=cs$ on the connected manifold $X$, so $f=c$ wherever $s\neq 0$, and by continuity everywhere. Conversely, for constant $f$ the translation rule shows that the second derivative vanishes. $\square$

Thus $\mathcal F_ m^{(r)}$ is a primitive of the partial kernel measures on every compact complex manifold. This does not affect the obstruction proved in chapter 3 to a primitive of the Hermitian Monge–Ampère operator, since the derivative here is a partial kernel measure and not a Monge–Ampère measure.

Corollary 27.5.2. If $N_ m=o(m^ n)$, then $\beta _ m^{(n)}\to 0$ in total variation, and $\mathcal F_ m^{(n)}(v)\to 0$ uniformly for $v$ in any bounded subset of $C(X)$.

Proof. The measures $\beta _ m^{(n)}$ are positive with total masses $n!N_ m/m^ n\to 0$. The second assertion follows from the Lipschitz estimate, since $\mathcal F_ m^{(n)}(0)=0$. $\square$

For example, suppose that $X$ is not Moishezon. By corollary 21.5.3, either every positive-degree section space of $L$ is zero, or $h^0(X,L^ m)=O(m^ d)$ for some $d<n$. Since $V_ m\subseteq H^0(X,L^ m)$, every sequence of selected spaces satisfies the hypothesis of corollary 27.5.2. By the last assertion of corollary 21.5.3, this applies in particular whenever $X$ fails the bounded mass property of definition 6.5.1.
