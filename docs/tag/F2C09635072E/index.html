Lemma 10.24.18F2C09635072E

Lemma 10.24.18.F2C09635072E On $\mathbb P^1$, let $z=Z_1/Z_0$, $r=|z|^2$, $\alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+r)$ and $h=r/(1+r)$, so that $\int _{\mathbb P^1}\alpha =1$. For every $c>2$ there is a $c\alpha $-plurisubharmonic function $u$, depending only on $|z|$ and smooth off $0$, such that

6251B9B58B26 10.24.19
\begin{equation} g_1:=c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm{e}^{u}h^{-1}c\alpha . \end{equation}

It has zero Lelong number at $0$, mass $\int g_1=c$, and

\[ \int _{\mathbb P^1}|u|\alpha +\int _{\mathbb P^1}|u|g_1<\infty . \]

Moreover, with $t=\log r$ and $s=-\log r$, there is $A\in \mathbb R$ such that, as $s\to \infty $,

\[ \begin{aligned} u& =\log (2/c)-2\log (s+A)+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}),\\ u_ t& =\frac{2}{s+A}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}),\qquad u_{tt}=\frac{2}{(s+A)^2}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}). \end{aligned} \]

Proof. Barriers and the radial inverse. Set $L=1-\log h=1+\log (1+1/r)$ and $w=-2\log L$. Differentiation gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=\left(-\frac2L+\frac2{rL^2}\right)\alpha ,\qquad Q(r):=\frac{c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w}{\mathrm{e}^{w}h^{-1}c\alpha } =\frac{crL^2-2rL+2}{c(1+r)}. \]

The function $Q$ is positive, since $L\geq 1$ and $c>2$. Its limits are $2/c$ at $0$ and $1-2/c$ at infinity, so $0<q_-\leq Q\leq q_+<\infty $ for some constants $q_\pm $. Choose $a_0\leq \min (0,\log q_-)$ and $b_0\geq \max (0,\log q_+)$. Then $l=w+a_0$ and $H=w+b_0$ satisfy

\[ c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}l\geq \mathrm{e}^{l}h^{-1}c\alpha ,\qquad c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H\leq \mathrm{e}^{H}h^{-1}c\alpha ,\qquad l\leq w\leq H. \]

Exhaust $\mathbb P^1\setminus \{ 0\} $ by the disks $|\zeta |<R$ in the coordinate $\zeta =1/z$, and solve the equation on them with boundary value $w$. For $q=|\zeta |^2$, the equation becomes

\[ \Delta u=f(q)\mathrm{e}^{u}-g(q),\qquad f(q)=\frac{4c}{1+q},\qquad g(q)=\frac{4c}{(1+q)^2}, \]

where $\Delta $ is the Euclidean real Laplacian.

For $k>0$, the regular radial solution $v=V(q)$ of $(-\Delta +k)v=F(q)$ satisfies

6AD6B6EC392C 10.24.20
\begin{equation} V(q)=A_0+\frac14\int _0^ q\log (q/t)\bigl (kV(t)-F(t)\bigr )\, \mathrm{d}t. \end{equation}

The $j$-fold iterate of the integral kernel applied to the constant $1$ is $q^ j/(4^ j(j!)^2)$, so successive approximation converges uniformly on every finite interval. The coefficient of $A_0$ in the solution is $\sum _{j\geq 0}(kq/4)^ j/(j!)^2>0$, so exactly one value of $A_0$ gives any prescribed value at $q=R^2$. Moreover,

\[ V'(q)=\frac14\int _0^1\bigl (kV(qt)-F(qt)\bigr )\, \mathrm{d}t, \]

and this formula gives smoothness at $q=0$ by induction. The maximum principle for $-\Delta +k$ shows that this radial inverse is unique and preserves order.

Choose $k\geq \sup f\mathrm{e}^{H}$ on the fixed disk, and let $T(v)$ be the radial solution of $(-\Delta +k)T(v)=kv-f\mathrm{e}^{v}+g$ with boundary value $w$; the right side is nondecreasing in $v$ on $[l,H]$. The barrier inequalities give $l\leq T(l)$ and $T(H)\leq H$, so the iterates of $T$ starting from $l$ increase and stay below $H$. The derivative formula gives a uniform bound for their first derivatives on the fixed interval. Compactness and monotonicity give uniform convergence; passing to the limit in (10.24.20) proves the equation, and the averaged derivative formula bootstraps the limit to smoothness.

The same barriers bound the solutions on every fixed disk in $\zeta $ as $R\to \infty $. For an exact solution $U(q)$ of the nonlinear equation,

\[ U'(q)=\frac14\int _0^1\bigl (f(qt)\mathrm{e}^{U(qt)}-g(qt)\bigr )\, \mathrm{d}t, \]

and induction gives bounds for all derivatives on compact intervals. A diagonal limit is therefore smooth on the $\zeta $-plane, including the point $z=\infty $, and satisfies $u=-2\log (-\log r)+O(1)$ at $0$.

The asymptotics, mass and energy. In the variable $t=\log r$, the equation reads

\[ u_{tt}=\frac{c\mathrm{e}^{u}}{1+\mathrm{e}^ t} -\frac{c\mathrm{e}^ t}{(1+\mathrm{e}^ t)^2}. \]

Put $v(t)=u(t)+c\log (1+\mathrm{e}^ t)$ and $V(s)=v(-s)$. Then

\[ V''=k_0(s)\mathrm{e}^{V},\qquad k_0(s)=c(1+\mathrm{e}^{-s})^{-(c+1)},\qquad V=-2\log s+O(1). \]

Convexity and this sublinear decay imply $V'<0$ and $V'\to 0$ at infinity. The function $J=(V')^2-2k_0\mathrm{e}^{V}$ satisfies $J'=-2k_0'\mathrm{e}^{V}$ and tends to zero. Since $k_0'=O(\mathrm{e}^{-s})$ and $\mathrm{e}^{V}\asymp s^{-2}$,

\[ J(s)=2\int _ s^\infty k_0'(\tau )\mathrm{e}^{V(\tau )}\, \mathrm{d}\tau =O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}s^{-2}). \]

Thus $(\mathrm{e}^{-V/2})'=\sqrt{c/2}(1+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}))$, and integration gives $\mathrm{e}^{-V/2}=\sqrt{c/2}(s+A)+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s})$. This identity and the differential equation give all three asymptotic formulas.

The local potential $u+c\log (1+r)$ extends subharmonically across $0$ by lemma 5.24.7. Its radial slope tends to zero at the puncture, so the extension adds no point mass. The density of $g_1$ is asymptotic to

\[ \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\frac{2}{r s^2} \, \mathrm{d}z\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z. \]

Stokes' theorem for currents gives mass $c$. The energy integral at the cusp is bounded by $C\int ^\infty (\log s)/s^2\, \mathrm{d}s$, and the integral against the smooth form $\alpha $ is finite as well. $\square$
