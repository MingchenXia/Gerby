Construction of continuous solutions

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 10.4 Construction of continuous solutions

Smooth approximation gives solutions with unknown normalization constants. A Gauduchon trace bounds these constants above, and the lower bound for the density norm in theorem 10.3.3 bounds them away from zero. The resulting uniform $L^ p$ bound puts the potentials in the compact family of theorem 10.3.4, so their limit solves the prescribed equation.

of theorem 10.1.1. Choose smooth strictly positive functions $f_ j\to f$ in $L^ p(X,\mathrm{d}V)$. By theorem 9.1.1, there are smooth $u_ j$ with $\omega _{u_ j}>0$ and constants $c_ j$ such that

\[ \omega _{u_ j}^ n=c_ jf_ j\, \mathrm{d}V,\qquad \sup _ Xu_ j=0,\qquad c_ j>0. \]

We first bound the constants. For $n\geq 2$, fix a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $. By proposition 4.2.3,

\[ c_ j^{1/n}A_ j\leq \int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}, \qquad A_ j=\int _ X(f_ j\mathrm{d}V/\gamma ^ n)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n. \]

Since $|x^{1/n}-y^{1/n}|\leq |x-y|^{1/n}$ for $x,y\geq 0$, Hölder's inequality gives $A_ j\to A>0$, where $A=\int _ X(f\mathrm{d}V/\gamma ^ n)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n$. Thus $c_ j$ is bounded above. In dimension one, Stokes' theorem gives the same estimate, with $A_ j=\int _ Xf_ j\, \mathrm{d}V$.

The lower bound for the density norm in theorem 10.3.3 gives $c_ j\| f_ j\| _ p\geq c_ p$, so the constants are bounded away from zero. The densities $c_ jf_ j$ are therefore uniformly bounded in $L^ p$, and the same theorem gives a common bound for the $u_ j$. By theorem 1.4.2, we may pass to a subsequence with $c_ j\to c>0$ and $u_ j$ Cauchy in $L^1$. By theorem 10.3.4, $u_ j\to u$ uniformly, with $u$ continuous and $\sup _ Xu=0$.

Bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity gives $\omega _{u_ j}^ n\rightharpoonup \omega _ u^ n$, while $c_ jf_ j\to cf$ in $L^ p$. Hence $\omega _ u^ n=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$, as required. $\square$

For the exponential equation, the role of the normalization constant is played by $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xu}$. Its upper bound comes from Jensen's inequality: weighting the Gauduchon volume by the $n$th root of the density relates $\sup _ Xu$ to the Gauduchon trace of $\omega _ u$, which does not depend on $u$. The lower bound for the density norm again gives the reverse bound.

Lemma 10.4.1. Fix $\lambda >0$. Suppose that smooth functions $u_ j$ with $\omega _{u_ j}>0$ solve

\[ \omega _{u_ j}^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where the smooth strictly positive functions $f_ j$ converge in $L^ p$ to a nonnegative nonzero $f$. Then $M_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j$ is bounded above and below, uniformly in $j$.

Proof. Absorbing the fixed smooth positive ratio $\mathrm{d}V/\omega ^ n$ into $f_ j$ and $f$ preserves their $L^ p$ convergence, so we may assume $\mathrm{d}V=\omega ^ n$. Choose $\rho >0$ smooth with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \omega ^{n-1})=0$, using theorem 4.1.2; for $n=1$, take $\rho =1$. Put

\[ \alpha =\int _ X\rho \omega ^ n,\quad A_ j=\int _ X f_ j^{1/n}\rho \omega ^ n,\quad \psi _ j=u_ j-M_ j\leq 0. \]

Set $A=\int _ X f^{1/n}\rho \omega ^ n$. The inequality for $n$th roots used in the preceding existence proof gives $A_ j\to A>0$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \omega ^{n-1})=0$, the trace $\int _ X\omega _{u_ j}\wedge \rho \omega ^{n-1}$ equals $\alpha $; the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality therefore implies

\[ \alpha =\int _ X\omega _{u_ j}\wedge \rho \omega ^{n-1} \geq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda M_ j/n}\int _ X \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \psi _ j/n}f_ j^{1/n}\rho \omega ^ n. \]

Applying Jensen's inequality to the probability measure $A_ j^{-1}f_ j^{1/n}\rho \omega ^ n$ and taking logarithms, we obtain

10.16
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gauduchon-exponential-mean} \lambda M_ j\leq n\log (\alpha /A_ j) +\frac{\lambda }{A_ j}\int _ X(-\psi _ j)f_ j^{1/n}\rho \omega ^ n. \end{equation}

By lemma 1.5.1, the normalized potentials $\psi _ j$ have uniform $L^ q$ bounds for every finite $q$. With $q=np/(np-1)$, Hölder's inequality gives a uniform bound for the last integral, since $\| f_ j^{1/n}\| _{np}=\| f_ j\| _ p^{1/n}$. This proves the upper bound for $M_ j$.

Finally, the density $F_ j=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j$ satisfies $\| F_ j\| _ p\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda M_ j}\| f_ j\| _ p$. The lower-density bound of theorem 10.3.3, applied to $\psi _ j$, gives $c_ p\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda M_ j}\| f_ j\| _ p$, which bounds $M_ j$ below. $\square$

of theorem 10.1.2. Choose smooth strictly positive functions $f_ j\to f$ in $L^ p$. By theorem 9.6.2, there are smooth $u_ j$ with $\omega _{u_ j}>0$ and $\omega _{u_ j}^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j\, \mathrm{d}V$. The preceding lemma bounds $M_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j$, and consequently bounds $F_ j=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j$ in $L^ p$. The normalized potentials $\psi _ j=u_ j-M_ j$ are uniformly bounded by theorem 10.3.3. We may pass to a subsequence with $M_ j\to M$ and $\psi _ j$ Cauchy in $L^1$. Theorem 10.3.4 gives uniform convergence $u_ j\to u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap C(X)$.

Uniform convergence and $f_ j\to f$ in $L^ p$ give $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f$ in $L^ p$, and continuity of bounded products therefore gives $\omega _ u^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\, \mathrm{d}V$. Uniqueness, even among all bounded solutions, follows from bounded exponential comparison, corollary 10.2.4. $\square$

For the exponential equation, a larger density gives a smaller solution, since the factor $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ has to compensate for the increase. Bounded exponential comparison gives this monotonicity even where the densities vanish.

Lemma 10.4.2. Let $\lambda >0$, and let $f_ a,f_ b$ be nonnegative nonzero $L^ p$ densities. Suppose that $a,b\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$ solve

\[ \omega _ a^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda a}f_ a\, \mathrm{d}V,\qquad \omega _ b^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda b}f_ b\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

If $f_ a\leq f_ b$ almost everywhere, then $b\leq a$ on $X$.

Proof. Since $f_ a\leq f_ b$, the two equations give $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda b}\omega _ b^ n\geq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a}\omega _ a^ n$. Hence $b\leq a$ by corollary 10.2.4, applied with $u=a$ and $v=b$. $\square$

Corollary 10.4.3. Every bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic solution of $\omega _ u^ n=f\, \mathrm{d}V$ is continuous.

Proof. Since $u$ is bounded, the density $\mathrm{e}^{-u}f$ is nonnegative, nonzero and belongs to $L^ p$. By theorem 10.1.2, applied with $\lambda =1$ to this density, there is a continuous solution $r$ of

\[ \omega _ r^ n=\mathrm{e}^{r-u}f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

The bounded potential $u$ solves the same exponential equation. Applying lemma 10.4.2 with $f_ a=f_ b=\mathrm{e}^{-u}f$ in both orders gives $u=r$, so $u$ is continuous. $\square$
