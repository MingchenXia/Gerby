Chapter 10 · Hermitian geometry, torsion, and Gauduchon duality · Tag 635FE1D11798

Nef annihilator determinant

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 26.20.2

Proposition 26.20.2. Let $\theta $ be nef with $\lambda _\mu (\theta )>0$. If $T\in \mathscr G_ h$ satisfies $\int _ X\theta \wedge T=0$, then the determinant-weighted measure of $T$, computed as in (26.39) with reference metric $h$ and with $\mu $ in place of $\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV$, vanishes.

Proof. In dimension one the hypotheses cannot hold, since $\int _ X\theta \wedge T=\lambda _\mu (\theta )/\int _ Xh>0$. Suppose $n\geq 2$, and write $D_\mu (T)$ for the total mass of this determinant-weighted measure; for $T=\gamma ^{n-1}$ it is the denominator $D_\mu (\gamma )$ of definition 26.1.1. If $\eta \in \mathscr P$, its solution form $\Omega $ satisfies $\Omega ^ n=\lambda _\mu (\eta )^ n\mu $, so $\Omega /\lambda _\mu (\eta )$ is an admissible test in the infimum of lemma 26.6.1. Together with Stokes' theorem, this gives

\[ \int _ X\eta \wedge T=\int _ X\Omega \wedge T \geq \lambda _\mu (\eta )D_\mu (T). \]

Apply this to $\eta =\theta +\varepsilon h$. The left side equals $\varepsilon $, while theorem 26.10.1 gives $\lambda _\mu (\eta )\to \lambda _\mu (\theta )>0$. Hence $D_\mu (T)=0$. The determinant-weighted measure is positive and has total mass zero, so it vanishes. $\square$
