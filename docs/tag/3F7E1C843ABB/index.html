27.12.2 The limiting envelope and the transition scale3F7E1C843ABB

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.12.2 The limiting envelope and the transition scale

A vanishing order proportional to $m$ removes the kernel density inside a fixed circle. Inside that circle the limit of the potentials is logarithmic, and outside it agrees with the smooth weight. Throughout this subsection, $m$ tends to infinity through positive integers. For $\lambda >0$, set $q_ m=\lfloor m\lambda \rfloor $ and $R_\lambda =(\lambda /r)^{1/(2r)}$. Define

\[ V_\lambda (z)= \begin{cases} |z|^{2r},& |z|\geq R_\lambda ,\\ \lambda \log (|z|^2/R_\lambda ^2)+\lambda /r,& 0<|z|<R_\lambda ,\\ -\infty ,& z=0. \end{cases} \]

In the following theorem, $\delta _0$ denotes the unit point mass at zero. Recall that in dimension one the non-pluripolar measure of a subharmonic function discards the mass carried by its polar set.

Theorem 27.12.4. One has

\[ \frac1m\log K_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z,z)\longrightarrow V_\lambda (z) \]

pointwise with extended values and in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb C)$. The kernel measures converge locally in total variation:

27.71
\begin{equation} \frac1m B_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}\, \mathrm{d}A \longrightarrow \mathbf1_{\{ |z|>R_\lambda \} }\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi . \end{equation}

The function $V_\lambda $ is the largest subharmonic function $v$ on $\mathbb C$ such that $v\leq \phi $ and $v(z)\leq \lambda \log |z|^2+C$ near zero for some constant $C$. Its curvature current is

27.72
\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\lambda =\lambda \delta _0+ \mathbf1_{\{ |z|>R_\lambda \} }\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi . \end{equation}

Thus the limit in (27.71) is the non-pluripolar measure of this envelope, rather than its full distributional Laplacian.

Proof. For fixed $z\ne 0$, put $\rho =|z|$ and $T=m\rho ^{2r}$. Then $L_ s(q_ m)/T\to \lambda /(r\rho ^{2r})$, and this limit differs from one when $\rho \neq R_\lambda $. Chebyshev's inequality and (27.65) therefore give

27.73
\begin{equation} \frac1m B_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z)\longrightarrow \begin{cases} 0,& 0<\rho <R_\lambda ,\\ r^2\rho ^{2r-2}/\pi ,& \rho >R_\lambda . \end{cases} \end{equation}

The selected density is at most the full density, that is, the density with $q=0$. When $T\geq 1$, the same comparison with $q=0$ bounds it by $C_ r m\rho ^{2r-2}$; when $T\leq 1$, (27.62) with $q=0$ bounds it by $C_ r m^{1/r}\leq C_ r m$. Thus $m^{-1}B_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}$ is uniformly bounded on each compact set. Since the circle $\rho =R_\lambda $ and the origin have zero area, dominated convergence and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi =(r^2/\pi )|z|^{2r-2}\mathrm{d}A$ prove (27.71).

To compute the limit of the potentials inside the circle, fix $0<\rho <R_\lambda $ and write

\[ d_{m,k}=\frac{m^{(k+1)/r}\rho ^{2k}}{\Gamma ((k+1)/r)} \quad (k\geq q_ m). \]

The estimate of Gamma ratios from Stirling's formula gives

\[ \frac{d_{m,k+1}}{d_{m,k}} \leq \left(\frac{rm\rho ^{2r}}{q_ m+1}\right)^{1/r} \left(1+\frac{C_ r}{q_ m+1}\right) \quad (k\geq q_ m) \]

for all sufficiently large $m$. The right side tends to $(r\rho ^{2r}/\lambda )^{1/r}<1$. Hence the sum of these terms lies between $d_{m,q_ m}$ and a fixed multiple of that term, and Stirling's formula yields

\[ \frac1m\log K_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z,z) \longrightarrow \lambda \log \rho ^2+ \frac\lambda r\bigl (1-\log (\lambda /r)\bigr ) =V_\lambda (z). \]

For $\rho >R_\lambda $, the limit of the densities in (27.73) is positive. Hence $m^{-1}\log B_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z)\to 0$, and therefore $m^{-1}\log K_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z,z)$ tends to $\phi (z)$.

At $\rho =R_\lambda $, put $k_ m=\max \{ q_ m,\lfloor rm\rho ^{2r}\rfloor \} $. Then $k_ m/m\to r\rho ^{2r}=\lambda $. The single monomial of degree $k_ m$ and Stirling's formula give $\liminf m^{-1}\log K_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z,z)\geq \phi (z)$, and the bound for the full kernel already proved gives the reverse inequality for the upper limit. At zero the selected kernel vanishes for all sufficiently large $m$. This proves pointwise convergence everywhere.

For local $L^1$ convergence, the bounds for the full kernel give a uniform upper bound for $m^{-1}\log K_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z,z)$ on each compact set. The single selected monomial of degree $q_ m$ gives the lower bound

\[ \frac1m\log K_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z,z) \geq c_ m+\frac{q_ m}{m}\log |z|^2, \]

where $c_ m$ is bounded as $m\to \infty $, again by Stirling's formula. Since $0\leq q_ m/m\leq \lambda $, the negative part on a fixed compact set is bounded by a constant plus $2\lambda |\log |z||$ near zero, which is locally integrable. Dominated convergence proves the $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ assertion.

We turn to the envelope. The two radial expressions for $V_\lambda $ and their first radial derivatives agree at $R_\lambda $, because $rR_\lambda ^{2r}=\lambda $. Away from zero and that circle, direct differentiation proves (27.72). Since the first derivatives match, the circle carries no measure, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^2=\delta _0$ gives the atom at zero. The right side is positive, so $V_\lambda $ is subharmonic. For $t=|z|^{2r}/(\lambda /r)<1$, the inequality $\log t+1\leq t$ proves $V_\lambda \leq \phi $ inside the circle; outside it there is equality.

Finally, let $v$ be subharmonic with $v\leq \phi $ and $v\leq \lambda \log |z|^2+C$ near zero. On the punctured disk $0<|z|<R_\lambda $, the function $v-\lambda \log |z|^2$ is subharmonic and bounded above near zero, so it extends subharmonically across zero. On the boundary circle it is at most $\lambda /r-\lambda \log R_\lambda ^2$, since $v\leq \phi =\lambda /r$ there. The maximum principle gives $v\leq V_\lambda $ inside the disk, and outside the disk this follows from $v\leq \phi $. Since $V_\lambda $ itself satisfies the conditions, it is the largest such function. Removing the atom at the pole gives the last assertion. $\square$

The measures in this theorem have infinite total mass on $\mathbb C$, so their convergence is asserted only on compact sets. The weighted potentials $m^{-1}\log B_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}$ converge to $V_\lambda -\phi $. The radius $R_\lambda $ is created by the selecting ideal even when $r=1$, whereas $r>1$ also reflects the curvature zero at the origin.

For any integer sequence $q_ m\to \infty $, the transition at the boundary circle is governed by the central limit theorem for the same Poisson tails. Let $\Phi $ be the standard Gaussian distribution function,

\[ \Phi (\tau )=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi }}\int _{-\infty }^{\tau } \mathrm{e}^{-s^2/2}\, \mathrm{d}s \quad (\tau \in \mathbb R). \]

Proposition 27.12.5. Fix a real $\tau $. Choose $z_ m\ne 0$ with

\[ m|z_ m|^{2r}=\frac{q_ m}{r}+\tau \sqrt{\frac{q_ m}{r}}, \]

which is positive for all sufficiently large $m$. Then

\[ \frac{\pi B_{m,r}^{(q_ m)}(z_ m)}{mr^2|z_ m|^{2r-2}} \longrightarrow \Phi (\tau ). \]

In particular, this applies both when $q_ m/m\to \lambda >0$ and when $q_ m/m\to 0$.

Proof. We first recall the central limit theorem for Poisson variables in the form used here. For a fixed real $M>0$, Stirling's formula for $\ell !$ gives, uniformly over integers $\ell $ with $|\ell -T|\leq M\sqrt T$,

\[ \mathbb P(N_ T=\ell )=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi T}} \exp \left(-\frac{(\ell -T)^2}{2T}\right) (1+O_ M(T^{-1/2}))\quad (T\to \infty ). \]

Indeed, insert the expansion of the factorial in $\mathrm{e}^{-T}T^\ell /\ell !$ and expand the logarithm in powers of $(\ell -T)/T$; the cubic remainder is $O_ M(T^{-1/2})$. Riemann sums over bounded intervals therefore converge to Gaussian integrals, and Chebyshev's bound $\mathbb P(|N_ T-T|>M\sqrt T)\leq M^{-2}$ then allows $M\to \infty $. Consequently, if real thresholds $a_ T$ satisfy $(a_ T-T)/\sqrt T\to a\in \mathbb R$, then $\mathbb P(N_ T\geq a_ T)\to 1-\Phi (a)$; rounding changes the normalized threshold by at most $T^{-1/2}$, and $\Phi $ is continuous.

Set $T_ m=m|z_ m|^{2r}$. Then $T_ m\to \infty $ and, for every integer $0\leq s<r$,

\[ \frac{L_ s(q_ m)-T_ m}{\sqrt{T_ m}}\longrightarrow -\tau . \]

Apply the preceding Poisson limit in (27.65). The uniform error there tends to zero, and each of the finitely many probabilities tends to $1-\Phi (-\tau )=\Phi (\tau )$. $\square$

For a sequence $q_ m\to \infty $, put $R_ m=(q_ m/(rm))^{1/(2r)}$. At fixed $\tau $, the radii in proposition 27.12.5 satisfy

\[ |z_ m|=R_ m\left(1+\frac{\tau }{2\sqrt{rq_ m}}+O_{r,\tau }(q_ m^{-1})\right). \]

If $q_ m=o(m)$, these radii tend to zero, while their ratio to the scale $m^{-1/(2r)}$ of the full kernel tends to infinity; when $r>1$, they shrink to a curvature zero. To transfer this transition to a compact manifold, both the local comparison of norms and the error of the global extension must be uniform in the growing vanishing order. The next section establishes both estimates on a curve.
