15.20.7 Second derivatives at positive exponentBDDD67E926DC

On the band where the density vanishes, the exponential factor plays no role, and the radial equation determines the slope of the solution independently of the exponent. Consequently the positive-exponent solutions have the same failure of second differentiability as the selected limit $u_*$. We keep the notation $A$, $\Phi $, $k$ and the transition radius $t_*$ of subsection 15.20.3.

Proposition 15.20.8.517F0BB04B80 For the symmetric projective data of theorem 15.20.4, let $u_\lambda $ solve

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\omega (u_\lambda ) =\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_\lambda }f\omega _ X^3, \qquad \lambda >0. \]

On the open band $\{ -1<r<1\} $, $u_\lambda -u_*$ is constant. Both potentials are $C^{1,1}$ there and fail to be $C^2$ at every point of the two hypersurfaces $r=\pm t_*$. In particular, none of these positive-exponent solutions is a globally $C^2$ potential.

Proof. Uniqueness and invariance of the data imply that $u_\lambda $ is $K$-invariant. Write $u_\lambda +\rho =G_\lambda (r)$ on $U$. The positivity argument in lemma 15.20.5 uses only boundedness and invariance, so $G_\lambda $ is locally Lipschitz and $h_\lambda =G_\lambda '$ is locally of bounded variation, with

\[ -x\leq h_\lambda (r\pm )\leq y, \qquad \mathrm{d}h_\lambda +z\, \mathrm{d}r\geq 0. \]

Its weak flux equation is the same one, with the prescribed coefficient replaced by

\[ k_\lambda (r)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (G_\lambda (r)-\rho (r))}k(r). \]

Indeed, the scalar convolution and signed-product argument of that lemma is unchanged, and $k_\lambda $ is exactly the radial coefficient of the right-hand measure. Since $G_\lambda $ is bounded on compact $r$-intervals, $k_\lambda $ is locally bounded. Hence the right side of the flux equation has no atoms. The same strictly positive jump integral eliminates every jump of $h_\lambda $. Thus $h_\lambda $ is continuous and $G_\lambda $ is $C^1$. The coefficient $k_\lambda $ is consequently continuous. Where $A(r,h_\lambda )>0$, inversion of the $C^1$ flux gives

\[ h_\lambda '=\frac{k_\lambda (r)}{A(r,h_\lambda )}-z. \]

On $-1<r<1$ the coefficient $k_\lambda $ is identically zero. Exchange invariance gives $h_\lambda (0)=0$ and oddness. On the positive half-band, the first-contact and no-departure arguments in the proof of theorem 15.20.4 apply verbatim: the first branch solves $h'=-z_0$, reaches $-p$ at $t_*$, and cannot leave that lower boundary to its right because $(h+p)'=p'-z_0<0$ on every proposed departure interval. Therefore

\[ h_\lambda (r)= \begin{cases} p(r),& -1<r\leq -t_*,\\ -\displaystyle \int _0^ r z_0(s)\, \mathrm{d}s,& -t_*\leq r\leq t_*,\\ -p(r),& t_*\leq r<1. \end{cases} \]

This is the derivative of $u_*+\rho $. Its primitive differs from $G_\lambda $ by one constant on the connected band. The claimed local $C^{1,1}$ regularity follows from the explicit slope.

At $t_*\in (0,1)$ the two one-sided derivatives of this slope are

\[ h_\lambda '(t_*-)=-z_0(t_*)<0, \qquad h_\lambda '(t_*+)=-p'(t_*)>0. \]

These unequal derivatives prevent the scalar primitive from having a second derivative at $t_*$. Through every point of that level set, the submersion $r$ provides a smooth curve parametrized by $r$, transferring the failure to $u_\lambda $. Smooth $\rho $ cannot cancel the jump. Oddness gives the conclusion at $-t_*$, and the identical slope proves it for $u_*$. $\square$

The zero set of $f$ contains the whole band. This example therefore does not concern the smooth strictly positive densities in the Hermitian Calabi–Yau theorem. It does show why smoothness of a nonnegative density alone is insufficient for second-order regularity, even when the equation includes a strictly increasing exponential factor.
