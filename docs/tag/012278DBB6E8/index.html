Chapter 5 · Plurisubharmonic functions and positive currents · Tag 012278DBB6E8

Changes of background

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.2 Changes of background

A positive current can be written as a smooth form plus a complex Hessian in more than one way. Changing the decomposition by a smooth $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact form changes the potential by a bounded function, and positive linear combinations allow us to compare potentials belonging to different backgrounds. In this section, $\eta $ denotes a second smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$.

Proposition 1.2.1. Let $f$ be a smooth real function on $X$, and set $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$. Then

\[ \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\longrightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta '), \qquad u\longmapsto u-f \]

is a bijection. It preserves the positive current and changes each potential by a bounded function. Moreover, $\mathrm{d}\theta '=\mathrm{d}\theta $.

Proof. For every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$,

\[ \theta '+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-f)=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u. \]

The inverse map is $v\mapsto v+f$, and $f$ is bounded because $X$ is compact. The last assertion follows from $\mathrm{d}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=0$. $\square$

Thus the natural object is the class of $\theta $ modulo smooth $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact forms. This quotient of all smooth real $(1,1)$-forms contains nonclosed classes; the usual Bott–Chern cohomology group is the corresponding quotient of closed forms.

Proposition 1.2.2. If $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\eta )$, then, for $a,b\geq 0$ with $a+b>0$,

\[ au+bv\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,a\theta +b\eta ). \]

In particular, $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is convex. If $\eta \geq \theta $, then $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\subseteq \mathrm{PSH}(X,\eta )$.

Proof. Omitting a term whose coefficient is zero, we see that $au+bv$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic. Moreover,

\[ a\theta +b\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(au+bv) =a(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)+b(\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)\geq 0. \]

For the inclusion, add the semipositive smooth form $\eta -\theta $ to $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. $\square$

By proposition 1.1.2, any two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,C\omega )$ for a common sufficiently large constant $C$. This allows us to compare their singularities within one positive background.
