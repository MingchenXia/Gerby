12.6 Birational change of variables and logarithmic thresholdsBC64CE221556

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 12.6 Birational change of variables and logarithmic thresholds

On a modification of $X$, the change of variables formula turns membership in a multiplier ideal into an integrability condition twisted by the Jacobian (proposition 12.6.4), which becomes explicit on a log resolution.

Let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map between complex manifolds of dimension $n$. Thus there is a nowhere dense analytic subset $A\subset X$ such that $\pi ^{-1}(A)$ is nowhere dense in $Y$ and $\pi $ maps $Y\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$ biholomorphically onto $X\setminus A$ (section A.3). In local coordinates $w$ on $Y$ and $z$ on $X$, the complex Jacobian determinant $J_\pi =\det (\partial z_ i/\partial w_ j)$ is holomorphic, and it does not vanish identically on any nonempty open set, since it has no zeros on the dense open set $Y\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$. By the chain rule, a change of coordinates on either side multiplies $J_\pi $ by a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function; equivalently, $\pi ^*(\mathrm dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dz_ n)=J_\pi \, \mathrm dw_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dw_ n$. Hence the functions $J_\pi $ are the local equations of an effective Cartier divisor $K_{Y/X}$ on $Y$ (section A.5), the relative canonical divisor, and $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})$ is generated locally by $J_\pi ^{-1}$.

We measure the vanishing of holomorphic functions along hypersurfaces as follows. Let $E\subset Y$ be a prime divisor, that is, an irreducible hypersurface. By corollary A.2.13, applied to the family consisting of $E$ alone, its regular locus $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a connected manifold, dense in $E$. Let $p\in E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, choose coordinates $w=(w_1,w')$ at $p$ with $E=\{ w_1=0\} $ near $p$, and let $h$ be holomorphic near $p$. The order of $h$ along $E$ at $p$ is the supremum, in $\{ 0,1,2,\ldots \} \cup \{ +\infty \} $, of the integers $k\geq 0$ such that $h$ is divisible by $w_1^ k$ near $p$, that is, $h=w_1^ kh_1$ near $p$ with $h_1$ holomorphic. By the next lemma, it does not depend on the coordinates, and for a function defined near $E$ it does not depend on the point of $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$.

Lemma 12.6.1. Let $E$ be a prime divisor on a complex manifold $Y$, let $p\in E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, let $w=(w_1,w')$ be holomorphic coordinates at $p$ with $E=\{ w_1=0\} $ near $p$, and let $h$ be holomorphic near $p$. Let $\Delta =\Delta _1\times \Delta '$ be a polydisc centered at $p$, where $\Delta _1$ is a disc in the $w_1$-plane, so small that $E\cap \Delta =\{ w_1=0\} \cap \Delta $ and that (7.1) gives the expansion

\[ h=\sum _{l\geq 0}a_ l(w')w_1^ l\quad \text{on }\Delta ,\qquad a_ l(w')=\frac1{l!}\partial _{w_1}^ lh(0,w'), \]

where the functions $a_ l$ are holomorphic on $\Delta '$.

  1. Let $k\geq 0$ be an integer. If $h$ is divisible by $w_1^ k$ near some point of $E\cap \Delta $, then $a_ l=0$ for all $l<k$; conversely, if $a_ l=0$ for all $l<k$, then $h$ is divisible by $w_1^ k$ near $p$. In particular, a holomorphic function that vanishes on $E$ near $p$ is divisible by $w_1$ near $p$, and if $\tilde w=(\tilde w_1,\tilde w')$ are other coordinates at $p$ with $E=\{ \tilde w_1=0\} $ near $p$, then $\tilde w_1$ is a unit times $w_1$ near $p$.

  2. The order of $h$ along $E$ at $p$ does not depend on the coordinates. It is the smallest $l$ with $a_ l\neq 0$, and it is $+\infty $ if and only if $h$ vanishes near $p$. In particular, $h$ is not divisible by $w_1$ near $p$ if and only if $h(0,\cdot )$ does not vanish identically near $p$. Moreover, the order of $h$ along $E$ is the same at all points of $E\cap \Delta $.

  3. If $h$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $E$, then its order along $E$ is the same at all points of $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$.

  4. Orders add under products: if $g$ is also holomorphic near $p$, then the order of $hg$ along $E$ at $p$ is the sum of the orders of $h$ and $g$.

Proof. (1) If $h=w_1^ kh_1$ near a point $(0,w'_0)$ of $E\cap \Delta $, with $h_1$ holomorphic, then the Leibniz rule gives $\partial _{w_1}^ lh(0,w')=0$ for $l<k$ and $w'$ near $w'_0$; so $a_ l=0$ near $w'_0$, and hence on the connected set $\Delta '$ by the identity theorem. Conversely, if $a_ l=0$ for $l<k$, then $h=w_1^ k\sum _{l\geq k}a_ lw_1^{l-k}$ near $p$; the last series is obtained from the Taylor series of $h$ at $p$ by lowering the exponent of $w_1$ in each term by $k$, so it converges on the same polydiscs and defines a holomorphic function (section A.1). If $h$ vanishes on $E$ near $p$, then $a_0=h(0,\cdot )$ vanishes near the center of $\Delta '$, hence on $\Delta '$, and the case $k=1$ shows that $h$ is divisible by $w_1$ near $p$. Applying this to $\tilde w_1$, and to $w_1$ with the roles of the two coordinate systems exchanged, we obtain $\tilde w_1=c\, w_1$ and $w_1=\tilde c\, \tilde w_1$ near $p$ with holomorphic $c$ and $\tilde c$. Then $c\tilde c=1$ off $E$; since the continuous function $c\tilde c$ equals $1$ on the dense set $\{ w_1\neq 0\} $, it equals $1$ near $p$, and $c$ is a unit.

(2) By (1), $h$ is divisible by $w_1^ k$ near $p$ if and only if it is divisible by $\tilde w_1^ k$ near $p$, so the order does not depend on the coordinates. Let $k_0$ be the smallest $l$ with $a_ l\neq 0$, and $k_0=+\infty $ if all $a_ l$ vanish. By (1), $h$ is divisible by $w_1^ k$ near $p$ if and only if $k\leq k_0$, so the order of $h$ along $E$ at $p$ is $k_0$. If all $a_ l$ vanish, then $h=0$ on $\Delta $; conversely, if $h$ vanishes near $p$, then all $a_ l$ vanish near the center of $\Delta '$, hence on $\Delta '$. The case $k_0=0$ gives the assertion about $h(0,\cdot )=a_0$, because $a_0$ vanishes identically on $\Delta '$ if and only if it vanishes near the center of $\Delta '$. Finally, let $q=(0,w'_ q)\in E\cap \Delta $. The coordinates $w$ are also coordinates at $q$ with $E=\{ w_1=0\} $ near $q$, and by their formula the coefficients of the expansion of $h$ on a small polydisc centered at $q$ are the restrictions of the same functions $a_ l$. Since $\Delta '$ is connected, $a_ l$ vanishes near $w'_ q$ if and only if it vanishes on $\Delta '$, by the identity theorem. Hence the orders of $h$ along $E$ at $q$ and at $p$ are equal.

(3) By the last assertion of (2), the order of $h$ along $E$ is a locally constant function on $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, which is connected (corollary A.2.13). Hence it is constant.

(4) Shrinking $\Delta $, we may assume that $g$ is defined on $\Delta $; let $b_ j$ and $e_ m$ be the coefficients of the expansions of $g$ and $hg$ on $\Delta $. By the Leibniz rule, $e_ m=\sum _{i+j=m}a_ ib_ j$. If $h$ or $g$ has infinite order, then $hg$ vanishes near $p$ and has infinite order by (2). Otherwise let $k$ and $l$ be the orders of $h$ and $g$. Then $e_ m=0$ for $m<k+l$, because each term $a_ ib_ j$ with $i+j<k+l$ has $i<k$ or $j<l$, and $e_{k+l}=a_ kb_ l$. Since $a_ k$ and $b_ l$ are not identically zero and the ring of holomorphic functions on the connected set $\Delta '$ is an integral domain, by the identity theorem, $e_{k+l}\neq 0$. By (2), the order of $hg$ is $k+l$. $\square$

If $h$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $E$, we call the common value in lemma 12.6.1(3) the order $\operatorname {ord}_ Eh$ of $h$ along $E$. The order of $J_\pi $ along a prime divisor $E\subset Y$ is defined in the same way, because local Jacobians differ by units, which have order zero, and orders add under products (lemma 12.6.1(4)); it is the coefficient $\operatorname {ord}_ EK_{Y/X}$ of $E$ in $K_{Y/X}$.

For divisors with simple normal crossings support we use local monomial equations.

Lemma 12.6.2. Let $w$ be holomorphic coordinates centered at a point $p$ of a complex manifold, let $0\leq \ell \leq n$, and let $h$ be holomorphic near $p$, not identically zero near $p$, with zero set near $p$ contained in $\{ w_1\cdots w_\ell =0\} $. For $i\leq \ell $ let $k_ i$ be the largest integer such that $h$ is divisible by $w_ i^{k_ i}$ near $p$. Then $h=u\prod _{i\leq \ell }w_ i^{k_ i}$ near $p$, with $u$ holomorphic and $u(p)\neq 0$. Moreover, a holomorphic function near $p$ that is divisible by $w_ i^{a_ i}$ near $p$ for each $i\leq \ell $ is divisible by $\prod _{i\leq \ell }w_ i^{a_ i}$.

Proof. Let $g=\sum _\alpha c_\alpha w^\alpha $ be the power series at $p$ of a holomorphic function, as in (7.1). If $g=w_ i^ ag'$ near $p$, then multiplying the series of $g'$ by $w_ i^ a$ shows that $c_\alpha =0$ whenever $\alpha _ i<a$. Conversely, if $c_\alpha =0$ whenever $\alpha _ i<a_ i$ for some $i\leq \ell $, then $g=\prod _ iw_ i^{a_ i}\cdot \sum _\alpha c_\alpha w^{\alpha -\sum _ ia_ ie_ i}$, where $e_ i$ is the $i$th unit vector, and the last series converges on the same polydiscs as that of $g$. This proves the last assertion, and it shows that $k_ i<\infty $, since otherwise all $c_\alpha $ of $h$ would vanish. Hence $h=u\prod _{i\leq \ell }w_ i^{k_ i}$ with $u$ holomorphic near $p$, and $u$ is not divisible by any $w_ i$, $i\leq \ell $, by the maximality of $k_ i$.

It remains to show that $u(p)\neq 0$. Suppose, to the contrary, that $u(p)=0$. The zero set of $u$ near $p$ lies in that of $h$, hence in $\{ w_1\cdots w_\ell =0\} $; so $\ell \geq 1$. We work in the coordinates $w$, with $p=0$. Let $P$ be the first nonzero homogeneous term of $u$; it has positive degree $m$. Choose $v\in \mathbb C^ n$ with $P(v)\neq 0$ and $v_ i\neq 0$ for all $i$, which is possible since the polynomial $P(v)v_1\cdots v_ n$ is not identically zero. Then $t\mapsto u(tv)$ has a zero of order $m$ at $t=0$; choose $\varepsilon >0$ such that it has no other zeros in $|t|\leq \varepsilon $, and such that all points $y+tv$ considered below lie in the neighborhood of $0$ where the hypothesis on the zero set holds. Since $(y,t)\mapsto u(y+tv)$ is continuous and does not vanish on the compact set $\{ 0\} \times \{ |t|=\varepsilon \} $, there is a connected neighborhood $N$ of $0$ such that $u(y+tv)\neq 0$ for $y\in N$ and $|t|=\varepsilon $. By the argument principle, the number of zeros of $t\mapsto u(y+tv)$ in $|t|<\varepsilon $, counted with multiplicity, is

\[ \frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\int _{|t|=\varepsilon }\frac{\partial _ t[u(y+tv)]}{u(y+tv)}\, \mathrm dt, \]

a continuous integer-valued function of $y\in N$; it equals $m\geq 1$ at $y=0$, hence everywhere on $N$. So for every $y\in N$ there is $t$ with $u(y+tv)=0$, and then $y_ i+tv_ i=0$ for some $i\leq \ell $, that is, $u(\sigma _ i(y))=0$ for the linear projection $\sigma _ i(y)=y-(y_ i/v_ i)v$ onto the hyperplane $H_ i=\{ y_ i=0\} $. Hence the product of the holomorphic functions $u\circ \sigma _ i$, $i\leq \ell $, vanishes on $N$. If none of them vanished identically on $N$, each would be nonzero on a dense open subset of $N$, by the identity theorem, and so would be their product. Thus some $u\circ \sigma _ i$ vanishes on $N$; since $\sigma _ i(y)=y$ for $y\in H_ i$, the function $u$ vanishes on $H_ i\cap N$, which is a neighborhood of $0$ in $H_ i$. By the first paragraph, applied with $a=1$, $u$ is then divisible by $w_ i$ near $0$: all coefficients $c_\alpha $ of $u$ with $\alpha _ i=0$ vanish, since they are the coefficients of the restriction of $u$ to $H_ i$. This contradiction shows that $u(p)\neq 0$. $\square$

Let $D$ be an effective divisor on $Y$ with simple normal crossings support, and let $E_1,E_2,\ldots $ be the irreducible components of its support; they are smooth, and connected because their regular loci are (corollary A.2.13). Near a point $p$ choose coordinates $w$ in which the support of $D$ is $\{ w_1\cdots w_\ell =0\} $, with $E_ i=\{ w_ i=0\} $ for the components through $p$. By lemma 12.6.2, a local equation of $D$ at $p$ is a unit times $\prod _{i\leq \ell }w_ i^{d_ i}$, where $d_ i\geq 1$ is its order along $E_ i$. Two local equations differ by a unit on the overlap of their domains, so $d_ i$ does not depend on the point of $E_ i$; it is the multiplicity of $E_ i$ in $D$, and we write $D=\sum _ id_ iE_ i$. Conversely, for integers $a_ i\geq 0$ we write $\sum _ ia_ iE_ i$ for the effective divisor whose local equations are the monomials $\prod _{i\leq \ell }w_ i^{a_ i}$. If $A$ and $B$ are effective divisors on $Y$ with local equations $s_ A$ and $s_ B$, then $\mathcal O_ Y(A-B)=\mathcal O_ Y(A)\mathcal O_ Y(-B)$ is the sheaf of functions on the complement of the support of $A$ that are locally of the form $g\, s_ B/s_ A$ with $g$ holomorphic.

Definition 12.6.3. Let $E\subset Y$ be a prime divisor. Its center on $X$ is $\pi (E)$, which is an analytic subset of $X$ by theorem A.6.2; it is connected, because $E$ is. Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on an open set $V\subset X$ containing the center, and let $V_0$ be the connected component of $V$ containing it. If $f$ vanishes identically on $V_0$, for instance if $f=0$, we set $\operatorname {ord}_ Ef=+\infty $. Otherwise $f\circ \pi $ does not vanish identically on any nonempty open subset of $\pi ^{-1}(V_0)$, because such a set contains a nonempty open set mapped biholomorphically onto an open subset of $V_0$, on which $f$ does not vanish identically by the identity theorem; since $E\subset \pi ^{-1}(V_0)$, the order of $f$ along $E$ is $\operatorname {ord}_ Ef=\operatorname {ord}_ E(f\circ \pi )<\infty $. This order along a divisor differs from the order at a point of proposition 12.1.4, although the two agree for the exceptional divisor of the blowup of a point (example 12.6.10). For a holomorphic germ $f$ at a point $x$, this defines $\operatorname {ord}_ Ef$ for the prime divisors $E$ on smooth models of open neighborhoods of $x$ on which $f$ is defined; this is how orders of germs enter the results below. The log discrepancy of $E$ is

\[ A_ X(E)=1+\operatorname {ord}_ E K_{Y/X}, \]

where $\operatorname {ord}_ EK_{Y/X}=\operatorname {ord}_ EJ_\pi $ is the coefficient of $E$ in $K_{Y/X}$. Since $J_\pi $ is holomorphic, $A_ X(E)\geq 1$, and a prime divisor on $X$ itself, with $\pi $ the identity, has log discrepancy one. The center, the order and the log discrepancy depend only on the prime divisor over $X$ represented by $E$.

To see that these notions depend only on the prime divisor over $X$, let $\rho \colon Y'\to Y$ be a map from a common smooth model and $E'$ the strict transform of $E$. By proposition A.7.3, $\rho $ is biholomorphic over $Y\setminus S$ for an analytic subset $S$ of codimension at least two, and it maps the nonempty open subset $E'\cap \rho ^{-1}(Y\setminus S)$ of $E'$ isomorphically onto the dense open subset $E\setminus S$ of $E$; hence $\rho (E')=E$, since $\rho (E')$ is closed by properness. At a point $q'$ of $E'\cap \rho ^{-1}(Y\setminus S)$ that is regular on $E'$ and maps to a regular point of $E$, the biholomorphism $\rho $ near $q'$ transforms local equations of $E$ into local equations of $E'$, so $\operatorname {ord}_{E'}(f\circ \pi \circ \rho )=\operatorname {ord}_ E(f\circ \pi )$ and $\operatorname {ord}_{E'}(J_\pi \circ \rho )=\operatorname {ord}_ EJ_\pi $, while $J_\rho $ does not vanish near $q'$. By the chain rule $J_{\pi \circ \rho }=(J_\pi \circ \rho )J_\rho $, and since orders add under products (lemma 12.6.1(4)), $\operatorname {ord}_{E'}K_{Y'/X}=\operatorname {ord}_ EK_{Y/X}$. Thus $E'$ has the same center $\pi (\rho (E'))=\pi (E)$ as $E$, and the same orders and log discrepancy. Since two representatives of the same prime divisor over $X$ have the same strict transform on a common smooth model, this proves the claim.

Proposition 12.6.4. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$, $x\in X$, and $f\in \mathcal O_{X,x}$. Then $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ if and only if, near every point of the compact fiber $\pi ^{-1}(x)$, the function

12.20
\begin{equation} |f\circ \pi |^2|J_\pi |^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi \circ \pi } \end{equation}

is integrable with respect to Lebesgue measure in local coordinates on $Y$. Moreover, $\pi _*\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})=\mathcal O_ X$: if $U\subset X$ is open, then every section of $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})$ over $\pi ^{-1}(U)$, that is, every function that is locally of the form $g/J_\pi $ with $g$ holomorphic, equals $f\circ \pi $ for a unique $f\in \mathcal O(U)$. Under this identification

12.21
\begin{equation} \mathcal I(\varphi ) =\pi _*\bigl (\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})\mathcal I(\varphi \circ \pi )\bigr ), \end{equation}

where $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})\mathcal I(\varphi \circ \pi )$ is the sheaf of functions that are locally of the form $g/J_\pi $ with $g$ a section of $\mathcal I(\varphi \circ \pi )$.

Proof. By proposition A.7.3, $\pi $ is biholomorphic over $X\setminus S$ for an analytic subset $S\subset X$ of codimension at least two; in particular $S$ is nowhere dense. The analytic subset $\pi ^{-1}(S)$ of $Y$ is nowhere dense as well, because every nonempty open subset of $Y$ contains a nonempty open set that $\pi $ maps biholomorphically onto an open subset of $X$, and such a set is not contained in $S$. Near each of their points, nowhere dense analytic subsets lie in the zero set of a holomorphic function that does not vanish identically on a connected neighborhood (section A.1); so $S$ and $\pi ^{-1}(S)$ are Lebesgue null sets in charts, by lemma 7.4.1.

In holomorphic coordinates the real Jacobian determinant of $\pi $, that is, the factor by which $\pi ^*$ multiplies the volume form $\mathrm dx_1\wedge \mathrm dy_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dx_ n\wedge \mathrm dy_ n$, is $|J_\pi |^2$. Indeed, since $\frac{\mathrm i}2\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j=\mathrm dx_ j\wedge \mathrm dy_ j$, this form equals $c_ n\, \mathrm dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dz_ n\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ n$ with $c_ n=(\mathrm i/2)^ n(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}$, the sign coming from moving each $\mathrm dz_ j$ to the left past $\mathrm d\bar z_1,\ldots ,\mathrm d\bar z_{j-1}$, and similarly in the coordinates $w$. Since $\pi ^*(\mathrm dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dz_ n)=J_\pi \, \mathrm dw_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dw_ n$ and the conjugate identity holds for the $\mathrm d\bar z_ j$, the claim follows. The change of variables formula for nonnegative measurable functions, applied to the biholomorphism $\pi $ from $W\setminus \pi ^{-1}(S)$ onto its image, therefore gives

\[ \int _{\pi (W\setminus \pi ^{-1}(S))}g\, \mathrm dV_ z =\int _ W(g\circ \pi )|J_\pi |^2\, \mathrm dV_ w \]

for every nonnegative measurable $g$ and every open set $W$ in a coordinate chart of $Y$ that is mapped into a coordinate chart of $X$, with $J_\pi $ computed in these coordinates.

Now suppose that $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }$ is integrable on a coordinate neighborhood $V$ of $x$. Applying the formula with $g=|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }$ to small coordinate neighborhoods $W\subset \pi ^{-1}(V)$, we see that (12.20) is integrable near every point of $\pi ^{-1}(V)$. Conversely, suppose that (12.20) is integrable near every point of $\pi ^{-1}(x)$, where $f$ is defined on a coordinate neighborhood $V_0$ of $x$. Cover the compact fiber by finitely many coordinate neighborhoods $W_1,\ldots ,W_ k\subset \pi ^{-1}(V_0)$ on which it is integrable. Since $\pi $ is proper, $\pi (Y\setminus (W_1\cup \cdots \cup W_ k))$ is closed and does not contain $x$, so there is a neighborhood $V\subset V_0$ of $x$ with $\pi ^{-1}(V)\subset W_1\cup \cdots \cup W_ k$. The sets $\pi (W_ l\setminus \pi ^{-1}(S))$ cover $V\setminus S$, and $S$ is a null set, so the formula gives $\int _ V|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }\, \mathrm dV\leq \sum _ l\int _{W_ l}|f\circ \pi |^2|J_\pi |^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi \circ \pi }\, \mathrm dV_ w<\infty $.

Next let $U\subset X$ be open and $h$ a section of $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})$ over $\pi ^{-1}(U)$; we may assume that $U$ is connected. Over $U\setminus S$ the map $\pi $ is biholomorphic and $J_\pi $ has no zeros, so $h$ is holomorphic on $\pi ^{-1}(U\setminus S)$, and $h=f\circ \pi $ there for the holomorphic function $f=h\circ (\pi |_{\pi ^{-1}(U\setminus S)})^{-1}$ on $U\setminus S$, which is connected by lemma A.1.7. If $f\not\equiv 0$, the function $\log |f|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $U\setminus S$ (proposition 8.3.3), so by corollary A.2.15 it is locally bounded above near $S$; hence $f$ is locally bounded near $S$, and lemma A.1.7 extends $f$ holomorphically to $U$. Locally $h=g/J_\pi $ with $g$ holomorphic, and $g=(f\circ \pi )J_\pi $ on the dense open set $\pi ^{-1}(U\setminus S)$, hence everywhere; so $h=f\circ \pi $. The function $f$ is unique, since it is determined on $U\setminus S$, which is dense in $U$.

Finally, let $U\subset X$ be open and $f\in \mathcal O(U)$. Since $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})$ is locally generated by $J_\pi ^{-1}$, the function $f\circ \pi $ is a section of $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})\mathcal I(\varphi \circ \pi )$ over $\pi ^{-1}(U)$ if and only if $(f\circ \pi )J_\pi $ is a section of $\mathcal I(\varphi \circ \pi )$ there, that is, if and only if (12.20) is integrable near every point of $\pi ^{-1}(U)$. By the first part, applied at every point of $U$, this holds if and only if $f$ is a section of $\mathcal I(\varphi )$ over $U$. Together with the second part, which shows that every section of the right side of (12.21) over $\pi ^{-1}(U)$ has the form $f\circ \pi $, this proves (12.21). $\square$

Log resolutions put weights of the form $c\log \sum _ j|g_ j|^2$ into monomial form. Let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r$ be holomorphic functions on $X$ that generate an ideal $\mathfrak a$ with $\mathfrak a\mathcal O_ Y=\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$, where $D=\sum _ id_ iE_ i$ is an effective divisor with simple normal crossings support. Let $p\in Y$, and choose coordinates $w$ at $p$ in which the support of $D$ is a union of coordinate hyperplanes; after renumbering, $E_1,\ldots ,E_\ell $ are the components of $D$ through $p$, with $E_ i=\{ w_ i=0\} $ near $p$. By lemma 12.6.2, the ideal $\mathfrak a\mathcal O_ Y$ is generated near $p$ by $s=\prod _{i=1}^{\ell }w_ i^{d_ i}$, so $g_ j\circ \pi =sh_ j$ with holomorphic $h_ j$. Writing $s=\sum _ j\beta _ j(g_ j\circ \pi )=s\sum _ j\beta _ jh_ j$ with holomorphic $\beta _ j$ and cancelling $s$, which does not vanish identically on any open set, we get $\sum _ j\beta _ jh_ j=1$. Hence the $h_ j$ have no common zero near $p$, the function $\sum _ j|h_ j|^2$ is bounded above and below by positive constants on a smaller neighborhood of $p$, and

12.22
\begin{equation} \log \sum _{j=1}^ r|g_ j\circ \pi |^2=\sum _{i=1}^{\ell }d_ i\log |w_ i|^2+O(1)\qquad \text{near }p. \end{equation}

Theorem 12.6.5. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$ such that near $x$

\[ \varphi =c\log \left(\sum _{j=1}^ r|g_ j|^2\right)+b, \qquad c>0,\quad b\text{ bounded}, \]

where the holomorphic functions $g_ j$ generate a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak a$. Let $V$ be an open neighborhood of $x$ on which this holds, and let $\pi \colon Y\to V$ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ (section A.5), as supplied by theorem A.5.4, theorem A.5.5, with $\mathfrak a\mathcal O_ Y=\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$, where $D=\sum _ id_ iE_ i$ is an effective divisor with simple normal crossings support, the $E_ i$ being prime divisors and the $d_ i$ positive integers. Then a holomorphic function $f$ on $V$ belongs to $\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ if and only if

12.23
\begin{equation} \operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}f+A_ X(E_ i)>c d_ i \end{equation}

for every component $E_ i$ of $D$ that meets $\pi ^{-1}(x)$, exceptional or not. Since $\pi $ restricts to a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ over every smaller open neighborhood $V'$ of $x$, the criterion applies to every germ at $x$, with the components of the restriction of $D$ to $\pi ^{-1}(V')$ in place of the $E_ i$. Equivalently, with $\lfloor cD\rfloor =\sum _ i\lfloor cd_ i\rfloor E_ i$, near $x$

12.24
\begin{equation} \mathcal I(\varphi )=\pi _*\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X}-\lfloor cD\rfloor ). \end{equation}

For rational $c$, this computes the multiplier ideals of weights with analytic singularities (chapter 13).

Proof. Let $p\in \pi ^{-1}(V)$, and choose coordinates $w$ at $p$ as before (12.22), so that $E_1,\ldots ,E_\ell $ are the components of $D$ through $p$ and $E_ i=\{ w_ i=0\} $ near $p$. Since $b$ is bounded, (12.22), applied to $\pi \colon Y\to V$, gives

\[ \varphi \circ \pi =c\sum _{i=1}^{\ell }d_ i\log |w_ i|^2+O(1) \]

near $p$.

Let $h$ be holomorphic near $p$; we decide when $|h|^2\prod _{i\leq \ell }|w_ i|^{-2cd_ i}$, or equivalently $|h|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi \circ \pi }$, is integrable near $p$. Let $k_ i$ be the order of $h$ along $E_ i$ at $p$, which is finite if $h$ does not vanish identically near $p$; if it does, the integrability is trivial and $k_ i=+\infty $. We claim that the integrability holds if and only if $k_ i+1>cd_ i$ for $i=1,\ldots ,\ell $. If these inequalities hold, write $h=\prod _ iw_ i^{k_ i}\tilde h$ with $\tilde h$ holomorphic, by lemma 12.6.2; the integrand is at most a constant times $\prod _ i|w_ i|^{2k_ i-2cd_ i}$, which is integrable near $p$ by Fubini's theorem and polar coordinates in each $w_ i$, since $2k_ i-2cd_ i>-2$. Conversely, suppose that $k_1+1\leq cd_1$, say, and write $h=w_1^{k_1}\tilde h$ with $\tilde h$ not divisible by $w_1$. Then $\tilde h$ does not vanish identically on $\{ w_1=0\} $ near $p$, by lemma 12.6.1(2), so arbitrarily close to $p$ there are an open set $W'$ of points $w'=(w_2,\ldots ,w_ n)$ and $\delta ,\rho >0$ with $|\tilde h(w_1,w')|\geq \delta $ for $|w_1|<\rho $ and $w'\in W'$. There the integrand is at least a positive constant times $|w_1|^{2k_1-2cd_1}$, since the other factors $|w_ i|^{-2cd_ i}$ are bounded below; as $2k_1-2cd_1\leq -2$, its integral in $w_1$ diverges, and by Fubini's theorem the integrand is not integrable near $p$.

Now let $f$ be holomorphic on $V$, and apply this to $h=(f\circ \pi )J_\pi $, with $J_\pi $ computed in local coordinates near $p$. The function $f\circ \pi $ is holomorphic on $\pi ^{-1}(V)$, which contains $E_ i$, so its order along $E_ i$ at $p$ is $\operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}f$, by lemma 12.6.1(3) and definition 12.6.3; the order of $J_\pi $ along $E_ i$ at $p$ is $\operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}J_\pi $. Since orders add under products (lemma 12.6.1(4)), $k_ i=\operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}f+\operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}J_\pi =\operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}f+A_ X(E_ i)-1$. Thus, for $p\in \pi ^{-1}(x)$, the condition at $p$ is (12.23) for the components through $p$. As $p$ ranges over $\pi ^{-1}(x)$, these are exactly the components meeting the fiber, and proposition 12.6.4 proves the first assertion.

For (12.24), let $U\subset V$ be open and $f\in \mathcal O(U)$, and put $h=(f\circ \pi )J_\pi $ on $\pi ^{-1}(U)$. For an integer $k$, the strict inequality $k+1>cd_ i$ is equivalent to $k\geq \lfloor cd_ i\rfloor $. By proposition 12.6.4 and the claim, applied at every point $p\in \pi ^{-1}(U)$, we have $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi )(U)$ if and only if at every such $p$ the order of $h$ at $p$ along each component $E_ i$ through $p$ is at least $\lfloor cd_ i\rfloor $. By lemma 12.6.2, this means that $h$ is divisible near every point of $\pi ^{-1}(U)$ by the local equation $\prod _ iw_ i^{\lfloor cd_ i\rfloor }$ of $\lfloor cD\rfloor $, that is, that $f\circ \pi =h/J_\pi $ is a section of $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X}-\lfloor cD\rfloor )$ over $\pi ^{-1}(U)$. Since $\lfloor cD\rfloor $ is effective, every section of $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X}-\lfloor cD\rfloor )$ over $\pi ^{-1}(U)$ is a section of $\mathcal O_ Y(K_{Y/X})$, and hence of the form $f\circ \pi $ with $f\in \mathcal O(U)$, by proposition 12.6.4. This proves (12.24) on $V$. $\square$

For general weights we combine the change of variables with an estimate on one-dimensional slices transversal to a divisor.

Lemma 12.6.6. Let $\psi $ be a weight on a complex manifold $Y$, let $E\subset Y$ be a prime divisor, let $p\in E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and let $h$ be holomorphic near $p$ with $|h|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi }$ integrable near $p$. If $k$ is the order of $h$ along $E$ at $p$, then $k+1>\nu (\psi ,E)$, where $\nu (\psi ,E)$ is the generic Lelong number of proposition 12.3.5.

Proof. We may assume $k<\infty $. Choose coordinates $w=(w_1,w')$ at $p$ with $E=\{ w_1=0\} $, and write $h=w_1^ kv$ with $v$ holomorphic and not divisible by $w_1$; then $v(0,\cdot )$ does not vanish identically near $p$, by lemma 12.6.1(2). Moving $p$ slightly along $E$, we may assume that $v(p)\neq 0$, so that $|v|\geq \delta >0$ on a neighborhood $\{ |w_1|<\rho \} \times W'$ of $p$ on which $|h|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi }$ is integrable. By Fubini's theorem, for almost every $w'\in W'$ the function $w_1\mapsto |w_1|^{2k}|v|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi (w_1,w')}$ is integrable on $|w_1|<\rho $; moreover, for almost every $w'$ the function $u=\psi (\cdot ,w')$ is not identically $-\infty $ on this disc, since the pole set of $\psi $ is a null set.

Fix such a $w'$. By proposition 8.2.3, $u$ is subharmonic on the disc, and its Lelong number at $0$ is at least $\nu (\psi ,(0,w'))$: restricting the bound $\psi (w)\leq c\log \bigl (|w-(0,w')|^2/r_0^2\bigr )+C$ of (12.2), with $c=\nu (\psi ,(0,w'))$, to the line through $(0,w')$ in the $w_1$-direction gives $u(w_1)\leq c\log (|w_1|^2/r_0^2)+C$. Moreover $\nu (\psi ,(0,w'))\geq \nu (\psi ,E)$, the latter being an infimum over $E$. Hence, by (12.2) for $u$, there are $r_1\in (0,\rho ]$ and $c'>0$ with $\mathrm e^{-u(w_1)}\geq c'|w_1|^{-2\nu (\psi ,E)}$ for $0<|w_1|<r_1$. Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \infty & >\int _{|w_1|<r_1}|w_1|^{2k}|v|^2\mathrm e^{-u}\, \mathrm dV_2 \geq \delta ^2c'\int _{|w_1|<r_1}|w_1|^{2k-2\nu (\psi ,E)}\, \mathrm dV_2\\ & =2\pi \delta ^2c'\int _0^{r_1}t^{2k-2\nu (\psi ,E)+1}\, \mathrm dt, \end{aligned} \]

which forces $2k-2\nu (\psi ,E)+2>0$, that is, $k+1>\nu (\psi ,E)$. $\square$

Proposition 12.6.7. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$ and $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$. Then there are $\varepsilon >0$ and a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ on which $f$ is defined such that

12.27
\begin{equation} \operatorname {ord}_ E f+A_ X(E)>(1+\varepsilon )\nu (\varphi ,E) \end{equation}

for every prime divisor $E$ over an open subset of $V$; in particular, this holds for every prime divisor over an open neighborhood of $x$ contained in $V$ whose center contains $x$.

Proof. By corollary 12.5.4 there are $\varepsilon >0$ and a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ on which $f$ is defined such that $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-(1+\varepsilon )\varphi }$ is integrable on $V$. Let $E$ be a prime divisor over an open set $U\subset V$, represented on a smooth model $\pi \colon Y\to U$; we may assume that $\operatorname {ord}_ Ef<\infty $. Since $f\in \mathcal I((1+\varepsilon )\varphi )_ y$ for every $y\in U$, proposition 12.6.4, applied to the weight $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi $ on $U$, shows that $|h|^2\mathrm e^{-(1+\varepsilon )\varphi \circ \pi }$, with $h=(f\circ \pi )J_\pi $, is integrable near every point of $Y$. Let $p\in E_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Since orders add under products, the order of $h$ along $E$ at $p$ is $\operatorname {ord}_ Ef+\operatorname {ord}_ EJ_\pi =\operatorname {ord}_ Ef+A_ X(E)-1$. By lemma 12.6.6, applied to the weight $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi \circ \pi $, whose generic Lelong number along $E$ is $(1+\varepsilon )\nu (\varphi ,E)$ by proposition 12.1.4, we obtain (12.27). $\square$

Corollary 12.6.8. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$, let $E$ be a prime divisor over $X$, and let $m>0$ be a real number. Then

12.28
\begin{equation} m\nu (\varphi ,E)-A_ X(E) <\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I(m\varphi ) \leq m\nu (\varphi ,E). \end{equation}

Here, for a smooth model $\pi \colon Y\to X$ on which $E$ lies, $\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ is the order along $E$ of the pulled-back ideal $\mathcal I(m\varphi )\mathcal O_ Y$, defined as follows. Near a point $p\in E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r$ generate $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ near $\pi (p)$. The minimum of the orders of the functions $g_ i\circ \pi $ along $E$ at $p$ does not depend on the choice of generators, since the members of a second system of generators are combinations of the $g_ i$ with holomorphic coefficients near $\pi (p)$, and conversely. This minimum is locally constant along $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, because the orders of the $g_ i\circ \pi $ are, by lemma 12.6.1(2); hence it is constant on the connected manifold $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and it is $\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I(m\varphi )$. As for the order of a function in definition 12.6.3, it depends only on the prime divisor over $X$ represented by $E$. In particular,

\[ \nu (\varphi ,E)=\lim _{m\to \infty }\frac1m \operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I(m\varphi ). \]

Proof. Let $k=\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathcal I(m\varphi )$, and choose a point $p\in E_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Put $y=\pi (p)$, choose a bounded polydisc $\Omega $ around $y$ in a chart on which $\varphi $ is bounded above, and choose $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r\in \mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$ generating $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ near $y$ (theorem 12.2.7(3)). Near $p$, in coordinates $w=(w_1,w')$ with $E=\{ w_1=0\} $, let $k_ i$ be the order of $g_ i\circ \pi $ along $E$ at $p$, so that $k=\min _ ik_ i$; since $\mathcal I(m\varphi )_ y\neq 0$, this minimum is finite.

For the lower bound, choose $i$ with $k_ i=k$. Since $|g_ i|^2\mathrm e^{-m\varphi }$ is integrable on $\Omega $, proposition 12.6.4, applied to the weight $m\varphi $, shows that $|h|^2\mathrm e^{-m\varphi \circ \pi }$, with $h=(g_ i\circ \pi )J_\pi $, is integrable near $p$. The order of $h$ along $E$ at $p$ is $k+A_ X(E)-1$, because orders add under products. By lemma 12.6.6, applied to the weight $m\varphi \circ \pi $, whose generic Lelong number along $E$ is $m\nu (\varphi ,E)$ by proposition 12.1.4, we obtain $k+A_ X(E)>m\nu (\varphi ,E)$. This is the left inequality.

For the upper bound, lemma 12.2.8 gives $m\varphi \leq \log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+mC_ m$ on a neighborhood of $y$. Near $p$ we have $g_ i\circ \pi =w_1^{k_ i}u_ i$ with $u_ i$ holomorphic, so $|g_ i\circ \pi |\leq C|w_1|^{k_ i}\leq C|w_1|^ k$ where $|w_1|\leq 1$; it follows that $m\varphi \circ \pi \leq k\log |w_1|^2+C''$ on a neighborhood $N$ of $p$. For $q\in E\cap N$ we have $|w_1|\leq |w-q|$, so $m\varphi \circ \pi (w)\leq k\log |w-q|^2+C''$ near $q$, and proposition 12.1.1, proposition 12.1.4 give $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,q)\geq k/m$. Since the generic value $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,E)$ is attained at points of $E$ arbitrarily close to $p$ (proposition 12.3.5), we conclude that $m\nu (\varphi ,E)\geq k$. Dividing (12.28) by $m$ and letting $m\to \infty $ gives the limit formula. $\square$

Theorem 12.6.9. Let $\varphi $ be a weight on $X$, $x\in X$, and $f\in \mathcal O_{X,x}$. Then $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ if and only if there are $\varepsilon >0$ and a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ on which $f$ is defined such that

12.29
\begin{equation} \operatorname {ord}_ E f+A_ X(E)\geq (1+\varepsilon )\nu (\varphi ,E) \end{equation}

for every prime divisor $E$ over an open neighborhood of $x$ contained in $V$ whose center contains $x$. The criterion remains valid if $\geq $ is replaced by $>$. For a weight with analytic singularities, theorem 12.6.5 needs instead only the finitely many strict inequalities (12.23) on one log resolution, without a parameter $\varepsilon $.

Proof. Necessity, even with strict inequality, is proposition 12.6.7. Since the strict condition implies the non-strict one, it suffices to show that the non-strict condition implies $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$; we may assume $f\neq 0$. We compare $\varphi $ with a weight $\varphi _ m$ with analytic singularities defined by generators of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$, apply theorem 12.6.5 to a multiple of $\varphi _ m$, and then return to $\varphi $. Put $\lambda =1+\varepsilon /2$ and choose an integer $m\geq 2$ with $\lambda \geq m/(m-1)$, that is, $(m-1)\lambda \geq m$. On a bounded polydisc $\Omega \subset V$ around $x$ on which $\varphi $ is bounded above, choose $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r\in \mathcal H_{m\varphi }(\Omega )$ generating $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$ near $x$, and put

\[ \varphi _ m=\frac1m\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2. \]

This is plurisubharmonic by proposition 8.3.3, since $\mathcal I(m\varphi )_ x\neq 0$ by lemma 12.3.1, so that the $g_ i$ do not all vanish identically. By (12.11), $\varphi \leq \varphi _ m+C_ m$ on a neighborhood $W\subset V$ of $x$. Pulling this back to a smooth model and using the characterization of Lelong numbers in proposition 12.1.1 at every point, we see that $\nu (\varphi _ m,E)\leq \nu (\varphi ,E)$ for every prime divisor $E$ over an open subset of $W$. Hence, for every prime divisor $E$ over an open neighborhood of $x$ contained in $W$ whose center contains $x$,

\[ \operatorname {ord}_ E f+A_ X(E)\geq (1+\varepsilon )\nu (\varphi ,E)\geq (1+\varepsilon )\nu (\varphi _ m,E), \]

and this is strictly larger than $\lambda \nu (\varphi _ m,E)$: when $\nu (\varphi _ m,E)>0$ because $1+\varepsilon >\lambda $, and when $\nu (\varphi _ m,E)=0$ because the left side is at least $A_ X(E)\geq 1$.

Now let $\pi \colon Y\to W'$ be a log resolution of the ideal $(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)$ over an open neighborhood $W'\subset W$ of $x$, with $(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)\mathcal O_ Y=\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$ and $D=\sum _ id_ iE_ i$. Let $q\in E_ i$, and choose coordinates $w$ at $q$ as before (12.22), so that the components of $D$ through $q$ are coordinate hyperplanes and $E_ i=\{ w_ i=0\} $. By (12.22), $\log \sum _ l|g_ l\circ \pi |^2=\sum _ jd_ j\log |w_ j|^2+O(1)\leq d_ i\log |w_ i|^2+C$ near $q$, where $j$ runs over the components through $q$; since $|w_ i|\leq |w-q|$, the characterization in proposition 12.1.1 gives $\nu (\varphi _ m\circ \pi ,q)\geq d_ i/m$. If $q$ lies on no other component of $D$, then $\varphi _ m\circ \pi =(d_ i/m)\log |w_ i|^2+O(1)$ near $q$, and $\nu (\varphi _ m\circ \pi ,q)=d_ i/m$ by proposition 12.1.4. Such points exist, because $E_ i$ is not contained in the union of the other components of the support of $D$ (corollary A.2.13). Hence $\nu (\varphi _ m,E_ i)=d_ i/m$. Every component $E_ i$ meeting $\pi ^{-1}(x)$ has a center containing $x$, so the strict inequality above reads $\operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}f+A_ X(E_ i)>(\lambda /m)d_ i$ for these components, and theorem 12.6.5, applied on $W'$ to the weight $\lambda \varphi _ m$ with $c=\lambda /m$ and $b=0$, gives $f\in \mathcal I(\lambda \varphi _ m)_ x$.

It remains to pass from $\lambda \varphi _ m$ to $\varphi $. By the definition of the generators,

12.30
\begin{equation} \mathrm e^{m(\varphi _ m-\varphi )} =\sum _ i|g_ i|^2\mathrm e^{-m\varphi }\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}} \end{equation}

near $x$. Shrinking the neighborhood and subtracting a constant from $\varphi _ m$, we may assume $\varphi _ m\leq 0$; this multiplies the functions in question by constants and changes none of the integrability statements. We split a small neighborhood of $x$ into the set where $\varphi \geq \lambda \varphi _ m$ and its complement, ignoring the null set formed by the poles of $\varphi $ and the common zeros of the $g_ i$, so that all exponents below are finite. On the first set, $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }\leq |f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda \varphi _ m}$, which is integrable because $f\in \mathcal I(\lambda \varphi _ m)_ x$. On the complement we have $(m-1)\varphi <(m-1)\lambda \varphi _ m$, since $m\geq 2$; as $(m-1)\lambda \geq m$ and $\varphi _ m\leq 0$, it follows that

\[ (m-1)\varphi -m\varphi _ m \leq ((m-1)\lambda -m)\varphi _ m\leq 0, \]

and therefore

\[ |f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi } =|f|^2\mathrm e^{m(\varphi _ m-\varphi )} \mathrm e^{(m-1)\varphi -m\varphi _ m} \leq C\mathrm e^{m(\varphi _ m-\varphi )}, \]

where $C$ is a local bound for $|f|^2$. By (12.30) the right side is integrable near $x$. Hence $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }$ is integrable near $x$, that is, $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$. $\square$

Example 12.6.10. Let $c>0$ and $\varphi =c\log |z_1|^2$ on a polydisc in $\mathbb C^ n$. The ideal $(z_1)$ is already principal with smooth zero set, so the identity map is a log resolution, with $D=E=\{ z_1=0\} $, $d=1$ and $A_ X(E)=1$. By theorem 12.6.5, a germ $f$ lies in $\mathcal I(\varphi )$ if and only if $\operatorname {ord}_ Ef+1>c$. Here the log resolution has no exceptional divisors, and the only condition comes from the non-exceptional component $E$; this is why theorem 12.6.5 involves all components of $D$ meeting the fiber, exceptional or not.

For the blowup $\pi $ of the origin in dimension $n\geq 2$, consider the chart $z_1=w_1$, $z_ j=w_1w_ j$ for $j>1$ of proposition A.5.3. The Jacobian matrix $(\partial z_ i/\partial w_ j)$ is lower triangular with diagonal entries $1,w_1,\ldots ,w_1$, so $J_\pi =w_1^{n-1}$, and the exceptional divisor $E=\{ w_1=0\} $ has $A_ X(E)=n$. Let $f$ be holomorphic near $0$ with homogeneous expansion $f=\sum _{k\geq k_0}P_ k$ and $P_{k_0}\neq 0$. Then $f\circ \pi (w)=\sum _{k\geq k_0}w_1^ kP_ k(1,w_2,\ldots ,w_ n)=w_1^{k_0}g(w)$ for small $w_1$ and bounded $(w_2,\ldots ,w_ n)$, where $g(0,w')=P_{k_0}(1,w')$; this polynomial is not identically zero, since $P_{k_0}(z)=z_1^{k_0}P_{k_0}(1,z_2/z_1,\ldots ,z_ n/z_1)$ for $z_1\neq 0$. Hence $\operatorname {ord}_ Ef=\operatorname {ord}_0f$.

For $\varphi =c\log |z|^2$, this blowup is a log resolution of the maximal ideal, with $D=E$ and $d=1$, by proposition A.5.3, so theorem 12.6.5 gives $f\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_0$ if and only if $\operatorname {ord}_0f+n>c$. This agrees with a direct computation in polar coordinates: since $|f(z)|\leq C|z|^{k_0}$ near $0$ by (7.2), and $\int _{S^{2n-1}}|f(t\omega )|^2\, \mathrm dS(\omega )\geq c't^{2k_0}$ for small $t>0$, with $c'>0$, by (12.10), the integral of $|f|^2|z|^{-2c}$ over a small ball $B(0,r)$ is finite if and only if $\int _0^ rt^{2k_0-2c+2n-1}\, \mathrm dt<\infty $, by (2.1), that is, if and only if $k_0+n>c$.
