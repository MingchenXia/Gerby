Perron interpolation and the geodesic equation

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 17 Perron interpolation and the geodesic equation

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ for the $L^1(X)$ topology.

This part studies two operations on singular potentials: interpolation between two potentials along an auxiliary real parameter, and restriction to a complex submanifold. A one-parameter family of $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions can be regarded as a single function on the product of $X$ with a strip in $\mathbb C$, and positivity of its curvature current on the product is a convexity condition in the parameter. The largest such family with prescribed endpoints, the Perron envelope, interpolates between two potentials; in Kähler geometry it gives the weak geodesics of the space of potentials. A Legendre transform in the parameter relates rays made of Perron segments to concave decreasing families of envelope fixed points, the test curves of chapter 18. Restriction behaves differently: the restriction of a potential to a submanifold can acquire poles that are invisible to its multiplier ideals, and chapter 19 works instead with the information carried by the restricted multiplier ideals. All these constructions use only positivity of currents, so they apply to nonclosed backgrounds.

In Kähler geometry, the smooth geodesic equation for the Mabuchi $L^2$ metric on potentials is a homogeneous Monge–Ampère equation on such a product. Positivity on the product still defines Perron interpolation for a nonclosed background, but the two constructions can now differ: integration by parts in the $L^2$ action produces torsion terms. Even a smooth Perron segment given by translation by constants can fail to be stationary for that action.

The Perron construction itself rests on convexity and barriers. Convexity in the parameter bounds every admissible path by the chord between its endpoints, while a lower barrier ensures that the envelope attains the endpoints. For two potentials of the same ordinary singularity type, two affine barriers suffice and give a Lipschitz bound in the parameter. Maximality on each finite subinterval is the property that links Perron segments with Legendre duality in chapter 18.
