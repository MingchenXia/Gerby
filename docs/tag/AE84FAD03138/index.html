Density zeros and metric degeneration

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 28.11 Density zeros and metric degeneration

Where the density vanishes, the metric defined by a solution degenerates, and the rate of degeneration depends on how the zero arises. In complex dimension $n\geq 2$, a zero of the determinant can come from a ramified holomorphic map, from an exceptional fiber of a blowup, or from a singular Hessian. These mechanisms give different relations between the regularity of the potential and the distance to the zero set. Bounded Hölder solutions in a positive background exist by theorem 10.1.1, theorem 14.4.1, but their existence gives no pointwise estimate for the metric at such a zero.

Proposition 28.11.1. Let $F:X\to Y$ be a finite surjective holomorphic map between compact Kähler manifolds of dimension $n$, let $\omega _ Y$ be a Kähler form on $Y$, and suppose that the class $F^*[\omega _ Y]$ contains a Kähler form $\theta $. Then the full-mass solution in the background $\theta $ with measure $(F^*\omega _ Y)^ n$ has a smooth bounded potential with minimal singularities, and its current is the smooth semipositive form $F^*\omega _ Y$. Fix a smooth positive local volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. In a chart at a generic point of the ramification divisor, away from its other components, in which $F(z,w)=(z^ k,w)$ with $k\geq 2$,

28.22
\begin{equation} \label{eq:branch-tensor} (F^*\omega _ Y)^ n=|z|^{2(k-1)}G\, \mathrm{d} V, \qquad F^*\omega _ Y\asymp |z|^{2(k-1)}|\mathrm{d} z|^2+|\mathrm{d} w|^2, \end{equation}

where $G$ is smooth and positive and $\asymp $ means that each side is bounded by a constant multiple of the other. The distance for $F^*\omega _ Y$ to the ramification divisor $\{ z=0\} $ is comparable to $|z|^ k$. Thus the metric on the complement of the ramification divisor is incomplete, although the current has no divisorial component. Finally, there is a strict barrier with logarithmic pole along the global ramification divisor.

Proof. By the $\partial \bar\partial $-lemma on the compact Kähler manifold $X$, the de Rham class $F^*[\omega _ Y]$ determines the Bott–Chern class, as in section 23.4. Thus $F^*\omega _ Y=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ for a smooth function $u$ on $X$. Its mass is the full mass of the class, and uniqueness of full-mass solutions in the closed theory, discussed in section 34.19, identifies $u$ with the solution up to a constant. The Jacobian determinant of $(z,w)\mapsto (z^ k,w)$ is $kz^{k-1}$, and comparison of $\omega _ Y$ with the Euclidean form gives (28.22). Hence every path to $\{ z=0\} $ has length bounded below by the variation of a fixed multiple of $|z|^ k$ along it, and a radial path with fixed $w$ attains this order.

The determinant of the differential $\mathrm{d}F$ defines the global ramification divisor $R$. Choose a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(R)$, with curvature form $\Theta _ R$ and canonical section $s_ R$. Since $\theta $ is Kähler, $\theta -\epsilon \Theta _ R>0$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon >0$, so by the Poincaré–Lelong formula $\epsilon \log |s_ R|^2-C$ is the potential of a strict positive $\theta $-current. The barrier is built from the global divisor $R$; no local branch coordinate is treated as a global line bundle. $\square$

Nonclosed backgrounds show the same behavior on manifolds that are not Kähler. On a Hopf manifold, the pullback of the standard invariant metric under a coordinate power map solves an equation in a nonclosed Hermitian background.

Example 28.11.2. Let $n\geq 2$, $q>1$ and let $k\geq 2$ be an integer. Let $H_ q=(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )/(Z\sim qZ)$ be the Hopf manifold, and on the covering space put $R=\sum _ j|Z_ j|^2$, $S=\sum _ j|Z_ j|^{2k}$,

\[ h_0=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\frac{\sum _ j\mathrm{d} Z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar Z_ j}{R}, \quad u_ k=\log S-k\log R, \]
\[ \theta _ k=k\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log R+ \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\partial \log S\wedge \bar\partial \log S. \]

These forms and functions are invariant under $Z\mapsto qZ$, so they descend to $H_ q$; the form $h_0$ descends to every Hopf manifold $H_{q'}$. The coordinate power map $F_ k([Z])=[(Z_1^ k,\ldots ,Z_ n^ k)]$ defines a finite surjective map $H_ q\to H_{q^ k}$: indeed $F_ k(qZ)=q^ kF_ k(Z)$, and the choices of roots are finite modulo scaling in the source. The form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log R$ is positive transverse to the complex Euler direction, while $\partial \log S$ is nonzero on that direction; hence $\theta _ k>0$. Direct differentiation gives

28.23
\begin{equation} \label{eq:hopf-pullback} \theta _ k+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ k=F_ k^*h_0 =\frac{\mathrm{i} k^2}{2\pi S}\sum _ j|Z_ j|^{2k-2}\mathrm{d} Z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar Z_ j. \end{equation}

The background $\theta _ k$ is not closed, since $\mathrm{d}\theta _ k=F_ k^*(\mathrm{d} h_0)$ is not identically zero. Along a coordinate divisor $D=\{ Z_ j=0\} $, at a generic point and in local coordinates $(z,w)$ with $D=\{ z=0\} $, the determinant vanishes to order $2(k-1)$, the metric $F_ k^*h_0$ is comparable to $|z|^{2(k-1)}|\mathrm{d} z|^2+|\mathrm{d} w|^2$, and the distance to $D$ is of order $|z|^ k$, as in proposition 28.11.1.

A small logarithmic potential along $D$ gives a strict barrier. Choose a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(D)$ with curvature form $\Theta _ D$ and canonical section $s_ D$. For small $c>0$, set $h_ c=\theta _ k+c\Theta _ D$ and $U_ c=c\log |s_ D|^2+u_ k$. Then $h_ c>0$ and

\[ h_ c+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ c=c[D]+F_ k^*h_0. \]

By theorem 6.1.2, the extended Monge–Ampère measure of $U_ c$ in the background $h_ c$ is the singular measure $(F_ k^*h_0)^ n$. The ordinary singularity type of $U_ c$ is exactly that of $c\log |s_ D|^2$, which is not minimal, while its metric off $D$ is the same smooth metric $F_ k^*h_0$. Finally, $H_ q\cong S^{2n-1}\times S^1$ has $H^2(H_ q;\mathbb R)=0$. A Kähler form would therefore be exact, and Stokes' theorem would make its positive top-degree integral zero; thus $H_ q$ is not Kähler.

In these constructions, the potential becomes bounded once its divisorial part is removed, and the zero set lies at finite distance. More generally, theorem 1.18.1 bounds the distance by the square root of a Hölder modulus of the potential wherever the current is a smooth positive metric. Continuity alone has no such consequence, as the radial examples of section 28.12 will show.
