14.12 Comparison above a strict singular barrier1FCAFCAE9C0E

From now on, $\theta $ is an arbitrary smooth big real $(1,1)$-form. Then $V_\theta $ may be unbounded, and the smaller-background construction used above is no longer available. Its role is taken by a strict analytic barrier. When two potentials bounded below by the barrier are compared, adding a small multiple of the barrier forces a negative infimum of their difference to be attained away from its pole set, and there a local comparison principle rules it out.

Since $\theta $ is big, theorem 1.15.3 provides $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with analytic singularities and a smooth remainder after resolution such that $\theta _\rho \geq \kappa \omega $ for some $\kappa >0$. Replacing $\omega $ by a small positive multiple and subtracting a constant from $\rho $, we fix such a $\rho $ with $\rho \leq 0$ and $\theta _\rho \geq \omega $. Put $Z=\{ \rho =-\infty \} $ and $\Omega =X\setminus Z$. From now on until the end of section 14.17, these data remain fixed, and $\Omega $ always denotes this open set rather than an auxiliary Hermitian form. A potential bounded below by $\rho -C$ is locally bounded on $\Omega $, and there its extended Monge–Ampère measure agrees with the local bounded product.

Lemma 14.12.1. Let $B\Subset \Omega $ be a coordinate ball, and let $u,v$ be bounded $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions on $B$. Assume

\[ \liminf _{x\to \partial B}(u-v)(x)\geq 0, \qquad \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v). \]

Then $u\geq v$ on $B$.

Proof. Fix $\varepsilon >0$ and set $v_\varepsilon =\max (u,v-\varepsilon )$, which equals $u$ near the boundary. We claim that

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq \mathrm{MA}_\theta (v_\varepsilon ) \]

throughout $B$. On $\{ u>v-\varepsilon \} $ this follows from locality, since $v_\varepsilon =u$ there. On $\{ u\leq v-\varepsilon \} $ it follows from ordered contact and from the assumed inequality, which applies because this set is contained in $\{ u<v\} $.

Let $q$ be the plurisubharmonic envelope on $B$ of the obstacle $u-v_\varepsilon $, in the sense of (1.9). This obstacle is bounded, quasi-continuous and zero near the boundary. Hence it lies between $Ah$ and zero for a sufficiently large $A$, where $h<0$ is a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic defining function for the ball. Thus $q$ tends to zero at the boundary, and (1.9) shows that $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n$ is carried by $D=\{ q=u-v_\varepsilon \} $.

Since $q\leq u-v_\varepsilon $ quasi-everywhere, the canonical representatives satisfy $q+v_\varepsilon \leq u$. Ordered contact, followed by the measure inequality above, yields

\[ \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (q+v_\varepsilon ) \leq \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u) \leq \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v_\varepsilon ). \]

The positive expansion of $(\theta _{v_\varepsilon }+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n$ contains both $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v_\varepsilon )$ and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n$. Thus $\mathbf1_ D(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n=0$, and since $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n$ is carried by $D$, we get $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n=0$. The classical Bedford–Taylor comparison principle for bounded plurisubharmonic functions with zero boundary values implies $q=0$. Consequently $u\geq v_\varepsilon $, first quasi-everywhere and then everywhere. Finally let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. $\square$

Theorem 14.12.2. Suppose $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy, for constants $C_0,C_1\geq 0$,

\[ \min (u,v)\geq \rho -C_0,\qquad v\leq u+C_1. \]

If, for some $0\leq c<1$,

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq c\, \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v), \]

then $u\geq v$.

The measures in the theorem need not have finite total mass.

Proof. We first modify the barrier. Let $r=a\rho -A$, where $a>1$ is sufficiently close to one and $A$ is sufficiently large. Since

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a\rho )=a\theta _\rho +(1-a)\theta , \]

we can arrange $\theta _ r\geq \omega /2$ and $r-v\leq -1$, and moreover $u-r\to +\infty $ at $Z$, because $u-r\geq (a-1)(-\rho )+A-C_0$. When $Z$ is empty, the last property is not needed.

Choose $0<\delta <1$ with $(1-\delta )^ n>c$, and put $v_\delta =(1-\delta )v+\delta r$. Then

\[ u-v_\delta \geq \delta (u-r)-(1-\delta )C_1. \]

Suppose that the infimum $m$ of $u-v_\delta $ on $\Omega $ were negative. Since $u-v_\delta \to +\infty $ at $Z$, a sequence $x_ j$ along which $u-v_\delta $ tends to $m$ has a subsequence converging to a point $x_0\in \Omega $. Choose a coordinate ball $B\Subset \Omega $ centered at $x_0$, identify it with $\{ |z|<1\} $, and take

\[ \eta (z)=b(1-|z|^2),\qquad 0<b<1, \qquad \omega /2+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta \geq 0. \]

The function $w=v_\delta +\delta \eta +m$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic on $B$, and $\liminf _{\partial B}(u-w)\geq 0$ because $\eta $ vanishes on $\partial B$. Also

\[ w-v=\delta (r-v+\eta )+m<0. \]

Thus $\{ u<w\} \subset \{ u<v\} $, and positivity of mixed products gives on this set

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq c\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)\leq (1-\delta )^ n\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)\leq \mathrm{MA}_\theta (w). \]

By lemma 14.12.1, $u\geq w$ on $B$. Evaluating at $x_ j$ gives $u(x_ j)-v_\delta (x_ j)\geq m+\delta \eta (x_ j)$, which contradicts $\eta (x_0)>0$ in the limit. Hence $u\geq v_\delta $ on $\Omega $. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ along values with $(1-\delta )^ n>c$, we obtain $u\geq v$ on $\Omega $, and lemma 1.3.3 extends this inequality to $X$. $\square$

Corollary 14.12.3. Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $u,v\geq \rho -C$ for some $C\geq 0$ and $u\sim v$.

  1. If $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq c\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)$ for some $c\geq 0$, then $c\geq 1$.

  2. If $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda (u-v)}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)$ for some $\lambda >0$, then $u\geq v$.

In particular, let $\mu $ be a positive measure. Among potentials bounded below by $\rho $ minus a constant, each ordinary singularity type contains at most one solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $, and the constant $c>0$ in $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $ is uniquely determined within each type.

Proof. If $c<1$, apply the theorem to $u$ and $v+A$ for every constant $A>0$; the constants in its hypotheses may depend on $A$. The resulting inequality $u\geq v+A$ for all $A$ is impossible. For the second assertion, fix $a>0$ and restrict the measure inequality to $\{ u<v-a\} $. There the factor is at most $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a}<1$, so the theorem gives $u\geq v-a$; now let $a\downarrow 0$. For two solutions of the exponential equation, apply the second assertion in both orders; for two solutions of the normalized equation, apply the first. $\square$
