A continuum of radial solutions

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 11.6.5 A continuum of radial solutions

The open band where the density vanishes allows more solutions than the two smooth ones constructed above. Writing a further potential as $G(r)-\rho $, with $G$ a function of one real variable, reduces the equation on the band to choosing the slope $G'$ so that one of the three coefficients vanishes. Switching the vanishing coefficient at two suitable levels produces a continuum of $C^{1,1}$ solutions for the same background $\omega $ and density $f$.

Recall that $q=p'$ and $z_0\geq |q|$. The strict inequality $z_0>|p'|$ near zero, where $\chi >0$, gives

11.47
\begin{equation} \label{eq:numa-half-excess} \int _0^1z_0(r)\, \mathrm{d}r>\int _0^1|p'(r)|\, \mathrm{d}r=p(0). \end{equation}

Proposition 11.6.2. For the smooth background $\omega $ and the smooth density $f$ of theorem 11.6.1, the equation

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\omega (u)=f\omega _ X^3 \]

has uncountably many distinct $C^{1,1}$ solutions normalized by $\sup _ Xu=0$. If the cutoff $\kappa $ defining $\rho $ is chosen even, one of them, constructed explicitly, is invariant under the block exchange $\iota [A:B]=[B:A]$.

Proof. Consider pairs $-1<I<0<J<1$ satisfying

11.48
\begin{equation} \label{eq:numa-transition-balance} \int _ I^ J z_0(r)\, \mathrm{d}r=p(I)+p(J). \end{equation}

We first show that such pairs exist, with $I$ ranging over an interval. Define

\[ K(I,J)=\int _ I^ Jz_0(r)\, \mathrm{d}r-p(I)-p(J). \]

We have $K(0,0)=-2p(0)<0$ and $K(0,1)>0$ by (11.47). By continuity, there is $\epsilon >0$ such that $K(I,0)<0<K(I,1)$ for every $I\in [-\epsilon ,0]$. For $0<J<1$,

\[ \partial _ JK(I,J)=z_0(J)-p'(J)>0. \]

The intermediate value theorem therefore gives a unique $J=J(I)\in (0,1)$ satisfying (11.48) for every $I\in [-\epsilon ,0)$; we may assume $\epsilon <1$.

For any pair satisfying (11.48), define the slope on $[-1,1]$ by

\[ h_{I,J}(r)= \begin{cases} p(r),& -1\leq r\leq I,\\ p(I)-\displaystyle \int _ I^ r z_0(s)\, \mathrm{d}s,& I\leq r\leq J,\\ -p(r),& J\leq r\leq 1, \end{cases} \]

and extend it by zero outside that interval. The balance identity makes it continuous at $J$, and continuity at $I$ is immediate. Each piece has bounded derivative, so $h_{I,J}$ is Lipschitz on $\mathbb R$; it vanishes to infinite order at $\pm 1$. Let $G_{I,J}$ be a primitive of $h_{I,J}$. It is $C^{1,1}$ and constant near each end of the real line. Consequently $G_{I,J}(r)$ extends to a $C^{1,1}$ function on $X$ that is smooth near $X\setminus U$.

On the middle interval $[I,J]$ the slope lies between $-p$ and $p$. Indeed,

\[ (h_{I,J}-p)'=-z_0-p'\leq 0, \qquad (h_{I,J}+p)'=-z_0+p'\leq 0. \]

The first difference vanishes at $I$, and the second vanishes at $J$. Since both are nonincreasing, $h_{I,J}\leq p$ and $h_{I,J}\geq -p$ on $[I,J]$.

Put $\widetilde u_{I,J}=G_{I,J}(r)-\rho $. By (11.41), almost everywhere on $U$,

11.52
\begin{equation} \label{eq:numa-slope-form} \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde u_{I,J} =\omega _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_{I,J}(r) =(x+h_{I,J})P+(y-h_{I,J})Q+(z+h_{I,J}')R. \end{equation}

On $I<r<J$, the first two coefficients are nonnegative because $x=p+a$, $y=p+b$ and $-p\leq h_{I,J}\leq p$, and the third is zero. On $-1<r<I$, we have $h_{I,J}=p$ and $b=0$, since $I<0$; so the second coefficient is zero, and the others are nonnegative because $z_0+p'\geq 0$. On $J<r<1$, we have $h_{I,J}=-p$ and $a=0$; so the first coefficient is zero, and the others are nonnegative because $z_0-p'\geq 0$. Outside the band, $h_{I,J}=h_{I,J}'=0$, so the form equals $\omega _0$.

Formula (11.52) has no additional term supported on the hypersurfaces $r=I$ and $r=J$: the function $G_{I,J}$ is $C^{1,1}$, so its first derivative is continuous at both transition points, and its distributional Hessian is its bounded almost-everywhere Hessian. The preceding coefficient inequalities therefore prove $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde u_{I,J}\geq 0$ as a current.

By proposition 1.7.4, its Monge–Ampère measure is the pointwise cube of (11.52), computed almost everywhere. By the rank-one identities (11.39) and (11.40), this cube is zero almost everywhere on the band, since one coefficient vanishes there, and it equals $\omega _0^3$ off the band. These are exactly the values of $f\omega _ X^3$ in the preceding construction. Thus $\widetilde u_{I,J}$ solves the equation, and subtracting its supremum gives a normalized solution $u_{I,J}$.

Different values of $I\in [-\epsilon ,0)$ give different normalized solutions. If $I_1<I_2<0$ and $I_1<r<I_2$, then the slope belonging to $I_2$ is $p(r)$, whereas

\[ p(r)-h_{I_1,J(I_1)}(r) =\int _{I_1}^ r(p'(s)+z_0(s))\, \mathrm{d}s>0, \]

because $p'>0$ on $(-1,0)$. The two primitives therefore do not differ by a constant. Since the interval $[-\epsilon ,0)$ is uncountable, this proves the first assertion. None of these slopes equals $p$ or $-p$ on all of $(-1,1)$, so the constructed solutions are also distinct from the two smooth solutions of theorem 11.6.1.

Finally, the function

\[ L(t)=\int _0^ t z_0(r)\, \mathrm{d}r-p(t),\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1, \]

is negative at zero, positive at one, and strictly increasing on $(0,1)$. Its unique zero $t_*$ gives a symmetric pair $I=-t_*$, $J=t_*$ satisfying (11.48). Since $z_0$ and $p$ are even, $h_{I,J}$ is odd: on the middle interval it equals $-\int _0^ r z_0(s)\, \mathrm{d}s$. Choose its primitive even. Since $\iota $ changes $r$ into $-r$, if $\rho $ is even, the potential $G_{I,J}(r)-\rho $ and its normalization are fixed by $\iota $. This proves the last assertion. $\square$

For these symmetric data, the normalized solutions of the equations with a positive exponent converge, as the exponent tends to zero, to the symmetric solution just constructed; see theorem 14.19.3.
