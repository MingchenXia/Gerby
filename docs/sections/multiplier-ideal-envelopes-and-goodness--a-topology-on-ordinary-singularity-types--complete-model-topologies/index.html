18.26 Complete model topologies987BD480177F

987BD480177F

We now compare the complete metrics obtained so far and ask how they might extend to all ordinary models. On a curve, the total variation of polar curvature is a complete metric on ordinary models, ideal projection keeps the atomic part of the polar curvature, and the distance to the closure of the analytic models is exactly the diffuse polar mass, by corollary 13.28.7, proposition 13.28.11. This topology is stronger than $\tau _{\omega ,0}$ on ordinary models: proposition 13.28.19 exhibits a concentration of diffuse polar curvature that is lost in the latter topology.

Adding the smooth curve background as a further coordinate gives the complete space of theorem 13.30.3. Its analytic closure is the locus of ideal models, also over backgrounds of zero area, and metric convergence controls the normalized potentials in $L^1$ and in capacity. This construction keeps the positive-volume topology but imposes a different Cauchy condition at the zero-volume boundary.

For a smooth big background with bounded mass, theorem 13.27.4 gives a complete topology on ordinary models of positive lower volume. Its locus of ideal models is the completion of the analytic models of positive volume, by corollary 13.27.9.

At zero volume, the product constructions of the preceding subsections keep information that top-degree mass cannot detect. A fixed divisor with unit coefficient forces every ordinary representative of the induced models to have zero mass, while polar curvature on the curve factors still distinguishes these models and gives a complete metric whose analytic closure is exactly the locus of ideal models, with ideal projection as the unique nearest-point retraction. These conclusions hold on the product loci of proposition 18.15.8, proposition 18.20.2, including the nonclosed background with unbounded ambient mass in example 18.21.1.

Changing the curve areas rescales the polar distances, and a zero area removes the corresponding coordinate. The comparisons in proposition 18.17.5 quantify this change even when different sets of coordinates survive. If the curve backgrounds converge uniformly to forms of area zero, the diameters tend to zero and the potentials converge in $L^1$, uniformly over the model tuples, and the limit still contains the normalized smooth potentials of the limiting backgrounds, by corollary 18.18.5. These loci thus describe explicit degenerations beyond the positive-volume theory.

A curve quotient can also describe the entire model space. If $p:X\to C$ is a surjective holomorphic map with connected fibers, including critical fibers, and $\beta $ is a smooth real form of nonnegative area on the compact curve $C$, every potential in the background $\theta =p^*\beta $ descends to $C$. For $\dim X\geq 2$ all top-degree masses vanish, but the polar trace, normalized by the constant Gauduchon volume of the fiber divisor with its multiplicities, gives the complete curve metric on all ordinary $\theta $-models by 18.22.3. Its ideal subspace is the analytic closure, and 18.22.4 characterizes equality of the ordinary and ideal envelopes below every smooth obstacle. For an arbitrary surjective map to a compact curve, 5.43.13 supplies the finite factor used in 18.23.1, so disconnected fibers have the same complete model-space description. The polar trace must be kept upstairs: in 18.23.2, two distinct analytic models have the same trace pushforward to the original base curve.

We do not know how to extend the complete topology to general ordinary models while keeping analytic closure and compatibility across backgrounds. Since polar curvature is the coordinate that achieves this on a curve, it is natural to test polar curvature on all modifications in higher dimension. The rest of this subsection shows that this candidate fails in three ways: the first polar current misses isolated poles unless a modification exposes them; normalizing polar traces by Gauduchon forms makes the topology depend on the chosen forms; and diffuse poles of product type are invisible to polar curvature on every modification, although they lower the volume.

18.26.1 Polar curvature in higher dimension83EE9021272B

On a curve, the polar curvature records every logarithmic pole. In higher dimension, the restriction of the curvature current to the pole set no longer does: it vanishes for an isolated logarithmic pole, although such a pole changes the multiplier ideals. The curve metric therefore cannot be extended simply by replacing the polar measure with this restricted current.

Here $X$ has dimension $n\geq 2$, and $\omega $ is a Hermitian form on $X$. For $p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, the first polar current of $p$ is

\[ \operatorname {Pol}_1(p) =\mathbf1_{\{ p=-\infty \} }(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p), \]

where the restriction to the pole set is applied to the coefficient measures of the positive current. In the notation of proposition 5.46.7, $\operatorname {Pol}_1(p)=\operatorname {Pol}_\omega (p)$; it is a closed positive current.

Proposition 18.26.2.088A7D40B1D1 For every $x\in X$, there is $a_0>0$ such that, for each rational $a\in (0,a_0)$, there is an ideal model $p_ a$ with analytic singularities and pole set $\{ x\} $ satisfying

\[ p_ a=a\log |z|^2+O(1)\quad \text{near }x,\qquad \operatorname {Pol}_1(p_ a)=0. \]

The models $p_ a$ are pairwise ordinarily inequivalent and have pairwise distinct families of multiplier ideals $\mathcal I(tp_ a)$, $t>0$, whereas also $\operatorname {Pol}_1(0)=0$. More precisely, with $\mathfrak m_ x$ the maximal ideal at $x$,

55D42653A018 18.26.3
\begin{equation} \mathcal I(tp_ a)_ x =\mathfrak m_ x^{\max \{ \lfloor ta\rfloor -n+1,0\} } \qquad (t>0). \end{equation}

Proof. Choose the cutoff potentials $u_ a$ of lemma 5.37.2 for the single point $x$, with $a_0$ smaller than the reciprocal of the constant in that lemma, and put $p_ a=P_\omega [u_ a]$. By proposition 18.4.8, $p_ a$ is an ideal model with analytic singularities, ordinarily equivalent to $u_ a$. It is locally bounded away from $x$, and its only pole has the stated logarithmic coefficient. Distinct coefficients give unbounded differences near $x$. Formula (18.26.3) follows from lemma 5.37.1 with codimension $n$, and its exponents distinguish any two coefficients at sufficiently large weights.

It remains to show that the current has no coefficient mass at $x$. In coordinates centered at $x$, let $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and let

\[ \nu =(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_ a)\wedge \beta ^{n-1}. \]

This is a positive measure. Choose smooth cutoff functions $0\leq \chi _\varepsilon \leq 1$, equal to one on the ball of radius $\varepsilon $ and supported in the ball of radius $2\varepsilon $, with $-C\varepsilon ^{-2}\beta \leq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _\varepsilon \leq C\varepsilon ^{-2}\beta $; their complex Hessians are supported in the annulus between the two balls. Since $\beta $ is closed, distributional integration by parts gives

\[ \int \chi _\varepsilon \, \mathrm d\nu =\int \chi _\varepsilon \omega \wedge \beta ^{n-1} +\int p_ a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _\varepsilon \wedge \beta ^{n-1}. \]

The logarithmic bound for $p_ a$ on the annulus and the Euclidean volume of the annulus therefore give

\[ 0\leq \nu (\{ x\} )\leq \int \chi _\varepsilon \, \mathrm d\nu \leq C_ a\bigl (\varepsilon ^{2n} +\varepsilon ^{2n-2}(1+|\log \varepsilon |)\bigr ) \longrightarrow 0. \]

This is where $n\geq 2$ is used. By positivity, the coefficient measures of $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_ a$ are bounded by its trace measure $\nu $, so they have no mass at $x$. Hence the restriction to the pole set is zero, as asserted. The zero potential is an ordinary and an ideal model because $\omega >0$, and its pole set is empty. $\square$

Consequently, no comparison that depends only on the fixed background and on $\operatorname {Pol}_1$ separates these analytic ideal models, and a complete topology in higher dimension must involve singularity data of higher codimension.

A modification can turn the isolated pole into a divisor. For a modification $\pi :Y\to X$, pullback maps $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ to $\mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\omega )$ by proposition 5.43.18, and we define

\[ \operatorname {Pol}_\pi (p) =\mathbf1_{\{ p\circ \pi =-\infty \} } (\pi ^*\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p\circ \pi )). \]

Corollary 18.26.4.91E1AFBF43BE Fix $x$ and the models $p_ a$ of proposition 18.26.2. Let $\pi :Y\to X$ be the blowup at $x$, with exceptional divisor $E$, and let $\eta $ be a Hermitian form on $Y$. Then

\[ \operatorname {Pol}_\pi (p_ a)=a[E]. \]

For $\mu _ a=\operatorname {Pol}_\pi (p_ a)\wedge \eta ^{n-1}$ and any two admissible coefficients $a,b$,

\[ \| \mu _ a-\mu _ b\| _{\mathrm{TV}} =|a-b|\int _ E\eta ^{n-1}. \]

Proof. On relatively compact blowup charts, with $z=(s,sw)$, one has $p_ a\circ \pi =a\log |s|^2+O(1)$ along $E=\{ s=0\} $. Thus the pole set is $E$ and the generic Lelong number along it is $a$. Add a local smooth function $\rho $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \pi ^*\omega $. By the argument of lemma 5.38.1, the restriction of the closed positive current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p_ a\circ \pi +\rho )$ to $E$ is $a[E]$, and subtracting the smooth correction does not change the coefficient mass on $E$. Consequently $\mu _ a=a[E]\wedge \eta ^{n-1}$. The positive measure $[E]\wedge \eta ^{n-1}$ has mass $\int _ E\eta ^{n-1}>0$, which proves the formula for the total variation. $\square$

A fixed modification detects isolated poles only over the set where it is not an isomorphism, so countably many modifications still miss the poles at all points outside a countable union of nowhere dense analytic sets.

Corollary 18.26.5.D9BE589BBC6E For every countable family of modifications $\pi _ j:Y_ j\to X$ between connected compact complex manifolds, there are pairwise ordinarily inequivalent ideal $\omega $-models $p_ a$ with analytic singularities and with pairwise distinct families of multiplier ideals, such that

\[ \operatorname {Pol}_{\pi _ j}(p_ a)=0 \qquad \text{for every }j\text{ and every }a. \]

Proof. For each $j$, choose a proper closed analytic subset $S_ j\subset X$ over whose complement $\pi _ j$ is an isomorphism. Each $S_ j$ is nowhere dense, so the Baire category theorem gives a point $x\notin \bigcup _ jS_ j$. Apply proposition 18.26.2 at this point.

Each $\pi _ j$ is biholomorphic near the unique pole $y_ j$ of $p_ a\circ \pi _ j$, and the pulled-back potential has the same logarithmic coefficient there. Its smooth background and positive curvature current satisfy the trace estimate in the proof of proposition 18.26.2, which does not use strict positivity of the background. Thus the coefficient measures have no mass at $y_ j$, and their restriction to the pole set is zero. The same proposition gives the ordinary inequivalence and the distinct multiplier ideals. $\square$

18.26.6 Gauduchon polar trace comparisons6C8B6C6B86C3

To use polar currents on all modifications at once, their sizes must be normalized compatibly. Pairing with Gauduchon forms does this: for a Gauduchon form $\eta $ on a modification, the total $\eta $-trace of the curvature current does not depend on the potential. Taking the supremum, over a family of modifications, of the normalized total variations of the polar traces gives a comparison that separates analytic models without any bound on top-degree mass. The space of all possible values of these coordinates is complete; whether its limit points are realized by ordinary models is a separate question.

Here $X$ again has arbitrary dimension $n\geq 1$, and $\omega $ is a Hermitian form on $X$. Fix a set $\mathscr R$ of smooth modifications $\pi :Y_\pi \to X$ that contains the identity and, for any two analytic singularity types, a common log resolution. On each $Y_\pi $, choose a Gauduchon form $\eta _\pi $, that is, a Hermitian form with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\eta _\pi ^{n-1})=0$ as in definition 11.4.1. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, set

\[ \mu _\pi (u)=\mathbf1_{\{ u\circ \pi =-\infty \} } \bigl (\pi ^*\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u\circ \pi )\bigr ) \wedge \eta _\pi ^{n-1}, \]

where the restriction is applied to the coefficient measures of the current, and define

B3860123F073 18.26.7
\begin{equation} \begin{gathered} A_\pi =\int _{Y_\pi }\pi ^*\omega \wedge \eta _\pi ^{n-1},\\ \delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(u,v) =\sup _{\pi \in \mathscr R} \frac{\| \mu _\pi (u)-\mu _\pi (v)\| _{\mathrm{TV}}}{A_\pi }. \end{gathered} \end{equation}

The Gauduchon forms are part of the data. Multiplying one of them by a positive constant does not change the corresponding term in the supremum.

Proposition 18.26.8.CB31D6224569 Every $A_\pi $ is positive. The comparison (18.26.7) is a pseudometric on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, bounded by two, and its restriction to normalized ordinary models with analytic singularities is a metric. If $X$ is a curve, then for all ordinary models $p,q$,

\[ \delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,q) =\frac{\| \mu _{\operatorname {id}_ X}(p)-\mu _{\operatorname {id}_ X}(q)\| _{\mathrm{TV}}}{\int _ X\omega }, \]

so $\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}$ induces the complete topology of the polar metric on curve models, in which the closure of the analytic models is exactly the set of ideal models.

Let $f$ be a smooth real function and $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$. If $\delta _{\omega '}^{\mathrm{pol}}$ is defined by the same formula with the same Gauduchon forms, then its denominators are positive and

\[ \delta _{\omega '}^{\mathrm{pol}}(u-f,v-f) =\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(u,v) \qquad (u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )). \]

Proof. The form $\pi ^*\omega $ is semipositive, and it is positive on the nonempty open set where $\pi $ is an isomorphism, so $A_\pi >0$. By proposition 5.43.18, $u\circ \pi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y_\pi ,\pi ^*\omega )$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\eta _\pi ^{n-1})=0$, distributional Stokes gives

\[ \int _{Y_\pi }\bigl (\pi ^*\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u\circ \pi )\bigr ) \wedge \eta _\pi ^{n-1}=A_\pi . \]

Thus $\mu _\pi (u)$ is a positive measure of mass at most $A_\pi $, and the normalized total variation distance of two such measures is at most two. Symmetry and the triangle inequality persist under the supremum, which proves the pseudometric assertion.

Let $p,q$ be ordinary models with analytic singularities, choose a common log resolution $\pi $ in $\mathscr R$, and suppose that $\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,q)=0$. Then their measures in this coordinate agree. Let $E_ i$ be the prime components of the resolved ideals on $Y_\pi $, and $a_ i,b_ i$ the coefficients of $p$ and $q$ along them. The divisorial restriction argument of lemma 5.38.1 gives

\[ \mu _\pi (p)=\sum _ i a_ i[E_ i]\wedge \eta _\pi ^{n-1},\qquad \mu _\pi (q)=\sum _ i b_ i[E_ i]\wedge \eta _\pi ^{n-1}. \]

Indeed, a smooth local correction makes the complex Hessian closed and positive without changing its coefficient mass on a divisor. Distinct components do not share trace mass on their intersections, and each component has strictly positive trace mass. Equality of these measures therefore implies $a_ i=b_ i$ for every $i$. The criterion of lemma 5.38.11, which uses only the finitely many divisors $E_ i$, applied in both directions gives $p\sim q$. An ordinary model is the ordinary envelope of every potential of its type, so ordinary models with the same type are equal. This proves separation.

On a curve, every smooth modification is an isomorphism and $\eta _\pi ^{n-1}=1$. Hence $A_\pi =\int _ X\omega $, and pullback preserves the total variation of polar curvature. The formula on curves and the assertions following it are therefore consequences of corollary 13.28.7, proposition 13.28.11.

Finally, $u-f$ has, in background $\omega '$, the same pulled-back curvature current and the same pole set as $u$ in background $\omega $, and Stokes' theorem gives $A_{\pi ,\omega '}=A_{\pi ,\omega }$. The gauge formula follows. It holds for the potentials themselves, before any ordinary projection. $\square$

The coordinates $\mu _\pi (u)$ take values in a complete space. For each $\pi $, let $\mathscr B_\pi $ be the set of positive finite Borel measures on $Y_\pi $ of mass at most $A_\pi $.

Proposition 18.26.9.B48EB7517450 The space $\prod _{\pi \in \mathscr R}\mathscr B_\pi $ is complete for the metric

\[ \widehat\delta ((\lambda _\pi ),(\nu _\pi )) =\sup _{\pi \in \mathscr R} \frac{\| \lambda _\pi -\nu _\pi \| _{\mathrm{TV}}}{A_\pi }. \]

The map $u\mapsto (\mu _\pi (u))_\pi $ takes values in this space and preserves the comparison $\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}$.

Proof. The metric is finite, bounded by two, and separates points. A Cauchy sequence is Cauchy in total variation in each coordinate, and completeness of the space of finite signed measures gives a limit there; positivity and the mass bound pass to the limit. Given $\varepsilon >0$, choose $N$ such that every normalized coordinate distance is at most $\varepsilon $ for all $j,k\geq N$. Letting $k$ tend to infinity separately in each coordinate preserves this inequality, and taking the supremum proves convergence for $\widehat\delta $. The last assertion follows from the definition and the mass bound proved above. $\square$

By proposition 5.46.3, the coordinates $\mu _\pi $ are invariant under bounded changes of the potential. This allows the gauge identity of proposition 18.26.8, which concerns the potentials themselves, to be transferred to normalized ordinary models.

Corollary 18.26.10.EA11DD744637 The comparison $\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}$ descends to ordinary classes. For a smooth real function $f$ and $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$, the map

\[ G_ f(p)=P_{\omega '}[p-f] \]

is a bijection between the normalized ordinary models in the two backgrounds, with inverse $q\mapsto P_\omega [q+f]$. If the same Gauduchon forms are used in both backgrounds, $G_ f$ preserves $\delta ^{\mathrm{pol}}$, analytic models and ideal models.

Proof. Boundedly equivalent potentials have boundedly equivalent pullbacks on every modification, with the same pole set. Proposition 5.46.3 therefore gives equality of their polar currents and of their measures $\mu _\pi $ in every coordinate, which proves descent to ordinary classes.

If $p=P_\omega [p]$, the version of proposition 18.5.1 for ordinary envelopes gives

\[ p+\min _ Xf\leq G_ f(p)+f\leq p+\max _ Xf. \]

Thus $G_ f(p)\sim p-f$. Since ordinary envelopes depend only on the ordinary type, $P_{\omega '}[G_ f(p)]=P_{\omega '}[p-f]=G_ f(p)$, and similarly $P_\omega [G_ f(p)+f]=P_\omega [p]=p$. The same argument applied to models in background $\omega '$ proves surjectivity and the formula for the inverse. Invariance of the polar coordinates and the gauge identity of proposition 18.26.8 now give

\[ \delta _{\omega '}^{\mathrm{pol}}(G_ f(p),G_ f(q)) =\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,q). \]

Subtraction of a smooth function and bounded equivalence preserve analytic singularities. If $p=P_\omega [p]_{\mathcal I}$, the gauge estimate for ideal envelopes gives $P_{\omega '}[p-f]_{\mathcal I}\sim p-f\sim G_ f(p)$. By corollary 18.4.4, this ideal envelope is an ordinary model, and two ordinary models of the same ordinary type coincide. Hence it equals $G_ f(p)$, which shows that $G_ f$ maps ideal models to ideal models; the inverse map gives the converse. $\square$

The traces $\mu _\pi $ determine the whole polar currents, because these currents are closed by the local cutoff argument of proposition 5.46.7, which also applies when the pole set is not closed.

Corollary 18.26.11.179E3F0D00D8 Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. For every $\pi \in \mathscr R$, the polar curvature currents of $u\circ \pi $ and $v\circ \pi $, defined by coefficientwise restriction, are closed, and they agree if and only if $\mu _\pi (u)=\mu _\pi (v)$. Consequently, $\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(u,v)=0$ exactly when all these polar currents agree.

Proof. By proposition 5.43.18, the pullbacks belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(Y_\pi ,\pi ^*\omega )$, and proposition 5.46.7, applied with background $\pi ^*\omega $, shows that their polar currents are closed and positive. Both currents are carried by the union of the two Borel pole sets, which has Lebesgue measure zero. Equality of their $\eta _\pi $-traces therefore gives equality of the currents by lemma 5.46.4, and the converse is immediate. Since every $A_\pi $ is positive, the last assertion follows. $\square$

For a sequence that is Cauchy for $\delta ^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega $, total-variation convergence of the traces keeps the limiting polar mass on the union of the pole sets of the sequence, and the recovery from traces then gives compatible limits of the full polar currents.

Proposition 18.26.12.7C9BBE3DA7BE Let $(p_ j)$ be normalized ordinary $\omega $-models forming a Cauchy sequence for $\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}$. For every $\pi \in \mathscr R$, their polar currents on $Y_\pi $ converge weakly to a unique closed positive current $R_\pi $, with

\[ R_\pi \wedge \eta _\pi ^{n-1} =\lim _{j\to \infty }\mu _\pi (p_ j) \quad \text{in total variation}. \]

If $g:Y_{\pi '}\to Y_\pi $ is a modification with $\pi '=\pi \circ g$ and $\pi ,\pi '\in \mathscr R$, then $g_*R_{\pi '}=R_\pi $. There is $h\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ such that every $R_\pi $ is carried coefficientwise by $\{ h\circ \pi =-\infty \} $.

Proof. Fix $\pi $. By proposition 18.26.9, the measures $\mu _\pi (p_ j)$ converge in total variation to a positive measure $\lambda _\pi $. Put

\[ B_\pi =\bigcup _{j\geq 1}\{ p_ j\circ \pi =-\infty \} . \]

This is a Borel set of Lebesgue measure zero. Every measure $\mu _\pi (p_ j)$ vanishes on its complement, so $\lambda _\pi (Y_\pi \setminus B_\pi )=0$. The polar currents are closed and positive by corollary 18.26.11, and their $\eta _\pi $-trace masses are at most $A_\pi $. By compactness of positive currents of bounded mass, a subsequence converges weakly. Its limit $R$ is closed and positive, and $R\wedge \eta _\pi ^{n-1}=\lambda _\pi $. Since positivity bounds all coefficient measures by the Hermitian trace, $R$ is carried by $B_\pi $. Any two subsequential limits have the same null carrier and the same trace, so they agree by lemma 5.46.4. As every subsequence has a further convergent subsequence, the whole sequence of currents converges weakly to this unique limit $R_\pi $.

Apply proposition 5.46.5 to $g$, with background $\pi ^*\omega $ and potential $p_ j\circ \pi $. It gives $g_*\operatorname {Pol}_{\pi '}(p_ j)=\operatorname {Pol}_\pi (p_ j)$. Proper pushforward is weakly continuous, since a smooth test form downstairs pulls back to a smooth test form upstairs, and passing to the limit proves compatibility. The limits are constructed separately in each coordinate, so no diagonal subsequence over $\mathscr R$ is needed.

To obtain one pole carrier, put $h_ m=\sum _{j=1}^ m2^{-j}p_ j$. These functions decrease, and they are $\omega $-plurisubharmonic because the coefficients have sum at most one and $\omega >0$. Normalized compactness, theorem 5.25.2, bounds their $L^1$ norms uniformly, so corollary 5.25.3 gives a limit $h\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. Since all summands are nonpositive, each pole set of a $p_ j$ is contained in the pole set of $h$. Hence $B_\pi \subset \{ h\circ \pi =-\infty \} $, which gives the last assertion. $\square$

The common pole carrier does not identify $R_\pi $ with the polar current of $h$. We do not know whether all the limits $R_\pi $ are the polar currents of a single ordinary model.

The topology defined by $\delta ^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega $ also depends on the choice of the Gauduchon forms. On a surface, a Gauduchon form close to the pullback of a Kähler form assigns little trace mass to exceptional curves, so choosing the forms independently on the different modifications can make a sequence of moving point poles of fixed strength converge to zero. We compare $\delta ^{\mathrm{pol}}_\omega $ with the upper-volume comparison $d_\omega $ and with the sets $\mathcal M_\delta (\omega )$ of ordinary models of lower volume at least $\delta $, both introduced in definition 13.22.1.

Proposition 18.26.13.6457B1027E36 Let $X=\mathbb P^2$ and let $\omega $ be the Fubini–Study form normalized by $\int _ X\omega ^2=1$. Every model in the family is not an ideal model, all its multiplier ideals are trivial, and its first polar currents vanish on every smooth modification. For $\alpha ^ n<P<\beta ^ n$, the subfamily defined by $\prod _ j a_ j=P$ is complete and homeomorphic to a convex polytope of real dimension $n-1$. Throughout this subfamily, the residual measure on $S$ is $Pt^ n\rho $ and both singularity volumes are $A-Pt^ n$. Hence these data, even together with all multiplier ideals and the first polar currents on all modifications, do not separate ordinary models.

Proof. Place the polydisk $D$ of lemma 18.26.16 in a coordinate chart, and use one cutoff function $0\leq \chi \leq 1$, compactly supported there and equal to one near $S=K^ n$. For $a\in B$, extend $H_ a=\chi h_ a$ by zero and put $u_ a=tH_ a$. We first prove a curvature bound that is uniform in $a$.

Choose a compact neighborhood $\Sigma $ of the supports of the derivatives of $\chi $, disjoint from $S$. The isotropic maximum $\max _ j g(z_ j)$ is bounded below on $\Sigma $ by $-B_0$, for some $B_0>0$. Since $g\leq 0$ and $a_ j\leq \beta $, it follows that $-\beta B_0\leq h_ a\leq 0$ there. If the $j$th branch $a_ jg(z_ j)$ attains the maximum, then $a_ jg(z_ j)\geq -\beta B_0$, so $g(z_ j)\geq -\beta B_0/\alpha $. Within the fixed compact range of the coordinates, this superlevel set stays away from $K$, and $g$ and its gradient are bounded on a neighborhood of it. Thus all maximizing branches have uniformly bounded gradients, and the maxima $h_ a$ satisfy one local Lipschitz bound on $\Sigma $, independent of $a\in B$.

In the distributional product rule for $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi h_ a)$, the term $\chi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ a$ is positive. The remaining terms contain derivatives of $\chi $ together with $h_ a$ or its first derivatives, so the preceding bounds control their coefficients uniformly. The cutoff argument of proposition 18.26.19 therefore gives one $C>0$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ a\geq -C\omega $ for every $a\in B$. Choose $t_0<1/C$ with $t_0^ n\beta ^ n<A-\delta $. Then all $u_ a$, $0<t\leq t_0$, are normalized and satisfy $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ a\geq (1-Ct)\omega $.

By theorem 11.1.7, all bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions have mass $A$, so $v_-(\omega )=v_+(\omega )=A$. For any $v\sim u_ a$, the global limit of the measures of the truncations has total mass $A$, as in the proof of corollary 18.26.20. Near $S$, the difference between $v$ plus a smooth local correction and $th_ a$ is bounded. By lemma 5.27.19, lemma 18.26.16, the residual measure of $v$ is

\[ t^ n\left(\prod _ j a_ j\right)\rho ,\qquad \rho =\sigma ^{\otimes n}, \]

extended by zero from the chart. Every corrected closed power of lower degree gives $S$ zero coefficient mass, so this is also the restriction to $S$ of the global limit of the truncations. Off $S$ that limit equals the non-pluripolar measure, which proves that every such representative has mass

\[ V(u_ a)=A-t^ n\prod _ j a_ j>\delta . \]

Hence, by theorem 13.19.5, ordinary projection preserves both singularity volumes, and by theorem 13.19.6 it preserves the residual measure. Setting $p_ a=P_\omega [u_ a]$ proves the formulas for the residual measure and the volumes simultaneously for all $a\in B$.

For $a,b\in B$ and $c_ j=\min (a_ j,b_ j)$, positivity of the weights and $g\leq 0$ give, pointwise and also at the poles,

\[ \max (h_ a,h_ b)=h_ c,\qquad \max (u_ a,u_ b)=u_ c. \]

The invariance of the comparison under ordinary projection in proposition 13.22.4 and the definition of the comparison give

\[ \begin{split} d_\omega (p_ a,p_ b)& =d_\omega (u_ a,u_ b)\\ & =t^ n\left(\prod _ j a_ j+\prod _ j b_ j-2\prod _ j c_ j\right). \end{split} \]

For the boxes $R_ a=\prod _ j[0,a_ j]\subset \mathbb R^ n$ and Lebesgue measure $\lambda $, the last expression is $t^ n\lambda (R_ a\mathbin {\triangle }R_ b)$, since $R_ a\cap R_ b=R_ c$; equivalently, it is $t^ n\| \mathbf1_{R_ a}-\mathbf1_{R_ b}\| _{L^1(\lambda )}$. This proves the triangle inequality within the family. A slab in the coordinate where $|a_ j-b_ j|$ is largest, with every other coordinate in $[0,\alpha ]$, lies in the symmetric difference up to its boundary, and its volume gives the lower bound. The symmetric difference is covered by the $n$ coordinate slabs between $a_ j$ and $b_ j$, with every other coordinate in $[0,\beta ]$, and their total volume gives the upper bound. In particular, the distance separates the parameters, and the two estimates show that it induces the Euclidean topology of $B$. A sequence that is Cauchy for the distance has Cauchy weights in the Euclidean sense, whose limit lies in $B$, and the upper bound gives convergence for the distance. Compactness follows from the same bounds.

The support and Lelong-number argument in the proof of proposition 18.26.17 applies for every positive weight vector. Every exceptional divisor mapping into $S$ maps to a point, and comparison with $ta_1g(z_1\circ \pi )$ shows that the generic Lelong number along it is zero. Thus every first polar current is zero. The same comparison gives zero Lelong numbers at all points and trivial multiplier ideals at all weights. The strict volume deficit $t^ n\prod _ j a_ j$ shows that $p_ a\ne 0$, whereas the ideal envelope of $p_ a$ is zero, so $p_ a$ is not an ideal model.

Finally, the constraint $\prod _ ja_ j=P$ defines a closed subset of $B$, so the subfamily is complete. Taking logarithms coordinatewise identifies it with

\[ [\log \alpha ,\log \beta ]^ n \cap \left\{ x\in \mathbb R^ n:\sum _ jx_ j=\log P\right\} . \]

For $\alpha ^ n<P<\beta ^ n$, this convex polytope meets the interior of the cube at the point with $x_ j=(\log P)/n$ for all $j$, and it has dimension $n-1$. The formulas for the volume and the residual measure are constant on it, while the distance separates its members. For example, taking $\alpha =1/2$, $\beta =2$, $P=1$, $a=(2,1/2,1,\ldots ,1)$ and $b=(1,1,1,\ldots ,1)$ gives two models with common residual measure $t^ n\rho $, both volumes equal to $A-t^ n$, and distance $t^ n$. $\square$

Finally, even the full residual measure on the pole set can miss anisotropy. In proposition 18.26.26, distinct ordinary models of positive volume have the same residual measure, the same singularity volumes, the same multiplier ideals and the same first polar currents on every modification, while their volume comparison is positive. A general extension must therefore record more than these data.
