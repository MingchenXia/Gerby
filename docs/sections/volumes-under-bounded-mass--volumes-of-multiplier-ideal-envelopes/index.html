13.24 Volumes of multiplier-ideal envelopes146F3DD22A85

146F3DD22A85

Analytic regularizations recover the multiplier-ideal envelope, whose volumes may exceed those of the original potential. Continuity along decreasing models computes this excess, and a strict analytic potential absorbs the curvature error of the regularization, so that the comparison can be carried out entirely in the fixed background. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, write

\[ \mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}),\qquad \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}) \]

for the two volumes of its ideal envelope. They depend only on the multiplier-ideal class of $u$. By proposition 18.5.1 and the gauge invariance of singularity volumes, they are also unchanged under a smooth change of decomposition $(\theta ,u)\mapsto (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f,u-f)$, $f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. Moreover, proposition 18.4.4, theorem 13.17.1 give

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u) \leq \mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u),\qquad \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u) \leq \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u). \]

The ideal envelope is an ordinary model by corollary 18.4.4, and its volumes are limits of the volumes of the dyadic analytic approximations.

Corollary 13.24.1.C72B68A062F4 Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, fix a Hermitian form $h$, and let $a_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$, $\theta _ j=\theta +\epsilon _ jh$, be the dyadic analytic regularizations of $u$ of analytic type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^ ju))$, with errors $\epsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, as in (18.7.5). Then

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(a_ j) \searrow \mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u). \]

If $\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u)>0$, then also

\[ \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(a_ j) \searrow \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u). \]

Proof. By theorem 18.7.6, the analytic ordinary models $q_ j=P_{\theta _ j}[a_ j]$ decrease to $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$, and the backgrounds $\theta _ j$ decrease to $\theta $. Thus theorem 13.21.2 applies to the $q_ j$. Since $q_ j\sim a_ j$ by proposition 18.4.8, the volumes of $q_ j$ and $a_ j$ agree. $\square$

When the ideal envelope has positive lower volume, the relative full-mass class detects whether $u$ and its ideal envelope have the same upper volume. Here $P_{\theta ,F}[u]$ and $P_{\theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$ denote the ordinary and the multiplier-ideal envelopes with the constraint $h\leq F$ in place of $h\leq 0$ (definition 5.40.4 and section 18.8).

Proposition 13.24.2.71B65CF5AD10 Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, put $q=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$, and suppose that $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0$. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$;

  2. $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)=\mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u)$;

  3. $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,q)$;

  4. $P_{\theta ,F}[u]=P_{\theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$ for every $F\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$.

If they hold, then also $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)=\mathcal V^-_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u)$.

Proof. Recall that $q$ is an ordinary model and $u\preceq P_\theta [u]\leq q$. If (i) holds, then $P_\theta [u]=q$ has positive lower volume, and theorem 13.19.5 shows that both volumes of $u$ equal those of $q$; in particular (ii) holds, together with the final assertion.

Conversely, (ii) and $u\preceq q$ give $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta ,q)$, and corollary 13.19.8 gives $P_\theta [u]=q$, which is (i). The same corollary shows that (i) is equivalent to (iii). By proposition 18.12.2, (iii) implies (iv), and (iv) with $F=0$ is (i). $\square$

Under the hypotheses of the proposition, corollary 18.7.7 also identifies equality of the upper volumes with convergence of the dyadic ordinary envelopes $P_{\theta _ j}[a_ j]$ to $P_\theta [u]$. These envelopes live in the larger backgrounds $\theta _ j$. The next construction mixes the $a_ j$ with a strict analytic potential, which gives analytic approximants in the fixed background $\theta $ with the same limiting upper volume; their singularity types, however, need no longer be ordered.

Theorem 13.24.3.E9FC137BBE4E Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, put $q=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$, and suppose $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0$. There are analytic $b_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $b_ j\to u$ in $L^1$ such that the ordinary models $r_ j=P_\theta [b_ j]$ satisfy

34D208A53BBD 13.24.4
\begin{equation} d_\theta (r_ j,q)\longrightarrow 0,\qquad d_\theta (r_ j,u)\longrightarrow \mathcal V^+_{\mathcal I,\theta }(u) -\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u). \end{equation}

Their lower volumes converge to $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)$, and $r_ j\to q$ in $L^1$ and capacity. In particular $d_\theta (r_ j,u)\to 0$ if and only if $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$.

Proof. Step 1: the approximants. Fix a Hermitian form $h$. Bigness and theorem 5.36.1 give an analytic $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\rho \leq 0$ and a rational $\kappa >0$ such that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \kappa h$. Choose the dyadic regularizations $a_ j$ as in (18.7.5), enlarging their errors to positive rational numbers $\epsilon _ j\downarrow 0$. Put

\[ c_ j=\frac{\kappa }{\kappa +\epsilon _ j},\qquad b_ j=c_ ja_ j+(1-c_ j)\rho . \]

Then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ j\geq ((1-c_ j)\kappa -c_ j\epsilon _ j)h=0$. The coefficients are rational, so $b_ j$ has analytic singularities by proposition 5.34.1. Since $a_ j\to u$ in $L^1$ and $c_ j\to 1$, also $b_ j\to u$ in $L^1$.

Step 2: two-sided bounds. Choose $A>0$ with $\theta \leq Ah$, and set

\[ \widehat\theta _ j=\theta +(1+A/\kappa )\epsilon _ jh, \qquad t_ j=P_{\widehat\theta _ j}[a_ j]. \]

These backgrounds decrease to $\theta $ and dominate $\theta +\epsilon _ jh$, so the $a_ j$ also satisfy (18.7.5) with the larger errors $(1+A/\kappa )\epsilon _ j$. Theorem 18.7.6 therefore gives $t_ j\searrow q$. Moreover

\[ c_ j\widehat\theta _ j-\theta =(1-c_ j)(Ah-\theta )+c_ j\epsilon _ jh\geq 0. \]

By proposition 18.4.8, $r_ j\sim b_ j$. Hence $r_ j/c_ j$ is a nonpositive $\widehat\theta _ j$-plurisubharmonic function that is more singular than $a_ j$, because $b_ j\leq c_ ja_ j$; it is therefore a competitor for $t_ j$, and $r_ j\leq c_ jt_ j$. Also $q\leq t_ j\leq c_ jt_ j$.

For a lower bound, recall that $q\sim _{\mathcal I}u$, and $u\preceq _{\mathcal I}a_ j$ because $a_ j\geq u$. Addition and scaling preserve this order by theorem 5.37.3, lemma 5.37.4. Thus $c_ jq+(1-c_ j)\rho \preceq _{\mathcal I}b_ j$. This potential lies below zero, so envelope order and proposition 18.4.8 give

\[ c_ jq+(1-c_ j)\rho \leq P_\theta [b_ j]_{\mathcal I}=r_ j \leq c_ jt_ j. \]

Step 3: volumes. For either singularity volume, homogeneity, addition and monotonicity now yield

\[ c_ j^ n\operatorname {vol}_\theta (q) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\theta (r_ j) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\theta (\max (r_ j,q)) \leq c_ j^ n\operatorname {vol}_{\widehat\theta _ j}(t_ j). \]

The last comparison uses both $\theta \leq c_ j\widehat\theta _ j$ and $\max (r_ j,q)\leq c_ jt_ j$. By theorem 13.21.2, the outer terms tend to $\operatorname {vol}_\theta (q)$. This proves the convergence of both volumes of $r_ j$ and the first limit in (13.24.4). In particular, after discarding finitely many indices, the ordinary models $r_ j$ and $q$ have a common positive lower-volume bound, so $r_ j\to q$ in $L^1$ and in capacity by proposition 13.22.4, theorem 13.23.3.

For the second limit, translate $u$ so that $u\leq q$; this does not change $d_\theta (r_ j,u)$. Then

\[ \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (r_ j) \leq \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\max (r_ j,u)) \leq \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\max (r_ j,q)). \]

Both bounds tend to $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)$, so $d_\theta (r_ j,u)=2U(\max (r_ j,u))-U(r_ j)-U(u)$ tends to $U(q)-U(u)$, as claimed. The last equivalence follows from proposition 13.24.2. $\square$

For a Hermitian background, the simpler rescaling in corollary 18.7.9 gives decreasing analytic models, with the same limits by theorem 13.21.2. In a general big background, the strict correction above may introduce additional poles, but the volume comparison still measures exactly the excess of the ideal envelope.

Theorem 13.24.5.8BB867A0263F Let $p$ be an ordinary model of positive lower volume. Then $p=P_\theta [p]_{\mathcal I}$ if and only if there are analytic ordinary models $p_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $d_\theta (p_ j,p)\to 0$.

More generally, if $p_ j=P_\theta [p_ j]_{\mathcal I}$ and $d_\theta (p_ j,p)\to 0$, then $p=P_\theta [p]_{\mathcal I}$.

Proof. If $p=P_\theta [p]_{\mathcal I}$, apply theorem 13.24.3 with $u=p$. For the converse it suffices to prove the more general assertion, because analytic ordinary models are ideal models by proposition 18.4.8.

Put $q=P_\theta [p]_{\mathcal I}$ and $\delta =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)>0$. Choose $s_ j\in (0,1)$ tending to zero with $d_\theta (p_ j,p)<\delta s_ j^ n$, after discarding finitely many indices. For $m_ j=\max (p_ j,p)$ one has

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (m_ j)\geq \delta , \qquad U(m_ j)-U(p_ j)\leq d_\theta (p_ j,p). \]

By lemma 13.18.1 with $b=s_ j^{-1}$, there is $h_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $(1-s_ j)m_ j+s_ jh_ j\leq p_ j$. Set $H_ j=P_\theta [h_ j]_{\mathcal I}$. Since $m_ j\geq p$, monotonicity, addition and scaling of ideal envelopes (proposition 18.4.6) give

\[ (1-s_ j)q+s_ jH_ j \leq P_\theta [(1-s_ j)m_ j+s_ jh_ j]_{\mathcal I} \leq P_\theta [p_ j]_{\mathcal I}=p_ j. \]

Consequently $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p_ j)\geq (1-s_ j)^ n\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)\geq (1-s_ j)^ n\delta $ by addition and volume monotonicity, since $q\geq p$. The models $p_ j,p$ therefore lie in $\mathcal M_{\delta /2}(\theta )$ for large $j$, so proposition 13.22.4 gives $p_ j\to p$ in $L^1$.

The $H_ j$ have supremum zero, so normalized compactness bounds $\int _ X(-H_ j)\, \mathrm{d}V$ by a constant $C_\theta $. Since $q\leq 0$, the convex inequality above gives $q-p_ j\leq s_ j(q-H_ j)\leq -s_ jH_ j$, and hence

\[ \int _ X(q-p_ j)_+\, \mathrm{d}V \leq s_ j\int _ X(-H_ j)\, \mathrm{d}V \leq C_\theta s_ j\longrightarrow 0. \]

Passing to the $L^1$ limit gives $q\leq p$ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere by lemma 5.24.3. The opposite inequality $p=P_\theta [p]\leq P_\theta [p]_{\mathcal I}=q$ holds by proposition 18.4.4, so $p=q$. $\square$

Thus the ideal models of positive lower volume are exactly the $d_\theta $-limits of analytic ordinary models. On each $\mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$ they form a closed set for this convergence and inherit Cauchy completeness from theorem 13.22.7. The approximants constructed above have a common positive lower-volume bound, which need not equal the lower volume of the limiting model.

13.24.6 A countable divisorial positivity criterion1D7E7321D20C

A prescribed divisorial part can determine an ideal envelope's ordinary type even when it has infinitely many components. On the projective plane with a pluriclosed background, the smooth residual determines the mass of every representative of that type. This gives a positivity test directly from divisorial orders.

Let $X=\mathbb P^2$, and let $\kappa $ be the Fubini–Study form with $\int _ X\kappa ^2=1$. Choose distinct hyperplanes $H_ j$ and coefficients $a_ j>0$ with $A=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j<\infty $. For unit defining sections $s_ j$, put

\[ \ell _ j(Z)=\log \frac{|s_ j(Z)|^2}{\| Z\| ^2}\leq 0, \qquad u_0=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j\ell _ j, \qquad u=u_0-\sup _ Xu_0. \]

Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form satisfying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta =0$ and $R=\theta -A\kappa >0$.

Lemma 13.24.7.487BF774470F The series defines a quasi-plurisubharmonic function, and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=S-A\kappa ,\qquad S=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j[H_ j],\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=R+S>0. \]

For every prime divisor $E$ on every smooth compact modification $\pi :Z\to X$, the convergent order profile is

\[ b(E):=\sum _{j\geq 1}a_ j\operatorname {ord}_ E(\pi ^*H_ j) =\nu (u\circ \pi ,E). \]

Proof. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ell _ j=[H_ j]-\kappa $. Unitary invariance reduces its integral to the distribution of $x=|Z_0|^2/\| Z\| ^2$, whose density on $[0,1]$ is $2(1-x)$. Thus

\[ \int _ X\ell _ j\kappa ^2=\int _0^1 2(1-x)\log x\, \mathrm dx=-\frac32. \]

The decreasing series has finite $L^1$ norm and a common lower curvature bound $-A\kappa $. Its canonical limit is quasi-plurisubharmonic. Distributional convergence gives the current identities and strict positivity.

Fix $\pi $ and $E$, and choose a Gauduchon form $\gamma $ on the compact surface $Z$. For the tail $r_ N=\sum _{j>N}a_ j\ell _ j$ of total coefficient $A_ N=\sum _{j>N}a_ j$, the closed positive current

\[ T_ N=A_ N\pi ^*\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(r_ N\circ \pi ) \]

has trace mass $A_ N\int _ Z\pi ^*\kappa \wedge \gamma $, by distributional Stokes. The pullback of $r_ N$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, since $\pi $ is an isomorphism on a dense open set where $r_ N$ is finite almost everywhere. Divisorial removal gives

\[ 0\leq \nu (r_ N\circ \pi ,E) \leq A_ N\frac{\int _ Z\pi ^*\kappa \wedge \gamma }{\int _ E\gamma }. \]

The denominator is positive and the ratio is finite. Finite additivity of Lelong numbers now identifies the partial profile with $\nu (u\circ \pi ,E)$ up to an error tending to zero. This proves convergence and the asserted equality. $\square$

Proposition 13.24.8.20027E32C14E Let $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The conditions

\[ \nu (v\circ \pi ,E)=b(E)\text{ for every }\pi ,E, \qquad v\sim _{\mathcal I}u \]

are equivalent. Under either condition,

\[ q=P_\theta [v]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}\sim u, \]

and every $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w\sim q$ satisfies

027648EDCB33 13.24.9
\begin{equation} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q) =\int _ XR^2>0. \end{equation}

The background is big and $X$ has the bounded mass property.

Proof. Ideal profile and envelope. Choose an integer $C>A$ with $C\kappa \geq \theta $. Both $u$ and $v$ are $C\kappa $-plurisubharmonic. The projective valuative characterization of all-weight ideal types applies in this closed background: generic orders on all smooth projective modifications recover point Lelong numbers by further point blowups, and equality of those numbers is equivalent to ideal equivalence. The stated profile therefore implies $v\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. Conversely, ideal equivalence pulls back under every compact modification by lemma 5.43.23; the local valuative criterion theorem 5.37.3 then gives equality of generic coefficients on every such modification. The common factor between growth against $\log |z|$ and the book's $\log |z|^2$ convention does not affect these equalities.

The potential $u$ has complex Hessian $S-A\kappa $. By corollary 18.4.9, its ordinary and ideal envelopes agree and are ordinarily equivalent to $u$. Ideal equivalence identifies the defining families for the two ideal envelopes in the statement, proving the assertion about $q$. The inequality $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq R>0$ proves bigness. For any Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, choose $D$ with $D\kappa \geq h$. Bounded $h$-plurisubharmonic potentials are $D\kappa $-plurisubharmonic, and positivity of bounded mixed products bounds their masses by $\int _ X(D\kappa )^2=D^2$. This proves the bounded mass property.

The bounded residual. Fix $w\sim u$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and put $T=C\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$. Its base divisorial coefficients are $a_ j$, so divisorial removal and passage through increasing finite sums give

\[ K=T-S=(C-A)\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\geq 0,\qquad h=w-u. \]

The distributional difference has a canonical quasi-plurisubharmonic representative, which is bounded by ordinary equivalence and almost-everywhere order. Thus $K$ has bounded local potentials, and its trace charges no pluripolar set. The current $S$ is carried by the actual pole union $\bigcup _ jH_ j\subset \{ w=-\infty \} $. Thus $\langle T\rangle =K$. For every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $L$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}L=0$, distributional Stokes gives

\[ \int _ X L\wedge \langle T\rangle =(C-A)\int _ XL\wedge \kappa . \]

Closed top mass and smooth correction. Put $T_ u=C\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=S+(C-A)\kappa $. For the finite partial sums $u_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j\ell _ j$, the closed non-pluripolar mass is $(C-\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j)^2$: outside the finite divisor union the current is the smooth residual, and the measure gives that union zero mass. Since $u_0\leq u_ N$, closed-background mass monotonicity, the closed case of theorem 13.17.1, gives

\[ \int _ X\langle T_ u^2\rangle \leq \left(C-\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j\right)^2. \]

For the reverse inequality, locally write $S=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b$ with $b$ plurisubharmonic. The currents $(C-A)\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (b,-k)$ have bounded local potentials and dominate $(C-A)\kappa $. Their squared products, restricted to $\{ b>-k\} $, therefore dominate $(C-A)^2\kappa ^2$ on that set. Letting $k$ increase and using the local truncation definition gives $\langle T_ u^2\rangle \geq (C-A)^2\kappa ^2$. The two bounds prove equality of total masses. Closed mass invariance under bounded changes then gives

\[ \int _ X\langle T^2\rangle =(C-A)^2. \]

Set $L=C\kappa -\theta \geq 0$, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}L=0$. Expanding the bounded products of $\max (w,-k)$ in the two backgrounds and restricting to $\{ w>-k\} $ gives, in the limit,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) =\langle T^2\rangle -2L\wedge \langle T\rangle +L^2. \]

All correction measures are finite: the closed top measure has the mass just computed, the degree-one limit is $K$, and the smooth measure $L^2$ gives the pole set zero mass. The same truncations are used in both backgrounds, so this identity is an equality of Borel measures. Integrating yields

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) =(C-A)^2-2(C-A)\int _ XL\wedge \kappa +\int _ XL^2 =\int _ X(\theta -A\kappa )^2. \]

This holds throughout the ordinary type, proving both singularity-volume identities and their strict positivity. $\square$

Example 13.24.10.E76D9C1B44B6 There is a nonclosed pluriclosed background on $\mathbb P^2$ with a nonanalytic ideal type satisfying 13.24.8. Its prescribed divisors are not contained in a single pencil, and both singularity volumes of its ideal model are at least $1/16$.

Proof. In a coordinate ball choose a smooth real cutoff $\chi $ equal to one near the origin, and extend $\alpha =\chi |z_2|^2\mathrm dz_1$ by zero. Put

\[ \tau =\partial \overline\alpha +\bar\partial \alpha . \]

It is real and smooth, with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0$. Where $\chi =1$,

\[ \mathrm d\tau =\mathrm dz_2\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_2 \wedge (\mathrm dz_1-\mathrm d\bar z_1)\ne 0. \]

Choose $\epsilon >0$ small enough that $\theta =\kappa +\epsilon \tau \geq \kappa /2$. Set $A=1/4$, $a_ j=A2^{-j}$, $t_ j=2^{-j}$ and

\[ H_ j=\{ Z_0+t_ jZ_1+t_ j^2Z_2=0\} . \]

The Vandermonde determinant shows that any three defining linear forms are independent; hence the hyperplanes are not in one pencil. Here $R=\theta -A\kappa \geq \kappa /4$, so $\int _ XR^2\geq 1/16$. The preceding proposition gives the mass throughout the ordinary type and both singularity volumes. There are infinitely many positive base divisor coefficients. An analytic singularity on a compact manifold has only finitely many prime divisorial components, so no analytic potential has this ideal type. $\square$

Problem 13.24.11.D0837305A3CF For a big nonclosed background $\theta $ on a manifold with the bounded mass property, find criteria for $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I})>0$ in terms of restricted multiplier ideals or divisorial orders. When this lower volume vanishes, which implications between envelope equality and upper-volume equality, conditions (i) and (ii) of proposition 13.24.2, remain valid?

One implication fails: when the ideal envelope has zero lower volume, equality of the upper volumes of a potential and of its ideal envelope does not force equality of the envelopes. In example 18.15.5, the background $\Theta $ is positive and nonclosed on a projective manifold with the bounded mass property, and $q=P_\Theta [a]_{\mathcal I}$ satisfies $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (a)=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (q)=0$ but $P_\Theta [a]\ne q$. The example leaves the other implications in 13.24.11 undecided.
