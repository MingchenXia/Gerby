12.34 Current tests for smooth positivity509A5021C77D

509A5021C77D

Lamari's duality, theorem 11.6.1, characterizes pseudoeffective backgrounds by their pairings with Gauduchon metrics. Smooth positivity, and hence nefness, is characterized in the same way by pairing with all positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-currents. By the Hahn–Banach theorem, the minimum of these pairings over normalized currents is exactly the largest $a$ such that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>ah$ for a smooth function $u$, and the nef boundary is where this minimum vanishes. The full set of currents is needed here: the closure of the smooth tests used in Lamari's duality detects pseudoeffectivity and bigness instead. The minimizing currents at the nef boundary appear again at the maximal time of the Chern–Ricci flow, and positive variational volume forces their determinant-weighted measures to vanish.

Let $X$ again be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. Fix a Hermitian metric $h$, and let $\mathscr G_ h$ be the set of normalized Gauduchon currents of section 11.7, that is, positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-currents $T$ with $\int _ Xh\wedge T=1$. This set is weakly compact for $n\geq 2$. For $n=1$, elliptic regularity and the maximum principle give $\mathscr G_ h=\{ 1/\int _ Xh\} $, so compactness holds in this case as well. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $, define

\[ m_ h(\theta )=\min _{T\in \mathscr G_ h}\int _ X\theta \wedge T. \]

The minimum exists by weak compactness, and it is finite since $-Ch\leq \theta \leq Ch$ for some constant $C$.

Theorem 12.34.1.FC99385C18B8 The background $\theta $ has a smooth positive representative if and only if $m_ h(\theta )>0$. Moreover, $\theta $ is nef if and only if $m_ h(\theta )\geq 0$, and

\[ m_ h(\theta )=\sup \{ a\in \mathbb R:\ \exists u\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R), \ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>ah\} . \]

Proof. If $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq ah$ with $a>0$ and $u$ smooth, then integration by parts gives $\int _ X\theta \wedge T\geq a$ for every $T\in \mathscr G_ h$. Since a smooth positive representative satisfies such a bound for some $a>0$ by compactness, this proves one implication.

For the converse, work in the real Fréchet space of smooth real $(1,1)$-forms. Let $\mathcal K$ be its open convex cone of strictly positive forms, and let $\mathcal L=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. If the affine subspace $\theta +\mathcal L$ is disjoint from $\mathcal K$, the Hahn–Banach separation theorem for an open convex set and a disjoint affine subspace gives a nonzero continuous real linear functional $T$ with

\[ T(\theta +\ell )\leq T(\eta )\qquad (\ell \in \mathcal L,\ \eta \in \mathcal K). \]

Replacing $\ell $ by arbitrary real multiples shows that $T(\mathcal L)=0$. Scaling a fixed $\eta \in \mathcal K$ to zero shows that $T(\theta )\leq 0$, while scaling it to infinity shows that $T(\eta )\geq 0$. Since every smooth semipositive form is a limit of strictly positive forms, $T$ is a positive current, and positivity makes it a current of order zero. The equality $T(\mathcal L)=0$ says exactly that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$. Since $T\ne 0$ and $h>0$, the trace $T(h)$ is positive, and normalizing $T$ gives an element of $\mathscr G_ h$ with nonpositive pairing against $\theta $. This contradicts $m_ h(\theta )>0$ and proves the converse.

Applying the equivalence just proved to $\theta -ah$, and using $m_ h(\theta -ah)=m_ h(\theta )-a$, shows that the set in the supremum is exactly $(-\infty ,m_ h(\theta ))$. Finally, $\theta $ is nef if and only if $\theta +\varepsilon h$ has a smooth positive representative for every $\varepsilon >0$, that is, if and only if $m_ h(\theta )+\varepsilon >0$ for all such $\varepsilon $, or equivalently $m_ h(\theta )\geq 0$. $\square$

For $n\geq 2$, let $\mathscr C_ h\subseteq \mathscr G_ h$ be the weak closure of the smooth strictly positive normalized Gauduchon forms, and let $\kappa _ h(\theta )$ be the linear positivity threshold of corollary 11.6.2, which is the minimum of $\int _ X\theta \wedge T$ over $T\in \mathscr C_ h$, by section 11.7. The inclusion $\mathscr C_ h\subseteq \mathscr G_ h$ gives $m_ h(\theta )\leq \kappa _ h(\theta )$. The larger test set detects smooth positivity and nefness, whereas the weak closure of the smooth tests detects the existence of positive and of strictly positive currents in the class, that is, pseudoeffectivity and bigness. The inequality can be strict, as example 10.18.3 shows.

Proposition 12.34.2.635FE1D11798 Let $\theta $ be nef with $\lambda _\mu (\theta )>0$. If $T\in \mathscr G_ h$ satisfies $\int _ X\theta \wedge T=0$, then the determinant-weighted measure of $T$, computed as in (12.7.1) with reference metric $h$ and with $\mu $ in place of $\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV$, vanishes.

Proof. In dimension one the hypotheses cannot hold, since $\int _ X\theta \wedge T=\lambda _\mu (\theta )/\int _ Xh>0$. Suppose $n\geq 2$, and write $D_\mu (T)$ for the total mass of this determinant-weighted measure; for $T=\gamma ^{n-1}$ it is the denominator $D_\mu (\gamma )$ of definition 12.2.1. If $\eta \in \mathscr P$, its solution form $\Omega $ satisfies $\Omega ^ n=\lambda _\mu (\eta )^ n\mu $, so $\Omega /\lambda _\mu (\eta )$ is an admissible test in the infimum of lemma 12.7.2. Together with Stokes' theorem, this gives

\[ \int _ X\eta \wedge T=\int _ X\Omega \wedge T \geq \lambda _\mu (\eta )D_\mu (T). \]

Apply this to $\eta =\theta +\varepsilon h$. The left side equals $\varepsilon $, while theorem 12.11.1 gives $\lambda _\mu (\eta )\to \lambda _\mu (\theta )>0$. Hence $D_\mu (T)=0$. The determinant-weighted measure is positive and has total mass zero, so it vanishes. $\square$

Thus, at a nef background of positive volume, every annihilating current in $\mathscr G_ h$ has a coefficient matrix of deficient rank almost everywhere with respect to its trace measure. This alone does not force the trace measure to be supported on an analytic subset.

12.34.3 Maximal time of the Chern–Ricci flow355CACEF1F3C

The Chern–Ricci flow deforms a Hermitian metric by $\partial _ t\omega =-\operatorname {Ric}(\omega )$. Since the Chern–Ricci forms of any two Hermitian metrics differ by a $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact form, a solution starting at $\omega _0$ stays in the affine family $\omega _0-t\operatorname {Ric}(\omega _0)$ modulo $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of smooth functions. It can therefore exist only as long as this family has smooth positive representatives. Conversely, a positive representative at a later time provides a uniformly positive reference path, and parabolic estimates exclude an earlier singularity. The current criterion of theorem 12.34.1 then computes the lifetime.

Let $\omega _0$ be the initial Hermitian metric. For a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$ we use the Chern–Ricci form

\[ \operatorname {Ric}(\omega )=-\mathrm{i}\partial \bar\partial \log \omega ^ n =-2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \omega ^ n, \]

where $\omega ^ n$ is identified with its coefficient in local holomorphic coordinates; a change of coordinates changes $\log \omega ^ n$ by a pluriharmonic function and does not affect the form. This normalization fixes the time scale of the flow $\partial _ t\omega =-\operatorname {Ric}(\omega )$. Put $\chi =\operatorname {Ric}(\omega _0)$ and $\theta _ t=\omega _0-t\chi $.

Theorem 12.34.4.B6FA0B70406A The Chern–Ricci flow starting from $\omega _0$ has a unique maximal smooth solution on $[0,\tau )$, where

\[ \tau =\sup \{ t\geq 0:\ \theta _ t\text{ has a smooth positive representative}\} . \]

Proof. Call a time $t\geq 0$ admissible if $\theta _ t$ has a smooth positive representative. The admissible times form an interval containing zero, since convex combinations of positive representatives at two times are positive representatives at the intermediate times, and the set is open because positivity is an open condition. We show that the flow exists on $[0,t_1)$ for every admissible $t_1>0$ and that it cannot exist beyond $\tau $.

Step 1: the scalar equation. Fix an admissible $t_1>0$, and choose a smooth real function $a$ with $\theta _{t_1}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a>0$. The reference forms

\[ \gamma _ t=\theta _ t+\frac t{t_1}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a =\left(1-\frac t{t_1}\right)\omega _0 +\frac t{t_1}(\theta _{t_1}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a),\qquad 0\leq t\leq t_1, \]

are uniformly positive, with all derivatives uniformly bounded. Put

\[ \Omega =\mathrm{e}^{a/(2\pi t_1)}\omega _0^ n. \]

Then $\dot\gamma _ t=-\operatorname {Ric}(\Omega )$, where the Ricci form of a volume form is defined by the same local expression. Consider the scalar equation

00A6644760C0 12.34.5
\begin{equation} \dot u=2\pi \log \frac{(\gamma _ t+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n}{\Omega }, \qquad \gamma _ t+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0,\qquad u(0)=0. \end{equation}

Applying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ shows that $\omega (t)=\gamma _ t+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u(t)$ solves the metric flow. Conversely, for a solution $\omega (t)$ of the metric flow, the function $u(t)=2\pi \int _0^ t\log (\omega (s)^ n/\Omega )\, \mathrm ds$ satisfies $\omega (t)=\gamma _ t+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u(t)$ and solves (12.34.5). The linearization of (12.34.5) is $\partial _ t-2\pi \operatorname {tr}_{\omega (t)}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$, a strictly parabolic operator, so the classical short-time existence theory for smooth strictly parabolic equations gives a solution. The difference of two solutions satisfies a linear parabolic equation whose principal coefficients, the averages of the inverse metrics along the segment between the two solutions, are positive; the maximum principle therefore proves uniqueness.

Suppose that the maximal time $t_*$ of this solution satisfies $t_*<t_1$. We derive estimates independent of $t<t_*$ that allow us to continue the solution.

Step 2: bounds for $u$ and $\dot u$. For a large constant $C$, the functions $Ct$ and $-Ct$ are a supersolution and a subsolution of (12.34.5), respectively: at a spatial extremum of $u$ the complex Hessian has the appropriate sign, and $\gamma _ t^ n/\Omega $ is bounded above and away from zero. The maximum principle gives $|u|\leq Ct_1$.

Set $v=\dot u$ and $\mathcal L=2\pi \operatorname {tr}_{\omega (t)}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$. Differentiation gives

\[ (\partial _ t-\mathcal L)v=2\pi \operatorname {tr}_{\omega (t)}\dot\gamma _ t, \qquad (\partial _ t-\mathcal L)u=v-2\pi n+2\pi \operatorname {tr}_{\omega (t)}\gamma _ t. \]

Since $\gamma _ t$ is affine in time, these identities imply

\begin{align*} (\partial _ t-\mathcal L)(t v-u-2\pi nt) & =-2\pi \operatorname {tr}_{\omega (t)}\omega _0<0,\\ (\partial _ t-\mathcal L)((t_1-t)v+u+2\pi nt) & =2\pi \operatorname {tr}_{\omega (t)}\gamma _{t_1}>0. \end{align*}

By the maximum principle, the first expression is bounded above by its initial value zero, and the second is bounded below by $\min _ Xt_1v(0)$. The bound for $u$ and $t_1-t\geq t_1-t_*>0$ give a lower bound for $v$. The first inequality gives an upper bound for $v$ on $[t_*/2,t_*)$, and the smooth solution bounds it on $[0,t_*/2]$. In particular, $\omega (t)^ n/\Omega =\mathrm{e}^{v/(2\pi )}$ is bounded above and away from zero.

Step 3: a parabolic trace inequality. We next bound the trace, keeping track of the time derivative of the reference metric. In the coordinates of lemma 14.5.1, write $g(t)$ for the matrix of $\gamma _ t$ and, as in that lemma, $h=g+(u_{i\bar j})$ for the matrix of $\omega (t)$. Put

\[ S=\operatorname {tr}_ g h,\qquad B=\operatorname {tr}_ h g, \qquad F=\log \frac{\Omega }{\gamma _ t^ n}, \]

so that $\log (\det h/\det g)=v/(2\pi )+F$. The family $g(t)$ has uniformly bounded Chern torsion and curvature, so at each fixed time lemma 14.5.1 applies with uniform constants. Direct differentiation of $S$ gives

\[ \partial _ t\log S =\frac{\Delta _ g v+\operatorname {tr}_ g\dot g}{S} -\frac{\operatorname {tr}(g^{-1}\dot g\, g^{-1}h)}{S}. \]

In $(\partial _ t-\mathcal L)\log S$, the term $\Delta _ gv/S$ cancels the corresponding term in the lower bound for $\mathcal L\log S$ given by the lemma. With $\tau _ k=\sum _ iT_{ik}^ i$ the torsion trace of $g(t)$, as in the lemma, we obtain on $\{ S\geq 1\} $

A5486C5A27CF 12.34.6
\begin{equation} (\partial _ t-\mathcal L)\log S \leq -\frac{4\pi }{S^2}\operatorname {Re}(h^{k\bar q}\tau _ k S_{\bar q})+CB+C. \end{equation}

Indeed, $\Delta _ gF$ and $\operatorname {tr}_ g\dot g$ are uniformly bounded, $S^{-1}\leq B/n^2$, and $-Cg\leq \dot g\leq Cg$ bounds the last quotient above. These bounds account for all the terms coming from the time dependence of the reference metric.

Step 4: the trace bound. Choose a constant $C_0$ such that $1\leq s=u+C_0\leq D$ on the whole time interval, and maximize

\[ Q=\log S-Au+s^{-1} \]

on $X\times [0,t_0]$, where $t_0<t_*$ and the constant $A\geq 1$ will be fixed independently of $t_0$. If the maximum is attained at time zero, the initial data bound $Q$. At a maximum with positive time, $\partial \log S=(A+s^{-2})\partial u$. Since $|\tau |_ h^2\leq CB$, Young's inequality bounds the absolute value of the first term on the right of (12.34.6) by

\[ 2\pi s^{-3}|\partial u|_ h^2 +\frac{C(A+1)^2s^3}{S^2}B. \]

If $S^2\leq (A+1)^2D^3$, the trace is already bounded at the maximum. Otherwise the last coefficient is at most $C$, and

\[ \begin{split} 0\leq (\partial _ t-\mathcal L)Q & \leq CB+C-(A+s^{-2})(v-2\pi n+2\pi B) -2\pi s^{-3}|\partial u|_ h^2\\ & \leq (C-2\pi A)B+C(A+1). \end{split} \]

Choosing $A$ with $2\pi A>C+1$ bounds $B$ at the maximum. In terms of the eigenvalues, $S\leq n(\det h/\det g)B^{n-1}$ for $n\geq 2$, and $S=\det h/\det g$ for $n=1$, so the determinant bound of Step 2 bounds $S$ at the maximum in every case. The bound for $u$ transfers this estimate from $Q$ to $S$ everywhere, uniformly in $t_0$, and the lower bound for the determinant then bounds the smallest eigenvalue away from zero. Thus $\omega (t)$ and $\gamma _ t$ are uniformly equivalent for $t<t_*$.

Step 5: higher estimates and continuation. Uniform ellipticity now gives the higher estimates by classical local parabolic regularity. In a coordinate ball, write the logarithmic determinant as a concave function of the complex part of the real Hessian. On the compact set of positive matrices just obtained, its supporting affine functions have uniformly positive and bounded slopes, and their infimum extends the operator to a concave, uniformly elliptic function of all real symmetric matrices. The dependence on space and time enters only through the smooth family $g(t)$ and the fixed density $\Omega $. The parabolic Evans–Krylov estimate gives uniform $C^{2+\alpha ,1+\alpha /2}$ bounds away from the initial time, and the smooth solution covers a fixed initial interval. Differentiating the equation and applying parabolic Schauder estimates then bounds all spatial and time derivatives uniformly. This is the parabolic counterpart of the local argument used in corollary 14.5.8.

The bounds for the time derivatives show that $u(t)$ converges in every spatial $C^ k$ norm as $t\uparrow t_*$. By uniform ellipticity, the smooth limit still has a strictly positive solution form, and short-time existence with this limit as initial datum extends the solution beyond $t_*$, a contradiction. Hence the flow exists up to every admissible time $t_1$, and by uniqueness these solutions patch together on $[0,\tau )$.

Step 6: maximality. Integrating the metric flow gives

\[ \omega (t)=\theta _ t+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\left(2\pi \int _0^ t \log \frac{\omega (s)^ n}{\omega _0^ n}\, \mathrm{d}s\right). \]

Hence every time at which a smooth positive solution exists is admissible, which proves maximality. $\square$

Combining the continuation theorem with the current criterion gives a numerical description of the lifetime.

Corollary 12.34.7.90A4213A9E87 Let

\[ b=\max _{T\in \mathscr G_{\omega _0}}\int _ X\chi \wedge T. \]

Then the maximal smooth existence time is

\[ \tau =\begin{cases} 1/b,& b>0,\\ +\infty ,& b\leq 0.\end{cases} \]

If $\tau <\infty $, the limiting background $\theta _\tau $ is nef and there is a current $T_*\in \mathscr G_{\omega _0}$ with $\int _ X\theta _\tau \wedge T_*=0$. If moreover $\lambda _\mu (\theta _\tau )>0$, every such annihilating current has zero determinant-weighted measure.

Proof. For $t\geq 0$, the normalization of the test currents gives the identity

\[ m_{\omega _0}(\theta _ t)=1-tb. \]

Theorem 12.34.1, Theorem 12.34.4 give the formula for $\tau $. When $b>0$, the maximum defining $b$ is attained by weak compactness, and a maximizer $T_*$ annihilates $\theta _\tau $. The same identity gives $m_{\omega _0}(\theta _\tau )=0$, hence nefness. The last assertion is proposition 12.34.2. $\square$

Along the flow, $\omega (t)=\theta _ t+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ t$ with a smooth potential $\varphi _ t$. Consequently, for every fixed $T\in \mathscr G_{\omega _0}$,

\[ \int _ X\omega (t)\wedge T =1-t\int _ X\chi \wedge T. \]

These traces describe the obstruction at the maximal time without any bound on the total Monge–Ampère mass. They do not locate the singularities of the flow, nor do they give smooth convergence away from an analytic set; such geometric questions require local estimates in addition to these scalar pairings.

12.34.8 A positive-volume endpoint that is not big382AAED0F384

Backgrounds reached at the maximal time of a Chern–Ricci flow are not better behaved, as far as the relation between positive volume and bigness is concerned. The radial background of section 12.14 occurs, up to a smooth $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ change, as the limiting background of a Chern–Ricci flow at its maximal time. The normalized currents annihilating it can also be described explicitly.

We use the notation of section 12.14: $n=m+1\geq 3$, $t=(m-1)/m$, $a=x/t$, and

\[ X=\mathbb P^ m\times \mathbb P^1,\qquad \theta =\omega +a w,\qquad \mu =\omega ^ m\wedge w, \qquad Z=\{ p\} \times \mathbb P^1, \]

where $\omega $ and $w$ are the normalized Fubini–Study forms pulled back from the two factors, so that $\mu $ is a probability form. Start the flow from the Hermitian metric

C648779A3DE9 12.34.9
\begin{equation} \omega _0=(n+1)\omega +(a+2)w. \end{equation}

Theorem 12.34.10.AFAF4566FE5D With the normalization of the Ricci form in 12.34.3, the maximal smooth existence time of the Chern–Ricci flow starting from $\omega _0$ is $\tau =1/(2\pi )$. The limiting background $\alpha _\tau =\omega _0-\tau \operatorname {Ric}(\omega _0)$ is nef and not big, and

\[ \alpha _\tau =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (a+2),\qquad \mathcal V_\mu (\alpha _\tau )=n. \]

Its variational volume is positive for every smooth strictly positive probability form on $X$.

Proof. The initial metric is strictly positive, and

\[ \omega _0^ n=n(n+1)^ m(a+2)\mu . \]

With our normalization, the Ricci forms of the normalized Fubini–Study metrics are $2\pi n\omega $ and $4\pi w$. Consequently

\[ \operatorname {Ric}(\omega _0) =2\pi \bigl (n\omega +2w-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (a+2)\bigr ). \]

Write $s$ for the flow time and put $r=2\pi s$. The affine background is

348CDC5BE9A1 12.34.11
\begin{equation} \alpha _ s=(n+1-nr)\omega +(a+2-2r)w +r\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (a+2). \end{equation}

For $0\leq r<1$, adding $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of the smooth function $-r\log (a+2)$ gives a strictly positive representative: the horizontal coefficient is at least one and the vertical coefficient is at least $2(1-r)>0$. At $r=1$ we obtain the identity $\alpha _\tau =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (a+2)$. Since the logarithm is a smooth function on $X$, the bijection $u\mapsto u-\log (a+2)$ between the potentials of the two backgrounds preserves nefness and bigness, and Stokes' theorem for Gauduchon metrics shows that the variational volume is unchanged. Theorem 12.14.6 therefore shows that the endpoint background is nef, not big, and of volume $n$.

For $r>1$, choose a nonzero smooth nonnegative function $f$ on the base supported in $\{ a+2-2r<0\} $, a neighborhood of $p$. Its pullback $S=f\omega ^ m$ is a closed semipositive $(n-1,n-1)$-form. For every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$, distributional Stokes gives

\[ \int _ X(\alpha _ s+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)\wedge S =\int _{\mathbb P^ m}f(a+2-2r)\omega ^ m<0, \]

so $\alpha _ s$ is not even pseudoeffective for $r>1$. At $r=1$, a smooth positive representative would contradict nonbigness. Thus $r=1$ is the supremum of the admissible times, and theorem 12.34.4 gives the smooth flow on $[0,\tau )$ with exactly this lifetime; the reference representatives used above need not solve the flow equation. Finally, proposition 12.15.1 gives positivity of the volume for every smooth positive probability form. $\square$

Proposition 12.34.12.83512AFDC859 The current $[Z]/2$ belongs to $\mathscr G_{\omega _0}$ and annihilates $\alpha _\tau $. Every $R\in \mathscr G_{\omega _0}$ with $\int _ X\alpha _\tau \wedge R=0$ has its trace probability $\sigma _ R=\omega _0\wedge R$ supported on $Z$. In the coefficient-matrix convention of section 12.7, its matrix is

\[ P_ R=n\Pi _{\mathrm{vert}}\qquad \sigma _ R\text{-almost everywhere}, \]

where $\Pi _{\mathrm{vert}}$ is the $\omega _0$-orthogonal projection onto the tangent line of the fiber $\{ p\} \times \mathbb P^1$. In particular, $P_ R$ has rank one, and the determinant-weighted measure of $R$ vanishes for every smooth positive reference density.

Proof. Only smooth forms need to be restricted to $Z$, and their restrictions satisfy $\omega _0|_ Z=2w$ and $\alpha _\tau |_ Z=0$. This proves that $[Z]/2$ is normalized and annihilates $\alpha _\tau $.

Let $R$ be a general annihilator. Since $\alpha _\tau $ and $\theta $ differ by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a smooth function, Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ X\theta \wedge R=0$. The measure $\theta \wedge R$ is nonnegative, so it vanishes. On every compact set $K\subset X\setminus Z$, the explicit form $\theta $ satisfies $\theta \geq c_ K\omega _0$ for some $c_ K>0$. Thus $\sigma _ R(K)=0$, and a countable exhaustion by compact sets shows that $\sigma _ R$ is supported on $Z$.

Relative to $\omega _0$, the matrix of $R$ is characterized, for smooth test forms $\beta $ with matrix $B_\beta $, by

\[ \beta \wedge R=\frac1n\operatorname {tr}(B_\beta P_ R)\sigma _ R, \qquad P_ R\geq 0,\qquad \operatorname {tr}P_ R=n. \]

At points of $Z$, the form $\theta $ is positive on the horizontal $m$-plane and vanishes on the vertical line. The vanishing of $\theta \wedge R$ therefore forces all horizontal diagonal entries of $P_ R$ to vanish, and the inequality $|(P_ R)_{ij}|^2\leq (P_ R)_{ii}(P_ R)_{jj}$ for positive matrices removes their rows and columns. The remaining vertical entry is $n$, which proves the formula and the vanishing of the determinant. $\square$

For each such annihilator $R$, the trace identity at the end of 12.34.3 becomes

\[ \int _ X\omega (s)\wedge R=1-2\pi s, \qquad 0\leq s<1/(2\pi ). \]

This is a statement about averaged traces. The support statement uses the explicit semipositive representative $\theta $ of the endpoint class; the vanishing of the determinant alone would not give it. Neither statement concerns pointwise collapse of the metrics, their local convergence, or uniqueness of the trace probability.

12.34.13 Twisting and distance boundsFFD5AB006AEF

A twisted Chern–Ricci flow is the equation $\partial _ t\omega =-\operatorname {Ric}(\omega )+\zeta $, where the twist $\zeta $ is a fixed smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form. Diameter bounds along such flows cannot depend only on the maximal time and on the initial Monge–Ampère density: the twist and the length of the time interval enter the geometry, even for flat Kähler metrics.

Example 12.34.14.706929790BE5 Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$, let $X=E^ n$ with $n\geq 2$, and let $\eta _ j=(\mathrm i/2)\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$ be the flat forms pulled back from the factors. Put $\omega _0=\sum _{j=1}^ n\eta _ j$. All the flows below start at $\omega _0$, with potential zero and initial density one relative to $\omega _0^ n$. In particular, hypotheses on the initial data expressed through a decomposition $\mathrm e^{\psi ^+-\psi ^-}$ of the density, with quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $\psi ^\pm $ subject to fixed $L^ p$ bounds, hold uniformly for all these flows, with $\psi ^+=\psi ^-=0$.

For each $M>0$, the twist $\zeta _ M=M\eta _1-\eta _2$ gives the explicit solution

\[ \omega _ M(t)=(1+Mt)\eta _1+(1-t)\eta _2+ \sum _{j=3}^ n\eta _ j, \qquad 0\leq t<1, \]

of $\partial _ t\omega =-\operatorname {Ric}(\omega )+\zeta _ M$. Its maximal smooth existence time is exactly one, while

\[ \operatorname {diam}(X,\omega _ M(1/2)) \geq \sqrt{1+M/2}\, \operatorname {diam}(E,\eta _1). \]

For the single fixed twist $\zeta =\omega _0$, the solution is $(1+t)\omega _0$ for all $t\geq 0$, and its diameter grows as $\sqrt{1+t}\, \operatorname {diam}(X,\omega _0)$.

Proof. Each of these metrics has constant coefficients, so its Chern–Ricci form vanishes; differentiation in $t$ proves the flow equations, and positivity of the coefficients gives the stated existence intervals. In the first family, pair a hypothetical positive representative at a time $t\geq 1$ with the closed positive form $\eta _1\wedge \eta _3\wedge \cdots \wedge \eta _ n$, where the empty product is $1$ when $n=2$. The potential term integrates to zero, and the pairing is a positive fixed multiple of $1-t$, so it cannot be strictly positive. Integrating the flow equation shows that every smooth solution differs from the affine background by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a smooth function, so no smooth positive continuation beyond $t=1$ is possible.

Projection to the first elliptic factor does not increase lengths once the metric of that factor is scaled by $1+Mt$. Taking two points that differ only in the first factor proves the lower bound for the diameter. For the second flow, every path length is multiplied by $\sqrt{1+t}$, which proves the exact formula for the diameter. $\square$

On a fixed finite time interval, a common Dini modulus for the potentials and a uniform bound for the affine backgrounds do give control of distances and diameters, by corollary 5.39.8. The example shows why such bounds must be imposed when the twist varies or the time interval grows. It does not concern the limit metric at the maximal time of an untwisted flow.
