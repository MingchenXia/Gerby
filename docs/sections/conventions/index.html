1.1 ConventionsDE921263

DE921263

In the whole book, we adopt the following conventions:

  • A complex space is always assumed to be reduced, paracompact and Hausdorff.

  • A modification of a complex space $X$ is a proper bimeromorphic morphism $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ that is locally obtained from a finite composition of blow-ups with smooth centers.

  • A subnet of a net refers to a Willard subnet. More precisely, if $f\colon I\rightarrow X$ and $f'\colon I'\rightarrow X$ are nets, namely, maps from directed sets $I$ and $I'$ to the same set $X$, we say $f'$ is a subnet of $f$ if there is an increasing final function $h\colon I'\rightarrow I$ such that $f\circ h=f'$.

  • A domain in $\mathbb {R}^ d$ or $\mathbb {C}^ n$ refers to a connected open subset.

  • A complex manifold is assumed to be paracompact.

  • A submanifold of a complex manifold means a closed complex submanifold.

  • A neighborhood is not necessarily open.

  • The set $\mathbb {N}$ of natural numbers includes $0$.

  • Increasing functions and decreasing functions are not necessarily strictly monotone.

  • Positive forms are not necessarily strictly positive.

  • The function $\mathbb {R}\ni x \mapsto x^2$ is convex instead of concave. This is different from the convention in [CLS11].

  • We say a positive measure $\mu $ on a measurable space $X$ is carried by a measurable subset $Y$ if $\mu (X\setminus Y)=0$.

  • $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ means $(2\pi )^{-1}\mathrm{i}\partial \overline{\partial }$.

We also use the following notation throughout the book.

  • The indicator function of a set $E$ is denoted by $\mathbf1_ E$.

  • For subsets $A$ and $B$ of a topological space, $A\Subset B$ means that the closure of $A$ is a compact subset of $B$; we then say that $A$ is relatively compact in $B$.

  • The closure, interior and boundary of a set $E$ in its ambient space are denoted by $\overline E$, $\operatorname{Int}E$ and $\partial E$. The support of a function, measure, distribution or current is denoted by $\operatorname{Supp}$.

  • For an open set $\Omega $ in $\mathbb R^ d$ or in a manifold, $C^ k(\Omega )$ is the space of $k$ times continuously differentiable functions, where $0\leq k\leq \infty $, and $C^ k_ c(\Omega )$ is its subspace of functions with compact support. We write $L^ p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega )$ for the functions whose restrictions to compact subsets of $\Omega $ are $p$-integrable.

  • The imaginary unit is $\mathrm{i}$, and $\operatorname{Re}z$ and $\operatorname{Im}z$ are the real and imaginary parts of a complex number $z$.

Unless otherwise stated, $X$ denotes a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$. A Hermitian form is a smooth real strictly positive $(1,1)$-form. The letter $\omega $ is reserved for such a form, while $\theta $ denotes an arbitrary smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Whenever $\theta $ or $\omega $ is assumed to be closed, this is stated explicitly.

We follow the normalizations of [SGPT]:

\[ \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi }(\bar\partial -\partial ), \qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial . \]

Thus, on a disk in $\mathbb C$ with coordinate $z$, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^2$ is the unit Dirac measure at the origin. Multiplier ideals of a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ are defined with the weight $\exp (-u)$, and Monge–Ampère masses carry no factor $1/n!$.

1.1.2 Scope, attribution and conventions61B2D7362CC0

These notes reconstruct material from Helms's 1969 book[Helms1969] that extends the current Gerby collection. The complete book, including its preliminary conventions, twelve chapters, notes and indexes, has a separate statement ledger; covered statements are mapped there to exact existing entries. This supplement gives the additional arguments, with local corrections recorded in the accompanying errata dossiers. Attribution follows Helms's historical notes; this is a mathematical exposition, not a claim of new research or a transcription of the book.

We work in real dimension $d\ge 2$, with ordinary Laplacian $\Delta $, unnormalized sphere area $s_{d-1}$, and unit ball volume $v_ d=s_{d-1}/d$. Put $\Phi (x)=|x|^{2-d}$ for $d\ge 3$ and $\Phi (x)=\log (1/|x|)$ for $d=2$. Then $-\Delta \Phi =c_ d\delta _0$, where $c_2=2\pi $ and $c_ d=(d-2)s_{d-1}$ for $d\ge 3$. Unless explicitly normalized otherwise, Green functions use this convention. Thus $G_{\rm Helms}=c_ dG_{\rm Foundations}$ and $C_{\rm Helms}=C_{\rm Foundations}/c_ d$. Superharmonic functions are lower semicontinuous, super-mean-valued and nontrivial on each component; positive means nonnegative unless strict positivity is stated. A potential is a nonnegative superharmonic function whose greatest harmonic minorant is zero. Measures are positive Radon measures unless signedness is specified. On noncompact spaces weak measure convergence in the source means vague convergence against $C_ c$.

We assume standard multivariable calculus, Lebesgue integration and measure decomposition, compact-metric measure representation, and the checked Foundations manuscript at revision da2be417c7b38b386d0b688251cf4978df94d682[Foundations2026]. Its exact prerequisite map accompanies this source. In particular the ball Poisson formula, Harnack estimates and derivative bounds, harmonic normal convergence and Weyl's lemma, canonical ball averages, local Riesz decomposition, polar removability and globalization, and the polar discrepancy theorem for subharmonic envelopes are existing inputs. General-domain kernels, capacities, sweeping, infinity conventions and Martin theory are established below. General topology inputs used only in the final section are stated there explicitly.
