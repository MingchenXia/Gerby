17.5 Variational Monge–Ampère equations16614E7E6F9C

16614E7E6F9C

We keep the big class of $\theta $, the model potential $\phi $ and its mass $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle >0$, and write $E=E_\theta ^\phi $. We solve the equation $\mu _ u=\nu $ for non-pluripolar measures $\nu $ of mass $m$ (definition 17.5.2) by a variational method. By the tangent bounds, a potential $u$ with $\mu _ u=\nu $ maximizes $v\mapsto E(v)-\int _ X(v-\phi )\, \mathrm d\nu $. For the converse we perturb a maximizer $u$ in the direction of a continuous function $f$. Since $u+tf$ need not be $\theta $-psh, we replace it by its envelope $P_\theta (u+tf)$ and compute the derivative of the energy along these envelopes (proposition 17.5.4).

Maximizers exist by the compactness of corollary 17.4.5 when the integral term of the functional is continuous for $L^1$ convergence, as it is for measures with bounded densities. For an arbitrary non-pluripolar measure $\nu $ we replace the linear term by the exponential term $\lambda ^{-1}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu $, $\lambda >0$, which is continuous for $L^1$ convergence of potentials, and then let $\lambda \to 0$. The remaining subsections prove uniqueness (subsection 17.5.16) and the boundedness of $u-\phi $ for $L^ p$ densities (subsection 17.5.19), and derive two consequences of the existence theorem (subsection 17.5.27, subsection 17.5.40).

17.5.1 Existence by maximization06665CC7749C

Definition 17.5.2.37BEFE25B673 A finite positive Radon measure $\nu $ on $X$ is non-pluripolar if $\nu (A)=0$ for every pluripolar Borel set $A\subset X$. Integrals against $\nu $ use its completion, so subsets of pluripolar sets are also $\nu $-null.

For example, every measure of the form $g\, \omega ^ n$ with $0\leq g\in L^1(X,\omega ^ n)$ is non-pluripolar, since pluripolar sets have volume zero (see the remarks after definition 5.47.2). Every $\theta $-psh function is finite $\nu $-almost everywhere, because its pole set is a pluripolar Borel set.

Finite positive Borel measures on $X$ are compared through their integrals of continuous functions.

Lemma 17.5.3.B408410BD203 Let $\lambda $ and $\sigma $ be finite positive Borel measures on $X$.

  1. If $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\lambda \geq \int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\sigma $ for every continuous function $g\geq 0$ on $X$, then $\lambda (E)\geq \sigma (E)$ for every Borel set $E\subset X$. In particular, two finite positive Borel measures on $X$ with the same integrals of all continuous functions coincide.

  2. If finite positive Borel measures $\lambda _ j$ converge weakly to $\lambda $ and satisfy $\lambda _ j\geq \sigma $ for every $j$, then $\lambda \geq \sigma $.

Proof. (1) This is lemma 16.12.1(2) with $W=X$; the second assertion follows by applying the first in both directions.

(2) Since $X$ is compact, weak convergence of measures on $X$ is convergence of the integrals of all continuous functions (see the discussion following definition 5.3.5). Hence $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\lambda =\lim _ j\int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\lambda _ j\geq \int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\sigma $ for every continuous function $g\geq 0$ on $X$, and (1) gives $\lambda \geq \sigma $. $\square$

Proposition 17.5.4.2099099BAE21 Let $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, and put $u_ t=P_\theta (u+tf)$ for $t\in \mathbb R$, the envelope of definition 16.11.3. Then $u_0=u$, and for every $t$ we have $u_ t\sim u$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$ on $X$. Each $u_ t$ has finite energy, and

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E(u_ t)=\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}. \]

The derivative is continuous on $\mathbb R$.

Proof. Let $B=\sup _ X|f|$. The potential $u-|t|B$ is a competitor for the obstacle $u+tf$, and every competitor lies below $u+|t|B$, so $u-|t|B\leq u_ t\leq u+|t|B$. Hence $u_ t\sim u$, and $E(u_ t)\geq E(u)-|t|Bm>-\infty $, so $u_ t\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by theorem 17.3.14. The obstacle $u+tf$ is upper semicontinuous, so $u_ t\leq u+tf$ everywhere, by proposition 16.11.4(1),(2). In particular $u_0\leq u$; since $u$ is itself a competitor for the obstacle $u$, also $u\leq u_0$, so $u_0=u$.

Next we show that $\mu _{u_{t+s}}\to \mu _{u_ t}$ weakly as $s\to 0$. The obstacles $u+tf$ and $u+(t+s)f$ differ by at most $|s|B$, so proposition 16.11.4(4) gives $u_ t-|s|B\leq u_{t+s}\leq u_ t+|s|B$. In particular all $u_ t$ have the pole set of $u$, and $|u_{t+s}-u_ t|\leq |s|B$ outside it. Since $|\arctan a-\arctan b|\leq |a-b|$ for real $a,b$, and adding the same smooth local potential of $\theta $ to $u_{t+s}$ and $u_ t$ does not change their difference, the distance $d_*$ of definition 13.7.2 between the local potentials of $u_{t+s}$ and $u_ t$ is at most $|s|B$ everywhere. Hence, for every $\delta >0$, the sets where it exceeds $\delta $ are empty once $|s|B<\delta $, and $u_{t+s}\to u_ t$ in extended capacity as $s\to 0$. These potentials have full mass relative to $\phi $, so corollary 13.9.4, applied along every sequence $s_ k\to 0$, gives the asserted weak convergence; in particular $\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_{t+s}}\to \int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}$.

The obstacle $u+tf$ is bounded above, and it is quasi-continuous, because $u$ is (section 16.11) and $f$ is continuous. Hence theorem 16.13.4 shows that $\mu _{u_ t}$ is carried by the contact set $\{ u_ t=u+tf\} $. On the contact set of $u_{t+s}$ we have $u_{t+s}=u+(t+s)f$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$, so $u_{t+s}-u_ t\geq sf$ there. On the contact set of $u_ t$ we have $u_ t=u+tf$ and $u_{t+s}\leq u+(t+s)f$, so $u_{t+s}-u_ t\leq sf$ there. The differences are bounded and the pole sets carry no mass, so the tangent bounds of lemma 17.4.10 give, for every real $s$,

\[ s\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_{t+s}} \leq E(u_{t+s})-E(u_ t) \leq s\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}. \]

Dividing by $s>0$, respectively $s<0$, and letting $s\to 0$, we obtain the right and left derivatives from the weak convergence just proved. Both equal $\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}$, which depends continuously on $t$ by the same weak convergence. $\square$

Theorem 17.5.5.011162B56B40 Let $\nu $ be a non-pluripolar measure on $X$, and suppose that $\mathcal D_\nu =\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )\cap L^1(\nu )$ is nonempty. Then $\phi \in L^1(\nu )$, and for the functional

\[ F_\nu (v)=E(v)-\int _ X(v-\phi )\, \mathrm d\nu , \qquad v\in \mathcal D_\nu , \]

a potential $u\in \mathcal D_\nu $ maximizes $F_\nu $ if and only if $\mu _ u=\nu $. In particular, $F_\nu $ has no maximizer unless $\nu (X)=m$.

Proof. Let $v\in \mathcal D_\nu $. Then $v\leq \phi +C$ for a constant $C$, and $\phi \leq 0$. Thus the negative part of $\phi $ is at most that of $v$ plus $C$, and the positive part of $\phi $ vanishes; hence $\phi \in L^1(\nu )$. If $\mu _ u=\nu $, the tangent bound of lemma 17.4.10 gives

\[ E(v)-E(u)\leq \int _ X(v-u)\, \mathrm d\nu , \]

that is, $F_\nu (v)\leq F_\nu (u)$.

Conversely, let $u$ be a maximizer and $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$. By proposition 17.5.4, the potentials $u_ t=P_\theta (u+tf)$ have bounded difference from $u$ and finite energy, so they belong to $\mathcal D_\nu $; moreover $u_0=u$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$ everywhere, hence $\nu $-almost everywhere. Therefore

\[ E(u_ t)-E(u)-t\int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu \leq F_\nu (u_ t)-F_\nu (u)\leq 0. \]

The left side vanishes at $t=0$ and is differentiable there by proposition 17.5.4; since it attains its maximum at $t=0$, its derivative vanishes there. Thus $\int _ Xf\, \mu _ u=\int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu $ for every $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, and lemma 17.5.3(1) gives $\mu _ u=\nu $. Since $\mu _ u(X)=m$, a maximizer can exist only if $\nu (X)=m$. $\square$

Theorem 17.5.6.8972585EBF32 Let $\mathrm dV$ be a smooth positive volume form on $X$, and let $g\in L^\infty (\mathrm dV)$ satisfy $g\geq 0$ and $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm dV=m$. Then there is $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with

\[ \mu _ u=g\, \mathrm dV,\qquad \sup _ Xu=0. \]

It maximizes $F_{g\, \mathrm dV}$.

Proof. Put $\nu =g\, \mathrm dV$, a non-pluripolar measure of mass $m$. Every $\theta $-psh potential is integrable for $\mathrm dV$, hence for $\nu $, so $\mathcal D_\nu =\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$; we write $F=F_\nu $. By the shift formula for the energy and $\nu (X)=m$, the functional $F$ is invariant under adding constants, so we may restrict it to potentials $v$ with $\sup _ Xv=0$. For such $v$, lemma 16.22.2 gives $v\leq \phi $, hence $E(v)\leq 0$. Since $\mathrm dV\leq c_ V\, \omega ^ n$ for a constant $c_ V$, proposition 10.3.1 shows that $\int _ X|v|\, \mathrm dV$ is bounded independently of $v$, and $\phi \in L^1(\mathrm dV)$, so

\[ 0\leq -\int _ X(v-\phi )g\, \mathrm dV\leq C \]

with $C$ independent of $v$. Consequently $\sup F\in [0,C]$, since $F(\phi )=0$ and $F(v)\leq 0+C$.

Let $(v_ j)$ be a normalized maximizing sequence with $F(v_ j)\geq -1$. Then $E(v_ j)=F(v_ j)+\int _ X(v_ j-\phi )g\, \mathrm dV\geq -C-1$. By corollary 17.4.5 a subsequence converges in $L^1(X)$ to some $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and $\sup _ Xu=0$, because the supremum is continuous for $L^1$ convergence of potentials (proposition 10.3.1). The linear term $v\mapsto \int _ X(v-\phi )g\, \mathrm dV$ is continuous for $L^1$ convergence because $g$ is bounded, and the energy is upper semicontinuous by proposition 17.4.4. Hence $F(u)\geq \varlimsup _ jF(v_ j)=\sup F$, so $u$ is a maximizer, and theorem 17.5.5 gives $\mu _ u=\nu $. $\square$

Proposition 17.5.7.5BDA01022188 Let $\lambda >0$ and let $\nu $ be a finite positive non-pluripolar measure on $X$. If $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ satisfy

\[ \mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu , \qquad \mu _ v\geq \mathrm e^{\lambda v}\nu , \]

then $u\geq v$. In particular, the equation $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $ has at most one solution in $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Proof. Let $A=\{ u<v\} $. The comparison principle of proposition 16.25.4 gives

\[ \int _ A\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu \leq \mu _ v(A)\leq \mu _ u(A) =\int _ A\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\, \mathrm d\nu \leq m. \]

Both potentials are finite $\nu $-almost everywhere, and on $A$ we have $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}>\mathrm e^{\lambda u}$. The difference $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}-\mathrm e^{\lambda u}$ is therefore positive on $A$ and has nonpositive integral over $A$, so $\nu (A)=0$. Hence $\mu _ u(A)=0$, and the domination principle (theorem 16.22.10) gives $u\geq v$ everywhere. If $u$ and $v$ are two solutions, applying this in both directions gives $u=v$. $\square$

Solutions of the exponential equations are obtained as maximizers of the functionals

20471D0DB1A7 17.5.8
\begin{equation} F_\lambda (v)=E(v)-\lambda ^{-1}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu ,\qquad v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi ), \end{equation}

where $\lambda >0$ and $\nu $ is a finite positive Borel measure on $X$. These functionals are finite: the energy is finite on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and the Borel function $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}$ satisfies $0\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda v}\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda \sup _ Xv}$, where $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}=0$ on the pole set of $v$.

Lemma 17.5.9.B4AA6C4C1C94 Let $\nu $ be a finite positive Borel measure on $X$ and $\lambda >0$. If $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ maximizes the functional $F_\lambda $ of (17.5.8) on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $.

Proof. Let $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ and $u_ t=P_\theta (u+tf)$ for $t\in \mathbb R$. By proposition 17.5.4, $u_ t\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, $u_0=u$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$ everywhere. Hence $\mathrm e^{\lambda u_ t}\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}$, and $F_\lambda (u_ t)\leq F_\lambda (u)$ gives

\[ E(u_ t)-E(u)-\lambda ^{-1} \int _ X\left(\mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}-\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\right) \, \mathrm d\nu \leq 0. \]

The left side vanishes at $t=0$. Its energy term is differentiable, with derivative $\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}$, by proposition 17.5.4. For $|t|\leq 1$ the $t$-derivative $\lambda f\mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}$ of the integrand is bounded by $\lambda \sup _ X|f|\, \mathrm e^{\lambda \sup _ Xu+\lambda \sup _ X|f|}$, which is integrable for the finite measure $\nu $; by the mean value theorem and dominated convergence, the integral is differentiable as well, with derivative $\lambda \int _ Xf\mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}\, \mathrm d\nu $. Since the left side is maximal at $t=0$, its derivative vanishes there: $\int _ Xf\, \mu _ u=\int _ Xf\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\, \mathrm d\nu $. Since $f$ is arbitrary and $\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $ is a finite measure, lemma 17.5.3(1) gives $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $. $\square$

To find maximizers for an arbitrary non-pluripolar measure $\nu $, we need the continuity of $v\mapsto \int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu $ for $L^1$ convergence of potentials. This rests on the following two lemmas, in which $\theta $ is an arbitrary smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form.

Lemma 17.5.10.DD59ECAA0656 Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. If $q_ j,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(X)$, then $\varlimsup _ jq_ j=q$ quasi-everywhere.

Proof. By lemma 17.4.3, the tail envelopes $a_ j=(\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k)^*$ are $\theta $-psh and decrease pointwise to $q$. For each $j$, corollary 5.47.11, applied after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $, gives $a_ j=\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k$ outside a pluripolar set. The countable union of these sets is pluripolar (theorem 5.47.8), and outside it $q=\lim _ ja_ j=\varlimsup _ jq_ j$. $\square$

Lemma 17.5.11.B5DC04E7729F Let $\nu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$, let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $B\geq 0$. Suppose that $q_ j,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $|q_ j|,|q|\leq B$ and $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(X)$. Then $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(\nu )$.

Proof. By lemma 17.5.10, $\varlimsup _ jq_ j=q$ outside a pluripolar set, hence $\nu $-almost everywhere. Thus $(q_ j-q)_+\to 0$ $\nu $-almost everywhere, and $\int _ X(q_ j-q)_+\, \mathrm d\nu \to 0$ by bounded convergence, since $0\leq (q_ j-q)_+\leq 2B$ and $\nu $ is finite. Since $|q_ j-q|=2(q_ j-q)_+-(q_ j-q)$, it remains to show that $\int _ Xq_ j\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int _ Xq\, \mathrm d\nu $. We have just seen that $\varlimsup _ j\int _ X(q_ j-q)\, \mathrm d\nu \leq 0$, so it suffices to prove that $L=\varliminf _ j\int _ Xq_ j\, \mathrm d\nu $ is at least $\int _ Xq\, \mathrm d\nu $.

Choose a subsequence $(q_{j_ k})$ with $\int _ Xq_{j_ k}\, \mathrm d\nu \to L$. For bounded Borel functions $y$ on $X$ put $\| y\| =(\int _ Xy^2\, \mathrm d\nu )^{1/2}$. For $l\geq 1$ let $C_ l$ be the set of finite convex combinations of the functions $q_{j_ k}$ with $k\geq l$, and let $d_ l=\inf \{ \| y\| :y\in C_ l\} $. Since $C_{l+1}\subset C_ l$ and $\| y\| \leq B\nu (X)^{1/2}$ for $y\in C_ l$, the numbers $d_ l$ increase to a finite limit. Choose $y_ l\in C_ l$ with $\| y_ l\| ^2\leq d_ l^2+1/l$. For $l\leq l'$ the function $\frac12(y_ l+y_{l'})$ belongs to the convex set $C_ l$, so its norm is at least $d_ l$. The parallelogram identity, obtained by integrating the pointwise identity $(a-b)^2+(a+b)^2=2a^2+2b^2$, therefore gives

\[ \| y_ l-y_{l'}\| ^2=2\| y_ l\| ^2+2\| y_{l'}\| ^2-4\bigl \| \tfrac 12(y_ l+y_{l'})\bigr \| ^2 \leq 2(d_{l'}^2-d_ l^2)+\frac2l+\frac2{l'}, \]

and the right side tends to zero as $l\to \infty $, uniformly in $l'\geq l$.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, $\int _ X|y_ l-y_{l'}|\, \mathrm d\nu \leq \nu (X)^{1/2}\| y_ l-y_{l'}\| $, so we can choose $l_1<l_2<\cdots $ with $\int _ X|y_{l_{i+1}}-y_{l_ i}|\, \mathrm d\nu \leq 2^{-i}$. By monotone convergence the series $\sum _ i|y_{l_{i+1}}-y_{l_ i}|$ has finite integral, hence converges $\nu $-almost everywhere, and therefore $y_{l_ i}$ converges $\nu $-almost everywhere; let $h=\varlimsup _ iy_{l_ i}$, a Borel function with $|h|\leq B$. By bounded convergence, $\int _ Xy_{l_ i}\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int _ Xh\, \mathrm d\nu $. On the other hand, $\int _ Xy_ l\, \mathrm d\nu $ is a convex combination of the numbers $\int _ Xq_{j_ k}\, \mathrm d\nu $ with $k\geq l$, which tend to $L$, so $\int _ Xy_ l\, \mathrm d\nu \to L$. Hence $L=\int _ Xh\, \mathrm d\nu $.

It remains to show that $h=q$ $\nu $-almost everywhere. Each $y_{l_ i}$ is a convex combination of $\theta $-psh functions, hence $\theta $-psh, and $\| y_{l_ i}-q\| _{L^1(X)}\leq \sup _{k\geq l_ i}\| q_{j_ k}-q\| _{L^1(X)}$, which tends to zero. By lemma 17.5.10, applied to the sequence $(y_{l_ i})_ i$, the function $h=\varlimsup _ iy_{l_ i}$ equals $q$ outside a pluripolar set, hence $\nu $-almost everywhere. Thus $L=\int _ Xq\, \mathrm d\nu $, as required. $\square$

Corollary 17.5.12.F89C0E4EBAC2 Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $q_ j,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(X)$, and let $\nu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$. Then for every $\lambda >0$,

\[ \mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}\longrightarrow \mathrm e^{\lambda q} \quad \text{in }L^1(\nu ). \]

Proof. By proposition 10.3.1 there is a constant $C$ with $q_ j,q\leq C$. We show that the functions $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}$ and $\mathrm e^{\lambda q}$, with value zero on the pole sets, are $C_1\omega $-psh for a constant $C_1$ independent of $j$; the preceding lemma then applies to them.

Choose $C_0\geq 0$ with $\theta \leq C_0\omega $, and a finite cover of $X$ by coordinate balls $W'\Subset W$ such that the smaller balls $W'$ still cover $X$, together with smooth functions $h_ W$ near $\overline W$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ W=\theta $. The function $q_ j+h_ W$ is plurisubharmonic on $W$, so for every $\delta >0$ smaller than the distance from $W'$ to the boundary of $W$, its regularization $g_\delta =(q_ j+h_ W)*\rho _\delta $ by the radial mollifier is a smooth plurisubharmonic function on the ball $W'$, and $g_\delta $ decreases to $q_ j+h_ W$ as $\delta \downarrow 0$, by proposition 5.9.2(2). Put $q_{j,\delta }=g_\delta -h_ W$. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_{j,\delta }\geq -\theta \geq -C_0\omega $. Moreover $g_\delta (x)$ is an average of values of $q_ j+h_ W\leq C+h_ W$ at points within distance $\delta $ of $x$, so by uniform continuity of $h_ W$ on a compact neighborhood of $\overline{W'}$ in $W$ we have $q_{j,\delta }\leq C+1$ on $W'$ for all small $\delta $, independently of $j$.

For a smooth real function $s$, the chain rule $\partial \bar\partial \, \mathrm e^{\lambda s}=\lambda \mathrm e^{\lambda s}\partial \bar\partial s+\lambda ^2\mathrm e^{\lambda s}\partial s\wedge \bar\partial s$ and the identity $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial s\wedge \bar\partial s=\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s$, which follows from (5.2.10), give

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }} =\lambda \mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_{j,\delta } +\lambda ^2\mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }} \mathrm dq_{j,\delta }\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}q_{j,\delta } \geq -\lambda C_0\mathrm e^{\lambda (C+1)}\omega , \]

because $\mathrm dq_{j,\delta }\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}q_{j,\delta }$ is a semipositive $(1,1)$-form. Thus $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }}$ is $C_1\omega $-psh on $W'$ with $C_1=\lambda C_0\mathrm e^{\lambda (C+1)}$, and so is its decreasing limit $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}$ as $\delta \downarrow 0$, by theorem 5.10.1 applied after adding a smooth local potential of $C_1\omega $; the limit is not identically $-\infty $, since it is nonnegative. Since the balls $W'$ cover $X$, the function $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,C_1\omega )$, and the same argument applies to $\mathrm e^{\lambda q}$.

The exponential is Lipschitz with constant $\lambda \mathrm e^{\lambda C}$ on $(-\infty ,C]$, so $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}\to \mathrm e^{\lambda q}$ in $L^1(X)$. These functions take values in $[0,\mathrm e^{\lambda C}]$, and lemma 17.5.11, with the background $C_1\omega $, gives the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 17.5.13.109ED281DA22 Let $\nu $ be a finite positive non-pluripolar measure on $X$ with $\nu (X)>0$, and let $\lambda >0$. There is a unique $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ satisfying

\[ \mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu . \]

This solution belongs to $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and maximizes $F_\lambda (v)=E(v)-\lambda ^{-1}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu $ on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Proof. By corollary 17.4.5, the set $K_\phi $ of potentials $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w\leq \phi $ and $\sup _ Xw=0$ is compact in $L^1(X)$; it contains $\phi $. By corollary 17.5.12, the function $w\mapsto \int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda w}\, \mathrm d\nu $ is continuous on $K_\phi $. Its values are positive, since the pole set of $w$ is $\nu $-null and $\nu (X)>0$. Hence it has a positive minimum $a$ on $K_\phi $.

\[ \begin{gathered} S_\varepsilon =\theta _\varepsilon +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon \geq 0, \qquad \langle S_\varepsilon ^ n\rangle =m_\varepsilon \omega \wedge \tau ,\\ V_{\theta _\varepsilon }-B_\varepsilon \leq u_\varepsilon \leq V_{\theta _\varepsilon }. \end{gathered} \]

The constants $B_\varepsilon $ need not be bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon $; we only use that $u_\varepsilon $ is locally bounded outside a closed set of measure zero. By lemma 16.13.2, applied to the big class $\{ \theta _\varepsilon \} $, there is a closed pluripolar set $A_\varepsilon \subset X$ such that $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus A_\varepsilon $; being pluripolar, $A_\varepsilon $ has Lebesgue measure zero in every chart (see the remarks after definition 5.47.2). Hence $u_\varepsilon $, which lies between $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }-B_\varepsilon $ and $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$, is locally bounded on $X\setminus A_\varepsilon $, and so are the local potentials of $S_\varepsilon $, which differ from $u_\varepsilon $ by smooth local potentials of $\theta _\varepsilon $ (lemma 5.4.2).

We may thus apply lemma 17.5.33 to $S_\varepsilon $, with $A_\varepsilon $, $\nu =\omega \wedge \tau $ and $m_\varepsilon $ in place of $A$, $\nu $ and $m$:

\[ \left(\int _ X S_\varepsilon \wedge \tau \right) \left(\int _ X\langle S_\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega \right) \geq \frac{m_\varepsilon }{n}\geq \frac mn. \]

We bound the two factors on the left. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0$, the first factor equals the cohomological pairing $\int _ X\theta _\varepsilon \wedge \tau =\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau +\varepsilon $. If $n\geq 2$, the form $\theta _\varepsilon +C\omega $ is positive definite, so theorem 13.5.4 gives

\[ \int _ X\langle S_\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega \leq \int _ X(\theta +(C+\varepsilon )\omega )^{n-1}\wedge \omega \leq C_0; \]

the last inequality holds because $\theta +(C+1)\omega =(\theta +(C+\varepsilon )\omega )+(1-\varepsilon )\omega $ and products of positive $(1,1)$-forms are positive. If $n=1$, the second factor equals $\int _ X\omega =C_0$. As the first factor is nonnegative, in both cases

\[ \left(\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau +\varepsilon \right)C_0\geq \frac mn. \]

Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau \geq m/(nC_0)$. Removing the normalization of $\tau $, we conclude that every smooth strictly positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\tau $ satisfies (17.5.30) with $\delta =m/(nC_0)>0$. By lemma 17.5.28, the class $\alpha $ is big. $\square$

If $\{ \theta \} $ is pseudo-effective but not big, the theorem shows that every $\theta $-psh potential has zero non-pluripolar mass. This allows us to extend the contact theorems of section 16.10 from big to pseudo-effective classes.

Corollary 17.5.38.C78DFCF82004 Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form, and let $f\colon X\to [-\infty ,+\infty ]$ be a quasi-continuous Borel function. Suppose that the family $\mathcal A_\theta (f)$ of definition 16.11.3 is nonempty and uniformly bounded above. Then $u=P_\theta (f)$ satisfies

\[ \mu _ u\bigl (X\setminus \{ u=f\} \bigr )=0. \]

If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, then $\mu _ u=0$. Moreover, if $r\geq 1$ and $u_1,\ldots ,u_ r\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ have a rooftop $w=P_\theta (\min _{1\leq j\leq r}u_ j)$ that is not identically $-\infty $, then

\[ \mu _ w\leq \sum _{j=1}^ r\mathbf1_{\{ w=u_ j\} }\mu _{u_ j}. \]

Proof. By proposition 16.11.4(1), $u\in \mathcal A_\theta (f)$; that is, $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, then $\mu _ u(X)=0$ by theorem 17.5.37, since otherwise $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ would be a closed positive current of positive non-pluripolar mass in $\{ \theta \} $. Hence $\mu _ u=0$, and in particular the first assertion holds.

If $\{ \theta \} $ is big, the first assertion is theorem 16.13.4.

For the rooftop, the function $\min _ ju_ j$ is quasi-continuous, as a finite minimum of potentials (see the beginning of section 16.11), and it is bounded above; its envelope is $w$. Since $w$ is not identically $-\infty $, the family $\mathcal A_\theta (\min _ ju_ j)$ is nonempty, and it is uniformly bounded above because the obstacle is. By the first assertion, $\mu _ w$ is carried by $\{ w=\min _ ju_ j\} \subset \bigcup _ j\{ w=u_ j\} $. The obstacle is upper semicontinuous, so $w\leq u_ j$ everywhere by proposition 16.11.4(1),(2), and lemma 16.13.1 gives $\mathbf1_{\{ w=u_ j\} }\mu _ w\leq \mathbf1_{\{ w=u_ j\} }\mu _{u_ j}$ for every $j$. Summing over $j$ proves the rooftop inequality. $\square$

Corollary 17.5.39.92C6B04981BA Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form with $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $, and let $f$ be a continuous real function on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian. Then

\[ \langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_\theta (f))^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ P_\theta (f)=f\} }(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ n, \]

where the right side is understood as in theorem 16.20.2. In particular $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\theta )^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ V_\theta =0\} }\theta ^ n$. If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, both sides of these two identities vanish.

Proof. The potential $V_\theta +\min _ Xf$ lies below $f$, so $\mathcal A_\theta (f)$ is nonempty, and it is bounded above by $\max _ Xf$. Put $u=P_\theta (f)$. Since $f$ is continuous, $u\leq f$ everywhere by proposition 16.11.4(1),(2). The scalar case of theorem 16.20.2, with all $\theta _ j=\theta $, $u_ j=u$ and $f_ j=f$, gives

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u=f\} }\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ u=f\} }(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ n, \]

and corollary 17.5.38 shows that $\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle $ is carried by $\{ u=f\} $. This proves the first identity. For $f=0$ the envelope $P_\theta (0)$ is $V_\theta $, by definition 10.3.7, which gives the second. If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, the left sides vanish by corollary 17.5.38, and hence so do the right sides. $\square$

17.5.40 Mixed masses and concavity69E4E21B68CA

We now compare the mixed non-pluripolar mass of $n$ closed positive currents, which may lie in different classes, with their individual masses. The idea is to replace each current by a solution of a Monge–Ampère equation with one and the same smooth right-hand side. Since singularity envelopes preserve mixed masses (proposition 16.21.2(3)), such a replacement does not change the mixed mass, and for the solutions the pointwise mixed lower bound of proposition 13.5.8 integrates to the desired inequality. The equations can be solved because, by theorem 17.5.37, every current of positive mass lies in a big class.

Theorem 17.5.41.0A392CD9486E Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then

AC065DF59BDD 17.5.42
\begin{equation} \int _ X\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n\rangle \geq \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle \right)^{1/n}. \end{equation}

Consequently the functions

\[ T\longmapsto \left(\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle \right)^{1/n}, \qquad T\longmapsto \log \left(\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle \right) \]

are concave on the convex cone of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, with the convention $\log 0=-\infty $ for the second function.

Proof. All the masses involved are finite by theorem 13.5.4. If one of the masses $\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle $ vanishes, the right side of (17.5.42) is zero and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise put $m_ i=\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle >0$. Both sides of (17.5.42) are positively homogeneous of degree one in each $T_ i$, by the multilinearity of non-pluripolar products (proposition 13.2.6), so after replacing $T_ i$ by $m_ i^{-1/n}T_ i$ we may assume $m_ i=1$ for all $i$.

By theorem 17.5.37, the class of each $T_ i$ is big. Let $\theta _ i$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form in the class of $T_ i$ (definition 10.2.17). By corollary 10.2.19 we can write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$ with $u_ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$. Set $\phi _ i=P_{\theta _ i}[u_ i]$. By the mass preservation in proposition 16.21.2(3) and by theorem 16.22.11, $\phi _ i$ is a model potential of mass one.

Let $\nu =\omega ^ n/\int _ X\omega ^ n$. This smooth probability measure is non-pluripolar, as noted after definition 17.5.2. In each big class $\{ \theta _ i\} $, theorem 17.5.15 gives

\[ w_ i\in \mathcal E(X,\theta _ i,\phi _ i),\qquad S_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ i\geq 0,\qquad \langle S_ i^ n\rangle =\nu . \]

By proposition 16.22.14, $P_{\theta _ i}[w_ i]=P_{\theta _ i}[\phi _ i]=\phi _ i$. Applying the mixed mass preservation of proposition 16.21.2(3) first to the tuple $(w_1,\ldots ,w_ n)$ and then to the tuple $(u_1,\ldots ,u_ n)$, we obtain

\[ \int _ X\langle S_1\wedge \cdots \wedge S_ n\rangle =\int _ X\left\langle \bigwedge _{i=1}^ n (\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi _ i)\right\rangle =\int _ X\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n\rangle . \]

On the other hand, proposition 13.5.8, with all densities equal to one, gives $\langle S_1\wedge \cdots \wedge S_ n\rangle \geq \nu $. Hence the common value of these mixed masses is at least $\nu (X)=1$, which is the normalized inequality. Undoing the scalings proves (17.5.42).

For the concavity, let $T,S$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents and $0<t<1$; for $t=0,1$ there is nothing to prove. Put $a=\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle $ and $b=\int _ X\langle S^ n\rangle $. By multilinearity,

\[ \int _ X\langle ((1-t)T+tS)^ n\rangle =\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(1-t)^ kt^{n-k} \int _ X\langle T^ k\wedge S^{n-k}\rangle . \]

For $1\leq k\leq n-1$, (17.5.42) with $k$ factors $T$ and $n-k$ factors $S$ gives $\int _ X\langle T^ k\wedge S^{n-k}\rangle \geq a^{k/n}b^{(n-k)/n}$; the terms with $k=n$ and $k=0$ are $a$ and $b$, also when one of them vanishes. The binomial formula therefore yields

\[ \int _ X\langle ((1-t)T+tS)^ n\rangle \geq \big ((1-t)a^{1/n}+tb^{1/n}\big )^ n. \]

Taking $n$th roots proves that the function $T\mapsto (\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle )^{1/n}$ is concave. If $a$ and $b$ are both positive, then, since the logarithm is increasing and concave,

\[ \begin{aligned} \log \Big(\int _ X\langle ((1-t)T+tS)^ n\rangle \Big) & \geq n\log \big ((1-t)a^{1/n}+tb^{1/n}\big )\\ & \geq (1-t)\log a+t\log b. \end{aligned} \]

If one of them is zero, the right side is $-\infty $ for $0<t<1$, so there is nothing to prove. $\square$

The connectedness of $X$ cannot be omitted when $n\geq 2$. Let $X$ be the disjoint union of two compact Kähler manifolds $X',X''$ of dimension $n\geq 2$, with Kähler forms $\omega '$ and $\omega ''$. Let $T_1$ equal $\omega '$ on $X'$ and zero on $X''$, let $T_2$ equal zero on $X'$ and $\omega ''$ on $X''$, and let $T_3,\ldots ,T_ n$ equal $\omega '$ on $X'$ and $\omega ''$ on $X''$. Every mass $\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle $ is positive, but $\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n\rangle =0$. The theorem applies separately on each connected component.
