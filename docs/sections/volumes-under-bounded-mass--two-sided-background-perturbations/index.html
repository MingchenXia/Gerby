13.26 Two-sided background perturbationsE6BBFE91C762

E6BBFE91C762

A potential in the original background may cease to be admissible when the background decreases. Mixing it with the strict potential $\rho $ restores positivity, and ordinary projection then gives a model in the new background. The volume comparison controls the error of this transport uniformly on models with a common positive lower-volume bound, and it shows that the resulting notion of analytic approximation does not depend on the strict potential used in the correction.

We first make the estimate of the preceding section uniform for pairs of forms near $\theta $.

Lemma 13.26.1.D5C96B9222A4 For every $\delta >0$ there are $C_\delta >0$ and $\varepsilon _0>0$ with the following property. Let $\beta ,\gamma $ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms with

\[ \theta -\varepsilon h\leq \beta \leq \gamma \leq \theta +\varepsilon h, \qquad 0\leq \varepsilon \leq \varepsilon _0, \]

and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$ satisfy $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\beta (u)\geq \delta $. Then both singularity volumes satisfy

\[ 0\leq \operatorname {vol}_\gamma (u)-\operatorname {vol}_\beta (u) \leq C_\delta \varepsilon . \]

For two such potentials $u,v$, one also has

\[ \left|d_\gamma (P_\gamma [u],P_\gamma [v])-d_\beta (u,v)\right| \leq 2C_\delta \varepsilon . \]

Proof. Put $\theta _0=\theta -\kappa h/2$ and $h_0=(1+\kappa /2)h$. Since $\theta _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \kappa h/2$, the form $\theta _0$ is big, and $\rho $ is a strict analytic potential for it with the rational margin $\kappa /(2+\kappa )$ relative to $h_0$. Let $\varepsilon \leq \min (\kappa /2,1/2)$. Then $\theta _0\leq \beta \leq \theta _0+h_0$ and $\beta \leq \gamma \leq \beta +2\varepsilon h_0$, so proposition 13.25.2 applies with $\theta _0$, $h_0$, $\rho $ and a fixed Hermitian form $\Omega _0\geq \theta _0+2h_0$ in place of $\theta $, $h$, $\rho $ and $\Omega $. These choices do not depend on $\beta $ and $\gamma $. Since the volumes in background $\beta $ are at most $v_+(\Omega _0)$, the multiplicative estimate of the proposition implies the additive estimate, with a constant $C_\delta $ independent of $\beta $, $\gamma $ and $u$. Applying it to $u$, $v$ and $\max (u,v)$, the three-error calculation in the proof of corollary 13.25.3 and projection invariance in background $\gamma $ give the second assertion. $\square$

We now define the transport. Choose $A>0$ with $\theta \leq Ah$ and put $B=2(1+A/\kappa )$. For $p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $p\leq 0$, a number $\varepsilon $ with $0<\varepsilon <\min (\kappa /2,1/B)$, and a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta $ with $\theta -\varepsilon h\leq \eta \leq \theta +\varepsilon h$, set

2826105ED7B3 13.26.2
\begin{equation} c_\varepsilon =1-\varepsilon /\kappa ,\qquad T^\rho _{\eta ,\varepsilon }(p) =P_\eta [c_\varepsilon p+(1-c_\varepsilon )\rho ],\qquad \widehat\theta _\varepsilon =\theta +B\varepsilon h. \end{equation}

The mixed potential $c_\varepsilon p+(1-c_\varepsilon )\rho $ is $\eta $-plurisubharmonic, since $1-c_\varepsilon =\varepsilon /\kappa $ and therefore $\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(c_\varepsilon p+(1-c_\varepsilon )\rho )\geq \eta -\theta +\varepsilon h\geq 0$.

Theorem 13.26.3.218C2CE831D1 Let $p\in \mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$, and let $\varepsilon $ and $\eta $ be as above. The transport $T^\rho _{\eta ,\varepsilon }(p)$ of (13.26.2) is an ordinary model in background $\eta $, of lower volume at least $c_\varepsilon ^ n\delta $, and its volumes satisfy

D190CC4FAB40 13.26.4
\begin{equation} c_\varepsilon ^ n\operatorname {vol}_\theta (p) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\eta (T^\rho _{\eta ,\varepsilon }(p)) \leq c_\varepsilon ^ n\operatorname {vol}_{\widehat\theta _\varepsilon }(p). \end{equation}

For $p,q\in \mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$,

5E725A41F096 13.26.5
\begin{equation} \begin{split} & \left|d_\eta (T^\rho _{\eta ,\varepsilon }(p),T^\rho _{\eta ,\varepsilon }(q)) -c_\varepsilon ^ n d_\theta (p,q)\right|\\ & \qquad \leq 2c_\varepsilon ^ nK \left((1+B\varepsilon /\kappa _\delta )^ n-1\right). \end{split} \end{equation}

For fixed $p$, these models tend to $p$ in $L^1$ and in capacity as $\varepsilon \to 0$. Finally, suppose that two choices of the strict analytic potential $\rho $, of its rational margin $\kappa $ and of error bounds $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$ are admissible for the same backgrounds $\eta _ j$. Then the volume comparison in background $\eta _ j$ between the two transports of $p$ tends to zero.

Proof. Write $c=c_\varepsilon $, $s=1-c$, $v=cp+s\rho $ and $T(p)=T^\rho _{\eta ,\varepsilon }(p)$. Since $s\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,s(\theta -\kappa h))$, addition gives $\operatorname {vol}_{\theta -s\kappa h}(v)\geq c^ n\operatorname {vol}_\theta (p)$, and since $\theta -s\kappa h=\theta -\varepsilon h\leq \eta $, monotonicity gives the lower bound in (13.26.4) for $v$. In particular $v$ has positive lower volume in background $\eta $, so theorem 13.19.5 shows that $T(p)=P_\eta [v]$ is an ordinary model with the same two volumes as $v$.

Since $\varepsilon <\kappa /2$, we have $c\geq 1/2$, and the choice of $B$ gives

\[ c\widehat\theta _\varepsilon -(\theta +\varepsilon h) \geq \varepsilon (cB-1-A/\kappa )h\geq 0. \]

Since $\eta \leq \theta +\varepsilon h\leq c\widehat\theta _\varepsilon $, every competitor $w$ for $T(p)$, that is, every nonpositive $\eta $-plurisubharmonic $w\preceq v$, satisfies $w/c\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\widehat\theta _\varepsilon )$ and $w/c\preceq p$, because $\rho \leq 0$. Taking envelopes, and using that $v\leq 0$ is itself a competitor, yields

14679059DAF2 13.26.6
\begin{equation} cp+s\rho \leq T(p)\leq cP_{\widehat\theta _\varepsilon }[p]. \end{equation}

Volume monotonicity, together with theorem 13.19.5 in the larger background $\widehat\theta _\varepsilon $, proves the upper bound in (13.26.4).

Now let $q\in \mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$ and $m=\max (p,q)$. The same pointwise bounds give

\[ cm+s\rho \leq \max (T(p),T(q)) \leq cP_{\widehat\theta _\varepsilon }[m]. \]

Consequently the upper volumes of $T(p)$, $T(q)$ and of their maximum lie between $c^ n$ times the respective upper volumes of $p$, $q$, $m$ in the backgrounds $\theta $ and $\widehat\theta _\varepsilon $. The lower volumes of $p$, $q$ and $m$ are at least $\delta $, and their upper volumes are at most $K$. By proposition 13.25.2 with $\beta =\theta $ and $\gamma =\widehat\theta _\varepsilon $, each of the three errors $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\eta (T(x))-c^ n\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (x)$, $x=p,q,m$ (with $T(m)$ read as $\max (T(p),T(q))$), is nonnegative and at most $c^ nK((1+B\varepsilon /\kappa _\delta )^ n-1)$. As in the proof of corollary 13.25.3, this proves (13.26.5).

For fixed $p$, corollary 13.21.4, lemma 5.29.19 give $P_{\widehat\theta _\varepsilon }[p]\to p$ in $L^1$ and in capacity along every decreasing sequence $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. An arbitrary sequence $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$ is handled by comparing with the decreasing sequence $\sup _{k\geq j}\varepsilon _ k$, since the envelopes increase with the background and lie above $p$. Quasi-continuity and integrability give $cp+s\rho \to p$ in both senses. The bounds (13.26.6) prove the asserted limits.

To compare two choices along a sequence $\eta _ j$, denote their coefficients by $c_ j^{(i)}$ and their upper backgrounds by $\theta +B_ i\varepsilon _ j^{(i)}h$, $i=0,1$. Put $c_ j=\min _ i c_ j^{(i)}$ and $a_ j=\max _ i B_ i\varepsilon _ j^{(i)}$. Since the envelopes are nonpositive, both transported models lie below $c_ jP_{\theta +a_ jh}[p]$. Moreover $\eta _ j\leq c_ j(\theta +a_ jh)$, by the form inequality above for an index attaining the smaller coefficient. The upper volume of the maximum of the two transported models is therefore at most $c_ j^ n\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta +a_ jh}(p)$, which tends to $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)$. Each of their individual upper volumes is at least $(c_ j^{(i)})^ n\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)$ and at most the upper volume of the maximum, so it has the same limit. Hence their $d_{\eta _ j}$-comparison tends to zero. $\square$

For $p_ j,p\in \mathcal M_\delta (\theta )$, (13.26.5) shows that $d_\theta (p_ j,p)\to 0$ if and only if the comparisons of the transported models tend to zero along backgrounds whose errors tend to zero. The next criterion allows approximants chosen directly in those backgrounds.

Theorem 13.26.7.279D427FE3F2 Let $p$ be an ordinary model of positive lower volume. Let $\varepsilon _ j$ be rational numbers with $0<\varepsilon _ j<\min (\kappa /2,1/B)$ tending to zero, let $\eta _ j$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms with $\theta -\varepsilon _ jh\leq \eta _ j\leq \theta +\varepsilon _ jh$, and put $p_ j=T^\rho _{\eta _ j,\varepsilon _ j}(p)$. Then $p=P_\theta [p]_{\mathcal I}$ if and only if there are analytic $a_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\eta _ j)$ with

\[ d_{\eta _ j}(a_ j,p_ j)\longrightarrow 0. \]

The approximants may be required to be ordinary models. This condition does not depend on the choice of the strict analytic potential and of the admissible error bounds used to transport $p$. For every such analytic approximation, the convex combinations with $\rho $ of theorem 13.25.5 give analytic potentials in the original background whose ordinary envelopes converge to $p$ for $d_\theta $, in $L^1$ and in capacity.

Proof. Suppose that $p=P_\theta [p]_{\mathcal I}$. By theorem 13.24.3, there are analytic ordinary models $r_ j$ in background $\theta $ with $d_\theta (r_ j,p)\to 0$ and a common positive lower-volume bound. The transports $a_ j=T^\rho _{\eta _ j,\varepsilon _ j}(r_ j)$ have analytic singularities: the convex coefficients are rational, and ordinary projection preserves the singularity type of an analytic potential by proposition 18.4.8. The estimate (13.26.5) gives $d_{\eta _ j}(a_ j,p_ j)\to 0$.

Conversely, assume that such analytic approximants exist, and put $\delta =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)$. By theorem 13.26.3, the lower volume of $p_ j$ is at least $c_{\varepsilon _ j}^ n\delta \geq 2^{-n}\delta $. The upper-volume deficit from $a_ j$ to $\max (a_ j,p_ j)$ is at most $d_{\eta _ j}(a_ j,p_ j)$, which tends to zero. Applying lemma 13.18.1 with $b=2$ in background $\eta _ j$, and then addition, gives

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\eta _ j}(a_ j)\geq 2^{-2n}\delta \]

for all sufficiently large $j$.

Put $\beta _ j=\theta +\varepsilon _ jh$. Lemma 13.26.1, with $\beta =\eta _ j$ and $\gamma =\beta _ j$, and projection invariance in background $\beta _ j$ imply $d_{\beta _ j}(a_ j,p_ j)\to 0$. Also $d_{\beta _ j}(p_ j,p)\to 0$. Indeed, (13.26.6) bounds $\max (p_ j,p)$ above by $c_{\varepsilon _ j}P_{\widehat\theta _{\varepsilon _ j}}[p]$, and $c_{\varepsilon _ j}\widehat\theta _{\varepsilon _ j}\geq \beta _ j$ by the form inequality in the proof of theorem 13.26.3. Hence the upper volume of this maximum in background $\beta _ j$ is at most $c_{\varepsilon _ j}^ n\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\widehat\theta _{\varepsilon _ j}}(p)$, which tends to $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)$. The upper volumes of $p_ j$ and $p$ in background $\beta _ j$ lie between $c_{\varepsilon _ j}^ n\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)$ and this bound, so they have the same limit.

Project $a_ j$, $p_ j$ and $p$ to ordinary models in background $\beta _ j$. Their lower volumes have a common positive bound, and their upper volumes are bounded by $v_+(\Omega )$, since $\beta _ j\leq \Omega $. The three-point estimate (13.22.6) in background $\beta _ j$ therefore gives $d_{\beta _ j}(a_ j,p)\to 0$, with constants independent of $j$. Now theorem 13.25.5, applied with the backgrounds $\beta _ j$, shows that $p$ is an ideal model and gives the stated approximation in the original background.

For another choice of transport, theorem 13.26.3 shows that the two reference models in background $\eta _ j$ have volume comparison tending to zero. The lower-volume bound just proved and the same uniform three-point estimate transfer the convergence condition in both directions. This proves independence of the choice. $\square$
