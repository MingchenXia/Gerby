10.13 The non-Kähler locusB0A0B0442070

B0A0B0442070

A big class contains Kähler currents, but in general none of them is smooth. The points at which every Kähler current in the class is singular form its non-Kähler locus. Analytic regularization, maxima of potentials and the descending chain condition for analytic sets produce a single Kähler current whose singular set is exactly this locus.

For an almost positive closed $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$, write

\[ E_+(T)=\{ x\in X:\nu (T,x)>0\} \]

for its positive Lelong set, where $\nu (T,x)$ is the Lelong number of a local potential of $T$ at $x$.

Definition 10.13.1.A39877CAB682 The non-Kähler locus of a big class $\alpha $ is

\[ E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\bigcap _{T}E_+(T), \]

where $T$ ranges over the Kähler currents in $\alpha $. For a pseudoeffective class that is not big, we set $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=X$.

Theorem 10.13.2.7B5F62BF9296 Let $\alpha $ be big. There is a Kähler current in $\alpha $ with analytic singularities, smooth off its polar set, whose positive Lelong set is exactly $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. In particular this locus is a proper analytic subset, and

\[ E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha ), \qquad E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\varnothing \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \alpha \text{ is Kähler}. \]

Proof. Step 1: reduction to analytic singularities. By the analytic regularization of 5.36.3, a Kähler current $T\in \alpha $ can be approximated by currents in $\alpha $ with analytic singularities and smooth remainder away from their poles. After a sufficiently small loss of positivity these are still Kähler currents, and their positive Lelong sets are contained in $E_+(T)$. Hence the intersection defining $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$ may be taken over Kähler currents with analytic singularities and smooth remainder.

Step 2: intersections. Let $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1$ and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2$ be two such currents. By lemma 5.24.1, $\max (u_1,u_2)$ is the potential of a current dominating $\delta \omega $ for a common $\delta >0$, and its Lelong number at each point is the minimum of those of $u_1$ and $u_2$. Regularizing this current as in Step 1 gives a Kähler current with analytic singularities whose positive Lelong set is contained in the intersection of the two previous sets.

Step 3: realization. The positive Lelong sets of Step 1 are analytic subsets of $X$. By the descending chain condition for analytic subsets of a compact complex manifold, some finite intersection of them is minimal, and it then equals the full intersection $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. Repeated use of Step 2 gives a single Kähler current with analytic singularities whose polar set, which is also its positive Lelong set, is contained in this finite intersection. Since $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$ is contained in the positive Lelong set of every Kähler current in $\alpha $, equality holds.

Step 4: the two consequences. For a positive current $T\in \alpha $ and $\varepsilon >0$, we have $T\in \alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$, so minimality gives $\nu (T_{\min ,\varepsilon },x)\leq \nu (T,x)$. Taking the supremum over $\varepsilon $ and then the intersection over Kähler currents $T$ gives $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. If the non-Kähler locus is empty, the current of Step 3 has no poles and is therefore a Kähler form in $\alpha $. Conversely, if $\alpha $ contains a Kähler form, using it in the defining intersection gives $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\varnothing $. $\square$

Proposition 10.13.3.93492323D1A9 The non-Kähler locus of a big class has no isolated points.

Proof. Let $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi \geq \delta \omega $ be a current as in theorem 10.13.2, and suppose that $x$ is an isolated point of its polar set. Choose holomorphic coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots ,z_ n)$ on a ball centered at $x$ that contains no other pole. For a sufficiently large constant $A>0$ and any real constant $C$, the local function $q(z)=A|z|^2-C$ satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \delta '\omega $ on a smaller ball, for some $\delta '>0$.

Since $\Phi $ is smooth on a closed annular collar near the boundary of this smaller ball, we can choose $C$ so that $q<\Phi $ on the collar. Since $\Phi \to -\infty $ at $x$, the function $\max (\Phi ,q)$ agrees with $q$ near $x$ and with $\Phi $ on the collar. By lemma 5.24.2, it glues with $\Phi $ outside the ball to the potential of a Kähler current in $\alpha $ that is smooth near $x$. Its Lelong number at $x$ is zero, contradicting $x\in E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. $\square$

Bigness of a closed class has a strong global consequence. A compact complex manifold carries a Kähler current if and only if it belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$; this is the classical modification criterion discussed in section 25.18. Hence a compact complex manifold outside class $\mathcal C$ has no big Bott–Chern class at all, whereas the strictly positive nonclosed backgrounds studied here exist on every compact complex manifold.
