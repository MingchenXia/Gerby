6.11 Strong openness by extension from moving slicesF441BF47EB53

F441BF47EB53

The constant in the Ohsawa–Takegoshi theorem (theorem 6.5.4) depends only on the width of the normal direction, not on the weight. This uniformity allows us to extend a germ from a sequence of parallel hyperplanes approaching a point, with weights that vary along the sequence. If a germ were not in the expected ideal, we would obtain extensions whose norms are too small to be compatible with their behavior along a fixed holomorphic curve. The two lemmas below provide this curve and the corresponding norm estimate.

Lemma 6.11.1.642017869F45 Let $n\geq 1$, let $F,g_1,\ldots ,g_ r$, $r\geq 0$, be holomorphic functions near $0\in \mathbb C^ n$ such that the germ of $F$ at $0$ is not zero, and put $|g|=(\sum _{j=1}^ r|g_ j|^2)^{1/2}$, with $|g|=0$ if $r=0$. Suppose that there are no neighborhood of $0$ and constant $C$ such that $|F|\leq C|g|$ on that neighborhood. Then, after a linear change of coordinates, there are an integer $q\geq 1$, a constant $C$ and a holomorphic map

\[ \gamma (t)=(t^ q,\gamma _2(t),\ldots ,\gamma _ n(t)),\qquad |\gamma _ j(t)|\leq C|t|^ q, \]

defined on a disc around $0\in \mathbb C$, such that the function $F\circ \gamma $ has no zeros other than $t=0$, and each quotient $(g_ j/F)\circ \gamma $ extends holomorphically across $t=0$ with value $0$ there.

Proof. We first construct a nonconstant holomorphic map $\delta $ from a disc around $0\in \mathbb C$ to $\mathbb C^ n$ with $\delta (0)=0$ such that $F\circ \delta $ has no zeros other than $\tau =0$ and each $(g_ j/F)\circ \delta $ extends holomorphically across $\tau =0$ with value $0$.

If $r=0$, choose a vector $v$ such that $\zeta \mapsto F(\zeta v)$ does not vanish identically near $0$; for instance, $v$ may be any vector at which the first nonzero homogeneous term of $F$ at $0$ does not vanish. The zeros of this one-variable function are isolated, so on a small disc it vanishes at most at $0$, and $\delta (\tau )=\tau v$ has the required properties.

Let $r\geq 1$. The bound $|F|\leq i|g|$ fails on the ball $B(0,1/i)$ for every large integer $i$, so there are points $z_ i$ with $|z_ i|<1/i$ and $|F(z_ i)|>i|g(z_ i)|$; in particular $F(z_ i)\neq 0$ and $|g(z_ i)|/|F(z_ i)|<1/i$. We may take $z_ i\neq 0$: if $z_ i=0$, the strict inequality $|F|>i|g|$ at $0$ persists at points $z\neq 0$ near $0$, because $F$ and $|g|$ are continuous.

Let $U$ be a connected neighborhood of $0$ on which all functions are defined, and let $\sigma \colon \widetilde U\to U$ be the blowup of the ideal $(F,g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)$, which is not identically zero on $U$ (definition 9.6.1). Thus $\widetilde U\subset U\times \mathbb P^ r$ is the closure of the graph of $z\mapsto [F(z):g_1(z):\cdots :g_ r(z)]$ over the complement of the common zeros, and it is a complex space by proposition 9.6.2. The graph points $\tilde z_ i=(z_ i,[F(z_ i):g(z_ i)])$ converge to $b=(0,[1:0:\cdots :0])$, because $g(z_ i)/F(z_ i)\to 0$. On the part of $\widetilde U$ lying over the chart $\{ \xi _0\neq 0\} $ of $\mathbb P^ r$, the functions $\xi _ j/\xi _0$ are holomorphic and vanish at $b$, and the identities $g_ j\circ \sigma =(\xi _ j/\xi _0)(F\circ \sigma )$ hold on the graph, hence, both sides being continuous, on its closure, which is this part of $\widetilde U$.

The germ of $\widetilde U$ at $b$ is the union of finitely many irreducible branches (proposition 9.3.1); passing to a subsequence, we may assume that all $\tilde z_ i$ lie on a representative $W$ of one branch. The analytic subgerm of $(W,b)$ formed by $\sigma ^{-1}(0)\cup \{ F\circ \sigma =0\} $ is different from $(W,b)$, because $W$ contains the points $\tilde z_ i$, arbitrarily close to $b$, at which $z_ i\neq 0$ and $F(z_ i)\neq 0$; for the same reason $(W,b)$ is not the point $b$ and has positive dimension. By proposition 9.5.2 there is a nonconstant holomorphic map $c$ from a disc into $W$ with $c(0)=b$ such that $c(\tau )\notin \sigma ^{-1}(0)$ and $F(\sigma (c(\tau )))\neq 0$ for $\tau \neq 0$; shrinking the disc, we may assume that $c$ takes values over the chart $\{ \xi _0\neq 0\} $. Put $\delta =\sigma \circ c$. Then $\delta (0)=0$, and for $\tau \neq 0$ we have $\delta (\tau )\neq 0$ and $F(\delta (\tau ))\neq 0$; so $\delta $ is not constant, and $F\circ \delta $ has no zeros other than $\tau =0$. For $\tau \neq 0$ the identities above give $(g_ j/F)(\delta (\tau ))=(\xi _ j/\xi _0)(c(\tau ))$, and the right side is holomorphic on the whole disc with value $(\xi _ j/\xi _0)(b)=0$ at $\tau =0$.

Finally we choose the coordinates and the parameter. Let $q$ be the smallest vanishing order at $0$ of the coordinate functions of $\delta $; it is finite because $\delta $ is not constant, and $q\geq 1$ because $\delta (0)=0$. Then $\delta (\tau )=\tau ^ qe+O(\tau ^{q+1})$ with a vector $e\neq 0$. Choose a linear form $\ell $ with $\ell (e)\neq 0$ and linear coordinates in which $\ell $ is the first coordinate; then $\delta _1(\tau )=\tau ^ qu(\tau )$ with $u$ holomorphic and $u(0)=\ell (e)\neq 0$, while the other coordinates satisfy $|\delta _ j(\tau )|\leq C|\tau |^ q$ near $0$. On a disc around $0$ on which $u$ has no zeros, $u$ has a holomorphic logarithm $h$, and $\kappa =\mathrm e^{h/q}$ is a holomorphic $q$th root of $u$. The function $s(\tau )=\tau \kappa (\tau )$ satisfies $s'(0)=\kappa (0)\neq 0$, so by the holomorphic inverse function theorem in one variable (proposition 5.2.4(1) with $n=1$) it maps a neighborhood of $0$ biholomorphically onto a disc around $0$. Its inverse $s^{-1}$ is holomorphic and vanishes at $0$, so that $|s^{-1}(t)|\leq C'|t|$ after shrinking the disc. Put $\gamma =\delta \circ s^{-1}$ on this disc. Then $\gamma _1(t)=s^{-1}(t)^ qu(s^{-1}(t))=s(s^{-1}(t))^ q=t^ q$, and $|\gamma _ j(t)|\leq CC'^ q|t|^ q$ for $j\geq 2$. Since $s^{-1}$ maps the punctured disc onto a punctured neighborhood of $0$, $F\circ \gamma $ has no zeros other than $t=0$, and each $(g_ j/F)\circ \gamma =((g_ j/F)\circ \delta )\circ s^{-1}$ extends holomorphically across $t=0$ with value $0$. $\square$

Lemma 6.11.2.D7BCCD73BD13 Let $q\geq 1$, and let $\gamma (t)=(t^ q,\gamma _2(t),\ldots ,\gamma _ n(t))$ be a holomorphic map from a disc around $0\in \mathbb C$ to $\mathbb C^ n$ with $\gamma (0)=0$, for instance a map as in lemma 6.11.1. Let $\Omega =\Delta _ R\times P\subset \mathbb C\times \mathbb C^{n-1}$, where $\Delta _ R=\{ |z_1|<R\} $, $R>0$, and $P$ is a polydisc centered at $0$, and let $F$ be holomorphic on $\Omega $ with $F(\gamma (t))\neq 0$ for small $t\neq 0$. Then there are $r,c>0$, depending only on $\gamma $, $F$ and $\Omega $, with the following property. If $0<|a|<r$ and $H\in \mathcal O(\Omega )$ satisfies

\[ H(a,\cdot )=F(a,\cdot )\text{ on }P,\qquad (H/F)\circ \gamma \text{ is holomorphic at }0\text{ with value }0, \]

then

7264E59B5291 6.11.3
\begin{equation} \int _\Omega |H|^2\, \mathrm dV\geq c|a|^{-2}. \end{equation}

For $n=1$, the polydisc $P$ is a point and carries counting measure.

Proof. We bound the norm of $H$ from below by a weighted norm of $H\circ \gamma $ on a disc, and then use that $H\circ \gamma =F\circ \gamma $ at the points $t$ with $t^ q=a$.

Choose $\rho >0$ with $\rho ^ q<R$ such that $\gamma $ is defined on a neighborhood of the closed disc $|t|\leq \rho $, $F\circ \gamma $ has no zeros for $0<|t|\leq \rho $, and, since $\gamma $ is continuous with $\gamma (0)=0$, $(\gamma _2(t),\ldots ,\gamma _ n(t))$ lies in a fixed polydisc $P'\Subset P$ centered at $0$ for $|t|\leq \rho $. Let $\delta >0$ be half the distance from $P'$ to the complement of $P$. For $0<|z_1|<\rho ^ q$, each of the $q$ points $\gamma (t)$ with $t^ q=z_1$ has the form $(z_1,w)$ with $w\in P'$, and (6.8.7) for the holomorphic function $H(z_1,\cdot )$ on the ball $B(w,\delta )\subset P$ gives $|H(z_1,w)|^2\leq C_\delta \int _ P|H(z_1,w')|^2\, \mathrm dV(w')$, where $C_\delta ^{-1}$ is the volume of a ball of radius $\delta $ in $\mathbb C^{n-1}$. Summing over the $q$ points, we obtain $\sum _{t^ q=z_1}|H(\gamma (t))|^2\leq qC_\delta \int _ P|H(z_1,w')|^2\, \mathrm dV(w')$.

Now we integrate over $|z_1|<\rho ^ q$. The map $t\mapsto t^ q$ maps each of the $q$ half-open sectors $\{ 0<|t|<\rho ,\ 2\pi k/q\leq \arg t<2\pi (k+1)/q\} $ bijectively onto the punctured disc $0<|z_1|<\rho ^ q$, and on the interior of the sector it is a diffeomorphism onto the complement of a ray, with real Jacobian $|qt^{q-1}|^2$. By the change of variables formula, applied on each sector, $\int _{|t|<\rho }\Phi (t)q^2|t|^{2q-2}\, \mathrm dV_2(t)=\int _{|z_1|<\rho ^ q}\sum _{t^ q=z_1}\Phi (t)\, \mathrm dV_2(z_1)$ for every nonnegative measurable $\Phi $. Since $\Delta _{\rho ^ q}\times P\subset \Omega $, it follows that

\[ \int _\Omega |H|^2\, \mathrm dV \geq c_0\int _{|t|<\rho }|H(\gamma (t))|^2 q^2|t|^{2q-2}\, \mathrm dV_2(t) \]

with $c_0=1/(qC_\delta )$. If $n=1$, the same inequality holds with $c_0=1/q$, without the submean step.

Write $F\circ \gamma (t)=t^ mv(t)$ with an integer $m\geq 0$ and $v$ without zeros on $|t|\leq \rho $, and put $h=(H/F)\circ \gamma $. This function is holomorphic for $0<|t|<\rho $, because $F\circ \gamma $ has no zeros there, and by hypothesis it extends holomorphically across $0$ with $h(0)=0$; thus $h(t)=\sum _{\ell \geq 1}h_\ell t^\ell $ for $|t|<\rho $. Then $H\circ \gamma =t^ mvh$, and $|v|$ is bounded below by a positive constant on the compact disc $|t|\leq \rho $, being continuous and without zeros there. On each circle $|t|=s<\rho $ the power series of $h$ converges uniformly, and the monomials $t^\ell $ are orthogonal there, so $\int _0^{2\pi }|h(s\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta })|^2\, \mathrm d\theta =2\pi \sum _\ell |h_\ell |^2s^{2\ell }$. Integrating in polar coordinates and using the monotone convergence theorem, we obtain

\[ \int _\Omega |H|^2\, \mathrm dV \geq c_1\sum _{\ell \geq 1} \frac{|h_\ell |^2\rho ^{2(\ell +m+q)}}{\ell +m+q} \]

with $c_1=\pi q^2c_0\min _{|t|\leq \rho }|v(t)|^2$. Now let $0<|a|<\rho ^ q$. For each of the $q$ roots $b$ of $b^ q=a$, the point $\gamma (b)$ lies on the slice $\{ z_1=a\} $, where $H=F$, and $F(\gamma (b))\neq 0$; hence $h(b)=1$. The average $q^{-1}\sum _{b^ q=a}b^\ell $ equals $a^{\ell /q}$ if $q$ divides $\ell $ and $0$ otherwise, so averaging $h$ over these roots gives $\sum _{j\geq 1}h_{qj}a^ j=1$. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\[ 1\leq c_1^{-1}\| H\| _{L^2(\Omega )}^2 \sum _{j\geq 1}\frac{(qj+m+q)|a|^{2j}}{\rho ^{2(qj+m+q)}}. \]

If $|a|\leq \rho ^ q/2$, the last sum equals $\rho ^{-2(m+q)}\sum _{j\geq 1}(qj+m+q)x^ j$ with $x=|a|^2\rho ^{-2q}\leq 1/4$, and this series is at most a constant times $x$. Hence (6.11.3) holds for $0<|a|<r=\rho ^ q/2$, with $c$ independent of $a$ and $H$. $\square$

Theorem 6.11.4.FF9DF4BE3CA8 Let $(\psi _ j)_{j\geq 1}$ be an increasing sequence of weights on $X$ that converges almost everywhere to a weight $\varphi $. Then at every $x\in X$,

CF22C9DDEA36 6.11.5
\begin{equation} \bigcup _ j\mathcal I(\psi _ j)_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x, \end{equation}

and $\mathcal I(\psi _ j)_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ for all sufficiently large $j$; the required index may depend on $x$ and on the sequence.

Proof. Since the $\psi _ j$ increase to $\varphi $ almost everywhere, $\psi _ j\leq \varphi $ almost everywhere. The plurisubharmonic functions $\max \{ \psi _ j,\varphi \} $ and $\varphi $ therefore agree almost everywhere, hence everywhere by theorem 5.9.1; that is, $\psi _ j\leq \varphi $ everywhere. Consequently $\mathcal I(\psi _ j)\subset \mathcal I(\varphi )$ for every $j$, and the left side of (6.11.5) is contained in the right side. We prove the reverse inclusion by induction on the dimension $n$ of $X$ at $x$. If $n=0$, all the weights are finite at $x$ and all the ideals equal $\mathcal O_{X,x}$. Let $n\geq 1$, assume the theorem in dimension $n-1$, and take $x=0$ in a coordinate chart. We argue by contradiction, extending a germ that is missing from $\bigcup _ j\mathcal I(\psi _ j)_0$ from slices $\{ z_1=a_ i\} $ with $a_ i\to 0$, as outlined at the beginning of this section.

Step 1: reduction to a curve. The union $J=\bigcup _ j\mathcal I(\psi _ j)_0$ is an ideal, because the ideals increase with $j$. By proposition 9.2.4 it is generated by finitely many germs $g_1,\ldots ,g_ k$, $k\geq 0$, and these all belong to $\mathcal I(\psi _{j_0})_0$ for some $j_0$. Suppose, to reach a contradiction, that some $F\in \mathcal I(\varphi )_0$ does not belong to $J$; in particular $F\neq 0$. Choose representatives of $F$ and of the $g_ l$ on a neighborhood $U$ of $0$ on which $|g_ l|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi _{j_0}}$ and $|F|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi }$ are integrable. If $|F|\leq C|g|$, with $|g|=(\sum _ l|g_ l|^2)^{1/2}$, held on a neighborhood of $0$, then $F\in \mathcal I(\psi _{j_0})_0\subset J$ by proposition 6.10.6; so no such bound holds. Lemma 6.11.1 now provides linear coordinates and a curve $\gamma (t)=(t^ q,\gamma _2(t),\ldots ,\gamma _ n(t))$ such that $F\circ \gamma $ has no zeros for small $t\neq 0$ and each $(g_ l/F)\circ \gamma $ extends holomorphically with value $0$ at $t=0$. In these coordinates choose polydiscs $\Delta _{R_0}\times P_0\Subset U$ and $\Omega =\Delta _ R\times P\Subset \Delta _{R_0}\times P_0$ centered at $0$, and let $r,c>0$ be the constants of lemma 6.11.2 for $\gamma $, $F$ and $\Omega $. Subtracting the same constant from $\varphi $ and all $\psi _ j$, we may assume that $\varphi \leq 0$ on $\Delta _{R_0}\times P_0$; then also $\psi _ j\leq 0$ there.

Step 2: choice of slices. By Fubini's theorem, the function

\[ G(a)=\int _{P_0}|F(a,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi (a,w)}\, \mathrm dV_ w \]

is integrable on $\Delta _{R_0}$. Hence, given any null set $N\subset \Delta _{R_0}$, there is a sequence of points $a_ i\notin N$, $a_ i\neq 0$, with $a_ i\to 0$ and $|a_ i|^2G(a_ i)\to 0$; otherwise $G(a)\geq c'|a|^{-2}$ for almost every $a$ in some punctured disc around $0$, and $G$ would not be integrable. We take for $N$ the union of three sets: the set where $G=\infty $; the set of $a$ for which one of the countably many functions $\varphi (a,\cdot )$, $\psi _ j(a,\cdot )$ is identically $-\infty $ on $P_0$; and the set of $a$ for which $\psi _ j(a,\cdot )\to \varphi (a,\cdot )$ fails on a subset of $P_0$ of positive measure. The first set is null because $G$ is integrable, and the other two are null by Fubini's theorem, because pole sets of weights have measure zero and $\psi _ j\to \varphi $ almost everywhere. Then the functions $\psi _ j(a_ i,\cdot )$ and $\varphi (a_ i,\cdot )$ are weights on $P_0$ (proposition 5.9.3), and the former increase to the latter almost everywhere on $P_0$. Moreover $G(a_ i)>0$ for all large $i$, because $F(a_ i,\cdot )$ does not vanish identically on $P_0$: for small $|a_ i|$, the slice $\{ a_ i\} \times P_0$ contains the points $\gamma (b)$ with $b^ q=a_ i$, at which $F\neq 0$. Discarding finitely many terms, we assume that this holds for all $i$.

Step 3: integrability on a fixed inner slice. Fix $i$. Since $G(a_ i)<\infty $, the germ of $F(a_ i,\cdot )$ at every point of $P_0$ belongs to $\mathcal I(\varphi (a_ i,\cdot ))$. By the induction hypothesis, applied on $P_0$, each point of the compact set $\overline P\subset P_0$ has a neighborhood on which $|F(a_ i,\cdot )|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi _ j(a_ i,\cdot )}$ is integrable for some $j$. Covering $\overline P$ by finitely many such neighborhoods and using that the $\psi _ j$ increase, we find one index $j$ for which $\int _ P|F(a_ i,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi _ j(a_ i,w)}\, \mathrm dV_ w<\infty $. For larger $j$ these integrands decrease and converge almost everywhere to $|F(a_ i,\cdot )|^2\mathrm e^{-\varphi (a_ i,\cdot )}$, so by dominated convergence their integrals over $P$ converge to a number at most $G(a_ i)$. Since $G(a_ i)>0$, we can choose an index $j_ i$ with

\[ \int _ P|F(a_ i,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi _{j_ i}(a_ i,w)}\, \mathrm dV_ w \leq 2G(a_ i). \]

The polydisc $P$ does not depend on $i$; this is what makes the estimates of the next step uniform.

Step 4: extension and contradiction. For large $i$ we have $|a_ i|<\min \{ r,R\} $, so that the normal coordinate $z_1-a_ i$ takes values in a disc of radius $2R$ on $\Omega $. The polydisc $\Omega $ is pseudoconvex by corollary 6.3.5, being a product of discs, each of which is a sublevel set of a function $|\zeta -\zeta _0|^2$ on $\mathbb C$. Applying theorem 6.5.4, after reordering the coordinates, with the normal coordinate $z_1-a_ i$, the radius $2R$ and the weight $\psi _{j_ i}$, and using the last display of Step 3, we obtain $H_ i\in \mathcal O(\Omega )$ with $H_ i(a_ i,\cdot )=F(a_ i,\cdot )$ on $P$ and

\[ \int _\Omega |H_ i|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi _{j_ i}}\, \mathrm dV\leq 8C_0R^2G(a_ i), \]

where $C_0$ is the absolute constant of that theorem. In particular the germ of $H_ i$ at $0$ lies in $\mathcal I(\psi _{j_ i})_0\subset J$, so $H_ i=\sum _ lb_ lg_ l$ near $0$ with holomorphic $b_ l$, and $(H_ i/F)\circ \gamma =\sum _ l(b_ l\circ \gamma )\cdot (g_ l/F)\circ \gamma $ extends holomorphically across $t=0$ with value $0$. Since $\psi _{j_ i}\leq 0$, the last display also bounds $\int _\Omega |H_ i|^2\, \mathrm dV$. Now lemma 6.11.2 gives $c|a_ i|^{-2}\leq 8C_0R^2G(a_ i)$ for all large $i$, contradicting $|a_ i|^2G(a_ i)\to 0$. Hence $F\in J$, which proves (6.11.5).

Finally, $\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ is generated by finitely many germs, which by (6.11.5) all lie in $\mathcal I(\psi _ j)_ x$ for one index $j$. Then $\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x\subset \mathcal I(\psi _ j)_ x$, and equality holds for this and every later index, since the ideals increase and are contained in $\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$. $\square$

Corollary 6.11.6 (Strong openness).EA6067858516 Let $\varphi $ and $\chi $ be weights on $X$, and let $x\in X$. Then

B5400B2F91DA 6.11.7
\begin{equation} \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x=\bigcup _{\varepsilon >0} \mathcal I((1+\varepsilon )\varphi )_ x \end{equation}

and, more generally,

\[ \mathcal I(\varphi )_ x=\bigcup _{\varepsilon >0} \mathcal I(\varphi +\varepsilon \chi )_ x. \]

Moreover, there is a single $\varepsilon >0$ with $\mathcal I((1+\varepsilon )\varphi )_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$, and similarly a single $\varepsilon >0$ with $\mathcal I(\varphi +\varepsilon \chi )_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$.

Proof. The assertions are local. On a relatively compact neighborhood of $x$ the weights are bounded above, and subtracting constants we may assume $\varphi \leq 0$ and $\chi \leq 0$ there; this changes $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi $ and $\varphi +\varepsilon \chi $ only by bounded amounts and hence changes none of the ideals. Then $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi $ and $\varphi +\varepsilon \chi $ decrease as $\varepsilon $ increases, so the corresponding ideals decrease with $\varepsilon $ and are contained in $\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$. The sequences $(1+1/j)\varphi $ and $\varphi +\chi /j$ increase with $j$ and converge to $\varphi $ wherever $\varphi $ and $\chi $ are finite, hence almost everywhere. By theorem 6.11.4, $\mathcal I((1+1/j)\varphi )_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ and $\mathcal I(\varphi +\chi /j)_ x=\mathcal I(\varphi )_ x$ for all large $j$, which proves both assertions. $\square$

Example 6.11.8.769FF9D2A9B9 Let $c\geq 0$ and $\varphi =c\log |z|^2$ on a disc around $0\in \mathbb C$, where $\varphi =0$ if $c=0$. A germ of order $k$ at $0$ is $z^ k$ times a unit, so by polar coordinates it belongs to $\mathcal I(\varphi )_0$ if and only if $\int _0^ rt^{2k-2c+1}\, \mathrm dt<\infty $, that is, $k+1>c$. Hence $\mathcal I(\varphi )=(z^{\lfloor c\rfloor })$. When $c$ is a positive integer, the germ $z^{c-1}$ just fails, because the radial integral diverges logarithmically. This strict threshold shows why theorem 6.11.4 concerns increasing sequences: on the unit disc, the weights $\varphi _ j=(1-1/j)\log |z|^2$, $j\geq 2$, decrease to $\log |z|^2$, yet $\mathcal I(\varphi _ j)=\mathcal O$ for every $j$, whereas $\mathcal I(\log |z|^2)=(z)$.
