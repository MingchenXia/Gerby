10.3 Gauduchon metrics and linear mass

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

4 Gauduchon metrics and linear mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Throughout this chapter, $\omega $ is a fixed Hermitian form on $X$, and $n\geq 2$ unless a section specifies another setting.

The mass variation in chapter 2 leaves a linear conservation law. If $\gamma $ is a Gauduchon metric, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\gamma ^{n-1})=0$, integration against $\gamma ^{n-1}$ annihilates every complex Hessian. Thus the trace mass $\int _ X(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ of a positive current depends only on its smooth background $\theta $, even when $u$ is unbounded. Gauduchon's theorem produces such a metric in every conformal class, providing conserved pairings when the background is not closed.

These pairings determine positivity as well as mass. A smooth form $\theta $ admits a positive current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ exactly when all its Gauduchon degrees are nonnegative. Normalizing the degrees gives the largest lower curvature bound obtainable by adding a complex Hessian. This bound is always attained by a potential; a minimizing test exists in the weak closure of the normalized smooth Gauduchon forms, although a smooth minimizing metric generally does not exist. Applied to $\omega _ u$, the conformal construction also gives the adjoint probability of the linearized Monge–Ampère operator. It identifies the compatibility condition for that operator and explains the extra term in entropy dissipation for a nonclosed background.

For a holomorphic line bundle, the conserved pairing is the degree of its curvature, and a positive degree excludes a metric with nonpositive curvature. Fiber averaging extends this obstruction to plurisubharmonic norms near a direct sum of line bundles. Polar duality and homogeneous regularization then turn it into an obstruction to semipositive Finsler curvature. The stable ample bundles constructed on Hirzebruch surfaces show that ampleness alone does not remove the obstruction; their projective complements give a geometric way to measure it.

The same scalar equation can improve curvature when the mean curvature is nonnegative: a conformal change spreads directional positivity from a nonempty open set to the whole manifold. Ricci-flat Kähler surfaces illustrate the distinction between this directional condition and positivity on every tangent and fiber pair. On a projective manifold, positivity on every such pair admits another duality criterion, expressed by positive measures over the dual bundle.

4.1 Conformal Gauduchon metrics

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

By proposition 2.2.3, the first variation of the mass at $u=0$ vanishes exactly when $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-1})=0$. This condition can be achieved in every conformal class: finding a conformal factor amounts to finding a positive generator of the kernel of the adjoint of a scalar elliptic operator.

Definition 4.1.1. A Hermitian form $\gamma $ is Gauduchon, or a Gauduchon metric, if

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\gamma ^{n-1})=0. \]

It is balanced if $\mathrm d(\gamma ^{n-1})=0$, and pluriclosed if $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\gamma =0$.

Theorem 4.1.2 (Gauduchon's theorem). Every Hermitian form $\eta $ has a conformal Gauduchon representative. More precisely, there is a smooth strictly positive function $\rho $, unique up to multiplication by a positive constant, such that

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \eta ^{n-1})=0. \]

The associated Gauduchon form is $\gamma =\rho ^{1/(n-1)}\eta $.

Proof. For a smooth real function $v$ on $X$, define

\[ Lv=n\frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \eta ^{n-1}}{\eta ^ n}. \]

This real second-order operator has positive definite principal symbol and no zeroth-order term. By the maximum principle, its kernel consists of the constants. Its index is zero, since the linear path joining it to a Laplace operator consists of elliptic operators. Fredholm theory and elliptic regularity therefore show that the formal adjoint $L^*$ of $L$ with respect to $\eta ^ n$ has a one-dimensional kernel, generated by a nonzero smooth real function $\rho $.

We first prove that $\int _ X\rho \eta ^ n\ne 0$. Otherwise the constant function $1$ would be orthogonal to the kernel of $L^*$, and the Fredholm alternative would solve $Lv=1$, which is impossible at a maximum point of $v$. We may therefore rescale so that $\int _ X\rho \eta ^ n=1$.

Next we show that $\rho \geq 0$. Suppose instead that $\rho $ is negative somewhere. Choose a nonzero smooth function $h\geq 0$ supported where $\rho <0$, and put $c=\int _ Xh\rho \eta ^ n<0$. The function $h-c$ is strictly positive and orthogonal to the kernel of $L^*$, so the Fredholm alternative would solve $Lv=h-c>0$, which is again impossible at a maximum point of $v$.

In real local coordinates $(x_1,\ldots ,x_{2n})$, write $L^*=A^{ij}\partial _ i\partial _ j+B^ i\partial _ i+C$, where $\partial _ i=\partial /\partial x_ i$, the coefficients are smooth, and repeated indices are summed from $1$ to $2n$. The leading symbol $(A^{ij})$ is positive definite. Since $\rho \geq 0$,

\[ (L^*-\max (C,0))\rho =-\max (C,0)\rho \leq 0, \]

and the operator $L^*-\max (C,0)$ has nonpositive zeroth-order term. By the strong minimum principle, a zero of $\rho $ would force $\rho $ to vanish identically on the connected manifold $X$. Hence $\rho >0$.

Finally, integration by parts gives

\[ \int _ X\rho Lv\, \eta ^ n =n\int _ Xv\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \eta ^{n-1}). \]

Thus the equation $L^*\rho =0$ is precisely $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \eta ^{n-1})=0$, and the one-dimensional adjoint kernel gives uniqueness up to positive scaling. Since $\gamma ^{n-1}=\rho \eta ^{n-1}$, the form $\gamma $ is Gauduchon. $\square$

A Kähler form, that is, a closed Hermitian form, is both balanced and pluriclosed, and every balanced form is Gauduchon. On surfaces, balanced forms are Kähler, and the Gauduchon and pluriclosed conditions coincide.

4.2 Conservation of the trace mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The power $\gamma ^{n-1}$ of a Gauduchon metric annihilates every complex Hessian under integration. It therefore assigns to a positive current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ a mass depending only on its smooth background $\theta $, also when the potential $u$ is unbounded.

Proposition 4.2.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, let $u$ be a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function, and let $\gamma $ be a Gauduchon metric. Then

4.1
\begin{equation} \int _ X(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}. \end{equation}

Proof. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\gamma ^{n-1})=0$, distributional integration by parts gives

\[ \langle \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u,\gamma ^{n-1}\rangle =\langle u,\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\gamma ^{n-1})\rangle =0. \]

The product on the left of (4.1) is a positive measure, whose total mass is therefore the finite number on the right. $\square$

We call $\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ the Gauduchon degree of $\theta $ with respect to $\gamma $. Here $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is the full distributional current, including any mass carried by the pole set of $u$. The non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of chapter 6 discards this mass; example 7.5.1 illustrates the difference on a curve.

Corollary 4.2.2. There are constants $0<a\leq b<+\infty $, depending only on $\omega $ and a chosen Gauduchon metric, such that every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ satisfies

\[ a\leq \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge \omega ^{n-1}\leq b. \]

Proof. Fix a Gauduchon form $\gamma $ on $X$ and choose positive constants $c,C$ with $c\gamma ^{n-1}\leq \omega ^{n-1}\leq C\gamma ^{n-1}$. Wedging these inequalities with the positive current $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and integrating, we obtain, by proposition 4.2.1, the bounds $cA$ and $CA$, where $A=\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}>0$. $\square$

The trace bound controls the mass of positive currents and hence their weak compactness. By itself it gives no bound for the Monge–Ampère mass, since a bound on the integral of a trace does not bound the integral of its $n$th power.

The pointwise arithmetic–geometric mean inequality relates the conserved trace to the density in a smooth Monge–Ampère equation. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ on $X$.

Proposition 4.2.3. For any two Hermitian forms $\eta ,\gamma $,

4.2
\begin{equation} \eta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} \geq \left(\frac{\eta ^ n}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n \end{equation}

pointwise. Consequently, let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $f$ a smooth strictly positive function. If $u\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$ and $c>0$ satisfy $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=c f\, \mathrm{d}V$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$, then

4.3
\begin{equation} c^{1/n} \int _ X\left(\frac{f\, \mathrm{d}V}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n \leq \int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} \end{equation}

for every Gauduchon metric $\gamma $.

Proof. In a frame where $\gamma $ is the identity, let $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ n>0$ be the eigenvalues of $\eta $. The ratios of the two sides of (4.2) to $\gamma ^ n$ are $n^{-1}\sum _ j\lambda _ j$ and $(\prod _ j\lambda _ j)^{1/n}$, respectively, and their comparison is the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality.

For the second assertion, apply (4.2) with $\eta =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$, integrate, and use (4.1). $\square$

The corresponding density inequality for unbounded potentials in a Hermitian background is proved in lemma 6.4.2.

4.3 The Gauduchon criterion for positivity

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The trace identity gives a necessary condition for $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ to be nonempty: every Gauduchon degree of $\theta $ must be nonnegative. The condition is also sufficient. A Hahn–Banach separation produces a positive current with the prescribed pairings, and a scalar elliptic equation shows that its difference from $\theta $ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact.

Theorem 4.3.1 (The generalized Lamari criterion). For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $, the following conditions are equivalent:

  1. There exists $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

  2. $\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}\geq 0$ for every Gauduchon metric $\gamma $.

To apply separation, we express the test in terms of smooth strictly positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-forms $\Gamma $. Every strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Gamma $ has a unique strictly positive $(1,1)$-root $\gamma $, that is, $\Gamma =\gamma ^{n-1}$. Indeed, in a frame in which $\Gamma $ is diagonal, and with the same wedge normalization on both sides, the coefficients $g_ i$ of $\Gamma $ are the products $\prod _{j\neq i}\lambda _ j$ of the eigenvalues $\lambda _ j$ of $\gamma $. Hence

\[ \lambda _ i=\frac{(\prod _ jg_ j)^{1/(n-1)}}{g_ i}. \]

This spectral formula uses only a positive determinant and a matrix inverse, so the root depends smoothly on the coefficients of $\Gamma $, also at repeated eigenvalues, and pointwise uniqueness makes the local roots agree on overlaps. Consequently, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma =0$ says exactly that the root $\gamma $ is Gauduchon.

of theorem 4.3.1. Necessity is proposition 4.2.1. For sufficiency, let $F$ be the real vector space of smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-forms, and let

\[ E=\{ \Gamma \in F:\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma =0\} ,\qquad \ell (\Gamma )=\int _ X\theta \wedge \Gamma \quad (\Gamma \in E). \]

Fix a strictly positive $\Gamma _0\in E$, which exists by theorem 4.1.2. By the root calculation above, the hypothesis says that $\ell $ is nonnegative on the strictly positive elements of $E$. Adding $\epsilon \Gamma _0$ and letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, we see that $\ell $ is also nonnegative on the semipositive elements of $E$.

Step 1: a positive extension of $\ell $. For $\Phi \in F$ put

\[ p(\Phi )=\inf \{ \ell (\Gamma ):\Gamma \in E,\ \Gamma \geq \Phi \} . \]

This is a finite sublinear function. To see finiteness, choose $C$ with $-C\Gamma _0\leq \Phi \leq C\Gamma _0$. The admissible element $C\Gamma _0$ gives an upper bound, while every admissible $\Gamma $ satisfies $\Gamma +C\Gamma _0\geq 0$ and hence $\ell (\Gamma )\geq -C\ell (\Gamma _0)$. Sublinearity follows by adding and rescaling competitors. For $\Phi \in E$, the nonnegativity of $\ell (\Gamma -\Phi )$ for admissible $\Gamma $ shows that $p(\Phi )=\ell (\Phi )$.

The Hahn–Banach theorem gives a real linear extension $T$ of $\ell $ to $F$ with $T(\Phi )\leq p(\Phi )$ for all $\Phi \in F$. If $\Phi \geq 0$, the zero form is admissible for $p(-\Phi )$, so $T(-\Phi )\leq 0$, that is, $T(\Phi )\geq 0$. Moreover, the bounds by $C\Gamma _0$ show that $T$ is continuous for the uniform norm. It is therefore a positive $(1,1)$-current, and the current $S=T-\theta $ annihilates $E$.

Step 2: $S$ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact. To recover a potential from $S$, consider the scalar operator

\[ Dv=\frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v\Gamma _0)}{\mathrm{d}V}, \]

where $\mathrm dV$ is the smooth positive volume form fixed above. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _0=0$, this is an elliptic scalar operator with no zeroth-order term. Its kernel consists of the constants and its index is zero, while Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ XDv\, \mathrm{d}V=0$. Consequently its range is exactly the space of smooth functions of integral zero, and by elliptic estimates its inverse on functions normalized by $\int _ Xv\, \mathrm{d}V=0$ is continuous in the smooth topology.

For a smooth top-degree form $\zeta $ with $\int _ X\zeta =0$, solve $Dv=\zeta /\mathrm{d}V$ with this normalization and set $R\zeta =v\Gamma _0$. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R\zeta =\zeta $, and $R$ is continuous. Define a distribution $u$ on such top-degree forms by

\[ \langle u,\zeta \rangle =S(R\zeta ), \]

and extend it to all top-degree forms by assigning an arbitrary value on one fixed volume form of integral one. For every $\Phi \in F$, the form $\Phi -R(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi )$ belongs to $E$, and $S$ annihilates it. It follows that

\[ \langle \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u,\Phi \rangle =S(R(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi ))=S(\Phi ). \]

Thus $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=T\geq 0$, and proposition 1.1.2 gives a representative of $u$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. $\square$

Applying the criterion to $\theta -a\omega $, for $a\in \mathbb R$, determines the largest lower curvature bound attainable in the background $\theta $.

Corollary 4.3.2. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $, set

\[ \kappa _\omega (\theta )= \inf _{\gamma \ \mathrm{Gauduchon}} \frac{\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}}{\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}}. \]

This is a finite real number, and it is the largest real $a$ for which there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ satisfying

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq a\omega . \]

In particular, this largest value is attained, and $\theta $ is big, in the sense of section 1.19, precisely when $\kappa _\omega (\theta )>0$.

Proof. Since $\theta $ is smooth, $-C\omega \leq \theta \leq C\omega $ for some $C$, so the infimum lies in $[-C,C]$. If $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq a\omega $, the trace identity applied to the background $\theta -a\omega $ shows that $a$ is at most each of the ratios in the definition, hence $a\leq \kappa _\omega (\theta )$. Conversely, for $a=\kappa _\omega (\theta )$, all Gauduchon degrees of $\theta -a\omega $ are nonnegative, and theorem 4.3.1 applied to this smooth form gives the required $u$. The last assertion follows, since bigness means the existence of a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic $u$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \delta \omega $ for some $\delta >0$. $\square$

Whether the infimum defining $\kappa _\omega (\theta )$ is attained by a smooth Gauduchon metric is decided by a rigidity property of the trace: a positive current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with vanishing Gauduchon degree is zero.

Lemma 4.3.3. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form with $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\ne \varnothing $. If

\[ \int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=0 \]

for one Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, then $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ for a smooth real function $f$. Conversely, a form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ with $f$ smooth has zero degree with respect to every Gauduchon metric. The same conclusions hold for $n=1$, with $\gamma ^{n-1}=1$.

Proof. Choose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and put $S=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$. By proposition 4.2.1, $\int _ XS\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=0$. Since the trace measure $S\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ of the positive current $S$ controls all its coefficient measures, $S=0$. Taking the trace of the equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=-\theta $ with respect to $\omega $ gives an elliptic equation with smooth right-hand side, so $u$ is smooth by elliptic regularity, and $f=-u$ has the required property. The converse is Stokes' theorem. For $n=1$, the trace measure is $S$ itself and the same argument applies. $\square$

For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $, put $a=\kappa _\omega (\theta )$. By corollary 4.3.2, the form $\theta -a\omega $ admits a positive current $\theta -a\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. A Gauduchon metric $\gamma $ attains the infimum defining $\kappa _\omega (\theta )$ precisely when the Gauduchon degree of $\theta -a\omega $ with respect to $\gamma $ vanishes. Hence lemma 4.3.3 shows that the infimum is attained by a smooth Gauduchon metric if and only if

\[ \theta -a\omega =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\quad \text{for some }f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R), \]

and in that case every Gauduchon metric attains it. Thus a positive current at the threshold always exists, whereas a smooth minimizing Gauduchon metric exists only in this exceptional situation.

4.4 Compactness and duality of Gauduchon currents

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

When no smooth Gauduchon metric attains the threshold, a minimizer can still be found among weak limits of normalized Gauduchon forms. The normalization bounds the coefficient measures and hence gives compactness. Let $\mathscr G_\omega $ be the set of positive $(n-1,n-1)$-currents $T$ on $X$ satisfying

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0,\qquad \int _ X\omega \wedge T=1. \]

We call them normalized Gauduchon currents. Let $\mathscr C_\omega $ be the weak closure of the set of smooth strictly positive members of $\mathscr G_\omega $. By the root calculation preceding the proof of theorem 4.3.1, these smooth members are exactly the normalized powers $\gamma ^{n-1}/\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ of Gauduchon metrics $\gamma $.

Proposition 4.4.1. The sets $\mathscr G_\omega $ and $\mathscr C_\omega $ are nonempty, compact and convex. For every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $,

4.4
\begin{equation} \kappa _\omega (\theta ) =\min _{T\in \mathscr C_\omega }\int _ X\theta \wedge T. \end{equation}

Moreover, a current $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ belongs to $\mathscr C_\omega $ if and only if

4.5
\begin{equation} \int _ X\theta \wedge T\geq 0 \quad \text{for every smooth real }\theta \text{ with }\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\ne \varnothing . \end{equation}

Proof. Gauduchon's theorem gives a smooth strictly positive member of $\mathscr G_\omega $ after normalization. The fixed trace mass bounds the total variation of all coefficient measures in finitely many local frames, so weak compactness of finite measures gives compactness of the family of positive currents with this trace. Positivity, the trace normalization and the equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$ pass to weak limits. On this bounded set, testing against smooth forms or against forms with continuous coefficients gives the same topology, by uniform approximation, and this topology is metrizable since the space of continuous test forms is separable. Thus the compactness can also be used sequentially. The defining conditions of $\mathscr G_\omega $ are convex. Its smooth strictly positive members form a convex set, so their closure $\mathscr C_\omega $ is compact and convex as well.

Pairing with a fixed smooth $\theta $ is weakly continuous. Its minimum on the closure of the smooth members is therefore their infimum, which proves (4.4). The Lamari criterion shows that (4.5) holds for every smooth strictly positive member, and hence for every member of their weak closure.

Conversely, suppose that $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ does not belong to $\mathscr C_\omega $, and separate this point from the compact convex set $\mathscr C_\omega $ in the space of currents with the weak topology. A continuous linear functional for that topology is evaluation against a smooth form. After changing its sign if necessary, we obtain a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta $ such that

\[ \int _ X\beta \wedge T <\min _{R\in \mathscr C_\omega }\int _ X\beta \wedge R =\kappa _\omega (\beta ). \]

By corollary 4.3.2, the smooth form $\beta -\kappa _\omega (\beta )\omega $ admits a positive current. Its pairing with $T$ is negative, because $\int _ X\omega \wedge T=1$. This contradicts (4.5) and proves the converse. $\square$

Corollary 4.4.2. The function $\kappa _\omega $ is concave, positively homogeneous and invariant under the addition of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ for $f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. Let $\theta $ and $\eta $ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms and let $\varepsilon \geq 0$. If $-\varepsilon \omega \leq \theta -\eta \leq \varepsilon \omega $, then

\[ |\kappa _\omega (\theta )-\kappa _\omega (\eta )|\leq \varepsilon . \]

If smooth real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta _ j$ converge uniformly to a smooth form $\theta $ and $T_ j\in \mathscr C_\omega $ minimize their pairings, every weak cluster point of $(T_ j)$ minimizes the pairing with $\theta $.

Proof. By (4.4), $\kappa _\omega $ is an infimum of linear functionals, which proves concavity and positive homogeneity. Invariance under the addition of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ follows from $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$. The normalization of the trace gives

\[ \left|\int _ X(\theta -\eta )\wedge T\right|\leq \varepsilon \quad (T\in \mathscr C_\omega ), \]

and taking infima proves the estimate. For the last assertion, pass to a subsequence $T_ j\rightharpoonup T\in \mathscr C_\omega $. The estimate and the uniform convergence of the backgrounds give

\[ \int _ X\theta \wedge T =\lim _ j\int _ X\theta _ j\wedge T_ j =\lim _ j\kappa _\omega (\theta _ j)=\kappa _\omega (\theta ). \]

Thus $T$ is a minimizer. $\square$

In general, the closure $\mathscr C_\omega $ cannot be replaced by all of $\mathscr G_\omega $: the exceptional-curve example in example 28.8.2 separates the two sets by a smooth closed form. Testing against all of $\mathscr G_\omega $ instead detects smooth positivity and nefness, as shown in theorem 26.20.1.

4.5 Transverse concentration of Gauduchon tests

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $\mathcal T_\omega $ be the smooth strictly positive members of the normalized current cone $\mathscr G_\omega $ of section 4.4. Thus every $\Theta \in \mathcal T_\omega $ satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Theta =0$ and $\int _ X\omega \wedge \Theta =1$. These tests form a weakly relatively compact family, but their pairings with a form concentrating transversely to a hypersurface need not stay bounded. A uniform bound would, by duality, produce a potential whose curvature charges the local hypersurface. Analyticity of Lelong level sets then forces a global hypersurface.

Choose a product coordinate chart $(z,\zeta )\in U\times \Delta _ r$, with $z\in \mathbb C^{n-1}$, and a smooth compactly supported $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ on $U$, equal to one on a nonempty connected smaller polydisk $U'$. Put $\sigma _\zeta =(\mathrm i/(2\pi ))\, \mathrm d\zeta \wedge \mathrm d\bar\zeta $. Fix $p\in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb C)$, nonnegative and supported in the closed unit disk, with $\int p\, \sigma _\zeta =1$. For small $\delta >0$ extend

\[ B_\delta =\chi (z)\delta ^{-2}p(\zeta /\delta )\sigma _\zeta \]

by zero to $X$, and set

\[ C_\delta =\sup _{\Theta \in \mathcal T_\omega } \int _ XB_\delta \wedge \Theta . \]

The form $B_\delta $ is smooth and semipositive. For fixed $\delta $ it is bounded above by a finite multiple of $\omega $, so $C_\delta <\infty $.

Theorem 4.5.1. If $C_{\delta _ j}$ stays bounded along a sequence $\delta _ j\downarrow 0$, then some global analytic hypersurface contains $U'\times \{ 0\} $. In particular, on a manifold without analytic hypersurfaces, $C_\delta \to \infty $ as $\delta \downarrow 0$.

Proof. Suppose $C_{\delta _ j}\leq M$ and put $\kappa =(M+1)^{-1}$. Normalizing any smooth strictly positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed complementary form gives

\[ \int _ X(\omega -\kappa B_{\delta _ j})\wedge \Theta \geq 0. \]

The generalized Lamari criterion, theorem 4.3.1, supplies $v_ j$ with

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j\geq \kappa B_{\delta _ j},\qquad \sup _ Xv_ j=0. \]

Since $B_{\delta _ j}\geq 0$, the potentials belong to the fixed normalized $\omega $-plurisubharmonic family. By theorem 1.4.2, a subsequence converges in $L^1$ to $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. The transverse approximate identity converges to $\chi [\zeta =0]$, so passing in distributions gives

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq \kappa \chi [\zeta =0]. \]

On a smaller chart meeting the inner hypersurface, add a smooth $\rho $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \omega $. The closed positive current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v+\rho )$ dominates $\kappa [\zeta =0]$ where $\chi =1$. Hence the Lelong number of $v$ is at least $\kappa $ at every point of $U'\times \{ 0\} $. By Siu's theorem in 1.13.1, the set $Z=\{ \nu (v,\cdot )\geq \kappa \} $ is analytic and has dimension at most $n-1$. Smooth local corrections do not change its definition. The compact analytic set $Z$ has finitely many global irreducible components. Their intersections with the connected inner hypersurface are analytic subsets covering it. At least one intersection is the whole hypersurface, since finitely many proper analytic subsets cannot cover an open polydisk. That component has dimension $n-1$ and is the required global hypersurface. If no such hypersurface exists, there is no bounded sequence $C_{\delta _ j}$ with $\delta _ j\downarrow 0$, which proves the asserted divergence. $\square$

Proposition 4.5.2. Suppose an effective Cartier divisor agrees, with multiplicity one, with $\{ \zeta =0\} $ throughout the chosen coordinate chart. Then $C_\delta $ is uniformly bounded for all sufficiently small $\delta $.

Proof. Let $s$ be the canonical section of its divisor line bundle. In the chart write $s=\zeta e$ with $e$ a nonvanishing holomorphic frame. Choose a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ with $|e|_ h=1$ on a neighborhood of $\operatorname {supp}\chi \times \{ |\zeta |\leq \delta _0\} $ for a fixed small $\delta _0>0$. The smooth global functions $\psi _\delta =\log (|s|_ h^2+\delta ^2)$ satisfy $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _\delta \geq -C\omega $ with one $C\geq 1$. Off the divisor their Hessians are a bounded scalar multiple of the negative curvature of $h$, plus a semipositive gradient term; continuity gives the same lower bound at the divisor. Fix $0<\epsilon \leq (2C)^{-1}$, so $\omega +\epsilon \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _\delta \geq \omega /2$. On the flat-frame region,

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _\delta =\frac{\delta ^2}{(|\zeta |^2+\delta ^2)^2}\sigma _\zeta \geq \frac1{4\delta ^2}\sigma _\zeta \quad (|\zeta |\leq \delta ). \]

If $P=\| p\| _\infty $, then $B_\delta \leq P\delta ^{-2}\sigma _\zeta $ on its support. Therefore

\[ \omega +\epsilon \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _\delta \geq \frac{\epsilon }{4P}B_\delta \]

globally for $\delta \leq \delta _0$. Every $\Theta \in \mathcal T_\omega $ gives, by smooth Stokes,

\[ 1=\int _ X(\omega +\epsilon \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _\delta )\wedge \Theta \geq \frac{\epsilon }{4P}\int _ XB_\delta \wedge \Theta . \]

Taking the supremum proves $C_\delta \leq 4P/\epsilon $. $\square$

The uniform bound requires the divisor to agree on the entire cutoff chart. A global hypersurface supplied by the preceding theorem can have further branches there, so the theorem does not provide this converse at arbitrary intersections. Divergence of the transverse pairing alone also gives no lower bound for the volume of the positive root of a test.

4.6 Convex norms and Gauduchon degrees

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For holomorphic line bundles, the trace identity of proposition 4.2.1 becomes a statement about curvature. Let $F$ be a holomorphic line bundle on $X$ and $k$ a smooth Hermitian metric on $F$. In a local holomorphic frame $e$ of $F$ we identify $k$ with the positive function $|e|_ k^2$, and the curvature form of $k$ is $R_ k=-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log k$. Let $\Theta $ be a smooth strictly positive real $(n-1,n-1)$-form with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Theta =0$; by the root calculation preceding the proof of theorem 4.3.1, $\Theta =\gamma ^{n-1}$ for a unique Gauduchon metric $\gamma $. The degree of $F$ with respect to $\Theta $ is

\[ \deg _\Theta F=\int _ XR_ k\wedge \Theta . \]

Two metrics on $F$ differ by a factor $\mathrm e^{-u}$ with $u$ smooth, which adds $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ to the curvature, so the degree is independent of $k$ by lemma 2.1.1. It is the Gauduchon degree of the curvature form with respect to $\gamma $; the form $\Theta $ need not be $\mathrm d$-closed.

The squared norm $v\mapsto |v|_ k^2$ is plurisubharmonic on the total space of $F$ off the zero section exactly when $\log k$ is plurisubharmonic, that is, when $R_ k\leq 0$; this follows from the Levi form computation in the proof of lemma 4.6.11 below. A line bundle of positive degree therefore admits no metric with plurisubharmonic squared norm. This section extends the obstruction to direct sums of line bundles of positive degree, to all holomorphic structures sufficiently close to such a direct sum, and to norms that need not be Hermitian. The mechanism is again integration against $\Theta $: averaging the horizontal complex Hessians of a norm near the line summands produces a negative multiple of the degrees, and this negativity is stable under small changes of the holomorphic structure.

For a smooth complex vector bundle $F$, a smooth complex norm is a continuous function $p:F\to [0,+\infty )$, positive away from the zero section and smooth there, such that

\[ p(\lambda v)=|\lambda |p(v),\qquad p(v+w)\leq p(v)+p(w) \]

for $\lambda \in \mathbb C$ and vectors $v,w$ in the same fiber. Its square $G=p^2$ satisfies $G(\lambda v)=|\lambda |^2G(v)$. Near each line summand of a direct sum, the triangle inequality controls the fiber derivatives of $G$, and these estimates control the averaged horizontal complex Hessians.

Lemma 4.6.1. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mu $ on $X$. Every holomorphic line bundle $F$ has a Hermitian metric $k$ satisfying

\[ R_ k\wedge \Theta =\lambda \mu , \qquad \lambda =\frac{\deg _\Theta F}{\int _ X\mu }. \]

Proof. Start with any metric $k^0$, and put $\mathcal Lu=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \Theta )/\mu $. This scalar operator is uniformly elliptic, has no zeroth-order term, and its kernel consists of the constants by the maximum principle. On Hölder spaces, the homotopy to a Laplace operator used in the proof of theorem 4.1.2 shows that its index is zero, so its cokernel is one-dimensional. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Theta =0$, integration by parts gives $\int _ X\mathcal Lu\, \mu =0$, and therefore its image consists exactly of the smooth functions of zero $\mu $-mean. Hence we can solve

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \Theta =\lambda \mu -R_{k^0}\wedge \Theta , \]

whose right-hand side has integral zero, and elliptic regularity makes $u$ smooth. Then $k=k^0\mathrm e^{-u}$ has the required curvature. $\square$

Let $F_0=\bigoplus _{s=1}^ rF_ s$ be a direct sum of holomorphic line bundles, with $r\geq 2$. Choose Hermitian metrics $k_ s$ on $F_ s$ and write $k=\bigoplus _ sk_ s$. Let $P_ s$ be the orthogonal projection onto $F_ s$, and let

\[ \Sigma =\{ v\in F_0:|v|_ k=1\} \]

be the unit sphere bundle, with fibers $\Sigma _ x$. On each fiber sphere $\Sigma _ x$ we use the rotation-invariant probability measure $\mathrm d\sigma _ x$. Fix a nonnegative smooth function $\chi $ supported in $(-1,1)$ and positive near zero. For $0<\varepsilon <1/2$, define weights $\rho _ s$ on $\Sigma $ by

\[ \rho _ s(v)=c_\varepsilon \chi \left(\frac{|(I-P_ s)v|_ k^2}{\varepsilon ^2}\right), \qquad \int _{\Sigma _ x}\rho _ s\, \mathrm d\sigma _ x=1. \]

In unitary frames respecting the splitting, these weights and the normalizing constant $c_\varepsilon $ are independent of the base point and of the index $s$. Independent phase rotations of the summands preserve them, so they are globally defined. The support of $\rho _ s$ lies within distance $\varepsilon $ of the unit circle of $F_ s$.

Lemma 4.6.2. For a smooth complex norm on $F_0$ with square $G$, put

\[ a_ s(x)=G(e_ s),\qquad N(x)=\sum _ sa_ s(x),\qquad D_ jG(v)=\mathrm d_ vG(P_ jv), \]

where $e_ s$ is any $k_ s$-unit vector in the fiber of $F_ s$ at $x$; complex homogeneity makes $a_ s$ well defined. Then

\[ G(v)\leq N|v|_ k^2,\qquad |\mathrm d_ vG(w)|\leq 2N|v|_ k|w|_ k. \]

On the support of $\rho _ s$,

\[ |D_ jG|\leq 2\varepsilon N\quad (j\ne s),\qquad |D_ sG-2a_ s|\leq 10\varepsilon N. \]

Proof. The triangle inequality and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give $p(v)\leq \sum _ s|v_ s|_ k\sqrt{a_ s}\leq \sqrt N|v|_ k$. Differentiating $|p(v+tw)-p(v)|\leq |t|p(w)$ in the real variable $t$ gives $|\mathrm d_ vp(w)|\leq p(w)$, and both global bounds follow.

For $v$ in the support of $\rho _ s$, set $w=(I-P_ s)v$ and $e_ s=P_ sv/|P_ sv|_ k$. Then $|w|_ k<\varepsilon $ and $|v-e_ s|_ k\leq |w|_ k+1-|P_ sv|_ k<2\varepsilon $. The segment from $e_ s$ to $v$ stays in the unit ball and avoids zero, so integrating the derivative bound along it gives $|G(v)-a_ s|\leq 4\varepsilon N$. For $j\ne s$, we have $|P_ jv|_ k<\varepsilon $, which gives the first axis bound. Euler's identity $\mathrm d_ vG(v)=2G(v)$ yields

\[ D_ sG-2a_ s=2(G(v)-a_ s)-\sum _{j\ne s}D_ jG. \]

The sum on the right is $\mathrm d_ vG(w)$, whose absolute value is at most $2\varepsilon N$. This proves the second axis bound. $\square$

Write $\pi :F_0\to X$ for the bundle projection. For $v\in F_0$ with $\pi (v)=x$, let $H_{0,v}:T_ xX\to T_ vF_0$ be the horizontal lift for the Chern connection of $k$. It is complex linear, and at unit vectors it is tangent to $\Sigma $, since the Chern connection preserves $k$. For a smooth real function $G$ on $F_0\setminus \{ 0\} $, define a $(1,1)$-form $\beta _ s(G)$ and functions $U_ s$, $V_{sj}$ on $X$ by

\[ \begin{aligned} \beta _ s(G)& =\int _{\Sigma _ x}\rho _ s H_{0,v}^*(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G)\, \mathrm d\sigma _ x,\\ U_ s& =\int _{\Sigma _ x}G\rho _ s\, \mathrm d\sigma _ x,\\ V_{sj}& =\int _{\Sigma _ x}D_ jG\rho _ s\, \mathrm d\sigma _ x. \end{aligned} \]

Lemma 4.6.3. With $R_ j$ the curvature of $k_ j$,

\[ \beta _ s(G)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ s-\frac12\sum _ jR_ jV_{sj}. \]

Proof. At a fixed base point, choose holomorphic frames of the line bundles with $k_ j=\mathrm e^{-\varphi _ j}$ and $\mathrm d\varphi _ j=0$ at that point. There the horizontal lift has zero vertical component. Write the smooth unitary fiber coordinates as $\zeta _ j=\mathrm e^{-\varphi _ j/2}v_ j$ and $G(z,v)=g(z,\zeta )$. At the base point, the first base derivatives of $\zeta _ j$ vanish, and its mixed second base derivatives are $-\frac12(\varphi _ j)_{\alpha \bar\beta }\zeta _ j$ and their conjugates. The chain rule therefore gives

\[ G_{\alpha \bar\beta } =g_{\alpha \bar\beta }-\frac12\sum _ j(\varphi _ j)_{\alpha \bar\beta } (\zeta _ jg_{\zeta _ j}+\bar\zeta _ jg_{\bar\zeta _ j}). \]

The expression in parentheses is $D_ jG$. Both the spherical measure and $\rho _ s$ are independent of $z$ in unitary coordinates, so averaging the identity gives the result, since $R_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j$. $\square$

Theorem 4.6.4. Suppose that for each $s$ there is a smooth strictly positive real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\Theta _ s$ satisfying

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Theta _ s=0,\qquad \deg _{\Theta _ s}F_ s>0. \]

Then there is a $C^4$ neighborhood of the split Dolbeault operator on the underlying smooth bundle of $F_0$ such that no integrable Dolbeault operator in this neighborhood admits a smooth complex norm whose square is plurisubharmonic off the zero section.

Proof. Step 1: the split structure. Fix $\mu $ and use lemma 4.6.1 to choose $k_ s$ with $R_ s\wedge \Theta _ s=\lambda _ s\mu $, where $\lambda _ s>0$. Put

\[ \lambda _* =\min _ s\lambda _ s,\qquad C=5\sum _ s\lambda _ s+ \sum _ s\sum _{j\ne s}\sup _ X\left|\frac{R_ j\wedge \Theta _ s}{\mu }\right|. \]

For a squared norm $G$, define $I_0(G)=\sum _ s\int _ X\beta _ s(G)\wedge \Theta _ s$. By lemma 4.6.3, the term $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ s$ integrates to zero against $\Theta _ s$, and the axis estimates of lemma 4.6.2 then give

\[ I_0(G)=-\frac12\sum _{s,j}\int _ XV_{sj}R_ j\wedge \Theta _ s \leq -(\lambda _* -\varepsilon C)\int _ XN\mu . \]

Choose $\varepsilon $ so that $\varepsilon C<\lambda _*/2$, and keep all weights fixed.

Step 2: nearby holomorphic structures. Write a nearby operator as $\bar\partial _ A=\bar\partial _0+A$, with $\bar\partial _ A^2=0$. Its Chern connection for $k$ is $D_ A=D_0+A-A^{*k}$, where the adjoint changes the type from $(0,1)$ to $(1,0)$. Let $J_ A$ be the corresponding complex structure on the total space and $H_{A,v}$ the horizontal lift of $D_ A$. These depend smoothly on $A$, and $H_{A,v}$ is complex linear and tangent to the fixed sphere bundle $\Sigma $.

For $g\in C^\infty (\Sigma ,\mathbb R)$, define its homogeneous extension by

\[ (\mathcal Eg)(v)=|v|_ k^2g(v/|v|_ k), \]

and define a real differential operator on $\Sigma $ by

\[ (\mathcal P_ Ag)(v)=\sum _ s\rho _ s(v) \frac{H_{A,v}^*(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_{J_ A}\mathcal Eg)_ v\wedge \Theta _ s(x)}{\mu (x)}, \qquad x=\pi (v). \]

It has order at most two. By the identity $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}_ Jf=-(4\pi )^{-1}\mathrm df\circ J$, its coefficients depend on $A$ and its first derivatives, as well as on the fixed smooth data. Consequently they converge in $C^2$ as $A\to 0$ in $C^4$.

Step 3: the adjoint operator. Take the formal adjoint $\mathcal P_ A^*$ for the fixed density $\mathrm d\nu =\mathrm d\sigma _ x\, \mu (x)$ on the compact sphere bundle, and put $b_ A=\mathcal P_ A^*1$. Locally, if $\mathrm d\nu =m(y)\, \mathrm dy$ and $\mathcal P_ A=\sum _{|\alpha |\leq 2}c_{\alpha ,A}\partial ^\alpha $, then

\[ b_ A=m^{-1}\sum _{|\alpha |\leq 2}(-1)^{|\alpha |} \partial ^\alpha (mc_{\alpha ,A}). \]

Thus $\| b_ A-b_0\| _{C^0}\to 0$ as $A\to 0$ in $C^4$. For $g=G|_\Sigma $, homogeneity gives $\mathcal Eg=G$ and

\[ I_ A(G):=\int _\Sigma \mathcal P_ Ag\, \mathrm d\nu =\int _\Sigma gb_ A\, \mathrm d\nu . \]

Since $0<g\leq N\circ \pi $ and the spherical measures have mass one,

\[ |I_ A(G)-I_0(G)| \leq \| b_ A-b_0\| _{C^0}\int _ XN\mu . \]

Choose the neighborhood so that $\| b_ A-b_0\| _{C^0}<\lambda _*/4$ in it. Then $I_ A(G)\leq -\frac14\lambda _*\int _ XN\mu <0$ for every smooth squared complex norm $G$. If $G$ were plurisubharmonic for $J_ A$, all horizontal pullbacks of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_{J_ A}G$ would be semipositive, and their pairings with $\Theta _ s$, weighted by $\rho _ s\geq 0$, would give $I_ A(G)\geq 0$, a contradiction. $\square$

The neighborhood depends on the bundle and on the test forms $\Theta _ s$, and different line summands may require different tests. Its size is independent of all derivatives of the candidate norms: integration over the fixed sphere bundle transfers those derivatives to the coefficients of the adjoint operator.

To apply theorem 4.6.4 to norms obtained by duality, we first need to regularize continuous norms while preserving a strict convexity margin. Polar duality then turns the obstruction into a failure of semipositive Kobayashi curvature on the dual bundle. Stable ample bundles on Hirzebruch surfaces provide concrete examples, and a fixed Stein hypersurface detects the failure for every Finsler metric on such a bundle.

Dual support norms also connect this obstruction with the geometry of neighborhoods of the zero section. This leads to bounds for the convexity of a projective complement. Curvature supplies a second way to study these complements, through explicit exhaustions. A uniformly negative bundle over a Kähler base with a proper psh exhaustion instead gives logarithmic weights and a complete metric on its punctured total space (4.6.10). The distinction between these spaces matters: the final flat and Hopf examples use restrictions and topology to obtain further bounds for the projective complement.

4.6.1 Homogeneous convex regularization

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Theorem 4.6.4 concerns smooth norms, whereas the norms produced by duality later in this section are in general only continuous. We show that a continuous homogeneous function with strict positivity margins can be approximated by smooth ones with the same margins, so the obstruction applies to it as well. The local step is Wu's convolution in projective coordinates, written as an average of holomorphic linear shears; the local approximations are glued by the argument of Greene and Wu. We give both steps in order to keep track of strict plurisubharmonicity and of strong convexity in the fibers; see section 34.3 for attribution.

Let $\pi :F\to X$ be a holomorphic vector bundle, and write $F^\times $ for the complement of its zero section. A continuous real function $Q$ on $F$ is complex homogeneous of degree two if

\[ Q(\lambda v)=|\lambda |^2Q(v)\qquad (\lambda \in \mathbb C). \]

We call $Q$ locally strongly convex in the fibers if, in a local bundle frame and near every point of $F^\times $, there is a constant $c>0$ such that $Q(z,v)-c|v|^2$ is convex on sufficiently small real fiber balls. For continuous functions, strict plurisubharmonicity means that subtracting a sufficiently small positive multiple of the Euclidean squared norm in local coordinates of the total space leaves a plurisubharmonic function. Both conditions are local, and on compact subsets of $F^\times $ they hold with uniform positive margins.

The strong convexity required here holds with one constant on a neighborhood in the total space. Strong convexity of every fixed fiber is weaker, even for a plurisubharmonic squared complex norm.

Example 4.6.5. On the trivial bundle $D\times \mathbb C^2$, where $D=\{ z\in \mathbb C:|z|<1/2\} $, put

\[ Q(z,v)=\max \left\{ |v_1|^2+|v_2|^2, \frac{|v_1|^2}{1-|z|^2}+|z|^2|v_2|^2 \right\} . \]

This is a continuous plurisubharmonic function, positive off the zero section and complex homogeneous of degree two. Its square root is a complex norm on every fiber, and for each fixed $z$, the real function $Q(z,\cdot )$ is strongly convex. Nevertheless, $Q$ is not locally strongly convex in the fibers near $(z,v)=(0,(1,0))$ in the sense defined above.

Proof. Write $t=|z|^2$. Both branches are continuous, positive semidefinite Hermitian quadratic forms in $v$, and the first is positive definite. Their square roots satisfy the triangle inequality and complex homogeneity; their maximum therefore is a complex norm. In particular $Q$ is positive off zero and continuous, also on the zero section.

The first branch is plurisubharmonic on the total space. For the second, the locally uniformly convergent expansion

\[ \frac{|v_1|^2}{1-|z|^2}+|z|^2|v_2|^2 =\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }|z^ kv_1|^2+|zv_2|^2 \]

expresses it as a limit of sums of squared moduli of holomorphic functions. Thus it too is plurisubharmonic, and so is their maximum.

For $0<t<1/4$, subtracting $t|v|^2$ gives

\[ Q(z,v)-t|v|^2 =\max \left\{ (1-t)|v|^2, \left(\frac1{1-t}-t\right)|v_1|^2\right\} , \]

which is convex. At $z=0$, one has $Q(0,v)=|v|^2$. Hence each fixed fiber has a positive strong convexity margin.

To test a margin on a neighborhood of $(0,(1,0))$, fix any $c>0$ and choose a nonzero $z$ arbitrarily close to zero with $t<c$. At $v=(1,0)$ the second branch is strictly larger than the first, so it remains the maximum on a small fiber ball. Along the real line $v=(1,s)$ in this ball,

\[ Q(z,(1,s))-c|(1,s)|^2 =\frac1{1-t}-c+(t-c)s^2. \]

Its second derivative is negative. Thus no positive $c$ makes $Q-c|v|^2$ convex on the fiber balls in any such total-space neighborhood. A fiber-linear change of smooth frame and its inverse are uniformly bounded on a smaller base neighborhood, so a positive quadratic margin in another frame would give one in this frame. This proves the failure of the local margin. $\square$

Lemma 4.6.6. Let $M$ be a compact smooth manifold and let $\mathcal S$ be a sheaf of germs of continuous real functions on $M$, whose elements we call sections. Suppose that $\mathcal S$ is closed under maxima and has the following two properties.

Stability: if $f$ is a section near a compact set $K$, then $f+u$ is a section near $K$ whenever $u$ is smooth and sufficiently small in the $C^\infty $ topology on a neighborhood of $K$.

Local approximation: every point has a neighborhood $U$ such that, for every compact $K\Subset U$, a section near $K$ can be approximated uniformly on $K$ by smooth sections near $K$, with arbitrarily small errors in any prescribed finite collection of derivative norms on compact subsets where the section is already smooth.

Then every global section can be approximated uniformly on $M$ by smooth global sections.

Proof. We first describe a gluing step. Let $f$ be a global section that is smooth near a compact set $L_1$, and let $L_2$ be a compact subset of one of the neighborhoods $U$ in the statement. Choose relatively compact open sets $W_3,W_4$ with $L_1\subset W_3$, $f$ smooth near $\overline W_3$, and $L_2\subset W_4\Subset U$. Choose a smooth function $0\leq \rho \leq 1$ equal to one near $\overline W_3$, with compact support in the region where $f$ is smooth. Put $V=W_3\cup W_4$, and choose a smooth function $-1\leq \psi \leq 1$ equal to one near $L_1\cup L_2$ and equal to minus one near $M\setminus V$.

By stability, for sufficiently small $\xi >0$ the function $f_\xi =f+\xi \psi $ is still a global section. Approximate $f_\xi $ on $\overline W_4$ by a smooth section $g$, with uniform error less than $\delta <\xi /2$, and with arbitrarily small derivative errors near the compact set $\overline W_4\cap \operatorname {supp}\rho $, where $f_\xi $ is smooth. On $V$ define

\[ h=f_\xi \quad \hbox{on }W_3,\qquad h=\rho f_\xi +(1-\rho )g\quad \hbox{on }W_4. \]

These definitions agree on the overlap, and $h$ is smooth. Where $\rho $ vanishes, $h$ is the section $g$. Where $\rho $ can be nonzero, $h-f_\xi =(1-\rho )(g-f_\xi )$, and stability applied to the smooth section $f_\xi $ makes $h$ a section if the derivative errors of $g-f_\xi $ are sufficiently small. Moreover, $|h-f_\xi |<\delta $.

Define $f_1=\max \{ f,h\} $ on $V$ and $f_1=f$ outside $V$. Near $L_1\cup L_2$ we have $h>f$, and near the boundary of $V$ we have $h<f$. Thus $f_1$ is continuous, it is a section because $\mathcal S$ is closed under maxima, and it is smooth near $L_1\cup L_2$. Also $\| f_1-f\| _{C^0}<\xi +\delta $. Near $L_1$ it equals $f+\xi $, so the previous smoothness is preserved.

Now cover $M$ by finitely many compact sets $L_2$ of this kind and repeat the step, starting with $L_1$ empty and then taking for $L_1$ the union of the previously treated compact sets. At every step choose $\xi $ and $\delta $ so small that the sum of the finitely many uniform errors is below the prescribed tolerance. The final section is smooth everywhere and has the required uniform error. $\square$

Lemma 4.6.7. In a holomorphic bundle chart, write $v=(v',v_ r)$ and use the affine projective coordinate $w=v'/v_ r$ on the cone $v_ r\ne 0$. Suppose that a positive continuous complex homogeneous function $Q$ of degree two is strictly plurisubharmonic and locally strongly convex in the fibers on this cone, and put $q(z,w)=Q(z,(w,1))$. For a nonnegative smooth approximate identity $\eta _\delta (s,t)$ of integral one, define

\[ q_\delta (z,w)=\int q(z-s,w-t)\eta _\delta (s,t)\, \mathrm ds\, \mathrm dt, \qquad Q_\delta (z,v)=|v_ r|^2q_\delta (z,v'/v_ r). \]

On every relatively compact projective subchart, for sufficiently small $\delta $, $Q_\delta $ is a smooth positive complex homogeneous function of degree two that is strictly plurisubharmonic and locally strongly convex in the fibers. As $\delta \to 0$, it converges to $Q$ uniformly on compact subsets of the cone consisting of vectors of norm one, and in every finite derivative norm wherever $Q$ is already smooth.

Proof. Complex homogeneity gives the identity

\[ Q_\delta (z,v) =\int Q(z-s,T_ tv)\eta _\delta (s,t)\, \mathrm ds\, \mathrm dt, \qquad T_ t(v',v_ r)=(v'-v_ rt,v_ r). \]

For fixed $(s,t)$, the map $(z,v)\mapsto (z-s,T_ tv)$ is holomorphic and invertible, and its restriction to each fiber is linear. On a compact subset of the cone consisting of vectors of norm one, all these maps with $(s,t)$ sufficiently small stay in a fixed compact subset of the original cone, and their differentials and inverses are uniformly bounded there. A positive local lower bound for the Levi form of $Q$, interpreted through quadratic barriers, therefore gives a positive lower bound for every pullback and for their average. The same argument with real quadratic barriers in the fibers preserves local strong convexity. Only fiber segments contained in the local cone neighborhood are used here.

Ordinary convolution makes $q_\delta $ smooth, and the formula for $Q_\delta $ makes it smooth on the cone and complex homogeneous. Uniform convergence, derivative convergence on compact regions where $Q$ is smooth, and positivity for small $\delta $ follow from the corresponding properties of convolution and from the positive minimum of $Q$ on compact sets of vectors of norm one. $\square$

Theorem 4.6.8. Let $Q:F\to [0,+\infty )$ be continuous, positive on $F^\times $, complex homogeneous of degree two, strictly plurisubharmonic on $F^\times $, and locally strongly convex in the fibers. Then there are continuous complex homogeneous functions $Q_ j$ of degree two, smooth on $F^\times $, strictly plurisubharmonic there and locally strongly convex in the fibers, which converge to $Q$ uniformly on the unit sphere bundle of any fixed smooth Hermitian metric. Each $p_ j=\sqrt{Q_ j}$ is a smooth complex norm.

Proof. Fix a smooth Hermitian squared norm $G$ on $F$, and let $M=\mathbb P(F)$ be the bundle of complex lines in $F$. The quotient $m=Q/G$ is a positive continuous function on the compact manifold $M$. Define a sheaf $\mathcal S$ on $M$ by requiring that the corresponding function $Gm$ on the cone be positive, strictly plurisubharmonic, and locally strongly convex in the fibers.

This sheaf is closed under maxima. Indeed, multiplication by $G>0$ commutes with maxima. Near a point of the cone, subtract a common positive quadratic barrier from both functions; their maximum remains plurisubharmonic, and on sufficiently small fiber balls it remains convex. Positivity is also preserved.

For stability on a compact subset $K$ of $M$, restrict to vectors of norm one lying over $K$, and to a fixed compact annulus around them. Finitely many bundle charts provide positive common lower bounds for both barriers. If $u$ is a smooth function on $M$ that is sufficiently small in $C^2$ near $K$, the Levi form and the real fiber Hessian of $Gu$ are smaller than half these bounds on that annulus. The perturbed function is therefore still positive and has both strict margins. Homogeneity transports the local conditions to the rest of the cone over $K$. This proves the stability required in lemma 4.6.6.

On a sufficiently small projective coordinate chart, lemma 4.6.7 gives local approximation, including derivative control on compact subsets where $m$ is smooth; dividing its regularizations by $G$ gives local smooth sections of $\mathcal S$. By lemma 4.6.6, there are smooth positive global sections $m_ j\to m$ uniformly. Put $Q_ j=Gm_ j$ off the zero section and $Q_ j=0$ on the zero section. Homogeneity gives continuity at zero, uniformly over the compact base, as well as the asserted convergence on a fixed unit sphere bundle.

It remains to see that $p_ j$ is a norm. Local convexity in the fibers away from zero implies convexity on the whole fiber. Indeed, the restriction of $Q_ j$ to a real line avoiding zero is locally convex and hence convex, while on a real line through zero, homogeneity gives $Q_ j(tv)=t^2Q_ j(v)$ for real $t$, which is convex including at zero. Thus the set $B_ x=\{ v:Q_ j(v)\leq 1\} $ is convex, balanced and absorbing, and its gauge is $\sqrt{Q_ j}$. If $v,w$ are nonzero, convexity applied to $v/p_ j(v)$ and $w/p_ j(w)$, with weights proportional to $p_ j(v)$ and $p_ j(w)$, gives the triangle inequality; the cases with a zero vector follow directly. Positivity, smoothness off zero and complex homogeneity give the remaining properties of a smooth complex norm. $\square$

Proposition 4.6.9. Suppose that $F$ admits a smooth positive complex homogeneous function $H$ of degree two that is strictly plurisubharmonic on $F^\times $. If a positive continuous complex homogeneous function $Q$ of degree two is plurisubharmonic on $F^\times $ and locally strongly convex in the fibers, then $F$ admits a smooth complex norm with strictly plurisubharmonic square on $F^\times $.

Proof. For $\tau >0$, set $Q_\tau =Q+\tau H$; it is positive, complex homogeneous and strictly plurisubharmonic. On a compact annulus of vectors of norm close to one, local strong convexity of $Q$ in the fibers gives a positive uniform margin in finitely many frames, while the real fiber Hessian of $H$ is uniformly bounded there. Choose $\tau $ so small that the possible negative part of $\tau $ times this Hessian consumes less than half the margin. Homogeneity then gives local strong convexity of $Q_\tau $ in the fibers everywhere off zero. Apply theorem 4.6.8 to $Q_\tau $ and take one of its smooth regularizations; its square root is the required smooth complex norm. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.10. For every holomorphic structure in the neighborhood of theorem 4.6.4, there is no positive continuous complex homogeneous function of degree two that is both strictly plurisubharmonic off zero and locally strongly convex in the fibers. If that structure admits a function $H$ as in proposition 4.6.9, there is also no positive continuous complex homogeneous function of degree two that is plurisubharmonic off zero and locally strongly convex in the fibers.

Proof. For the first assertion, theorem 4.6.8 would produce a smooth complex norm with plurisubharmonic square, contrary to theorem 4.6.4. For the second, use proposition 4.6.9 and the same obstruction. $\square$

The second assertion requires the strictly plurisubharmonic homogeneous function $H$, which need not exist for an arbitrary holomorphic bundle; 4.6.2 shows that it exists when the dual bundle is ample. Without a strong convexity margin in the fibers, the regularization gives no obstruction for continuous fiber norms.

4.6.2 Strict positivity from the tautological metric

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The second part of corollary 4.6.10 requires a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function on $F^\times $ that is complex homogeneous of degree two. Such a function is the squared norm of a vector, viewed as a point of the tautological line it spans, for a suitable metric on the tautological line bundle. Its Levi form is strictly positive exactly when the dual tautological metric is positively curved, so the function exists when $F^*$ is ample.

Let $F\to X$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank. In this subsection, $\mathbb P(F)$ parametrizes complex lines in $F$, $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(F)}(-1)$ is the tautological line bundle whose fiber over a line is that line, and

\[ L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(F)}(1) =\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(F)}(-1)^*. \]

The map $v\mapsto ([v],v)$ identifies $F^\times $ holomorphically with the complement of the zero section in $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(F)}(-1)$. We use the curvature convention $R_ h=-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log h$ of section 4.6.

Lemma 4.6.11. A smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $L$ determines a positive smooth complex homogeneous function $H$ of degree two on $F^\times $: the squared norm of $v$ in the dual metric on the tautological line $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(F)}(-1)_{[v]}$. The function $H$ is strictly plurisubharmonic if and only if $R_ h$ is strictly positive on $\mathbb P(F)$.

Proof. Take local holomorphic coordinates $y$ on $\mathbb P(F)$ and a holomorphic frame $e$ of the tautological line bundle, and write the metric of the dual frame as $h=\mathrm e^{-\varphi }$. The dual metric gives

\[ H(y,\lambda e)=|\lambda |^2\mathrm e^{\varphi (y)},\qquad R_ h=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi . \]

Let $\mathcal L$ denote the complex Hessian, without the factor $\mathrm i/(2\pi )$ of the curvature normalization. Differentiating at a point with $\lambda \ne 0$ gives

\[ \mathcal L H(u,t) =H\left(\mathcal L\varphi (u)+ \left|\frac{t}{\lambda }+\partial \varphi (u)\right|^2\right), \qquad (u,t)\in T_ y^{1,0}\mathbb P(F)\oplus \mathbb C. \]

If $\mathcal L\varphi $ is positive definite, the right-hand side is positive for every nonzero $(u,t)$: the first term detects $u\ne 0$, and the second detects $t\ne 0$ when $u=0$. Conversely, for $u\ne 0$, set $t=-\lambda \partial \varphi (u)$; strict positivity of $\mathcal L H$ then forces $\mathcal L\varphi (u)>0$. This is exactly positivity of $R_ h$. The squared tautological norm is independent of the chosen frame and is complex homogeneous, so the local functions define the asserted global function $H$. $\square$

A line bundle is very ample when its complete linear system defines a holomorphic embedding, and ample when some positive tensor power is very ample. With the convention above, a vector bundle $F^*$ is called ample when $L$ is ample.

Proposition 4.6.12. If $F^*$ is ample, there is a positive complex homogeneous function $H$ of degree two on $F^\times $ that is smooth and strictly plurisubharmonic there and, after extension by zero, continuous on $F$.

Proof. Choose $m\geq 1$ such that $L^ m$ is very ample. Its complete linear system gives an embedding $\iota :\mathbb P(F)\to \mathbb P^ N$ with $L^ m=\iota ^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^ N}(1)$. Pull back the Fubini–Study metric to a metric $k$ on $L^ m$, and take its positive $m$th root $h$ on $L$: in a local frame $h=k^{1/m}$, and the transformation law for $L^ m$ makes these local roots a metric on $L$. Its curvature is

\[ R_ h=\frac1m\iota ^*\omega _{\rm FS}>0, \]

because the differential of the embedding is injective. Apply lemma 4.6.11. Since the base is compact, the resulting $H$ is bounded on the unit sphere bundle of a fixed Hermitian metric. Homogeneity then gives $0<H(v)\leq C|v|^2$ off zero, which proves continuity of the extension by zero. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.13. Let $F$ carry a holomorphic structure in the neighborhood of theorem 4.6.4. If $F^*$ is ample, there is no positive continuous complex homogeneous function of degree two on $F$ that is plurisubharmonic off zero and locally strongly convex in the fibers.

Proof. By proposition 4.6.12, ampleness of $F^*$ gives the function $H$ required in proposition 4.6.9. A function $Q$ as in the statement would therefore give a smooth complex norm on $F$ with strictly plurisubharmonic square, contradicting theorem 4.6.4. $\square$

The function $H$ is not the square of a Hermitian norm in general, since the metric on the tautological line may vary with the line. It provides the strict plurisubharmonicity on the total space needed for regularization, whereas local strong convexity in the fibers remains a separate hypothesis in the continuous obstruction.

4.6.3 Polar norms of semipositive Finsler metrics

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a holomorphic vector bundle that is not assumed to carry a positively curved Hermitian metric, positivity can be measured by Finsler metrics and their Kobayashi curvature. Polar duality converts a Finsler metric on $E$ with semipositive Kobayashi curvature into a continuous norm on $E^*$ whose square is plurisubharmonic and uniformly convex in the fibers. With the regularization of 4.6.1, theorem 4.6.4 therefore excludes such Finsler metrics on an ample bundle whose dual is close to a split bundle with positive Gauduchon degrees.

Let $E\to X$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank; in this subsection $n\geq 1$. Write $E^\times $ for the complement of its zero section. A squared Finsler metric on $E$ is a positive smooth function $G$ on $E^\times $ with

\[ G(x,tv)=|t|^2G(x,v),\qquad t\in \mathbb C\setminus \{ 0\} , \]

extended continuously by zero to the zero section. It is strongly pseudoconvex if its vertical complex Hessian $(G_{i\bar j})$ is positive definite; this does not require convexity in the real fiber variables.

In local holomorphic base and fiber coordinates $(z,v)$, with Greek indices for base coordinates, Latin indices for fiber coordinates and summation over repeated indices, put

\[ \begin{gathered} S_{\alpha \bar\beta } =G_{\alpha \bar\beta } -G_{\alpha \bar j}G^{\bar j i}G_{i\bar\beta },\\ \mathcal K_ G=-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi G} S_{\alpha \bar\beta }\, \mathrm dz^\alpha \wedge \mathrm d\bar z^\beta , \end{gathered} \]

where $(G^{\bar j i})$ is the inverse of the vertical Hessian. We use this convention for the Kobayashi curvature $\mathcal K_ G$, a horizontal $(1,1)$-form at each point of $E^\times $. Thus $\mathcal K_ G\geq 0$ means $S\leq 0$, and $\mathcal K_ G>0$ means that $S$ is negative definite.

Lemma 4.6.14. Let $G$ be a strongly pseudoconvex squared Finsler metric. If $\mathcal K_ G>0$, then for every $v_0\in E_{x_0}^\times $ there is a local holomorphic section $\sigma $ with $\sigma (x_0)=v_0$ and

\[ G(z,\sigma (z))\leq G(x_0,v_0) \]

near $x_0$. Moreover, without any curvature assumption, on a coordinate ball, for $\varphi =|z|^2$ and $\varepsilon >0$, the metric $G_\varepsilon =\mathrm e^{-\varepsilon \varphi }G$ is strongly pseudoconvex and satisfies

\[ \mathcal K_{G_\varepsilon }=\mathcal K_ G+\varepsilon \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi . \]

Proof. Since the vertical Hessian is positive, the Levi-orthogonal complement $H_{G,v}$ of the vertical tangent space at $v$ projects isomorphically onto the base tangent space. The Levi form of $G$ on $H_{G,v}$ is the Schur complement $S$, which is negative definite when $\mathcal K_ G>0$. For the vertical radial vector $R=v_ i\partial _{v_ i}$, complex homogeneity gives

\[ \partial G(R)=G,\qquad \mathcal L_ G(a,\bar R)=\partial G(a) \]

for every tangent vector $a$, where $\mathcal L_ G$ is the Levi form. Since $R$ is vertical, the second identity implies $\partial G|_{H_{G,v}}=0$.

Choose a holomorphic section $\sigma _0$ through $v_0$ whose tangent space at $x_0$ is $H_{G,v_0}$. In coordinates centered at $x_0$, the function $G\circ \sigma _0$ has zero first derivative and Taylor expansion

\[ G(x_0,v_0)+P(z)+\overline{P(z)} +S_{\alpha \bar\beta }z^\alpha \bar z^\beta +O(|z|^3), \]

where $P$ is a homogeneous holomorphic quadratic polynomial. The identity $\partial _ vG(v_0)(v_0)=G(v_0)>0$ gives a vertical vector $b$ with $\partial _ vG(v_0)(b)=1$. Set $\sigma =\sigma _0-bP$ in the local frame. This changes only the pure quadratic terms and cancels $P+\bar P$. The remaining quadratic form is negative definite, and therefore dominates the cubic remainder on a sufficiently small ball. The section stays nonzero there and satisfies the asserted inequality.

For the last assertion, differentiate $\mathrm e^{-\varepsilon \varphi }G$ in the Schur complement. Euler's identities give $G_{\bar j}=v_ iG_{i\bar j}$ and $G_\alpha =G_{\alpha \bar j}\bar v_ j$. Hence the terms containing $\partial \varphi $, including its square, cancel between the base Hessian and the mixed block product, leaving

\[ S(G_\varepsilon )=\mathrm e^{-\varepsilon \varphi } \bigl (S(G)-\varepsilon G(\varphi _{\alpha \bar\beta })\bigr ). \]

Dividing by $G_\varepsilon $ gives the curvature formula. The vertical Hessian of $G_\varepsilon $ is the positive multiple $\mathrm e^{-\varepsilon \varphi }(G_{i\bar j})$, so $G_\varepsilon $ is again strongly pseudoconvex. $\square$

Proposition 4.6.15. Let $G$ be a strongly pseudoconvex squared Finsler metric with $\mathcal K_ G\geq 0$. Its polar square

\[ Q(x,\xi )=\max _{[v]\in \mathbb P(E_ x)} \frac{|\xi (v)|^2}{G(x,v)},\qquad \xi \in E_ x^*, \]

is continuous and plurisubharmonic on the total space of $E^*$, positive off the zero section and complex homogeneous of degree two. The function $\log Q$ is plurisubharmonic off the zero section, and $\sqrt Q$ is a complex norm on each fiber.

Proof. The ratio is invariant under complex rescaling of $v$, so the maximum can be taken over the compact unit sphere in a local frame, which proves continuity. Positive upper and lower bounds for $G$ on that sphere compare $Q$ with a Hermitian squared dual norm; this gives positivity off zero and continuity at zero, and complex homogeneity is clear. Moreover,

\[ \sqrt{Q(x,\xi )}=\sup _{v\ne 0}\frac{|\xi (v)|}{\sqrt{G(x,v)}}. \]

Each term satisfies the triangle inequality in $\xi $, and so does their supremum.

On a coordinate ball, set $G_\varepsilon =\mathrm e^{-\varepsilon |z|^2}G$. By lemma 4.6.14, its Kobayashi curvature is strictly positive, and its polar square is $Q_\varepsilon =\mathrm e^{\varepsilon |z|^2}Q$. At a point $(x_0,\xi _0)$ with $\xi _0\ne 0$, choose a maximizing line and a vector $v_0$ on it. The lemma, applied to $G_\varepsilon $, gives a holomorphic section $\sigma $ such that

\[ \log Q_\varepsilon (z,\xi ) \geq \log |\xi (\sigma (z))|^2-\log G_\varepsilon (x_0,v_0), \]

with equality at $(x_0,\xi _0)$. The pairing $\xi (\sigma (z))$ is nonzero near that point, so the right-hand side is pluriharmonic there. Restriction to sufficiently small holomorphic disks centered at the point therefore gives the submean inequality for $\log Q_\varepsilon $. This argument applies at every point off the zero section, and together with continuity it shows that $\log Q_\varepsilon $ is plurisubharmonic there.

As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, the functions $\log Q_\varepsilon =\log Q+\varepsilon |z|^2$ converge locally uniformly off the zero section. Passing to the limit in the submean inequalities on disks proves that $\log Q$ is plurisubharmonic off the zero section. Since the exponential is increasing and convex, Jensen's inequality shows that $Q=\exp (\log Q)$ is plurisubharmonic there. At a point of the zero section, $Q=0$ and $Q\geq 0$, so every centered disk satisfies the submean inequality there. Together with continuity and the submean inequalities at nonzero points, this proves plurisubharmonicity on the whole total space of $E^*$. $\square$

Lemma 4.6.16. Let $k$ be a smooth Hermitian metric on $E$, and let $G$ be a positive smooth complex homogeneous function of degree two on $E^\times $, extended by zero. Then its polar square $Q$, defined as in proposition 4.6.15, has a constant $c>0$ such that

\[ Q(x,\xi )-c|\xi |_{k^*}^2 \]

is real convex on every dual fiber. Neither strong pseudoconvexity nor real convexity of $G$ is required here.

Proof. The real fiber Hessian of $G$ is bounded above by $2L$ times $k$ on the compact unit sphere bundle of $k$, for some $L>0$, and homogeneity extends this bound to every nonzero vector. Also, $G$ is $C^1$ at zero with derivative zero, since its value is $O(|v|^2)$ and its first fiber derivative is $O(|v|)$, uniformly over the base. Integrating the Hessian estimate along a segment gives

4.9
\begin{equation} G(v+w)\leq G(v)+\mathrm d_ vG(w)+L|w|_ k^2; \end{equation}

a segment passing through zero is handled by splitting it there and using the continuity of the first derivative.

Complex homogeneity and completing the square on each complex line give the identity

4.10
\begin{equation} Q(x,\xi )=\max _{v\in E_ x} \{ 2\operatorname {Re}\xi (v)-G(x,v)\} . \end{equation}

The maximum exists because $G(v)\geq a|v|_ k^2$ for a uniform constant $a>0$. Fix a maximizer $v_0$ for $\xi _0$. Differentiation, including the case $\xi _0=v_0=0$, gives $\mathrm dG(v_0)=2\operatorname {Re}\xi _0$. Evaluate the expression in (4.10) for $\xi _0+\zeta $ at $v_0+w$ and apply (4.9). The terms involving $\xi _0(w)$ cancel, and we obtain

\[ Q(\xi _0+\zeta )\geq Q(\xi _0)+2\operatorname {Re}\zeta (v_0) +2\operatorname {Re}\zeta (w)-L|w|_ k^2. \]

Taking the supremum over $w$ yields

\[ Q(\xi _0+\zeta )\geq Q(\xi _0)+2\operatorname {Re}\zeta (v_0) +L^{-1}|\zeta |_{k^*}^2. \]

Set $c=L^{-1}$ and $\Phi =Q-c|\cdot |_{k^*}^2$. After subtracting the square, this inequality gives a real affine function on the whole dual fiber that lies below $\Phi $ and equals $\Phi $ at $\xi _0$. Hence $\Phi $ is the supremum of such affine functions, and therefore convex. Neither uniqueness of the maximizer nor real convexity of $G$ has been used. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.17. If $E$ is ample and has a strongly pseudoconvex squared Finsler metric with semipositive Kobayashi curvature, then $E^*$ admits a smooth complex norm whose square is strictly plurisubharmonic off zero and locally strongly convex in the real fibers.

Proof. By proposition 4.6.15, the polar square is a positive continuous plurisubharmonic complex homogeneous function of degree two, and by lemma 4.6.16 it has the strong convexity in the fibers required in proposition 4.6.9. Applying proposition 4.6.12 to $F=E^*$, whose dual $F^*=E$ is ample, gives the positive smooth strictly plurisubharmonic homogeneous function $H$ on $E^*$. Hence proposition 4.6.9 applies. Its proof uses theorem 4.6.8, which preserves strong convexity in the fibers, and the square root of the resulting function is the asserted smooth norm. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.18. Let $F$ be the underlying smooth bundle of $F_0$ in theorem 4.6.4, with a holomorphic structure in the neighborhood given there. If $F^*$ is ample, then $F^*$ admits no strongly pseudoconvex squared Finsler metric with semipositive Kobayashi curvature.

Proof. Apply corollary 4.6.17 to $E=F^*$. Such a metric would produce a smooth complex norm on $F$ with plurisubharmonic square, contrary to theorem 4.6.4. $\square$

The corollary applies only near a split bundle whose line summands have positive Gauduchon degrees, and it is not vacuous only if such a neighborhood contains holomorphic structures with ample dual. On Hirzebruch surfaces, theorem 4.6.30 gives stable ample bundles whose duals lie in this neighborhood. Since the polar square need not be smooth, the regularization of 4.6.1 is an essential step of the argument.

4.6.4 Ample comparison quotients on Hirzebruch surfaces

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

To apply corollary 4.6.18, we need ample vector bundles whose duals are close to a split bundle $F_1\oplus F_2$ with $\deg _{\Theta _ s}F_ s>0$ for suitable test forms $\Theta _ s$. The split bundle $F_1^*\oplus F_2^*$ is not ample itself: otherwise its quotient line bundles $F_ s^*$ would be ample, hence carry positively curved metrics, and each $F_ s$ would have negative degree for every test form. The ample bundles must therefore be obtained by deformation. On a Hirzebruch surface we choose a split bundle $E_0=L_1\oplus L_2$ whose dual summands have positive degree for suitable Kähler forms, and we construct an ample bundle $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ with the same Chern classes as a quotient of a trivial bundle. Both bundles belong to the family of bundles with these Chern classes that are prioritary for the ruling. This family is irreducible, which will allow us to find ample bundles in every neighborhood of $E_0$.

Let $e\geq 0$ be an integer, and let

\[ X=\mathbb F_ e=\mathbb P_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal O\oplus \mathcal O(e)), \qquad \pi :X\longrightarrow \mathbb P^1, \]

be the Hirzebruch surface, where the projective bundle parametrizes lines. Write $S$ for the section defined by the subbundle $\mathcal O(e)$ and $f$ for a fiber of $\pi $, called a ruling fiber; we identify line bundles with divisor classes and write $\mathcal O_ X(aS+bf)$. The normal bundle of $S$ is $\mathcal O(-e)$, so

\[ S^2=-e,\qquad S\cdot f=1,\qquad f^2=0. \]

The dual of the tautological line bundle is $\mathcal O_ X(S)$. A line bundle of degree zero on the ruling fibers is pulled back from $\mathbb P^1$: its direct image has rank one, and the evaluation map from the pullback of the direct image is an isomorphism on each fiber. Consequently $\operatorname {Pic}(X)=\mathbb ZS\oplus \mathbb Zf$.

Lemma 4.6.19. For integers $a\geq 0$ and $b$,

\[ \pi _*\mathcal O_ X(aS+bf)= \bigoplus _{i=0}^ a\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(b-ie), \qquad R^1\pi _*\mathcal O_ X(aS+bf)=0. \]

A class $aS+bf$ is effective exactly when $a,b\geq 0$. If $a\geq 1$ and $b>ae$, its line bundle is very ample. In particular,

\[ H=S+(3e+1)f, \qquad H^2=5e+2, \qquad h^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(H))=5e+4. \]

The canonical class is $K_ X=-2S-(e+2)f$.

Proof. Over a trivializing chart of the base, sections of $\mathcal O_ X(aS)$ are homogeneous polynomials of degree $a$ in the fiber coordinates. Their transition law gives

\[ \pi _*\mathcal O_ X(aS)= \operatorname {Sym}^ a(\mathcal O\oplus \mathcal O(-e)), \]

and twisting by the base gives the formula. The first cohomology of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(a)$ on each fiber vanishes, which gives the assertion on the higher direct image. If $a<0$, restriction to a general ruling fiber excludes a nonzero section. For $a\geq 0$, the direct-image formula gives a nonzero section exactly when $b\geq 0$.

Suppose that $a\geq 1$ and $b>ae$. All line bundles in the direct image then have degree at least one. Fiber polynomials of degree $a$ separate points and tangent directions within a fiber. To separate points over different base points, choose a fiber monomial that is nonzero at the first point and a coefficient section vanishing at the second base point but not at the first. At any point, choose such a nonzero monomial and a coefficient section with a simple zero at its base point; the resulting global section vanishes there, has nonzero derivative in a prescribed base direction and has zero vertical derivative. The complete linear system therefore separates points and first jets, so it defines an embedding. The formulas for $H^2$ and $h^0(H)$ follow from the intersection numbers and from the direct image $\mathcal O(3e+1)\oplus \mathcal O(2e+1)$. Finally, the relative Euler sequence gives $K_{X/\mathbb P^1}=-2S-ef$, and adding the pullback of the canonical class of $\mathbb P^1$ proves the last identity. $\square$

Put

\[ L_1=\mathcal O_ X(5S-f),\qquad L_2=\mathcal O_ X(-S+(12e+5)f),\qquad E_0=L_1\oplus L_2. \]

Their Chern classes satisfy

\[ c_1(E_0)=4H,\qquad c_2(E_0)=65e+26=13H^2. \]

An ample bundle with these Chern classes can be constructed as a quotient of a trivial bundle. The main point of the proof is to make the fibers of the associated map to projective space finite, which requires excluding common curves of the defining sections uniformly over the projective parameter.

Proposition 4.6.20. There exist linearly independent sections $a_0,\ldots ,a_3$ of $\mathcal O_ X(H)$ with no common zero. For any such quadruple and a general quadruple $b_0,\ldots ,b_3$ of sections of $\mathcal O_ X(3H)$, the sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow \mathcal O_ X(-H)\oplus \mathcal O_ X(-3H) \xrightarrow {(a,b)}\mathcal O_ X^{\oplus 4} \longrightarrow E_{\mathrm{amp}}\longrightarrow 0 \]

has a locally free ample cokernel $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ of rank two, with $c_1(E_{\mathrm{amp}})=4H$ and $c_2(E_{\mathrm{amp}})=13H^2$. Here general means outside a proper Zariski-closed subset of the space of quadruples.

Proof. Step 1: the quadruple $a$. Since $\mathcal O_ X(H)$ is globally generated, a common zero at a fixed point imposes four independent linear conditions on a quadruple $a$. The incidence variety of quadruples with a common zero at some point of the surface therefore has codimension at least two in the space of quadruples, and its image there is closed by properness. Linear independence is also a nonempty open condition, because $h^0(H)\geq 4$, and the two open conditions intersect. Fix such an $a$ and write

\[ V=H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(3H))^{\oplus 4},\qquad N=\dim V. \]

Step 2: local freeness. At a fixed point $x$, the map $(a,b)$ drops rank exactly when $b(x)$ is proportional to the nonzero vector $a(x)$. Since evaluation on $V$ is surjective, this is a condition of codimension three. Its incidence over $X$ has dimension at most $N-1$, and its image in $V$ is closed. Outside that image the cokernel is locally free of rank two.

Step 3: the map to projective space. Dualizing the sequence identifies $E_{\mathrm{amp}}^*$ with a subbundle of $(\mathbb C^4)^*\otimes \mathcal O_ X$. This inclusion defines a proper morphism

\[ q_ b:\mathbb P(E_{\mathrm{amp}}^*)\longrightarrow \mathbb P((\mathbb C^4)^*), \qquad \mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E_{\mathrm{amp}}^*)}(1) =q_ b^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^3}(1). \]

For $w=[w_0:\cdots :w_3]$, its fiber is the common zero scheme of

\[ s_ w=\sum _ iw_ i a_ i\in H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(H)), \qquad t_ w=\sum _ iw_ i b_ i\in H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(3H)). \]

Linear independence of $a$ gives $s_ w\neq 0$ for every $w$.

Step 4: no common curves. A nonzero effective subdivisor $D$ of $\operatorname {div}(s_ w)$ has class $uS+vf$, where

\[ u\in \{ 0,1\} ,\qquad 0\leq v\leq 3e+1, \qquad (u,v)\neq (0,0). \]

Write $|D|=\mathbb P H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(D))$ for the complete linear system. For each of these finitely many classes $D_0$, let $T_{D_0}$ parametrize the triples $(w,[\sigma ],[\tau ])$ with

\[ [\sigma ]\in |D_0|,\qquad [\tau ]\in |H-D_0|, \qquad [s_ w]=[\sigma \tau ]. \]

Since products of nonzero sections are nonzero, this is a closed projective parameter scheme. Its fiber over $w$ consists of the finitely many effective subdivisors of the fixed divisor $\operatorname {div}(s_ w)$, counted with their possible multiplicities. Thus $\dim T_{D_0}\leq 3$.

Vanishing on a nonzero effective divisor $D$ imposes at least four independent conditions on $H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(3H))$. Indeed, choose an integral curve $C$ in its support. Very ampleness gives sections $r_0,r_1$ of $\mathcal O_ X(H)$ with nonconstant ratio on $C$. The four cubic monomials in their restrictions are linearly independent, since a linear relation would make the nonconstant ratio algebraic over $\mathbb C$, hence constant.

For fixed $(w,[\sigma ],[\tau ])$, the map $V\to H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(3H))$, $b\mapsto t_ w$, is surjective. The condition that $t_ w$ vanish on $D=\operatorname {div}(\sigma )$ therefore has codimension at least four in $V$. This condition is closed in families: the universal multiplication map

\[ H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(3H-D_0))\otimes \mathcal O_{|D_0|}(-1) \longrightarrow H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(3H))\otimes \mathcal O_{|D_0|} \]

is injective on fibers, so its image is a subbundle, and the condition is the vanishing of the image of $t_ w$ in the quotient bundle. Its incidence over $T_{D_0}$ has dimension at most $N-1$, and its image in $V$ is closed.

Step 5: conclusion. Choose $b$ outside these finitely many proper closed images and outside the image of Step 2. Then $s_ w$ and $t_ w$ have no common curve for any $w$, so every fiber of $q_ b$ is finite. A proper morphism with finite fibers is finite, and the pullback of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^3}(1)$ by a finite morphism is ample; hence $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ is ample. Finally, the Whitney formula gives

\[ c(E_{\mathrm{amp}})=\frac1{(1-H)(1-3H)}=1+4H+13H^2. \]
$\square$

A vector bundle $E$ on $X$ is called prioritary for the ruling if $\operatorname {Ext}^2_ X(E,E(-f))=0$. Since all ruling fibers are linearly equivalent, this condition does not depend on the chosen fiber, and by Serre duality it is equivalent to

\[ \operatorname {Hom}_ X(E,E(K_ X+f))=0. \]

Prioritary bundles of fixed rank and Chern classes form an irreducible family, by the theorem of Walter recalled in 4.6.6.

Lemma 4.6.21. Both $E_0$ and $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ are prioritary for the ruling.

Proof. Write $D=K_ X+f=-2S-(e+1)f$. The summands of $\operatorname {End}(E_0)(D)$ have classes

\[ D,\quad D,\quad 4S-(13e+7)f,\quad -8S+(11e+5)f. \]

Each has a negative coefficient in the basis $S,f$, so by lemma 4.6.19 none of them has a nonzero section. This proves the required vanishing of $\operatorname {Hom}$ for $E_0$.

Precomposition with the surjection $\mathcal O_ X^4\to E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ gives an injection

\[ \operatorname {Hom}(E_{\mathrm{amp}},E_{\mathrm{amp}}(D)) \hookrightarrow H^0(X,E_{\mathrm{amp}}(D))^4. \]

Twist the defining sequence by $D$. Its middle term has no sections. For $j=1,3$, Serre duality and the formula of lemma 4.6.19 give

\[ \begin{aligned} H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X(D-jH))& =H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X(jH-f))^*,\\ \pi _*\mathcal O_ X(jH-f)& =\bigoplus _{i=0}^ j\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(j(3e+1)-1-ie). \end{aligned} \]

The higher direct image vanishes, and every degree in this sum is at least $j(2e+1)-1\geq 0$. Hence all these first cohomology groups vanish, and the long exact sequence of the twisted sequence gives $H^0(X,E_{\mathrm{amp}}(D))=0$. This proves the vanishing of $\operatorname {Hom}$ for $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$. $\square$

Proposition 4.6.22. For $F_ s=L_ s^*$, $s=1,2$, there are Kähler forms $\eta _ s$ with $\deg _{\eta _ s}F_ s=1$. Consequently, the split bundle $E_0^*=F_1\oplus F_2$ satisfies the hypotheses of theorem 4.6.4.

Proof. The integral classes

\[ A_1=6S+(6e+1)f,\qquad A_2=S+(13e+6)f \]

are very ample by lemma 4.6.19. Pullbacks of normalized Fubini–Study forms under the corresponding embeddings give Kähler forms $\eta _ s$ representing $c_1(\mathcal O_ X(A_ s))$. A direct computation of intersection numbers gives

so the degrees of the duals are equal to one. Since $n=2$, the closed positive $(1,1)$-forms $\eta _ s$ are admissible test forms $\Theta _ s$ in theorem 4.6.4. $\square$

4.6.5 Stable extensions approaching the split comparison bundle

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The bundles near $E_0$ that we construct will also be slope-stable for a fixed polarization. The split bundle $E_0$ is unstable, and the ample bundle $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ need not be stable. Nonsplit extensions of $L_2$ by $L_1$ have the same Chern classes as $E_0$, are stable, and converge to $E_0$ as the extension class tends to zero; they are not ample, however. The next subsection combines them with $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$.

Keep the Hirzebruch surface $X=\mathbb F_ e$, its ruling and the line bundles of 4.6.4. Thus

\[ L_1=\mathcal O_ X(5S-f),\qquad L_2=\mathcal O_ X(-S+(12e+5)f),\qquad E_0=L_1\oplus L_2. \]

We use the very ample polarization $P=4H-f=4S+(12e+3)f$. For a torsion-free coherent sheaf $E$ of positive rank on $X$, put

\[ \deg _ PE=c_1(E)\cdot P, \qquad \mu _ P(E)=\frac{\deg _ PE}{\operatorname {rank}E}. \]

A vector bundle $V$ is $\mu _ P$-stable if $\mu _ P(M)<\mu _ P(V)$ for every coherent subsheaf $M\subset V$ with $0<\operatorname {rank}M<\operatorname {rank}V$. Since $\mu _ P(L_2)>\mu _ P(E_0)$ by the computation below, the split bundle $E_0$ is not $\mu _ P$-stable.

Proposition 4.6.23. The extension space has dimension

\[ \dim \operatorname {Ext}^1_ X(L_2,L_1)=105e+35. \]

Every nonsplit extension

\[ 0\longrightarrow L_1\longrightarrow V\xrightarrow {q}L_2 \longrightarrow 0 \]

is a locally free, $\mu _ P$-stable bundle with $c_1(V)=4H$ and $c_2(V)=13H^2$.

Proof. Extensions of line bundles are locally free: the quotient is free on a small trivializing neighborhood, so the sequence splits there. Their classes form

\[ \operatorname {Ext}^1_ X(L_2,L_1) =H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X(6S-(12e+6)f)). \]

By lemma 4.6.19, the higher direct image of this line bundle vanishes and its direct image is the sum of the seven line bundles $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-12e-6-ie)$, $0\leq i\leq 6$. Their first cohomology groups have total dimension

\[ \sum _{i=0}^6(12e+5+ie)=105e+35>0. \]

The Chern classes follow from the extension sequence. Direct computations of intersection numbers give

\[ \mu _ P(L_1)=40e+11,\qquad \mu _ P(V)=40e+14, \qquad \mu _ P(L_2)=40e+17. \]

It suffices to test line-bundle subsheaves $M\hookrightarrow V$. Indeed, a rank-one torsion-free subsheaf on a smooth surface has a line bundle as its double dual, and its inclusion extends across the finitely many missing points by Hartogs extension in local bundle frames. The extended map remains injective and has the same first Chern class. This argument does not require the quotient to be locally free.

If the composite $M\to L_2$ is zero, then $M$ maps nontrivially into $L_1$, so $\deg _ P M\leq \mu _ P(L_1)<\mu _ P(V)$. Otherwise its zero divisor $D$ is effective and $M=L_2(-D)$. If $D=0$, the composite is an isomorphism and its inverse splits $q$, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $D\neq 0$, and

\[ \deg _ P M=\mu _ P(V)+3-D\cdot P. \]

Every effective class is $aS+bf$ with $a,b\geq 0$, and

\[ S\cdot P=8e+3,\qquad f\cdot P=4. \]

For $e\geq 1$, every nonzero effective divisor has $P$-degree at least four, which proves the strict slope inequality.

For $e=0$, the only nonzero effective class of $P$-degree at most three is $S$. A lift of multiplication by a nonzero section $s\in H^0(X,\mathcal O_ X(S))$ from $L_2(-S)$ to $V$ would make the pullback of the nonzero extension class vanish. That pullback is the image of the class under the map

\[ H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X(6S-6f)) \xrightarrow {\ \cdot s\ }H^1(X,\mathcal O_ X(7S-6f)). \]

On $X=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, order the factors so that $\mathcal O_ X(S)=\mathcal O(1,0)$ and $\mathcal O_ X(f)=\mathcal O(0,1)$. The Künneth formula identifies this map with

\[ \bigl [H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(6)) \xrightarrow {\ \cdot s\ }H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(7))\bigr ] \otimes \operatorname {Id}_{H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-6))}. \]

Multiplication by a nonzero section is injective on global sections, and tensoring with a vector space preserves injectivity. Thus the nonzero extension class cannot pull back to zero. This excludes the possible equality of slopes and proves stability also for $e=0$. $\square$

Proposition 4.6.24. Every extension of $L_2$ by $L_1$, including the split extension, is prioritary for the ruling and is not ample. Its restriction to every ruling fiber is $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(5)\oplus \mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$.

Proof. Put $D=K_ X+f=-2S-(e+1)f$. The four line bundles $L_ i^*\otimes L_ j(D)$ have classes

\[ D,\quad D,\quad 4S-(13e+7)f,\quad -8S+(11e+5)f. \]

Each has a negative coefficient in the basis $S,f$, and hence no nonzero section. Applying $\operatorname {Hom}_ X(L_ i,\cdot )$ to the extension twisted by $D$ gives $\operatorname {Hom}_ X(L_ i,V(D))=0$ for $i=1,2$. Applying $\operatorname {Hom}_ X(\cdot ,V(D))$ to the original extension then gives $\operatorname {Hom}_ X(V,V(D))=0$. By Serre duality this is the prioritary condition $\operatorname {Ext}^2_ X(V,V(-f))=0$.

Restriction to a ruling fiber is exact, because the quotient is locally free. The restricted line bundles have degrees five and minus one, and the restricted extension splits, since $\operatorname {Ext}^1_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal O(-1),\mathcal O(5)) =H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(6))=0$. The quotient of negative degree on this curve excludes ampleness. $\square$

Proposition 4.6.25. On the smooth bundle underlying $E_0$ there is a holomorphic one-parameter family of Dolbeault operators $\bar\partial _ t=\bar\partial _0+tA$, $t\in \mathbb C$, such that for $t\ne 0$ the holomorphic bundle defined by $\bar\partial _ t$ is $\mu _ P$-stable and prioritary, but not ample. Proof. Choose a nonzero extension class and a smooth Dolbeault representative $\eta \in \Omega ^{0,1}(X,L_2^*\otimes L_1)$ of it, so that $\bar\partial \eta =0$. On the smooth direct sum, set

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix} 0 & \eta \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}. \]

The covariant Dolbeault derivative of $A$ is zero and $A\wedge A=0$, so $\bar\partial _ t^2=0$. Adding the Dolbeault operator of the base $\mathbb C$ of the parameter $t$ also gives an integrable operator on $X\times \mathbb C$, because the family depends holomorphically on $t$. This defines a holomorphic family of bundles whose extension class is $t[\eta ]$. It is nonsplit for $t\neq 0$, so the two preceding propositions apply. For a fixed smooth background connection and fixed metrics,

\[ \| \bar\partial _ t-\bar\partial _0\| _{C^ m} =|t|\, \| A\| _{C^ m}\longrightarrow 0, \]

and the norm $\| A\| _{C^ m}$ is finite for every $m$ because $X$ is compact. $\square$

Thus stability and ampleness have been realized separately in the prioritary family of $E_0$: the nonsplit extensions are stable but not ample, while $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ is ample. The deformation argument of theorem 4.6.30 joins the stable locus to the ample one and provides stable ample bundles arbitrarily close to $E_0$.

4.6.6 Deforming to stable ample bundles

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We keep the Hirzebruch surface $X=\mathbb F_ e$ and the data $H,P,L_1,L_2,E_0$ of 4.6.4, 4.6.5. The ample quotient $E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ and a nonsplit extension are two prioritary bundles with the same Chern classes as $E_0$; the former is ample and the latter is stable. Prioritary bundles with these invariants form an irreducible family in which ampleness and stability are open conditions, so bundles with both properties are dense in the family, and in particular they accumulate at $E_0$. We first prove the required openness assertions for algebraic families.

Throughout this subsection, $T$ denotes a smooth complex algebraic variety. A flat family is a coherent sheaf $\mathcal E$ on $X\times T$ that is flat over $T$, and its fiber $\mathcal E_ t$ at $t\in T$ is its restriction to $X\times \{ t\} $.

Lemma 4.6.26. If a flat family $\mathcal E$ has a locally free fiber at $t_0\in T$, then $\mathcal E$ is a vector bundle on $X\times T'$ for some Zariski-open neighborhood $T'$ of $t_0$.

Proof. At a point of $X\times \{ t_0\} $, lift a local basis of the fiber to $\mathcal E$. By Nakayama's lemma, the resulting map from a free module of finite rank is surjective near that point. Tensor its kernel sequence with the residue field of $t_0$. Flatness over $T$ makes the first Tor term zero, so the kernel restricts to the kernel of the chosen isomorphism on the fiber, namely zero, and Nakayama's lemma shows that the kernel vanishes near that point. Thus $\mathcal E$ is locally free near every point of the central fiber. The locus where $\mathcal E$ is not locally free is closed. Since the projection $X\times T\to T$ is proper, the image of that locus is closed and misses $t_0$, and we remove this image. $\square$

Lemma 4.6.27. For a vector bundle $\mathcal E$ on $X\times T$, the parameters $t$ for which $\mathcal E_ t$ is ample form a Zariski-open subset of $T$.

Proof. Put $Y=\mathbb P_{X\times T}(\mathcal E^*)$, with the line convention, and let $p:Y\to T$ be the projection, a projective flat morphism. Write $\mathcal L=\mathcal O_ Y(1)$. If $\mathcal E_{t_0}$ is ample, then $\mathcal L_{t_0}$ is ample. Choose $k$ so that its $k$th power is very ample and has vanishing higher cohomology. By Serre vanishing, semicontinuity, and cohomology and base change, after shrinking $T$ around $t_0$ the sheaf $\mathcal V=p_*\mathcal L^ k$ is locally free and commutes with restriction to fibers. After a further shrinking, the evaluation map $p^*\mathcal V\to \mathcal L^ k$ is surjective: its cokernel vanishes on the central fiber, and the image of its support under the proper map $p$ is closed and misses $t_0$.

Evaluation gives a proper morphism

\[ g:Y\longrightarrow \mathbb P_ T(\mathcal V^*), \qquad g^*\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal L^ k, \]

whose restriction to the central fiber is a closed immersion. The locus where $g$ is quasi-finite is open; its complement has closed image in $T$ by properness and misses $t_0$, so after a further shrinking $g$ is proper and quasi-finite, hence finite. For a finite morphism, pushforward commutes with base change. The cokernel of

\[ \mathcal O_{\mathbb P_ T(\mathcal V^*)}\longrightarrow g_*\mathcal O_ Y \]

therefore vanishes on the central fiber, and its support projects to a closed subset of $T$ missing $t_0$. After removing that subset, the map is surjective, so $g$ is a closed immersion. Consequently $\mathcal L^ k$ is very ample on every fiber, and $\mathcal E_ t$ is ample there. $\square$

Lemma 4.6.28. For a family $\mathcal E$ of rank-two vector bundles on $X\times T$ whose fibers have a fixed first Chern class, the parameters $t$ for which $\mathcal E_ t$ is $\mu _ P$-stable form a Zariski-open subset of $T$.

Proof. Fix a stable fiber at $t_0$ and put $\nu =c_1(\mathcal E_ t)\cdot P/2$, which is independent of $t$. Choose $k\geq 1$ such that $\mathcal E_{t_0}^*(kP)$ is globally generated and has vanishing higher cohomology. The argument with Serre vanishing, base change and evaluation used in the previous proof gives, over a neighborhood of $t_0$, maps

\[ \mathcal E_ t\longrightarrow \mathcal O_ X(kP)^{\oplus N} \]

that are injective on every fiber. By the double-dual argument for rank-one subsheaves in the proof of proposition 4.6.23, a destabilizing subsheaf of $\mathcal E_ t$ yields a nonzero map from a line bundle $M=\mathcal O_ X(aS+bf)$ with $\deg _ P M\geq \nu $. At least one component of its composite with the injection above is nonzero, and since effective classes have nonnegative coefficients,

\[ a\leq 4k,\qquad b\leq (12e+3)k. \]

Since $\deg _ P M=(8e+3)a+4b$, the inequality $\deg _ PM\geq \nu $ also gives

\[ a\geq \frac{\nu -4(12e+3)k}{8e+3}, \qquad b\geq \frac{\nu -4k(8e+3)}4. \]

Only finitely many integral pairs $(a,b)$ satisfy these four bounds. For each such pair with $\deg _ P M\geq \nu $, semicontinuity makes the set

\[ \{ t:H^0(X,\mathcal E_ t\otimes M^*)\neq 0\} \]

closed, and it does not contain $t_0$. Conversely, a nonzero map from such an $M$ is injective as a map of sheaves and violates stability. Thus the set of parameters with unstable fibers is, on this neighborhood, exactly the finite union of these closed subsets, and removing the union proves the claim. $\square$

Lemma 4.6.29. Let $T$ be smooth and irreducible, let $U\subset T$ be a nonempty Zariski-open subset, and let $\mathcal E$ be a vector bundle on $X\times T$. For every $t_0\in T$, there are points $t_ j\in U$ tending to $t_0$ in the analytic topology, together with smooth complex-linear identifications of the bundles underlying $\mathcal E_{t_ j}$ and $\mathcal E_{t_0}$, under which the Dolbeault operators of the fibers converge in the $C^ m$ norm for every integer $m\geq 0$. The dual operators converge in the same sense.

Proof. The proper algebraic closed subset $T\setminus U$ has empty interior in the analytic topology. In an analytic coordinate ball $B$ around $t_0$, choose $t_ j\in U\cap B$ with $t_ j\to t_0$. Choose a smooth complex connection on $\mathcal E|_{X\times B}$. Parallel transport along radial paths in $B$, keeping $x\in X$ fixed, gives identifications

\[ \Phi _ t:\mathcal E_{t_0}^{\mathrm{sm}}\longrightarrow \mathcal E_ t^{\mathrm{sm}},\qquad \Phi _{t_0}=\operatorname {id}, \]

depending smoothly on $(x,t)$, where the superscript $\mathrm{sm}$ denotes the underlying smooth complex bundle. These identifications need not be holomorphic. Transport the Dolbeault operators by these maps and write

\[ \bar\partial _ t=\Phi _ t^{-1}\bar\partial _{\mathcal E_ t}\Phi _ t, \qquad A_ t=\bar\partial _ t-\bar\partial _{\mathcal E_{t_0}}. \]

Since both operators satisfy the same Leibniz rule, $A_ t$ is an endomorphism-valued $(0,1)$-form. Its coefficients are smooth in $(x,t)$, and $A_{t_0}=0$. Fix background metrics and a smooth connection on the central bundle. Compactness of $X$ and continuity of every finite collection of derivatives give

\[ \| A_{t_ j}\| _{C^ m(X)}\longrightarrow 0 \qquad \text{for every integer }m\geq 0. \]

The dual identifications are $(\Phi _ t^{-1})^*$. In transported dual frames the perturbation is $-A_ t^{\mathsf T}$, so the same convergence holds for the dual Dolbeault operators. $\square$

We use Walter's theorem on prioritary stacks: on a birationally ruled surface, a nonempty stack of prioritary torsion-free sheaves with fixed rank $r\geq 2$ and fixed numerical Chern classes is smooth and irreducible. Here prioritary means that $\operatorname {Ext}^2(E,E(-f))=0$ for every ruling fiber $f$; on $X$ all ruling fibers are linearly equivalent. The theorem concerns the full prioritary stack and has no semistability hypothesis.

Theorem 4.6.30. Fix an identification $\mathcal E_{t_0}\simeq E_0$. The variety $T$ is smooth because the stack and the chart are smooth. Shrink $T$ to an irreducible neighborhood of $t_0$ and use lemma 4.6.26 to make $\mathcal E$ a vector bundle on the whole product. The inverse image $q^{-1}(\mathcal U)$ is dense: every nonempty open $T'\subset T$ has nonempty open image $q(T')$ under the smooth map $q$, and this image meets $\mathcal U$. 4.6.7 A fixed Stein hypersurface for all Finsler metrics

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Corollary 4.6.31 shows that no Finsler metric on $E$ has semipositive Kobayashi curvature, but it does not locate the failure. For a single metric, a point where the curvature is not semipositive gives a local hypersurface through that point on which the metric has a nondegenerate critical point with many positive Levi directions. Holomorphic approximation on a Stein manifold allows one hypersurface to serve for all metrics at once.

In this subsection, $X$ is a connected smooth projective variety of dimension $n\geq 1$, and $E\to X$ is an algebraic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 2$, viewed as a holomorphic bundle. We use the squared Finsler metrics and the convention for Kobayashi curvature of 4.6.3. A compact subset $K$ of a Stein manifold $Y$ is holomorphically convex if it coincides with its holomorphic hull $\{ y\in Y:|f(y)|\leq \sup _ K|f|\text{ for all }f\in \mathcal O(Y)\} $. We use the classical Oka–Weil theorem in the following form: a function holomorphic near a holomorphically convex compact subset of a Stein manifold can be approximated uniformly there by global holomorphic functions. We also use polynomial Runge approximation in one variable on a union of two disjoint closed disks.

Lemma 4.6.32. Let $K,L$ be holomorphically convex compact subsets of a Stein manifold $Y$. If a global holomorphic function maps them into disjoint closed disks in $\mathbb C$, then $K\cup L$ is holomorphically convex.

Proof. Write $h$ for the function and $\Delta _ K,\Delta _ L$ for the disks. Their union is polynomially convex, so testing with polynomials in $h$ shows that $h$ maps the holomorphic hull of $K\cup L$ into $\Delta _ K\cup \Delta _ L$. Suppose that $p\notin K\cup L$ and $h(p)\in \Delta _ K$. Holomorphic convexity of $K$ gives $a\in \mathcal O(Y)$ with $|a(p)|>\sup _ K|a|$. Choose polynomials tending uniformly to one on $\Delta _ K$ and to zero on $\Delta _ L$. Their compositions with $h$, multiplied by $a$, converge to $a$ at $p$ and on $K$, and to zero on $L$. A sufficiently good approximation therefore separates $p$ from $K\cup L$. The case $h(p)\in \Delta _ L$ is identical, with $K$ and $L$ interchanged. Thus no point outside $K\cup L$ belongs to its hull. $\square$

Lemma 4.6.33. Let $U$ be a connected smooth affine variety and let $q\geq 0$ be an integer. Put $B=U\times \mathbb C^*\times \mathbb C^ q$, with coordinates $(x,t,s)$, and for $\lambda \in \mathbb C^*$ let $d_\lambda (x,t,s)=(x,\lambda t,\lambda s)$. Suppose that $t(C_ j)$ lies in the disk $|t-t_ j|\leq |t_ j|/4$ for some $t_ j\neq 0$. Then there are $\lambda _ j\in \mathbb C^*$ and $f\in \mathcal O(B)$ such that

\[ \sup _{d_{\lambda _ j}(C_ j)} |f-\lambda _ j g_ j\circ d_{\lambda _ j}^{-1}|<|\lambda _ j|\delta _ j \qquad (j\geq 1). \]

Proof. Choose an exhaustion $(L_ j)_{j\geq 0}$ of the affine, hence Stein, manifold $B$ by holomorphically convex compact sets with $L_ j\subset \operatorname {int}L_{j+1}$. We construct $f_ j\in \mathcal O(B)$ and compact sets $K_ j$ inductively, starting with $f_0=0$ and $K_0=L_0$.

At stage $j\geq 1$, choose $M>0$ with $t(K_{j-1})\subset \{ |t|\leq M\} $, and choose $R>4M/3$. Dilation by $\lambda _ j=R/t_ j$ moves the $t$-projection of $C_ j$ into the disk $|t-R|\leq R/4$, which is disjoint from $|t|\leq M$. For the rest of the induction we replace $C_ j$, $g_ j$ and $\delta _ j$ by the dilated compact set $d_{\lambda _ j}(C_ j)$, the function $\lambda _ jg_ j\circ d_{\lambda _ j}^{-1}$ and the tolerance $|\lambda _ j|\delta _ j$, keeping the same notation. Lemma 4.6.32, applied with the function $t$, makes $K_{j-1}\cup C_ j$ holomorphically convex.

On disjoint neighborhoods of $K_{j-1}$ and $C_ j$, prescribe $f_{j-1}$ and $g_ j$, respectively. Oka–Weil approximation gives $f_ j\in \mathcal O(B)$ with

\[ \sup _{K_{j-1}}|f_ j-f_{j-1}|<\epsilon _ j, \qquad \sup _{C_ j}|f_ j-g_ j|<\epsilon _ j, \qquad \epsilon _ j=2^{-j-2}\min (1,\delta _1,\ldots ,\delta _ j). \]

Let $K_ j$ be a member of the fixed exhaustion containing $K_{j-1}\cup C_ j\cup L_ j$ in its interior. The interiors of the sets $K_ j$ exhaust $B$, and the summable errors make $f_ j$ converge locally uniformly to some $f\in \mathcal O(B)$. For a fixed $j$, every later approximation controls its error on $C_ j$, since $C_ j\subset K_{m-1}$ for $m>j$. Consequently

\[ \sup _{C_ j}|f-g_ j| \leq \sum _{m\geq j}\epsilon _ m \leq 2^{-j-1}\delta _ j<\delta _ j. \]

Returning to the original notation gives the stated tolerances. $\square$

Theorem 4.6.34. Suppose that $E$ admits no smooth strongly pseudoconvex squared Finsler metric with semipositive Kobayashi curvature. Then there is a connected smooth Stein hypersurface $\mathcal S\subset E^\times $, locally closed in the analytic topology, with the following property: for every smooth strongly pseudoconvex squared Finsler metric $G$ on $E$, the restriction $G|_{\mathcal S}$ has a nondegenerate critical point with at least $r$ positive Levi eigenvalues, and hence with at most $n-1$ negative ones.

Proof. Step 1: coordinates. An algebraic frame trivializes $E$ over a nonempty affine open subset $U\subset X$. Since $X$ is smooth and connected, it is irreducible, so $U$ is dense and connected. Write the fiber coordinates as $(w,t,s)\in \mathbb C\times \mathbb C\times \mathbb C^{r-2}$, and put $B=U\times \mathbb C^*\times \mathbb C^{r-2}$. We seek $\mathcal S$ as a graph $w=f(x,t,s)$ over $B$; such a graph avoids the zero section because $t\ne 0$.

Step 2: a local test for one metric. Fix a candidate metric $G$. Since its Kobayashi curvature is not semipositive, its horizontal Schur complement has a positive direction somewhere. This condition is open, so it holds at a point $v_0=(x_0,w_0,t_0,s_0)$ with $x_0\in U$, $t_0\neq 0$ and $G_ w(v_0)\neq 0$. Indeed, the first two conditions define a dense open set, and the zero set of $G_ w$ has empty interior, since vanishing of $G_ w$ on an open set would give $G_{w\bar w}=0$ there, contradicting the positivity of the vertical Hessian.

Let $H$ be the Levi-orthogonal complement of the vertical tangent space $V$ at $v_0$. The Euler identities in the proof of lemma 4.6.14 give

\[ K:=\ker \partial G(v_0)=H\oplus (V\cap K), \qquad \dim _{\mathbb C}(V\cap K)=r-1. \]

Indeed, $\partial G$ vanishes on $H$, and its value on the vertical radial vector is $G(v_0)>0$. The two summands are Levi orthogonal, the vertical summand is positive definite, and the horizontal summand has a positive direction. Thus the Levi form of $G$ restricted to $K$ has at least $r$ positive eigenvalues.

Since $G_ w(v_0)\neq 0$, the space $K$ is the graph of a linear map defined on $T_{b_0}^{1,0}B$, where $b_0=(x_0,t_0,s_0)$. Choose a local holomorphic function $g$ near $b_0$ with $g(b_0)=w_0$ whose graph has tangent space $K$ at $v_0$. Then $F=G\circ \operatorname {Graph}(g)$ has a critical point at $b_0$, and its Levi form there is the restriction of the Levi form of $G$ to $K$. Keep this first jet of $g$ fixed and vary its quadratic jet. The mixed second derivatives of $F$ do not change, while in local coordinates $z$ on $B$ the pure second derivatives satisfy

\[ F_{\alpha \beta }(b_0)=A_{\alpha \beta }+G_ w(v_0)g_{\alpha \beta }(b_0), \]

where $A$ is fixed. Since $G_ w(v_0)\ne 0$, we can prescribe the pure quadratic part of $F$ to be $\tau \operatorname {Re}\sum _\alpha z_\alpha ^2$, whose real Hessian has eigenvalues $\pm 2\tau $. If $\tau $ is larger than half the operator norm of the real Hessian of the fixed mixed part, the full real Hessian is invertible by the Neumann-series criterion. The critical point is then nondegenerate, with the same Levi form.

Step 3: stability of the test. Choose compact neighborhoods $C'\subset \operatorname {int}C$ of $b_0$ on which $g$ is defined, with $C$ holomorphically convex and $t(C)\subset \{ |t-t_0|\leq |t_0|/4\} $. A small closed Euclidean ball in a closed affine embedding of $B$ is such a set $C$, since linear functions separate points outside the ball from the ball. Fix a smooth Hermitian background metric on $E$ and its compact unit sphere bundle $\Sigma $. By homogeneity, the $C^2$ distance between metrics on $\Sigma $ controls their $C^2$ distance on each fixed compact subset of $E^\times $. Persistence of nondegenerate critical points and openness of the conditions on the Levi eigenvalues therefore give a neighborhood $\mathcal V_ G$ of $G|_\Sigma $ for the $C^2$ topology and a number $\delta _ G>0$ with the following property: for every metric whose restriction to $\Sigma $ lies in $\mathcal V_ G$ and every holomorphic function within $\delta _ G$ of $g$ uniformly on $C$, the restriction of the metric to the graph of the function still has a nondegenerate critical point over $C'$ with at least $r$ positive Levi eigenvalues. Here the Cauchy estimates on $C'$ turn uniform closeness on $C$ into $C^2$ closeness on $C'$.

Step 4: one hypersurface for all metrics. The restrictions of the candidate metrics to $\Sigma $ form a subspace of the separable metrizable space $C^2(\Sigma )$. This subspace is second countable, hence Lindelöf, so the cover by the neighborhoods $\mathcal V_ G$ has a countable subcover $(\mathcal V_ j)_{j\geq 1}$, with associated data $C_ j,g_ j,\delta _ j$. For $D_\lambda (x,w,t,s)=(x,\lambda w,\lambda t,\lambda s)$,

\[ G\circ D_\lambda =|\lambda |^2G. \]

Dilation therefore preserves critical points of restrictions to graphs, their real nondegeneracy and their Levi inertia, for every candidate metric. It maps the graph of a function $g$ over $C$ to the graph of $\lambda g\circ d_\lambda ^{-1}$ over $d_\lambda (C)$, with $d_\lambda $ as in lemma 4.6.33. A function within $|\lambda |\delta _ j$ of $\lambda g_ j\circ d_\lambda ^{-1}$ on $d_\lambda (C_ j)$ pulls back under the dilation to a function within $\delta _ j$ of $g_ j$ on $C_ j$. In particular, the metric neighborhood $\mathcal V_ j$ needs no change.

Applying lemma 4.6.33, we obtain one function $f\in \mathcal O(B)$ that satisfies all the dilated tests. Its graph $\mathcal S$ is biholomorphic to the connected affine Stein manifold $B$, and it is smooth and closed in the open subset $\{ x\in U,\ t\neq 0\} $ of $E^\times $. Every candidate metric belongs to some $\mathcal V_ j$, so its restriction to $\mathcal S$ has the required nondegenerate critical point. Since $\dim _{\mathbb C}\mathcal S=n+r-1$, at least $r$ positive Levi eigenvalues leave at most $n-1$ negative ones. $\square$

4.6.8 Concave neighborhoods and dual norms

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

A relatively compact neighborhood $\Omega $ of the zero section of a vector bundle $E$ determines a norm on $E^*$, the support function $\xi \mapsto \max _{v\in \overline\Omega \cap E_ x}|\xi (v)|$ of its fibers. If the boundary of $\Omega $ is sufficiently concave in the horizontal directions, holomorphic sections touching the boundary from inside show that this support norm has plurisubharmonic square, and supporting balls give it uniform convexity in the fibers. Consequently, when the dual norms are obstructed as in theorem 4.6.4, such neighborhoods cannot exist. In terms of exhaustion functions, this bounds the convexity of the complement of the zero section in the projective completion of $E$.

In this subsection, $E\to X$ is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 1$, and $n\geq 1$. Fix a smooth Hermitian metric $k$ on $E$, with real inner product $k_{\mathbb R}=\operatorname {Re}k$ and dual norm $|\cdot |_{k^*}$. For a smooth real function $f$, write $\mathcal L_ f$ for its complex Hessian. Let $\Omega $ be an open set with smooth boundary, defined near $\partial \Omega $ by $f<0$ with $\mathrm df\ne 0$ on $\partial \Omega $. We say that $\partial \Omega $ has at least $n$ negative Levi directions if, at every boundary point, $\mathcal L_ f$ has at least $n$ negative eigenvalues on $\ker \partial f$.

Lemma 4.6.36. Fix $\delta ,B>0$. On a finite-dimensional Hermitian vector space, put

\[ s_ c(\xi )=|\xi (c)|+\delta |\xi | \]

for covectors $\xi $ and vectors $c$ with $|c|\leq B$. There is $\gamma >0$, depending only on $\delta $ and $B$, such that $s_ c^2-\gamma |\xi |^2$ is real convex for every such $c$.

Proof. For $0<\tau \leq 1$, replace $s_ c$ by the norm

\[ s_{c,\tau }(\xi )=\sqrt{|\xi (c)|^2+\tau |\xi |^2}+\delta |\xi |, \]

which is smooth off zero. Its Lipschitz constant is at most $L=\sqrt{B^2+1}+\delta $, uniformly in $c$ and $\tau $. All derivatives below are real derivatives on the dual space. At $\xi \ne 0$, set $u=\xi /|\xi |$ and decompose a vector as $w=\alpha u+b$ with $b$ orthogonal to $u$ for the real inner product. Write $s=s_{c,\tau }$ and $A=\mathrm ds_\xi (w)$. Since the first summand of $s$ is convex and $s\geq \delta |\xi |$,

\[ D^2s(w,w)\geq \delta \frac{|b|^2}{|\xi |},\qquad D^2(s^2)(w,w)\geq 2A^2+2\delta ^2|b|^2. \]

Homogeneity gives $\mathrm ds_\xi (u)=s(\xi )/|\xi |\geq \delta $. Since $|\mathrm ds_\xi (b)|\leq L|b|$, we have

\[ |w|^2\leq \frac{2A^2}{\delta ^2} +\left(1+\frac{2L^2}{\delta ^2}\right)|b|^2. \]

Take $\gamma =\delta ^4/(\delta ^2+2L^2)$. Then $\gamma \leq \delta ^2/2$ and $\gamma (1+2L^2/\delta ^2)=\delta ^2$, so $D^2(s^2)(w,w)\geq 2\gamma |w|^2$.

The homogeneous function $s^2-\gamma |\xi |^2$ of degree two extends as a $C^1$ function at zero with differential zero. Its restriction to every real line is convex, including at zero, because its second derivative is nonnegative away from zero and its first derivatives match there. Thus it is convex on the whole space. Finally,

\[ 0\leq s_{c,\tau }(\xi )-s_ c(\xi )\leq \sqrt\tau \, |\xi |, \]

and the locally uniform limit as $\tau \downarrow 0$ proves the assertion with the same $\gamma $. $\square$

Theorem 4.6.37. Suppose that a relatively compact neighborhood $\Omega \Subset E$ of the zero section has smooth boundary with at least $n$ negative Levi directions. Then

\[ p(x,\xi )=\max _{v\in \overline\Omega \cap E_ x}|\xi (v)| \]

is a continuous complex norm on $E^*$ whose square $Q=p^2$ is plurisubharmonic on the total space of $E^*$. For some $\gamma >0$, each function $Q(x,\cdot )-\gamma |\cdot |_{k^*}^2$ is real convex. If $E$ is ample, $E^*$ also admits a smooth complex norm with strictly plurisubharmonic square off the zero section.

Proof. Step 1: basic bounds. Put $A_ x=\overline\Omega \cap E_ x$. By compactness of $X$ there are $0<a_0<b_0$ such that, in every fiber, the closed $k$-ball of radius $a_0$ is contained in $\Omega $ and $\overline\Omega $ lies in the open ball of radius $b_0$. The maximum defining $p$ is attained, and

\[ a_0|\xi |_{k^*}\leq p(x,\xi )\leq b_0|\xi |_{k^*}. \]

As a supremum of absolute values of linear functionals, $p$ is complex homogeneous and satisfies the triangle inequality.

Step 2: support points. Fix $\xi _0\ne 0$ and a maximizing point $v_0\in A_{x_0}$. It lies on $\partial \Omega $, since at an interior point a small vertical displacement would increase $|\xi _0(v)|$. Multiply $\xi _0$ by a complex number of modulus one to obtain $\eta $ with $\eta (v_0)=p(x_0,\xi _0)=a>0$. Then $\ell =\operatorname {Re}\eta $ satisfies $\ell \leq a$ on $A_{x_0}$. Choose a smooth defining function $f$ near the boundary, with $\Omega =\{ f<0\} $ and $\mathrm df\ne 0$ there.

Let $V=T^{1,0}_{v_0}E_{x_0}$ be the vertical tangent space and $T=\ker \partial f(v_0)$. Choose an $n$-dimensional complex subspace $W\subset T$ on which $\mathcal L_ f$ is negative definite. We first show that the vertical differential $\mathrm d_ vf(v_0)$ is nonzero. Otherwise $V\subset T$, and counting dimensions gives $W\cap V\ne \{ 0\} $. A vector in this intersection has negative vertical Levi form, and the two corresponding real directions show that the real vertical Hessian of $f$ has a negative direction. Its negative cone is open, hence contains a direction $w$ with $\ell (w)>0$. Since the vertical first derivative vanishes, Taylor expansion gives $f(v_0+tw)<0$ for small $t>0$, contradicting $\ell \leq a$ on $A_{x_0}$.

The boundary of $A_{x_0}$ is therefore smooth at $v_0$. Since the supporting functional $\ell $ attains its maximum on $A_{x_0}$ there,

\[ \mathrm d_ vf(v_0)=c\, \mathrm d\ell ,\qquad c>0; \]

the sign follows because an inward direction along which $\ell $ increases would contradict the maximality. On the affine hyperplane $\{ \ell =a\} $ of the fiber, the restriction of $f$ has a local minimum zero at $v_0$: a point there with $f<0$ would allow an additional displacement increasing $\ell $ within $\Omega $. The real vertical Hessian of $f$ is thus nonnegative on $\ker \mathrm d\ell $. Consequently $\mathcal L_ f$ is nonnegative on the complex space $V\cap T$, of dimension $r-1$. Hence $W\cap V=\{ 0\} $, and the projection $W\to T^{1,0}_{x_0}X$ is an isomorphism.

Step 3: plurisubharmonicity. In local coordinates $z$ centered at $x_0$, choose a holomorphic section $\sigma _0$ through $v_0$ with tangent space $W$. The function $f\circ \sigma _0$ has zero first derivative and negative definite mixed Hessian. Its quadratic expansion is

\[ f(\sigma _0(z))=\sum H_{\alpha \bar\beta }z_\alpha \bar z_\beta +2\operatorname {Re}P(z)+O(|z|^3), \]

where $P$ is a holomorphic quadratic polynomial. Choose a vertical vector $b$ with $\partial _ vf(b)=1$, and replace $\sigma _0$ by $\sigma =\sigma _0-bP$. This preserves the first jet and cancels the pure quadratic part. Thus, after shrinking the chart,

\[ f(\sigma (z))\leq -\epsilon |z|^2, \qquad \sigma (0)=v_0, \]

for some $\epsilon >0$. In particular $\sigma (z)\in A_ z$ throughout the chart.

Compactness of $\overline\Omega $ makes $p$ upper semicontinuous: for convergent covectors, the corresponding maximizing vectors have a subsequence whose limit lies in the limiting fiber of $\overline\Omega $. The supporting section gives the opposite inequality $p(z,\xi )\geq |\xi (\sigma (z))|$, with equality at $(x_0,\xi _0)$. Therefore $p$ is continuous off the zero section, and its upper bound gives continuity at zero. The holomorphic pairing $\xi (\sigma (z))$ is nonzero near the contact point, and its logarithmic absolute value is a pluriharmonic function lying below $\log p$ and equal to it at $(x_0,\xi _0)$. The mean-value property on small holomorphic disks therefore gives the submean inequality for $\log p$ at every point off the zero section. Thus $\log p$, and then $Q=\exp (2\log p)$ by Jensen's inequality, are plurisubharmonic there. At the zero section the continuous nonnegative function $Q$ vanishes, so its submean inequality on disks also holds there.

Step 4: uniform convexity in the fibers. Consider all maximizing triples $(x,\xi ,v)$ with $|\xi |_{k^*}=1$. They form a compact set, since $p$ is continuous, and by Step 2 the vertical differential $\mathrm d_ vf$ does not vanish at these support points. Thus there are uniform constants $m>0$, $\rho >0$ and $C\geq 1$ such that, for vertical vectors $w$,

\[ |\mathrm d_ vf|\geq m,\qquad f(v+w)\leq \mathrm d_ vf(w)+C|w|_ k^2\quad (|w|_ k\leq \rho ). \]

Choose $0<\delta <\min \{ a_0/2,\rho /2,m/(2C)\} $. At a support point, write $N$ for the outward real vertical unit normal and set $c=v-\delta N$. If $|w+\delta N|_ k\leq \delta $, then

\[ k_{\mathbb R}(w,N)\leq -\frac{|w|_ k^2}{2\delta },\qquad f(v+w)\leq -\left(\frac{m}{2\delta }-C\right)|w|_ k^2\leq 0. \]

All these points lie in the neighborhood where $f$ is defined, so the closed ball $\overline B_ k(c,\delta )$ is contained in $A_ x$.

The support function of this ball is $s_ c(\xi )=|\xi (c)|+\delta |\xi |_{k^*}\leq p(x,\xi )$. For a maximizing covector of unit norm, with its phase normalized as in Step 2, the real part $\operatorname {Re}\eta $ has real dual norm one and its metric dual vector is $N$. Hence $\operatorname {Re}\eta (N)=1$, and since $|\eta (N)|\leq 1$, also $\eta (N)=1$. We obtain $\eta (c)=a-\delta >0$, and therefore $s_ c(\xi )=a$. Homogeneity now gives

\[ Q(x,\xi )=\sup _ c s_ c(\xi )^2, \]

where $c$ ranges over the centers of the supporting balls in the fiber. Every such center satisfies $|c|_ k\leq b_0+\delta $, so by lemma 4.6.36 all the functions $s_ c^2-\gamma |\xi |_{k^*}^2$ are convex for the same $\gamma >0$. Their supremum is finite, and it proves the asserted strong convexity of $Q$.

Step 5: smoothing. Finally, if $E$ is ample, proposition 4.6.12 gives the smooth strictly plurisubharmonic homogeneous function on $E^*$ required in proposition 4.6.9, and applying that proposition to $Q$ gives the smooth norm. $\square$

For a complex manifold of dimension $N$ and an integer $1\leq q\leq N$, we say that the manifold is $q$-convex if it has a smooth real exhaustion function with at least $N-q+1$ positive Levi eigenvalues outside a compact set; here an exhaustion function is one whose sublevel sets are compact. A $q$-convex manifold is also $q'$-convex for $q\leq q'\leq N$. We call such integers $q$ the smooth convexity indices of the manifold, and the smallest of them, when one exists, its least smooth convexity index.

Lemma 4.6.38. Let $Y$ be a complex manifold of dimension $N$, and let $Z\subset Y$ be a closed complex submanifold of dimension $D\geq 1$. If the least smooth convexity index of $Z$ is $\kappa \leq D$, then every smooth convexity index of $Y$ is at least $\kappa $.

Proof. Suppose that $Y$ is $p$-convex for an integer $1\leq p<\kappa $. Then a smooth exhaustion function $\psi $ has at least $N-p+1$ positive Levi eigenvalues outside a compact set $K\subset Y$. Its restriction to the closed submanifold $Z$ is an exhaustion function, since its sublevel sets are closed subsets of the compact sublevel sets of $\psi $, and $K\cap Z$ is compact.

At a point of $Z\setminus K$, choose a complex subspace $P\subset T^{1,0}Y$ of dimension $N-p+1$ on which the Levi form of $\psi $ is positive definite. Then

\[ \dim _{\mathbb C}(P\cap T^{1,0}Z)\geq (N-p+1)+D-N=D-p+1. \]

The Levi form of $\psi |_ Z$ is the restriction of the Levi form of $\psi $, since the inclusion is holomorphic, so it has at least $D-p+1$ positive eigenvalues. Since $p<\kappa \leq D$, this shows that $Z$ is $p$-convex, which contradicts the definition of $\kappa $. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.39. Suppose that $E$ is ample and that $E^*$ admits no smooth complex norm with plurisubharmonic square off the zero section. Then the complement

\[ Z\setminus Y,\qquad Z=\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X\oplus E), \quad Y=\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X), \]

is not $r$-convex.

Proof. The chart of $Z$ where the $\mathcal O_ X$-coordinate is nonzero is identified with the total space of $E$, and $Y$ is its zero section. Suppose that $Z\setminus Y$ is $r$-convex. Since $\dim Z=n+r$, a smooth exhaustion function $\psi $ then has at least $n+1$ positive Levi eigenvalues outside a compact set $K$. Choose a relatively compact disk-bundle neighborhood $D\Subset E$ of $Y$. The set $Z\setminus D$ is compact and disjoint from $Y$. Choose a regular value $c$ of $\psi $ greater than its maximum on $Z\setminus D$ and on $K$.

Since $\{ \psi \leq c\} $ is compact in $Z\setminus Y$, the set $\Omega =\{ \psi >c\} \cup Y$ is an open neighborhood of $Y$. Its closure is a compact subset of $D$, and its boundary is the regular level set $\{ \psi =c\} $. The defining function $f=c-\psi $ has at least $n+1$ negative Levi eigenvalues there. Restriction to the complex tangent hyperplane removes at most one negative direction, leaving at least $n$. Since $E$ is ample, theorem 4.6.37 produces a smooth complex norm on $E^*$ with plurisubharmonic square, a contradiction. $\square$

4.6.9 Curvature and projective complement exhaustions

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Corollary 4.6.39 excludes $r$-convexity of the complement of the zero section in the projective completion, but it does not decide which larger indices occur. Upper bounds come from curvature. The reciprocal of the squared norm of a Hermitian metric is an exhaustion near the removed zero section, and every positive direction of the curvature, evaluated at a fiber vector, contributes a positive Levi eigenvalue. For a stable bundle of positive Gauduchon degree, the Hermitian–Einstein metric provides such a direction at every fiber vector.

In this subsection, $E\to X$ is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 1$, $n\geq 1$, and $h$ is a smooth Hermitian metric on $E$. In a holomorphic frame, write the matrix of $h$ as $M$, using column vectors and an inner product linear in its first argument, so that $h(v,v)=v^*Mv$. Put

\[ F_ h=\bar\partial (M^{-1}\partial M) =\sum _{\alpha ,\beta }F_{\alpha \bar\beta }\, \mathrm dz_\alpha \wedge \mathrm d\bar z_\beta , \qquad R_ h(u,\bar u)=\sum _{\alpha ,\beta }F_{\alpha \bar\beta }u_\alpha \bar u_\beta . \]

For $v\in E_ x$, the real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha _{h,v}=\mathrm i\, h(F_ hv,v)$ has the Hermitian coefficient form $u\mapsto h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)$. For a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$, the mean curvature of $h$ is the $h$-Hermitian endomorphism $K_{\omega ,h}=\Lambda _\omega (\mathrm iF_ h)$, where $\Lambda _\omega $ denotes contraction with $\omega $, so that $\Lambda _\omega (\beta )\, \omega ^ n=n\beta \wedge \omega ^{n-1}$ for $(1,1)$-forms $\beta $. The curvature $F_ h$ is unscaled: for a line bundle, the curvature $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log h$ of section 4.6 is $\mathrm iF_ h/(2\pi )$.

Use the line convention for projectivization and put

\[ U_ E=\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X\oplus E)\setminus \mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X). \]

The chart $[1:v]$ identifies the total space of $E$ with an open subset of $\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X\oplus E)$, in which $\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X)$ is the zero section; thus $U_ E$ contains $E^\times $. Its complex dimension is $n+r$. Convexity is understood in the sense of the smooth exhaustions of 4.6.8.

Lemma 4.6.41. Put $G(v)=h(v,v)$ and $\rho =G^{-1}$ on $E^\times $. In a holomorphic frame that is unitary and Chern-normal at $x$, meaning that $M(x)$ is the identity and the first derivatives of $M$ vanish at $x$, the complex Hessian of $\rho $ at $(x,v)$ is

\[ \mathcal L_\rho (u,w) =\frac{h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)}{G^2} -\frac{|w|_ h^2}{G^2} +\frac{2|h(w,v)|^2}{G^3}, \qquad u\in T_ x^{1,0}X,\quad w\in E_ x, \]

where $(u,w)$ are the horizontal and vertical components of a tangent vector. There are no mixed horizontal–vertical terms, and the vertical block has exactly one positive and $r-1$ negative eigenvalues.

Proof. Such a frame is obtained by a constant change of frame making it unitary at $x$, followed by a holomorphic change of frame, linear in the coordinates, that cancels $\partial M$. Then $F_{\alpha \bar\beta }=-\partial _\alpha \partial _{\bar\beta }M$ at $x$. The horizontal first derivatives of $G$ and its mixed horizontal–vertical second derivatives vanish. The horizontal complex Hessian of $G$ is $-h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)$, its vertical complex Hessian is $|w|_ h^2$, and its vertical first derivative in the direction $w$ is $h(w,v)$. Apply

\[ \partial \bar\partial (G^{-1}) =2G^{-3}\partial G\wedge \bar\partial G-G^{-2}\partial \bar\partial G. \]

The vertical block equals $G^{-1}>0$ at $w=v$ and equals $-|w|_ h^2/G^2$ when $w\perp _ h v$. These two subspaces are orthogonal for the vertical block, which proves the statement about its eigenvalues. $\square$

Theorem 4.6.42. Let $s$ be an integer with $0\leq s\leq n$. Suppose that for every $x\in X$ and every $0\ne v\in E_ x$, the coefficient form of $\alpha _{h,v}$ has at least $s$ positive eigenvalues. Then $U_ E$ is $(n+r-s)$-convex.

Proof. By lemma 4.6.41, the horizontal block of $\mathcal L_\rho $ has at least $s$ positive eigenvalues and the vertical block has one. Their direct sum thus has at least $s+1$ positive eigenvalues at every point of $E^\times $.

Choose $\epsilon >0$ and a smooth function $\chi :[0,+\infty )\to [0,1]$ equal to one on $[0,\epsilon ]$ and to zero on $[2\epsilon ,+\infty )$. Define

\[ \psi ([1:v])=\frac{\chi (G(v))}{G(v)}\quad (v\ne 0), \]

and extend $\psi $ by zero on the divisor at infinity $\mathbb P(E)$. This extension is smooth. Indeed, near a point $[0:v]$ with $v\ne 0$, use a local representative $[u:v]$ in a projective chart. On its intersection with the affine chart, $G(v/u)=h(v,v)/|u|^2$ tends to infinity as $u\to 0$, so $\chi (G)$ vanishes in a neighborhood of the point.

The function $\psi $ tends to infinity near the removed zero section. Since the ambient projective bundle is compact, every sublevel set of $\psi $ is compact in $U_ E$: it is closed there and cannot accumulate on the removed section. Put $D=\{ G<\epsilon \} \subset E$, including the zero section, and $K=\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X\oplus E)\setminus D$. Then $K$ is a compact subset of $U_ E$, and $\psi =\rho $ on $U_ E\setminus K$. Thus the exhaustion $\psi $ has at least $s+1$ positive Levi eigenvalues outside a compact set. In dimension $n+r$, this is $(n+r-s)$-convexity. $\square$

Taking $s=0$ gives $(n+r)$-convexity for every bundle. For $s=1$, the curvature hypothesis is precisely RC-positivity of the metric, as in definition 4.11.1. A positive definite mean curvature provides such a direction by taking a trace.

To apply this to stable bundles, we extend the degree of line bundles of section 4.6 to vector bundles through their determinants.

Definition 4.6.43. For a Gauduchon form $\omega $ on $X$, define

\[ \deg _\omega E=\deg _{\omega ^{n-1}}\det E =\int _ X\frac{\mathrm i\operatorname {tr}F_ h}{2\pi }\wedge \omega ^{n-1}, \qquad \mu _\omega (E)=\frac{\deg _\omega E}{r}. \]

For a nonzero torsion-free coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$, use the determinant line bundle $\det \mathcal F=(\bigwedge ^{\operatorname {rank}\mathcal F}\mathcal F)^{**}$ to define its degree and slope. The bundle $E$ is $\omega $-slope-stable if $\mu _\omega (\mathcal F)<\mu _\omega (E)$ for every coherent subsheaf $\mathcal F\subset E$ with $0<\operatorname {rank}\mathcal F<r$ and torsion-free quotient.

If $\det h'=\mathrm e^ f\det h$, the two curvature representatives differ by $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$, whose integral against $\omega ^{n-1}$ vanishes by lemma 2.1.1. Thus the degree depends only on the Bott–Chern class $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(E)$ of the closed form $\mathrm i\operatorname {tr}F_ h/(2\pi )$, and it is the pairing of this class with $\omega ^{n-1}$. The Hopf example in example 4.6.55 shows that the de Rham first Chern class of $E$ does not determine the degree.

Corollary 4.6.44. If $K_{\omega ,h}$ is positive definite for a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$, then $U_ E$ is $(n+r-1)$-convex. This holds, in particular, when $\omega $ is Gauduchon, $E$ is $\omega $-slope-stable, and $\deg _\omega E>0$.

Proof. For $v\ne 0$, the trace of $\alpha _{h,v}$ with respect to $\omega $ is $h(K_{\omega ,h}v,v)>0$, and a Hermitian form with positive trace has a positive eigenvalue. Apply theorem 4.6.42 with $s=1$.

Under the Gauduchon and stability hypotheses, the classical existence theorem for Hermitian–Einstein metrics on stable bundles gives a metric $h$ with $K_{\omega ,h}=\lambda \operatorname {Id}_ E$, where $\lambda $ is a constant. Taking the trace and integrating the contraction identity gives

\[ r\lambda \int _ X\omega ^ n =n\int _ X\operatorname {tr}(\mathrm iF_ h)\wedge \omega ^{n-1} =2\pi n\deg _\omega E>0. \]

Here $\operatorname {tr}F_ h=\bar\partial \partial \log \det M$, so $\mathrm i\operatorname {tr}F_ h/(2\pi )$ represents $c_1^{\mathrm{BC}}(E)$. Thus $\lambda >0$, and the first assertion applies. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.45. For every $e\geq 0$, there is a stable ample rank-two bundle $E$ on $X=\mathbb F_ e$ as in corollary 4.6.40 for which $U_ E$ is $3$-convex but not $2$-convex. Its least smooth convexity index is three.

Proof. Use the ruling section $S$ and fiber $f$ of lemma 4.6.19, and put $H=S+(3e+1)f$ and $P=4H-f$. Choose $E$ as in corollary 4.6.40, $\mu _ P$-stable and with $c_1(E)=4H$. The intersection numbers of lemma 4.6.19 give

\[ c_1(E)\cdot P=4H\cdot (4H-f)=80e+28>0. \]

By the same lemma, $P=4S+(12e+3)f$ is very ample. Take for $\omega $ the pulled-back Fubini–Study Kähler form representing $c_1(\mathcal O_ X(P))$. Degrees with respect to $\omega $ are intersection numbers with $P$, so $E$ is $\omega $-slope-stable with $\deg _\omega E>0$, and corollary 4.6.44 gives $3$-convexity, since $n=r=2$. By the choice of $E$, the complement is not $2$-convex. Finally, $1$-convexity would imply $2$-convexity, so the least index is three. $\square$

4.6.10 Negative bundle metrics and logarithmic weights

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For positive curvature, the reciprocal norm in lemma 4.6.41 is useful near a removed zero section. With negative curvature, the squared norm itself is plurisubharmonic. Adding its logarithm gives a weight whose Levi form controls both the base and the fiber directions.

In this subsection the base $X$ need not be compact. Suppose that it has a Kähler form $\omega $ and a smooth nonnegative plurisubharmonic exhaustion $\rho $. Here an exhaustion has compact sublevel sets; a constant function qualifies on a compact base. Let $\pi :E\to X$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 1$, with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$, and let $Z$ be its zero section. Use the unscaled curvature and coefficient forms $\alpha _{h,v}$ of 4.6.9, and assume

\[ \alpha _{h,v}\leq -|v|_ h^2\omega \qquad (x\in X,\ v\in E_ x). \]

This is a uniform negative curvature bound with respect to the chosen base form.

Proposition 4.6.46. Put $U(v)=|v|_ h^2$, $q=U\mathrm e^ U$ and $\Psi =\rho \circ \pi +U$. Then $\Psi $ is a smooth plurisubharmonic exhaustion of $E$ and

\[ \Omega =\pi ^*\omega +2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U \]

is a Kähler form on $E$. The function $\log q$, extended by $-\infty $ on $Z$, is plurisubharmonic and satisfies

\[ 2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log q\geq \Omega \quad \hbox{on }E\setminus Z. \]

Both $U$ and $q$ are strictly plurisubharmonic off $Z$. For the real radial vector field $\mathcal R$, whose flow is $v\mapsto \mathrm e^ s v$, one has $\mathcal Rq=2(1+U)q$. In particular $q$ has no critical points off $Z$.

Proof. At $x$, choose a Chern-normal unitary holomorphic frame as in lemma 4.6.41. Write a tangent vector at $(x,v)$ as $(a,b)$, with $a$ horizontal and $b$ vertical. The first base derivatives and mixed base–fiber derivatives of $U$ vanish there. The same normal-frame calculation gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal L_ U(a,b) & =-h(R_ h(a,\bar a)v,v)+|b|_ h^2 \geq U\omega (a,\bar a)+|b|_ h^2,\\ \mathcal L_{\log U}(a,b) & =-\frac{h(R_ h(a,\bar a)v,v)}U +\frac{|b|_ h^2U-|h(b,v)|^2}{U^2} \geq \omega (a,\bar a)\quad (v\ne 0). \end{aligned} \]

The last vertical term is nonnegative by Cauchy–Schwarz. Here $\mathcal L$ is the unscaled complex Hessian, corresponding to $2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}=\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial $. These formulas show that $U$ is plurisubharmonic, that $\Omega $ is positive definite even on $Z$, and that $2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\log U+U)\geq \Omega $ off $Z$. The form $\Omega $ is closed because $\omega $ is closed. The first formula gives strict positivity for $U$ off $Z$; exponentiating $\log q$ gives it for $q$. Locally, $U$ is comparable to the squared Euclidean fiber norm, so $\log q$ tends to $-\infty $ at $Z$ and is locally bounded above. By lemma 1.3.7, the upper-limit extension is plurisubharmonic and has value $-\infty $ on $Z$.

The function $\Psi $ is plurisubharmonic. Its sublevel set of height $M$ lies over the compact set $\{ \rho \leq M\} $ and in the disk bundle $\{ U\leq M\} $ over that set, which is compact. The sublevel is closed, so $\Psi $ is an exhaustion. Finally, $\mathcal RU=2U$ gives the radial identity and excludes critical points where $q>0$. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.47. For every $m>0$,

\[ \mathcal I(m\log q) =\mathcal I_ Z^{\max \{ \lfloor m\rfloor -r+1,0\} }. \]

In particular, if $k\geq 1$ is an integer and $m\geq r+k-1$, a holomorphic function that is locally square integrable against $q^{-m}$ times a smooth positive volume form vanishes to order at least $k$ along $Z$. If $m<r+k-1$, this conclusion fails for some local holomorphic functions.

Proof. In a local bundle frame, $\log q=\log |v|^2+O(1)$ near $Z$. Apply lemma 1.16.1 in codimension $r$. Its radial integrability condition for a homogeneous fiber term of degree $a$ is $a+r>m$. For $m<r+k-1$, a monomial of degree $k-1$ satisfies this condition and has insufficient vanishing order. Smooth positive volume forms do not change local integrability. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.48. The manifold $E\setminus Z$ admits a complete Kähler metric.

Proof. Choose a smooth convex increasing function $\eta $ on $\mathbb R$ equal to $-\log (-t)$ for $t\leq -1$. On $E\setminus Z$ put

\[ \widehat\Omega =\Omega +2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\Psi ^2) +2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta (\log q). \]

The added forms are nonnegative, so this is Kähler. Since $\Psi \geq 0$ is plurisubharmonic, $2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\Psi ^2)\geq 2\mathrm i\partial \Psi \wedge \bar\partial \Psi $. Thus $\Psi $ has bounded gradient for $\widehat\Omega $, and a curve of finite length stays in a compact sublevel of $\Psi $ in $E$. The only possible escape from that compact set within $E\setminus Z$ is approach to $Z$. Where $\log q\leq -1$, set $\tau =\log (-\log q)$. Convexity and the chain rule give

\[ 2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta (\log q) \geq \mathrm i\partial \tau \wedge \bar\partial \tau . \]

Hence $\tau $ also has bounded gradient there, while $\tau \to +\infty $ on approach to $Z$. A finite-length curve cannot escape in this way either. The criterion that every escaping curve has infinite length proves completeness. $\square$

For example, let $B\to X$ have a smooth Hermitian metric with positive unscaled curvature form $\omega $. The direct sum $E=(B^*)^{\oplus r}$ with its induced metric satisfies $\alpha _{h,v}=-|v|_ h^2\omega $. Thus the construction applies whenever this base also has the stated plurisubharmonic exhaustion, including a compact base.

If the base is connected, compact and has positive dimension, one may take $\rho =0$. Then $U$ is an exhaustion that is strictly plurisubharmonic off $Z$, but the total space $E$ is not Stein: every holomorphic function on $E$ restricts to a constant on the compact connected zero section and therefore fails to separate its distinct points. Strict positivity away from a compact analytic set does not remove this obstruction.

4.6.11 External tensor products and complement convexity

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

On a product of $k$ manifolds, the external tensor product of bundles with positive mean curvature has, at every nonzero fiber vector, a positive curvature direction tangent to each factor. Indeed, the curvature of the tensor product metric has no components mixing different factors, so this count applies to every fiber vector, including vectors that are not decomposable tensors. Theorem 4.6.42 then improves the convexity bound by $k$.

Let $k\geq 2$. For $1\leq i\leq k$, let $X_ i$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n_ i\geq 1$, let $E_ i\to X_ i$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r_ i\geq 1$, and choose a smooth Hermitian metric $h_ i$ on $E_ i$ and a Hermitian form $\omega _ i$ on $X_ i$. With $p_ i:X=\prod _ iX_ i\to X_ i$ the projections, put

\[ E=\bigotimes _{i=1}^ kp_ i^*E_ i,\qquad h=\bigotimes _{i=1}^ kp_ i^*h_ i, \qquad n=\sum _ i n_ i,\quad r=\prod _ i r_ i. \]

For an endomorphism $A_ i$ of $E_ i$, write $\widehat A_ i$ for its action on the $i$th tensor factor, with the identity on every other factor. We use the Chern curvature, the forms $\alpha _{h,v}$ and the mean curvatures of 4.6.9.

Lemma 4.6.49. The curvature of $h$ is

\[ F_ h=\sum _ i\widehat{p_ i^*F_{h_ i}}. \]

For every $v\in E_ x$, the coefficient form of $\alpha _{h,v}$ is block diagonal with respect to the decomposition $T_ x^{1,0}X=\bigoplus _ iT_{x_ i}^{1,0}X_ i$, and

\[ \operatorname {tr}_{\omega _ i}\bigl (\alpha _{h,v}|_{T_{x_ i}X_ i}\bigr ) =h\bigl (\widehat K_ i v,v\bigr ),\qquad K_ i=K_{\omega _ i,h_ i}. \]

Proof. In product holomorphic frames, the matrix of the metric is $M=\bigotimes _ iM_ i$, and its connection matrix is

\[ M^{-1}\partial M=\sum _ i\widehat{M_ i^{-1}\partial M_ i}. \]

Each summand depends only on the coordinates of $X_ i$. Applying $\bar\partial $ gives the curvature formula and shows that all components mixing different factors vanish. Pairing with $v$ gives the block diagonal form. Contracting the $i$th block with $\omega _ i$ commutes with the identity actions on the other tensor factors, which gives the trace identity. $\square$

Theorem 4.6.50. If every $K_ i$ is positive definite, then

\[ \mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X\oplus E)\setminus \mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X) \]

is $(n+r-k)$-convex.

Proof. At each point, choose an $h_ i$-orthonormal eigenbasis of $K_ i$. The resulting product basis is $h$-orthonormal, and $\widehat K_ i$ has the positive eigenvalues of $K_ i$, repeated according to the dimensions of the other tensor factors. Hence $h(\widehat K_ i v,v)>0$ for every nonzero $v$, whether or not $v$ is decomposable.

By lemma 4.6.49, each diagonal block of $\alpha _{h,v}$ has positive trace and therefore a positive eigenvalue, so the block diagonal form has at least $k$ positive eigenvalues. Since $k\leq n$, theorem 4.6.42 with $s=k$ gives the assertion. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.51. Suppose that each $\omega _ i$ is Gauduchon, that $E_ i$ is $\omega _ i$-slope-stable as in definition 4.6.43, and that $\deg _{\omega _ i}E_ i>0$. Then the complement $\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X\oplus E)\setminus \mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X)$ is $(n+r-k)$-convex. For $k$ stable ample Hirzebruch examples as in corollary 4.6.45, with their polarizations, this gives $(2^ k+k)$-convexity.

Proof. As in the proof of corollary 4.6.44, choose a Hermitian–Einstein metric on each $E_ i$. Its mean curvature is $\lambda _ i\operatorname {Id}_{E_ i}$, with $\lambda _ i>0$ by the positivity of the degree and the integrated trace identity. Apply theorem 4.6.50. For the Hirzebruch factors, $n_ i=r_ i=2$, so $n+r-k=2k+2^ k-k=2^ k+k$. $\square$

4.6.12 Homogeneous plurisubharmonic functions and Hopf complements

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Curvature gives upper bounds for the least convexity index of a projective complement. Lower bounds come from plurisubharmonic functions: a smooth positive plurisubharmonic function on the punctured bundle that is homogeneous of degree two excludes every improvement of the trivial bound, and closed submanifolds transfer such lower bounds by lemma 4.6.38. On Hopf manifolds these tools determine the least index for line bundles whose de Rham first Chern class vanishes. The answer depends on the sign of their Gauduchon degree, which therefore detects information invisible to de Rham cohomology.

Let $F\to B$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r\geq 1$ over a connected compact complex manifold $B$ of dimension $n\geq 1$. Write $F^\times $ for the complement of its zero section, and put $N=n+r$ and

\[ U_ F=\mathbb P(\mathcal O_ B\oplus F)\setminus \mathbb P(\mathcal O_ B). \]

The reciprocal-norm exhaustion in theorem 4.6.42 with $s=0$ shows that $U_ F$ is $N$-convex.

Proposition 4.6.52. Suppose that $F^\times $ admits a smooth positive plurisubharmonic function $G$ satisfying $G(cv)=|c|^2G(v)$ for every $c\in \mathbb C^*$ and $v\in F^\times $. Then the least smooth convexity index of $U_ F$ is $N$.

Proof. Since every smaller convexity index would imply it, it suffices to exclude $(N-1)$-convexity. Suppose that a smooth exhaustion function $\psi $ of $U_ F$ has at least two positive Levi eigenvalues outside a compact set $K\subset U_ F$.

Fix a reference Hermitian norm on $F$. On its compact unit sphere bundle, $G$ has a positive minimum and a finite maximum, so by homogeneity $G$ is comparable to the squared reference norm uniformly on $F^\times $. Thus the level sets

\[ \Sigma _ t=\{ G=t^2\} ,\qquad t>0, \]

are compact, and multiplication by $t$ maps $\Sigma _1$ onto $\Sigma _ t$. They are smooth hypersurfaces by the Euler identity: if $R_ v=v$ is the real radial vector at $v$, then $\mathrm dG(R_ v)=2G(v)>0$. Since $K$ stays away from the removed zero section, there is $\epsilon >0$ such that $\Sigma _ t$ is disjoint from $K$ for $0<t<\epsilon $.

Put

\[ M(t)=\max _{v\in \Sigma _1}\psi ([1:tv]),\qquad 0<t<\epsilon . \]

On any closed interval in $(0,\epsilon )$, the radial derivatives of the smooth function under the maximum are uniformly bounded over the compact level set, so $M$ is locally Lipschitz. Since $\psi $ is an exhaustion, $M(t)\to +\infty $ as $t\to 0$: otherwise maximizing points along a sequence $t\to 0$ would lie in one compact sublevel set while accumulating on the removed section. Choose $0<t_0<t_1<\epsilon $ with $M(t_0)>M(t_1)$. Since a Lipschitz function is absolutely continuous and is the integral of its derivative, there is a point $t\in (t_0,t_1)$ at which $M$ is differentiable with $M'(t)<0$.

Choose $v\in \Sigma _1$ attaining $M(t)$, and set $p=tv$. For both signs of $h$, the inequality

\[ M(t+h)\geq \psi ([1:(t+h)v]) \]

is an equality at $h=0$, so differentiability of $M$ gives $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\psi ([1:tv])=M'(t)$. Since $\psi $ restricted to $\Sigma _ t$ attains its maximum at $p$ and $\mathrm dG\ne 0$, there is a real number $\lambda $ with $\mathrm d\psi =\lambda \, \mathrm dG$ at $p$. Evaluating on the radial direction gives

\[ \lambda =\frac{M'(t)}{2t}<0. \]

The function $\psi -\lambda G$ has zero differential at $p$ and nonpositive real Hessian on $T_ p\Sigma _ t$. Indeed, its restriction to $\Sigma _ t$ is $\psi $ minus a constant, which has a maximum at $p$, and since its differential vanishes at $p$, its second derivative along a curve in $\Sigma _ t$ is the Hessian evaluated on the tangent vector. On the complex tangent hyperplane $H=T_ p\Sigma _ t\cap \mathrm iT_ p\Sigma _ t$, this implies

\[ \mathcal L_\psi |_ H\leq \lambda \mathcal L_ G|_ H\leq 0. \]

The first inequality follows by adding the real Hessian inequalities for a tangent vector and its complex rotation; the second uses plurisubharmonicity of $G$ and $\lambda <0$. A two-dimensional complex subspace on which $\mathcal L_\psi $ is positive definite would meet the hyperplane $H$ nontrivially, so $\psi $ has at most one positive Levi eigenvalue at $p\notin K$. This contradiction excludes $(N-1)$-convexity. Together with theorem 4.6.42 for $s=0$, it proves the proposition. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.53. If $F$ admits a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ with

\[ h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)\leq 0 \quad \text{for every }x\in B,\ u\in T_ x^{1,0}B,\ v\in F_ x, \]

then the least smooth convexity index of $U_ F$ is $n+r$.

Proof. Put $G(v)=h(v,v)$. At $x\in B$, take a holomorphic frame that is unitary and Chern-normal at $x$, and write a tangent vector to $F$ at $v\in F_ x$ as $(u,w)$, with $u\in T_ x^{1,0}B$ and $w\in F_ x$. The calculation in the proof of lemma 4.6.41 gives

\[ \mathcal L_ G(u,w)=-h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)+|w|_ h^2\geq 0, \]

with zero mixed block. Thus $G$ is smooth, plurisubharmonic and positive off the zero section, and it satisfies $G(cv)=|c|^2G(v)$. Apply proposition 4.6.52. $\square$

Corollary 4.6.54. If $F$ admits a Hermitian metric with flat Chern connection, then the least smooth convexity index of $U_ F$ is $n+r$.

Proof. In parallel holomorphic unitary frames, the squared norm is $G(v)=\sum _ j|v_ j|^2$, which is plurisubharmonic on the total space. Since the transition matrices are constant and unitary, $G$ is globally defined; it is smooth, positive on $F^\times $ and homogeneous of degree two under complex scalars. Apply proposition 4.6.52. $\square$

Fix $n\geq 2$, a real number $0<q<1$ and a complex number $a\ne 0$. On the scalar Hopf manifold

\[ X_ q=(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto qz\rangle , \]

let $E_ a$ be the holomorphic line bundle obtained as the quotient of $(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )\times \mathbb C$ by $(z,v)\sim (qz,av)$. Put

\[ b=-\frac{\log |a|}{\log q}, \qquad G(z,v)=|z|^{2b}|v|^2. \]

Since $q^{2b}|a|^2=1$, the function $G$ descends to the squared norm of a Hermitian metric on $E_ a$.

Example 4.6.55. The least smooth convexity index of $U_{E_ a}$ is two when $0<|a|<1$, and $n+1$ when $|a|\geq 1$. For every Gauduchon form $\gamma $ on $X_ q$, the degree of $E_ a$ with respect to $\gamma ^{n-1}$ is positive, zero or negative according as $|a|<1$, $|a|=1$ or $|a|>1$. The de Rham first Chern class of $E_ a$ vanishes in all cases.

Proof. Let $\alpha $ be the form on $X_ q$ induced by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^2$; it descends because the contraction changes $\log |z|^2$ by a constant. For $u\in T_ z^{1,0}(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )$, its coefficient form is, up to the positive factor $1/(2\pi )$,

\[ \frac{|u|^2}{|z|^2}-\frac{|\langle u,z\rangle |^2}{|z|^4}, \]

where $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle $ is the Euclidean Hermitian inner product. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, it is positive semidefinite with kernel $\mathbb Cz$, hence of rank $n-1$. The curvature $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^{2b}$ of the metric $G$ is $-b\alpha $. The integral of $\alpha \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ is strictly positive, so the degree has the sign of $-b$, which gives the three stated cases. The map $z\mapsto (z/|z|,\log |z|)$ identifies the covering space with $S^{2n-1}\times \mathbb R$, and the contraction translates the second coordinate by $\log q$. Hence $X_ q$ is diffeomorphic to $S^{2n-1}\times S^1$, and $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_ q,\mathbb R)=0$ since $n\geq 2$.

If $|a|<1$, then $b<0$, so every nonzero fiber vector gives $n-1$ positive curvature directions. Applying theorem 4.6.42 with $r=1$ and $s=n-1$, we obtain $2$-convexity. For the lower bound, consider the map $X_ q\to \mathbb P^{n-1}$, $[z]\mapsto [z]$, whose fibers are compact embedded elliptic curves $C\simeq \mathbb C^*/q^{\mathbb Z}$. On a lifted fiber $z=\zeta z_0$, the logarithm of the metric coefficient is $b\log |\zeta |^2+b\log |z_0|^2$, which is pluriharmonic in $\zeta $. Thus $E_ a|_ C$ has flat Chern connection. The complement $U_{E_ a|_ C}$ is a closed complex submanifold of $U_{E_ a}$. A $1$-convex exhaustion function of $U_{E_ a}$ would restrict to an exhaustion function of this submanifold that is strictly plurisubharmonic outside a compact set. This contradicts corollary 4.6.54, since the restricted bundle has base dimension one and rank one. The phase of $a$ affects the gluing but not these metric and curvature calculations.

If $|a|\geq 1$, then $b\geq 0$. On the covering space of $E_ a^\times $, the function

\[ \log G=b\log |z|^2+\log |v|^2 \]

is plurisubharmonic: the first summand has semipositive Levi form and the second is pluriharmonic for $v\ne 0$. Consequently

\[ \mathcal L_ G=G\bigl (\mathcal L_{\log G}+\partial \log G\otimes \overline{\partial \log G}\bigr )\geq 0. \]

The descended function $G$ is smooth, positive and homogeneous of degree two under complex scalars. Apply proposition 4.6.52 with rank one and total dimension $n+1$. $\square$

On the same scalar Hopf manifold $X_ q$, consider a direct sum of rank $r\geq 1$ with complex parameters $a_1,\ldots ,a_ r\ne 0$:

\[ F=\bigoplus _{j=1}^ r E_{a_ j},\qquad b_ j=-\frac{\log |a_ j|}{\log q},\qquad G(z,v)=\sum _{j=1}^ r |z|^{2b_ j}|v_ j|^2. \]

Here $E_{a_ j}$ is defined by the rule $(z,v_ j)\sim (qz,a_ jv_ j)$. Since $q^{2b_ j}|a_ j|^2=1$, the function $G$ descends to the squared norm of the direct sum metric on $F$.

Proposition 4.6.56. The least smooth convexity index of $U_ F$ is $r+1$ if every $|a_ j|<1$, and $n+r$ if every $|a_ j|\geq 1$.

Proof. Let $\alpha $ be the form induced by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^2$. By the calculation in example 4.6.55, it is semipositive of rank $n-1$, with radial kernel. The direct sum metric has diagonal curvature, and the normalized curvature evaluated at $v$ is

\[ \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }h(F_ hv,v) =\left(\sum _{j=1}^ r(-b_ j)|z|^{2b_ j}|v_ j|^2\right)\alpha . \]

If every $|a_ j|<1$, then every $b_ j<0$. The coefficient is then strictly positive for $v\ne 0$, so theorem 4.6.42 with $s=n-1$ shows that $U_ F$ is $(r+1)$-convex. On any embedded elliptic fiber $C$ of $X_ q\to \mathbb P^{n-1}$, each line metric has flat Chern connection by the logarithmic calculation in example 4.6.55, and so does their direct sum. Hence corollary 4.6.54 gives least index $r+1$ for $U_{F|_ C}$. This is a closed complex submanifold of $U_ F$, being its inverse image over $C$, and lemma 4.6.38 gives the lower bound.

If every $|a_ j|\geq 1$, then every $b_ j\geq 0$. For $v_ j\ne 0$, the function $|z|^{2b_ j}|v_ j|^2$ is plurisubharmonic by the calculation with the logarithm and the exponential in example 4.6.55. It is also smooth at $v_ j=0$, because $z\ne 0$, and by continuity its complex Hessian is nonnegative there as well. The sum $G$ is therefore plurisubharmonic on the covering space of the total space and descends to $F$. It is positive on $F^\times $ and homogeneous of degree two under complex scalars, so proposition 4.6.52 applies. $\square$

For a mixed sum, let $t=\# \{ j:|a_ j|\geq 1\} $ and assume $0<t<r$. If $\kappa (F)$ denotes the least smooth convexity index of $U_ F$, then

\[ \max \{ r+1,n+t\} \leq \kappa (F)\leq n+r. \]

Indeed, the same flat restriction to elliptic fibers gives the lower bound $r+1$. The direct sum of the $t$ summands with $|a_ j|\geq 1$ is a holomorphic subbundle $F_0\subset F$, and $U_{F_0}$ is closed in $U_ F$ by the inclusion of projective subbundles. Its least index is $n+t$ by proposition 4.6.56, and lemma 4.6.38 applies again. The upper bound is theorem 4.6.42 with $s=0$. In the mixed case these bounds leave the least index undetermined.

4.7 Pluriharmonic currents

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

A current is pluriharmonic if its $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ vanishes. The normalized Gauduchon currents of section 4.4 are positive pluriharmonic currents of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$. This section proves four basic properties of positive pluriharmonic currents of arbitrary bidegree. Positivity and the second-order equation control their concentration on a smooth hypersurface. On compact Kähler manifolds, a closed test form bounds the mass of their pullbacks, and the heat equation smooths those with continuous coefficients. Finally, a positive pluriharmonic $(1,1)$-current with vanishing vertical trace descends along a fiber bundle whose fibers are entire lines or compact curves; this is used for elliptic fibrations in chapter 28.

Lemma 4.7.1. Let $Z$ be a complex manifold of dimension $m\geq 2$, and let $j:D\hookrightarrow Z$ be a smooth closed hypersurface. If a positive $(q,q)$-current $R$, $1\leq q\leq m$, is supported on $D$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R$ has order zero, then there is a unique positive $(q-1,q-1)$-current $S$ on $D$ such that $R=j_*S$. If $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R=0$, then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S=0$. In particular, if $q=1$, $D$ is compact and connected, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R=0$, then $R=c[D]$ for some $c\geq 0$.

Proof. Use local coordinates $(z,w_1,\ldots ,w_{m-1})$ with $D=\{ z=0\} $. Both $R$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R$ have measure coefficients supported on $D$. Suppose first that $q<m$. For every compactly supported smooth form $\eta $ of bidegree $(m-q-1,m-q-1)$,

\[ 0=\langle \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R,|z|^2\eta \rangle =\langle R,\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(|z|^2\eta )\rangle =\langle R,\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2\wedge \eta \rangle , \]

where the first equality holds because $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R$ is a measure supported on $D$, on which $|z|^2$ vanishes, and the last one because the terms containing $|z|^2$ or its first derivatives vanish on $D$. Thus $R\wedge \mathrm{i}\, \mathrm{d}z\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z=0$, which removes the coefficients of $R$ containing neither normal differential.

Fix a constant positive Hermitian form $\tau $ in the $w$ variables. The current $M_\tau =R\wedge \tau ^{m-q-1}$ is positive of bidegree $(m-1,m-1)$, and $M_\tau \wedge \mathrm{i}\, \mathrm{d}z\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z=0$. Its Hermitian matrix of measure densities therefore has zero diagonal entry in the normal direction, and positivity removes the corresponding row and column, so

\[ M_\tau \wedge \mathrm{d}z=0, \qquad M_\tau \wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z=0. \]

Put $s=m-q-1$. The powers $\tau ^ s$, as $\tau $ varies over the positive Hermitian forms, span the real horizontal $(s,s)$-forms. Indeed, a linear functional vanishing on these powers vanishes on a polynomial over an open cone; polarization makes it vanish on products of arbitrary real $(1,1)$-forms, and these products span the forms of that bidegree. Their products with a horizontal one-form span the complementary test forms for the mixed coefficients of $R$. The identities above therefore remove every coefficient containing exactly one normal differential. For $q=m$, every top-degree coefficient already contains both normal differentials, and this reduction is unnecessary.

The remaining coefficients are measures on $\{ z=0\} $ multiplying both normal differentials. They therefore define a unique current $S$ on $D$ with $R=j_*S$. Testing against extensions of strongly positive forms on $D$ proves positivity of $S$, and uniqueness makes these local currents agree on overlaps. Pushforward commutes with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ and is injective for a closed embedding, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R=0$ implies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S=0$.

When $q=1$, the current $S$ is a nonnegative distribution on $D$. The equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S=0$ and Weyl's lemma make it a smooth nonnegative pluriharmonic function. If $D$ is compact and connected, the maximum principle makes it a constant $c\geq 0$, proving the last assertion. $\square$

For smooth pluriharmonic forms on compact Kähler manifolds, the mass of the pullback under a fixed holomorphic map is controlled by pairing with a fixed closed form. The test object is the pushforward of a power of the Kähler form on the source, which is closed; the form being pulled back need only be $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed.

Lemma 4.7.2. Let $F:Y\to X$ be a holomorphic map between compact Kähler manifolds of dimensions $N\geq n$, with Kähler forms $\Omega $ and $\omega $. For $1\leq p\leq n$, there is a constant $C$ such that every smooth positive $(p,p)$-form $S$ on $X$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S=0$ satisfies

\[ \int _ Y F^*S\wedge \Omega ^{N-p} \leq C\int _ X S\wedge \omega ^{n-p}. \]

Proof. The positive closed current $\Gamma =F_*(\Omega ^{N-p})$ has bidegree $(n-p,n-p)$ on $X$. The classical $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-lemma for currents on compact Kähler manifolds gives a smooth real closed form $\gamma $ of this bidegree and a current $U$ with $\Gamma =\gamma +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$. For $p=n$, a closed current of degree zero is already a smooth locally constant function, and one may take $U=0$. Since $S$ is smooth, pairing it with $U$ is legitimate, and

\[ \int _ Y F^*S\wedge \Omega ^{N-p} =\langle \Gamma ,S\rangle =\int _ X\gamma \wedge S+\langle U,\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S\rangle =\int _ X\gamma \wedge S. \]

The pairing with the fixed smooth form $\gamma $ is bounded in absolute value by a constant times the trace mass $\int _ XS\wedge \omega ^{n-p}$ of the positive form $S$. This proves the estimate. $\square$

For continuous coefficients on a compact Kähler manifold, the Hodge heat flow smooths a positive form while preserving the pluriharmonic equation, and a small multiple of a power of the Kähler form restores positivity.

Lemma 4.7.3. Let $(X,\omega )$ be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$, and let $1\leq p\leq n$. Every continuous positive real $(p,p)$-form $R$ satisfying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R=0$ in the sense of currents is a uniform limit of smooth positive real $(p,p)$-forms $R_ j$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ j=0$. If $R$ is closed, the approximants can also be chosen closed.

Proof. Let $\Delta $ be the real Hodge Laplacian of the Kähler metric. Its heat operators $H_ t=\mathrm e^{-t\Delta }$ are smoothing for $t>0$. The Kähler identities give $\Delta =2\Delta _\partial =2\Delta _{\bar\partial }$, so $H_ t$ preserves bidegree, reality and the equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R=0$.

We recall why $H_ tR\to R$ uniformly for a continuous form $R$. The Weitzenböck formula writes $\Delta =\nabla ^*\nabla +A$, with a bounded curvature term $A$ of order zero. For smooth initial data $\beta $, the heat equation and the maximum principle give

\[ \| H_ t\beta \| _{C^0}\leq \mathrm e^{Ct}\| \beta \| _{C^0} \qquad (0<t\leq 1), \]

where $C$ depends only on the fixed metric and the degree. Indeed, the scalar equation for $|H_ t\beta |^2$ has a nonpositive gradient term and a curvature term bounded above by $2C|H_ t\beta |^2$. The classical smooth heat solution converges uniformly to its smooth initial data. Approximating a continuous form by smooth forms and using this bound proves uniform convergence for continuous initial data. The resulting heat solution is smooth for $t>0$, and since the commutation with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ extends to currents, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ tR=0$.

Set $\epsilon _ t=\| H_ tR-R\| _{C^0}$, which tends to zero. A fixed constant $c>0$ satisfies

\[ E+c\| E\| _{C^0}\omega ^ p\geq 0 \]

for every continuous real $(p,p)$-form $E$; to see this, evaluate on decomposable unit $p$-vectors, on which $\omega ^ p$ has a uniformly positive value. Consequently

\[ R_ t=H_ tR+c\epsilon _ t\omega ^ p \]

is smooth and positive. Since $\omega $ is Kähler, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega ^ p=0$, and hence $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ t=0$. Moreover, $R_ t\to R$ uniformly. The Hodge heat flow also commutes with $\mathrm{d}$, and $\omega ^ p$ is closed, so $\mathrm{d}R=0$ implies $\mathrm{d}R_ t=0$. Choosing any sequence $t_ j\downarrow 0$ proves the assertion. $\square$

We next consider a positive $(1,1)$-current with vanishing vertical trace on the total space of a fiber bundle. Positivity removes its vertical and mixed coefficients. For a closed current the remaining coefficients are constant along the fibers. For a pluriharmonic current they are only harmonic along the fibers, but positivity gives the same conclusion when the fiber is the whole complex line or a compact connected curve.

Lemma 4.7.4. Let $Y$ be a complex manifold of dimension $\ell \geq 1$, let $\eta $ be a Hermitian form on $Y$, and let $\pi :Z\to Y$ be a holomorphic fiber bundle whose fiber is either $\mathbb C$ or a compact connected Riemann surface. If $S$ is a positive real $(1,1)$-current on $Z$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S=0$ and $S\wedge \pi ^*\eta ^\ell =0$, then there is a unique positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(1,1)$-current $R$ on $Y$ such that $S=\pi ^*R$. If $S$ is closed, then $R$ is closed.

Proof. Fix a local trivialization $\pi ^{-1}(U)=U\times F$ of the bundle, with base coordinates $w=(w_1,\ldots ,w_\ell )$ and a local fiber coordinate $z$. The coefficient measures of $S$ have a positive Hermitian matrix of densities relative to their trace measure. The identity $S\wedge \pi ^*\eta ^\ell =0$ makes the vertical diagonal entry zero, and the inequality $|s_{\alpha \bar z}|^2\leq s_{\alpha \bar\alpha }s_{z\bar z}$ then removes all mixed entries. Thus

\[ S=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\sum _{\alpha ,\beta =1}^\ell s_{\alpha \bar\beta }(w,z)\, \mathrm{d}w_\alpha \wedge \mathrm{d}\bar w_\beta , \]

with distribution coefficients. In the equation $\partial \bar\partial S=0$, the coefficient containing both vertical differentials and the horizontal pair $(\alpha ,\beta )$ gives $\partial _ z\partial _{\bar z}s_{\alpha \bar\beta }=0$.

Fix $v\in \mathbb C^\ell $ and a nonnegative smooth function $\xi $ with compact support in $U$. Pairing the positive coefficient distribution $\sum _{\alpha ,\beta }s_{\alpha \bar\beta }v_\alpha \bar v_\beta $, in the base variables, with $\xi $ times the Euclidean volume form defines a nonnegative harmonic distribution on the whole fiber $F$. Its definition does not depend on the fiber coordinate, since the remaining differentials are horizontal. Weyl's lemma makes it a nonnegative harmonic function. Such a function is constant on $\mathbb C$ by the Liouville theorem for harmonic functions, and on a compact connected curve by the maximum principle.

Polarization in $v$ and linearity in the base test function show that every coefficient $s_{\alpha \bar\beta }$ is the pullback of a distribution $r_{\alpha \bar\beta }$ on $U$. To identify this distribution, pair against any nonnegative smooth fiber density of total integral one; this also proves positivity of the resulting current $R_ U$ on $U$ and injectivity of the pullback. Consequently $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S=\pi ^*(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ U)=0$ implies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ U=0$. On overlaps, injectivity makes the currents $R_ U$ agree, so they glue to a current $R$, and injectivity also proves uniqueness. If $\mathrm{d}S=0$, the same argument gives $\mathrm{d}R=0$. $\square$

The assumption that the fibers are entire lines or compact curves is essential. On the bidisk $\Delta _ z\times \Delta _ w$, the current

\[ S=(2+\operatorname {Re}z)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\, \mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar w \]

is positive and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed, and its vertical trace vanishes. Its horizontal coefficient depends on $z$, so it does not descend under the projection to $\Delta _ w$.

The derivative of the logarithmic Monge–Ampère operator $u\mapsto \log (\omega _ u^ n/\mathrm dV)$ annihilates the constants, so its linearized equation can be solved only under a compatibility condition. The Gauduchon multiplier of theorem 4.1.2 identifies this condition. Fix $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $ and write $\eta =\omega _ u$. For a smooth real direction $v$, logarithmic differentiation gives the linearized operator

4.15
\begin{equation} L_ u v=n\, \frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \eta ^{n-1}}{\eta ^ n}. \end{equation}

Choose the Gauduchon multiplier $\rho _ u$ of $\eta $ given by theorem 4.1.2, normalized by

\[ \int _ X\rho _ u\eta ^ n=1, \qquad \text{and put}\qquad \mathrm{d}\mu _ u:=\rho _ u\eta ^ n. \]

The adjoint measure $\mu _ u$ is a smooth positive probability measure.

Proposition 4.8.1. For every smooth real $v$,

4.16
\begin{equation} \int _ X L_ uv\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ u=0. \end{equation}

Moreover, for smooth real $h$, the equation $L_ uv=h$ has a smooth solution if and only if $\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ u=0$. When it exists, the solution is unique modulo constants.

Proof. The integral in (4.16) is

\[ n\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \rho _ u\eta ^{n-1} =n\int _ Xv\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ u\eta ^{n-1})=0. \]

This proves that the compatibility condition is necessary.

By the Fredholm argument in the proof of theorem 4.1.2, the range of $L_ u:C^{k+2,\alpha }(X)\to C^{k,\alpha }(X)$ has codimension one for every integer $k\geq 0$ and $0<\alpha <1$. The nonzero functional $h\mapsto \int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ u$ annihilates that range, so its kernel is the range. Elliptic regularity gives a smooth solution, and since the kernel of $L_ u$ consists of the constants, the solution is unique modulo constants. $\square$

By its defining equation and the uniqueness in theorem 4.1.2, the multiplier $\rho _ u$ is constant precisely when $\eta $ is Gauduchon. Thus

\[ \mu _ u=\frac{\eta ^ n}{\int _ X\eta ^ n} \quad \text{if }\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\eta ^{n-1})=0. \]

On a surface, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega _ u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $, so every positive potential perturbation of a Gauduchon form is still Gauduchon. In higher dimension this persistence requires more than the Gauduchon condition at $u=0$. Write $M=\int _ X\omega ^ n$.

Proposition 4.8.2. Every form $\omega _ u$ with $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $ is Gauduchon if and only if

\[ \int _ X\omega _ u^ n=M \quad \text{for every }u\in \mathcal H_\omega . \]

In that case the normalized multiplier is $\rho _ u=M^{-1}$ and $\mu _ u=M^{-1}\omega _ u^ n$ for every $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $.

Proof. If all the forms $\omega _ u$ are Gauduchon, the mass derivative (2.2) vanishes at every point of $\mathcal H_\omega $ and in every direction. Since $\mathcal H_\omega $ is convex, integration along the segment from $0$ to $u$ gives the mass identity. Conversely, proposition 2.2.3 shows that constant mass forces $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _ u^{n-1})=0$ for every $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $. In that case the normalized multiplier is the constant $M^{-1}$, by theorem 4.1.2. $\square$

By theorem 2.2.2, this persistence is equivalent to $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ j)=0$ for all integers $1\leq j\leq n-1$. The following example distinguishes it from the single Gauduchon identity in dimension three.

Example 4.8.3. Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$ and $X=E^3$. For $1\leq j\leq 3$, write $z_ j=x_ j+\mathrm i y_ j$ for the coordinates on the covering space, and let $\eta _ j$ be the form on $X$ induced by $(\mathrm i/2)\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$. The periodic functions

\[ b=\exp (\cos (2\pi x_1)),\qquad c=b^{-1} \]

define a Hermitian form

\[ \omega =\eta _1+b\eta _2+c\eta _3. \]

Since $bc=1$,

\[ \omega ^2=2b\eta _1\wedge \eta _2 +2c\eta _1\wedge \eta _3+2\eta _2\wedge \eta _3. \]

The functions $b$ and $c$ depend only on $x_1$, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}c$ are multiples of $\eta _1$, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)=0$. Thus $\omega $ is Gauduchon.

Put $v=\cos (2\pi x_2)$ and $h=-\pi \cos (2\pi x_2)$, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v=h\eta _2$. For a real $s$ with $0<|s|<1/(\pi \mathrm e)$, the form

\[ \omega _{sv}=\eta _1+(b+sh)\eta _2+c\eta _3 \]

is positive, since $b\geq \mathrm e^{-1}$ and $|h|\leq \pi $. However,

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _{sv}^2) =2s\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v =2sh\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}c\wedge \eta _2\wedge \eta _3\ne 0. \]

Indeed, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}c=(c''/(4\pi ))\eta _1$, where the prime denotes differentiation in $x_1$, and $c''(0)=4\pi ^2\mathrm e^{-1}$, while $h=-\pi $ at $x_2=0$. Thus a Gauduchon form can have arbitrarily small positive potential perturbations that are not Gauduchon.

When persistence holds, the one-form $v\mapsto \int _ Xv\, \mathrm d\mu _ u$ on $\mathcal H_\omega $ is the Monge–Ampère one-form of chapter 3 divided by $M$. By theorem 3.1.3, it therefore has a primitive exactly when $\omega $ is closed.

4.8.1 Convex entropies along a Monge–Ampère flow

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The logarithmic Monge–Ampère operator generates a parabolic flow whose linearization is the operator $L_ u$ of (4.15). Along this flow, the ratio of the Monge–Ampère measure to a fixed target measure satisfies a heat-type equation. Integrating a convex function of this ratio by parts produces, besides the usual nonpositive gradient term, a term involving $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _{u_ t}^{n-1})$. If the forms along the flow are Gauduchon, this additional term vanishes and all convex entropies decrease. Requiring decrease for every smooth positive target at the initial time characterizes the Gauduchon condition.

Recall that $M=\int _ X\omega ^ n$, and fix a smooth strictly positive probability volume form $\nu $ on $X$. Consider a smooth solution on an interval $[0,T)$ of the logarithmic Monge–Ampère flow

4.17
\begin{equation} \dot u_ t=\log \rho _ t,\qquad \rho _ t=\frac{\omega _{u_ t}^ n}{M\nu },\qquad u_0=0,\qquad \omega _{u_ t}>0. \end{equation}

We keep the initial normalizing constant $M$ fixed; the evolving mass $\int _ X\omega _{u_ t}^ n$ need not equal $M$. Define the linearized operator $\Delta _ t$ by

\[ \Delta _ t v\, \omega _{u_ t}^ n =n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \omega _{u_ t}^{n-1}; \]

it is the operator $L_{u_ t}$ of (4.15). Logarithmic differentiation gives $\partial _ t\log \rho _ t=\Delta _ t\dot u_ t=\Delta _ t\log \rho _ t$. Integration by parts against $\omega _{u_ t}^{n-1}$ then produces the same derivative of the background power that appears in the mass variation of proposition 2.2.3.

Proposition 4.8.4. Let $\Phi \in C^2((0,+\infty ),\mathbb R)$, and choose a function $G$ with $G'(r)=\Phi '(r)/r$. Along (4.17),

4.18
\begin{equation} \begin{split} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int _ X\Phi (\rho _ t)\nu ={}& \frac nM\int _ XG(\rho _ t)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _{u_ t}^{n-1})\\ & -\frac nM\int _ X\frac{\Phi ''(\rho _ t)}{\rho _ t} \mathrm{d}\rho _ t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\rho _ t\wedge \omega _{u_ t}^{n-1}. \end{split} \end{equation}

In particular, for $p>1$,

4.21
\begin{equation} \begin{split} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int _ X\rho _ t^ p\nu ={}& \frac{np}{(p-1)M}\int _ X\rho _ t^{p-1}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _{u_ t}^{n-1})\\ & -\frac{np(p-1)}M\int _ X\rho _ t^{p-3} \mathrm{d}\rho _ t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\rho _ t\wedge \omega _{u_ t}^{n-1}. \end{split} \end{equation}

Proof. Using $\partial _ t\rho _ t=\rho _ t\Delta _ t\log \rho _ t$ and $\rho _ t\nu =M^{-1}\omega _{u_ t}^ n$, we find that the derivative of the entropy is

\[ \frac nM\int _ X\Phi '(\rho _ t)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \rho _ t \wedge \omega _{u_ t}^{n-1}. \]

The chain rule and the definition of $G$ give

\[ \Phi '(r)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log r =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G(r)-\frac{\Phi ''(r)}r\mathrm{d}r\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}r, \]

and lemma 2.1.1 applies to the first term. Adding a constant to $G$ does not change the result, since $\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _{u_ t}^{n-1})=0$. Taking $\Phi (r)=r^ p$ and $G(r)=p r^{p-1}/(p-1)$ proves the second formula. $\square$

Corollary 4.8.5. Suppose that every form $\omega _{u_ t}$ in (4.17) is Gauduchon. Then $\int _ X\rho _ t\nu =1$, and $t\mapsto \int _ X\Phi (\rho _ t)\nu $ is nonincreasing for every convex function $\Phi \in C^2((0,+\infty ),\mathbb R)$. In particular,

\[ \| \rho _ t\| _{L^ p(\nu )}\leq \| \rho _ s\| _{L^ p(\nu )} \quad (0\leq s\leq t<T,\ p>1). \]

If $\Phi ''>0$, the derivative of the entropy vanishes at a time $t$ precisely when $\rho _ t=1$, in which case $\dot u_ t=0$.

The hypothesis holds on a Gauduchon surface, and in any dimension it holds if $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ j)=0$ for $1\leq j\leq n-1$.

Proof. The derivative of the mass is

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int _ X\rho _ t\nu =\frac nM\int _ X\dot u_ t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _{u_ t}^{n-1})=0, \]

and its initial value is one. In (4.18) the first term vanishes, while the second is nonpositive, because $\mathrm{d}\rho _ t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\rho _ t$ is a semipositive $(1,1)$-form and $\Phi ''\geq 0$. If $\Phi ''>0$, the vanishing of this term forces $\mathrm{d}\rho _ t=0$, and connectedness together with the mass normalization gives $\rho _ t=1$. The converse and the assertion about $\dot u_ t$ follow from the equation.

On a surface, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega _{u_ t}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$. Under the power identities, expand $\omega _{u_ t}^{n-1}$ by the binomial theorem; since the factors $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t$ are annihilated by $\mathrm d$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$, each term has vanishing complex Hessian. These are also the persistence conditions of proposition 4.8.2, theorem 2.2.2. $\square$

For $\Phi (r)=r\log r$, the same formula gives the dissipation of the relative entropy. In higher dimension, the Gauduchon condition on the initial form alone need not persist along potential perturbations, as example 4.8.3 shows. The next criterion determines the role of this condition at the initial time, without assuming that the flow exists.

Proposition 4.8.6. Fix $p>1$. For each smooth strictly positive probability volume form $\nu $, put $\rho _\nu =\omega ^ n/(M\nu )$ and define

\[ \mathcal D_ p(\omega ,\nu ) :=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0} \int _ X\left(\frac{(\omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \rho _\nu )^ n}{M\nu }\right)^ p\nu . \]

Then $\mathcal D_ p(\omega ,\nu )\leq 0$ for every such $\nu $ if and only if $\omega $ is Gauduchon. If $\omega $ is not Gauduchon, there is such a $\nu $ with $\mathcal D_ p(\omega ,\nu )>0$.

Proof. The form in the definition is positive for sufficiently small $|t|$. The path $u_ t=t\log \rho _\nu $ starts at $u_0=0$ with the velocity $\log \rho _\nu $ prescribed by (4.17) at time zero. The proof of proposition 4.8.4 at a given time uses only the potential and its velocity at that time, so (4.21) holds for the derivative at $t=0$. If $\omega $ is Gauduchon, its first term vanishes and the derivative is nonpositive.

Conversely, suppose that $\lambda :=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-1})\ne 0$. Write $\lambda =f\omega ^ n/M$, where $f$ is smooth and real. Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ Xf\omega ^ n=0$, whereas

\[ \int _ Xf\lambda =\frac1M\int _ Xf^2\omega ^ n>0. \]

For small $\varepsilon >0$, set

\[ c_\varepsilon =\frac1M\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon f}\omega ^ n, \qquad r_\varepsilon =c_\varepsilon \mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon f}, \qquad \nu _\varepsilon =\frac{\omega ^ n}{M r_\varepsilon }. \]

Then $\nu _\varepsilon $ is a smooth strictly positive probability volume form, and $\rho _{\nu _\varepsilon }=r_\varepsilon $. Since $c_\varepsilon =1+O(\varepsilon ^2)$, $\int _ X\lambda =0$ and $\mathrm{d}r_\varepsilon =\varepsilon r_\varepsilon \mathrm{d}f$, the two terms in (4.21) give

\[ \mathcal D_ p(\omega ,\nu _\varepsilon ) =\frac{np}M\varepsilon \int _ Xf\lambda +O(\varepsilon ^2)>0 \]

for sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$. This proves necessity and the last assertion. $\square$

The criterion involves only the initial velocity: every smooth solution of (4.17) with target $\nu $ has the same initial entropy derivative, independently of its existence for later times or its convergence. Quantification over all targets is essential. For the target $\nu =\omega ^ n/M$, the flow $u_ t=0$ is stationary for every Hermitian $\omega $, and all entropy derivatives vanish.

4.8.2 Total variation between two evolving probabilities

The entropies of 4.8.1 compare one evolving probability with the fixed target. Two solutions of the flow with different initial potentials can nevertheless move apart. We show this by computing the initial derivatives explicitly. By a logarithmic Monge–Ampère flow with target $\nu $ we mean a smooth solution of the equation in (4.17), with an arbitrary smooth initial potential.

For this example, let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$ and $X=E^2$, with covering coordinates $z_ j=x_ j+\mathrm i y_ j$. Write

\[ \eta _ j=\frac{\mathrm i}2\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j, \qquad \kappa =\eta _1+\eta _2, \qquad \mu =\eta _1\wedge \eta _2=\frac{\kappa ^2}2. \]

Thus $\mu $ is a probability volume form and $\int _ X\kappa ^2=2$. Put

\[ c_ j=\cos (2\pi x_ j),\qquad S=c_1+c_2,\qquad P=c_1c_2. \]

Lemma 4.8.7. The averages of $|S|$ and $P|S|$ are

\[ \int _ X|S|\mu =\frac8{\pi ^2}, \qquad \int _ XP|S|\mu =\frac8{3\pi ^2}. \]

Proof. The integrands do not depend on $y_1,y_2$. The degree-two map of real tori $(a,b)\mapsto (a+b,a-b)$ preserves normalized Haar averages. With the angles $A=2\pi a$ and $B=2\pi b$, it gives

\[ S=2\cos A\cos B,\qquad P=\frac{\cos (2A)+\cos (2B)}2. \]

The one-variable averages of $|\cos A|$ and $|\cos A|^3$ are $2/\pi $ and $4/(3\pi )$, respectively, so the average of $\cos (2A)|\cos A|$ is $2/(3\pi )$. Multiplying the averages over the two independent angles gives both formulas. $\square$

For a fixed real number $K>3$, define the smooth target probability $\nu _ K$ by

\[ Z_ K=\int _ X\mathrm e^{KP}\mu , \qquad f_ K=Z_ K^{-1}\mathrm e^{KP}, \qquad \nu _ K=f_ K\mu . \]

For $0<\varepsilon <1/\pi $, the initial potentials $\varepsilon S$ and zero give positive forms. Set

\[ A_\varepsilon =1-\pi \varepsilon c_1, \qquad B_\varepsilon =1-\pi \varepsilon c_2, \qquad r_\varepsilon =A_\varepsilon B_\varepsilon , \]

so that $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varepsilon S)=A_\varepsilon \eta _1+B_\varepsilon \eta _2$ and its normalized Monge–Ampère probability is $r_\varepsilon \mu $.

Consider the short smooth paths

4.24
\begin{equation} U_\varepsilon (t)=\varepsilon S+t\log \frac{r_\varepsilon }{f_ K}, \qquad V(t)=-t\log f_ K. \end{equation}

\[ \mu _\varepsilon (t)=\frac{(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_\varepsilon (t))^2}2, \qquad \mu _0(t)=\frac{(\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V(t))^2}2, \]

\[ D_\varepsilon (t) =\frac12\int _ X|\mu _\varepsilon (t)-\mu _0(t)|. \]

\[ D'_\varepsilon (0) =4\varepsilon \left(\frac K3-1\right)+o(\varepsilon ) \quad \text{as }\varepsilon \downarrow 0. \]

Proof. For a real function $h$ depending only on $x_1,x_2$, the coefficient matrix of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ with respect to the forms $(\mathrm i/2)\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$ is $(4\pi )^{-1}(h_{x_ jx_ k})$. 4.9 Fredholm theory for a continuous Hermitian form

A continuous positive Hermitian form still defines a uniformly elliptic trace operator. Its range is most conveniently described in Sobolev spaces: continuity of the coefficients suffices for the local $W^{2,p}$ estimate, while the maximum principle identifies the kernel.

Lemma 4.9.1. Let $\sigma $ be a continuous positive real $(1,1)$-form on a connected compact complex $n$-fold, and let $\mathrm dV$ be a smooth positive volume. For $p>2n$, the real operator

\[ L_\sigma v=\operatorname {tr}_\sigma \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v, \qquad L_\sigma :W^{2,p}(X)\longrightarrow L^ p(\mathrm dV), \]

is Fredholm of index zero. Its kernel consists of the constants, and its range has a one-dimensional annihilator in $L^{p'}(\mathrm dV)$, where $p'=p/(p-1)$.

Proof. Fix a smooth positive Hermitian form $\eta $ and put $L_ s=(1-s)L_\eta +sL_\sigma $, $0\leq s\leq 1$. On a finite holomorphic cover these operators have uniformly positive real principal matrices and a common modulus of continuity. They have no lower-order terms in these coordinates. Write $d=2n$ for the real dimension.

Local estimates. For a constant positive real matrix $a_0$, the symbols $\xi _ i\xi _ j/(1+a_0^{kl}\xi _ k\xi _ l)$ satisfy the order-zero multiplier estimates. Consequently

\[ \| D^2z\| _ p\leq C\bigl (\| a_0^{ij}D_{ij}z\| _ p+\| z\| _ p\bigr ) \qquad (z\in C_ c^\infty ). \]

Freeze the principal matrix of $L_ s$ at a point and shrink the ball until the coefficient oscillation times this constant is less than $1/2$. The oscillation term can then be absorbed on the left. Differentiating a cutoff adds first derivatives; the interpolation inequality $\| Dz\| _ p\leq \varepsilon \| D^2z\| _ p+C_\varepsilon \| z\| _ p$ absorbs them. This inequality follows by comparing $Dz$ with its mollification at scale $\varepsilon $ and estimating the derivative of the mollified function by $C\varepsilon ^{-1}\| z\| _ p$. A partition of unity and smooth density give, uniformly in $s$,

4.27
\begin{equation} \| v\| _{W^{2,p}}\leq C\bigl (\| L_ sv\| _ p+\| v\| _ p\bigr ). \end{equation}

Maximum principles. We give the local estimate needed to pass comparison to $W^{2,p}$. Let $L=a^{ij}D_{ij}$ on a bounded smooth coordinate domain $D$, with $a\geq \vartheta I$, and let $z$ be smooth with nonnegative boundary values and minimum $-M<0$. Set $R=\operatorname {diam}D$. Every vector $\xi $ with $|\xi |<M/(2R)$ is the gradient at an interior lower supporting point of $z$: minimize $z(x)-\xi \cdot (x-x_0)$, where $z(x_0)=-M$. Its boundary values exceed $-M/2$, whereas its value at $x_0$ is $-M$. At a supporting point $D^2z\geq 0$, and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives

\[ \det D^2z\leq \left(\frac{\operatorname {tr}(aD^2z)}{d\vartheta }\right)^ d. \]

The area formula for the gradient, restricted to these supporting points, therefore bounds the volume of the slope ball by $(d\vartheta )^{-d}\int _ D((Lz)^+)^ d$. Thus

4.28
\begin{equation} -\min _ Dz\leq C_ d\vartheta ^{-1}R\| (Lz)^+\| _{L^ d(D)}. \end{equation}

This calculation uses no derivative of $a$. Approximate a $W^{2,p}$ function smoothly on the closure of a smaller smooth domain. The approximation converges in $C^1$, and its $L$ errors converge in $L^ d$ because $p>d$. Correct the vanishing boundary error in (4.28). Passage to the limit shows that $Lv\leq 0$ and $v|_{\partial D}\geq 0$ imply $v\geq 0$.

The same comparison proves the strong maximum principle. If $v\in W^{2,p}$, $Lv\geq 0$, and $v$ has an interior maximum $M$, put $w=M-v\geq 0$. If $w$ is positive somewhere and zero somewhere, a small ball contained in $\{ w>0\} $ and tangent to its zero set can be chosen inside a coordinate chart. On the outer half-annulus of this ball,

\[ \zeta (x)=\exp (-k|x-c|^2)-\exp (-kr^2) \]

is positive and has $L\zeta >0$ for sufficiently large $k$, by uniform ellipticity. A small positive multiple of $\zeta $ is bounded above by $w$ on both boundary spheres, hence throughout the annulus by comparison. At the tangent point this forces a positive inward derivative of $w$, contradicting $Dw=0$. Connectedness proves that $v$ is constant. Applied to $L_ s$ on $X$, it gives $\ker L_ s=\mathbb R$. If $(L_ s-1)v=0$ and $v$ has a positive maximum, apply the strong maximum principle on its positive component, where $L_ sv=v>0$. Constancy contradicts that equation. Applying the argument to $-v$ rules out a negative minimum. Hence $\ker (L_ s-1)=0$.

Index and range. Compactness of $W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow L^ p$ and (4.27) now give

4.29
\begin{equation} \| v\| _{W^{2,p}}\leq C\| (L_ s-1)v\| _ p \end{equation}

uniformly in $s$. Otherwise normalize a contradicting sequence in $W^{2,p}$, pass to $s_ j\to s$ and a strongly $L^ p$ convergent subsequence, and apply (4.27) to differences. Operator norm continuity in $s$ gives strong $W^{2,p}$ convergence to a nonzero member of $\ker (L_ s-1)$, which is impossible. The smooth operator $L_\eta -1$ has index zero and trivial kernel, hence is an isomorphism, by smooth elliptic theory. The method of continuity and (4.29) show that every $L_ s-1$ is an isomorphism. Finally, $L_\sigma $ differs from $L_\sigma -1$ by the compact inclusion $W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow L^ p$, so it is Fredholm of index zero. Its one-dimensional kernel makes its range have codimension one. $L^ p$ duality gives the annihilator assertion. $\square$

4.10 Limits of adjoint measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

When every positive potential perturbation of $\omega $ is Gauduchon, the adjoint probability is the normalized Monge–Ampère measure. Mass invariance and the continuity of bounded products then extend this description to bounded potentials.

Proposition 4.10.1. Assume that $\omega _ v$ is Gauduchon for every $v\in \mathcal H_\omega $. For every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$, the measure

\[ \mu _ u:=M^{-1}(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n \]

is a probability measure, which agrees with the preceding definition when $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $. If $u_ j,u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$ are uniformly bounded and $u_ j\to u$ in capacity, then $\mu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mu _ u$.

Proof. By proposition 4.8.2, theorem 2.2.2, the positive bounded product $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ has mass $M$, and for $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $ its normalization is the smooth adjoint measure by proposition 4.8.2. The continuity of bounded products under convergence in capacity, in the sense of section 1.8, gives weak convergence of the products, and division by $M$ preserves it. $\square$

The proposition applies in particular to Gauduchon surfaces. For a general Hermitian background, passing from smooth $u_ j\in \mathcal H_\omega $ to a limit $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ requires control of the multipliers $\rho _{u_ j}$ as well as of the potentials. The supporting probabilities of chapter 15 recover $\mu _ u$ in the smooth case. If a bounded solution has a continuous positive complex Hessian, theorem 15.2.6 gives a stationary supporting probability and approximation independence under uniform convergence of the logarithmic densities. We do not know whether, in general, the limit is independent of the approximating sequence, or whether a weak linearization satisfies an adjoint identity.

4.11 Conformal changes and directional curvature positivity

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The compatibility condition of proposition 4.8.1 decides which right-hand sides a scalar trace equation can have, and it can be used to improve the curvature of a Hermitian metric on a vector bundle. A conformal change $h\mapsto \mathrm e^{-tf}h$ adds the same form $t\, \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f$ to the curvature in every fiber direction. Solving a Poisson equation for a Gauduchon form, we can make the trace of this form positive outside any prescribed ball. If the original curvature already has a positive direction for every fiber vector over that ball, a small conformal change produces such a direction everywhere.

Throughout this section, $E\to X$ is a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank, and $n\geq 2$. For a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $E$, we use the Chern curvature $F_ h$, the Hermitian endomorphisms $R_ h(u,\bar u)$, the directional forms $\alpha _{h,v}$ and the mean curvature $K_{\omega ,h}$ of 4.6.9; recall that $h$ is linear in its first argument.

Definition 4.11.1. The metric $h$ is RC-positive at $x$ if, for every $0\ne v\in E_ x$, there is a tangent vector $u\in T_ x^{1,0}X$ such that

\[ h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)>0. \]

It is uniformly RC-positive at $x$ if one tangent vector $u$ works for every nonzero $v\in E_ x$. A metric has either property on a set if it has that property at every point of the set.

Lemma 4.11.2. Suppose that $K_{\omega ,h}(x)\geq 0$. Then $h$ is RC-positive at $x$ if and only if $\alpha _{h,v}\ne 0$ for every $0\ne v\in E_ x$.

Proof. The trace of $\alpha _{h,v}$ with respect to $\omega $ is $h(K_{\omega ,h}v,v)\geq 0$. A nonzero Hermitian form with nonnegative trace has a positive eigenvalue, since if all its eigenvalues were nonpositive, their nonnegative sum would force all of them to vanish. This gives the required tangent direction. Conversely, a tangent vector with positive evaluation shows that $\alpha _{h,v}\ne 0$. $\square$

Theorem 4.11.3. Suppose that $K_{\omega ,h}\geq 0$ on $X$ and that $h$ is RC-positive on a nonempty open set $U$. Then there is a smooth real function $f$ such that $h_ t=\mathrm e^{-tf}h$ is RC-positive on all of $X$ for every sufficiently small $t>0$.

Proof. By theorem 4.1.2, choose a conformal Gauduchon form $\gamma =\mathrm e^ a\omega $. Then $K_{\gamma ,h}=\mathrm e^{-a}K_{\omega ,h}\geq 0$. Choose a closed coordinate ball $Q\subset U$ and a smooth function $\chi \geq 0$ supported in its interior, with $\int _ X\chi \gamma ^ n>0$, and set

\[ \rho =1-\frac{\int _ X\gamma ^ n}{\int _ X\chi \gamma ^ n}\chi . \]

Thus $\rho =1$ on $X\setminus Q$ and $\int _ X\rho \gamma ^ n=0$. Since $\gamma $ is Gauduchon, proposition 4.8.1, applied with $\gamma $ in place of $\omega $ and $u=0$, gives a smooth real solution $f$ of

4.30
\begin{equation} P_\gamma f:=\operatorname {tr}_\gamma (\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f)=\rho . \end{equation}

Indeed, with $L_\gamma f=n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}/\gamma ^ n$ as in (4.15), we have $P_\gamma =2\pi L_\gamma $, and the adjoint probability of $L_\gamma $ is $\gamma ^ n/\int _ X\gamma ^ n$.

The curvature transforms by

4.31
\begin{equation} F_{h_ t}=F_ h+t\partial \bar\partial f\, \operatorname {Id}_ E, \qquad \alpha _{h_ t,v}=\mathrm e^{-tf} \bigl (\alpha _{h,v}+t|v|_ h^2\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f\bigr ). \end{equation}

On $X\setminus Q$, the $\gamma $-trace of the expression in parentheses is at least $t|v|_ h^2\rho =t|v|_ h^2>0$ for $v\ne 0$, so it has a positive eigenvalue there.

Over $Q$, the unit sphere bundle $\{ |v|_ h=1\} $ is compact. The largest $\gamma $-eigenvalue of $\alpha _{h,v}$ is continuous and strictly positive on it, so it has a positive minimum $\delta $. Let $C$ be the maximum on $X$ of the operator norm of $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f$ relative to $\gamma $. For every unit vector over $Q$, the largest eigenvalue of the expression in parentheses in (4.31) is at least $\delta -tC$, which is positive for small $t>0$. Homogeneity in $v$ and the positive factor $\mathrm e^{-tf}$ prove the assertion on $Q$ as well. $\square$

In this conclusion, the positive direction may depend on the fiber vector $v$. The distinction from uniform RC-positivity already occurs for a local metric with zero mean curvature.

Example 4.11.4. On a sufficiently small ball in $\mathbb C^2$, equip the trivial bundle of rank two with the metric

\[ H(z)=\operatorname {Id}-zz^*+\tfrac 12|z|^2\operatorname {Id}. \]

At the origin this metric has zero mean curvature for the Euclidean Hermitian form and is RC-positive, but it is not uniformly RC-positive.

Proof. The two eigenvalues of $H(z)$ are $1-\tfrac 12|z|^2$ and $1+\tfrac 12|z|^2$, so the metric is positive on a small ball. Its first derivatives vanish at the origin, where

\[ F_{i\bar j}=e_ ie_ j^*-\tfrac 12\delta _{ij}\operatorname {Id},\qquad h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)=|v^*u|^2-\tfrac 12|u|^2|v|^2. \]

The sum $F_{1\bar1}+F_{2\bar2}$ is zero. For each nonzero $v$, taking $u=v$ gives a positive evaluation. For each nonzero $u$, taking $v\perp u$ gives a negative evaluation, so no single $u$ works for every $v$. $\square$

Proposition 4.11.5. If $\det E$ admits a flat Hermitian metric, then no smooth Hermitian metric on $E$ is uniformly RC-positive on $X$ or has positive definite mean curvature relative to a Hermitian form. This applies in particular when $\det E$ is holomorphically trivial.

Proof. If $h$ were uniformly RC-positive, then at each point some $u$ would make the $h$-Hermitian endomorphism $R_ h(u,\bar u)$ positive definite, and its trace would be strictly positive. Since the curvature of the induced metric on $\det E$ is $\operatorname {tr}F_ h$, the determinant metric would be RC-positive as a metric on a line bundle. If instead $K_{\omega ,h}>0$, the $\omega $-trace of the determinant curvature would be $\operatorname {tr}K_{\omega ,h}>0$, which again gives a positive tangent evaluation.

Fix a flat Hermitian metric $h_0$ on $\det E$ and write $\det h=\mathrm e^ b h_0$ for a smooth real function $b$. The determinant curvature is then $-\partial \bar\partial b$. At a global minimum of $b$, this curvature has no positive tangent evaluation, contradicting the RC-positivity of the determinant metric. Finally, a holomorphic trivialization of $\det E$ gives a flat metric, by assigning constant norm to the trivializing frame. $\square$

Consequently, if $\det E$ admits a flat Hermitian metric and the hypotheses of theorem 4.11.3 hold, the conformally changed metric is RC-positive, whereas $E$ admits neither a uniformly RC-positive metric nor a metric with positive definite mean curvature. The next section shows that the tangent bundle of a nonflat Ricci-flat Kähler surface has these properties, and the local example above shows the pointwise distinction.

4.12 Ricci-flat Kähler surfaces

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The tangent bundle of a Ricci-flat Kähler surface gives an example for the conformal construction of section 4.11. Ricci-flatness makes the mean curvature of the tangent metric zero and its determinant metric flat, but it does not make every directional curvature zero. In real dimension four, the remaining curvature acts on the three-dimensional space of anti-self-dual two-forms, and its invertibility gives exactly the positive directions needed for the conformal construction.

In this section, $X$ is a compact complex surface, so $n=2$, and $\omega $ is a Ricci-flat Kähler form on $X$. Write $h$ for the Hermitian metric on $T^{1,0}X$ defined by $\omega $, and $g$ for the associated Riemannian metric, so that $\omega (S,T)=g(JS,T)$.

Use $g$ to identify bivectors and two-forms. For the complex orientation, the Hodge star gives

\[ \Lambda ^2=\Lambda ^+\oplus \Lambda ^-,\qquad \Lambda ^\pm =\{ \zeta :*\zeta =\pm \zeta \} . \]

Each summand has real rank three. The first is spanned by $\omega $ and the real parts of $(2,0)$-forms, and the second consists of the real primitive $(1,1)$-forms; both descriptions follow by writing the star in a unitary real frame.

Let $R^\nabla $ be the curvature of the Levi–Civita connection, with $R^\nabla (S,T)=\nabla _ S\nabla _ T-\nabla _ T\nabla _ S-\nabla _{[S,T]}$, and define the self-adjoint curvature operator $\mathscr R$ on $\Lambda ^2$ by

\[ \langle \mathscr R(S\wedge T),U\wedge V\rangle =g(R^\nabla (S,T)V,U). \]

Since the curvature of a Kähler metric has type $(1,1)$, the operator $\mathscr R$ annihilates the real parts of $(2,0)$-forms. Its value on $\omega $ is the Ricci form, which vanishes. Thus

\[ \mathscr R=0\oplus A,\qquad A:\Lambda ^-\longrightarrow \Lambda ^-. \]

The endomorphism $A$ is symmetric and trace free, since the trace of $\mathscr R$ is half the scalar curvature; in this Ricci-flat setting it is the anti-self-dual Weyl operator. Norms of curvature tensors below are Hilbert–Schmidt norms of these endomorphisms.

Lemma 4.12.1. At a point $x$, the metric $h$ is RC-positive if and only if $A_ x$ is invertible.

Proof. For a real tangent vector $V$, set $v=\tfrac 12(V-\mathrm iJV)$. The Chern and Levi–Civita connections agree on a Kähler manifold. Their curvature pairings give

\[ \alpha _{h,v}=\tfrac 12\bigl (\mathscr R(V\wedge JV)\bigr )^\flat . \]

Indeed, $h(v,w)=\tfrac 12(g(V,W)-\mathrm i g(JV,W))$ for $w=\tfrac 12(W-\mathrm iJW)$, and $R^\nabla $ commutes with $J$. Since $R^\nabla $ is skew-adjoint, the real part of $h(F_ hv,v)$ vanishes, which gives the formula.

The anti-self-dual projection of $V\wedge JV$ is

\[ \Pi (V)=V\wedge JV-\tfrac 12|V|^2\omega . \]

It is nonzero for $V\ne 0$, and every nonzero element of $\Lambda ^-$ is of this form. To see this, diagonalize a nonzero real primitive $(1,1)$-form in a unitary real frame. It has the form $c(e_1\wedge Je_1-e_2\wedge Je_2)$, with $c>0$ after interchanging the two complex lines. Taking $V=\sqrt{2c}\, e_1$ gives exactly this form as $\Pi (V)$. Also $|\Pi (V)|^2=|V|^4/2$.

Consequently, since $\mathscr R$ vanishes on $\Lambda ^+$, we have $\alpha _{h,v}\ne 0$ for every nonzero $v$ if and only if $\ker A_ x=0$. Since $K_{\omega ,h}=0$, lemma 4.11.2 gives the assertion. $\square$

Lemma 4.12.2. If $g$ is not flat, the open set $\{ x:\det A_ x\ne 0\} $ is nonempty.

Proof. Suppose that $\det A=0$ everywhere. On the open set where $A\ne 0$, its three eigenvalues are $\lambda _0,-\lambda _0,0$ with $\lambda _0>0$; they are distinct, so $A$ has smooth local eigenframes there.

Step 1: a Bochner identity. On an open set where $A$ has distinct eigenvalues, we compute $\Delta |A|^2$, keeping track of the normalizations.

Choose an oriented local eigenframe $\xi _1,\xi _2,\xi _3$ of $\Lambda ^-$ with $|\xi _ i|^2=2$, and denote the corresponding eigenvalues by $\lambda ,\mu ,\nu $. As skew-adjoint endomorphisms, these forms satisfy the multiplication rules of the quaternions. Write

\[ \nabla \xi _1=c\otimes \xi _2-b\otimes \xi _3,\quad \nabla \xi _2=-c\otimes \xi _1+a\otimes \xi _3,\quad \nabla \xi _3=b\otimes \xi _1-a\otimes \xi _2. \]

Here $a,b,c$ are real one-forms. The Ricci identity and the contracted Bianchi identity, using $\operatorname {Ric}(g)=0$, give

\[ \mathrm da+b\wedge c=\lambda \xi _1,\qquad \mathrm d\lambda =(\lambda -\mu )\xi _3c+(\lambda -\nu )\xi _2b, \]

and their simultaneous cyclic permutations. In the second formula the two-forms act as skew-adjoint endomorphisms on one-forms. Put $D=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d=-\operatorname {div}_ g\nabla $ on functions. Applying $\mathrm d^*$ to the second formula yields

\[ D\lambda =2\lambda ^2+4\mu \nu +2(\nu -\lambda )|b|^2+2(\mu -\lambda )|c|^2. \]

For example, the calculation uses

\begin{align*} \mathrm d^*(\xi _3c)& =-\langle b,\xi _1c\rangle +\langle a,\xi _2c\rangle +\langle a,\xi _3b\rangle -2\nu ,\\ \mathrm d^*(\xi _2b)& =-\langle b,\xi _1c\rangle -\langle a,\xi _2c\rangle -\langle a,\xi _3b\rangle -2\mu , \end{align*}

which follow from the connection and curvature formulas above; substitution and the cyclic formulas for $\mathrm d\mu ,\mathrm d\nu $ cancel the mixed pairings.

Now $\lambda +\mu +\nu =0$ and $|A|^2=\lambda ^2+\mu ^2+\nu ^2$. Multiplying the three scalar equations by their eigenvalues and adding gives

\[ \lambda D\lambda +\mu D\mu +\nu D\nu =18\lambda \mu \nu -2\bigl ((\mu -\nu )^2|a|^2 +(\nu -\lambda )^2|b|^2+(\lambda -\mu )^2|c|^2\bigr ). \]

The connection expression also gives

\[ |\nabla A|^2=|\mathrm d\lambda |^2+|\mathrm d\mu |^2+|\mathrm d\nu |^2 +2\bigl ((\mu -\nu )^2|a|^2+(\nu -\lambda )^2|b|^2 +(\lambda -\mu )^2|c|^2\bigr ). \]

The product rule therefore gives, with $\Delta =\operatorname {div}_ g\nabla =-D$,

4.32
\begin{equation} \Delta |A|^2=2|\nabla A|^2-36\det A=2|\nabla A|^2. \end{equation}

This identity also holds on the interior of $\{ A=0\} $. Both sides are smooth functions, and the two open sets have dense union in $X$, so the identity holds everywhere. Integration over $X$ gives $\nabla A=0$.

Step 2: a parallel operator $A$ forces flatness. If $g$ is not flat, then $A\ne 0$ somewhere, and since $A$ is parallel and $\det A=0$, it is nonzero everywhere and has three distinct constant eigenvalues. Its eigenlines are parallel; local unit sections of them are parallel as well. The induced connection on $\Lambda ^-$ therefore has zero curvature. Under $\mathfrak {so}(4)=\Lambda ^+\oplus \Lambda ^-$, the two summands commute, while the action of $\Lambda ^-$ on itself is faithful. These facts follow directly from the bases $e_1\wedge e_2\pm e_3\wedge e_4$, $e_1\wedge e_3\mp e_2\wedge e_4$, $e_1\wedge e_4\pm e_2\wedge e_3$. Thus zero induced curvature forces $\mathscr R(S\wedge T)$ to lie in $\Lambda ^+$ for every $S,T$. It already lies in $\Lambda ^-$, so $\mathscr R=0$, a contradiction. $\square$

Theorem 4.12.3. If $X$ has a nonflat Ricci-flat Kähler metric, then $T^{1,0}X$ admits an RC-positive Hermitian metric whose associated $(1,1)$-form is nonclosed. It admits neither a uniformly RC-positive Hermitian metric nor a metric with positive definite mean curvature relative to any Hermitian form.

Proof. By lemma 4.12.2, lemma 4.12.1, the original tangent metric $h$ is RC-positive on a nonempty open set. Its mean curvature is zero, so theorem 4.11.3 gives a metric $h_ t=\mathrm e^{-tf}h$ that is RC-positive everywhere.

In that construction we may take the Gauduchon form to be $\omega $ itself, and the closed coordinate ball to be a proper subset of $X$. Since $P_\omega f=1$ off that ball, $f$ is not constant. The associated form is $\omega _ t=\mathrm e^{-tf}\omega $, with

\[ \mathrm d\omega _ t=-t\mathrm e^{-tf}\mathrm df\wedge \omega . \]

On a real four-dimensional symplectic vector space, $\zeta \mapsto \zeta \wedge \omega $ is injective on one-forms, as a unitary real frame shows. Thus $\mathrm d\omega _ t\ne 0$ somewhere.

Finally, the induced determinant metric of $h$ is flat because its Chern curvature is the Ricci form of $\omega $, which vanishes. The two obstructions follow from proposition 4.11.5. $\square$

The construction does not change the complex surface: it starts from a Ricci-flat Kähler form and produces a nonclosed Hermitian form whose tangent metric is RC-positive. Nonflat Ricci-flat Kähler metrics exist, for example, on K3 surfaces, by Yau's solution of the Calabi conjecture.

Combined with theorem 4.6.42, the RC-positive tangent metric of theorem 4.12.3 bounds the convexity of the complement of the zero section in the projective completion of the tangent bundle. This bundle admits no metric with positive definite mean curvature, so corollary 4.6.44 does not apply to it.

Corollary 4.12.4. For a connected compact complex surface $X$ admitting a nonflat Ricci-flat Kähler metric, the complement

\[ \mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X\oplus T^{1,0}X)\setminus \mathbb P(\mathcal O_ X) \]

is $3$-convex. The tangent bundle admits no Hermitian metric with positive definite mean curvature relative to any Hermitian form.

Proof. By theorem 4.12.3, the tangent bundle has an RC-positive Hermitian metric. Its directional curvature form has a positive eigenvalue for every nonzero fiber vector. Apply theorem 4.6.42 with $n=r=2$ and $s=1$. The mean-curvature obstruction is the second assertion of theorem 4.12.3. $\square$

4.13 Holomorphic sectional curvature

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The curvature conditions above test a tangent direction and a fiber vector separately. For a metric on the tangent bundle, holomorphic sectional curvature tests the same vector in both places. Averaging these diagonal evaluations controls a symmetrized curvature trace. A second trace occurs when one differentiates the trace of the metric itself; their difference contains torsion and an exact form. With a Kähler metric for the contraction, integration removes the exact form and the torsion terms cancel. This gives an obstruction even when the metric whose curvature is tested is not closed.

In this section, $g$ is a Kähler metric on $X$, with form $\omega _ g$, and $h$ is any smooth Hermitian metric on $T^{1,0}X$, with form $\omega _ h$. We retain $n\geq 2$. The unscaled Chern curvature convention of 4.6.9 gives the coefficients

\[ R^ h_{i\bar j k\bar l} =-\partial _ i\partial _{\bar j}h_{k\bar l} +h^{p\bar q}(\partial _ i h_{k\bar q}) (\partial _{\bar j}h_{p\bar l}). \]

The Chern holomorphic sectional curvature is

4.33
\begin{equation} \index{Holomorphic sectional curvature!Chern connection} H_ h(x,[v])=\frac{R^ h(v,\bar v,v,\bar v)}{|v|_ h^4}, \qquad 0\ne v\in T_ x^{1,0}X. \end{equation}

It is quasi-negative if it is nonpositive everywhere and strictly negative in every direction at some point. Put

\[ u=\operatorname {tr}_ g h,\qquad q=\operatorname {tr}\bigl ((g^{-1}h)^2\bigr ). \]

In a $g$-unitary frame, define the real contractions

\[ Q_ g(h)=\sum _{i,k}R^ h_{i\bar i k\bar k},\qquad B_ g(h)=\sum _{i,k}R^ h_{i\bar k k\bar i},\qquad \mathcal R_ g(h)=\tfrac 12\bigl (Q_ g(h)+B_ g(h)\bigr ). \]

These expressions are invariant under a change of unitary frame. For a general $h$, the two traces $Q_ g(h)$ and $B_ g(h)$ can differ.

Lemma 4.13.1. At a point where $H_ h\leq -\kappa $ for some $\kappa \geq 0$, one has

\[ \mathcal R_ g(h)\leq -\frac{\kappa }{2}(u^2+q). \]

In particular, nonpositive holomorphic sectional curvature gives $\mathcal R_ g(h)\leq 0$.

Proof. In a $g$-unitary frame, let $Z_1,\ldots ,Z_ n$ be independent standard complex Gaussian variables, with density $\pi ^{-1}\mathrm e^{-|z|^2}$ on $\mathbb C$. Their second and fourth moments are $\mathbb E|Z_ i|^2=1$ and $\mathbb E|Z_ i|^4=2$. Independence and invariance under coordinate phases give

\[ \mathbb E[Z_ i\overline{Z_ j}Z_ k\overline{Z_ l}] =\delta _{ij}\delta _{kl}+\delta _{il}\delta _{kj}. \]

Thus $\mathbb E R^ h(Z,\bar Z,Z,\bar Z)=Q_ g(h)+B_ g(h)$ and $\mathbb E|Z|_ h^4=u^2+q$. Taking expectations in the homogeneous inequality $R^ h(Z,\bar Z,Z,\bar Z)\leq -\kappa |Z|_ h^4$ proves the result. $\square$

Let $A=\nabla ^ h-\nabla ^ g$ be the difference of the Chern connections, viewed as a tensor with two holomorphic covariant indices and a tangent-bundle value. Write

\[ S(v,w)=\tfrac 12\bigl (A(v,w)+A(w,v)\bigr ),\qquad T(v,w)=A(v,w)-A(w,v). \]

Since $g$ is Kähler, $T$ is the Chern torsion of $h$. Norms such as $|S|_{g,h}^2=\sum _{i,k}|S(e_ i,e_ k)|_ h^2$ use $g$ in the covariant indices and $h$ in the value. Let $r_{i\bar j}$ be the coefficients of the Ricci form $\operatorname {Ric}(g)=-\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \log \det (g_{i\bar j})$, and in a $g$-unitary frame set

\[ I_ g(h)=\sum _{i,k}r_{i\bar k}h_{k\bar i},\qquad \mathrm dV_ g=\frac{\omega _ g^ n}{n!},\qquad P_ g f=\operatorname {tr}_{\omega _ g}(\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f). \]

Proposition 4.13.2. The two metrics satisfy

4.34
\begin{equation} \int _ X\bigl (|S|_{g,h}^2+I_ g(h)-\mathcal R_ g(h)\bigr )\, \mathrm dV_ g=0. \end{equation}

Proof. We first compute the exact term that distinguishes the two curvature traces. Define

\begin{align*} D_{i\bar j k\bar l} ={}& \partial _ i\partial _{\bar j}h_{k\bar l} +\partial _ k\partial _{\bar l}h_{i\bar j}\\ & -\partial _ i\partial _{\bar l}h_{k\bar j} -\partial _ k\partial _{\bar j}h_{i\bar l}. \end{align*}

This is a tensor: putting holomorphic differentials first gives

\[ \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \omega _ h =\sum _{i<k,\, j<l}D_{i\bar j k\bar l} \, \mathrm dz^ i\wedge \mathrm dz^ k \wedge \mathrm d\bar z^ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z^ l. \]

For $D_ g=\sum _{i,k}D_{i\bar i k\bar k}$ in a $g$-unitary frame, expansion of the wedge product gives

\[ D_ g\, \mathrm dV_ g =2\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \omega _ h \wedge \frac{\omega _ g^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}. \]

Indeed, each pair $i<k$ contributes $D_{i\bar i k\bar k}\mathrm dV_ g$ to the wedge product and occurs twice in the contraction. Closedness of $\omega _ g$ and Stokes' theorem therefore give $\int _ XD_ g\, \mathrm dV_ g=0$.

The four-term antisymmetrization of the curvature formula is

\begin{align*} & R^ h_{i\bar j k\bar l}+R^ h_{k\bar l i\bar j} -R^ h_{i\bar l k\bar j}-R^ h_{k\bar j i\bar l}\\ & \hspace{3em}=h_{p\bar q}T^ p_{ik}\overline{T^ q_{jl}} -D_{i\bar j k\bar l}. \end{align*}

The second derivatives give $-D$, and the quadratic terms give the squared connection difference in the two skew indices. Contracting gives $2(Q_ g(h)-B_ g(h))=|T|_{g,h}^2-D_ g$, hence

\[ Q_ g(h)=\mathcal R_ g(h)+\tfrac 14|T|_{g,h}^2-\tfrac 14D_ g. \]

\begin{align*} P_ g u& =|A|_{g,h}^2+I_ g(h)-Q_ g(h)\\ & =|S|_{g,h}^2+I_ g(h)-\mathcal R_ g(h)+\tfrac 14D_ g, \end{align*}

where symmetric and skew tensors are orthogonal and $|A|_{g,h}^2=|S|_{g,h}^2+|T|_{g,h}^2/4$. Integrating removes both $P_ g u$ and $D_ g$, proving (4.34). $\square$

Corollary 4.13.3. Suppose that $\operatorname {Ric}(g)\geq 0$. Every Hermitian tangent metric $h$ with $H_ h\leq 0$ satisfies $\operatorname {Ric}(g)=0$ and $H_ h=0$ in every direction. In particular, $X$ admits no Hermitian tangent metric with quasi-negative Chern holomorphic sectional curvature.

Proof. The Ricci assumption gives $I_ g(h)\geq 0$, and lemma 4.13.1 gives $-\mathcal R_ g(h)\geq 0$. All three continuous terms in (4.34) are nonnegative, so each vanishes everywhere. At each point, the Gaussian expectation in the averaging proof is now zero. Its integrand $R^ h(Z,\bar Z,Z,\bar Z)$ is nonpositive. If it were negative in one direction, continuity and the strictly positive Gaussian density would make its expectation negative. It is therefore zero in every direction, which gives $H_ h=0$ and excludes quasi-negativity. $\square$

4.13.1 Rigidity on complex tori

For a flat reference metric, the vanishing symmetric connection term has a stronger consequence. Periodic integration by parts forces the second metric to be translation invariant, and hence to have zero full Chern curvature.

Proposition 4.13.4. Let $Y=\mathbb C^ m/\Lambda $ be a complex torus, with $m\geq 1$. A smooth Hermitian tangent metric $h$ on $Y$ has nonpositive Chern holomorphic sectional curvature if and only if it is translation invariant. Such a metric is Kähler and has zero Chern curvature.

Proof. Suppose first that $m\geq 2$, and take the flat Kähler metric $g$ induced by a constant Hermitian inner product on $\mathbb C^ m$. Choose linear coordinates in which this inner product is the identity. The coordinate vector fields descend to $Y$, even though the coordinates themselves need not. By corollary 4.13.3, $H_ h=0$; (4.34) also gives $S=0$. Since $g$ has zero connection coefficients, multiplying $S=0$ by $h$ gives

\[ \partial _ i h_{k\bar l}+\partial _ k h_{i\bar l}=0 \qquad \text{for all }i,k,l. \]

Fix $l$ and put $a_ k=h_{k\bar l}$. With respect to the flat volume, integration by parts and commutation of constant coordinate derivatives give

\[ \sum _{i,k}\int _ Y\partial _ i a_ k\, \overline{\partial _ k a_ i}\, \mathrm dV_ g =\int _ Y\left|\sum _ k\partial _{\bar k}a_ k\right|^2\mathrm dV_ g. \]

Consequently,

\[ \begin{aligned} 0& =\sum _{i,k}\int _ Y|\partial _ i a_ k+\partial _ k a_ i|^2\, \mathrm dV_ g\\ & =2\sum _{i,k}\int _ Y|\partial _ i a_ k|^2\, \mathrm dV_ g +2\int _ Y\left|\sum _ k\partial _{\bar k}a_ k\right|^2\mathrm dV_ g. \end{aligned} \]

Thus every $\partial _ i h_{k\bar l}$ vanishes. Hermitian symmetry gives the vanishing of the conjugate derivatives as well, so all coefficients of $h$ are constant.

For $m=1$, write the lifted metric as $h(z)|\mathrm dz|^2$, with $h$ positive and $\Lambda $-periodic. Its curvature coefficient is $-h\, \partial _ z\partial _{\bar z}\log h$. Nonpositive sectional curvature therefore gives $\partial _ z\partial _{\bar z}\log h\geq 0$. The complex Laplacian has zero integral over $Y$, so it vanishes. Integration by parts then shows that $\log h$ has zero gradient, and $h$ is constant.

Conversely, a constant positive Hermitian matrix defines a closed form and has zero connection and curvature coefficients. This proves all the assertions. $\square$

Corollary 4.13.5. Let $Y$ be a connected compact complex manifold admitting a finite unramified holomorphic covering $\pi :Z\to Y$ by a complex torus. Every smooth Hermitian tangent metric on $Y$ with nonpositive Chern holomorphic sectional curvature is Kähler and Chern flat.

Proof. The covering is locally biholomorphic, so the pullback metric has the same sectional-curvature sign. By proposition 4.13.4, its associated form is closed and its Chern curvature is zero. Both conclusions descend through the covering. $\square$

4.14 Projective first jets and positive bundle metrics

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

RC-positivity asks for one positive tangent direction for each fiber vector, whereas Griffiths positivity asks for positivity on every pair of nonzero tangent and fiber vectors. The Lamari criterion of theorem 4.3.1 characterizes positivity of a $(1,1)$-form by a duality with Gauduchon tests, and Griffiths positivity on a projective manifold admits an analogous duality. A Griffiths-positive metric on $E$ is the same as a strictly plurisubharmonic squared norm on the dual bundle, which is a linear condition on the dual Hermitian form. Twisting by powers of a very ample line bundle gives finite-dimensional spaces of candidate forms. At each level, either some candidate is strictly plurisubharmonic or a finite atomic positive measure annihilates the Levi forms of all candidates, and if every level fails, a weak limit of these measures annihilates the Levi forms of all smooth Hermitian forms. This measure lives over the dual bundle, whereas the Gauduchon currents in theorem 4.3.1 live on the base.

Throughout this section, $X$ is a projective manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$, $E\to X$ is a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank, and $F=E^*$. Fix a very ample line bundle $A$, a basis $t_1,\ldots ,t_ d$ of $H^0(X,A)$, and the Fubini–Study metric $a$ on $A$ determined by this basis. Fix also an integer $m_0\geq 0$ for which $E\otimes A^{m_0}$ is globally generated; this integer is part of the data. In a local frame of $A$, put

\[ \rho =\sum _{\alpha =1}^ d|t_\alpha |^2, \qquad a=\mathrm e^{-\phi },\qquad \phi =\log \rho . \]

We use the curvature conventions of 4.6.9. A Hermitian metric $g$ on a holomorphic vector bundle over $X$ is strictly Griffiths-positive if $g(R_ g(u,\bar u)v,v)>0$ for every $x\in X$, every nonzero $u\in T_ x^{1,0}X$ and every nonzero vector $v$ in the fiber over $x$; it is strictly Griffiths-negative if the reverse strict inequality holds.

For a smooth Hermitian form $h$ on $F$, not initially assumed positive, let $G_ h(v)=h(v,v)$ on its total space. Write $\mathcal L G_ h(\xi )$ for the complex Hessian evaluated on $\xi \in T^{1,0}F$; in local coordinates it is $\sum (G_ h)_{j\bar k}\xi _ j\bar\xi _ k$, the coefficient form of $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial G_ h$, without the factor $1/(2\pi )$.

Lemma 4.14.1. The function $G_ h$ is strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section if and only if $h$ is a smooth positive Hermitian metric with strictly Griffiths-negative curvature. Equivalently, its dual metric on $E$ is strictly Griffiths-positive.

Proof. On vertical tangent vectors the Levi form is $h$ itself, so strict positivity forces $h>0$. At a fixed base point, choose a holomorphic frame in which the matrix $H$ of $h$ is the identity and has vanishing first derivatives. Then $R_ h(u,\bar u)=-H_{u\bar u}$ at this point. A tangent vector to $F$ has horizontal and vertical components $(u,w)$, and

\[ \mathcal L G_ h(u,w)=|w|^2+v^*H_{u\bar u}v =|w|^2-h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v). \]

For $v\ne 0$, this is positive for every nonzero $(u,w)$ precisely when the curvature evaluation is negative for every nonzero $u$. The curvature of the dual metric is the negative transpose of the original curvature, with respect to the dual Hermitian pairing, which gives the last assertion. $\square$

The Levi forms will be tested on a compact set. Choose any smooth Hermitian metric $k$ on $F$, let $S=\{ v:k(v,v)=1\} $ be its unit sphere bundle, and choose a smooth positive Hermitian form $b$ on the complex vector bundle $W=T^{1,0}F|_ S$ over $S$. The set

\[ K=\{ (v,\xi ):v\in S,\ \xi \in W_ v,\ b(\xi ,\xi )=1\} \]

is compact. Define the real-linear operator $D$ by $D h=\mathcal L G_ h|_ S$, viewed as a Hermitian form on $W$. It is continuous from the $C^2$ norm of Hermitian forms on $F$ to the uniform norm on $W$, since its coefficients involve at most two base derivatives of $h$ and the fiber vectors range over a compact set. Fiber dilation $v\mapsto cv$ gives

\[ G_ h(cv)=|c|^2G_ h(v),\qquad \mathcal L G_ h|_{cv}(\mathrm d(v\mapsto cv)\xi ) =|c|^2\mathcal L G_ h|_ v(\xi ). \]

Thus strict positivity of $\mathcal LG_ h$ on $K$ is equivalent to strict positivity off the zero section.

\[ \operatorname {tr}(Z_0Z)\geq \operatorname {tr}(Z_0^2)>0. \]

4.14.2 Approximation with two derivatives

Let $\mathcal H_ m=\{ h_{m,B}:B=B^*\} $. To pass from these finite-dimensional tests to all smooth metrics, we need approximation in $C^2$, since $D$ involves two derivatives.

Lemma 4.14.3. The matrix-valued map $Y=tt^*/\rho $ embeds $X$ as a compact smooth submanifold of the real vector space of Hermitian $d\times d$ matrices. Restrictions of real polynomials in its real coordinates are dense in $C^2(X,\mathbb R)$ for the $C^2$ norm.

Proof. The normalized rank-one projector $Y$ determines the point $[t]$ of projective space, and its differential vanishes only in directions in which $\mathrm dt$ is proportional to $t$. Since $A$ is very ample, $Y$ is therefore injective with injective differential, and compactness makes it an embedding. A $C^2$ function on this image extends to a $C^2$ function on an ambient cube: use local coordinates in which the image is a graph, extend constantly in the normal coordinates, and combine these extensions with a finite smooth partition of unity and a cutoff. Rescale the cube to $[0,1]^ q$.

For such an extension $f$, its multivariate Bernstein polynomial is

\[ B_ Nf(y)=\sum _{0\leq j_1,\ldots ,j_ q\leq N} f(j/N)\prod _{\ell =1}^ q \binom N{j_\ell }y_\ell ^{j_\ell }(1-y_\ell )^{N-j_\ell }, \]

where $j=(j_1,\ldots ,j_ q)$. For a multi-index $\alpha $ with $|\alpha |\leq 2$, differentiation expresses $\partial ^\alpha B_ Nf$ as an average of $\Delta _{1/N}^\alpha f(j/N)$, with prefactor $\prod _\ell (N)_{\alpha _\ell }$ and Bernstein degrees $N-\alpha _\ell $. Here $(N)_ r=N(N-1)\cdots (N-r+1)$, and $\Delta ^\alpha _{1/N}$ is the corresponding iterated forward difference with step $1/N$. Repeated use of the fundamental theorem of calculus expresses $N^{|\alpha |}\Delta _{1/N}^\alpha f(j/N)$ as an average of $\partial ^\alpha f$ at points within $2/N$ in each affected coordinate.

The remaining Bernstein weights are the law of independent binomial variables of degrees $N-\alpha _\ell $, divided by $N$. Their means differ from $y_\ell $ by at most $2/N$ and their variances are at most $1/(4N)$. Uniform continuity of $\partial ^\alpha f$, splitting the average into a fixed small neighborhood of $y$ and its complement, and Chebyshev's inequality give uniform convergence to $\partial ^\alpha f$. The prefactor divided by $N^{|\alpha |}$ tends to one. This proves convergence for every derivative of order at most two, including the function itself. Restrict to the embedded image and use the chain rule in a finite collection of charts to obtain the asserted $C^2$ approximation. $\square$

Lemma 4.14.4. The spaces $\mathcal H_ m$ are nested, and their union is $C^2$ dense in the space of $C^2$ Hermitian forms on $F$.

Proof. For nesting, multiply the sections in $V_ m$ by each $t_\alpha $ and express the products in the basis of $V_{m+1}$. The identity $\sum _\alpha \mathrm e^{-\phi }|t_\alpha |^2=1$ writes $G_{m,B}$ as the sum of the corresponding weighted quadratic functions at level $m+1$, which combine into one Hermitian coefficient matrix at that level. Surjectivity of the multiplication map is not needed.

For density, write $p_{m_0}(v)=P_0v$ in a local frame of $F$, and put $L=\mathrm e^{-m_0\phi /2}P_0$. Global generation of $E\otimes A^{m_0}$ means that $L$ is injective, so $M=L^*L$ is positive definite. If a $C^2$ Hermitian form has matrix $H$, define

\[ C(x)=L M^{-1}H M^{-1}L^*. \]

Then $L^*CL=H$. A change of frame of $F$ cancels in this expression; a change of frame of $A$ multiplies $L$ by a scalar of modulus one, which also cancels. Thus $C$ is a globally defined $C^2$ Hermitian matrix function on $X$.

Applying lemma 4.14.3 to its real coordinates in the vector space of Hermitian matrices, we obtain Hermitian matrix polynomials in the entries of $Y$ that converge to $C$ in $C^2$; their degrees may increase along the sequence. Let $C_ q(Y)$ be such a polynomial, of degree at most $q$. Every monomial of degree $k$ in $Y$ has the form $t^ I\bar t^ J/\rho ^ k$, with $|I|=|J|=k$. For a polynomial of degree at most $q$, multiply each numerator by $\rho ^{q-k}$ to use the common denominator $\rho ^ q$. Consequently

\[ \mathrm e^{-m_0\phi }p_{m_0}^*C_ q(Y)p_{m_0} \]

is a quadratic expression in the product sections $\sigma _ i t^ I$ of $E\otimes A^{m_0+q}$, with weight $\mathrm e^{-(m_0+q)\phi }$. Express these products in the basis of $V_{m_0+q}$ and replace the coefficient matrix by its Hermitian part, which does not change the real function. The result is an element of $\mathcal H_{m_0+q}$. Since $L^*CL=H$ and multiplication by the fixed smooth matrix $L$ is continuous in $C^2$, these elements converge to $H$. $\square$

4.14.3 The metric and measure alternatives

A positive matrix-valued measure on $W\to S$ is a linear functional $T$ on the space of continuous Hermitian forms $\Phi $ on $W$ such that $\langle T,\Phi \rangle \geq 0$ whenever $\Phi \geq 0$. Its trace mass relative to $b$ is $\langle T,b\rangle $. We write $D^*T=0$ if $\langle T,Dh\rangle =0$ for every smooth Hermitian form $h$ on $F$.

Theorem 4.14.5. With the fixed projective data above, the following are equivalent:

  1. $E$ admits a smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric.

  2. For some $m\geq m_0$, a Hermitian matrix $B$ makes $G_{m,B}$ strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section.

  3. For some $m\geq m_0$, $0\notin \operatorname {conv}(\Psi _ m(K))$.

  4. There is no nonzero positive matrix-valued measure $T$ on $W\to S$ satisfying $D^*T=0$.

Proof. Given a metric as in the first assertion, its dual metric $h$ on $F$ satisfies $Dh\geq \delta b$ on $S$ for some $\delta >0$, by lemma 4.14.1 and compactness. The $C^2$ density in lemma 4.14.4 and the continuity of $D$ give a form $h_{m,B}$ with $D h_{m,B}\geq \delta b/2$, which proves the second assertion. Conversely, if $G_{m,B}$ is strictly plurisubharmonic, its vertical Levi form shows that $h_{m,B}$ is positive, and lemma 4.14.1 shows that its dual metric is strictly Griffiths-positive. The second and third assertions are equivalent by proposition 4.14.2.

If the first assertion holds, every nonzero positive matrix-valued measure $T$ has positive trace mass, and

\[ \langle T,Dh\rangle \geq \delta \langle T,b\rangle >0, \]

so such a measure cannot satisfy $D^*T=0$. Conversely, suppose that the third assertion fails at every level. By proposition 4.14.2, choose at each level $m$ an atomic probability measure $\mu _ m$ on $K$ with $\int _ K\Psi _ m\, \mathrm d\mu _ m=0$, so that $\mu _ m$ annihilates $Dh$ for every $h\in \mathcal H_ m$. A subsequence converges weakly to a probability measure $\mu $ on the compact set $K$. Since the spaces $\mathcal H_ m$ are nested, the limit annihilates $Dh$ for every $h$ in each fixed $\mathcal H_ j$, and the $C^2$ density and the continuity of $D$ extend this to every smooth Hermitian form.

Define

\[ \langle T,\Phi \rangle =\int _ K\Phi _ v(\xi ,\xi )\, \mathrm d\mu (v,\xi ). \]

This is a positive matrix-valued measure on $W\to S$ with $\langle T,b\rangle =1$ and $D^*T=0$. Its entries in local frames are represented by scalar measures: positivity bounds the functional on Hermitian forms of uniform norm at most one by the trace mass, so the Riesz representation theorem applies to the coordinate functionals, and a finite partition of unity combines these local representations. This contradicts the fourth assertion and completes the proof. $\square$

The equation $D^*T=0$ concerns a bundle over the sphere bundle $S\subset F=E^*$; it should not be confused with the equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$ for currents on $X$. The theorem gives no bound for the first successful level, and no finite procedure for deciding that all levels fail.

Corollary 4.14.6. If $E$ is globally generated and $m_0=0$, the level-zero test succeeds exactly when the complete evaluation map $\Phi :F\to H^0(X,E)^*$ is an immersion off the zero section.

Proof. At $m=0$, one has $\Psi _0=\mathrm d\Phi (\xi )\mathrm d\Phi (\xi )^*$, where $\Phi $ is written in the coordinates given by the chosen basis. If $\mathrm d\Phi $ is injective off the zero section, $B=\operatorname {Id}$ gives a strictly positive Levi form. If $\mathrm d\Phi $ has a nonzero kernel vector there, fiber dilation and normalization of the tangent vector place such a vector in $K$, where $\Psi _0=0$, and no Hermitian matrix can separate the image from zero. $\square$

Keep the root metric $\mathrm e^{-\phi }$ on $L$, and choose the sphere bundle $S$ and the compact set $K$ for $F$ as in section 4.14. For each integer $j\geq 0$, use the complete space

\[ \widetilde V_ j=H^0(X,L^{j+1}) \]

and pair its basis with $v\in F_ x$ to get a local column $p_ j(v)$, written in a local frame of $L^ j$. For every Hermitian coefficient matrix $B$, define

\[ \widetilde G_{j,B}(v)=\mathrm e^{-j\phi (x)}p_ j(v)^*B p_ j(v). \]

These are globally defined functions, quadratic on each fiber. Their Levi matrices are

\[ \begin{gathered} \widetilde\Psi _ j(v,\xi )=\mathrm e^{-j\phi } (r_ jr_ j^*-j\kappa p_ jp_ j^*),\\ r_ j=\mathrm dp_ j(\xi )-j\phi _ u p_ j,\qquad \kappa =\phi _{u\bar u}, \end{gathered} \]

where $u$ is the projection of $\xi $ to the base. As in (4.37), direct differentiation gives $\mathcal L\widetilde G_{j,B}(\xi )=\operatorname {tr}(B\widetilde\Psi _ j)$, and $\widetilde\Psi _ j$ transforms in the same way under changes of frames.

Proposition 4.15.2. The first integer $j\geq 0$ for which some $\widetilde G_{j,B}$, with $B$ an arbitrary Hermitian matrix, is strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section is $j=q-1$. At every lower level, zero belongs to $\operatorname {conv}(\widetilde\Psi _ j(K))$; one atom witnesses this at $j=0$, and two atoms suffice at each $1\leq j<q-1$.

Proof. At $j=q-1$, choose the basis $\sigma _ i$ that defines $\phi _ A$ and take $B=\operatorname {Id}$. Since $p_ j(v)=v(\sigma _ i(x))_ i$ in the chosen frames,

\[ \widetilde G_{q-1,\operatorname {Id}}(v) =\mathrm e^{-(q-1)\phi }|v|^2\mathrm e^{q\phi } =\mathrm e^\phi |v|^2. \]

This function is strictly plurisubharmonic by lemma 4.15.1.

Now suppose that $0\leq j<q-1$. Then $j+1<q$, so by lemma 4.15.1 every section in $\widetilde V_ j$ comes from the first summand of the decomposition, which does not involve $w$. At a ramification point of $C$, choose a local coordinate $t$ on $C$, and use frames of the bundles pulled back from $\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^{n-1}$. Writing $\zeta $ for the coordinates of the factor $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, the column has the form $p_ j(v)=vU_ j(\pi (t),\zeta )$. It is nonzero for $v\ne 0$, since $L^{j+1}$ is globally generated, and since $\pi $ ramifies at the point, its derivative in the $t$ direction vanishes there when $v$ is held fixed.

Choose such a fiber vector $v$ on the sphere bundle $S$. Write tangent vectors to the total space of $F$ by their components in the $t$ direction, in the $\zeta $ directions and in the fiber direction. Let $\xi _ v=(0,0,v)$ be the vertical radial vector, let $u=\partial /\partial t$, and let $\xi _ h=(u,0,j\phi _ u v)$. At this point $\mathrm dp_ j(\xi _ v)=p_ j$ and $r_ j(\xi _ h)=0$. Thus

\[ \begin{aligned} \widetilde\Psi _ j(v,\xi _ v)& =\mathrm e^{-j\phi }p_ jp_ j^*,\\ \widetilde\Psi _ j(v,\xi _ h)& =-j\kappa \mathrm e^{-j\phi }p_ jp_ j^*, \qquad \kappa >0. \end{aligned} \]

Let $a_ v$ and $a_ h$ be the squared norms of $\xi _ v$ and $\xi _ h$ for the form $b$, and divide each tangent vector by its norm to obtain points of $K$. At $j=0$ the second matrix is zero, which gives the witness with one atom. For $j>0$, the matrix at the second normalized point is $-c$ times the matrix at the first, with $c=j\kappa a_ v/a_ h>0$. The probability measure giving these two points the weights $c/(1+c)$ and $1/(1+c)$ has zero matrix integral, which excludes strict positivity for every Hermitian $B$. This proves failure at all lower levels, so the first successful level is $j=q-1$. $\square$

Corollary 4.15.3. For the data $E=L$ and $A=L^ q$, with the Fubini–Study metric of a basis of $H^0(X,A)$, the criterion of theorem 4.14.5 applies with $m_0=0$. Its untwisted level fails, although $E$ carries a strictly Griffiths-positive metric, and a higher level succeeds. The atom witnessing the failure at level zero annihilates only the Levi forms of the level-zero test space, and does not give a measure with $D^*T=0$.

Proof. By lemma 4.15.1, $L$ is globally generated and $A$ is very ample, as the criterion requires. At a ramification point, the complete untwisted evaluation map has a nonzero tangent vector in its kernel, in the direction of the cover, as in the preceding proof; hence corollary 4.14.6 gives failure at level zero. The positive root metric and theorem 4.14.5 give success at some higher level. Moreover, the Levi form of the dual of this positive metric pairs strictly positively with every nonzero positive matrix-valued measure on $W\to S$, so the atom at level zero cannot annihilate the Levi forms of all smooth Hermitian forms. $\square$

The level $m$ of the criterion in theorem 4.14.5 corresponds to $j=qm$ in the root-weight family, since $E\otimes A^ m=L^{qm+1}$ and $m\phi _ A=qm\phi $. The first success at $j=q-1$ therefore gives no bound on the first successful level $m$ of that criterion. The root-weight family is nested in steps of $q$, because multiplication by the sections of $A$ uses $\sum _ i|\sigma _ i|^2=\mathrm e^{q\phi }$; we do not claim that it is nested in steps of one. As $q$ grows, the root-weight family has arbitrarily many failed initial levels, although the bundle carries a strictly positive metric.
