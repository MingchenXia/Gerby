Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Fix a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

proposition 17.12.16 allows us to talk about the Legendre transforms of test curves in the expected way.

The general definition of the Legendre transform definition 21.11.22 can be translated as follows:

Definition 17.13.1.2105331E66C7 Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We define its Legendre transform as the map $\Gamma ^*\colon (0,\infty )\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ given by

22C9794122C8 17.13.2
\begin{equation} \Gamma ^*_ t=\sup _{\tau \in \mathbb {R}} \left(t\tau + \Gamma _{\tau }\right)\footnotemark . \end{equation}
Note. There is no usc regularization in the preceding formula. This is not a typo.
Thanks to 17.12.3, (17.13.2) can be equivalently written as
\[ \Gamma ^*_ t=\sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(t\tau + \Gamma _{\tau }\right)=\sup _{\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }} \left(t\tau + \Gamma _{\tau }\right). \]

It is sometimes handy to define

1824FEE5F9FF 17.13.3
\begin{equation} \Gamma ^*_0\coloneqq \phi \end{equation}

at $t=0$. But it is important to remember that, with this convention, (17.13.2) is not true at $t=0$ in general.

Remark 17.13.4.ABB931E35703 Here we do not discuss the case $t<0$ because its behavior is straightforward: take $x\in X$; if $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)=-\infty $ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, then $\Gamma _ t^*(x)=-\infty $; otherwise, $\Gamma _ t^*(x)=\infty $.

The information about $t>0$ suffices to characterize $\Gamma $.

Proposition 17.13.5.1F48BF7602CA Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

24A2034A1287 17.13.6
\begin{equation} \Gamma _{\tau }=\inf _{t>0} \left(\Gamma ^*_ t-t\tau \right) \end{equation}

for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$.

Due to our convention (17.13.3), in (17.13.6) we may equivalently take $t\geq 0$.

Proof. Fix $x\in X$. We want to establish (17.13.6) at $x$. We distinguish two cases. First suppose that $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)=-\infty $ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$ and hence all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. In this case, we have $\Gamma _ t^*(x)=-\infty $ for all $t>0$. Therefore, (17.13.6) follows trivially.

Otherwise, by 17.13.4, we know that $\Gamma ^*_ t(x)=\infty $ for all $t<0$. The relative interior of the domain of $t\mapsto \Gamma ^*_ t(x)$ is contained in $(0,\infty )$. Therefore, (17.13.6) follows from theorem 21.11.24, proposition 17.12.16. $\square$

In definition 17.13.1, we made the nontrivial claim that $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for all $t>0$. Let us prove this.

Lemma 17.13.7.BC74B8EA334F Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for all $t>0$. In fact, $\Gamma ^*$ is upper semicontinuous as a function of $X\times (0,\infty )$.

Proof. We first observe that for each $x\in X$, we have

\[ \Gamma _ t^*(x)\leq t\Gamma _{\max }<\infty . \]

Let $R=\{ a+\mathrm{i}b\in \mathbb {C}:a>0,b\in \mathbb {R}\} $. We consider

\[ F\colon X\times R\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad (x,a+\mathrm{i}b)\mapsto \Gamma _ a^*(x). \]

Let $\pi \colon X\times R\rightarrow X$ be the natural projection. Observe that the upper semicontinuous regularization $G$ of $F$ is $\pi ^*\theta $-psh by proposition 5.14.2. It suffices to show that $F=G$. We let

\[ E\coloneqq \left\{ (x,z)\in X\times R: F(x,z)<G(x,z) \right\} . \]

We want to argue that $E=\varnothing $. Since $F$ is invariant under translations in the imaginary direction of $R$, the same is true for its upper semicontinuous regularization $G$. So $E$ can be written as $B\times \mathrm{i}\mathbb {R}$ for some set $B\subseteq X\times (0,\infty )$. Since $E$ is a pluripolar set by proposition 5.14.5, it has zero Lebesgue measure. Hence, $B$ has zero Lebesgue measure. For each $x\in X$, write

\[ B_ x=\left\{ t\in (0,\infty ): (x,t)\in B \right\} . \]

By Fubini's theorem, $B_ x$ has vanishing $1$-dimensional Lebesgue measure for all $x\in X\setminus Z$, where $Z\subseteq X$ is a subset of measure $0$. We may assume that $Z\supseteq \{ \Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}=-\infty \} $ so that for $x\in X\setminus Z$, $\Gamma ^*_ t(x)\neq -\infty $ for all $t>0$.

For any $x\in X\setminus Z$, both $t\mapsto F(x,t)$ and $G(x,t)$ are convex functions with values in $\mathbb {R}$ on $(0,\infty )$. They agree almost everywhere, hence everywhere by their continuity. It follows that for $x\in X\setminus Z$, we have $B_ x=\varnothing $.

By proposition 17.13.5, for any $x\in X$, we have

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }(x)=\inf _{t>0} (F(x,t)-t\tau ),\quad \tau <\Gamma _{\max }. \]

On the other hand, let

DBF41B839C63 17.13.8
\begin{equation} \chi _{\tau }(x)=\inf _{t>0} (G(x,t)-t\tau ),\quad \tau < \Gamma _{\max },\quad x\in X. \end{equation}

By Kiselman's principle proposition 5.14.15, $\chi _{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. But on $X\setminus Z$, we already know that $\Gamma _{\tau }=\chi _{\tau }$ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. By proposition 5.14.6,

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=\chi _{\tau },\quad \tau <\Gamma _{\max }. \]

Now we conclude that $F(x,t)=G(x,t)$ by corollary 21.11.26. $\square$

Corollary 17.13.9.4B87EF03E3EA Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ for all $t>0$.

Proof. Fix $t>0$. We already know that $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by 17.13.7. It suffices to show that

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t\sim _ P \phi . \]

From (17.13.2) and proposition 16.8.28, we know that

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t\sim _ P \operatorname *{sup}_{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma _{\tau }=\phi . \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Lemma 17.13.10.C6092031D5B3 Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

\[ \sup _ X \Gamma _ t^*=t\Gamma _{\max } \]

for all $t>0$.

In particular, $t\mapsto \Gamma ^*_ t-t\Gamma _{\max }$ is a decreasing function in $t>0$.

Proof. Since $\Gamma _{\tau }\leq 0$ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \sup _ X \Gamma _ t^*\leq t\Gamma _{\max } \]

for all $t>0$. Conversely, for each $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$,

\[ \sup _ X\Gamma _ t^*\geq \sup _ X(t\tau +\Gamma _{\tau })=t\tau . \]

Letting $\tau \to \Gamma _{\max }$ gives the reverse inequality. The final assertion follows directly from the formula

\[ \Gamma _ t^*-t\Gamma _{\max }=\sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}\Bigl (\Gamma _\tau -t(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau )\Bigr ). \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Lemma 17.13.11.9C6538C4298E Given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we have $\Gamma ^*\in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

See definition 17.10.38 for the notation $\mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Proof. It follows from 17.13.7, (17.13.2) and proposition 5.14.2 that the restriction of $\Gamma ^*$ to each compact subinterval of $(0,\infty )$ is a subgeodesic. By corollary 17.13.9, for any $t>0$, $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

First observe that as $t\to 0+$, we have

E5C60BE9B124 17.13.12
\begin{equation} \Gamma _ t^*\xrightarrow {L^1}\phi . \end{equation}

By 17.13.10 and proposition 5.17.3, it suffices to show each $L^1$-cluster point $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ of $\Gamma _ t^*$ as $t\to 0$ is equal to $\phi $.

To see this, first observe that by (17.13.2), for any fixed $t>0$,

\[ \Gamma _ t^*\leq t\Gamma _{\max }+\phi . \]

Therefore, $\psi \leq \phi $. On the other hand, for any fixed $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, by (17.13.2), we have

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t\geq \Gamma _{\tau }+t\tau \]

for any $t>0$. So $\psi \geq \Gamma _{\tau }$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 5.14.6. It follows that $\psi \geq \phi $. Therefore, $\psi =\phi $.

Assume that $\Gamma ^*$ is not a geodesic ray. Then we can find $0\leq a<b$ such that $(\Gamma ^*_ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$ differs from the geodesic $(\eta _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$ from $\Gamma ^*_ a$ to $\Gamma ^*_ b$. The existence of $(\eta _ t)_ t$ is guaranteed by proposition 17.10.24. We consider the subgeodesic $(\ell _ t)_{t>0}$ given by $\ell _ t=\eta _ t$ for $t\in (a,b)$ and $\ell _ t=\Gamma ^*_ t$ otherwise. Note that $\ell $ is a subgeodesic due to the gluing lemma 17.10.16.

Consider the Legendre transform

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }=\inf _{t>0} (\ell _ t-t\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \]

Then $\Gamma '_{\tau }\geq \Gamma _{\tau }$ and $\Gamma '_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $ by proposition 5.14.15 for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$.

We claim that

BD13D6DEBB93 17.13.13
\begin{equation} \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _{\tau }+(b-a)(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \end{equation}

Observe that $\Gamma '_{\tau }\equiv -\infty $ when $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$ by 17.13.10. Thus it suffices to consider $\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }$. Put

\[ C_{\tau }\coloneqq (b-a)(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau )\geq 0. \]

For any $s\in [a,b]$, the monotonicity of $t\mapsto \Gamma _ t^*-t\Gamma _{\max }$ from 17.13.10 gives

\[ \Gamma _ b^*-b\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau }. \]

Since $\ell _ b=\Gamma _ b^*$, we get

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _ b^*-b\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau },\quad s\in [a,b]. \]

For $s\notin [a,b]$, we have $\ell _ s=\Gamma _ s^*$, hence

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau }. \]

Taking the infimum over all $s>0$ gives

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _{\tau }+C_{\tau }. \]

Therefore, (17.13.13) follows. In particular, for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have $\Gamma '_{\tau }\sim \Gamma _{\tau }$. Moreover, $\sup _ X\ell _ t\leq t\Gamma _{\max }$ for all $t>0$: this is clear outside $(a,b)$ from 17.13.10, and on $(a,b)$ it follows from the convexity of subgeodesics applied to the geodesic $(\eta _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$. Hence, for $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$,

\[ \sup _ X(\ell _ t-t\tau )\leq t(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau ). \]

Letting $t\to 0+$ gives $\Gamma '_{\tau }\leq 0$. It follows from the fact that $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is a model potential that $\Gamma _{\tau }=\Gamma '_{\tau }$ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. Therefore, by theorem 21.11.24, we have $\Gamma ^*_ t=\ell _ t$ for all $t>0$, which is a contradiction. $\square$

Given $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, define its Legendre transform

10B32891AA98 17.13.14
\begin{equation} \ell ^*_{\tau }\coloneqq \inf _{t>0} (\ell _ t-t\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \end{equation}

Lemma 17.13.15.7B83BD1D5FB9 Given $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, then $\ell ^*=(\ell ^*_\tau )_{\tau <\sup _ X \ell _1}\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Proof. Note that it follows from proposition 5.14.15 that $\ell ^*_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $ for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. It is clear that $\mathbb {R}\ni \tau \mapsto \ell ^*_{\tau }$ is a decreasing and concave function.

By proposition 17.10.39,

\[ \sup _ X\ell _ t= t\sup _ X \ell _1 \quad \forall t\geq 0. \]

Set $C=\sup _ X\ell _1$. By proposition 17.10.39, $\sup _ X(\ell _ t-Ct)=0$ for all $t>0$. For each $x$ outside a pluripolar set, the function $t\mapsto \ell _ t(x)-Ct$ is convex on $(0,\infty )$ and bounded above by $0$, hence it is decreasing. By proposition 5.14.6, the same monotonicity holds as an inequality of $\theta $-psh functions. Thus $(\ell _ t-Ct)_{t>0}$ is a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Its infimum is not identically $-\infty $, since the family is normalized by $\sup _ X(\ell _ t-Ct)=0$ and is compact in $L^1$ by proposition 5.17.3. Therefore

\[ \ell ^*_{C}=\inf _{t>0}(\ell _ t-Ct)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ) \]

by proposition 5.14.2.

On the other hand, for $\tau >\sup _ X \ell _1$, the same argument shows that

\[ \ell ^*_{\tau }\equiv -\infty . \]

Therefore, $\ell ^*_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ if and only if $\tau \leq \ell ^*_{\max }\coloneqq \sup _ X \ell _1$.

We claim that $(\ell ^*_{\tau })_{\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }}$ is a test curve. We first observe that for $\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }$, we have

\[ \ell ^*_{\tau }\leq \ell _1-\tau \sim _ P \phi . \]

Therefore,

5BF1777D23C2 17.13.16
\begin{equation} \ell ^*_{\tau }\preceq _ P \phi ,\quad \forall \tau <\ell ^*_{\max }. \end{equation}

Also observe that for any $\tau \leq \ell ^*_{\max }$ and any $t>0$, we have

\[ \sup _ X \ell ^*_{\tau }\leq \sup _ X \ell _ t-t\tau =\ell ^*_{\max }t-t\tau . \]

Letting $t\to 0+$, we find that for any $\tau \leq \ell ^*_{\max }$, we have

6F01D0840C12 17.13.17
\begin{equation} \sup _ X \ell ^*_{\tau }\leq 0. \end{equation}

Fix $\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }$. We want to argue that

59C9610616F1 17.13.18
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }\left[\ell ^*_{\tau }\right]=\ell ^*_{\tau }. \end{equation}

First we claim that for any $C>0$, we have

25F16D030FA9 17.13.19
\begin{equation} (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land \phi =(\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land V_{\theta }. \end{equation}

The $\leq $ direction is trivial. We argue the reverse inequality, which reduces to

\[ \phi \geq (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land V_{\theta }. \]

Since $\phi $ is model and $(\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land V_{\theta }\leq 0$, it suffices to show that

\[ \phi \succeq _ P (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land V_{\theta }, \]

which follows from (17.13.16). Therefore, (17.13.19) is established. Thanks to (17.13.17), we have the obvious inequality

\[ (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land V_{\theta }\geq \ell ^*_{\tau } \]

for any $C>0$. Therefore, in order to prove (17.13.18), it remains to argue that for any $C>0$,

CBA3E07AB082 17.13.20
\begin{equation} (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land \phi \leq \ell ^*_{\tau }. \end{equation}

For this purpose, let us consider the following geodesics: For any $M>0$ and $t\in [0,1]$, let

\[ \ell ^{1,M}_ t= \ell _{tM}-tM\tau ,\quad \ell ^{2,M}_ t= (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land \phi -Ct. \]

It is clear that at $t=0,1$, we have $\ell ^{2,M}_ t\leq \ell ^{1,M}_ t$. Hence, the same holds for all $t\in [0,1]$. In particular, for any fixed $s\in (0,1]$, we have

\[ (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land \phi -Cs\leq \ell _{sM}-sM\tau \]

for all $M>0$. Taking infimum with respect to $M>0$, we find

\[ (\ell ^*_{\tau }+C)\land \phi -Cs\leq \ell ^*_{\tau }. \]

Since $s\in (0,1]$ is arbitrary, we conclude (17.13.20).

Finally, we check the condition at $-\infty $. Set

\[ \psi \coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }}{}\! {}^{*}\ell ^*_{\tau }. \]

The family $(\ell ^*_{\tau })_{\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }}$ is locally uniformly bounded from above, so $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Thanks to corollary 17.10.9, outside a pluripolar set, the function $t\mapsto \ell _ t(x)$ is a finite closed convex function on $[0,\infty )$ with value $\phi (x)$ at $t=0$. Applying the one-variable Legendre duality gives

\[ \psi (x)=\adjustlimits \sup _{\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }}\inf _{t>0}\bigl (\ell _ t(x)-t\tau \bigr )=\ell _0(x)=\phi (x)\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]

Hence $\psi =\phi $ by proposition 5.14.6. In particular, $\ell ^*_{-\infty }=\phi $ has positive mass. $\square$

Theorem 17.13.21.B28F0A28CB3E The Legendre transform in definition 17.13.1 is a bijection

6D5BA4827FF5 17.13.22
\begin{equation} \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi ). \end{equation}

Moreover, this bijection restricts to the following bijections:

AE510D6D0C2F 17.13.23
\begin{equation} \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi ),\quad \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi ). \end{equation}

For any $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we have

4884EC5C8FA9 17.13.24
\begin{equation} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )=\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ^*). \end{equation}
Recall that the two energy functionals in (17.13.24) are defined in (17.12.12) and definition 17.10.82 respectively.

The correspondence (17.13.22) will be referred to as the Ross–Witt Nyström correspondence.

To appreciate this result, just consider the simple case where $\theta $ is a Kähler form and $\phi =0$. In this case, elements in $\mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta )$ are rays of bounded potentials, while elements in $\mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta )$ are rays of singular potentials. This result establishes a bridge between the pluripotential theory of regular potentials and that of singular potentials!

Proof. Step 1. We first establish (17.13.22).

It follows from 17.13.11 that the forward map is well-defined. The inverse map is given by (17.13.14). We show that the inverse map is also well-defined. Given $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we know from 17.13.15 that $\ell ^*\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We need to show that $\ell ^*\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

The equality $\ell ^*_{-\infty }=\phi $ was proved in 17.13.15. Therefore, $\ell ^*\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ as expected.

The two operations are inverse to each other thanks to corollary 21.11.26. Hence, (17.13.22) is established.

Step 2. Next we consider the bounded situation. Namely, we want to establish the second half of (17.13.23).

Suppose that $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Take $\tau _0\in \mathbb {R}$ so that $\Gamma _{\tau }=\phi $ for all $\tau \leq \tau _0$. It follows from (17.13.2) that

\[ \Gamma _ t^*\geq \phi +t\tau _0 \]

for all $t>0$. Therefore, $\Gamma ^*_ t\sim \phi $ for all $t>0$ and hence $\Gamma ^*\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Conversely, suppose that $\ell \in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Thanks to proposition 17.10.37, there is a constant $C>0$ such that

\[ \ell _ t\geq \phi -Ct. \]

Therefore, according to (17.13.14), we have

\[ \ell ^*_{\tau }\geq \inf _{t>0} \Bigl ( \phi -(C+\tau )t \Bigr )=\phi \]

if $\tau \leq -C$. Therefore, $\ell ^*_{\tau }=\phi $ for all $\tau \leq -C$.

Step 3. We establish (17.13.24) and the first half of (17.13.23).

Step 3.1. We reduce to the case where $\Gamma _{\max }=0$.

Suppose that we define

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }=\Gamma _{\tau +\Gamma _{\max }},\quad \forall \tau <0. \]

Then $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ as well and for all $t>0$,

\[ \Gamma '^*_ t=\sup _{\tau <0} \left( t\tau +\Gamma '_{\tau } \right)=\sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left( t\tau +\Gamma _{\tau } \right)-t\Gamma _{\max }=\Gamma ^*_ t-t\Gamma _{\max }. \]

Therefore,

\[ \mathbf{E}^{\phi }\left(\Gamma '^*\right)=\mathbf{E}^{\phi }\left(\Gamma ^*\right)-\Gamma _{\max }\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n. \]

by (16.5.13). Using (17.12.12), we also have

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ')=& \int _{-\infty }^0 \left( \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma '_{\tau }}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n \right)\, \mathrm{d}\tau \\ =& \int _{-\infty }^{\Gamma _{\max }}\left( \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau }}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n \right)\, \mathrm{d}\tau \\ =& \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )-\Gamma _{\max }\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, it suffices to establish (17.13.24) for $\Gamma '$ in place of $\Gamma $.

Step 3.2. We assume that $\Gamma _{\max }=0$ and $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We prove (17.13.24).

For $N\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, $M \in \mathbb {Z}$, we introduce the following:

\[ \Gamma ^{*,N,M}_ t \coloneqq \max _{\substack {k \in \mathbb {Z} \\ k \leq M}} \left(\Gamma _{k/2^ N} + tk/2^ N\right)\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi ),\quad t>0. \]

We first claim that for all $t>0$, $N\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ and $M\in \mathbb {Z}_{<0}$,

1C55A624E61E 17.13.25
\begin{equation} \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{(M+1)/2^ N}}\leq E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M+1}\right)-E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\leq \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{M/2^ N}}. \end{equation}

Assuming this, let us prove (17.13.24).

Fixing $N$, let $M=\lfloor 2^{N}\Gamma _{\min }\rfloor $. Recall that $\Gamma _{\min }$ is defined in (17.12.11). Then repeated applications of (17.13.25) yield

\[ \sum _{j=M+1}^0 \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{j/2^ N}}\leq E^{\phi }_{\theta }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,0}\right)-E^{\phi }_{\theta }\left(\Gamma _ t^{*,N,M}\right)\leq \sum _{j=M}^{-1} \frac{t}{2^ N} \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\Gamma _{j/2^ N}}. \]

Since $M \leq 2^{N}\Gamma _{\min }$, we have

Proposition 17.13.35 (He–Testorf–Wang).E6915C7339CD Let $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Given any $\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }$ and $x\in X$, we have

\begin{equation} \mathcal{I}\left(\ell ^*_{\tau }\right)_{x}=\left\{ f\in \mathcal{O}_{X,x}: |f|^2\int _0^{\infty } \exp (-\ell _ t+t\tau )\, \mathrm{d}t \textup{ is integrable near }x \right\} . \end{equation}

Proof. Fix $x\in X$, $\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }$ and $f\in \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$.

Step 1. We first assume that $f$ lies in the right-hand side of (17.13.36).

Given any $y\in X$, it follows from (17.13.14) that there is $t_0>0$ with

\[ \ell ^*_{\tau }(y)+1\geq \ell _{t_0}(y)-t_0\tau . \]

Observe that $t\mapsto \ell _ t-t\ell ^*_{\max }$ is decreasing in $t$ by corollary 17.13.31, it follows that for $t\in [t_0,t_0+1]$, we have

\[ \ell ^*_{\tau }(y)+1-t_0(\ell ^*_{\max }-\tau )\geq \ell _{t_0}(y)-t_0\ell ^*_{\max }\geq \ell _{t}(y)-t\ell ^*_{\max }. \]

Since $\tau < \ell ^*_{\max }$ and $t_0\geq t-1$, we deduce that

77E18B7AC795 17.13.37
\begin{equation} \ell ^*_{\tau }(y)+1+\ell ^*_{\max }-\tau \geq \ell _{t}(y)-t\tau ,\quad t\in [t_0,t_0+1]. \end{equation}

Take a sufficiently small open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that

\[ \int _ U |f|^2\int _0^{\infty } \exp (-\ell _ t+t\tau )\, \mathrm{d}t\, \omega ^ n<\infty . \]

Applying (17.13.37), we deduce that

\[ \int _ U |f|^2\exp \left(-\ell ^*_{\tau }\right)\, \omega ^ n<\infty . \]

Therefore, $f\in \mathcal{I}(\ell ^*_{\tau })_ x$.
