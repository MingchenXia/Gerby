14.14 The largest-eigenvalue estimate148E6041EC3F

148E6041EC3F

The matrix structure now gives a bound for the complex Hessian in terms of the squared gradient. We apply the maximum principle to the largest eigenvalue of $g$, corrected by functions of the gradient and of the potential. The missing torsion indices allow the concavity square to absorb the third derivatives. If the smallest eigenvalue is not too negative, the positive background closes the estimate; otherwise that eigenvalue produces a large positive Hessian square.

We keep the notation of section 14.13. Plain subscripts on scalar functions and subscripts after a comma on matrix coefficients denote coordinate derivatives, and the coefficients of the Chern connection of $\gamma $ are written $\Gamma ^ q_{ik}$. Choose $\tau >0$ with $Q_0\geq \tau \gamma $.

Theorem 14.14.1.2763FB514B34 Fix smooth Hermitian forms $\gamma _0,\gamma $ on a compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n\geq 2$. For a smooth real function $h$ on $X$, suppose that $v$ is a smooth solution of

\[ Q=Q_0+P_\gamma (\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial v)+Z(\partial v)>0, \qquad \log \det _\gamma Q=h, \]

with the tensors of section 14.13. If $\| v\| _{C^0}$ and $\| h\| _{C^2}$ are bounded, then

\[ \sup _ X|\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial v|_\gamma \leq C\bigl (1+\sup _ X|\partial v|_\gamma ^2\bigr ), \]

where $C$ depends only on these bounds and the fixed smooth data.

Proof. Adding a constant, we may assume $\sup _ Xv=0$, and we write $-M\leq v\leq 0$. Set

\[ K=1+\sup _ X|\partial v|_\gamma ^2,\qquad \phi (s)=-\tfrac 12\log (1-s/(2K)),\qquad \psi (t)=D_1\mathrm{e}^{-D_2t}. \]

The positive constants $D_1,D_2$ will be chosen below using only the fixed data. On $0\leq s\leq K$,

\[ \frac1{4K}\leq \phi '\leq \frac1{2K},\qquad \phi ''=2(\phi ')^2,\qquad 0\leq \phi \leq \tfrac 12\log 2. \]

Let $\lambda _1$ be the largest eigenvalue of $g=\chi +\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial v+W$ relative to $\gamma $. It is positive, because $\operatorname {tr}_\gamma g=\operatorname {tr}_\gamma Q>0$. Consider a maximum point of

\[ H=\log \lambda _1+\phi (|\partial v|_\gamma ^2)+\psi (v). \]

It suffices to bound $\lambda _1/K$ at this point, because the other two terms of $H$ are uniformly bounded once $D_1,D_2$ are fixed. We may assume that this ratio exceeds any fixed constant specified in the course of the argument.

Choose holomorphic coordinates that are unitary at the maximum point, with $g$ diagonal there and eigenvalues ordered as $\lambda _1\geq \cdots \geq \lambda _ n$. All estimates below are at this point, and repeated indices are summed only when a sum is written. Put $\lambda =\lambda _1$ and use the weights $t_ i,f_ k$ and their sum $\mathcal F$ from lemma 14.13.5. Since $\sum _ i\lambda _ i>0$ and the eigenvalues are ordered,

\[ |\lambda _ i|\leq (n-1)\lambda ,\qquad |v_{i\bar j}|\leq C\lambda \quad \hbox{if }\lambda \geq K. \]

The constant $C$ may change from line to line; it is independent of $K$ and, until $D_1,D_2$ are fixed below, also of $D_1,D_2$. The pure second derivatives $v_{ij}$ are not covered by this bound. $\square$

14.14.2 Differentiating the largest eigenvalue.9994CF39B2B0

Since $\lambda _1$ need not be smooth where it is a multiple eigenvalue, we first perturb $g$. Choose a smooth local unitary frame that is parallel for the Chern connection at the point, with first vector an eigenvector for $\lambda $. Let $B$ be the tensor that is diagonal in this frame, with eigenvalues $0,\epsilon _0,\ldots ,\epsilon _0$, where $\epsilon _0>0$ is fixed and small. Then $B\geq 0$, $\nabla B=0$ at the point, and its second derivatives are bounded by fixed constants. The largest eigenvalue $\widetilde\lambda $ of $g-B$ is simple near the point, is at most $\lambda _1(g)$, and equals $\lambda $ at the point. Hence the smooth function $\widetilde H$, defined like $H$ with $\widetilde\lambda $ in place of $\lambda _1$, also has a local maximum there. Only the largest eigenvalue needs to be simple.

For a family of self-adjoint matrices with a simple largest eigenvalue, differentiate the equation for the normalized eigenvector. Its component in the $p$th eigenspace is the corresponding derivative of the matrix divided by the eigenvalue gap. Differentiating the eigenvalue once more therefore gives

\begin{align*} a_ k:=\widetilde\lambda _ k& =\nabla _ k g_{1\bar1},\\ \widetilde\lambda _{k\bar k} & =\nabla _{\bar k}\nabla _ k g_{1\bar1}+O(1) +\sum _{p>1}\frac{|\nabla _ k g_{1\bar p}|^2+ |\nabla _ k g_{p\bar1}|^2}{\lambda -\lambda _ p+\epsilon _0}. \end{align*}

This formula remains valid if some of the lower eigenvalues coincide, with the sum taken over any orthonormal basis of their eigenspaces. The gaps are positive and at most $n\lambda +\epsilon _0$. In holomorphic coordinates, $a_ k=g_{1\bar1,k}+O(\lambda )$, while converting the second covariant derivative to partial derivatives introduces an error of at most $C\sum _ p|g_{1\bar p,k}|+C\lambda $. We also convert the off-diagonal covariant derivatives in the positive squares, with errors $O(\lambda )$, and absorb the linear off-diagonal terms by Young's inequality. Thus, after decreasing a fixed positive coefficient,

E4B294710238 14.14.3
\begin{equation} \widetilde\lambda _{k\bar k} \geq g_{1\bar1,k\bar k} +\frac1{8n\lambda }\sum _{p>1} (|g_{1\bar p,k}|^2+|g_{p\bar1,k}|^2) -C(|g_{1\bar1,k}|+\lambda ). \end{equation}

The precise positive constant will not be used.

Write $V_ k=\partial _ k(|\partial v|_\gamma ^2)$. The first derivative of $\widetilde H$ gives

1E20E0CF9A10 14.14.4
\begin{equation} \frac{a_ k}{\lambda }=-\phi ' V_ k-\psi 'v_ k, \qquad |V_ k|\leq C\sqrt K\sum _ p(|v_{pk}|+|v_{p\bar k}|)+CK. \end{equation}

In the second derivative, the derivatives of the fixed metric in $|\partial v|_\gamma ^2$ cost only a small fraction of the positive Hessian squares and a constant. Therefore

9.36
\begin{align} \widetilde H_{k\bar k}\geq {}& \frac{\widetilde\lambda _{k\bar k}}\lambda -\frac{|a_ k|^2}{\lambda ^2} +\phi '\sum _ p(v_ pv_{\bar p k\bar k}+v_{\bar p}v_{pk\bar k}) +\frac1{5K}\sum _ p(|v_{pk}|^2+|v_{p\bar k}|^2)\notag \\ & +\phi ”|V_ k|^2+\psi ”|v_ k|^2+\psi 'v_{k\bar k}-C. \tag{14.14.5} \end{align}

The third and fourth terms are obtained by differentiating $\sum \gamma ^{p\bar q}v_ pv_{\bar q}$ twice and applying $ab\leq \varepsilon a^2+C_\varepsilon b^2$ to each term containing a derivative of the metric.

14.14.6 The third derivatives and the torsion term.D5E11D27FAAA

We now estimate the third derivatives in this inequality. Define

\begin{gather*} E=\sum _{k,p} f_ k(|v_{pk}|^2+|v_{p\bar k}|^2),\qquad S=\sum _{k,p} f_ k(|v_{pk}|+|v_{p\bar k}|),\\ \mathcal C=-D^2\Phi _ g(\nabla _1g,(\nabla _1g)^*)\geq 0. \end{gather*}

The first differentiated equation is

BE0A6E37B652 14.14.7
\begin{equation} \sum _ k f_ k\nabla _ p g_{k\bar k}=h_ p. \end{equation}

Since $P_\gamma (W)=Z$ and the connection preserves $\gamma $, one has the exact contractions

5D951DE4F0FD 14.14.8
\begin{equation} \sum _ k f_ k\nabla _ p W_{k\bar k}=\sum _ i t_ i\nabla _ p Z_{i\bar i}, \qquad \sum _ k f_ k\nabla _{\bar1}\nabla _1W_{k\bar k} =\sum _ i t_ i\nabla _{\bar1}\nabla _1Z_{i\bar i}. \end{equation}

The weights in these identities are evaluated at the point; they are not differentiated.

The large weights are controlled as follows. By (14.13.6), the only weight $t_ i$ not bounded by $(n-1)f_1$ is $t_1$. In $Z_{1\bar1}$, every gradient index differs from $1$, by (14.13.4). Thus a pure second derivative in $\nabla _ pZ_{1\bar1}$ has an index $q>1$, and it can be weighted by $f_ q$, which is comparable to $\mathcal F$; here we use that pure derivatives commute in these coordinates. A mixed second derivative can similarly be placed in column $q$ by conjugate symmetry. All other $t_ i$ are bounded by a constant times every $f_ k$, and derivatives of coefficients cost $C\sqrt K\mathcal F$. Applying these observations to the first identity in (14.14.8), and then to (14.14.7), we obtain, for each $p$,

EACA66A12E82 14.14.9
\begin{equation} \left|\sum _ k f_ kv_{k\bar k p}\right| +\left|\sum _ k f_ kv_{k\bar k\bar p}\right| \leq C(S+\sqrt K\mathcal F). \end{equation}

The connection terms in the differentiated equation contribute weighted mixed second derivatives and terms bounded by $C\sqrt K\mathcal F$, which are already on the right. The bounded derivatives of $h$ are absorbed using the positive lower bound for $\mathcal F$ in lemma 14.13.5.

Next we insert the second derivative of the equation into the eigenvalue estimate. Since partial derivatives of $v$ commute,

\[ g_{1\bar1,k\bar k} =g_{k\bar k,1\bar1}+W_{1\bar1,k\bar k} -W_{k\bar k,1\bar1}+O(1). \]

Differentiating the equation twice covariantly gives

\[ \sum _ k f_ k\nabla _{\bar1}\nabla _1g_{k\bar k} =h_{1\bar1}+\mathcal C. \]

The conversion to partial derivatives uses

\[ \nabla _{\bar1}\nabla _1g_{k\bar k} =g_{k\bar k,1\bar1} -2\operatorname {Re}\sum _ q\overline{\Gamma ^ q_{1k}}g_{k\bar q,1} +O(\lambda ). \]

We rewrite the derivative of $g$ in this term using $g_{k\bar q,1}=g_{1\bar q,k}+W_{k\bar q,1}-W_{1\bar q,k}+O(1)$ and its conjugate. The first part is absorbed by the off-diagonal squares in (14.14.3), at the cost of $C\lambda \mathcal F+C\sum _ k f_ k|g_{1\bar1,k}|$. The part involving $W_{1\bar q,k}$ is bounded by $C\lambda \mathcal F+C\sum _{k,p}f_ k|v_{pk}|$. For the remaining part we use

\[ 2\operatorname {Re}\sum _ q\overline{\Gamma ^ q_{1k}}W_{k\bar q,1} =W_{k\bar k,1\bar1} -\nabla _{\bar1}\nabla _1W_{k\bar k}+O(\sqrt K). \]

This cancels the partial second derivative $-W_{k\bar k,1\bar1}$ above, and we obtain

9.40
\begin{align} \sum _ k f_ k\widetilde\lambda _{k\bar k} \geq {}& \mathcal C+\sum _ k f_ k (W_{1\bar1,k\bar k}-\nabla _{\bar1}\nabla _1W_{k\bar k})\notag \\ & -C\left(\sum _ k f_ k|g_{1\bar1,k}| +\sum _{k,p}f_ k|v_{pk}|+\lambda \mathcal F\right). \tag{14.14.10} \end{align}

The second contraction in (14.14.8) bounds the negative torsion term here. In the part with weight $t_1$, the possible third derivatives are $v_{q1\bar1}$ and their conjugates with $q>1$; all other parts have weights bounded by $Cf_1$. We commute partial derivatives and use

\[ v_{1\bar1,k}=g_{1\bar1,k}-\chi _{1\bar1,k}-W_{1\bar1,k}. \]

The exclusions in lemma 14.13.2 ensure that any remaining pure derivative $v_{11}$ carries only the small weight $Cf_1$, and derivatives of the connection contribute second derivatives with the same weighted bounds. It follows that

\[ \left|\sum _ k f_ k\nabla _{\bar1}\nabla _1W_{k\bar k}\right| \leq C\left(\sum _ k f_ k|g_{1\bar1,k}| +\sum _{k,p}f_ k|v_{pk}|+\lambda \mathcal F\right). \]

For the positive torsion term, write $W_{1\bar1}=\sum _ p(c^ pv_ p+\overline{c^ p}v_{\bar p})$ with fixed smooth coefficients $c^ p$. Two derivatives give $c^ p\sum _ k f_ kv_{pk\bar k}$ and its conjugate, plus terms bounded by $C(S+\sqrt K\mathcal F)$. The former are bounded by (14.14.9), and in $S$ the mixed second derivatives can be bounded by $C\lambda $, giving

\[ \left|\sum _ k f_ k W_{1\bar1,k\bar k}\right| \leq C\left(\sum _{k,p}f_ k|v_{pk}|+\lambda \mathcal F\right). \]

Substitute these estimates in (14.14.10), multiply (14.14.5) by $f_ k$ and sum over $k$. The third derivatives in the latter are bounded by (14.14.9) and $\phi '|\partial v|\leq C/\sqrt K$, and the resulting linear terms in the second derivatives are absorbed by a small part of $E/K$, at the cost of $C\mathcal F$. Since $\widetilde H$ has a local maximum, $\sum _ kf_ k\widetilde H_{k\bar k}\leq 0$, and thus

35CF4803162E 14.14.11
\begin{equation} \begin{split} 0\geq {}& \frac{\mathcal C}{\lambda } -\sum _ k f_ k\frac{|a_ k|^2}{\lambda ^2} +\frac{E}{6K}+\phi ''\sum _ k f_ k|V_ k|^2 +\psi ''\sum _ k f_ k|v_ k|^2+\psi '\sum _ k f_ kv_{k\bar k}\\ & -C\left(\sum _ k f_ k\frac{|g_{1\bar1,k}|}{\lambda }+\mathcal F\right). \end{split} \end{equation}

14.14.12 Cancellation when the smallest eigenvalue is controlled.DA6F05F8F203

Fix $0<\delta <1/2$, to be chosen after $D_1,D_2$. First suppose that $\lambda _ n\geq -\delta \lambda $. Put $I=\{ k:f_ k>\delta ^{-1}f_1\} $, so that $1\notin I$. Equation (14.14.4) and $\phi ''=2(\phi ')^2$ give

\begin{align*} -\sum _{k\notin I}f_ k|a_ k|^2/\lambda ^2 & \geq -\phi ''\sum _{k\notin I}f_ k|V_ k|^2 -2(\psi ')^2\delta ^{-1}f_1K,\\ -2\delta \sum _{k\in I}f_ k|a_ k|^2/\lambda ^2 & \geq -2\delta \phi ”\sum _{k\in I}f_ k|V_ k|^2 -4\delta (\psi ')^2\sum _{k\in I}f_ k|v_ k|^2. \end{align*}

Choose $\delta $ small enough that $4\delta (\psi ')^2\leq \psi ''/2$ throughout $[-M,0]$. By (14.13.8),

\[ \frac{\mathcal C}{\lambda } \geq \sum _{k\in I}\frac{f_ k-f_1}{\lambda (\lambda -\lambda _ k)} |\nabla _1g_{k\bar1}|^2 \geq (1-2\delta )\sum _{k\in I} \frac{f_ k|\nabla _1g_{k\bar1}|^2}{\lambda ^2}. \]

Here we used $(1-\delta )/(1+\delta )\geq 1-2\delta $.

We need the following comparison of the two derivatives $\nabla _1g_{k\bar1}$ and $a_ k=\nabla _ kg_{1\bar1}$. For every $\varepsilon >0$, provided $\lambda /K$ is sufficiently large depending on the fixed function $\psi $,

7B27AFD05F7E 14.14.13
\begin{equation} \sum _{k\in I}f_ k\frac{|\nabla _1g_{k\bar1}|^2-|a_ k|^2}{\lambda ^2} \geq -\frac{E}{12K}-C\mathcal F -\varepsilon C|\psi '|\mathcal F -C_\varepsilon |\psi '|\sum _ k f_ k|v_ k|^2. \end{equation}

To prove this, let $U=\sum _{p>1,q}|v_{pq}|$. Commuting partial derivatives in $g$ gives, for $k>1$,

\[ \nabla _1g_{k\bar1}-a_ k =W_{k\bar1,1}-W_{1\bar1,k}+O(\lambda ),\qquad |W_{k\bar1,1}|+|W_{1\bar1,k}|\leq C(\lambda +U). \]

The second inequality uses the absence of $v_1$ in $Z_{k\bar1}$, so that differentiation in the direction $1$ introduces no pure derivative $v_{11}$. All mixed second derivatives are bounded by $C\lambda $, and every other pure second derivative occurs in $U$.

Use $|a+e|^2-|a|^2\geq -2|a||e|$, and then (14.14.4). For $k>1$, the pure second derivatives in $V_ k$ all occur in $U$. With $H_ k=\sum _ p|v_{p\bar k}|$, the resulting negative terms, divided by $\lambda ^2$, are bounded by a constant times

\[ K^{-1/2}H_ k+K^{-1/2}U +\frac{U^2}{\lambda \sqrt K}+1 +|\psi '||v_ k|+\frac{|\psi '|}{\lambda }|v_ k|U. \]

Here we used $H_ k\leq C\lambda $ and $\lambda \geq K$ to simplify the cross terms. The weight comparison (14.13.6), the symmetry of pure derivatives and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give $\mathcal F U^2\leq C E$. Consequently, after multiplication by $f_ k$ and summation, the first two terms are bounded by any fixed small fraction of $E/K$ plus $C\mathcal F$. The third is at most $CE/(\lambda \sqrt K)$, which is absorbed by another such fraction when $\lambda /K$ is large. For the last two terms we use

\[ |v_ k|\leq \varepsilon +C_\varepsilon |v_ k|^2,\qquad |v_ k|U\leq |v_ k|^2+U^2. \]

The contribution of $U^2$ is at most $C|\psi '|E/\lambda $, which can again be absorbed when $\lambda /K$ is large. Choosing the fractions with sum $1/12$ proves (14.14.13).

Insert this cancellation and the preceding inequalities into (14.14.11). The remaining derivative error satisfies, by (14.14.4),

\[ C\sum _ k f_ k|g_{1\bar1,k}|/\lambda \leq \frac{E}{60K}+C\mathcal F +\varepsilon C|\psi '|\mathcal F +C_\varepsilon |\psi '|\sum _ k f_ k|v_ k|^2, \]

after Young's inequality and an adjustment of constants. The torsion contraction also satisfies

\[ \left|\sum _ k f_ kW_{k\bar k}\right| =\left|\sum _ i t_ iZ_{i\bar i}\right| \leq C\sum _ k f_ k|v_ k| \leq \varepsilon C\mathcal F+C_\varepsilon \sum _ k f_ k|v_ k|^2. \]

The first inequality uses again the missing gradient index in $Z_{1\bar1}$. Since $\psi '<0$ and $v_{k\bar k}=\lambda _ k-\chi _{k\bar k}-W_{k\bar k}$, we conclude, after decreasing the remaining coefficient of $E$ if necessary, that

9CFA88376131 14.14.14
\begin{equation} \begin{split} 0\geq {}& f_1\left(\frac{\lambda ^2}{40K} -2(\psi ')^2\delta ^{-1}K\right) +\left(\frac{\psi ''}{2}-C_\varepsilon |\psi '|\right) \sum _ k f_ k|v_ k|^2\\ & -C_0\mathcal F-\varepsilon C_0|\psi '|\mathcal F +|\psi '|\sum _ k f_ k(\chi _{k\bar k}-\lambda _ k). \end{split} \end{equation}

For the first term we used $|v_{1\bar1}|^2\geq \lambda ^2/2-CK$, which follows from $\chi +W=O(\sqrt K)$, and $E\geq f_1|v_{1\bar1}|^2$.

For large $\lambda $, lemma 14.13.5 bounds the last sum below by $\tau \mathcal F/2$. Choose first $\varepsilon $ with $\varepsilon C_0<\tau /4$. Next choose $D_2$ so that $\psi ''/2>C_\varepsilon |\psi '|$, and then choose $D_1$ so that $|\psi '|\tau /4>C_0$ on $[-M,0]$. Finally choose $\delta $ as above. All these choices depend only on the fixed data. Inequality (14.14.14) then forces $\lambda ^2\leq 80\, \delta ^{-1}\sup _{[-M,0]}|\psi '|^2K^2$, which proves the desired bound in the first case.

14.14.15 The remaining eigenvalue configuration.57C7A82680F3

Suppose now that $\lambda _ n<-\delta \lambda $, keeping the same constants. Since $f_ n\geq \mathcal F/n$ and $|\chi +W|\leq C\sqrt K$,

\[ \frac{E}{6K}\geq \frac{f_ n}{6K}|v_{n\bar n}|^2 \geq \frac{\delta ^2\lambda ^2}{12nK}\mathcal F-C\mathcal F. \]

In (14.14.11), discard $\mathcal C$ and the term with $\psi ''$. Since $\psi $ is now fixed, $|\psi '\sum _ k f_ kv_{k\bar k}|\leq C\lambda \mathcal F$. Using $g_{1\bar1,k}=a_ k+O(\lambda )$ and Young's inequality, we bound the last error term by $\frac12\sum _ k f_ k|a_ k|^2/\lambda ^2+C\mathcal F$. This leaves

\[ 0\geq -\tfrac 32\sum _ k f_ k|a_ k|^2/\lambda ^2 +\frac{\delta ^2\lambda ^2}{12nK}\mathcal F +\phi ''\sum _ k f_ k|V_ k|^2-C\lambda \mathcal F. \]

By (14.14.4) and the scalar inequality $\tfrac 32|a+b|^2\leq 2|a|^2+6|b|^2$,

\[ \tfrac 32\sum _ k f_ k|a_ k|^2/\lambda ^2 \leq \phi ''\sum _ k f_ k|V_ k|^2+CK\mathcal F. \]

Since $\lambda \geq K$, combining the last two inequalities and dividing by $\lambda \mathcal F>0$ gives $\lambda /K\leq C$. This finishes both cases. Since $H$ attains its maximum at the chosen point, the bound for $\lambda _1/K$ there gives a bound at all points, and $|\lambda _ i|\leq (n-1)\lambda _1$ then bounds every entry of the complex Hessian, as asserted.
