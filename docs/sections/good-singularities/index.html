18.1 I-good singularitiesA058A93BF381

A058A93BF381

The class of $\mathcal I$-good singularities is the central object of this part of the book. These are the singularities for which the pluripotential-theoretic and multiplier-ideal notions of singularity type agree. Equivalently, they are the positive-mass potentials for which the ordinary model envelope and the $\mathcal I$-model envelope coincide, or for which the non-pluripolar mass equals the volume defined through multiplier ideals.

The starting point is the characterization theorem theorem 18.1.2. For $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, it shows that the following three conditions are equivalent: $\varphi $ lies in the $d_ S$-closure of potentials with analytic singularities; the mass-volume identity

\[ \int _ X\theta _\varphi ^ n=\operatorname{vol}\theta _\varphi \]

holds; and

\[ P_\theta [\varphi ]=P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

After establishing this characterization, the chapter develops the basic calculus of $\mathcal I$-good singularities. We prove stability under natural operations, including linear combinations and suprema, and record the main examples: analytic singularities, $\mathcal I$-model potentials, elementary metrics, and toric potentials. These closure properties are used repeatedly in later chapters, especially for traces, test curves and non-Archimedean potentials.

The next part of the chapter introduces mixed volumes for singular currents. The definition is first made in positive-volume classes by replacing each current with its $\mathcal I$-model envelope, and is then extended by Kähler perturbation. We prove symmetry, multilinearity, monotonicity, birational invariance, log-concavity and continuity with respect to $d_ S$-convergence.

The final section proves the asymptotic Riemann–Roch formula theorem 18.1.46. For a Hermitian pseudo-effective line bundle $(L,h)$ on a connected compact Kähler manifold $X$ of dimension $n$, the theorem identifies

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n} h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal I(h^ k)\right) \]

with the volume of the Chern current of $h$. This result is the main algebraic manifestation of $\mathcal I$-goodness. It will be used later in the construction of partial Okounkov bodies and in the study of partial Bergman kernels.

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0. \]

We may therefore assume that $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n>0$ for all $j \geq 1$. It follows from proposition 16.6.22 that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ j} \]

for any $j\geq 1$. Using theorem 16.9.57, we conclude (18.1.3).

2 $\iff $ 3. By definition,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }= \int _ X\theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}}^ n. \]

Moreover, $\varphi -\sup _ X\varphi \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$. Thus, if 2 holds, theorem 16.3.15 gives $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$; the reverse inequality follows from proposition 16.6.18. Conversely, if 3 holds, then 2 follows from proposition 16.3.10.

3 $\implies $ 1. Note that the condition in 1 characterizes the closure of analytic singularities in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Step 1. We first assume that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. We will prove the following more general result in this case: Without assuming 3, $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ always lies in the closure of analytic singularities.

Let $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ be a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We will show that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$. Let

\[ \psi =\inf _{j\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]. \]

Since $\varphi \leq \varphi _ j$ for all $j>0$, we have

\[ \varphi -\sup _ X\varphi \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j] \]

for all $j$, and hence

\[ \varphi -\sup _ X\varphi \leq \psi . \]

In particular, $\int _ X\theta _\psi ^ n>0$.

Now proposition 16.9.6, proposition 16.4.4 and corollary 16.9.14 give

\[ \varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi . \]

Moreover, observe that $\psi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-model by proposition 16.6.29 and proposition 16.6.22. Hence, if $\varphi \sim _{\mathcal I}\psi $, then

\[ P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}=P_\theta [\psi ]_{\mathcal I}=\psi . \]

So it suffices to show that $\varphi \sim _{\mathcal I}\psi $. It only remains to argue that $\psi \preceq _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi $. For this purpose, take $\lambda >0$. We need to show that

\[ \mathcal{I}(\lambda \psi )\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi ). \]

By the strong openness theorem 5.16.21, we may take $\lambda '>\lambda $ such that $\mathcal{I}(\lambda \psi )=\mathcal{I}(\lambda ' \psi )$. It follows from the definition of the quasi-equisingular approximation that

\[ \mathcal{I}(\lambda '\psi )\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda '\varphi _ j)\subseteq \mathcal{I}(\lambda \varphi ) \]

for large enough $j$, where the first inclusion uses $\psi \leq P_\theta [\varphi _ j]$ and $P_\theta [\varphi _ j]\sim _{\mathcal{I}}\varphi _ j$. Our assertion follows.

It follows from the proof that we may take $\varphi _ j$ so that $\theta _{\varphi _ j}$ is a Kähler current for all $j\geq 1$.

Step 2. We handle the general case.

Assume 3 holds. By 13.13.47, we can find $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ so that $\theta _{\psi }$ is a Kähler current and $\psi \leq \varphi $. We let

\[ \psi _ j=(1-j^{-1})\varphi +j^{-1}\psi \]

for each $j\in \mathbb {Z}_{>1}$. Then $(\psi _ j)_ j$ is an increasing sequence converging almost everywhere to $\varphi $. Then

\[ P_{\theta }[\psi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\varphi ] \]

by proposition 16.6.30, corollary 16.9.12. From Step 1, we know that each $P_{\theta }[\psi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}$ lies in the closure of analytic singularities, hence so is $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\sim _ P \varphi $. Therefore, 1 follows. $\square$

We show that $\mathcal{I}$-goodness is preserved by a number of natural operations.

  1. DF6905DB50A6

    $\varphi +\psi $;

  2. 0771DD6805B8

    $\varphi \lor \psi $;

  3. 51229C9B0C0E

    $\lambda \varphi $.

Proof. Take a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$ such that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. It follows from theorem 18.1.2 that there are sequences $(\varphi _ j)_ j,(\psi _ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with analytic singularities such that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $ and $\psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi $.

By theorem 16.9.45, proposition 16.9.42, we have

\[ \varphi _ j+\psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi +\psi ,\quad \varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi \lor \psi . \]

By proposition 5.18.2, the potentials $\varphi _ j+\psi _ j$ and $\varphi _ j\lor \psi _ j$ have analytic singularities. So $\varphi +\psi $ and $\varphi \lor \psi $ are $\mathcal{I}$-good by theorem 18.1.2.

On the other hand, 16.9.41 gives

\[ \lambda \varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\lambda \varphi . \]

Here the convergence is understood in the scaled class. So by theorem 18.1.2, in order to show that $\lambda \varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-good, it suffices to show that $\lambda \varphi _ j$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good. In other words, we may assume that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities. We may further assume that $\varphi \leq 0$.

In this case, take a decreasing sequence of rational numbers $\lambda _ j\searrow \lambda $. Take a sufficiently large Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$ so that $\lambda _ j\varphi ,\lambda \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )_{>0}$ for any $j$. Then observe that

\[ \lambda \varphi =\operatorname *{sup}_{j>0}{}\! {}^{*} \lambda _ j \varphi . \]

Therefore, $\lambda _ j\varphi \xrightarrow {d_ S} \lambda \varphi $ by corollary 16.9.12. Each $\lambda _ j \varphi $ has analytic singularities, and hence is $\mathcal{I}$-good by example 18.1.10. Therefore, $\lambda \varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-good by theorem 18.1.2. $\square$

\[ \psi _ i=\inf _{j\in J}P_{\theta _ i}\left[\psi _ i^ j\right]. \]

In particular, $\psi _ i\geq \varphi _ i$, so $\int _ X\theta _{i,\psi _ i}^ n>0$. Since $\psi _ i^ j$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good, $P_{\theta _ i}[\psi _ i^ j]$ is both model and $\mathcal{I}$-model. Due to proposition 16.6.29, $\psi _ i$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model. Thanks to proposition 16.4.4, we know

\[ \nu (\psi _ i,E)=\nu (\varphi _ i,E) \]

for any $i=1,\ldots ,n$ and any prime divisor $E$ over $X$. In other words, $\psi _ i\sim _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi _ i$ for $i=1,\ldots ,n$. But both $\varphi _ i$ and $\psi _ i$ are $\mathcal{I}$-good, therefore,

\[ \psi _ i\sim _{P} \varphi _ i,\quad i=1,\ldots ,n. \]

By proposition 16.8.22, we have

\[ \int _ X (\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n)=\int _ X (\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n). \]

Next by proposition 16.8.22 again,

\[ \begin{split} & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right)\\ \leq & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1^ j\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n^ j \right). \end{split} \]

On the other hand, due to proposition 16.4.4 and corollary 16.9.14, for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$, we have

\[ \psi _ i^ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi _ i. \]

We conclude from theorem 16.9.30 that

\[ \varlimsup _{j\in J} \int _ X \left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1^ j\right)\wedge \dots \wedge \left(\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n^ j \right)=\int _ X (\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ n). \]

Putting these equations together, (18.1.43) follows.

Step 2. Next we handle the general case.

Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. For any $\epsilon \in (0,1]$, from Step 1, we know that

\[ \begin{split} & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right) \\ \leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right). \end{split} \]

Using 18.1.31, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j, \dots ,\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right)\\ \leq & \varlimsup _{j\in J} \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1^ j, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n^ j \right)\\ \leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1, \dots ,\theta _ n+\epsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right)\\ \leq & \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1, \dots ,\theta _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ n\right)+C\epsilon . \end{aligned} \]

But since $\epsilon $ is arbitrary, our assertion follows. $\square$

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$.

Definition 18.1.45.4B65B5AA614D A Hermitian pseudo-effective line bundle $(L,h)$ on a complex manifold $Y$ consists of a holomorphic line bundle $L$ on $Y$ together with a plurisubharmonic metric $h$ on $L$.

Theorem 18.1.46 (Asymptotic Riemann–Roch formula).FEA63C5AE11F Let $(L,h)$ be a Hermitian pseudo-effective line bundle on $X$ and $T$ be a holomorphic line bundle on $X$. We have

\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(h^ k)\right)=\operatorname{vol}\left(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\right). \end{equation}

In particular, the limit exists.

For the proof, let us fix a smooth Hermitian metric $h_0$ on $L$ with $\theta =c_1(L,h_0)$. We identify $h$ with $h_0\exp (-\varphi )$ for some $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. See section 5.20 for the relevant notations.

Recall that when $X$ admits a big line bundle, it is necessarily projective. See [Theorem 2.2.26, MM07].

We first handle the case where $\varphi $ has analytic singularities.

Proposition 18.1.48.A51A6DE92117 Under the assumptions of theorem 18.1.46, assume furthermore that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities such that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current, then (18.1.47) holds.

Proof. Step 1. Reduce to the case of log singularities.

Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a log resolution of $\varphi $. In this case, for each $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we have

\[ h^0\Bigl (X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\Bigr )=h^0\Bigl (Y,K_{Y/X}\otimes \pi ^*T\otimes \pi ^*L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\pi ^*\varphi )\Bigr ). \]

By proposition 16.6.10, we have

\[ \operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)=\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\pi ^*h). \]

Therefore, it suffices to argue (18.1.47) with $K_{Y/X}\otimes \pi ^*T$, $\pi ^*L$ and $\pi ^*h$ in place of $T$, $L$ and $h$.

Note that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is no longer a Kähler current, but it still has positive mass.

Step 2. Assume that $\varphi $ has log singularities along an effective $\mathbb {Q}$-divisor $D$. We may assume that $D$ is simple normal crossing by replacing $X$ by a modification and $D$ by the pull-back. We decompose $D$ into irreducible components, say

\[ D=\sum _{i=1}^ N a_ iD_ i. \]

In this case, we can easily compute

\[ \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )=\mathcal{O}_ X\left(-\sum _{i=1}^ N\lfloor k a_ i \rfloor D_ i\right) \]

for each $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. Observe that $L-D$ is nef (see 5.18.5). Choose an integer $q>0$ so that $qD$ is integral. For each residue class $s=0,\ldots ,q-1$, we have

\[ \left\lfloor (mq+s)D\right\rfloor =mqD+\lfloor sD\rfloor . \]

Hence, on the residue class $k=mq+s$, the line bundle

\[ T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{O}_ X\left(-\sum _{i=1}^ N\lfloor k a_ i \rfloor D_ i\right) \]

is a fixed twist of $\left(q(L-D)\right)^ m$. Applying the asymptotic Riemann–Roch theorem [Corollary 1.4.41, PAG1] to the nef line bundle $q(L-D)$ and then letting $m\to \infty $ in each residue class, we conclude that

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{O}_ X\left(-\sum _{i=1}^ N\lfloor k a_ i \rfloor D_ i\right)\right)= (L-D)^ n. \]

Observe that by proposition 5.20.4,

\[ \theta _{\varphi }=[D]+S, \]

where $S$ is a closed positive $(1,1)$-current with bounded potential. Therefore,

\[ (L-D)^ n=\int _ X S^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

By example 18.1.10, we know that the right-hand side is exactly $\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }$. $\square$

Proof of theorem 18.1.46. Step 1. We first handle the case where $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. Fix a Kähler form $\omega \geq \theta $ on $X$ such that $\theta _{\varphi }\geq 2\delta \omega $ for some $\delta \in (0,1)$.

Let $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ be a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We may assume that $\theta _{\varphi _ j}\geq \delta \omega $ for all $j$. From proposition 18.1.48, we know that for each $j\geq 1$,

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\leq \lim _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi _ j)\right)=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ j}. \]

It follows from theorem 18.1.2 and theorem 16.9.57 that the right-hand side converges to $\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }$ as $j\to \infty $. Therefore,

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\leq \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \]

Conversely, fix an integer $N>\delta ^{-1}$. From theorem 18.1.2 and theorem 16.9.30, we know that

\begin{equation} \lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}}^ n>0. \end{equation}

Therefore, by 13.13.30, we can find $j_0>0$ such that for $j\geq j_0$, there is $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ (depending on $j$) with

\begin{equation} (1-N^{-1})\varphi _ j+N^{-1}\psi \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \end{equation}

For each $k>0$, we write $k=k'N-r$, where $k'\in \mathbb {N}$ and $r\in \{ 0,1,\ldots ,N-1\} $. Then we compute for $j\geq j_0$ and large enough $k$ (to be specified shortly) that

\[ \begin{aligned} & h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{k}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\\ \geq & h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{-r}\otimes L^{k'N}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k'N\varphi )\right)\\ \geq & h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{-r}\otimes L^{k'N}\otimes \mathcal{I}\left(k'(\psi +(N-1)\varphi _ j)\right)\right)\\ \geq & h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{-r}\otimes L^{k'(N-1)}\otimes \mathcal{I}\left(k'N\varphi _ j\right)\right), \end{aligned} \]

where the third line follows from (18.1.50), the fourth line can be argued as follows: For large enough $k$, there is a non-zero section $s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^{k'}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k'\psi ))$ by 13.13.48. It follows from 5.18.15 that for large enough $k$,

\[ \mathcal{I}\left(k'N\varphi _ j\right)\subseteq \mathcal{I}_{\infty }\left(k'(N-1)\varphi _ j\right). \]

It follows that multiplication by $s$ gives an injective map

\[ \begin{split} \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{-r}\otimes L^{k'(N-1)}\otimes \mathcal{I}\left(k'N\varphi _ j\right)\right)\hookrightarrow \\ \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{-r}\otimes L^{k'N}\otimes \mathcal{I}\left(k'\psi +k'(N-1)\varphi _ j\right)\right). \end{split} \]

Next observe that

\[ (N-1)\theta +N\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j= N\theta _{\varphi _ j}-\theta \geq N\delta \omega -\theta \geq (N\delta -1)\omega . \]

Since $N>\delta ^{-1}$, this is a Kähler current. Hence proposition 18.1.48 is applicable. We let $k\to \infty $ to conclude that

\[ \begin{aligned} \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{k}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right) \geq & N^{-n}\int _{X} \left( (N-1)\theta +N\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j\right)^ n\\ =& \int _ X \left((1-N^{-1})\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j \right)^ n\\ \geq & \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j \right)^ n -C N^{-1}, \end{aligned} \]

where $C$ is a constant independent of $N$ and $j$. Letting $j\to \infty $ and then $N\to \infty $ and using (18.1.49), we find that

\[ \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^{k}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\geq \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}}^ n. \]

Therefore, (18.1.47) follows.

Step 2. We handle the case where $\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }>0$. We may assume that $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-model.

First observe that $L$ is big by proposition 13.13.3. Hence $X$ is projective. Take a very ample line bundle $A$ on $X$ and a Kähler form $\omega $ in $c_1(A)$. Take a Hermitian metric $h_ A$ on $A$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ A=\omega $.

\[ \begin{aligned} & \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\\ \leq & \max _{r=0,\ldots ,N-1}\varlimsup _{k'\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k'^ nN^ n} h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ r \otimes L^{k'N}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k'N\varphi )\right)\\ \leq & \max _{r=0,\ldots ,N-1}\varlimsup _{k'\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k'^ nN^ n} h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ r \otimes L^{k'N}\otimes A^{k'}\otimes \mathcal{I}(k'N\varphi )\right)\\ =& \int _ X \left(N^{-1}\omega +\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\theta +N^{-1}\omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)^{n}, \end{aligned} \]

where the third line uses multiplication by a non-zero section of $A^{k'}$, and we have applied Step 1 to the Hermitian pseudo-effective line bundle $(L^ N\otimes A,h^{\otimes N}\otimes h_ A)$ on the fourth line. On the other hand, since $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-good by example 18.1.11, 18.1.8 gives

\[ P_{\theta +N^{-1}\omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta +N^{-1}\omega }[\varphi ]. \]

It follows from proposition 16.3.10 that

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\leq \int _ X \left(N^{-1}\omega +\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \right)^ n. \]

Letting $N\to \infty $, we conclude

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

Choose $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\theta _{\psi }$ is a Kähler current and $\psi \leq \varphi $. The existence of $\psi $ is guaranteed by 13.13.47. Then for any $t\in (0,1)$, we set

\[ \varphi _ t=(1-t)\varphi +t\psi . \]

It follows again from Step 1 that

\[ \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\geq \lim _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi _ t)\right)=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ t}. \]

On the other hand, by theorem 18.1.32 and corollary 16.9.38,

\[ \lim _{t\to 0+}\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ t}=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \]

So we find

\[ \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\geq \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \]

We conclude (18.1.47) in this case.

Assume that (18.1.47) fails. That is,

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\right)\geq \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } \frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)>0, \]
