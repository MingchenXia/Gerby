11.26 Conformal changes and directional curvature positivityA4E01A8B6F30

A4E01A8B6F30

The compatibility condition of proposition 11.23.2 decides which right-hand sides a scalar trace equation can have, and it can be used to improve the curvature of a Hermitian metric on a vector bundle. A conformal change $h\mapsto \mathrm e^{-tf}h$ adds the same form $t\, \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f$ to the curvature in every fiber direction. Solving a Poisson equation for a Gauduchon form, we can make the trace of this form positive outside any prescribed ball. If the original curvature already has a positive direction for every fiber vector over that ball, a small conformal change produces such a direction everywhere.

Throughout this section, $E\to X$ is a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank, and $n\geq 2$. For a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $E$, we use the Chern curvature $F_ h$, the Hermitian endomorphisms $R_ h(u,\bar u)$, the directional forms $\alpha _{h,v}$ and the mean curvature $K_{\omega ,h}$ of 11.18; recall that $h$ is linear in its first argument.

Definition 11.26.1.2C6FED9B1909 The metric $h$ is RC-positive at $x$ if, for every $0\ne v\in E_ x$, there is a tangent vector $u\in T_ x^{1,0}X$ such that

\[ h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)>0. \]

It is uniformly RC-positive at $x$ if one tangent vector $u$ works for every nonzero $v\in E_ x$. A metric has either property on a set if it has that property at every point of the set.

Lemma 11.26.2.23EB10AE428C Suppose that $K_{\omega ,h}(x)\geq 0$. Then $h$ is RC-positive at $x$ if and only if $\alpha _{h,v}\ne 0$ for every $0\ne v\in E_ x$.

Proof. The trace of $\alpha _{h,v}$ with respect to $\omega $ is $h(K_{\omega ,h}v,v)\geq 0$. A nonzero Hermitian form with nonnegative trace has a positive eigenvalue, since if all its eigenvalues were nonpositive, their nonnegative sum would force all of them to vanish. This gives the required tangent direction. Conversely, a tangent vector with positive evaluation shows that $\alpha _{h,v}\ne 0$. $\square$

Theorem 11.26.3.AFF6DB5A7BE1 Suppose that $K_{\omega ,h}\geq 0$ on $X$ and that $h$ is RC-positive on a nonempty open set $U$. Then there is a smooth real function $f$ such that $h_ t=\mathrm e^{-tf}h$ is RC-positive on all of $X$ for every sufficiently small $t>0$.

Proof. By theorem 11.4.2, choose a conformal Gauduchon form $\gamma =\mathrm e^ a\omega $. Then $K_{\gamma ,h}=\mathrm e^{-a}K_{\omega ,h}\geq 0$. Choose a closed coordinate ball $Q\subset U$ and a smooth function $\chi \geq 0$ supported in its interior, with $\int _ X\chi \gamma ^ n>0$, and set

\[ \rho =1-\frac{\int _ X\gamma ^ n}{\int _ X\chi \gamma ^ n}\chi . \]

Thus $\rho =1$ on $X\setminus Q$ and $\int _ X\rho \gamma ^ n=0$. Since $\gamma $ is Gauduchon, proposition 11.23.2, applied with $\gamma $ in place of $\omega $ and $u=0$, gives a smooth real solution $f$ of

78D702B6F891 11.26.4
\begin{equation} P_\gamma f:=\operatorname {tr}_\gamma (\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f)=\rho . \end{equation}

Indeed, with $L_\gamma f=n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}/\gamma ^ n$ as in (11.23.1), we have $P_\gamma =2\pi L_\gamma $, and the adjoint probability of $L_\gamma $ is $\gamma ^ n/\int _ X\gamma ^ n$.

The curvature transforms by

0F0F9E5CC398 11.26.5
\begin{equation} F_{h_ t}=F_ h+t\partial \bar\partial f\, \operatorname {Id}_ E, \qquad \alpha _{h_ t,v}=\mathrm e^{-tf} \bigl (\alpha _{h,v}+t|v|_ h^2\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f\bigr ). \end{equation}

On $X\setminus Q$, the $\gamma $-trace of the expression in parentheses is at least $t|v|_ h^2\rho =t|v|_ h^2>0$ for $v\ne 0$, so it has a positive eigenvalue there.

Over $Q$, the unit sphere bundle $\{ |v|_ h=1\} $ is compact. The largest $\gamma $-eigenvalue of $\alpha _{h,v}$ is continuous and strictly positive on it, so it has a positive minimum $\delta $. Let $C$ be the maximum on $X$ of the operator norm of $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial f$ relative to $\gamma $. For every unit vector over $Q$, the largest eigenvalue of the expression in parentheses in (11.26.5) is at least $\delta -tC$, which is positive for small $t>0$. Homogeneity in $v$ and the positive factor $\mathrm e^{-tf}$ prove the assertion on $Q$ as well. $\square$

In this conclusion, the positive direction may depend on the fiber vector $v$. The distinction from uniform RC-positivity already occurs for a local metric with zero mean curvature.

Example 11.26.6.8C86B880454A On a sufficiently small ball in $\mathbb C^2$, equip the trivial bundle of rank two with the metric

\[ H(z)=\operatorname {Id}-zz^*+\tfrac 12|z|^2\operatorname {Id}. \]

At the origin this metric has zero mean curvature for the Euclidean Hermitian form and is RC-positive, but it is not uniformly RC-positive.

Proof. The two eigenvalues of $H(z)$ are $1-\tfrac 12|z|^2$ and $1+\tfrac 12|z|^2$, so the metric is positive on a small ball. Its first derivatives vanish at the origin, where

\[ F_{i\bar j}=e_ ie_ j^*-\tfrac 12\delta _{ij}\operatorname {Id},\qquad h(R_ h(u,\bar u)v,v)=|v^*u|^2-\tfrac 12|u|^2|v|^2. \]

The sum $F_{1\bar1}+F_{2\bar2}$ is zero. For each nonzero $v$, taking $u=v$ gives a positive evaluation. For each nonzero $u$, taking $v\perp u$ gives a negative evaluation, so no single $u$ works for every $v$. $\square$

Proposition 11.26.7.9EFFDFAE03AF If $\det E$ admits a flat Hermitian metric, then no smooth Hermitian metric on $E$ is uniformly RC-positive on $X$ or has positive definite mean curvature relative to a Hermitian form. This applies in particular when $\det E$ is holomorphically trivial.

Proof. If $h$ were uniformly RC-positive, then at each point some $u$ would make the $h$-Hermitian endomorphism $R_ h(u,\bar u)$ positive definite, and its trace would be strictly positive. Since the curvature of the induced metric on $\det E$ is $\operatorname {tr}F_ h$, the determinant metric would be RC-positive as a metric on a line bundle. If instead $K_{\omega ,h}>0$, the $\omega $-trace of the determinant curvature would be $\operatorname {tr}K_{\omega ,h}>0$, which again gives a positive tangent evaluation.

Fix a flat Hermitian metric $h_0$ on $\det E$ and write $\det h=\mathrm e^ b h_0$ for a smooth real function $b$. The determinant curvature is then $-\partial \bar\partial b$. At a global minimum of $b$, this curvature has no positive tangent evaluation, contradicting the RC-positivity of the determinant metric. Finally, a holomorphic trivialization of $\det E$ gives a flat metric, by assigning constant norm to the trivializing frame. $\square$

Consequently, if $\det E$ admits a flat Hermitian metric and the hypotheses of theorem 11.26.3 hold, the conformally changed metric is RC-positive, whereas $E$ admits neither a uniformly RC-positive metric nor a metric with positive definite mean curvature. The next section shows that the tangent bundle of a nonflat Ricci-flat Kähler surface has these properties, and the local example above shows the pointwise distinction.
