13.21 Monotone limitsA8EAC258D61B

A8EAC258D61B

We now show that ordinary envelopes and singularity volumes are continuous along monotone sequences whose limit has positive lower volume. For increasing sequences, contact support identifies the limit of the envelopes before its volume is known. For decreasing sequences of models, a uniform small stretch keeps a positive lower-volume bound. Normalized compactness then preserves the convex inequalities given by the deficit lemma, and strict barriers allow the background to decrease at the same time.

Theorem 13.21.1.453D4DA50352 Let $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be an increasing sequence such that $u=(\lim _ j u_ j)^*$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and has positive lower volume. Then

\[ \left(\lim _ jP_\theta [u_ j]\right)^*=P_\theta [u], \qquad P_\theta [u_ j]\longrightarrow P_\theta [u] \quad \text{in capacity}, \]

and

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u_ j)\nearrow \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u),\qquad \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u_ j)\nearrow \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u). \]

For every fixed smooth compact modification $\pi :Z\to X$,

\[ \sup _{z\in Z}\bigl |\nu (u_ j\circ \pi ,z)-\nu (u\circ \pi ,z)\bigr | \longrightarrow 0. \]

Proof. Step 1: the envelopes. Translate so that $u\leq 0$, and set $p_ j=P_\theta [u_ j]$, $p=P_\theta [u]$ and $s=(\lim _ jp_ j)^*$. The $p_ j$ increase, and $u\leq s\leq p\leq V_\theta $. By theorem 13.19.5, the lower volume of $p$ is positive and $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,p)$. Upward stability (proposition 16.28.62) puts $s$ in the same class.

The ordinary envelope can use $V_\theta $ in place of zero as obstacle (see the remark after definition 5.40.4); that is, $p_ j=P_\theta [u_ j](V_\theta )$. Hence lemma 13.19.4 gives

\[ \mu _{p_ j}\leq \mathbf1_{\{ p_ j=V_\theta \} }\mu _{V_\theta }. \]

The fixed bounded quasi-continuous weight $\chi =\min (1,(p-s)_+)$, defined arbitrarily on the pluripolar set where $p-s$ is undefined, vanishes on $\{ p_ j=V_\theta \} $, because $p_ j\leq s\leq p\leq V_\theta $. Since $p_ j\to s$ in capacity by lemma 5.29.19, lemma 13.14.8 gives $\int _ X\chi \, \mu _ s=0$. Thus $\mu _ s$ vanishes on $\{ s<p\} $, and theorem 13.19.1, with reference $p$ and coefficient $c=0$, yields $s=p$. This proves the assertions about the envelopes.

Step 2: the upper volumes of the envelopes. Choose any $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w\sim p$, and $K\geq 0$ with $p-K\leq w\leq p+K$. Define $r_ j=P_\theta [u_ j](w)$; this is meaningful because $u_ j-K\leq p-K\leq w$, so that $P_\theta (u_ j,w)\not\equiv -\infty $. For $C\geq 0$ the competitors compare as follows:

\[ P_\theta (u_ j+C+K,0)-K \leq P_\theta (u_ j+C,w)\leq p_ j+K. \]

The leftmost potential lies below $u_ j+C$ and below $p_ j-K\leq p-K\leq w$. For the upper bound, note that the middle term minus $K$ lies below $w-K\leq p\leq 0$ and is more singular than $u_ j$, so it is at most $p_ j$. Taking the increasing limit in $C$ gives

\[ p_ j-K\leq r_ j\leq p_ j+K,\qquad r_ j\leq w. \]

The $r_ j$ increase with $j$ and converge in capacity to $r=(\lim _ jr_ j)^*$, and $p-K\leq r\leq w\leq p+K$. Thus $r\sim p$, so $r\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,p)$. Lemma 13.19.4 gives $\mu _{r_ j}\leq \mathbf1_{\{ r_ j=w\} }\mu _ w$, and weighted lower semicontinuity with the weight $\min (1,(w-r)_+)$ shows that $\mu _ r$ vanishes on $\{ r<w\} $. Domination with reference $p$ gives $r=w$. Therefore

\[ \int _ X\mu _ w\leq \liminf _ j\int _ X\mu _{r_ j} \leq \lim _ j\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p_ j), \]

where $r_ j\sim p_ j$ justifies the last inequality. Taking the supremum over $w\sim p$ and using volume monotonicity proves

\[ \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p_ j)\nearrow \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p). \]

Step 3: the volumes of $u_ j$. Put $\delta =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)>0$ and $D_ j=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p)-\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p_ j)$. Since $D_ j\to 0$, we can choose $b_ j>1$ with $b_ j\to \infty $ and $b_ j^ nD_ j<\delta $ for all large $j$. With $a_ j=1-b_ j^{-1}$, lemma 13.18.1 gives $h_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

\[ a_ jp+(1-a_ j)h_ j\leq p_ j \]

for all sufficiently large $j$. Addition, homogeneity and monotonicity of the lower volume, together with theorem 13.19.5, give

\[ a_ j^ n\delta \leq \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (p_ j) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u_ j)\leq \delta . \]

Since $a_ j\to 1$, this proves the convergence of the lower volumes, and shows that $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u_ j)>0$ for all large $j$. For such $j$, theorem 13.19.5 identifies the upper volumes of $u_ j$ and $p_ j$, and also those of $u$ and $p$; Step 2 therefore gives the asserted upper-volume limit. Finally, proposition 13.18.3, applied to $u_ j\preceq u$, gives the uniform convergence of Lelong numbers on each fixed modification. $\square$

For the results on decreasing sequences, fix smooth real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta _ j$ with $\theta \leq \theta _{j+1}\leq \theta _ j$ that converge uniformly to $\theta $; the constant sequence $\theta _ j=\theta $ is allowed. The proofs first treat a fixed background, using normalized auxiliary potentials, and then handle the varying forms by means of corrected envelopes of tail suprema.

Theorem 13.21.2.BE27C58EE55B Suppose that $q_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ are ordinary models, $q_ j=P_{\theta _ j}[q_ j]$, decreasing to $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then $P_\theta [q]=q$ and

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j)\searrow \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q). \]

If $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0$, then also

\[ \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j)\searrow \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q), \]

and, for every fixed smooth compact modification $\pi :Z\to X$,

\[ \sup _{z\in Z}\bigl |\nu (q_ j\circ \pi ,z)-\nu (q\circ \pi ,z)\bigr | \longrightarrow 0. \]

In this case all generic divisorial Lelong numbers converge.

Proof. Step 1: the fixed point and positivity of the limit volume. Since $q\leq q_ j\leq 0$, monotonicity of the envelope in the background and in the potential gives $q\leq P_\theta [q]\leq P_{\theta _ j}[q]\leq P_{\theta _ j}[q_ j]=q_ j$. Letting $j\to \infty $ proves $P_\theta [q]=q$. By theorem 13.17.1, the lower volumes $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j)$ decrease; let $\ell $ be their limit. Monotonicity also gives $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)\leq \ell $, so the lower-volume assertion holds if $\ell =0$.

Suppose $\ell >0$. We show that then $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0$; the lower-volume assertion will then follow from the volume limits proved below for limits of positive lower volume. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta _1$, put $K=v_+(\Omega )$, and choose

\[ b=1+\frac{\ell }{2^{n+1}nK},\qquad a=1-b^{-1}. \]

Since $\Omega \geq \theta _ j$ and $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j)\geq \ell $, lemma 13.20.1, applied in background $\theta _ j$ with $r=V_{\theta _ j}$, shows that $Q_ b(q_ j,V_{\theta _ j})$ is defined for every $j$, with the same $b$. Put $h_ j=P_{\theta _ j}[Q_ b(q_ j,V_{\theta _ j})]$. For each competitor $s$ for this envelope ($s\leq 0$ and $s\preceq Q_ b(q_ j,V_{\theta _ j})$), the potential $aV_{\theta _ j}+(1-a)s$ lies below zero and is more singular than $aV_{\theta _ j}+(1-a)Q_ b(q_ j,V_{\theta _ j})\leq q_ j$. Since $q_ j=P_{\theta _ j}[q_ j]$, it lies below $q_ j$, and taking the envelope gives

\[ aV_{\theta _ j}+(1-a)h_ j\leq q_ j. \]

The functions $h_ j$ have supremum zero and are $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic. Normalized compactness and the uniform convergence of the backgrounds give a subsequential $L^1$ limit $h\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Also $V_{\theta _ j}\searrow V_\theta $, since the decreasing limit is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and lies between $V_\theta $ and zero. Passing to the limit in the convex inequality and using lemma 5.24.3 gives

\[ aV_\theta +(1-a)h\leq q. \]

Moreover, $v_-(\Omega )=\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Omega (0)\geq \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _1}(q_1)\geq \ell >0$ by volume monotonicity. By bigness, choose $\rho \leq V_\theta $ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \kappa \Omega $ for some $\kappa >0$. Proposition 13.17.2, applied to $0\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa \Omega )$ and $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta -\kappa \Omega )$, gives $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\rho )\geq \kappa ^ n v_-(\Omega )$. Monotonicity and addition applied to the convex combination above now yield

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q) \geq a^ n\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (V_\theta ) \geq (a\kappa )^ n v_-(\Omega )>0. \]

It remains to prove the volume limits when $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0$.

Step 2: a fixed background. Suppose first that $\theta _ j=\theta $ for every $j$. Put

\[ \delta =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0, \qquad L=\lim _ j\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q_ j), \qquad e_ k=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q_ k)-L. \]

The limit exists because the upper volumes decrease, by theorem 13.17.1, and are finite, by bounded mass; thus $e_ k\searrow 0$. After discarding finitely many indices, choose $b_ k>1$ tending to infinity with $b_ k^ n e_ k<\delta $, and set $a_ k=1-b_ k^{-1}$.

For every $j\geq k$, the upper-volume deficit $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q_ k)-\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q_ j)$ is at most $e_ k$, whereas the lower volume of $q_ k$ is at least $\delta $. Thus lemma 13.18.1 gives $h_{k,j}\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with

\[ a_ kq_ k+(1-a_ k)h_{k,j}\leq q_ j. \]

Since $q_ k\leq 0$ and $q_ j=P_\theta [q_ j]$, combining $q_ k$ with the competitors for $P_\theta [h_{k,j}]$, as in the proof of proposition 16.28.65, gives

\[ a_ kq_ k+(1-a_ k)P_\theta [h_{k,j}]\leq q_ j. \]

The potentials $P_\theta [h_{k,j}]$ have supremum zero. For fixed $k$, normalized compactness gives a subsequential $L^1$ limit $h_ k$ as $j\to \infty $. Passing to the limit and applying lemma 5.24.3 gives

\[ a_ kq_ k+(1-a_ k)h_ k\leq q. \]

For either volume, homogeneity, addition and monotonicity now yield

\[ a_ k^ n\operatorname {vol}_\theta (q_ k) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\theta \bigl (a_ kq_ k+(1-a_ k)h_ k\bigr ) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\theta (q) \leq \operatorname {vol}_\theta (q_ k). \]

Letting $k\to \infty $ proves both volume limits in the fixed background.

Step 3: varying backgrounds. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega $. By corollary 13.20.3, choose $w\leq q$ and $d>0$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\geq d\omega $. Choose $\varepsilon _ j>0$ decreasing to zero with $\theta _ j\leq \theta +\varepsilon _ j\omega $, and put

\[ c_ j=\frac d{d+\varepsilon _ j},\qquad f_ j=c_ jq_ j+(1-c_ j)w, \]
\[ F_ j=\left(\sup _{k\geq j}f_ k\right)^*,\qquad r_ j=P_\theta [F_ j]. \]

The convex combination $f_ j$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, because its background is bounded above by $\theta $:

\[ c_ j\theta _ j+(1-c_ j)(\theta -d\omega )\leq \theta . \]

Since $w\leq q\leq q_ k$, we have $f_ k\leq q_ k\leq q_ j$ for $k\geq j$, and upper regularization gives $F_ j\leq q_ j$. Also $f_ k\to q$ in $L^1$, so a subsequence converges almost everywhere, and $q\leq F_ j$ by lemma 5.24.3.

Background monotonicity now gives

\[ q\leq r_ j=P_\theta [F_ j]\leq P_{\theta _ j}[F_ j] \leq P_{\theta _ j}[q_ j]=q_ j. \]

The $r_ j$ decrease to $q$. Their lower volumes are at least $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0$, so theorem 13.19.5 makes them ordinary models, and Step 2 applies to the sequence $r_ j$ in the fixed background $\theta $. On the other hand, addition (with $\eta =(1-c_ j)(\theta -d\omega )$) and background monotonicity give, for either volume,

\[ \operatorname {vol}_\theta (q) \leq \operatorname {vol}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j) \leq c_ j^{-n}\operatorname {vol}_\theta (f_ j) \leq c_ j^{-n}\operatorname {vol}_\theta (r_ j). \]

Since $c_ j\to 1$, Step 2 shows that the outer terms tend to the same limit. The volumes $\operatorname {vol}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j)$ decrease by monotonicity in both background and potential, which proves the asserted decreasing convergence.

Step 4: Lelong numbers. By proposition 13.20.6, the volumes of the fixed potential $q$ in background $\theta _ j$ tend to its volumes in background $\theta $. Hence the upper-volume deficit between $q$ and $q_ j$, measured in $\theta _ j$, tends to zero, while the lower volume of $q_ j$ is at least $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0$. Apply proposition 13.18.3 in each background $\theta _ j$. Since $\theta _ j\leq \theta _1$, its proof works with one Lelong bound $C_\pi $ for $\pi ^*\theta _1$, independent of $j$. This proves the uniform convergence on each fixed modification, and taking generic values gives the divisorial assertion. $\square$

For an arbitrary decreasing sequence, convergence of the ordinary envelopes is equivalent to continuity of the upper volumes.

Proposition 13.21.3.EE58E71478F6 Suppose that $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ decrease to $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and that $u$ has positive lower volume. Then

\[ P_{\theta _ j}[u_ j]\searrow P_\theta [u] \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(u_ j)\searrow \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u). \]

When these conditions hold, the lower volumes converge to that of $u$ in background $\theta $, and the Lelong numbers converge uniformly on every fixed smooth compact modification.

Proof. Normalize $u_ j\leq 0$ by a common translation. Set $p_ j=P_{\theta _ j}[u_ j]$, $p=P_\theta [u]$ and $q=\lim _ jp_ j$. Since $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(u_ j)\geq \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)>0$, theorem 13.19.5 in background $\theta _ j$ shows that every $p_ j$ is an ordinary model with the same volumes as $u_ j$. The envelopes decrease, and the uniform convergence of the backgrounds gives $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Since $u\leq p\leq q$, the limit $q$ has positive lower volume, and theorem 13.21.2 gives, for either volume,

\[ \operatorname {vol}_\theta (q) =\lim _ j\operatorname {vol}_{\theta _ j}(p_ j) =\lim _ j\operatorname {vol}_{\theta _ j}(u_ j), \qquad P_\theta [q]=q. \]

If $q=p$, these equalities and theorem 13.19.5 show that both volumes of $u_ j$ converge to those of $u$; in particular the upper volumes do. Conversely, if the upper volumes converge, then $u$ and $q$ have the same upper volume, so $u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta ,q)$. By corollary 13.19.8, $P_\theta [u]=q$, that is, $q=p$. For the Lelong assertion, proposition 13.20.6 shows that the upper-volume deficit between $u$ and $u_ j$ in background $\theta _ j$ tends to zero. Apply proposition 13.18.3 with the common lower bound $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)>0$ and the uniform Lelong bound for $\pi ^*\theta _1$. $\square$

Corollary 13.21.4.D1480FEAFF87 For every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$,

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(u)\searrow \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u). \]

If this limiting volume is positive, then also

\[ \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(u)\searrow \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u),\qquad P_{\theta _ j}[u]\searrow P_\theta [u]. \]

Proof. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega $. Uniform convergence gives $\theta _ j\leq \theta +\varepsilon _ j\omega $ with $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$, so the volume limits follow from the two estimates in proposition 13.20.6; monotonicity makes them decreasing. In the positive-lower-volume case, apply proposition 13.21.3 to the constant sequence $u_ j=u$. $\square$

As an application, we show that trace envelopes realize their trace data exactly. Let $Y\subset X$ be a connected compact complex submanifold of positive dimension, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $\nu (u,Y)=0$. We use the notation of section 20.3: the restricted multiplier ideals $\mathfrak a_ m(u;Y)=\mathcal I(mu)\mathcal O_ Y$, the divisorial trace data $\tau _{Y,u}(E)=\lim _ mm^{-1}\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathfrak a_ m(u;Y)$ of theorem 20.3.2, the restrictions $u_ m|_ Y$ of the analytic approximants $u_ m$ of $u$, and, for a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\Omega $ on $Y$, the trace envelope $\mathcal T_\Omega (u;Y)$ of (20.3.25). The latter is the largest nonpositive $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$ whose divisorial Lelong numbers dominate $\tau _{Y,u}$. A majorant in a smaller background gives the trace envelope positive lower volume, so theorem 13.21.2 applies on $Y$.

Corollary 13.21.5.293D12D027CC Suppose that $Y$ has the bounded mass property. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega _0\geq \theta |_ Y$ on $Y$ and a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq 2\Omega _0$ on $Y$ with $v_-(\Omega )>0$. Then

\[ \nu \bigl (\mathcal T_\Omega (u;Y)\circ \pi ,E\bigr )=\tau _{Y,u}(E) \]

for every prime divisor $E$ on every smooth compact modification $\pi :Z\to Y$.

Proof. Put $T=\mathcal T_\Omega (u;Y)$ and $r=\dim Y$. Normalize the majorant $v$ of proposition 20.3.24 by $\sup _ Yv=0$. It belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta |_ Y)\subset \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\Omega /2)$ and is a competitor for $T$, so $v\leq T$. For every $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\Omega )$ with $w\sim T$, a constant translate of $v$ lies below $w$, so $P_{\Omega /2}(w)\not\equiv -\infty $. By lemma 16.28.69, applied on $Y$ with $a=1/2$,

\[ \int _ Y\mathrm{MA}_\Omega (w)\geq 2^{-r}v_-(\Omega ), \qquad \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Omega (T) \geq 2^{-r}v_-(\Omega )>0. \]

Since $\Omega -\theta |_ Y\geq \Omega _0$ is Hermitian and the curvature errors of the approximants tend to zero, the restrictions $u_ m|_ Y$ belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(Y,\Omega )$ for all sufficiently large $m$. By theorem 20.3.27, their dyadic ordinary envelopes $q_ j=P_\Omega [u_{2^ j}|_ Y]$ decrease to $T$. Theorem 13.21.2, applied on $Y$ in the fixed background $\Omega $, and the analytic type of $u_{2^ j}|_ Y$ give

\[ \nu (T\circ \pi ,E) =\lim _ j\nu (q_ j\circ \pi ,E) =\lim _ j2^{-j}\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathfrak a_{2^ j}(u;Y) =\tau _{Y,u}(E), \]

as required. $\square$

13.21.6 Recovery of envelope systems237A3786C187

We return to the big background $\theta $ on $X$ and apply the monotone limits above to the envelope systems of section 16.29. Fix real numbers $a<c$. Recall that $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$ consists of the pointwise concave, decreasing families $\Gamma =(\Gamma _\tau )_{\tau <c}$ of ideal models in background $\theta $ that are constant for $\tau \leq a$ (definition 16.29.2). An envelope system $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta )$ is a compatible family of such curves $\Gamma ^\eta =(\Gamma ^\eta _\tau )_{\tau <c}\in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta +\eta )$, indexed by the Hermitian forms $\eta $ (definition 16.29.6). The map $\mathsf E$ projects a curve in background $\theta $ to a system, and $\mathsf R$ takes a system to its limiting curve $(\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau =\lim _ j\Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau $, for any Hermitian form $h$ (theorem 16.29.9). The system is recovered from its limit if $\boldsymbol \Gamma =\mathsf E\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma $.

Compatibility fixes every local divisorial Lelong number of the slices before the perturbation tends to zero, and recovery means that these numbers do not increase in the limit. A positive limiting lower volume rules out such an increase. Concavity propagates positivity from the constant left part to every finite parameter, so a single condition on the initial slice suffices for the whole system.

Theorem 13.21.7.9EA752566EF2 Let $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta )$, fix a Hermitian form $h$, and put $\Gamma =\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma $. Then

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty }\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta +h/j} (\Gamma ^{h/j}_ a) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\Gamma _ a). \]

In particular this limit is independent of $h$. If it is positive, then $\boldsymbol \Gamma =\mathsf E(\Gamma )$, and for every $\tau <c$ both singularity volumes satisfy

\[ \operatorname {vol}_{\theta +h/j}(\Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau ) \searrow \operatorname {vol}_\theta (\Gamma _\tau ), \qquad \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\Gamma _\tau )>0. \]

Proof. Every ideal model is an ordinary model by corollary 18.4.4, and the retraction theorem gives $\Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau \searrow \Gamma _\tau $ in the decreasing backgrounds $\theta +h/j$. Thus theorem 13.21.2 proves the identity for the initial lower volumes, including the case where the limit is zero.

Suppose that this limit $\ell $ is positive. For $\tau \leq a$, the slice is $\Gamma _ a$. For $a<\tau <c$, choose $r\in (\tau ,c)$ and put $s=(\tau -a)/(r-a)$, so that $\tau =(1-s)a+sr$. Concavity gives

\[ (1-s)\Gamma _ a+s\Gamma _ r\leq \Gamma _\tau . \]

Homogeneity, proposition 13.17.2 and volume monotonicity imply

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\Gamma _\tau ) \geq (1-s)^ n\ell >0. \]

Theorem 13.21.2 now gives both volume limits for every fixed $\tau <c$.

It remains to show that the slices keep their multiplier ideals in the limit. Fix $\tau <c$ and write $q=\Gamma _\tau $, $q_ j=\Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau $ and $\theta _ j=\theta +h/j$. By proposition 13.20.6, the upper volumes of the fixed potential $q$ in the backgrounds $\theta _ j$ converge to its upper volume in $\theta $, which is the limit of the upper volumes of the $q_ j$. Consequently

\[ D_ j=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j) -\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q)\longrightarrow 0, \qquad \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j) \geq \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (q)>0. \]

After discarding finitely many indices, choose $b_ j>1$ with $b_ j\to +\infty $ and $b_ j^ nD_ j<\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\theta _ j}(q_ j)$. Since $q\leq q_ j$, the stretching lemma lemma 13.18.1 in background $\theta _ j$ gives $v_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ such that

\[ (1-b_ j^{-1})q_ j+b_ j^{-1}v_ j\leq q. \]

Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta +h$, so that all $v_ j$ are $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic. By lemma 5.26.2, there is $\alpha >0$ such that $\mathcal I(\alpha v_ j)=\mathcal O_ X$ for all $j$; additive normalization does not change these ideals. Let $E$ be a local divisor over a coordinate neighborhood $U$, with generic Lelong number $\nu _ E$ and log discrepancy $A_ U(E)$ as in section 5.38. Lemma 5.38.9, with $m=\alpha $, gives $\nu _ E(v_ j)\leq A_ U(E)/\alpha $. Taking Lelong numbers in the convex inequality therefore yields

\[ 0\leq \nu _ E(q)-\nu _ E(q_ j) \leq \frac{A_ U(E)}{\alpha b_ j}\longrightarrow 0. \]

Compatibility gives all components the same multiplier ideals, so $\nu _ E(q_ j)$ is independent of $j$, and hence equal to $\nu _ E(q)$. Blowing up a point on a local modification converts its pointwise Lelong number into such a divisorial number; on a curve the point itself is a divisor. Thus all Lelong numbers of $q$ and $q_ j$ on all local modifications agree, and theorem 5.37.3 gives $q\sim _{\mathcal I}q_ j$. This holds at every parameter $\tau <c$, so proposition 16.29.11 proves recovery. $\square$

The lower bound $(1-s)^ n\ell $ may tend to zero as $\tau \uparrow c$; the theorem gives no positive lower bound that is uniform up to the cutoff. The hypothesis is invariant under a smooth change of background $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$, $f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$, through the gauge bijection of proposition 16.29.13: the estimate there makes the transported initial slice ordinarily equivalent to $\Gamma _ a-f$, and the map $u\mapsto u-f$ preserves the curvature currents and hence both singularity volumes. The ruled-surface system of theorem 16.29.17, which is not recovered from its limit, has zero initial limiting lower volume, so the theorem does not apply to it.
