11.43 Convex-body criteria for varying references7D0D0E9AC5C3

7D0D0E9AC5C3

When the singularities of a torus-invariant reference are described by a convex body, the closed mass formula of theorem 11.42.1 turns convergence of the convex bodies into convergence of the functionals. A translated simplex contained in all the bodies keeps the limiting reference solvable, and the Hausdorff distance then controls the functionals uniformly over all continuous tests, even though the ordinary types vary.

Let $n\geq 2$, and let $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ n$, normalized by $\int _{\mathbb P^ n}\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^ n=1$. In homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots :Z_ n]$, consider the torus $T=\{ Z_0\cdots Z_ n\ne 0\} $, and put

\[ x_ i=\log |Z_ i/Z_0|^2,\qquad L(x)=\log \bigl (1+\textstyle \sum _ i\mathrm{e}^{x_ i}\bigr ),\qquad \Sigma =\{ p_ i\geq 0:\textstyle \sum _ i p_ i\leq 1\} , \]

where $1\leq i\leq n$ and $\Sigma \subset \mathbb R^ n$ is the standard simplex. Functions of $x=(x_1,\ldots ,x_ n)$ are regarded as functions on $T$; thus $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}L$ on $T$. Define the continuous function $\ell $ on $\Sigma $ by

\[ \ell (p)=\sum _ i p_ i\log p_ i+ (1-\textstyle \sum _ i p_ i)\log (1-\textstyle \sum _ i p_ i), \qquad 0\log 0=0. \]

For a convex body $K\subset \Sigma $, that is, a compact convex set with nonempty interior, set

\[ h_ K(x)=\sup _{p\in K}p\cdot x,\qquad g_ K(x)=\sup _{p\in K}\{ p\cdot x-\ell (p)\} . \]

11.43.1 Canonical models and their mass6439CBEB8575

Lemma 11.43.2.832F0D3BF7D6 The functions $h_ K-L$ and $g_ K-L$ on $T$ extend uniquely to $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic functions $\psi _ K,\Phi _ K$ on $\mathbb P^ n$. They satisfy

\[ \begin{aligned} & \psi _ K\leq \Phi _ K\leq \psi _ K+\log (n+1),\qquad \sup \Phi _ K=0,\\ & P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\psi _ K]=\Phi _ K =P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\Phi _ K]. \end{aligned} \]

If $p_*+\delta \Sigma \subset K$ for some $p_*\in \mathbb R^ n$ and $0<\delta <1$, then $P_{(1-\delta )\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\Phi _ K)\not\equiv -\infty $.

Proof. The Legendre transform of $L$ is $L^*=\ell $ on $\Sigma $ and $+\infty $ outside it. Since $-\log (n+1)\leq \ell \leq 0$,

\[ h_ K\leq g_ K\leq h_ K+\log (n+1),\qquad g_ K\leq L,\qquad h_ K\leq h_\Sigma \leq L. \]

A finite convex function of the logarithmic moduli is plurisubharmonic: for a smooth convex function the complex Hessian is positive, and convolution gives the general case. On $T\cap \{ Z_ j\ne 0\} $, add the local Fubini–Study potential $\log \sum _ i|Z_ i/Z_ j|^2$; the resulting functions are again convex functions of the logarithmic moduli of the affine coordinates, and they are bounded above by that smooth potential because $h_ K,g_ K\leq L$. Lemma 5.24.7 therefore gives the extensions across the coordinate hyperplanes and the stated bounds. If $p\in \operatorname {int}K$, then $p\in \operatorname {int}\Sigma $, and at $x_ i=\log (p_ i/(1-\sum _ k p_ k))$ the supremum defining $L=\ell ^*$ is attained at $p\in K$, so $g_ K(x)=L(x)$. This proves $\sup \Phi _ K=0$.

For $C\geq \log (n+1)$, let $v_ C=P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\psi _ K+C,0)$, the envelope of the obstacle $\min (\psi _ K+C,0)$. Since $\Phi _ K$ is a competitor, $\Phi _ K\leq v_ C\leq 0$. The obstacle is upper semicontinuous, so $v_ C\leq \psi _ K+C$ everywhere. The compact torus acting by rotations of the coordinates preserves the obstacle, so $v_ C$ is invariant, and on $T$ the function $f=v_ C+L$ is a finite convex function of $x$ with $f\leq L$ and $f\leq h_ K+C$. The second inequality shows that the Legendre transform $f^*$ is $+\infty $ outside $K$, and the first gives $f^*\geq \ell $. Thus

\[ f(x)=\sup _{p\in \operatorname {dom}f^*}\{ p\cdot x-f^*(p)\} \leq g_ K(x). \]

Hence $v_ C\leq \Phi _ K$ on $T$, and since both functions are quasi-plurisubharmonic and $T$ is the complement of an analytic set, $v_ C=\Phi _ K$ on $\mathbb P^ n$. Letting $C\to \infty $ gives $P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\psi _ K]=\Phi _ K$. Since $\psi _ K\leq \Phi _ K\leq \psi _ K+\log (n+1)$, the envelopes of $\min (\Phi _ K+C,0)$ and $\min (\psi _ K+C,0)$ interlace as $C\to \infty $, so also $P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\Phi _ K]=\Phi _ K$.

For the final assertion put $\varepsilon =1-\delta $, $b=\sum _ i p_{*,i}$ and $\chi =p_*\cdot x-\varepsilon L$ on $T$. The inclusion $p_*+\delta \Sigma \subset \Sigma $ implies $p_{*,i}\geq 0$ and $b\leq \varepsilon $, and the extension of $\chi $ to $\mathbb P^ n$ satisfies

\[ \varepsilon \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi =\sum _ i p_{*,i}[Z_ i=0]+(\varepsilon -b)[Z_0=0]\geq 0. \]

Since $h_ K\geq p_*\cdot x+\delta h_\Sigma $ and $L-h_\Sigma \leq \log (n+1)$, one has $\chi -\delta \log (n+1)\leq \Phi _ K$. Thus $\chi -\delta \log (n+1)$ is an $\varepsilon \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic competitor for $P_{\varepsilon \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\Phi _ K)$, which proves the claim. $\square$

In the rest of this section, either $X=\mathbb P^ n$ and $\pi =\mathrm{id}$, or $\pi :X=\operatorname {Bl}_ q\mathbb P^ n\to \mathbb P^ n$ is the blowup at a fixed point $q$, with exceptional divisor $E_ q$; put $\Theta =\pi ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$.

Lemma 11.43.3.59D09F5BD0FB The manifold $X$ is Kähler, and $\Theta $ is smooth, closed, semipositive and big. For $r>0$, the reference $r\pi ^*\Phi _ K$ satisfies $P_{r\Theta }[r\pi ^*\Phi _ K]=r\pi ^*\Phi _ K$, has small unbounded locus, and satisfies

C1218CE2B784 11.43.4
\begin{equation} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_{r\Theta }(r\pi ^*\Phi _ K) =r^ n n!\operatorname {Vol}_ n(K). \end{equation}

If $p_*+\delta \Sigma \subset K$ with $0<\delta <1$, then $P_{(1-\delta )r\Theta }(r\pi ^*\Phi _ K)\not\equiv -\infty $.

Proof. On $\mathbb P^ n$, the envelope identity is lemma 11.43.2. In the blowup case, every $\pi ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic function descends to $\mathbb P^ n$. Indeed, off $E_ q$ add a local potential of $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$; the resulting plurisubharmonic function is bounded above near $q$, because the original function is bounded above near the compact fiber $E_ q$, and therefore extends across $q$. The pullback of the extension agrees with the original function off $E_ q$, hence everywhere. Pullback is therefore a bijection from $\mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^ n,\omega _{\mathrm{FS}})$ onto $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\Theta )$ preserving inequalities and envelopes, and positive scaling preserves them as well.

On projective space, $\Theta =\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ is Kähler. For bigness in the blowup case, choose unitary homogeneous coordinates $[W_0:\cdots :W_ n]$, used only in this paragraph, in which $q=[1:0:\cdots :0]$, set

\[ \psi _ q=\log \frac{\sum _{i=1}^ n|W_ i|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ n|W_ i|^2}, \]

and let $Q:X\to \mathbb P^{n-1}$ be the map induced by the linear projection from $q$. For $0<t<1$,

\[ \Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(t\pi ^*\psi _ q) =(1-t)\pi ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+tQ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS},n-1}+t[E_ q]. \]

The smooth part is a Kähler form, since $(\pi ,Q)$ embeds $X$ as the incidence graph in $\mathbb P^ n\times \mathbb P^{n-1}$ and both coefficients are positive. This proves the assertions about the manifold and the form $\Theta $.

For a smooth convex $g$, the coordinates $z_ i=\mathrm{e}^{x_ i/2+\mathrm{i}\vartheta _ i}$ give

\[ \mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}g=\frac1{2\pi }\sum _ i g_ i\, \mathrm{d}\vartheta _ i, \qquad \int _ T(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g)^ n=n!\int _{\mathbb R^ n}\det (D^2g)\, \mathrm{d}x. \]

For a finite convex $g$, convolution and the continuity of bounded Monge–Ampère products under local uniform convergence identify $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g)^ n$ on $T$ in the same way: its image under $z\mapsto x$ is $n!M_ g$, where $M_ g$ is the Alexandrov measure, which assigns to a Borel set of $\mathbb R^ n$ the Euclidean volume of its subgradient image. For $g_ K$, the bound $g_ K\leq h_ K+\log (n+1)$ and a separation argument put every subgradient in $K$. Conversely, if $p\in \operatorname {int}K$ and $p+c\mathbb B\subset K$, where $\mathbb B$ is the closed unit ball of $\mathbb R^ n$ and $c>0$, then $g_ K(x)-p\cdot x\geq c|x|$; this function attains a minimum, so $p$ is a subgradient of $g_ K$ at some point. Consequently

\[ \operatorname {int}K\subset \partial g_ K(\mathbb R^ n)\subset K, \qquad M_{g_ K}(\mathbb R^ n)=\operatorname {Vol}_ n(K), \]

since the boundary of a convex body has zero volume. The analytic complement of $T$ receives no non-pluripolar mass, so this is the full mass. Locality off the exceptional divisor proves the formula after pullback, since the divisor and its image carry no non-pluripolar mass, and homogeneity gives the factor $r^ n$. The reference is locally bounded off the coordinate hyperplanes and their inverse image, an analytic set, hence has small unbounded locus. Finally, scaling by $r$ and pulling back the competitor found at the end of the proof of lemma 11.43.2 gives the last assertion. $\square$

11.43.5 Hausdorff control of the functional3EB28248B606

Theorem 11.43.6.93F19A4F37D3 Let convex bodies $P_ j,P\subset \Sigma $ contain the same translated simplex $p_*+\delta \Sigma $, with $0<\delta <1$, and suppose that their Hausdorff distance $d_ j=d_ H(P_ j,P)$ tends to zero. Let $r_ j>0$ tend to $r>0$. On the fixed $X$ above, set

\[ (\theta _ j,\varphi _ j)=(r_ j\Theta ,r_ j\pi ^*\Phi _{P_ j}), \qquad (\theta ,\varphi )=(r\Theta ,r\pi ^*\Phi _ P). \]

For every bounded real Borel function $F$, the equation (11.33.1) is solvable in the ordinary type of each $\varphi _ j$ and of $\varphi $; these types have masses $m_ j=r_ j^ n n!\operatorname {Vol}_ n(P_ j)$ and $m=r^ n n!\operatorname {Vol}_ n(P)>0$. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ on $X$, put $Z_ F=\int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V$, and let $B_ j,B$ and $\mathcal A_ j,\mathcal A$ be the corresponding normalization scalars and supporting sets. Then

\[ B_ j(F)=\log m_ j-\log Z_ F,\qquad B(F)=\log m-\log Z_ F. \]

Writing $s=\delta /(n+\sqrt n)$, one has

720678E2362C 11.43.7
\begin{equation} \sup _{G\in C(X,\mathbb R)}|B_ j(G)-B(G)| \leq n|\log (r_ j/r)|+n\log (1+d_ j/s). \end{equation}

Moreover, $n!\operatorname {Vol}_ n(P_ j)\geq \delta ^ n$ and $n!\operatorname {Vol}_ n(P)\geq \delta ^ n$, the backgrounds converge smoothly, and the references converge in $L^1(X)$ and locally uniformly on $\pi ^{-1}(T)$. For continuous real $F$, every supporting set at $F$ equals $\{ \nu _ F\} $, where $\nu _ F=\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V/Z_ F$. If continuous $F_ j$ converge uniformly to $F$ and $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_ j(F_ j)$, then

08069BF2F396 11.43.8
\begin{equation} \int _ X\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu _ j}{\mathrm{d}V} -\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu _ F}{\mathrm{d}V}\right|\mathrm{d}V \leq \mathrm{e}^{2\| F_ j-F\| _\infty }-1. \end{equation}

Proof. By the two preceding lemmas, each $\varphi _ j$ and $\varphi $ is an envelope fixed point with small unbounded locus and the stated positive mass, and it satisfies the smaller-background condition. Theorem 11.42.1 therefore gives solvability and the formulas for the scalars and supporting sets.

The common simplex contains a ball $c+s\mathbb B$ of radius $s$, the inradius of $\delta \Sigma $, where $\mathbb B$ is the closed unit ball. The Hausdorff distance gives $P_ j\subset P+d_ j\mathbb B$, while $s\mathbb B\subset P-c$ and convexity give

\[ P+d_ j\mathbb B\subset c+(1+d_ j/s)(P-c). \]

Exchanging the roles of $P$ and $P_ j$ bounds both volume ratios by $(1+d_ j/s)^ n$. Subtracting the scalar formulas and including the factor $r_ j^ n/r^ n$ gives (11.43.7). The volume bound holds because $\delta \Sigma $ has volume $\delta ^ n/n!$.

Let $\omega _\ell $ be the modulus of uniform continuity of $\ell $ on $\Sigma $. Matching the maximizers in the definitions of $g_{P_ j}$ and $g_ P$ by points at distance at most $d_ j$ gives

\[ |g_{P_ j}(x)-g_ P(x)|\leq d_ j|x|+\omega _\ell (d_ j). \]

This proves local uniform convergence on $T$. The common lower bound $\chi -\delta \log (n+1)\leq \Phi _{P_ j}$ obtained at the end of the proof of lemma 11.43.2 is integrable on $X$, also after pullback, so boundedness of $r_ j$ and dominated convergence off the coordinate hyperplanes give $L^1$ convergence. Finally, the ratio of the normalized exponential densities lies in $[\mathrm{e}^{-2\eta },\mathrm{e}^{2\eta }]$, where $\eta =\| F_ j-F\| _\infty $. This proves (11.43.8) and hence convergence in total variation. $\square$

11.43.9 A family with nonanalytic isolated poles592E13B7D2ED

The hypothesis of a common simplex allows ordinary singularity types that contain no potential with analytic singularities. Let $o=[1:0:\cdots :0]$ be the origin of the affine chart $Z_0\ne 0$, with affine coordinates $z_ i=Z_ i/Z_0$. For $0<a\leq 1$, define

\[ P_ a=\{ p\in \Sigma :\textstyle \sum _ i\sqrt{p_ i}\geq \sqrt a\} , \qquad \kappa _ n=\frac{2^ n n!}{(2n)!}=\frac1{(2n-1)!!}. \]

Proposition 11.43.10.70BE47F32080 The function $\Phi _{P_ a}$ is locally bounded outside the single point $o$, where it has a pole, and it does not have the ordinary singularity type of any function with analytic singularities. Its generic Lelong number along every prime divisor of $\mathbb P^ n$ is zero, and

47DDB1E89607 11.43.11
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb P^ n}\mathrm{MA}_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\Phi _{P_ a}) =1-\kappa _ n a^ n>0. \end{equation}

The same assertions about the pole, nonanalyticity and generic Lelong numbers hold for $\pi ^*\Phi _{P_ a}$ on the blowup $\operatorname {Bl}_ q\mathbb P^ n$ at a fixed point $q\ne o$.

Proof. The function $p\mapsto \sum _ i\sqrt{p_ i}$ is concave, so $P_ a$ is convex. Set

\[ b=\frac{n+1}{2n},\qquad p_{*,i}=\frac bn, \qquad \delta =\frac{n-1}{4n}. \]

Then $b+\delta <1$ and $\sum _ i\sqrt{p_{*,i}}=\sqrt{(n+1)/2}>1$. Adding a point of $\delta \Sigma $ preserves the last inequality and keeps the sum of the coordinates below one, so $p_*+\delta \Sigma $ lies in every $P_ a$.

All the standard basis vectors $e_ i$ belong to $P_ a$, and therefore

\[ \psi _{P_ a}\geq \log \frac{\max _{1\leq i\leq n}|Z_ i|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ n|Z_ i|^2}. \]

Hence the reference is locally bounded away from $o$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $\sum _ ip_ i\geq a/n$ on $P_ a$. When all $x_ i<0$, this yields $h_{P_ a}(x)\leq (a/n)\max _ i x_ i$, which tends to $-\infty $ as $z\to o$, and lemma 11.43.2 gives the pole at $o$. Local boundedness outside one point implies that all generic Lelong numbers along prime divisors vanish, and pulling back to the blowup at a different point introduces no pole.

To test analyticity, fix positive $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ n$ and approach $o$ along $|z_ i|^2=\mathrm{e}^{-R\lambda _ i}$, $R\to \infty $. Writing $y_ i=\sqrt{p_ i}$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

6654DDDC658F 11.43.12
\begin{equation} \min _{p\in P_ a}\sum _ i\lambda _ i p_ i =\frac a{\sum _ i\lambda _ i^{-1}}=:d_ a(\lambda ),\qquad y_ i=\frac{\sqrt a}{\lambda _ i\sum _ k\lambda _ k^{-1}}, \end{equation}

where the minimizer satisfies $\sum _ ip_ i\leq a\leq 1$, so that it lies in $P_ a$. Since $L(-R\lambda )\to 0$ and $\Phi _{P_ a}-\psi _{P_ a}$ is bounded, $-\Phi _{P_ a}/R\to d_ a(\lambda )$ along these curves.

Suppose that the type were that of $c\log \sum _ k|f_ k|^2$ near $o$, with $c>0$ and holomorphic germs $f_ k=\sum _\alpha c_{k,\alpha }z^\alpha $. Exponentiating the two inequalities expressing this equivalence and averaging over the arguments of the $z_ i$ gives the positive series

\[ \sum _{k,\alpha }|c_{k,\alpha }|^2\mathrm{e}^{-R\alpha \cdot \lambda }, \]

whose exponential decay rate in $R$ must be $d_ a(\lambda )/c$. That rate is the minimum of $\alpha \cdot \lambda $ over the monomials with nonzero coefficients: a minimizing term gives one bound, and convergence at a fixed $R_0$ bounds the series above by a constant times $\mathrm{e}^{-(R-R_0)\min \alpha \cdot \lambda }$. Near any $\lambda $ with positive coordinates, only finitely many monomials can attain the minimum, because these coordinates have a common positive lower bound and any one nonzero coefficient bounds the degree of the minimizing monomials. The rate is therefore locally piecewise linear in $\lambda $. But

\[ d_ a(s,1,\ldots ,1)=\frac{as}{1+(n-1)s},\qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}s^2}d_ a(s,1,\ldots ,1) =-\frac{2a(n-1)}{(1+(n-1)s)^3}<0. \]

This contradiction proves nonanalyticity. The assertion persists on a blowup at a point different from $o$, which does not change a neighborhood of the pole.

The removed corner $C_ a=\{ p\in \mathbb R^ n:p_ i\geq 0,\ \sum _ i\sqrt{p_ i}<\sqrt a\} =aC_1$ lies in $\Sigma $. The substitution $p_ i=y_ i^2$ gives

\[ \operatorname {Vol}_ n(C_1) =2^ n\int _{y_ i\geq 0,\, \sum y_ i<1}\prod _ i y_ i\, \mathrm{d}y =\frac{2^ n}{(2n)!}. \]

The last equality follows by iterated integration of monomials over a simplex. Removing the corner from $\Sigma $, whose volume is $1/n!$, and applying (11.43.4) gives (11.43.11). Since $n\geq 2$, one has $\kappa _ n<1$. $\square$

Corollary 11.43.13.F57433433789 Put $a_ j=1-1/(j+2)$ and $r_ j=1+1/(j+2)$ for $j\geq 1$. On $\mathbb P^ n$, or on its blowup at a fixed point $q\ne o$, the references $r_ j\pi ^*\Phi _{P_{a_ j}}$ satisfy the hypotheses of theorem 11.43.6, with limit $\pi ^*\Phi _{P_1}$. Their ordinary types and masses vary with $j$, and

\[ \sup _ G|B_ j(G)-B(G)| \leq n|\log r_ j|+\frac{n\kappa _ n}{1-\kappa _ n}|1-a_ j|\longrightarrow 0. \]

The equation is solvable in the limiting ordinary type, whose mass is $1-\kappa _ n$; for $n=2$ this mass is $2/3$.

Proof. If $p\in P_{a_ j}\setminus P_1$, then $u=(\sum _ i\sqrt{p_ i})^2\in [a_ j,1)$ and $p/u\in P_1$. Since $|p|\leq \sum _ ip_ i\leq u$, one has $|p/u-p|\leq 1-u\leq 1-a_ j$. Thus $d_ H(P_{a_ j},P_1)\leq 1-a_ j$, and the hypotheses of the theorem hold with the simplex found in the proof of proposition 11.43.10. The mass formula (11.43.11) and the derivative bound $|\mathrm{d}\log (1-\kappa _ n a^ n)/\mathrm{d}a|\leq n\kappa _ n/(1-\kappa _ n)$ give the estimate for the functionals. By (11.43.12), the directional coefficients of the $j$th reference are multiplied by $r_ ja_ j=1-(j+2)^{-2}$, which strictly increases with $j$, so the ordinary types are distinct, while the masses $r_ j^ n(1-\kappa _ n a_ j^ n)$ strictly decrease to $1-\kappa _ n>0$. $\square$

11.43.14 Positive mass with a degenerate smooth residualD4A7E78D4C33

A second family has a logarithmic pole whose resolution leaves a smooth residual form degenerating along a different exceptional divisor. Let $o=[1:0:\cdots :0]$ as in subsection 11.43.9, and fix $q\ne o$ in $\mathbb P^ n$. On $X=\operatorname {Bl}_ q\mathbb P^ n$, keep $\Theta =\pi ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$. For $0<t<1$, put

\[ \psi _ t=t\log \frac{\sum _{i=1}^ n|Z_ i|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ n|Z_ i|^2}, \qquad K_ t=\{ p\in \Sigma :\textstyle \sum _ i p_ i\geq t\} . \]

On $T$, the function $\psi _ t+L$, a potential of $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ t$, equals $(1-t)L+t\log \sum _ i\mathrm{e}^{x_ i}$. It differs by a bounded function from

\[ (1-t)h_\Sigma +t\max _ i x_ i=h_{K_ t},\qquad K_ t=(1-t)\Sigma +t\operatorname {conv}(e_1,\ldots ,e_ n). \]

Thus $\psi _ t\sim \psi _{K_ t}\sim \Phi _{K_ t}$, and $n!\operatorname {Vol}_ n(K_ t)=1-t^ n$. We call $\psi _ t$ the prototype of this ordinary type.

Proposition 11.43.15.C9CD848ECB1A Let $\tau :Z\to X$ be the blowup of the point of $X$ above $o$, with exceptional divisor $E_ o$, and denote the inverse image of $E_ q$ in $Z$ again by $E_ q$. There is a smooth semipositive closed form $\alpha _ t$ on $Z$ such that

B2ABC5CB86A3 11.43.16
\begin{equation} (\pi \circ \tau )^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}} +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\psi _ t\circ \pi \circ \tau )=\alpha _ t+t[E_ o]. \end{equation}

It is positive away from $E_ q$, has rank exactly one at every point of $E_ q$, and satisfies $\int _ Z\alpha _ t^ n=1-t^ n>0$. Moreover, for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\Theta )$ with the ordinary type of $\pi ^*\Phi _{K_ t}$, in particular for every solution of the equation in this type, the function $u\circ \tau -\psi _ t\circ \pi \circ \tau $ extends from $Z\setminus E_ o$ to a bounded $\alpha _ t$-plurisubharmonic function on $Z$.

Proof. On $Y=\operatorname {Bl}_ o\mathbb P^ n$, let $\pi _ o$ be the blowdown map and $Q_ o:Y\to \mathbb P^{n-1}$ the map induced by the linear projection from $o$. As in the proof of lemma 11.43.3, the incidence graph embedding shows that

\[ \beta _ t=(1-t)\pi _ o^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}} +tQ_ o^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS},n-1} \]

is a Kähler form, where $\omega _{\mathrm{FS},n-1}$ is the Fubini–Study form of $\mathbb P^{n-1}$. Since the two centers are distinct, $Z$ is also the blowup $\sigma :Z\to Y$ of the point above $q$. Put $\alpha _ t=\sigma ^*\beta _ t$. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives (11.43.16); there is no term along $E_ q$, since $\psi _ t$ is locally bounded near $q$.

In a blowup chart, $\sigma $ is $(s,w_2,\ldots ,w_ n)\mapsto (s,sw_2,\ldots ,sw_ n)$. At $s=0$ its differential has rank one and kills the $n-1$ tangent directions of $E_ q$. Positivity of $\beta _ t$ therefore gives the assertion about the rank; off $E_ q$ the map is biholomorphic.

Write $H=\pi _ o^*[\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}]$ and $E=[E_ o]$ for the cohomology classes on $Y$. The current identity gives $[\beta _ t]=H-tE$. Since $H|_{E_ o}=0$, all mixed top intersections vanish. The normal bundle of $E_ o\cong \mathbb P^{n-1}$ is $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{n-1}}(-1)$, so $\int _ YE^ n=(-1)^{n-1}$. Therefore

\[ \int _ Z\alpha _ t^ n=\int _ Y(H-tE)^ n=1-t^ n. \]

The first equality holds because $\sigma $ has degree one. Finally, if $u\sim \pi ^*\Phi _{K_ t}\sim \pi ^*\psi _ t$, then $u\circ \tau $ differs from the pulled-back prototype $\psi _ t\circ \pi \circ \tau $ by a bounded function off $E_ o$. By (11.43.16), after adding a local potential of $\alpha _ t$ this bounded difference becomes plurisubharmonic off $E_ o$, and it extends across $E_ o$ by lemma 5.24.7. No Monge–Ampère product of unbounded potentials is involved. $\square$

Corollary 11.43.17.FB3A15C67170 Let $t_ j,t\in [t_{\min },t_{\max }]\subset (0,1)$ with $t_ j\to t$, and let $r_ j>0$ tend to $r>0$. The references $r_ j\pi ^*\Phi _{K_{t_ j}}$ satisfy the hypotheses of theorem 11.43.6, so in particular the equation is solvable in the limiting ordinary type, and

\[ \sup _ G|B_ j(G)-B(G)| \leq n|\log (r_ j/r)|+ \frac{nt_{\max }^{n-1}}{1-t_{\max }^ n}|t_ j-t|. \]

At continuous tests, all supporting sets consist of the normalized exponential probabilities of that theorem. At the endpoint $t=1$, where the mass vanishes, no equation with a positive bounded density is solvable in the ordinary type of the prototype $\pi ^*\psi _1$.

Proof. Choose $p_{*,i}=t_{\max }/n$ and $\delta =(1-t_{\max })/2$; then $p_*+\delta \Sigma $ lies in every $K_ t$ with $t\leq t_{\max }$. For $s\geq t$, moving a point of $K_ t$ toward a vertex $e_ i$ until its coordinate sum reaches $s$ gives

\[ d_ H(K_ s,K_ t)\leq \frac{\sqrt2}{1-t_{\max }}|s-t|. \]

Hence the stability theorem applies. Instead of its general bound, the derivative bound for $\log (1-t^ n)$ on $[t_{\min },t_{\max }]$ and the mass formula give the sharper estimate for the functionals.

At $t=1$, the resolved residual form $\alpha _1$ is pulled back from $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, so $\int _ Z\alpha _1^ n=0$. The argument of proposition 11.43.15 shows that every potential of the ordinary type of $\pi ^*\psi _1$ has a bounded $\alpha _1$-plurisubharmonic remainder. Stokes' theorem for bounded products in a closed background gives this remainder mass zero, and positivity forces its Monge–Ampère measure to vanish. Thus no such potential can solve the equation with density $c\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V$ for bounded $F$ and $c>0$. In contrast, the endpoint $a=1$ in corollary 11.43.13 has positive mass and remains solvable. $\square$

Scaling by $r>0$ multiplies both the resolved residual form and the divisor coefficient by $r$. Solvability is obtained on $X$ through the criterion of theorem 11.42.1, without requiring the resolved residual form to be strictly positive. These criteria use closed projective backgrounds and fixed modifications; we do not know analogous convergence criteria for general nonclosed Hermitian backgrounds, for moving modifications, or for unrestricted degenerations of the singularities. Moreover, the supporting probabilities here only identify the limiting functional: we make no claim about convergence of the solutions or of weighted adjoint products.
