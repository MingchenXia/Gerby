14.2 Geodesic rays in the space of potentials

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

4 Geodesic rays in the space of potentials

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

When $\omega $ is a Kähler form, the space of smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic potentials carries the Mabuchi $L^2$-metric. Its geodesic equation is the homogeneous complex Monge–Ampère equation on $X\times \{ 0<\operatorname{Re}z<1\} $. Subsolutions of this equation are subgeodesics, and the geodesic between two boundary potentials is obtained as a Perron envelope. This construction continues to make sense for singular potentials, even when no smooth geodesic exists.

The goal of this chapter is to further develop the corresponding theory for $\theta $-plurisubharmonic potentials, where $\theta $ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form representing a big class. We define subgeodesics by requiring the complexification $\Phi (x,z)=\varphi _{\operatorname{Re}z}(x)$ to be plurisubharmonic. Geodesics are then defined as Perron envelopes of subgeodesics with prescribed boundary values. This point of view is flexible enough to handle unbounded potentials and singularity constraints.

The first section sets up the basic properties of subgeodesics. We then construct geodesics with prescribed singular endpoints and study geodesic rays. The results are formulated relative to a model potential $\phi $, so that the endpoints and rays live in a fixed relative singularity class.

The final part of the chapter studies the finite-energy space $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and the Darvas $d_1$-metric. We prove the basic metric properties needed later, including completeness and monotone convergence criteria. The radial version of the metric on geodesic rays will be used in the study of test curves and singularity types.

This chapter provides the analytic bridge between envelopes and the later non-Archimedean theory. The space $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and the $d_1$-geometry developed here feed into the pseudometric $d_ S$ of chapter 6, while geodesic rays will later be related to test curves and non-Archimedean potentials.

4.1 Subgeodesics

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class.

Definition 4.1.1. Let us fix $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. A subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ is a family $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

  1. if we define

    \[ \Phi \colon X\times \Bigl \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z\in (0,1) \Bigr \} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad (x,z)\mapsto \varphi _{\operatorname{Re}z}(x), \]

    then $\Phi $ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh, where $p_1\colon X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z\in (0,1)\} \rightarrow X$ is the natural projection;

  2. when $t\to 0+$ (resp. $t\to 1-$), $\varphi _ t$ converges to $\varphi _0$ (resp. $\varphi _1$) with respect to the $L^1$-topology.

We also say $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic.

We call $\Phi $ the complexification of the subgeodesic $(\varphi _ t)_ t$.

When we do not want to specify $\varphi _0$ and $\varphi _1$, we shall say $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ is a subgeodesic
Note. Note that it is implicit in the definition that $\varphi _ t$ as $t\to 0+$ or $t\to 1-$ admits an $L^1$-limit.
.

More generally, a family $(\psi _ t)_{t\in [a,b]}$ (resp. $(\psi _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$) in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for some $a\leq b$ is called a subgeodesic if $(\psi _{tb+(1-t)a})_{t\in [0,1]}$ (resp. $(\psi _{tb+(1-t)a})_{t\in (0,1)}$) is a subgeodesic.

Remark 4.1.2.Note that given a subgeodesic $(\psi _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$, the endpoints $\psi _ a$ and $\psi _ b$ are uniquely determined, in view of proposition 1.2.6.

Remark 4.1.3. In the literature, people sometimes regard $\Phi $ as a function defined on $X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}: \mathrm{e}^{-1}<|z|<1\} $, with $\Phi (x,z)=\varphi _{-\log |z|}(x)$. We sometimes also use this convention; the ambient strip or annulus will make the convention clear.

In general, there are no subgeodesics from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. In fact, by proposition 4.1.6 below, the existence of a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ forces $\varphi _0\land \varphi _1\not\equiv -\infty $, a condition that may fail (see example 5.2.8).

We first note that the subgeodesics are well-behaved under the change of $\theta $:

Proposition 4.1.4. Let $g$ be a smooth real function on $X$. Let $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$. Suppose that $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then $(\varphi _ t-g)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$.

Proof. This follows directly from the definition of the complexification. $\square$

Example 4.1.5. Let $\varphi _0\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Let

\[ \varphi _ t=\varphi _0+tC,\quad t\in [0,1]. \]

Then $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic. Indeed, since $\operatorname{Re}z$ is harmonic in $z$, the complexification $\Phi (x,z)=\varphi _0(x)+C\, \operatorname{Re}z$ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh.

As a consequence, the constant $(\varphi _0)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic, called the constant subgeodesic at $\varphi _0$.

A more general version is as follows: Suppose that $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $C_1,C_2\in \mathbb {R}$. Then $(\varphi _ t+C_1t+C_2)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is also a subgeodesic.

Proposition 4.1.6. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Then for each $x\in X$, $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is a convex function. Furthermore,

\[ \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\quad \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0\land \varphi _1. \]

Proof. Let $\Phi $ be the complexification of $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$.

For each $x\in X$, the map

\[ \Bigl \{ z\in \mathbb {C}: \operatorname{Re}z\in (0,1) \Bigr \} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad z\mapsto \Phi (x,z) \]

is either subharmonic or constantly $-\infty $, as follows from definition 4.1.1 1 and proposition 1.1.13. In the latter case, the convexity of $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is trivial. In the former case, the convexity on the interval $(0,1)$ follows from proposition 1.1.9.

In order to verify the convexity at the boundary, let us fix $s\in (0,1)$. We need to show that

4.1
\begin{equation} \varphi _ s(x)\leq s\varphi _1(x)+(1-s)\varphi _0(x) \end{equation}

for all $x\in X$. Thanks to proposition 1.2.6, it suffices to prove this for almost all $x$.

Choose sequences $a_ j\searrow 0$ and $b_ j\nearrow 1$ such that $\varphi _{a_ j}\to \varphi _0$ and $\varphi _{b_ j}\to \varphi _1$ almost everywhere. Outside a set $Z\subseteq X$ of zero Lebesgue measure, all these convergences hold and all the values involved are finite. For $x\in X\setminus Z$ and $j$ large enough that $a_ j<s<b_ j$, the convexity on $(0,1)$ gives

\[ \varphi _ s(x)\leq \frac{b_ j-s}{b_ j-a_ j}\varphi _{a_ j}(x)+\frac{s-a_ j}{b_ j-a_ j}\varphi _{b_ j}(x). \]

Letting $j\to \infty $ gives (4.1) almost everywhere.

Let us prove the last assertion. Let

\[ \varphi \coloneqq \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t. \]

By Kiselman's principle proposition 1.2.11, we know that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $. Since the complexification $\Phi $ is not identically $-\infty $, Fubini's theorem shows that for almost every $x\in X$, the function $t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is finite for almost every $t\in (0,1)$. For such an $x$, the convexity just proved implies that $t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is a finite convex function on $(0,1)$, hence it is bounded from below on $(0,1)$. Thus $\varphi \not\equiv -\infty $, and so $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

For any $t\in (0,1)$, using the convexity established above, we have

\[ \varphi \leq (1-t)\varphi _0+t\varphi _1. \]

It follows that $\varphi \leq \varphi _0$ and $\varphi \leq \varphi _1$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. Our assertion follows. $\square$

Corollary 4.1.7. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Then

4.2
\begin{equation} \lim _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0,\quad \lim _{t\to 1-}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _1. \end{equation}

Moreover,

\[ \lim _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t=\varphi _0,\quad \lim _{t\to 1-}\varphi _ t=\varphi _1\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]
The inequalities in (4.2) are not equalities everywhere in general. For example, consider an arbitrary subgeodesic from an unbounded $\varphi _0\in \mathcal{E}(X,\omega )$ to a bounded $\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ for some Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$.

Proof. The inequalities in (4.2) and the existence of the limits follow from the convexity in proposition 4.1.6. Let us prove the quasi-everywhere equality at $t=0$. Take a decreasing sequence $t_ j\searrow 0$ with $t_ j>0$. Then $\varphi _{t_ j}\to \varphi _0$ in $L^1(X)$ as $j\to \infty $. So by corollary 1.2.8, we have

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\varphi _{t_ j}= \varphi _0\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]

Therefore, we have

\[ \varphi _0=\varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\varphi _{t_ j}=\lim _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0\quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]

The assertion at $t=1$ is similar. $\square$

Corollary 4.1.8. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

4.3
\begin{equation} \adjustlimits \sup _ X \inf _{s\in (0,1)}\varphi _ s \leq \sup _ X \varphi _ t\leq \sup _ X \varphi _0\lor \sup _ X \varphi _1 \end{equation}

for all $t\in (0,1)$.

Furthermore, $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t$ is continuous with respect to the $L^1$-topology.

In particular, in view of proposition 1.5.4, the family $\{ \varphi _ t\} _{t\in [0,1]}$ is relatively compact with respect to the $L^1$-topology on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. (4.3) follows immediately from proposition 4.1.6. Let us verify the continuity of $t\mapsto \varphi _ t$. The continuity at $t=0$ or $t=1$ follows from the definition of subgeodesic. While the continuity on $(0,1)$ follows from the convexity of $t\mapsto \varphi _ t$, and the fact that $\varphi _ t\in L^1(X)$ for each $t\in (0,1)$. $\square$

Proposition 4.1.9. Let $(\varphi ^ i_0)_{i\in I}$, $(\varphi ^ i_1)_{i\in I}$ be two non-empty uniformly bounded from above families in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let $(\varphi ^ i_ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be subgeodesics from $\varphi _0^ i$ to $\varphi _1^ i$ for each $i\in I$. Then

\[ \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi ^ i_ t\right)_{t\in (0,1)} \]

is a subgeodesic from $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_0$ to $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_1$.

Proof. We may assume that $\varphi _0^ i,\varphi _1^ i\leq 0$ for all $i\in I$. Then it follows that $\varphi _ t^ i\leq 0$ for all $t\in (0,1)$ and all $i\in I$ by proposition 4.1.6.

We define

\[ \varphi _ t\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ) \]

for all $t\in [0,1]$. Observe that $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t$ is convex by the same argument leading to (4.1).

Let $(\psi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be the subgeodesic whose complexification $\Phi _{\psi }$ corresponds to $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \Phi _{\varphi ^ i}$, where $\Phi _{\varphi ^ i}$ is the complexification of $(\varphi ^ i_ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$. Then clearly, $\varphi _ t\leq \psi _ t$ for each $t\in (0,1)$. On the other hand, by proposition 1.2.5,

\[ \psi _ t=\sup _{i\in I}\varphi ^ i_ t=\varphi _ t\quad \textup{almost everywhere} \]

for almost all $t\in (0,1)$. Therefore, using proposition 1.2.6, we find $\psi _ t=\varphi _ t$ for almost all $t\in (0,1)$. Since both functions are convex in $t$, we conclude that $\psi _ t=\varphi _ t$ for all $t\in (0,1)$.

It remains to argue that $\varphi _ t\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _0$ as $t\to 0+$ and $\varphi _ t\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _1$ as $t\to 1-$. By symmetry, it suffices to argue the former.

By Choquet's lemma proposition 1.2.3, applied to the uniformly bounded above family of complexifications $(\Phi _{\varphi ^ i})_{i\in I}$ on the strip, there is a countable subset $I'\subset I$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Phi _{\varphi ^ i}=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Phi _{\varphi ^ i}. \]

Applying Choquet's lemma proposition 1.2.3 once more to the endpoint families if necessary, and enlarging $I'$, we may also assume

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _0^ i=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _0^ i,\quad \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _1^ i=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _1^ i. \]

Thus, after replacing $I$ by $I'$, we may assume that $I$ is countable.

We know that for any $t\in (0,1)$ and any $j\in I$,

\[ \varphi ^ j_ t\leq \varphi _ t\leq t\varphi _1+(1-t)\varphi _0. \]

Letting $t\to 0+$, we find that

\[ \varphi _0^ j\leq \varlimsup _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0 \]

almost everywhere. Since $I$ is countable, we conclude that

4.4
\begin{equation} \varphi _0=\varlimsup _{t\to 0+}\varphi _ t \end{equation}

almost everywhere.

Fix $i_0\in I$. Recall that by proposition 4.1.6, for each $t\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ \adjustlimits \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\sup _ X \varphi _ t\geq \adjustlimits \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\sup _ X \varphi ^{i_0}_ t\geq \adjustlimits \sup _ X \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi ^{i_0}_ t>-\infty , \]

so the set $\{ \varphi _ t\} _{t\in (0,1)}$ is relatively compact with respect to the $L^1$-topology by proposition 1.5.4. Let $\psi $ be a cluster point as $t\to 0+$. It suffices to show that $\psi =\varphi _0$. By corollary 1.2.8 and (4.4), this holds almost everywhere. Therefore, it holds everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. $\square$

Proposition 4.1.10. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then for any $0\leq a\leq b\leq 1$, the segment $(\varphi _{t})_{t\in [a,b]}$ is a subgeodesic.

Proof. It suffices to show that

\[ \varphi _{tb+(1-t)a}\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _ a,\quad \varphi _{tb+(1-t)a}\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _ b \]

as $t\to 0+$ and $t\to 1-$ respectively. In other words, we need to show that for any $c\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ \varphi _ t\xrightarrow {L^1}\varphi _ c \]

as $t\to c$. For this purpose, observe that by proposition 4.1.6,

\[ \adjustlimits \sup _ X \inf _{s\in (0,1)}\varphi _ s\leq \sup _{X}\varphi _ t\leq \left( \sup _ X \varphi _0 \right) \lor \left( \sup _ X\varphi _1 \right) \]

for any $t\in (0,1)$. Therefore, $\{ \varphi _ t\} _{t\in (0,1)}$ is a relatively compact family with respect to the $L^1$-topology on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by proposition 1.5.4. It suffices to show that any cluster point $\psi $ of $\varphi _ t$ as $t\to c$ is equal to $\varphi _ c$. By corollary 1.2.8 and the convexity proposition 4.1.6, we have $\varphi _ c=\psi $ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. $\square$

For the purpose of this chapter, it is useful to reformulate the gluing lemma corollary 1.2.13 in the following way, which allows us to glue subgeodesics directly:

Lemma 4.1.11. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let $0\leq a<b\leq 1$ and let $(\psi _ t)_{t\in [a,b]}$ be a subgeodesic such that $\psi _ a=\varphi _ a$, $\psi _ b=\varphi _ b$, and

\[ \psi _ t\geq \varphi _ t,\quad t\in (a,b). \]

Define

\[ \gamma _ t= \begin{cases} \psi _ t,& \textup{if }t\in (a,b),\\ \varphi _ t,& \textup{if }t\in [0,1]\setminus (a,b). \end{cases} \]

Then $(\gamma _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a subgeodesic.

Proof. The $L^1$-convergence of $\gamma _ t$ to $\varphi _0$ at $0$ and $\varphi _1$ at $1$ follows immediately from the corresponding property for $(\varphi _ t)_ t$ and $(\psi _ t)_ t$. It remains to prove that the complexification of $(\gamma _ t)_ t$ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh.

Let

\[ S=\{ z\in \mathbb {C}:0<\operatorname{Re}z<1\} ,\quad S'=\{ z\in \mathbb {C}:a<\operatorname{Re}z<b\} . \]

Let $\Phi $ and $\Psi $ be the complexifications of $(\varphi _ t)_ t$ and $(\psi _ t)_ t$ on the corresponding strips. Since $\Psi \geq \Phi $ on $X\times S'$, the complexification of $(\gamma _ t)_ t$ is equal to $\Phi \lor \Psi $ on $X\times S'$ and to $\Phi $ on $X\times (S\setminus S')$. By the gluing lemma corollary 1.2.13, it is enough to verify that

\[ \varlimsup _{\substack {S'\ni z'\to z\\ x'\to x}}\Psi (x',z')\leq \Phi (x,z) \]

at every point $(x,z)\in X\times S$ with $\operatorname{Re}z=a$ or $\operatorname{Re}z=b$.

Suppose for instance that $a>0$, and let $(x_ j,z_ j)$ tend to $(x,z)$ with $\operatorname{Re}z=a$ and $a<\operatorname{Re}z_ j<b$. Set $t_ j=\operatorname{Re}z_ j$. Since $(\psi _ t)_ t$ is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _ a$ to $\varphi _ b$, the sequence $\psi _{t_ j}$ converges to $\varphi _ a$ in $L^1$. Applying corollary 1.2.8 to this sequence gives

\[ \varphi _ a=\inf _{m>0}\left(\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq m}{}\! {}^{*}\psi _{t_ j}\right). \]

For fixed $m$, the upper semicontinuity of $\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq m}{}\! {}^{*}\psi _{t_ j}$ gives

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\psi _{t_ j}(x_ j)\leq \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq m}{}\! {}^{*}\psi _{t_ j}\right)(x). \]

Letting $m\to \infty $, we get

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\psi _{t_ j}(x_ j)\leq \varphi _ a(x). \]

Since $\Phi (x,z)=\varphi _ a(x)$ when $\operatorname{Re}z=a$, this is the desired boundary inequality along $\{ \operatorname{Re}z=a\} $. The same argument applies along $\{ \operatorname{Re}z=b\} $ when $b<1$. Hence corollary 1.2.13 shows that the pasted complexification is $p_1^*\theta $-psh. $\square$

Definition 4.1.12. A ray $\ell =(\ell _ t)_{t\geq 0}$ is a subgeodesic ray in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ if for any $0\leq a\leq b$, the segment $(\ell _{t})_{t\in [a,b]}$ is a subgeodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We say $\ell $ emanates from $\ell _0$.

The complexification of a subgeodesic ray $\ell $ is defined as the potential

\[ \Phi \colon X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z>0\} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad (x,z)\mapsto \ell _{\operatorname{Re}z}(x). \]

Note that $\Phi $ is $p_1^*\theta $-psh, where $p_1\colon X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}:\operatorname{Re}z>0\} \rightarrow X$ is the natural projection.

Remark 4.1.13.As in 4.1.3, one may also define the complexification as a function $X\times \{ z\in \mathbb {C}: 0<|z|<1\} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$.

4.2 Geodesics in the space of potentials

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Fix a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. See definition 3.1.10 for the definition.

Definition 4.2.1. Let $\varphi _0, \varphi _1 \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The geodesic $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ is the family of potentials $\varphi _ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $ such that

4.7
\begin{equation} \begin{split} \varphi _ t=\operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}& \left\{ \psi _ t: (\psi _{s})_ s \textup{ is a subgeodesic from }\psi _0\textup{ to }\psi _1,\right.\\ & \psi _0,\psi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\left.\psi _0\leq \varphi _0,\psi _1\leq \varphi _1\right\} . \end{split} \end{equation}

More generally, let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [a,b]}$ ($a,b\in \mathbb {R}$, $a\leq b$) be a curve in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We say $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [a,b]}$ is a geodesic if the curve $(\varphi _{tb+(1-t)a})_{t\in (0,1)}$ is a geodesic from $\varphi _ a$ to $\varphi _ b$.

We also say $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$ is a geodesic in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ from $\varphi _ a$ to $\varphi _ b$.

The envelopes of the form (4.7) are usually referred to as the Perron envelopes. In general, the geodesic defined by (4.7) fails to have the correct limit when $t\to 0+$ or $t\to 1-$. Therefore, a priori it is not a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. In the sequel, we use the word geodesic only in situations where the endpoint behavior has been verified.

Lemma 4.2.2. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that there is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Then the geodesic $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$.

Proof. This is an immediate consequence of proposition 4.1.9. $\square$

Example 4.2.3. Let $\varphi _0\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Then the subgeodesic $(\varphi _0+tC)_{t\in [0,1]}$ studied in example 4.1.5 is a geodesic. This follows easily from proposition 4.1.6.

In particular, when $C=0$, we find that the constant subgeodesic at $\varphi _0$ is indeed a geodesic, which we call the constant geodesic at $\varphi _0$.

More generally, suppose that $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is a geodesic and $C_1,C_2\in \mathbb {R}$, then $(\varphi _ t+C_1t+C_2)_{t\in [0,1]}$ is also a geodesic. This follows immediately from example 4.1.5.

Next we want to show that under mild assumptions, there exist subgeodesics between two potentials. The assumption below turns out to be necessary as well, as we shall prove in theorem 6.1.23 later.

Proposition 4.2.4. Given $\varphi _0, \varphi _1 \in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, the geodesic $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ defined by (4.7) is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$ and $\varphi _ t\in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ for each $t\in (0,1)$.

Moreover, for any $0\leq a\leq b\leq 1$, the restriction $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [a,b]}$ is a geodesic.

If furthermore $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ (resp. $\mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$), then $\varphi _ t\in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ (resp. $\mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$) for all $t\in (0,1)$.

4.3 The metrics on the spaces of potentials and rays

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Fix a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

We first study a natural metric on $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Definition 4.3.1. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we define the Darvas metric $d_1$ as follows:

\[ d_1(\varphi ,\psi )=E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )+E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi )-2E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi ). \]

Note that by proposition 3.1.51, $\varphi \land \psi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Recall that $E_{\theta }^{\phi }$ is defined in definition 3.1.35.

In particular, if $\varphi \leq \psi $, we have

4.21
\begin{equation} d_1(\varphi ,\psi )=E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi ). \end{equation}
We wish to show that $d_1$ is a complete metric. We first prove a contraction property:

Proposition 4.3.2. Let $\varphi ,\psi ,\gamma \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

4.22
\begin{equation} d_1(\varphi ,\psi )\geq d_1(\varphi \land \gamma ,\psi \land \gamma ). \end{equation}

Proof. Step 1. We first assume that $\varphi \geq \psi $. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} d_1(\varphi ,\psi )=& E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi )\\ \geq & E_{\theta }^{\phi }\Bigl ((\varphi \land \gamma )\lor \psi \Bigr )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi ) & \textup{by \href{../../tag/93210731F7FD/}{3.1.36}}\\ \geq & E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \gamma )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi \land \gamma )& \textup{by corollary \href{../../tag/4A849C02F386/}{3.1.57}}. \end{aligned} \]

Step 2. We prove the general case.

By Step 1, we have

\[ d_1(\varphi ,\varphi \land \psi )\geq d_1(\varphi \land \gamma ,\varphi \land \psi \land \gamma ),\quad d_1(\psi ,\varphi \land \psi )\geq d_1(\psi \land \gamma ,\varphi \land \psi \land \gamma ). \]

Adding the two inequalities together, we conclude (4.22). $\square$

Lemma 4.3.3. The function $d_1$ is a metric on $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Proof. There are two facts to prove, as in the two steps below.

Step 1. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Assume that $d_1(\varphi ,\psi )=0$. Then we will show that $\varphi =\psi $.

We first treat the case $\psi \leq \varphi $. Then $d_1(\varphi ,\psi )=E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi )-E_\theta ^\phi (\psi )=0$, and proposition 3.1.45 gives

\[ \int _ X (\varphi -\psi )\, \theta _{\psi }^ n=0. \]

We conclude that $\psi =\varphi $ using the domination principle theorem 2.4.17.

In general, put $\gamma =\varphi \land \psi $. Since

\[ d_1(\varphi ,\psi )=d_1(\varphi ,\gamma )+d_1(\psi ,\gamma ), \]

both terms on the right vanish. Applying the comparable case to the pairs $(\varphi ,\gamma )$ and $(\psi ,\gamma )$ gives $\varphi =\gamma =\psi $.

Step 2. Let $\varphi ,\psi ,\gamma \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We prove the triangle inequality:

\[ d_1(\varphi ,\psi )\leq d_1(\varphi ,\gamma )+d_1(\psi ,\gamma ). \]

This can be translated to

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \gamma )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi )\leq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\gamma )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi \land \gamma ). \]

We just have to compute using proposition 4.3.2:

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\gamma )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi \land \gamma )\geq & E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \gamma )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi \land \gamma )\\ \geq & E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \gamma )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi ), \end{aligned} \]

where the second line follows from 3.1.36. $\square$

We introduce an auxiliary functional.

Definition 4.3.4. Define $I_{\theta }\colon \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )\times \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )\rightarrow \mathbb {R}\cup \{ \infty \} $ as follows:

4.28
\begin{equation} I_{\theta }(\varphi ,\psi )\coloneqq \int _ X |\varphi -\psi |\left( \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\theta _{\psi }^ n \right). \end{equation}
We will soon see that $I_{\theta }$ is always finite, see theorem 4.3.6. We observe the following elementary equality.

Lemma 4.3.5. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

\[ I_{\theta }(\varphi ,\psi )=I_{\theta }(\varphi \lor \psi ,\varphi )+I_{\theta }(\varphi \lor \psi ,\psi ). \]

Proof. By the plurifine locality of non-pluripolar products proposition 2.2.2 1, we can write

\[ \begin{aligned} I_{\theta }(\varphi ,\psi )=& \int _{ \{ \varphi <\psi \} } (\psi -\varphi ) \left( \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\theta _{\psi }^ n \right)+\int _{ \{ \varphi >\psi \} } (\varphi -\psi ) \left( \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\theta _{\psi }^ n \right),\\ I_{\theta }(\varphi \lor \psi ,\varphi )=& \int _{\{ \psi >\varphi \} } (\psi -\varphi )\left(\theta _{\psi }^ n+\theta _{\varphi }^ n \right),\\ I_{\theta }(\varphi \lor \psi ,\psi )=& \int _{\{ \psi <\varphi \} } (\varphi -\psi )\left(\theta _{\psi }^ n+\theta _{\varphi }^ n \right). \end{aligned} \]

The desired equality follows by comparing these terms. $\square$

We have an interesting relation between $d_1$ and $I_{\theta }$ defined in definition 4.3.4.

Theorem 4.3.6. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

4.32
\begin{equation} \frac{1}{C_ n} I_{\theta }(\varphi ,\psi )\leq d_1(\varphi ,\psi )\leq I_{\theta }(\varphi ,\psi ), \end{equation}

where $C_ n=3\cdot 2^{n+2}(n+1)$.

Proof. Step 1. We first prove the right-hand part of (4.32).

Thanks to proposition 3.1.46 and 3.1.3, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi )\leq & \int _ X (\varphi -\varphi \land \psi )\, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n \\ \leq & \int _{\{ \psi =\varphi \land \psi \} } (\varphi -\psi )\, \theta _{\psi }^ n\\ \leq & \int _ X |\varphi -\psi |\, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Similarly,

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi )\leq \int _ X |\varphi -\psi |\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

Adding these inequalities up, we find

\[ d_1(\varphi ,\psi )\leq I_{\theta }(\varphi ,\psi ). \]

Step 2. We prove the left-hand part of (4.32).

We claim that

4.36
\begin{equation} d_1\left(\varphi ,\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\leq \frac{3(n+1)}{2} d_1(\varphi ,\psi ). \end{equation}

For this purpose, we compute directly

\[ \begin{aligned} & d_1\left(\varphi ,\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\\ =& d_1\left(\varphi ,\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)+d_1\left(\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2},\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\\ \leq & d_1\left(\varphi ,\varphi \land \psi \right)+d_1\left(\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2},\varphi \land \psi \right)& \textup{by \href{../../tag/93210731F7FD/}{3.1.36}}\\ \leq & \int _ X \left( \varphi -\varphi \land \psi \right)\, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n+ \int _ X \left( \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}-\varphi \land \psi \right)\, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n & \textup{by proposition \href{../../tag/51CFFEE97C34/}{3.1.46}}\\ =& \frac{3}{2}\int _ X \left( \varphi -\varphi \land \psi \right)\, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n+\frac{1}{2}\int _ X \left( \psi -\varphi \land \psi \right)\, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n \\ \leq & \frac{3(n+1)}{2}d_1\left(\varphi ,\varphi \land \psi \right) + \frac{n+1}{2}d_1(\psi ,\varphi \land \psi )& \textup{by (\href{../../tag/F8225060CBAC/}{3.110})}\\ \leq & \frac{3(n+1)}{2}d_1(\varphi ,\psi ), \end{aligned} \]

and (4.36) follows.

We now estimate the left-hand side of (4.36):

\[ \begin{aligned} d_1\left(\varphi ,\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\geq & d_1\left(\varphi ,\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\\ \geq & \int _ X \left(\varphi -\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n\\ \geq & \int _{\{ \varphi >\psi \} }\left(\varphi -\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n \\ \geq & \int _{\{ \varphi >\psi \} }\left(\varphi -\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n\\ =& \frac{1}{2}\int _{\{ \varphi >\psi \} }\left(\varphi -\psi \right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where the second inequality follows from proposition 3.1.46.

Similarly,

\[ \begin{aligned} d_1\left(\varphi ,\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\geq & d_1\left(\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2},\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\\ \geq & \int _ X \left(\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}-\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\, \theta _{(\varphi +\psi )/2}^ n\\ \geq & 2^{-n} \int _ X \left(\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}-\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n\\ \geq & 2^{-n} \int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } \left(\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}-\varphi \land \frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n\\ \geq & 2^{-n} \int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } \left(\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}-\varphi \right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n\\ =& 2^{-n-1} \int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } \left(\psi -\varphi \right)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where the third inequality follows from the multi-linearity of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 6.

Adding these estimates up, we find

\[ 2^{n+2} d_1\left(\varphi ,\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\geq \left(2^{n+1}+2\right) d_1\left(\varphi ,\frac{\varphi +\psi }{2}\right)\geq \int _ X |\varphi -\psi |\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

Therefore, combined with (4.36) we conclude

\[ 3\cdot 2^{n+1}(n+1)d_1(\varphi ,\psi )\geq \int _ X |\varphi -\psi |\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

By symmetry, we also get a similar expression after exchanging $\varphi $ and $\psi $. Adding these inequalities together, we find

\[ 3\cdot 2^{n+2}(n+1)d_1(\varphi ,\psi )\geq I_{\theta }(\varphi ,\psi ). \]

The left-hand part of (4.32) then follows. $\square$

Lemma 4.3.8. There is $A,B>0$ so that for any $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we have

\begin{equation} d_1(\varphi ,\phi )\geq -\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n\cdot \sup _ X \varphi \geq -Ad_1(\varphi ,\phi )-B. \end{equation}

Proof. When $\sup _ X \varphi \leq 0$, the right-hand part of (4.56) is trivial. Since $\varphi \leq \phi $, we find

where the last equality follows from proposition 3.1.13.

\[ \theta _{\phi }^ n\leq C\omega ^ n. \]

Next we handle the completeness of $d_1$. The completeness can be proved in a more general framework.

Definition 4.3.9. Let $E$ be a set. A pre-rooftop structure on $E$ is a binary operator $\land :E\times E\rightarrow E$, satisfying the following axioms: For $x,y,z\in E$,

  1. $x\land y=y\land x$.

  2. $(x\land y)\land z=x\land (y\land z)$.

  3. $x\land x=x$.

We call $(E,\land )$ a pre-rooftop space.

A pre-rooftop structure $\land $ defines a partial order $\leq $ on $E$ as follows:

\[ x\leq y\quad \text{if and only if } \quad x\land y=x. \]

Here by abuse of notation, we use $\leq $ to denote the partial order.

In particular, it makes sense to talk about increasing and decreasing sequences in $E$.

Definition 4.3.10. Let $(E,d)$ be a metric space. A pre-rooftop structure on $(E,d)$ is a pre-rooftop structure $\land $ on $E$. We say $(E,d,\land )$ is a pre-rooftop metric space.

A rooftop structure on $(E,d)$ is a pre-rooftop structure $\land $ on $E$ such that

4.60
\begin{equation} d(x\land z,y\land z)\leq d(x,y),\quad \forall x,y,z\in E. \end{equation}

We call $(E,d,\land )$ a rooftop metric space.

Lemma 4.3.11. Let $(E,d,\land )$ be a rooftop metric space. Let $x,y,x',y'\in E$. Then

4.61
\begin{equation} d(x\land y,x'\land y')\leq d(x,x')+d(y,y'). \end{equation}
In particular, $\land \colon E\times E\rightarrow E$ is continuous.

Proof. We compute

\[ d(x\land y,x'\land y')\leq d(x\land y,x\land y')+d(x\land y',x'\land y')\leq d(x,x')+d(y,y'). \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Proposition 4.3.12. Let $(E,d,\land )$ be a rooftop metric space. Then $(E,d)$ is complete if and only if both of the following hold:

  1. Each increasing Cauchy sequence converges.

  2. Each decreasing Cauchy sequence converges.

Proof. The direct implication is trivial.

Conversely, assume that both conditions are true. Let $(x_ j)_{j>0}$ be a Cauchy sequence in $E$. We want to prove that $(x_ j)_ j$ converges. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\[ d\left(x_ j,x_{j+1}\right)\leq 2^{-j}. \]

For $k,j\geq 1$, let

\[ y_ j^ k\coloneqq x_ k\land \cdots \land x_{k+j}. \]

Then $(y_ j^ k)_ j$ is decreasing, and

\[ d\left(y_ j^ k,y_{j+1}^ k\right)\leq d\left(x_{k+j},x_{k+j+1}\right)\leq 2^{-k-j}. \]

So $(y_ j^ k)_ j$ is a decreasing Cauchy sequence. Define

\[ y^ k\coloneqq \lim _{j\to \infty }y_ j^ k. \]

Then

\[ d\left(y^ k,y^{k+1}\right)=\lim _{j\to \infty }d\left(y_{j+1}^ k,y_ j^{k+1}\right)\leq d\left(x_ k,x_{k+1}\right)\leq 2^{-k}. \]

We also need to check that the sequence $(y^ k)_ k$ is increasing. For each $j\geq 1$, we have

\[ y_ j^ k=x_ k\land y_{j-1}^{k+1}\leq y_{j-1}^{k+1}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ y_ j^ k\land y_{j-1}^{k+1}=y_ j^ k. \]

Letting $j\to \infty $ and using the continuity of $\land $ from 4.3.11, we obtain

\[ y^ k\land y^{k+1}=y^ k. \]

Thus $y^ k\leq y^{k+1}$. So $(y^ k)_ k$ is an increasing Cauchy sequence. Let

\[ y\coloneqq \lim _{k\to \infty }y^ k. \]

For every $j\geq 0$, we have

\[ d\left(y_ j^ k,x_ k\right)\leq \sum _{r=k}^{k+j-1}d\left(x_ r,x_{r+1}\right), \]

with the empty sum understood as $0$. Indeed, this is trivial for $j=0$, and the induction step follows from

\[ y_ j^ k=x_ k\land y_{j-1}^{k+1} \]

and

\[ d\left(y_ j^ k,x_ k\right)\leq d\left(y_{j-1}^{k+1},x_ k\right)\leq d\left(y_{j-1}^{k+1},x_{k+1}\right)+d\left(x_{k+1},x_ k\right). \]

Letting $j\to \infty $, we get

\[ d\left(y^ k,x_ k\right)\leq \sum _{r=k}^{\infty }d\left(x_ r,x_{r+1}\right)\leq 2^{1-k}. \]

So $(x_ k)_ k$ converges to $y$. $\square$

Theorem 4.3.13. The metric space $(\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi ),d_1)$ is complete.

It follows from the proof below that if $(\varphi _ j)_{j>0}$ is a monotone sequence in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ with $d_1$-limit $\varphi $, then $\varphi $ is the almost everywhere limit of $(\varphi _ j)_{j>0}$. We will use this result without further explanation in the sequel.

Proof. As we have seen in 4.3.3 and proposition 4.3.2, the triple

\[ \left(\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi ),d_1,\land \right) \]

is a rooftop metric space. Hence thanks to proposition 4.3.12, it remains to show that each increasing or decreasing Cauchy sequence in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ converges.

We first consider an increasing sequence $(\varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. The Cauchy property simply means that

\[ \sup _{i>0} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ i)<\infty . \]

We claim that

\[ \varphi \coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i>0}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i \]

is the $d_1$-limit of the sequence. We first observe that $(\sup _ X \varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ is bounded, as a consequence of 4.3.8. Therefore, proposition 3.1.53 guarantees that $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$, and hence our assertion follows from proposition 3.1.47.

Next we consider a decreasing sequence $(\varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. The Cauchy property simply means that

\[ \inf _{i>0} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ i)>-\infty . \]

We claim that

\[ \varphi \coloneqq \inf _{i>0}\varphi _ i \]

is the $d_1$-limit of the sequence. Equivalently, we need to show that $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and

4.62
\begin{equation} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )=\lim _{i\to \infty } E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ i). \end{equation}

We first observe that $(\sup _ X \varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ is bounded, as a consequence of 4.3.8. Therefore, $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and $\varphi _ i\xlongrightarrow {L^1} \varphi $, see corollary 1.5.6. We claim that $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Lemma 4.3.14. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ be a sequence in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ with $d_1$-limit $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then after replacing $(\varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ by a subsequence, we can find two sequences $(\psi _ i)_{i>0}$ and $(\eta _ i)_{i>0}$ in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ such that

  1. $(\psi _ i)_{i>0}$ is decreasing with $d_1$ and pointwise limit $\varphi $;

  2. $(\eta _ i)_{i>0}$ is increasing with $d_1$ and almost everywhere limit $\varphi $.

  3. $\eta _ i\leq \varphi _ i\leq \psi _ i$ for all $i>0$.

Proof. We first note that as a consequence of 4.3.8, $(\sup _ X \varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ is bounded. Let $C_ n$ be the constant in theorem 4.3.6. After replacing $(\varphi _ i)_{i>0}$ by a subsequence, we may assume that

\[ d_1(\varphi _ i,\varphi _{i+1})\leq \min \{ C_ n^{-2i},2^{-i}\} . \]

We first construct $(\psi _ i)_{i>0}$. For this purpose, it suffices to define

\[ \psi _ i\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq i}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi _ j \]

for each $i>0$. It follows from proposition 3.1.53 that $\psi _ i\in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ for each $i>0$. For $k\geq 0$, set

\[ \psi _ i^ k\coloneqq \varphi _ i\lor \varphi _{i+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{i+k}. \]

Iterating corollary 4.3.7 gives

\[ d_1(\varphi _ i,\psi _ i^ k)\leq \sum _{a=i}^{i+k-1}C_ n^{a+1-i}d_1(\varphi _ a,\varphi _{a+1})\leq \sum _{a=i}^{i+k-1}C_ n^{a+1-i}C_ n^{-2a}. \]

Since $\psi _ i^ k$ increases almost everywhere to $\psi _ i$, letting $k\to \infty $ and using proposition 3.1.47, we get

\[ d_1(\varphi _ i,\psi _ i)\leq \frac{C_ n^{1-2i}}{1-C_ n^{-1}}. \]

Thus $\psi _ i\to \varphi $ in $d_1$. Since $(\psi _ i)_ i$ is decreasing, the monotone case in the proof of theorem 4.3.13 shows that $\psi _ i$ converges almost everywhere to $\varphi $, hence pointwise by proposition 1.2.6.

Next we construct $(\eta _ i)_{i>0}$. For each $i>0$ and $k\geq 0$, we let

\[ \eta _ i^ k\coloneqq \varphi _ i\land \varphi _{i+1}\land \cdots \land \varphi _{i+k}. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} d_1(\varphi _ i,\eta _ i^ k)\leq & \sum _{j=0}^{k-1} d_1\left(\eta _ i^ j,\eta _ i^{j+1}\right)\\ \leq & \sum _{j=0}^{k-1} d_1\left(\varphi _{i+j},\varphi _{i+j+1}\right) & \textup{by proposition \href{../../tag/61DD221912DE/}{4.3.2}}\\ \leq & \sum _{j=0}^{k-1} 2^{-i-j}\\ \leq & 2^{1-i}. \end{aligned} \]

Since $\eta _ i^ k\leq \varphi _ i$, the preceding estimate gives

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\eta _ i^ k)\geq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ i)-2^{1-i},\quad k\geq 0. \]

The sequence $(\eta _ i^ k)_{k\geq 0}$ is decreasing, and the monotonicity of $E_{\theta }^{\phi }$ shows that the sequence $\left(E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\eta _ i^ k)\right)_{k\geq 0}$ is decreasing and bounded from below, hence convergent. Thus, for $k\leq l$,

\[ d_1(\eta _ i^ k,\eta _ i^ l)=E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\eta _ i^ k)-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\eta _ i^ l), \]

and $(\eta _ i^ k)_{k\geq 0}$ is a decreasing Cauchy sequence. Therefore, the monotone case in the proof of theorem 4.3.13 shows that

\[ \eta _ i\coloneqq \lim _{k\to \infty }\eta _ i^ k=\inf _{k\geq 0} \eta _ i^ k \]

is the $d_1$-limit of $(\eta _ i^ k)_{k\geq 0}$ and proposition 3.1.47 shows that

\[ d_1(\varphi _ i,\eta _ i)\leq 2^{1-i}. \]

By construction, $\eta _ i\leq \varphi _ i$ and $(\eta _ i)_ i$ is increasing. Therefore, $\varphi $ is the $d_1$-limit of the increasing sequence $(\eta _ i)_{i>0}$. As we have seen in the proof of theorem 4.3.13, this implies that $\varphi $ is also the almost everywhere limit of $(\eta _ i)_{i>0}$. $\square$

when the endpoints belong to $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Step 3. Let now $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. By example 4.2.3 and (3.71), we may add different constants to the endpoints and assume that $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\leq \phi $. Put

\[ \varphi _ j^{(k)}=\varphi _ j\lor (\phi -k),\qquad j=0,1, \]

and let $(\varphi _ t^{(k)})_{t\in [0,1]}$ be the corresponding geodesic. The endpoint truncations decrease to $\varphi _ j$, and Perron monotonicity gives

\[ \varphi _ t\leq \varphi _ t^{(k+1)}\leq \varphi _ t^{(k)}. \]

Let $h_ t=\lim _{k\to \infty }\varphi _ t^{(k)}$. The decreasing limit of the complexifications is $p_1^*\theta $-psh and is not identically $-\infty $. Taking the limit in the convex upper bound gives, first almost everywhere and then everywhere by proposition 1.2.6,

4.86
\begin{equation} \varphi _ t\leq h_ t\leq (1-t)\varphi _0+t\varphi _1. \end{equation}

Note that

\[ |h_ t-\varphi _0| \leq |\varphi _ t-\varphi _0|+t|\varphi _1-\varphi _0|\quad \textup{a.e.}. \]

Since $\varphi _ t\to \varphi _0$ in $L^1$ as $t\to 0+$ and $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in L^1(X)$, it follows that $h_ t\to \varphi _0$ in $L^1$. The same argument at $t=1$ gives $h_ t\to \varphi _1$ in $L^1$. Together with the $p_1^*\theta $-psh complexification constructed above, these endpoint limits show by definition 4.1.1 that $(h_ t)$ is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Hence (4.7) gives $h_ t\leq \varphi _ t$. Together with (4.86), this proves

\[ \varphi _ t^{(k)}\searrow \varphi _ t,\qquad 0<t<1. \]

Applying proposition 3.1.47 at the endpoints and at each interior slice, and then using (4.85), gives

\[ \begin{aligned} E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _ t) & =\lim _{k\to \infty }E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _ t^{(k)})\\ & =\lim _{k\to \infty }\left((1-t)E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _0^{(k)}) +tE_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _1^{(k)})\right)\\ & =(1-t)E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _0)+tE_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _1). \end{aligned} \]

This concludes the proof. $\square$

Proposition 4.3.17. Let $(\varphi ^ i_0)_{i\in I}$, $(\varphi ^ i_1)_{i\in I}$ be two uniformly bounded from above increasing nets in $\mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Let $(\varphi ^ i_ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be the geodesic from $\varphi _0^ i$ to $\varphi _1^ i$ for each $i\in I$. Then

\[ \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi ^ i_ t\right)_{t\in (0,1)} \]

is the geodesic from $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_0$ to $\operatorname *{sup}_{i}{}\! {}^{*} \varphi ^ i_1$.

Proof. Put

\[ \varphi _ j=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ j^ i\quad (j=0,1), \qquad h_ t=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ t^ i, \]

and let $(g_ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be the geodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. By proposition 3.1.53, $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. The slice families are uniformly bounded from above by proposition 4.1.6; hence $h_ t\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ by proposition 3.1.53. Moreover, proposition 4.1.9 shows that $(h_ t)$ is a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Thus

4.90
\begin{equation} h_ t\leq g_ t. \end{equation}

For each $t$, the net $(\varphi _ t^ i)_{i\in I}$ is increasing. By proposition 1.2.5, its pointwise supremum agrees with $h_ t$ almost everywhere, and the analogous assertion holds at the endpoints. Therefore proposition 3.1.47, proposition 4.3.16 gives

\[ \begin{aligned} E_\theta ^\phi (h_ t) & =\lim _{i\in I}E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _ t^ i)\\ & =\lim _{i\in I}\left((1-t)E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _0^ i) +tE_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _1^ i)\right)\\ & =(1-t)E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _0)+tE_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _1) =E_\theta ^\phi (g_ t). \end{aligned} \]

By (4.21), (4.90), and 4.3.3,

\[ d_1(h_ t,g_ t)=E_\theta ^\phi (g_ t)-E_\theta ^\phi (h_ t)=0, \]

so $h_ t=g_ t$ for all $t\in (0,1)$. $\square$

Proposition 4.3.18. Let $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$, $(\psi _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be geodesics in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then the distance $d_1(\varphi _ t,\psi _ t)$ is a convex function of $t\in [0,1]$.

Proof. By definition of $d_1$ and proposition 4.3.16, it suffices to show the concavity of

\[ [0,1]\ni t\mapsto E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ t\land \psi _ t). \]

Let $(\eta _ t)_{t\in [0,1]}$ be the geodesic from $\varphi _0\land \psi _0$ to $\varphi _1\land \psi _1$. Then for each $t\in [0,1]$, we have $\eta _ t\leq \varphi _ t\land \psi _ t$. Hence, by the monotonicity of $E_\theta ^\phi $ and proposition 4.3.16,

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }( \varphi _ t\land \psi _ t)\geq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\eta _ t)=(1-t)E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _0\land \psi _0)+tE_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _1\land \psi _1) \]

for each $t\in [0,1]$. Applying the same argument to the restrictions of $(\varphi _ t)_ t$ and $(\psi _ t)_ t$ to any subinterval of $[0,1]$, which are again geodesics by proposition 4.2.4, gives a similar inequality on every subinterval. Hence $t\mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (\varphi _ t\land \psi _ t)$ is concave, and our assertion follows. $\square$

In particular, we can introduce:

Definition 4.3.19. Let $\ell ,\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We define

\[ d_1(\ell ,\ell ')\coloneqq \lim _{t\to \infty }\frac{1}{t}d_1(\ell _ t,\ell '_ t). \]

The limit exists in $[0,\infty ]$ by proposition 4.3.18.

Theorem 4.3.20. The function $d_1$ defined in definition 4.3.19 is a metric on $\mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Proof. Symmetry and the triangle inequality follow from the corresponding pointwise properties of $d_1$ on $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

We next show that $d_1(\ell ,\ell ')<\infty $ for any $\ell ,\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Let $C_\ell $ be the constant in proposition 4.2.9, and set $\tilde{\ell }_ t=\ell _ t-C_\ell t$. Then $\tilde{\ell }$ is again a geodesic ray, $\sup _ X\tilde{\ell }_ t=0$, and hence $\tilde{\ell }_ t\leq \phi $ by proposition 3.1.13. Thus, by proposition 4.3.16,

\[ d_1(\tilde{\ell }_ t,\phi )=-E_\theta ^\phi (\tilde{\ell }_ t)=-tE_\theta ^\phi (\tilde{\ell }_1). \]

Moreover, by (3.71), $d_1(\ell _ t,\tilde{\ell }_ t)=|C_\ell |t\int _ X\theta _{\phi }^ n$. Hence $d_1(\ell _ t,\phi )$ grows at most linearly in $t$. The same argument applies to $\ell '$, and the triangle inequality gives $d_1(\ell ,\ell ')<\infty $.

It remains to prove separation: suppose that $\ell ,\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and $d_1(\ell ,\ell ')=0$.

Fix $s>0$. Then it follows from proposition 4.3.18 that

\[ \frac{d_1(\ell _ s,\ell '_ s)}{s}\leq \lim _{t\to \infty }\frac{d_1(\ell _ t,\ell '_ t)}{t}=d_1(\ell ,\ell ')=0. \]

Therefore, $\ell _ s=\ell '_ s$ and hence $\ell =\ell '$. $\square$

Definition 4.3.21. We define the radial Monge–Ampère energy $\mathbf{E}^{\phi }\colon \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )\rightarrow \mathbb {R}\cup \{ \pm \infty \} $ as follows:

\[ \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell )\coloneqq \varlimsup _{t\to \infty }\frac{E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell _ t)}{t}. \]

When $\phi =V_{\theta }$, we write $\mathbf{E}$ instead of $\mathbf{E}^{V_{\theta }}$.

Proposition 4.3.22. Let $\ell ,\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and $\ell \leq \ell '$. Then

4.94
\begin{equation} d_1(\ell ,\ell ')=\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell ')-\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell ). \end{equation}

Proof. By proposition 4.3.16, the energy is linear along finite energy rays. Hence

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell _ t)=tE_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell _1),\quad E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell '_ t)=tE_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell '_1). \]

Since $\ell _ t\leq \ell '_ t$ for all $t\geq 0$, (4.21) gives

\[ \frac{1}{t}d_1(\ell _ t,\ell '_ t)=\frac{1}{t}\left(E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell '_ t)-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell _ t)\right). \]

Letting $t\to \infty $ proves the assertion. $\square$

Next we recall the $\lor $ operator at the level of geodesic rays.

Definition 4.3.23. Let $\ell ,\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We define $\ell \lor \ell '$ as the minimal ray in $\mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$ lying above both $\ell $ and $\ell '$.

Proposition 4.3.24. Given $\ell ,\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then $\ell \lor \ell '\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$ exists, and

4.95
\begin{equation} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell \lor \ell ')=\lim _{t\to \infty }\frac{1}{t}E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t\right). \end{equation}

Proof. For each $t>0$, let $(\ell ''^ t_{s})_{s\in [0,t]}$ be the geodesic from $\phi $ to $\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t$.

Step 1. We first show that for each fixed $s\geq 0$, $\ell ''^ t_{s}$ is increasing in $t\in [s,\infty )$.

To see this, fix $s\geq 0$ and choose $t'>t\geq s$. We need to show that

4.96
\begin{equation} \ell ''^{t'}_ s\geq \ell ''^{t}_ s. \end{equation}

Since $(\ell ''^{t'}_ a)_{a\in [0,t]}$ is a geodesic, it suffices to show that $(\ell ''^ t_ a)_{a\in [0,t]}$ is a candidate in the Perron envelope defining the former geodesic. In other words, in verifying (4.96), we may assume that either $s=0$ or $s=t$. The case $s=0$ is of course trivial. So it remains to prove the following:

\[ \ell ''^{t'}_ t\geq \ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t. \]

By symmetry, it suffices to prove

\[ \ell ''^{t'}_ t\geq \ell _ t. \]

But since $(\ell _ a)_{a\in [0,t']}$ is a candidate in the Perron envelope defining $\ell ''^{t'}$, this inequality follows.

Step 2. Next, observe that for a fixed $s\geq 0$, we have

\[ \sup _ X \ell ''^ t_ s\leq \frac{s}{t}\sup _ X\ell ''^ t_ t+ \frac{t-s}{t}\sup _ X \ell ''^ t_0=\frac{s}{t}\left( \sup _ X\ell _ t \right)\lor \left( \sup _ X\ell '_ t \right) \]

for all $t>s$. The right-hand side is bounded from above by a constant independent of $t\geq s$ by proposition 4.2.9. Let

4.97
\begin{equation} (\ell \lor \ell ')_ s\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{t>s}{}\! {}^{*} \ell ''^ t_{s}. \end{equation}

Then proposition 4.3.17 guarantees that $\ell \lor \ell '\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Step 3. We need to show that $\ell \lor \ell '$ defined in this way is indeed the minimal ray lying above $\ell $ and $\ell '$.

First, by Step 1, we have

\[ \ell ''^ t_ s\geq \ell ''^ s_ s\geq \ell _ s \]

for any $t\geq s\geq 0$. Therefore,

\[ (\ell \lor \ell ')_ s\geq \ell _ s \]

for all $s\geq 0$. In other words, $\ell \lor \ell '\geq \ell $. Similarly, $\ell \lor \ell '\geq \ell '$.

Next, let $L\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$ be a ray lying above both $\ell $ and $\ell '$. Then we have

\[ L_ t\geq \ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t \]

for all $t\geq 0$. In particular,

\[ L_ s\geq \ell ''^ t_ s \]

for all $t\geq s\geq 0$. It follows that

\[ L_ s\geq (\ell \lor \ell ')_ s \]

for all $s\geq 0$.

Step 4. It remains to argue (4.95). Since the energy is linear along bounded rays,

\[ \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell \lor \ell ')=E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left((\ell \lor \ell ')_1\right). \]

Moreover, $\ell ''^ t_1$ increases to $(\ell \lor \ell ')_1$ as $t\to \infty $. By proposition 3.1.47,

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left((\ell \lor \ell ')_1\right)=\lim _{t\to \infty } E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\ell ''^ t_1\right). \]

Finally, by proposition 4.3.16 applied to $(\ell ''^ t_ s)_{s\in [0,t]}$,

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\ell ''^ t_1\right)=\frac{1}{t}E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t\right). \]

Combining these identities gives (4.95). $\square$

Lemma 4.3.25. For any $\ell ,\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we have

4.98
\begin{equation} d_1(\ell ,\ell ')\leq d_1(\ell ,\ell \lor \ell ')+d_1(\ell ',\ell \lor \ell ')\leq C_ nd_1(\ell ,\ell '), \end{equation}

where $C_ n=3(n+1)2^{n+2}$.

Proof. The first inequality is trivial. As for the second, we estimate

\[ \begin{aligned} d_1(\ell ,\ell \lor \ell ')=& \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell \lor \ell ')-\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell )& \textup{by proposition \href{../../tag/3A33DCAAE061/}{4.3.22}}\\ =& \lim _{t\to \infty } \frac{1}{t}E_{\theta }^{\phi }\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t\right)-\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell )& \textup{by (\href{../../tag/CA6EEFF029A5/}{4.95})}\\ =& \lim _{t\to \infty } \frac{1}{t} d_1\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t,\ell _ t\right)& \textup{by (\href{../../tag/D8C8C0BB6585/}{4.21})}. \end{aligned} \]

By symmetry, we find

\[ d_1(\ell ,\ell \lor \ell ')+d_1(\ell ',\ell \lor \ell ')\leq \lim _{t\to \infty } \frac{1}{t} \Bigl (d_1\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t,\ell _ t\right)+d_1\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t,\ell '_ t\right)\Bigr ). \]

By theorem 4.3.6 and 4.3.5, for each $t>0$,

\[ d_1\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t,\ell _ t\right)+d_1\left(\ell _ t\lor \ell '_ t,\ell '_ t\right)\leq 3(n+1)2^{n+2} d_1(\ell _ t,\ell '_ t). \]

Now (4.98) follows. $\square$

Example 4.3.26. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\varphi \leq 0$. For each $C>0$, let $(\ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t)_{t\in [0,C]}$ be the geodesic from $V_{\theta }$ to $(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi $. For each $t\geq 0$, there is $\ell ^{\varphi }_ t\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta )$ such that

4.102
\begin{equation} \ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t\xrightarrow {d_1}\ell ^{\varphi }_ t \end{equation}

as $C\to \infty $. Then $\ell ^{\varphi }\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta )$ and

4.103
\begin{equation} \mathbf{E}(\ell ^{\varphi })=\frac{1}{n+1} \sum _{j=0}^ n \left(\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n\right). \end{equation}
Comparing the above approximants with cutoff parameters shifted by a bounded amount shows that $\ell ^{\varphi +C}=\ell ^{\varphi }$ for any $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Therefore, for a general $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we may simply define
\[ \ell ^{\varphi }\coloneqq \ell ^{\varphi -\sup _ X\varphi }. \]

Then the conclusions of this example continue to hold.

Proof. We first show that for each fixed $t\geq 0$, $\ell _ t^{\varphi ,C}$ is increasing in $C\geq t$.

To see this, choose $t\leq C_1<C_2$. We need to show that

\[ \ell ^{\varphi ,C_1}_ t\leq \ell ^{\varphi ,C_2}_ t. \]

Since both sides are geodesics for $t\in [0,C_1]$, it suffices to show that

4.104
\begin{equation} (V_{\theta }-C_1)\lor \varphi \leq \ell _{C_1}^{\varphi ,C_2}. \end{equation}
Now $((V_{\theta }-t)\lor \varphi )_{t\in [0,C_2]}$ is a subgeodesic from $V_{\theta }$ to $(V_{\theta }-C_2)\lor \varphi $ by proposition 4.1.9.
Note. Here we need $\varphi \leq 0$.
It is therefore a candidate in the Perron envelope defining $\ell ^{\varphi ,C_2}$, and evaluating at $t=C_1$ gives exactly (4.104).

From proposition 4.1.6, we know that for any $C>t>0$, we have

\[ \ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t\leq \frac{t}{C}\Bigl ((V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi \Bigr )+\frac{C-t}{C}\cdot V_{\theta }\leq 0, \]

while the linear subgeodesic $V_\theta -t$ is a candidate in the same Perron envelope, so $V_\theta -t\leq \ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t$. Therefore, by proposition 1.2.2,

4.105
\begin{equation} \ell _ t^{\varphi }\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{C>t}{}\! {}^{*}\ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ) \end{equation}

for all $t\geq 0$. Thanks to proposition 3.1.47, we have

\[ \ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t\xrightarrow {d_1} \ell _ t^{\varphi } \]

as $C\to \infty $ for all $t\geq 0$. Applying proposition 4.3.17 on each interval $[0,T]$ to the increasing family $(\ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t)_{t\in [0,T]}$, $C\geq T$, shows that $\ell ^{\varphi }\in \mathcal{R}^{\infty }(X,\theta )$.

It remains to compute the energy of $\ell ^{\varphi }$. We first fix $C\geq t>0$ and compute using proposition 4.3.16:

\[ E_{\theta }\left(\ell ^{\varphi ,C}_ t\right)=\frac{t}{C}E_{\theta }\Bigl ((V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi \Bigr ). \]

Letting $C\to \infty $ and applying proposition 3.1.47, we find that

\[ E_{\theta }(\ell ^{\varphi }_ t)=\lim _{C\to \infty }\frac{t}{C}E_{\theta }\Bigl ((V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi \Bigr ) \]

for any $t\geq 0$. It follows that

\[ \mathbf{E}(\ell ^{\varphi })=\lim _{C\to \infty }\frac{1}{C}E_{\theta }\Bigl ((V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi \Bigr ). \]

Using the definition of $E_{\theta }$, in order to obtain (4.103), it suffices to show that for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$, we have

4.106
\begin{equation} \lim _{C\to \infty }\int _ X \frac{(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi -V_{\theta }}{C} \, \theta _{(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^{j}\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n. \end{equation}

For this purpose, for each $C>0$, we decompose $X$ as $\{ \varphi > V_{\theta }-C\} $ and $\{ \varphi \leq V_{\theta }-C\} $. We have

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _{\{ \varphi > V_{\theta }-C\} } \frac{(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi -V_{\theta }}{C} \, \theta _{(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\\ =& \int _{\{ \varphi > V_{\theta }-C\} } \frac{\varphi -V_{\theta }}{C} \, \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \end{aligned} \]

On the other hand,

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _{\{ \varphi \leq V_{\theta }-C\} } \frac{(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi -V_{\theta }}{C} \, \theta _{(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\\ =& -\int _{\{ \varphi \leq V_{\theta }-C\} } \theta _{(V_{\theta }-C)\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\\ =& -\int _ X \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n+\int _{\{ \varphi > V_{\theta }-C\} } \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \end{aligned} \]

Observe that for $C>0$, the function $\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi >V_{\theta }-C\} }C^{-1}(\varphi -V_{\theta })$ is defined quasi-everywhere. Since $\varphi \leq V_\theta $, it takes values in $[-1,0]$ outside a pluripolar set. When $C\to \infty $, these functions converge to $0$ quasi-everywhere, so the first integral above converges to $0$ by dominated convergence. Similarly, the indicators $\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi >V_\theta -C\} }$ increase to $1$ outside a pluripolar set, hence

\[ \lim _{C\to \infty }\int _{\{ \varphi >V_{\theta }-C\} } \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j} = \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \]

Therefore, (4.106) follows. $\square$
