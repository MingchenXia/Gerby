10.29 Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds9F59E018A291

9F59E018A291

The compact manifolds studied so far in this chapter carry many closed positive currents, coming from divisors, fibrations or blowups. Inoue surfaces and their higher-dimensional analogues, the Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds, behave very differently. They are built from number fields, contain few or no complex subvarieties, and carry a natural closed semipositive $(1,1)$-form whose kernel is a holomorphic foliation by complex affine spaces. These leaves are Liouville and fill out real tori. As a result, every potential in this class is, up to a fixed logarithmic term, a continuous convex function of the transverse coordinates, so the class has no singular potentials at all, even though it contains positive currents supported on real hypersurfaces. In dimension three, a natural Gauduchon metric has an exactly quadratic mass functional of indefinite sign. Its masses lie on both sides of the background mass. These threefolds carry no Hermitian metric whose $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ has a sign, so the known curvature criteria for bounded masses do not apply, and whether the masses remain bounded is an open question (24.4.33).

10.29.1 The constructionCA83ED725CCA

Let $K$ be a number field of degree $s+2t$, with $s\geq 1$ real embeddings $\sigma _1,\ldots ,\sigma _ s$ and $t\geq 1$ pairs of complex conjugate embeddings $\sigma _{s+k},\overline{\sigma }_{s+k}$, $1\leq k\leq t$. Write $\mathcal O_ K$ for its ring of integers and $\mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ for the group of totally positive units, that is, of units $\varepsilon $ with $\sigma _ j(\varepsilon )>0$ for $1\leq j\leq s$. Let $\mathbb H\subset \mathbb C$ be the upper half-plane. Write points of $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$ as $(w,z)$, with $w_ j=x_ j+\mathrm i y_ j$, and put

\[ \sigma _{\mathbb R}(a)=(\sigma _1(a),\ldots ,\sigma _ s(a)),\qquad \sigma _{\mathbb C}(a)=(\sigma _{s+1}(a),\ldots ,\sigma _{s+t}(a)). \]

An element $a\in \mathcal O_ K$ acts by the translation $(w,z)\mapsto (w+\sigma _{\mathbb R}(a),z+\sigma _{\mathbb C}(a))$, and $\varepsilon \in \mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ acts by

\[ (w,z)\longmapsto \bigl (\sigma _1(\varepsilon )w_1,\ldots ,\sigma _ s(\varepsilon )w_ s,\, \sigma _{s+1}(\varepsilon )z_1,\ldots ,\sigma _{s+t}(\varepsilon )z_ t\bigr ). \]

A subgroup $U\subset \mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ is admissible if its image

\[ \Lambda _ U=\{ (\log \sigma _1(\varepsilon ),\ldots ,\log \sigma _ s(\varepsilon )):\varepsilon \in U\} \]

is a lattice in $\mathbb R^ s$. By Dirichlet's unit theorem, when $t=1$ every subgroup of finite index in $\mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ is admissible. For admissible $U$, Oeljeklaus and Toma showed that the semidirect product $\Gamma =\mathcal O_ K\rtimes U$ acts freely and properly discontinuously on $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$ with compact quotient. The compact complex manifold

\[ X=X(K,U)=(\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t)/\Gamma , \qquad n=\dim X=s+t, \]

is the Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold of type $(s,t)$. The same construction works if $\mathcal O_ K$ is replaced by any lattice $L\subset K$ of rank $s+2t$ that is stable under multiplication by $U$; for $s=t=1$ this produces the Inoue surfaces $S_ M$. The action is still free and cocompact: a point fixed by $(a,\varepsilon )$ with $\varepsilon \neq 1$ would have real coordinates $w_ j$, since $\sigma _ j(\varepsilon )\neq 1$ by injectivity of $\sigma _ j$, and $(\sigma _{\mathbb R},\sigma _{\mathbb C})(L)$ is again a lattice in $\mathbb R^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$. All results of this section other than corollary 10.29.7 hold, with the same proofs, in this generality, with $L$ in place of $\mathcal O_ K$; the only property of $\mathcal O_ K$ they use is that it spans $K$ over $\mathbb Q$. Corollary 10.29.7 also holds for every Inoue surface $S_ M$, since these contain no curves. The sources are discussed in section 25.18.

The functions $y_ j=\operatorname {Im}w_ j$ are invariant under the translations and are multiplied by $\sigma _ j(\varepsilon )$ under the unit $\varepsilon $. Hence the closed real $(1,1)$-form

\[ \theta =-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\sum _{j=1}^ s\log y_ j =\sum _{j=1}^ s\frac{\mathrm i\, \mathrm dw_ j\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_ j}{8\pi y_ j^2} \]

on $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$ is $\Gamma $-invariant and descends to a closed semipositive form on $X$, still denoted $\theta $. It has rank $s$ everywhere, and its kernel is spanned by the vectors $\partial /\partial z_ k$. The potential $-\sum _ j\log y_ j$ itself is not invariant, since a unit adds $-\sum _ j\log \sigma _ j(\varepsilon )$ to it.

10.29.2 Potentials of the transverse form992E9A24F805

Two properties of the action force rigidity: the leaves $\{ w\} \times \mathbb C^ t$ are Liouville, and the real parts of the translations are dense.

Lemma 10.29.3.72EDF5773FAC Since $t\geq 1$, the subgroup $\sigma _{\mathbb R}(\mathcal O_ K)$ is dense in $\mathbb R^ s$.

Proof. A subgroup $G$ of $\mathbb R^ s$ is dense unless there is a nonzero linear form $\xi $ on $\mathbb R^ s$ with $\xi (G)\subseteq \mathbb Z$: the closure of $G$ is the sum of a linear subspace $V$ and a discrete subgroup of a complement of $V$, and if $V\neq \mathbb R^ s$ one can take $\xi $ vanishing on $V$ and integral on that discrete subgroup. Suppose $\xi (\sigma _{\mathbb R}(a))=\sum _ j\xi _ j\sigma _ j(a)\in \mathbb Z$ for every $a\in \mathcal O_ K$. Since $\mathcal O_ K$ spans $K$ over $\mathbb Q$, the map $a\mapsto \sum _ j\xi _ j\sigma _ j(a)$ is a $\mathbb Q$-linear map $K\to \mathbb Q$. The trace form of $K$ is nondegenerate, so there is $b\in K$ with

\[ \sum _{j=1}^ s\xi _ j\sigma _ j(a)=\operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(ba) =\sum _{\sigma }\sigma (b)\sigma (a) \qquad (a\in K), \]

where $\sigma $ runs over all $s+2t$ embeddings of $K$ into $\mathbb C$. Distinct field embeddings are linearly independent over $\mathbb C$, by Dedekind's lemma on characters. Comparing the coefficients of a complex embedding $\sigma _{s+1}$, which occurs only on the right, gives $\sigma _{s+1}(b)=0$. Hence $b=0$, and then $\xi _ j=\sigma _ j(b)=0$ for every $j$. $\square$

For $\varepsilon \in U$ and $y\in (0,\infty )^ s$, write $\sigma (\varepsilon )y=(\sigma _1(\varepsilon )y_1,\ldots ,\sigma _ s(\varepsilon )y_ s)$.

Theorem 10.29.4.244BCBFB750A Let $\mathcal F_ U$ be the set of convex functions $f:(0,\infty )^ s\to \mathbb R$ such that

8DB42C059FB9 10.29.5
\begin{equation} f(\sigma (\varepsilon )y)=f(y)-\sum _{j=1}^ s\log \sigma _ j(\varepsilon ) \qquad (\varepsilon \in U). \end{equation}

For $f\in \mathcal F_ U$, the function $u_ f(w,z)=f(y)+\sum _ j\log y_ j$ is $\Gamma $-invariant, and $f\mapsto u_ f$ is a bijection from $\mathcal F_ U$ onto $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. In particular, every $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ is continuous and depends only on $y=\operatorname {Im}w$.

Proof. We first note that a function $g$ on $\mathbb H^ s$ that depends only on $y$ is plurisubharmonic if and only if, as a function on $(0,\infty )^ s$, it is convex (with values in $[-\infty ,+\infty )$ and not identically $-\infty $). For smooth $g$ this follows from $\partial ^2g/\partial w_ j\partial \overline w_ k=\frac14\partial ^2g/\partial y_ j\partial y_ k$. In general, convolution with a smooth radial kernel in $w$ produces smooth functions of $y$ alone; they decrease to $g$ if $g$ is plurisubharmonic, and they converge locally uniformly to $g$ if $g$ is convex, because a convex function that is not identically $-\infty $ on the open convex set $(0,\infty )^ s$ is finite and continuous there. Since decreasing limits of convex functions are convex and locally uniform limits of plurisubharmonic functions are plurisubharmonic, the claim follows.

The identity (10.29.5) says exactly that $u_ f$ is invariant under $U$, and $u_ f$ is clearly invariant under the translations. On the universal cover, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ f=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f(y)\geq 0$. Hence $u_ f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $f$ is recovered from $u_ f$, so the map is injective.

Conversely, let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and let $\tilde u$ be its lift to $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$. Since $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tilde u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\tilde u-\sum _ j\log y_ j)$ there, the function $q=\tilde u-\sum _ j\log y_ j$ is plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$.

Step 1: constancy on the leaves. Fix $w^0\in \mathbb H^ s$. The restriction of $q$ to the slice $\{ w^0\} \times \mathbb C^ t$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $, and it is bounded above because $u$ is upper semicontinuous on the compact manifold $X$. A plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb C^ t$ that is bounded above is constant, since its restriction to every complex line through two given points is a subharmonic function on $\mathbb C$ bounded above. Hence $\tilde u(w,z)=\tilde u(w,0)$ for all $(w,z)$. In particular, the function $F(w)=q(w,0)$ is not identically $-\infty $, because $\tilde u$ is not, and it is plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb H^ s$.

Step 2: invariance under real translations. The translations by $\mathcal O_ K$ and Step 1 give $F(w+\sigma _{\mathbb R}(a))=F(w)$ for every $a\in \mathcal O_ K$, since $\log y_ j$ does not change under real translations. For $r>0$, the averages $F_ r$ of $F$ over the Euclidean balls of radius $r$ are defined on $\{ w:\min _ jy_ j>r\} $ and continuous there, because $F$ is locally integrable, and they are invariant under the same translations. By lemma 10.29.3 and continuity, each $F_ r$ is invariant under all translations by $\mathbb R^ s$. Since $F_ r$ decreases to $F$ as $r\downarrow 0$, the function $F$ is invariant under all real translations as well, so $F(w)=f(y)$ for a function $f$ on $(0,\infty )^ s$.

Step 3: convexity and automorphy. Since $F=f(y)$ is plurisubharmonic, the first paragraph of the proof shows that $f$ is convex, finite and continuous. Finally, $u=u_ f$, and invariance of $u$ under $U$ is (10.29.5). $\square$

Corollary 10.29.6.98BD3A283B8E On every Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold, all functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ are continuous. Consequently, they all have the ordinary singularity type of $0$, all their Lelong numbers vanish, all their multiplier ideals are trivial, and $P_\theta [u]=V_\theta =0$ for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Moreover $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=0$ for every $u$, so $\theta $ is nef but not big.

Proof. Continuous functions on a compact manifold are bounded, so any two of them are equivalent, and their Lelong numbers and multiplier ideals are those of a bounded function. Since $\theta \geq 0$, the constant $0$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, so $V_\theta =0$, and $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [0]=V_\theta $. By theorem 10.29.4, locally $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f(y)$ with $f$ continuous and depending on the $s$ real variables $y_ j$; the Bedford–Taylor products of such a current vanish in degree $s+1\leq n$, because they are limits of the corresponding products for smooth convex approximants of $f$, whose complex Hessians have rank at most $s$. Thus $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=0$. The form $\theta $ is smooth and semipositive, hence nef. Suppose that $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfied $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \omega $ with $\delta >0$. The function $\rho $ is continuous, and on a small coordinate ball with coordinates $\zeta $ we may write $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ with $a$ smooth and $\delta \omega \geq \delta '\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|\zeta |^2$ with $\delta '>0$. Then $a+\rho -\delta '|\zeta |^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and expanding $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a+\rho ))^ n$ as a sum of positive mixed Bedford–Taylor products gives $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\rho )\geq \delta '^ n(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|\zeta |^2)^ n\neq 0$ on the ball, a contradiction. Hence $\theta $ is not big. $\square$

Singular currents nevertheless occur in this class. Let $s=t=1$, so that $X$ is an Inoue surface and $U$ is generated by a unit $\varepsilon _0$ with $\lambda =\sigma _1(\varepsilon _0)>1$. Fix $y_0>0$, put $y_ k=\lambda ^ ky_0$ for $k\in \mathbb Z$, and let $f$ be the continuous function that is affine on each interval $[y_ k,y_{k+1}]$ with slope

\[ a_ k=-\frac{\log \lambda }{(\lambda -1)y_ k}, \qquad f(y_0)=0. \]

Then $a_ k(y_{k+1}-y_ k)=-\log \lambda $, so $f(\lambda y)=f(y)-\log \lambda $, and the slopes increase because $a_{k+1}=a_ k/\lambda $ and $a_ k<0$. Thus $f\in \mathcal F_ U$. On the universal cover,

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ f=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f(y)=\frac1{4\pi }\sum _{k\in \mathbb Z}(a_ k-a_{k-1})\, \delta (y-y_ k)\, \mathrm dx\wedge \mathrm dy, \]

with $a_ k-a_{k-1}>0$. The closed positive current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ f$ therefore has a continuous potential but is supported on the compact real hypersurface of $X$ formed by the images of the hyperplanes $\{ y=y_ k\} $, a three-dimensional torus that is a union of leaves of the foliation $\{ w=\text{const}\} $.

On an Inoue surface, $\theta $ represents the canonical Bott–Chern class. Indeed, the Tricerri metric $\omega _ T=8\pi \theta +y\, \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$ has $\omega _ T^2=(2/y)\, \mathrm i\, \mathrm dw\wedge \mathrm d\overline w\wedge \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$, so its normalized Chern–Ricci form $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (\omega _ T^2/\mathrm i\, \mathrm dw\wedge \mathrm d\overline w\wedge \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z)$, which is $-\mathrm i\partial \overline\partial \log \det \omega _ T$ divided by $2\pi $ and represents $c_1^{BC}(X)$, equals $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log y=-\theta $. Hence theorem 10.29.4 describes all positive currents in $c_1^{BC}(K_ X)$, and none of them has a pole.

Arbitrary backgrounds behave differently, since a Hermitian form has potentials with logarithmic poles. Nevertheless, when $t=1$ their singularities are confined to finite sets. By a theorem of Ornea and Verbitsky (section 25.18), an Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold with $t=1$ contains no complex subvarieties of positive dimension.

Corollary 10.29.7.CBF3E42884D1 Let $X$ be an Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold with $t=1$, let $\eta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\eta )$. For every $c>0$, the set $\{ x:\nu (u,x)\geq c\} $ is finite. Consequently, for every $k>0$ the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(ku)$ is trivial outside a finite set, and $\mathcal I(ku)$ has finite colength.

Proof. By Siu's theorem (5.34.2), the set $E_ c=\{ \nu (u,\cdot )\geq c\} $ is a compact analytic subset of $X$. It is a proper subset, because $\nu (u,x)=0$ outside the pluripolar set $\{ u=-\infty \} $, which has Lebesgue measure zero. Hence $E_ c$ is finite by the theorem of Ornea and Verbitsky. By Skoda's integrability criterion, recalled in [SGPT], $\mathcal I(ku)_ x=\mathcal O_{X,x}$ whenever $k\nu (u,x)<1$, so $\mathcal I(ku)$ is trivial outside the finite set $E_{1/k}$. A coherent ideal sheaf that is trivial outside a finite set has finite colength. $\square$

10.29.8 Monge–Ampère masses on threefoldsDF61FAE7D603

Now let $s=2$ and $t=1$, so that $X$ is a threefold. A totally positive unit has norm $\sigma _1(\varepsilon )\sigma _2(\varepsilon )|\sigma _3(\varepsilon )|^2=1$, so the form $y_1y_2\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ is $\Gamma $-invariant. The Hermitian form

\[ \omega =\theta +\beta ,\qquad \beta =y_1y_2\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2, \]

descends to $X$. Write $\mathrm dV_0=(\mathrm i\, \mathrm dw_1\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_1)\wedge (\mathrm i\, \mathrm dw_2\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_2)\wedge (\mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z)$ on the universal cover, and $\mathrm M_\omega (u)=\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3$.

A unit that fixes $y_1$ satisfies $\sigma _1(\varepsilon )=1$, hence $\varepsilon =1$ because $\sigma _1$ is injective. Hence the map $X\to T_ U=\mathbb R^2/\Lambda _ U$, $[w,z]\mapsto (\log y_1,\log y_2)$, has as fibers the real tori $(\mathbb R^2\times \mathbb C)/(\sigma _{\mathbb R},\sigma _{\mathbb C})(\mathcal O_ K)$, and for every $\Lambda _ U$-periodic function $g$ of $\varsigma =(\log y_1,\log y_2)$ there is a constant $c_ X>0$, independent of $g$, with

3C8DE164722C 10.29.9
\begin{equation} \int _ Xg(\varsigma )\, \frac{\mathrm dV_0}{y_1y_2} =c_ X\int _{T_ U}g\, \mathrm d\varsigma . \end{equation}

We shall use an elementary property of lattices in the plane: every lattice $\Lambda \subset \mathbb R^2$ contains vectors in each of the four open quadrants. Indeed, let $\ell >0$ be the length of a shortest nonzero vector of $\Lambda $, and let $0<\delta <\ell $. By Minkowski's theorem, the symmetric convex set $\{ k:|k_1-k_2|<\delta ,\ |k_1+k_2|<N\} $ contains a nonzero lattice point once $N$ is large. Such a point satisfies $(k_1+k_2)^2=2|k|^2-(k_1-k_2)^2\geq 2\ell ^2-\delta ^2$, hence $4k_1k_2=(k_1+k_2)^2-(k_1-k_2)^2>0$; replacing $k$ by $-k$ if necessary gives a point in the open first quadrant, and the line $\mathbb R(1,-1)$ gives the other two quadrants in the same way. If $\lambda $ lies in the open first quadrant and $\mu \in \Lambda $ is independent of $\lambda $, then $N\lambda +\mu $ also lies there for large $N$, so the first quadrant contains two independent lattice vectors.

Proposition 10.29.10.E080ADB5BEF1 Let $X$ be an Oeljeklaus–Toma threefold of type $(2,1)$ with the Hermitian form $\omega $ above.

  1. The form $\omega $ is Gauduchon, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(y_1y_2)\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ is neither semipositive nor seminegative.

  2. For every smooth real function $u$ on $X$,

    \[ \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\int _ X\omega ^3+\frac{3}{16\pi ^3}\int _ X \operatorname {Re}\Bigl (\frac{\partial u}{\partial w_1}\, \overline{\frac{\partial u}{\partial w_2}}\Bigr )\mathrm dV_0. \]

  3. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ be bounded and depend only on $y$, and write $u=g(\varsigma )$ with $g$ periodic under $\Lambda _ U$. Then $g$ is Lipschitz, $P=1-\partial _1g$ and $Q=1-\partial _2g$ are nonnegative almost everywhere, and

    \[ \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\frac{\int _ X\omega ^3}{|T_ U|}\int _{T_ U}PQ\, \mathrm d\varsigma . \]

    There is a constant $c_ U\geq 1$, depending only on $\Lambda _ U$, such that every such $u$ satisfies $c_ U^{-1}\int _ X\omega ^3\leq \mathrm M_\omega (u)\leq c_ U\int _ X\omega ^3$.

  4. There are smooth $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, depending only on $y$, with $\mathrm M_\omega (u)>\int _ X\omega ^3$, and others with $\mathrm M_\omega (u)<\int _ X\omega ^3$. Consequently $v_-(\omega )<\int _ X\omega ^3<v_+(\omega )$.

Proof. (1) Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ has constant coefficients and $y_ j$ is pluriharmonic,

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(y_1y_2)\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2, \]

where

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(y_1y_2)=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\bigl (\partial y_1\wedge \overline\partial y_2 +\partial y_2\wedge \overline\partial y_1\bigr ) =\frac{\mathrm i}{8\pi }\bigl (\mathrm dw_1\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_2 +\mathrm dw_2\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_1\bigr ). \]

The Hermitian matrix of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(y_1y_2)$ in the basis $\mathrm dw_1,\mathrm dw_2$ is a positive multiple of $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0& 1\\ 1& 0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, which is indefinite. Accordingly, for $\eta _\pm =\mathrm dw_1\pm \mathrm dw_2$ the $(3,3)$-forms $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \mathrm i\eta _\pm \wedge \overline\eta _\pm $ are respectively negative and positive multiples of $\mathrm dV_0$, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\beta $ is neither semipositive nor seminegative. Since $\theta $ is closed and $\beta ^2=0$, we have $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)=2\theta \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\beta $, and this vanishes because every term of $\theta \wedge (\mathrm dw_1\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_2+\mathrm dw_2\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_1)$ repeats a differential.

(2) By theorem 11.1.5 with $n=3$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)=0$, $\mathrm M_\omega (u)-\int _ X\omega ^3=3\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $. The form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $ is closed, so Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =-\int _ X\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $. Now $\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u=(\mathrm i/2\pi )\, \partial u\wedge \overline\partial u$, and a direct computation in the coordinates $w_1,w_2,z$ gives

\[ \mathrm i\, \partial u\wedge \overline\partial u\wedge \mathrm i\bigl (\mathrm dw_1\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_2+\mathrm dw_2\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_1\bigr ) \wedge \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z =-2\operatorname {Re}\Bigl (\frac{\partial u}{\partial w_1}\, \overline{\frac{\partial u}{\partial w_2}}\Bigr )\mathrm dV_0. \]

Collecting the constants $(1/2\pi )(1/8\pi )(1/2\pi )$ gives the formula.

(3) Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ has no components in the $z$-direction, $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)+\beta $ splits into blocks, and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ if and only if $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. By theorem 10.29.4, $u=f(y)+\log (y_1y_2)$ with $f$ convex, so $g$ is locally Lipschitz, hence Lipschitz by periodicity. In the variables $\varsigma _ j=\log y_ j$, convexity of $f$ is equivalent to positivity of the symmetric matrix

\[ H=I+D^2g-\operatorname {diag}(\partial _1g,\partial _2g), \]

as one checks from $y_ iy_ j\, \partial ^2f/\partial y_ i\partial y_ j=\partial ^2\Phi /\partial \varsigma _ i\partial \varsigma _ j-\delta _{ij}\, \partial \Phi /\partial \varsigma _ i$ for $\Phi (\varsigma )=f(\mathrm e^{\varsigma })=g(\varsigma )-\varsigma _1-\varsigma _2$. This condition is linear in $g$ with constant coefficients, so it is preserved by convolution with a smooth kernel on $\mathbb R^2$; the regularized functions are periodic, and they converge uniformly to $g$ with gradients converging almost everywhere. By the continuity of bounded Monge–Ampère products under uniform convergence and dominated convergence, it suffices to prove all assertions for smooth $g$ with $H\geq 0$.

For such $g$, the function $u$ depends only on $y$, so $\partial u/\partial w_ j=-\frac{\mathrm i}2\partial u/\partial y_ j$ and the integrand in (2) is $\frac14\partial _{y_1}u\, \partial _{y_2}u=\partial _1g\, \partial _2g/(4y_1y_2)$. A direct computation gives $\omega ^3=3\theta ^2\wedge \beta =\frac{3}{64\pi ^3}\, \mathrm dV_0/(y_1y_2)$. By (10.29.9) and (2),

\[ \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\frac{\int _ X\omega ^3}{|T_ U|}\int _{T_ U} \bigl (1+\partial _1g\, \partial _2g\bigr )\, \mathrm d\varsigma =\frac{\int _ X\omega ^3}{|T_ U|}\int _{T_ U}PQ\, \mathrm d\varsigma , \]

where the second equality uses $\int _{T_ U}\partial _ jg\, \mathrm d\varsigma =0$.

The diagonal entry $H_{11}=P-\partial _1P\geq 0$ says that $\mathrm e^{-\varsigma _1}P$ is nonincreasing in $\varsigma _1$, that is,

62F4D45FFF73 10.29.11
\begin{equation} P(\varsigma +\tau e_1)\leq \mathrm e^{\tau }P(\varsigma )\qquad (\tau \geq 0). \end{equation}

If $P(\varsigma )<0$, the right side tends to $-\infty $ as $\tau \to \infty $, which is impossible for the bounded function $P$; hence $P\geq 0$, and likewise $Q\geq 0$. Thus $\partial _ jg\leq 1$. The lattice $\Lambda _ U$ contains two independent vectors $\lambda ,\lambda '$ with positive coordinates, and every point of $T_ U$ has the form $\varsigma +\tau \lambda +\tau '\lambda '$ with $\tau ,\tau '\in [0,1)$. Along $\tau \mapsto \varsigma +\tau \lambda $, the derivative of $g$ is at most $|\lambda |_1=\lambda _1+\lambda _2$, and its integral over $[0,1]$ vanishes by periodicity, so $|g(\varsigma +\tau \lambda )-g(\varsigma )|\leq |\lambda |_1$. Hence $\operatorname {osc}g\leq L$, with $L=|\lambda |_1+|\lambda '|_1$ depending only on $\Lambda _ U$.

Averaging (10.29.11) in the form $P(\varsigma -\tau e_1)\geq \mathrm e^{-\tau }P(\varsigma )$ over $\tau \in [0,1]$ gives

\[ (1-\mathrm e^{-1})P(\varsigma )\leq \int _0^1P(\varsigma -\tau e_1)\, \mathrm d\tau =1-g(\varsigma )+g(\varsigma -e_1)\leq 1+L. \]

On the other hand, averaging $P(\varsigma )\geq \mathrm e^{-\tau }P(\varsigma +\tau e_1)$ over $\tau \in [0,R]$ with $R=\max (2L,1)$ gives

\[ P(\varsigma )\geq \frac{\mathrm e^{-R}}R\int _0^ RP(\varsigma +\tau e_1)\, \mathrm d\tau =\frac{\mathrm e^{-R}}R\bigl (R-g(\varsigma +Re_1)+g(\varsigma )\bigr ) \geq \frac{\mathrm e^{-R}}2. \]

The same bounds hold for $Q$. Therefore the average of $PQ$ over $T_ U$ lies between $\mathrm e^{-2R}/4$ and $\bigl ((1+L)/(1-\mathrm e^{-1})\bigr )^2$, which proves (3).

(4) Let $k$ belong to the dual lattice $\Lambda _ U^*=\{ k\in \mathbb R^2:\langle k,\lambda \rangle \in \mathbb Z\text{ for all }\lambda \in \Lambda _ U\} $, and put $g_\epsilon (\varsigma )=\epsilon \cos (2\pi \langle k,\varsigma \rangle )$. For small $\epsilon >0$, the matrix $H$ of $g_\epsilon $ is positive definite, so $u_\epsilon =g_\epsilon (\varsigma )$ is a smooth function with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\epsilon >0$. By (3),

\[ \frac{\mathrm M_\omega (u_\epsilon )}{\int _ X\omega ^3} =1+\frac1{|T_ U|}\int _{T_ U}\partial _1g_\epsilon \, \partial _2g_\epsilon \, \mathrm d\varsigma =1+2\pi ^2\epsilon ^2k_1k_2. \]

Since the lattice $\Lambda _ U^*$ has vectors in every open quadrant, we may choose $k$ with $k_1k_2>0$, respectively $k_1k_2<0$, which gives the two inequalities. $\square$

For a general smooth potential, averaging over the fibers separates the invariant part from the fluctuations. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ let $Au$ be the average of $u$ over the fibers of $X\to T_ U$, that is, over the translations $(w,z)\mapsto (w+r,z+\zeta )$ with $(r,\zeta )\in (\mathbb R^2\times \mathbb C)/(\sigma _{\mathbb R},\sigma _{\mathbb C})(\mathcal O_ K)$. These translations preserve $\omega $ on the universal cover. They do not descend to $X$ individually, but conjugation by a unit $\varepsilon $ carries the translation by $(r,\zeta )$ to the translation by $(\sigma _1(\varepsilon )r_1,\sigma _2(\varepsilon )r_2,\sigma _3(\varepsilon )\zeta )$, and this automorphism of the translation torus preserves its Haar measure because $\varepsilon $ has norm one. Hence $Au$ is a well-defined $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function depending only on $y$, and $c_ U^{-1}\int _ X\omega ^3\leq \mathrm M_\omega (Au)\leq c_ U\int _ X\omega ^3$ by proposition 10.29.10. For smooth $u$, the integral of a $y$-derivative of $u-Au$ over a fiber vanishes, and so does the integral of an $x$-derivative. The cross terms between $Au$ and $u-Au$ in proposition 10.29.10(2) therefore integrate to zero, and

61E93097052D 10.29.12
\begin{equation} \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\mathrm M_\omega (Au)+\frac{3}{16\pi ^3}\int _ X \operatorname {Re}\Bigl (\frac{\partial v}{\partial w_1}\, \overline{\frac{\partial v}{\partial w_2}}\Bigr )\mathrm dV_0, \qquad v=u-Au. \end{equation}

Since the extrema of the masses of bounded potentials equal the extrema over smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic potentials (proposition 13.14.15, theorem 12.15.4), $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $ if and only if the fluctuation term in (10.29.12) is bounded above on smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions; likewise, $v_-(\omega )>0$ if and only if $\mathrm M_\omega (Au)$ plus the fluctuation term is bounded below by a positive constant on such functions.

10.29.13 Hermitian metrics whose $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ has a signD5CAAE8D5B60

Finally, we show that no Hermitian metric on these manifolds has a $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of constant sign, so that curvature criteria based on such metrics cannot settle the boundedness of the masses.

Proposition 10.29.14.48299489DE9D Let $X$ be an Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold of type $(s,1)$ with $s\geq 2$. Then $X$ admits no Hermitian form $\widetilde\omega $ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde\omega $ is semipositive, or seminegative, on every complex $2$-plane.

Proof. Suppose $\widetilde\omega $ is such a form, with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde\omega \geq 0$ on every complex $2$-plane; the other case is analogous. Average the lift of $\widetilde\omega $ over the translations $(w,z)\mapsto (w+r,z+\zeta )$, $(r,\zeta )\in (\mathbb R^ s\times \mathbb C)/(\sigma _{\mathbb R},\sigma _{\mathbb C})(\mathcal O_ K)$, as in the definition of $Au$ above. The averaged form $\overline\omega $ is again $\Gamma $-invariant and Hermitian, its coefficients in the frame $\mathrm dw_1,\ldots ,\mathrm dw_ s,\mathrm dz$ depend only on $y$, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\overline\omega \geq 0$ on complex $2$-planes, because translations are holomorphic and positivity on $2$-planes is preserved by averaging.

Let $h(y)>0$ be the coefficient of $\mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$ in $\overline\omega $. Invariance under a unit $\varepsilon $ gives $h(\sigma (\varepsilon )y)|\sigma _{s+1}(\varepsilon )|^2=h(y)$, that is, $h(\sigma (\varepsilon )y)=\sigma _1(\varepsilon )\cdots \sigma _ s(\varepsilon )\, h(y)$, since $\varepsilon $ has norm one. Hence $h/(y_1\cdots y_ s)$ is invariant under $U$, and since $\Lambda _ U$ is a lattice it is bounded between two positive constants:

73E1BF5F8F36 10.29.15
\begin{equation} c\, y_1\cdots y_ s\leq h(y)\leq C\, y_1\cdots y_ s. \end{equation}

Let $v$ be a nonzero vector in the span of $\partial /\partial w_1,\ldots ,\partial /\partial w_ s$, and let $P$ be the complex $2$-plane spanned by $v$ and $\partial /\partial z$. Write $\overline\omega =\sum _{a,b}g_{ab}(y)\, \mathrm i\, \xi _ a\wedge \overline\xi _ b$ with $\xi =(\mathrm dw_1,\ldots ,\mathrm dw_ s,\mathrm dz)$. Since the forms $\xi _ a$ are closed, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\overline\omega =\sum _{a,b}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_{ab}\wedge \mathrm i\, \xi _ a\wedge \overline\xi _ b$, and each $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_{ab}$ has only $w$-components. A term restricted to $P$ is nonzero only if it contains both $\mathrm dz$ and $\mathrm d\overline z$, which happens only for $\xi _ a=\xi _ b=\mathrm dz$. Hence the restriction of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\overline\omega $ to $P$ equals the restriction of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$, and its sign is that of the value of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ on $v$. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\geq 0$ in the $w$-variables, and $h$ is convex in $y$.

Let $R>1$ and put $A=(R,R^{-1},1,\ldots ,1)$, $B=(R^{-1},R,1,\ldots ,1)$ and $M=(A+B)/2$. By (10.29.15), $h(A),h(B)\leq C$, whereas $h(M)\geq c(R+R^{-1})^2/4$, which contradicts convexity for large $R$. In the seminegative case, $h$ is concave, so $t\mapsto h(t,\ldots ,t)$ grows at most linearly as $t\to \infty $; this contradicts the lower bound $h(t,\ldots ,t)\geq ct^ s$ with $s\geq 2$. $\square$

For $s=t=1$ the same averaging produces a coefficient $h$ comparable to $y$, which may be affine; indeed the Tricerri metric is pluriclosed. In dimension three, the curvature criteria for finite upper and positive lower mass recalled in section 25.18 require a metric with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \geq 0$, respectively $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \leq 0$, and proposition 10.29.14 excludes both on threefolds of type $(2,1)$.
