11.45 Selection for logarithmic models on productsA167FA614566

A167FA614566

For a logarithmic reference with a positive residual background, removing the divisor turns the singular exponential equation into a bounded equation. On a product with a curve, the selected residual can then be determined by the Green operator. This also controls simultaneous changes of the reference, background and density, for which the centering constant must follow the changing data.

Let $(Y,\kappa )$ be a connected compact Hermitian manifold of dimension $k\geq 1$, with $\int _ Y\kappa ^ k=1$, and put $X=\mathbb P^1\times Y$, $N=k+1$. Write $\omega _1$ for the mass-one Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^1$ and $\mathrm{d}W=\omega _1\wedge \kappa ^ k$; pullbacks to $X$ are implicit. Fix $a,d>0$, a smooth positive nonconstant function $b$ on $\mathbb P^1$, and a continuous positive function $H$ on $\mathbb P^1$. Set

\[ \begin{aligned} \theta & =(a+d)\omega _1+b\kappa ,& \eta & =a\omega _1+b\kappa ,\\ f& =N b^ kH,& m& =\int _{\mathbb P^1}H\omega _1,\qquad c_0=\frac am. \end{aligned} \]

Both forms are positive and nonclosed: the component $\mathrm{d}b\wedge \kappa $ of horizontal degree one cannot cancel the vertical component $b\, \mathrm{d}\kappa $. For homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:Z_1]$, put

\[ \ell =\frac{|Z_0|^2}{|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2},\qquad D=\{ Z_0=0\} \times Y,\qquad \psi =d\log \ell . \]

The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\eta +d[D]$.

11.45.1 The ordinary model and the greatest solutions27CD354DC77F

Put $K=a+d$, $R=a/d$ and $C_*=K\log (K/d)-a\log (a/d)$. In the coordinate $z=Z_1/Z_0$ define

254FB5175831 11.45.2
\begin{equation} \phi _{a,d}=\begin{cases} 0,& |z|^2\leq R,\\ a\log |z|^2+C_*-K\log (1+|z|^2),& |z|^2>R, \end{cases} \qquad \phi _{a,d}|_ D=-\infty . \end{equation}

Proposition 11.45.3.DA11AEC1C7C7 The function $\phi _{a,d}$ is an ordinary model reference for $\theta $, with

\[ P_\theta [\psi ]=P_\theta [\phi _{a,d}]=\phi _{a,d},\qquad \psi \leq \phi _{a,d}\leq \psi +C_*,\qquad \sup _ X\phi _{a,d}=0. \]

For $d/(a+d)<\delta <1$, the envelope $P_{\delta \theta }(\phi _{a,d})$ is not identically $-\infty $. The model has finite positive Monge–Ampère measure

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (\phi _{a,d}) =N b^ kK\mathbf1_{\{ |z|^2<R\} }\, \mathrm{d}W. \]

The difference $\phi _{a,d}-\psi $ extends continuously across $D$. If $(a_ j,d_ j)\to (a,d)$ in $(0,+\infty )^2$, these residual extensions converge uniformly.

Proof. First take the ordinary envelope on the curve with background $K\omega _1$ and obstacle $\min (\psi +C,0)$. Rotation of $z$ preserves both the obstacle and the admissible family, so the envelope is rotation invariant. With $s=\log |z|^2$ and $F(s)=K\log (1+\mathrm{e}^ s)$, its profile plus $F$ is convex and lies below $F$. The comparison with $\psi +C$ bounds its final slope by $a$. At $s_*=\log R$, one has $F'(s_*)=a$. Above $s_*$, convexity and the final slope bound give

\[ u(s)+F(s)\leq F(s_*)+a(s-s_*), \]

and below $s_*$ the obstacle gives $u\leq 0$. The function in (11.45.2) attains both bounds. Its convex profile, including its logarithmic value at infinity, gives a curve potential, and direct calculation gives $\psi \leq \phi _{a,d}\leq \psi +C_*$. It is therefore a competitor for every $C\geq C_*$, so the curve envelope is exactly $\phi _{a,d}$.

A product competitor restricts to each curve $\mathbb P^1\times \{ y\} $ as either the constant $-\infty $ or a curve competitor. It is thus bounded above by $\phi _{a,d}$. Conversely, the curve model is a product competitor because the vertical form $b\kappa $ is positive. Hence the product envelope is the same function. Ordinary-type invariance of the singularity envelope gives its model property. Moreover,

\[ \delta \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =(\delta K-d)\omega _1+\delta b\kappa +d[D]\geq 0, \]

which proves the smaller-background assertion.

Off $D$, the horizontal curvature of $\phi _{a,d}$ is $K\mathbf1_{\{ |z|^2<R\} }\omega _1$. The first derivatives of its convex profile agree at $R$, so there is no boundary-circle atom. Expanding the product off $D$ gives the displayed measure, and the non-pluripolar product assigns $D$ zero mass. Finally, in terms of $\ell $ the residual extension is

\[ \phi _{a,d}-\psi =\begin{cases} -d\log \ell ,& \ell \geq d/K,\\ a\log (1-\ell )+C_*,& \ell <d/K. \end{cases} \]

The formulas agree at the moving interface and extend to both endpoints. On compact positive parameter ranges each logarithm is used away from its singular endpoint. Joint continuity on the resulting compact set proves uniform variation. $\square$

Lemma 11.45.4.688DA0FB893F For every $\sigma >0$, the equation

D9B5F3712AAE 11.45.5
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}_\theta (s_\sigma )=\mathrm{e}^{\sigma s_\sigma }f\, \mathrm{d}W \end{equation}

has a solution of ordinary type $\phi _{a,d}$, unique in that type. It is the greatest exponential subsolution among all $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi _{a,d}$, and its measure has finite mass.

Proof. The one-sided ordinary class has the description

37E96D4A1D80 11.45.6
\begin{equation} u\preceq \psi \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad u=\psi +r,\quad r\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\eta ),\qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi +r) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\eta (r). \end{equation}

Indeed, $u\preceq \psi $ gives $\nu (u,D)\geq d$. Divisorial removal, lemma 5.38.1, shows that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-d[D]\geq 0$. Thus the locally integrable difference $u-\psi $ has an $\eta $-plurisubharmonic representative $r$. The equality $u=\psi +r$ holds off $D$ by canonical equality and on $D$ because both sides equal $-\infty $. Conversely, adding $\psi $ to $r$ adds the positive current $d[D]$; since $r$ has a finite compact upper bound, the one-sided comparison follows. Off $D$ the currents agree, so finite-level locality identifies their extended products, including at any additional poles of $r$. Both measures give $D$ zero mass, proving the global equality. The bounded ordinary type corresponds exactly to bounded $r$, as in theorem 11.41.1.

The function $g_\sigma =\mathrm{e}^{\sigma \psi }f$ extends continuously by zero on $D$ and is positive elsewhere. Since $\eta >0$, theorem 14.19.3 gives a continuous solution $R_\sigma $ of

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\eta (R_\sigma )=\mathrm{e}^{\sigma R_\sigma }g_\sigma \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

For this bounded density, the greatest solution of theorem 15.35.2 is bounded and coincides with $R_\sigma $ by corollary 14.19.12. Thus it dominates every $\eta $-plurisubharmonic exponential subsolution. The correspondence (11.45.6) transfers this order to the entire one-sided ordinary class and gives $s_\sigma =\psi +R_\sigma $. Bounded residual uniqueness gives uniqueness in the ordinary type. The bounded residual equation has finite mass, so the singular equation does too. $\square$

11.45.7 The selected limit with varying dataA00D72781B06

For $x,z\in \mathbb P^1$, let $\operatorname {dist}_{\mathrm{chord}}(x,z)$ be the chordal distance and put

\[ G(x,z)=\log \operatorname {dist}_{\mathrm{chord}}(x,z)^2+1. \]

This kernel satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_ xG=\delta _ z-\omega _1$ and $\int G(x,z)\omega _1(x)=0$. For a bounded density $q$, write $G*q(x)=\int G(x,z)q(z)\omega _1(z)$. Define

E96D6BB82F6C 11.45.8
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} A_0& =\frac{aH}{m},& w_0& =G*(A_0-a),& S_0& =\sup _ X(\psi +w_0),\\ \mathrm{d}\nu _0& =\frac Hm\, \mathrm{d}W,& J& =\int _ X(\psi +w_0)\, \mathrm{d}\nu _0. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

The function $w_0$ is continuous, has mean zero on the curve and satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_0=(A_0-a)\omega _1$. We use the probability $\nu _0$ to normalize the separated potentials.

Theorem 11.45.9.72A7418012F7 Let $a_ j,d_ j>0$ converge to $a,d>0$, let smooth positive functions $b_ j$ converge uniformly to $b$, and let continuous positive functions $H_ j$ converge uniformly to $H$. For any $\sigma _ j>0$ tending to zero, put

\[ \begin{aligned} \theta _ j& =(a_ j+d_ j)\omega _1+b_ j\kappa ,& \psi _ j& =d_ j\log \ell ,& \phi _ j& =\phi _{a_ j,d_ j},\\ f_ j& =N b_ j^ kH_ j,& m_ j& =\int _{\mathbb P^1}H_ j\omega _1,& c_{0,j}& =a_ j/m_ j. \end{aligned} \]

Let $s_ j$ be the greatest solution of (11.45.5) for these data, and set $v_ j=s_ j-\sup _ Xs_ j$, $c_ j=\mathrm{e}^{\sigma _ j\sup _ Xs_ j}$. Then

\[ v_ j\longrightarrow v_0=\psi +w_0-S_0,\qquad s_ j-\sigma _ j^{-1}\log c_{0,j}\longrightarrow s_0=\psi +w_0-J \]

in $L^1(X)$, in capacity, and locally uniformly on $X\setminus D$. Moreover,

97B5C8E6D45D 11.45.10
\begin{equation} c_ j\longrightarrow c_0,\qquad \sigma _ j^{-1}\log (c_ j/c_{0,j})\longrightarrow S_0-J, \end{equation}

and $\mathrm{MA}_{\theta _ j}(v_ j)\to c_0 f\, \mathrm{d}W$ in total variation. The limit has ordinary type $\phi _{a,d}$ and belongs to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\phi _{a,d})$, with

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (v_0)=c_0 f\, \mathrm{d}W. \]

There is no restriction on the relative rates of parameter and exponent convergence.

Proof. We construct uniformly bounded residuals on the curve, then use their equation to determine both the normalized limit and the scalar error.

Uniform construction. The kernel has uniformly integrable logarithmic tails and $C_ G=\sup _ x\int |G(x,z)|\omega _1(z)<\infty $. These facts follow by rotation invariance and the radial integral of $|\log \ell +1|$ on $0<\ell <1$; the Poincaré–Lelong formula and $\int _0^1\log \ell \, \mathrm{d}\ell =-1$ give its curvature and mean. Truncating the kernel shows that convolution of bounded densities is continuous.

Temporarily omit subscripts. On continuous mean-zero functions on the curve define

\[ T_\sigma (w)=G*(A(w)-a),\qquad A(w)=\frac{aH\mathrm{e}^{\sigma (\psi +w)}}{Z(w)},\qquad Z(w)=\int _{\mathbb P^1}H\mathrm{e}^{\sigma (\psi +w)}\omega _1. \]

For all late parameters choose fixed bounds $a\leq a_{\max }$, $d\leq d_{\max }$ and $0<h_{\min }\leq H\leq h_{\max }$. Put $B=4a_{\max }h_{\max }/h_{\min }$ and choose $R_0>C_ G(B+a_{\max })$. On the ball $\| w\| _\infty \leq R_0$, for sufficiently small $\sigma $,

\[ Z(w)\geq \frac{h_{\min }\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma R_0}}{1+\sigma d_{\max }} \geq h_{\min }/2,\qquad 0\leq A(w)\leq B. \]

Here $\int \ell ^{\sigma d}\omega _1=(1+\sigma d)^{-1}$. Thus $T_\sigma $ maps the mean-zero ball into itself. Since $\int A(w)\omega _1=a$, its density derivative in direction $h$ is

\[ \sigma A(w)\left(h-a^{-1}\int hA(w)\omega _1\right). \]

It follows that $\| T_\sigma (w)-T_\sigma (v)\| _\infty \leq 2\sigma B C_ G\| w-v\| _\infty $. The map is a contraction for all sufficiently small exponents, uniformly over the late parameters. Its fixed point $w_ j$ has mean zero and $\| w_ j\| _\infty \leq R_0$.

Write $Z_ j=Z(w_ j)$ and $A_ j=A(w_ j)$. The fixed-point equation gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j=(A_ j-a_ j)\omega _1$, and the potential

9E6339686644 11.45.11
\begin{equation} \psi _ j+w_ j+\sigma _ j^{-1}\log (a_ j/Z_ j) \end{equation}

has residual form $A_ j\omega _1+b_ j\kappa $, continuous and semipositive. On coordinate balls, mollification of $w_ j$ converges together with its continuous complex Hessian. The local bounded product, expanded after a common strict smooth correction, therefore equals $N b_ j^ kA_ j\, \mathrm{d}W$. Removing $D$ identifies the singular product with the same measure. The identity

\[ N b_ j^ kA_ j=\mathrm{e}^{\sigma _ j(\psi _ j+w_ j)}(a_ j/Z_ j)f_ j \]

shows that (11.45.11) solves the exponential equation in the ordinary type. By lemma 11.45.4, it is $s_ j$.

Residual and normalized limits. Uniform boundedness and dominated convergence give $Z_ j\to m$ and $A_ j\to A_0$ in $L^1(\omega _1)$, with a common bound on $A_ j$. For uniformly bounded densities $F_ j\to 0$ in $L^1$, one has

\[ \| G*F_ j\| _\infty \leq \| F_ j\| _\infty \sup _ x\int _{\{ |G(x,z)|>M\} }|G(x,z)|\omega _1(z) +M\| F_ j\| _1. \]

First letting $j\to \infty $ and then $M\to \infty $ proves $w_ j\to w_0$ uniformly. Although $A_ j=0$ on $D$ and $A_0>0$ there, the $L^1$ convergence suffices for this argument.

Put $S_ j=\sup _ X(\psi _ j+w_ j)$. A point with $\ell =1$ supplies a uniformly bounded competing value. Since $d_ j\geq d_{\min }>0$, the upper bound $d_{\min }\log \ell +R_0$ tends uniformly to $-\infty $ near $D$, so all maximizing points stay in a fixed compact subset of $X\setminus D$. Uniform convergence there gives $S_ j\to S_0$. Hence $v_ j=\psi _ j+w_ j-S_ j$ has the asserted $L^1$ and local uniform limits. For capacity convergence, the complements of $\{ \psi <-T\} $ are compact subsets of $X\setminus D$. Choose a fixed Hermitian form for which $\psi $ is plurisubharmonic and apply (5.29.4); their omitted sublevels have capacity at most $C/T$. Local uniform convergence, followed by $T\to \infty $, proves convergence in capacity, with the common poles omitted.

The measure formula and uniform convergence of $b_ j$ give total-variation convergence to $N b^ kA_0\, \mathrm{d}W=c_0 f\, \mathrm{d}W$. The same bounded product calculation gives this equation for $v_0$. The residual extensions in proposition 11.45.3 show directly that $v_0\sim \phi _{a,d}$. Choose $C$ with $v_0\geq \phi _{a,d}-C$. For every $t\geq 1$, the potential $q_ t=\phi _{a,d}-tC$ satisfies

\[ t^{-1}q_ t+(1-t^{-1})\phi _{a,d}\leq v_0, \]

which proves membership in the relative envelope class by definition 16.28.61.

Scalar centering. The exact formula is $c_ j=a_ j\mathrm{e}^{\sigma _ j S_ j}/Z_ j$. In the difference quotient

\[ \frac{Z_ j-m_ j}{\sigma _ j} =\int H_ j\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\sigma _ j(\psi _ j+w_ j)}-1}{\sigma _ j}\omega _1, \]

the negative part is bounded by $h_{\max }(d_{\max }|\log \ell |+R_0)$ and the positive part by a constant. Dominated convergence gives a limit $\int H(\psi +w_0)\omega _1=mJ$. Since $m_ j\to m>0$,

\[ \sigma _ j^{-1}\log (Z_ j/m_ j)\longrightarrow J. \]

The identity $c_ j/c_{0,j}=m_ j\mathrm{e}^{\sigma _ j S_ j}/Z_ j$ proves (11.45.10). Subtracting $\sigma _ j^{-1}\log c_{0,j}$ in (11.45.11) leaves $\psi _ j+w_ j-\sigma _ j^{-1}\log (Z_ j/m_ j)$, proving the centered limit in all three topologies. $\square$

11.45.12 The critical coefficient and separated orderCEEDD0460992

Corollary 11.45.13.86F268014BE0 For the fixed data, $c_0$ is the largest coefficient $c>0$ for which $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi _{a,d}$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)\geq c f\, \mathrm{d}W$. Among separated potentials $u=\psi +r(x)\preceq \phi _{a,d}$, the critical subsolutions are exactly the translates of $\psi +w_0$. Thus $v_0$ is their unique supremum-zero member, and $s_0$ is their greatest member with $\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{d}\nu _0\leq 0$.

Proof. For a separated critical subsolution, (11.45.6) and the product expansion give $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{a\omega _1}(r)\geq A_0\omega _1$. Closed curve truncations have mass $a$, so the non-pluripolar measure has mass at most $a$. Since $\int A_0\omega _1=a$, it must equal $A_0\omega _1$. The positive closed current $a\omega _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r$ also has mass $a$, so it has no remaining measure on the pole set. Consequently $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r=(A_0-a)\omega _1$ as distributions, and $r=w_0+C$ because harmonic distributions on $\mathbb P^1$ are constant. Supremum zero gives $C=-S_0$, while the mean condition gives $C\leq -J$.

For a general $u\preceq \phi _{a,d}$ with coefficient $c>0$, write $u=\psi +r$ by (11.45.6) and put $B_ r=\sup _ Xr$. Since $\psi \leq 0$, the potential $u+\sigma ^{-1}\log c-B_ r$ is an exponential subsolution for every $\sigma >0$. Greatestness gives

\[ u+\sigma ^{-1}\log c-B_ r\leq s_\sigma . \]

At a point off $D$ where $r$ is finite, subtract $\sigma ^{-1}\log c_0$, multiply by $\sigma $ and use the fixed-data case of theorem 11.45.9. This yields $\log c\leq \log c_0$. The potential $v_0$ attains $c_0$. $\square$

The scalar in the centering follows the varying data. Replacing $c_{0,j}$ by $c_0$ adds $\sigma _ j^{-1}\log (c_{0,j}/c_0)$ to the centered potential, and this term need not vanish. For example, take $a=1$, $d=1/4$, $H=1$ and $b=2+t$, where $t$ is the sphere height. Then $w_0=0$, $S_0=0$, $J=-1/4$ and $c_0=1$, so $s_0=\psi +1/4$ and the scalar-error limit is $1/4$. If $a_ j=1+\sqrt{\sigma _ j}$ and the other data are fixed, the appropriate centering uses $c_{0,j}=a_ j$. Centering by $1$ instead adds

\[ \frac{\log (1+\sqrt{\sigma _ j})}{\sigma _ j}\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

These examples can be taken on a non-Kähler manifold by choosing $Y$ to be the scalar Hopf surface with its Hermitian form rescaled to unit volume. The product cannot be Kähler, since a Kähler form would restrict to one on the Hopf fiber. The pole divisor remains present in the selected ordinary type.

11.45.14 Weak adjoints on a product surface2FFAD548CA54

On the product of two projective lines, the averaging probability of (11.45.8) is the full supporting probability. The continuous positive residual defines a weak linearization, and spherical harmonics give invariant-measure uniqueness without a regularity assumption.

In this subsection take $Y=\mathbb P^1_ y$, $\kappa =\omega _2$ and $k=1$. Keep $a,d,b,H,\theta ,\eta ,\psi $ and $\mathrm{d}W=\omega _1\wedge \omega _2$ as above, and write $\phi =\phi _{a,d}$, $A=A_0=c_0H$ and $u=\psi +w_0$. Choose a smooth positive probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V=q\, \mathrm{d}W$ and put $F=\log (2bH/q)$. Then

\[ \Omega =A\omega _1+b\omega _2,\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\Omega +d[D],\qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\Omega ^2=c_0\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V. \]

The model is admissible by proposition 11.45.3, and $u\sim \phi $ gives $B_\phi (F)=\log c_0$. Nonconstant $b$ cannot be harmonic, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta =(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)\wedge \omega _2\ne 0$. Every smooth potential perturbation has the same $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta $ and is therefore non-Gauduchon.

For a smooth function $h$ on the product, define its partial Laplacians by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_ i h=(\Delta _ i h)\omega _ i$. With the mass-one Fubini–Study normalization, $\Delta _ i$ is the Laplacian of the unit round sphere.

Lemma 11.45.15.85744C3971DC Let $V>0$ be continuous on $\mathbb P^1$, and let $T$ be a finite signed measure such that

\[ \int _{\mathbb P^1}(\Delta f-Vf)\, \mathrm{d}T=0 \qquad (f\in C^\infty (\mathbb P^1)). \]

Then $T=0$.

Proof. Let $H_ t$ be the sphere heat operator. On spherical harmonics of degree $\ell $, it acts by $\mathrm{e}^{-t\ell (\ell +1)}$. The harmonic series defines a smooth heat solution for $t>0$; for smooth initial functions, the maximum principle gives positivity and contraction in the uniform norm. Density of smooth functions extends $H_ t$ to a positive, strongly continuous contraction on $C(\mathbb P^1)$. Choose $M\geq \max V$ and define

\[ R_ Mg=\int _0^\infty \mathrm{e}^{-Mt}H_ tg\, \mathrm{d}t. \]

Then $\| R_ M\| \leq M^{-1}$ and $(M-\Delta )R_ Mg=g$ in distributions. For any $g\in C(\mathbb P^1)$, the map

\[ f\longmapsto R_ M((M-V)f-g) \]

contracts by at most $1-\min V/M<1$. Its continuous fixed point satisfies $\Delta f=Vf+g$ in distributions, with a continuous right-hand side. The functions $f_\varepsilon =H_\varepsilon f$ are smooth and satisfy

\[ f_\varepsilon \longrightarrow f,\qquad \Delta f_\varepsilon =H_\varepsilon (Vf+g)\longrightarrow Vf+g \]

uniformly. Thus $(\Delta -V)f_\varepsilon \to g$ uniformly. Testing against the finite measure $T$ gives $\int g\, \mathrm{d}T=0$ for every continuous $g$, hence $T=0$. $\square$

Theorem 11.45.16.23BDF62163E8 The weight and current

\[ \rho =\frac1{2ab},\qquad Q=\rho \Omega =\frac{A}{2ab}\omega _1+\frac1{2a}\omega _2 \]

satisfy $\mathrm{d}Q=0$ and $\int _ X\rho \Omega ^2=1$. For smooth real $h$, the measure-valued and logarithmic linearizations at $u$ are

\[ D_ u\mathrm{MA}_\theta (h)=2\Omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h,\qquad L_ uh=\frac{2\Omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h}{\Omega ^2} =\frac{\Delta _1h}{A}+\frac{\Delta _2h}{b}. \]

The only Borel probability satisfying $\int _ XL_ uh\, \mathrm{d}\mu =0$ for every smooth real $h$ is

6EEDC24A3093 11.45.17
\begin{equation} \nu =\rho \Omega ^2=\frac Aa\, \mathrm{d}W,\qquad \mathcal A_\phi (F)=\{ \nu \} . \end{equation}

If $r\geq 0$ is measurable, $\int _ Xr\Omega ^2=1$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(r\Omega )=0$, then $r=\rho $ almost everywhere.

Proof. We first define the weighted current and variation, then prove invariant-measure uniqueness and apply the supporting inequality on the full space $C(X)$.

The current and operator. Since $A/b$ depends only on the first curve, $\mathrm{d}((A/b)\omega _1)=0$ distributionally; this follows also by uniform smooth approximation of its coefficient. Thus $Q$ is closed, and $\int \rho \Omega ^2=\int A\omega _1/a=1$. The coefficient $\rho $ is smooth and $\Omega $ is continuous, so the product defining $Q$ is well defined. Uniform positivity of $\Omega $ gives $\Omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h>0$ for sufficiently small signed $t$. The bounded-product calculation used in theorem 11.45.9, now with the smooth perturbation $th$, gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u+th) =\Omega ^2+2t\Omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h+t^2(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^2. \]

This proves the two linearizations; mixed components of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ have zero wedge with the diagonal $\Omega $. Distributional Stokes gives $\int L_ uh\, \mathrm{d}\nu =2\int \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge Q=0$.

Invariant probabilities. Choose real spherical harmonics $e_ j$ on the second sphere, with $e_0=1$ and $\Delta _2e_ j=-\lambda _ je_ j$. Only the constant mode has eigenvalue zero. For an invariant probability $\mu $, define finite signed measures $T_ j$ on the first sphere by

\[ \int f\, \mathrm{d}T_ j =\int _ X\frac{f(x)e_ j(y)}{A(x)}\, \mathrm{d}\mu . \]

Testing $L_ u$ on $f(x)e_ j(y)$ gives

\[ \int \left(\Delta _1f-\lambda _ j\frac Ab f\right)\, \mathrm{d}T_ j=0. \]

For $j=0$, testing on spherical harmonics of the first sphere shows that $T_0=C\omega _1$. Their finite span is uniformly dense, because restrictions of polynomials decompose into harmonics and polynomials are dense on the sphere. The first marginal of $\mu $ is therefore $CA\omega _1$, and probability normalization gives $C=1/a$. For every nonconstant mode, lemma 11.45.15 with $V=\lambda _ jA/b>0$ gives $T_ j=0$. As these are identities of measures, their test functions may then be multiplied by the continuous function $A$. They determine all spherical harmonic moments of $\mu $. Uniform density of harmonics and of finite product tests gives $\mu =(A/a)\omega _1\wedge \omega _2$.

Supporting probabilities and weights. Let $\mu \in \mathcal A_\phi (F)$ and, for smooth real $h$, set

\[ F_ t=F+\log \frac{(\Omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^2}{\Omega ^2}. \]

This is continuous, and $u+th$ solves the ordinary-type equation with density $\mathrm{e}^{F_ t}$ and scalar $c_0$. Thus $B_\phi (F_ t)=B_\phi (F)$, by uniqueness of the scalar. Uniform positivity gives $F_ t-F=tL_ uh+O(t^2)$ in the uniform norm. Apply (11.34.1) for both signs of $t$ to obtain $\int L_ uh\, \mathrm{d}\mu =0$. The perturbed density need not depend on the first curve alone: the inequality is the one on all of $C(X)$. Invariant uniqueness and nonemptiness of $\mathcal A_\phi (F)$ from theorem 11.34.2 now prove (11.45.17).

Finally, $\Omega ^2/\mathrm{d}W$ is bounded above and below by positive constants, so a normalized $r$ as in the statement belongs to $L^1(\mathrm{d}W)$ and $r\Omega $ is an order-zero current. Distributional Stokes makes $r\Omega ^2$ invariant. The probability uniqueness just proved gives $r\Omega ^2=\rho \Omega ^2$, hence $r=\rho $ almost everywhere. $\square$

Proposition 11.45.18.132C5E1825BF Let $w_ j\in C^\infty (X)$ be real and suppose

\[ \Omega _ j=\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j>0,\qquad \Omega _ j\longrightarrow \Omega \quad \text{uniformly as }(1,1)\text{-forms}. \]

Put $u_ j=\psi +w_ j$ and $F_ j=\log (\Omega _ j^2/(c_0\mathrm{d}V))$. Let $\rho _ j>0$ be the smooth residual adjoint weight, normalized by

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ j\Omega _ j)=0,\qquad \int _ X\rho _ j\Omega _ j^2=1. \]

Then $F_ j\to F$ uniformly, and the entire sequence satisfies

\[ \rho _ j\Omega _ j^2\rightharpoonup \nu ,\qquad \rho _ j\Omega _ j\rightharpoonup Q,\qquad \rho _ j\, \mathrm{d}W\rightharpoonup \rho \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

Such approximations exist with $w_ j\to w_0$ uniformly and can depend on both curve variables.

Proof. Existence of the smooth positive weights is theorem 11.4.2, applied to the residual metrics $\Omega _ j$ on the surface. Put $\nu _ j=\rho _ j\Omega _ j^2$ and $L_ jh=2\Omega _ j\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h/\Omega _ j^2$. Distributional Stokes gives $\int L_ jh\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j=0$. Uniform convergence to the positive form $\Omega $ supplies a common determinant lower bound and gives $L_ jh\to L_ uh$ uniformly for every fixed smooth $h$. Every weak cluster point of the probabilities $\nu _ j$ is therefore invariant for $L_ u$. By theorem 11.45.16, it equals $\nu $, proving convergence of the whole sequence. The same determinant convergence gives $F_ j\to F$ uniformly.

For a smooth real $(1,1)$ test form $\xi $, the ratios $\Omega _ j\wedge \xi /\Omega _ j^2$ converge uniformly to $\Omega \wedge \xi /\Omega ^2$. Consequently,

\[ \int _ X\rho _ j\Omega _ j\wedge \xi =\int _ X\frac{\Omega _ j\wedge \xi }{\Omega _ j^2}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \longrightarrow \int _ X\rho \Omega \wedge \xi . \]

Similarly, the positive densities $p_ j=\Omega _ j^2/\mathrm{d}W$ converge uniformly to $p=\Omega ^2/\mathrm{d}W$. For every $g\in C(X)$,

\[ \int _ Xg\rho _ j\, \mathrm{d}W =\int _ X(g/p_ j)\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \longrightarrow \int _ X(g/p)\, \mathrm{d}\nu =\int _ Xg\rho \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

These arguments require no pointwise control of the weights.

For existence, heat-smooth $A$ on the first sphere to positive smooth functions $A_ j$ of the same mean $a$, converging uniformly to $A$. Green inversion gives smooth mean-zero $w_ j(x)$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j=(A_ j-a)\omega _1$ and $w_ j\to w_0$ uniformly. Their residuals converge uniformly to $\Omega $. Adding $\varepsilon _ j h(x,y)$ for a fixed smooth function depending on both variables and $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$ preserves positivity for all sufficiently small coefficients and supplies nonsymmetric examples. Each $u_ j$ retains the fixed logarithmic divisor, and its non-pluripolar equation has scalar $c_0$ and logarithmic density $F_ j$. $\square$

Example 11.45.19.3C22B09DCF87 Take $a=1$, $d=1/4$, $q=1$, and let $t$ be the sphere height

\[ t=\frac{|Z_0|^2-|Z_1|^2}{|Z_0|^2+|Z_1|^2},\qquad b=2+t,\qquad H=A=\tfrac 12+|t|. \]

Here $c_0=1$ and $F=\log (2bA)$ is Lipschitz but not $C^1$. The residual has continuous positive coefficients with the same loss of differentiability. Its supporting probability differs from normalized Monge–Ampère volume:

\[ \int _ X\Omega ^2=4,\qquad \int _ Xt\, \mathrm{d}\nu =0,\qquad \int _ Xt\, \frac{\Omega ^2}{4}=\frac5{24}. \]

Even for smooth constant $H$, approximation of the pole by globally smooth potentials need not recover the weak adjoint. For $H=a$, so that $w_0=0$ and $\nu =\mathrm{d}W$, put

\[ \psi _\delta =d\log (\ell +\delta (1-\ell )),\qquad P_\delta =\omega _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\psi _\delta /d),\qquad 0<\delta \leq 1. \]

The smooth potentials $\psi _\delta $ decrease pointwise to $\psi $, converge in $L^1$ and smoothly off $D$, but the classical adjoint probabilities of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _\delta $ converge to

\[ \frac{a\omega _1+d\delta _ p}{a+d}\wedge \omega _2, \qquad p=\{ Z_0=0\} . \]

This limit assigns mass $d/(a+d)>0$ to $D$, and the weighted currents also retain a nonzero divisor component.

Proof. The height $t$ is uniform on $[-1,1]$, so $\int A\omega _1=1$ and $\int tA\omega _1=0$. Since $\Omega ^2=2bA\, \mathrm{d}W$, its mass is four and its normalized height moment is

\[ \frac12\int _{-1}^1 t^2(\tfrac 12+|t|)\, \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{2} =\frac5{24}. \]

The weight is $1/(2b)$ and $\nu =A\, \mathrm{d}W$. Moreover, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta =-2t\, \mathrm{d}W$, so the background has no smooth positive Gauduchon potential perturbation.

For the smoothing family, direct differentiation gives

\[ \frac{P_\delta }{\omega _1} =\frac{4\delta }{((1+\delta )+(1-\delta )t)^2}. \]
