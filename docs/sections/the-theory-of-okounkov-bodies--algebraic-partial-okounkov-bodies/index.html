19.4 Algebraic partial Okounkov bodiesC9B9B0985C6B

C9B9B0985C6B

Let $X$ be a connected smooth complex projective variety of dimension $n$ and $(L,h)$ be a Hermitian big line bundle on $X$.

Let $h_0$ be a smooth Hermitian metric on $L$. Let $\theta =c_1(L,h_0)$. Then we can identify $h$ with a function $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We will use interchangeably the notations $(\theta ,\varphi )$ and $(L,h)$. We assume that $\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }>0$ in this section.

Fix a rank $n$ valuation $\nu \colon \mathbb {C}(X)^{\times }\rightarrow \mathbb {Z}^ n$, which without loss of generality can be assumed to be surjective. We shall frequently use the standard fact that such a full-rank valuation has one-dimensional leaves: for every finite-dimensional subspace $V\subset \mathbb C(X)$,

\[ \# \nu (V^\times )=\dim V. \]

See [Lemma 1.3, LM09] for instance.

We will adopt the notations of section 19.2.

19.4.1 The spaces of sections41E099D72C80

Definition 19.4.2.0F5DB984097B We will write

\[ \begin{aligned} \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )\coloneqq & \left\{ (\nu (s),k) : k\in \mathbb {N}, s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))^{\times } \right\} ,\\ \Delta _ k(\theta ,\varphi )\coloneqq & \mathrm{Conv}\left\{ k^{-1}\nu (s): s\in \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)^{\times } \right\} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where the second set is defined only for those $k$ such that $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))\neq 0$.

When $\varphi =V_{\theta }$, we simply write $\Gamma (L)$ and $\Delta _ k(L)$ instead.

Here and in the sequel, the cross notation means excluding $0$. Here $\mathrm{Conv}$ denotes the convex hull. For large enough $k$, $\Delta _ k(\theta ,\varphi )$ is non-empty thanks to theorem 18.1.46.

Definition 19.4.3.DCCC835656D0 Assume that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities. We define

3A54C829B8CC 19.4.4
\begin{equation} \Gamma ^{\infty }(\theta ,\varphi )\coloneqq \left\{ (\nu (s),k):k\in \mathbb {N}, s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}_{\infty }(k\varphi ))^{\times } \right\} . \end{equation}
Recall that $\mathcal{I}_{\infty }$ is introduced in definition 5.18.13.

For later use, we introduce a twisted version as well.

Definition 19.4.5.51F245B40203 If $T$ is a holomorphic line bundle on $X$, we introduce

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta _{k,T}(\theta ,\varphi )\coloneqq & \mathrm{Conv}\left\{ k^{-1}\nu (s): s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,T\otimes L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))^{\times } \right\} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n,\\ \Delta _{k,T}(L)\coloneqq & \mathrm{Conv}\left\{ k^{-1}\nu (s): s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,T\otimes L^ k)^{\times } \right\} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n \end{aligned} \]

for all $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ such that the relevant spaces of sections are non-zero.

19.4.6 Algebraic Okounkov bodiesA81BAAF5B45F

Proposition 19.4.7.632B1323B741 There is a convex body $\Delta \in \mathcal{K}_ n$ such that $\Gamma (L)\in \mathcal{S}'(\Delta )$.

Here and in the sequel, the notation $\mathcal{S}'(\Delta )$ means $\mathcal{S}'(C(\Delta ))$, where $C(\Delta )$ is the cone over $\Delta $. Recall that the notations $\mathcal{K}_ n$ and $\mathcal{S}'(\Delta )$ are introduced in section 19.7.

Proof. Step 1. We first show that there is $\Delta \in \mathcal{K}_ n$ such that $\Delta _ k(L)\subseteq \Delta $. For this purpose, using 19.3.3, we may assume that $\nu $ is induced by an admissible flag $Y_{\bullet }$ on $X$. We may further assume that each $Y_ i$ is smooth.

Fix $s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k)^{\times }$ for some $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. Assume that $s\neq 0$. We need to show that for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$, $\nu (s)_ i\leq Ck$ for some constant $C>0$, independent of the choices of $k$ and $s$.

Fix an ample divisor $H$ on $X$. Take a large enough integer $b_1>0$ such that

\[ (L-b_1 Y_1)\cdot H^{n-1}<0. \]

Then $\nu (s)_1\leq b_1 k$. Next take a large enough integer $b_2$ such that, for every $a\in [0,b_1]$,

\[ \left((L-aY_1)|_{Y_1}-b_2Y_2\right) \cdot H^{n-2}<0. \]

After removing the first vanishing order of $s$ along $Y_1$, the resulting section on $Y_1$ is a section of $k((L-aY_1)|_{Y_1})$ for some $a\in [0,b_1]$. Hence $\nu (s)_2\leq b_2k$. Continuing in this manner and choosing the constants uniformly over the compact ranges of the preceding normalized vanishing orders, we conclude that $\nu (s)_ i/k$ is bounded for each $i$.

Here the expressions involving $L-aY_1$ are understood numerically. At the $i$-th step the previous normalized vanishing orders lie in a compact polytope, and the intersection number is continuous in these parameters; hence the constants $b_ i$ can be chosen uniformly.

Step 2. Observe that $\Gamma (L)$ is clearly a semigroup. It remains to show that $\Gamma (L)$ generates $\mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$ as an Abelian group.

For this purpose, take two very ample divisors $A$ and $B$ so that $L=\mathcal{O}_ X(A-B)$. After choosing $A$ and $B$ ample enough, we may guarantee that there exist sections $s_0\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,A)$, $t_ i\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,B)$ for $i=0,\dots ,n$ such that

\[ \nu (s_0)=\nu (t_0)=0 \]

and $\nu (t_ i)$ is the $i$-th unit vector $e_ i\in \mathbb {R}^ n$ for $i=1,\ldots ,n$.

Since $L$ is big, we can find $m_0>0$ such that for any $m\geq m_0$, we can find an effective divisor $F_ m$ on $X$ linearly equivalent to $mL-B$. Let $f_ m=\nu ([F_ m])$. Then

\[ (f_ m,m),(f_ m+e_1,m),\dots ,(f_ m+e_ n,m)\in \Gamma (L). \]

Since $(m+1)L$ is linearly equivalent to $A+F_ m$, we have

\[ (f_ m,m+1)\in \Gamma (L). \]

It follows that $\Gamma (L)$ generates $\mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$. $\square$

Thanks to proposition 19.4.7, we can introduce the next definition.

Definition 19.4.8.0114DCC2E021 We define the Okounkov body of $L$ with respect to the valuation $\nu $ as

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(L)\coloneqq \Delta (\Gamma (L)). \]
When $\nu $ is induced by a smooth flag $Y_{\bullet }$ on $X$, we also write $\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}(L)$ instead. The same convention applies to the partial Okounkov bodies studied below as well.

Proposition 19.4.9.572B003B2680 The Okounkov body $\Delta _{\nu }(L)$ depends only on the numerical class of $L$.

See [Proposition 4.1, LM09] for the elegant proof.

Corollary 19.4.10.8494DFD8DF4D We have

10.38
\begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}\Delta _{\nu }(L)=\frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{vol}L. \end{equation}

Proof. This follows immediately from proposition 19.4.7 and theorem 19.2.5. $\square$

Proposition 19.4.11.AE88967EEE84 Assume that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities and $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. Then we have

\[ \Gamma ^{\infty }(\theta ,\varphi )\in \mathcal{S}'(\Delta _\nu (L)) \]

and

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\infty }(\theta ,\varphi )=\frac{1}{n!}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

Proof. By theorem 5.18.8, we can choose a modification $\pi \colon Y\to X$ such that $\pi ^*\varphi $ has log singularities along an effective simple normal crossing $\mathbb Q$-divisor $D$. We regard $\nu $ as a valuation of $\mathbb C(Y)=\mathbb C(X)$. By the definition of $\mathcal I_\infty $, the section spaces defining $\Gamma ^\infty (\theta ,\varphi )$ are naturally identified with

\[ \mathrm{H}^0\left(Y,\pi ^*L^ k\otimes \mathcal O_ Y(-\lceil kD\rceil )\right). \]

Thus we may compute $\Gamma ^\infty (\theta ,\varphi )$ on $Y$.

In this case,

\[ \Gamma ^{\infty }(\theta ,\varphi )= \Biggl \{ (\nu (s),k):k\in \mathbb {N}, s\in \mathrm{H}^0\left(Y,\pi ^*L^ k\otimes \mathcal{O}_ Y(-\lceil k D \rceil )\right)^{\times } \Biggr \} \]

Since $\pi ^*L-D$ is nef and big by 5.18.5, our assertion follows from the same argument as proposition 19.4.7. $\square$

We first extend theorem 19.2.5 to the twisted case.

Proposition 19.4.12.1ED4AF0D9A23 For any holomorphic line bundle $T$ on $X$, as $k\to \infty $

\[ \Delta _{k,T}(L)\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}} \Delta _{\nu }(L). \]

Proof. As $L$ is big, we can take $k_0\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ so that

  1. 6BF24D61A02C

    $T^{-1}\otimes L^{k_0}$ admits a non-zero global holomorphic section $s_0$, and

  2. 0D44384F7F7B

    $T\otimes L^{k_0}$ admits a non-zero global holomorphic section $s_1$.

Then for $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>k_0}$, we have injective linear maps

\[ \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^{k-k_0})\xrightarrow {\times s_1}\mathrm{H}^0(X,T\otimes L^{k})\xrightarrow {\times s_0}\mathrm{H}^0(X, L^{k+k_0}). \]

It follows that

\[ (k-k_0)\Delta _{k-k_0}(L)+\nu (s_1)\subseteq k\Delta _{k,T}(L)\subseteq (k+k_0)\Delta _{k+k_0}(L)-\nu (s_0). \]

Using theorem 19.2.5, we conclude. $\square$

Proposition 19.4.13.AE9EBC9C5252 Let $L'$ be another big line bundle on $X$. Then

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(L)+\Delta _{\nu }(L')\subseteq \Delta _{\nu }(L\otimes L'). \]

Proof. Observe that for each $k\in \mathbb {N}$, we have

\[ \Delta _ k(L)+\Delta _ k(L')\subseteq \Delta _ k(L\otimes L'). \]

So our assertion follows immediately from theorem 19.2.5. $\square$

Proposition 19.4.14.2B94C07754DD For any $a\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we have

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(L^ a)=a\Delta _{\nu }(L). \]

Proof. This is an immediate consequence of theorem 19.2.5. $\square$

19.4.15 Construction of partial Okounkov bodies2A53BABD6994

Theorem 19.4.16.A653D079A694 We have

\[ \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )\in \overline{\mathcal{S}'(\Delta _{\nu }(L))}_{>0}. \]
We refer to definition 19.2.9 for the definition of $\overline{\mathcal{S}'(\Delta _{\nu }(L))}_{>0}$. This theorem allows us to give the following definition:

Definition 19.4.17.FAA070EFD0CC The partial Okounkov body of $(L,h)$ is defined as

076205529197 19.4.18
\begin{equation} \Delta _{\nu }(L,h)=\Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi )\coloneqq \Delta \left(\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )\right). \end{equation}
When $\nu $ is induced by an admissible flag $Y_{\bullet }$ on $X$ (see definition 19.3.2), we also say that $\Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi )$ is the partial Okounkov body of $(L,h)$ or of $(\theta ,\varphi )$ with respect to $Y_{\bullet }$. In this case, we also write $\Delta _{Y_{\bullet }}$ instead of $\Delta _{\nu }$.

Note that when $h$ has minimal singularities, we have

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(L,h)=\Delta _{\nu }(L). \]

So partial Okounkov bodies generalize Okounkov bodies.

Corollary 19.4.19.56E59F7A3150 We have

3DAADFC49A31 19.4.20
\begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}\Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi )=\frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \end{equation}

We will prove theorem 19.4.16 and corollary 19.4.19 at the same time. The proof relies on the pseudometric $d_{\mathrm{sg}}$ introduced in (19.2.1).

Proof. Step 1. We first assume that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities and $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current.

We claim that

2AE36B08DED5 19.4.21
\begin{equation} d_{\mathrm{sg}}(\Gamma ^{\infty }(\theta ,\varphi ),\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi ))=0. \end{equation}

Observe that for each $\epsilon \in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}$, we have

\[ \mathrm{H}^0\Bigl (X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}_{\infty }(k\varphi )\Bigr )\subseteq \mathrm{H}^0\Bigl (X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\Bigr )\subseteq \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}_{\infty }(k(1-\epsilon )\varphi )\right) \]

for all large enough $k$. This is a consequence of 5.18.15. Therefore, it suffices to show that

\[ \lim _{\mathbb {Q}\ni \epsilon \to 0+}\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\infty }(\theta ,(1-\epsilon )\varphi )=\operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\infty }(\theta ,\varphi ). \]

This follows from the explicit formula in proposition 19.4.11.

Step 2. We next handle the case where $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current.

Let $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ be a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ by corollary 18.1.14.

In this case, it suffices to prove that

653C3FE433F4 19.4.22
\begin{equation} \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi _ j)\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{sg}}} \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi ). \end{equation}

In fact, by theorem 18.1.46, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} & d_{\mathrm{sg}}\left(\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi _ j),\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )\right) \\ =& \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } k^{-n} \left( h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi _ j)\right)-h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\right)\\ = & \lim _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi _ j)\right)-\lim _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right) \\ =& \frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ j}-\frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \end{aligned} \]

Letting $j\to \infty $, we conclude (19.4.22) by theorem 16.9.57.

Step 3. Now we only assume that $\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }>0$. We may replace $\varphi $ with $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ and then assume that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Take a potential $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\psi \leq \varphi $ and $\theta _{\psi }$ is a Kähler current. The existence of $\psi $ is proved in 13.13.47. For each $\epsilon \in (0,1)$, let $\varphi _{\epsilon }=(1-\epsilon )\varphi +\epsilon \psi $. It suffices to show that

\[ \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi _{\epsilon })\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{sg}}}\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi ) \]

as $\epsilon \to 0+$. We compute using theorem 18.1.46:

\[ \begin{aligned} & d_{\mathrm{sg}}\left(\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi _{\epsilon }),\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )\right)\\ =& \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } k^{-n} \left( h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)- h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi _{\epsilon })\right) \right)\\ =& \lim _{k\to \infty }k^{-n}h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)-\lim _{k\to \infty }k^{-n}h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi _{\epsilon })\right)\\ =& \frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }- \frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _{\epsilon }}\\ \to & 0 \end{aligned} \]

by theorem 16.9.57, as $\epsilon \to 0+$. Combining the three steps, $\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )$ lies in $\overline{\mathcal S'(\Delta _\nu (L))}_{>0}$. This proves theorem 19.4.16. Moreover, by the continuity of the volume on $\overline{\mathcal S}(\Delta _\nu (L))$ and by theorem 18.1.46,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )=\lim _{k\to \infty }k^{-n}h^0(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal I(k\varphi ))=\frac{1}{n!}\operatorname{vol}\theta _\varphi . \]

Since $\operatorname{vol}\Delta (\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi ))=\operatorname{vol}\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )$ by theorem 19.2.10, (19.4.20) follows. $\square$

19.4.25 Basic properties of partial Okounkov bodies1FAA4BD1CBCB

Proposition 19.4.26.97A03B9F1F87 The partial Okounkov body $\Delta _{\nu }(L,h)$ depends only on $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$, not on the explicit choices of $L,h_0,h$.

Thanks to this result, given a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T\in c_1(L)$ on $X$ with $\int _ X T^ n>0$, we can write
\[ \Delta _{\nu }(T)\coloneqq \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi ) \]

if $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ for some $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. There are two different claims to prove, as detailed in the two steps below.

Step 1. Let $h_0'$ be another Hermitian metric on $L$. Set $\theta '=c_1(L,h_0')$. Write $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\theta -\theta '$. Let $\varphi '=\varphi +f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$. Then

C90692A83F74 19.4.27
\begin{equation} \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi )=\Delta _{\nu }(\theta ',\varphi '). \end{equation}

This is obvious since $\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )=\Gamma (\theta ',\varphi ')$.

Step 2. Let $L'$ be another big line bundle on $X$. By Step 1, we may assume that the reference Hermitian metric $h'_0$ on $L'$ is such that $c_1(L',h'_0)=\theta $.

Let $h'$ be a plurisubharmonic metric on $L'$ with $c_1(L,h)=c_1(L',h')$. Then

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(L,h)=\Delta _{\nu }(L',h'). \]

By the approximation step in the construction of $\Delta _\nu (L,h)$ and the continuity of the extension in theorem 19.2.10, it is enough to prove the assertion when $c_1(L,h)$ has analytic singularities. After taking a birational resolution, it suffices to deal with the case where $c_1(L,h)$ has analytic singularities along an effective $\mathbb {Q}$-divisor $D$. By rescaling, we may also assume that $D$ is a divisor. By 19.4.23, we further reduce to the case where $c_1(L,h)$ is not singular.

In this case, the assertion is proved in proposition 19.4.9. $\square$

Proposition 19.4.28.1934C9D19460 Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Assume that $\varphi \preceq _{\mathcal{I}} \psi $. Then

44ACC02FAF04 19.4.29
\begin{equation} \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi ) \subseteq \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\psi ). \end{equation}
In particular, we always have
\[ \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi )\subseteq \Delta _{\nu }(L). \]

Proof. This follows from corollary 19.2.13. $\square$

Theorem 19.4.30.30EEF3A985B7 The Okounkov body map

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\bullet ):\left( \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0},d_ S\right)\rightarrow \left(\mathcal{K}_ n,d_{\mathrm{Haus}}\right) \]

is continuous.

Proof. Let $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ be a $d_ S$-convergent sequence in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. We want to show that

6AAC6A3D90A2 19.4.31
\begin{equation} \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi _ j)\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}\Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi ). \end{equation}

Replacing the potentials by their $P$-envelopes does not change their partial Okounkov bodies, since $P$-equivalent potentials are $\mathcal{I}$-equivalent, nor does it change their $d_ S$-classes. Thus we may assume that all $\varphi _ j$'s and $\varphi $ are model potentials.

By proposition 16.9.17, after passing to a subsequence we can sandwich $\varphi _ j$ between a decreasing sequence and an increasing sequence, both converging to $\varphi $ in $d_ S$. Using proposition 19.4.28 and theorem 19.7.4, it is enough to prove (19.4.31) when $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is monotone. By theorem 16.9.49, replacing $\varphi _ j$ and $\varphi $ by their $\mathcal{I}$-envelopes preserves this reduction, so we may further assume that the $\varphi _ j$'s and $\varphi $ are $\mathcal{I}$-model. In both cases, we claim that

\[ \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi _ j)\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{sg}}} \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi ) \]

as $j\to \infty $. In fact, using theorem 18.1.46, we can compute

\[ \begin{aligned} d_{\mathrm{sg}}\left(\Gamma (\theta ,\varphi _ j), \Gamma (\theta ,\varphi )\right) = & \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}\left| h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi _ j)\right)-h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right) \right|\\ =& \frac{1}{n!} \left| \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi _ j}-\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }\right|, \end{aligned} \]

which converges to $0$ by theorem 16.9.57. $\square$

Proposition 19.4.32.CFE94018A80A Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a modification. Then

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(\pi ^*L,\pi ^*h)=\Delta _{\nu }(L,h). \]

Proof. Thanks to proposition 16.6.10, we may assume that $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-model. By theorem 18.1.2, we can find a sequence $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ with analytic singularities in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. It is clear that $\pi ^*\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\pi ^*\varphi $. By theorem 19.4.30, we may then reduce to the case where $\varphi $ has analytic singularities. In this case, it suffices to apply 19.4.23. $\square$

Proposition 19.4.33.86E922DD5140 Let $(L', h')$ be another Hermitian big line bundle on $X$. Then

\[ \Delta _{\nu }(L,h)+\Delta _{\nu }(L', h')\subseteq \Delta _{\nu }(L\otimes L',h\otimes h'). \]

Proof. Take a Hermitian metric $h_0'$ on $L'$ and let $\theta '=c_1(L',h_0')$. We identify $h'$ with $\varphi '\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$. Then we need to show

70AAFA1F54AB 19.4.34
\begin{equation} \Delta _{\nu }(\theta ,\varphi )+\Delta _{\nu }(\theta ',\varphi ')\subseteq \Delta _{\nu }(\theta +\theta ',\varphi +\varphi '). \end{equation}

We observe that

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}+P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']_{\mathcal{I}}\sim _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi +\varphi '. \]

Thus, after replacing $\varphi $ and $\varphi '$ by their $\mathcal{I}$-envelopes, in view of proposition 19.4.28, we may assume that $\varphi $ and $\varphi '$ are $\mathcal{I}$-good.

By theorem 18.1.2, we can find sequences $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ and $(\varphi '_ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$ respectively such that

  1. 9C52F43A7B75

    $\varphi _ j$ and $\varphi '_ j$ both have analytic singularities for all $j\geq 1$, and

  2. 3F2CA81FC88F

    $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $, $\varphi '_ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi '$.

Then $\varphi _ j+\varphi '_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$ and $\varphi _ j+\varphi '_ j\xrightarrow {d_ S} \varphi +\varphi '$ by theorem 16.9.45. Thus, by theorem 19.4.30, we may assume that $\varphi $ and $\varphi '$ both have analytic singularities. Taking a birational resolution, we may further assume that they have log singularities. By 19.4.23, we reduce to the case without singularities, in which case the result is just proposition 19.4.13. $\square$
