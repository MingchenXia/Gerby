18.11 Transport with an obstacle2C83B2C35668

2C83B2C35668

A smooth change of background $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $ identifies $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega ')$ by $u\mapsto u-\chi $, but it moves the zero obstacle to $-\chi $. To compare the topologies in different backgrounds, we therefore need envelopes below an arbitrary smooth obstacle.

For a smooth real obstacle $F$, we use the ordinary envelope $P_{\omega ,F}[u]$ of definition 5.40.4, and we write $P_{\omega ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I}$ for the ideal envelope defined with $v\leq F$ in place of $v\leq 0$. The competitor family of the latter contains a translate of $u$. Replacing a competitor by its maximum with this translate shows that the ideal envelope may equivalently be defined with the condition $v\preceq _{\mathcal I}u$. The same notation applies to any smooth real background that admits $u$. Define $\tau _{\omega ,F}$ by replacing the two envelope coordinates in proposition 18.8.4 by these obstacle envelopes.

Proposition 18.11.1.760C8726BAE2 Let $\chi \in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$ and $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi >0$. The map $[u]\mapsto [u-\chi ]$ is a uniform isomorphism

\[ (\mathfrak S(X,\omega ),\tau _{\omega ,F}) \longrightarrow (\mathfrak S(X,\omega '),\tau _{\omega ',F-\chi }). \]

For $s>0$, $[u]\mapsto [su]$ is a uniform isomorphism from $\tau _{\omega ,F}$ to $\tau _{s\omega ,sF}$. Both maps transport the ordinary and the ideal envelopes exactly.

Proof. For either envelope operator $P$, the correspondence of competitors gives

\[ P_{\omega ',F-\chi }[u-\chi ]=P_{\omega ,F}[u]-\chi , \qquad P_{s\omega ,sF}[su]=sP_{\omega ,F}[u]. \]

Smooth additions leave all multiplier ideals and divisorial Lelong numbers unchanged. Scaling multiplies the divisorial and envelope distances by $s$ and changes the ideal weights by $t\mapsto st$. Each map and its inverse therefore transport the generating entourages to generating entourages. $\square$

For two smooth obstacles, translation of competitors gives

\[ P_{\omega ,F}[u]+\min _ X(G-F) \leq P_{\omega ,G}[u] \leq P_{\omega ,F}[u]+\max _ X(G-F), \]

and the same inequalities hold for the ideal envelopes. By the local construction in theorem 13.14.3, the extended measures satisfy $\mathrm{MA}_{\omega '}(u-\chi )=\mathrm{MA}_\omega (u)$ and $\mathrm{MA}_{s\omega }(su)=s^ n\mathrm{MA}_\omega (u)$, also when the total mass is infinite. If instead the zero obstacle is kept in both backgrounds, the transport gives $P_{\omega ',0}[u-\chi ]=P_{\omega ,\chi }[u]-\chi $, so the change of background compares the obstacles $0$ and $\chi $. The bounded comparison of obstacles does not make this change continuous, even on a curve.

Proposition 18.11.2.8B44DB3F02D3 Let $Y$ be a compact connected Riemann surface and $\theta >0$ a smooth form of mass one. If $F$ is a nonconstant smooth function with $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F>0$, then the bijection

\[ \Gamma _ F:(\mathfrak S(Y,\theta ),\tau _{\theta ,0}) \longrightarrow (\mathfrak S(Y,\theta '),\tau _{\theta ',0}), \qquad [u]\longmapsto [u-F], \]

and its inverse are both discontinuous. Equivalently, $\tau _{\theta ,0}$ and $\tau _{\theta ,F}$ are incomparable topologies on the same space of ordinary types.

Proof. The smooth function $\theta '/\theta $ has integral one against the probability measure $\theta $, and it is not constant: otherwise $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F=0$, and the harmonic function $F$ would be constant on $Y$. Choose $c$ with $\min _ Y(\theta '/\theta )<c<1$, so that $\theta '\not\geq c\theta $.

Next choose probability measures $\mu _ j$ without atoms, each carried by a polar set, that converge weakly to $\theta $. To construct them, start with finite atomic probability measures converging weakly to $\theta $, as in theorem 18.6.4, and replace each atom by a nonatomic probability measure carried by a polar Cantor set in a small coordinate disk about that atom, keeping its weight; the construction in example 13.28.14 provides such measures in every disk. If the diameters of the disks tend to zero uniformly, uniform continuity of continuous test functions preserves weak convergence, and the finite union of the polar carriers is polar.

Let $q_ j$ be the normalized Green potentials with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_ j=\mu _ j$ and $\sup _ Yq_ j=0$. By normalized compactness, the sequence has $L^1$ cluster values, and each of them has curvature $\theta $ and supremum zero, hence vanishes. Thus $q_ j\to 0$ in $L^1$. Put $u_ j=cq_ j$. Then

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j=(1-c)\theta +c\mu _ j,\qquad u_ j\leq P_\theta [u_ j]\leq 0. \]

Since $\mu _ j$ has no atoms, all point Lelong numbers of $u_ j$ vanish. Because modifications of a smooth curve are isomorphisms, theorem 5.37.3 gives trivial multiplier ideals at every weight. Thus $P_\theta [u_ j]_{\mathcal I}=0$, all controlled ideal relations with $[0]$ hold, and every divisorial distance to $[0]$ vanishes. The bounds above give convergence of the ordinary envelopes to zero in $L^1$. Consequently $[u_ j]\to [0]$ in $\tau _{\theta ,0}$.

Set $v_ j=P_{\theta ,F}[u_ j]$. Every competitor $h\preceq u_ j$ satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\geq c\mu _ j$, by corollary 5.27.15. The family of competitors below $F$ contains a translate of $u_ j$ and is stable under finite maxima, so Choquet selection and distributional convergence of increasing maxima show that $v_ j$ satisfies the same inequality. If $[u_ j]\to [0]$ in $\tau _{\theta ,F}$, the ordinary-envelope coordinate would give $v_ j\to P_{\theta ,F}[0]=F$ in $L^1$, because $F$ is a bounded $\theta $-plurisubharmonic competitor. Passing to the limit in the current inequality would then give $\theta '\geq c\theta $, a contradiction.

By proposition 18.11.1, $[u]\mapsto [u-F]$ is a uniform isomorphism from $\tau _{\theta ,F}$ onto $\tau _{\theta ',0}$, so the sequence above shows that $\Gamma _ F$ is discontinuous. The same argument applied to $\theta '$ and $-F$ shows that the inverse is discontinuous. Under this transport, continuity of $\Gamma _ F$ and of its inverse are precisely the two comparisons between $\tau _{\theta ,0}$ and $\tau _{\theta ,F}$, which proves incomparability. $\square$

The sequence $u_ j$ has trivial multiplier ideals, and its polar curvature in the sense of subsection 5.27.13, that is, the restriction $\mathbf1_{\{ u_ j=-\infty \} }(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)$ of its curvature to its pole set, is $c\mu _ j$. By the classification of curve models through their polar curvature in lemma 13.28.1 and the bounded obstacle comparison, its ordinary envelopes in both backgrounds have non-pluripolar mass $1-c$. The discontinuity therefore persists among models whose masses are bounded below by a fixed positive constant. On the same curve, the total variation of polar curvature is a complete metric on ordinary models, by theorem 13.28.4, and after ordinary projection it is invariant under smooth changes of background; it keeps the diffuse curvature that the sequence above loses.
