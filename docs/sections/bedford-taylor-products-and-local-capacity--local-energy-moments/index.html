7.11 Local energy moments867F8B92120A

867F8B92120A

To solve $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=\mu $ for a measure $\mu $ which need not be absolutely continuous, we approximate $\mu $ by the measures $\mu _ j$ obtained by averaging $\mu $ over the cubes of finer and finer grids, and we solve the equations for $\mu _ j$ by theorem 7.10.2. Each $\mu _ j$ has a bounded density, but the bounds are not uniform in $j$, so the arguments of the preceding section do not apply.

A bounded subsolution provides a substitute. If $\mu \le (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ for a bounded plurisubharmonic function $w$, then $\mu $ integrates the powers $(-\varphi )^ p$ of plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi $ with zero boundary values, with a bound in terms of the local energy moments $\int (-\varphi )^ p(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n$ (theorem 7.11.2). The cube averages inherit this control (proposition 7.11.5). In the subsections that follow, this control replaces the common density bound. It yields convergence of the energy integrals and convergence in capacity of the solutions for $\mu _ j$, and hence the solution of the Dirichlet problem for compactly supported measures $\mu $ of this kind (theorem 7.11.30). The section closes with an inequality for mixed energies (theorem 7.11.33), which is needed in section 7.12. Throughout this section, $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ is a ball, $n\geq 1$.

Definition 7.11.1.E1129C8F823C Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, be a ball. We denote by $\mathcal E_0(B)$ the class of bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi \leq 0$ on $B$ such that

\[ \lim _{B\ni z\to \zeta }\varphi (z)=0\quad (\zeta \in \partial B), \qquad \int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n<\infty . \]

For $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$ and real $p\geq 1$, the local energy moment of order $p$ is

\[ e_ p(\varphi )=\int _ B(-\varphi )^ p(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n. \]
This class is due to Cegrell; see subsection 25.1.9. The zero function belongs to $\mathcal E_0(B)$. Since $\varphi $ is bounded and its Monge–Ampère measure has finite mass, every moment $e_ p(\varphi )$ is finite. Since $\partial B$ is compact, the boundary limit is uniform: for every $s>0$ there is a compact set $K_ s\subset B$ such that $\varphi >-s$ on $B\setminus K_ s$. Indeed, every $\zeta \in \partial B$ has an open neighborhood $N_\zeta $ with $\varphi >-s$ on $N_\zeta \cap B$; finitely many of these neighborhoods cover $\partial B$, and we may take for $K_ s$ the complement of their union in $\overline B$. In particular, each set $\{ \varphi \leq -s\} $, $s>0$, lies in a compact subset of $B$.

Theorem 7.11.2.05F71889C324 Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a ball, and let $\mu $ be a finite positive Borel measure on $B$ such that $\mu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ for some bounded plurisubharmonic function $w$ on $B$. Put $m=\mu (B)$ and $M=\sup _ Bw-\inf _ Bw$, and for real $p\geq 1$ put

\[ \kappa _{n,p}=2^{n+p}(n+1)!M^ n, \qquad A_ p=\Bigl (1+\frac pn\Bigr )\kappa _{n,p}^{p/(n+p)}m^{n/(n+p)}. \]

Then, for every $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$, every real $p\geq 1$ and every $t>0$,

D1E50BAC729C 7.11.3
\begin{equation} \mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} ) \leq \min \{ m,\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )t^{-(n+p)}\} . \end{equation}

Consequently,

9778E8C0159E 7.11.4
\begin{equation} \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu \leq A_ p\, e_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}. \end{equation}

The constant $A_ p$ depends only on $n$, $p$, $m$ and $M$.

Proof. We prove the tail bound by applying Błocki's estimate (proposition 7.4.5) to truncations of $\varphi $, and then integrate it. For $s>0$ let

\[ h_ s=\max \{ \varphi ,-s\} ,\qquad d_ s=h_ s-\varphi =(-\varphi -s)_+. \]

By the remark following definition 7.11.1, there is a compact set $K_ s\subset B$ with $\varphi >-s$ on $B\setminus K_ s$, and since $K_ s$ is compact there is a ball $B_ s\Subset B$, concentric with $B$, which contains $K_ s$. Thus $h_ s=\varphi $ on $B_ s\setminus K_ s$, which is a collar of $\partial B_ s$, and $d_ s$ vanishes outside $K_ s$. The functions $\varphi $, $h_ s$ and $w$ are bounded and plurisubharmonic near $\overline{B_ s}$, $\varphi \le h_ s$, and the oscillation of $w$ on $B_ s$ is at most $M$. Błocki's estimate on $B_ s$, together with $\mu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$, therefore gives

\[ \int _ B d_ s^{n+1}\, \mathrm d\mu \leq \int _{B_ s}d_ s^{n+1}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n \leq (n+1)!M^ n\int _{B_ s}d_ s(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n. \]

Now take $s=t/2$. On $\{ \varphi <-t\} $ we have $d_ s>t/2$. On the other hand, $0\le d_ s\le -\varphi $, and $d_ s=0$ where $-\varphi \le t/2$; since $p\geq 1$, we have $-\varphi \le (t/2)^{1-p}(-\varphi )^ p$ where $-\varphi >t/2$, and hence

\[ \int _{B_ s}d_ s(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n \leq \int _{\{ -\varphi >t/2\} }(-\varphi )(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n \leq (t/2)^{1-p}e_ p(\varphi ). \]

Combining the two estimates and dividing by $(t/2)^{n+1}$, we obtain

\[ \mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )\le (n+1)!M^ n(t/2)^{-n-p}e_ p(\varphi )=\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )t^{-(n+p)}. \]

Since also $\mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )\le m$, this proves (7.11.3).

If $m=0$, both sides of (7.11.4) vanish. If $\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )=0$, then (7.11.3) shows that $\mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )=0$ for every $t>0$, so the left side of (7.11.4) vanishes. Otherwise put

\[ T=\left(\frac{\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )}m\right)^{1/(n+p)}. \]

The first equality below is the layer-cake formula, which follows from Tonelli's theorem applied to the nonnegative function $(z,t)\mapsto pt^{p-1}\mathbf1_{\{ \varphi (z)<-t\} }$ on $B\times (0,\infty )$. Using (7.11.3) with the bound $m$ for $t<T$ and the second bound for $t\ge T$, we obtain

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu & =p\int _0^\infty t^{p-1}\mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )\, \mathrm dt \leq mT^ p +p\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )\int _ T^\infty t^{-n-1}\, \mathrm dt\\ & =mT^ p+\frac pn\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )T^{-n}. \end{aligned} \]

By the choice of $T$,

\[ mT^ p=\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )T^{-n}=\kappa _{n,p}^{p/(n+p)}m^{n/(n+p)}e_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}, \]

so the right-hand side equals $(1+p/n)mT^ p=A_ pe_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}$. This proves (7.11.4). $\square$

We now show that the cube averages of a compactly supported measure $\mu $ as in theorem 7.11.2 satisfy the same moment estimate up to a constant depending only on $n$ and $p$. We call the solutions $U_ j$ of $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ j)^ n=\mu _ j$ with zero boundary values constructed in the following proposition the canonical approximants of $\mu $ (for the chosen grids).

Proposition 7.11.5.1151DF7B32E4 Let $\mu $, $w$, $m$ and $A_ p$ be as in theorem 7.11.2, and assume moreover that the support $K$ of $\mu $ is a compact subset of $B$. Extend $\mu $ by zero to $\mathbb C^ n$. Let $h_ j\downarrow 0$, and for each $j$ let $\mathcal Q_ j$ be a partition of $\mathbb C^ n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ into half-open cubes of side length $h_ j$. Put

\[ \mu _ j=\sum _{Q\in \mathcal Q_ j}\frac{\mu (Q)}{|Q|}\mathbf1_ Q\, \mathrm dV, \]

where $|Q|=h_ j^{2n}$ is the volume of $Q$ and $\mathrm dV=\mathrm dV_{2n}$ is Lebesgue measure. For $z\in \mathbb C^ n$ and $r>0$ let $P(z,r)=\{ \zeta \in \mathbb C^ n:|\zeta _ k-z_ k|<r,\ 1\le k\le n\} $ be the polydisc of radius $r$ centered at $z$. Then $\mu _ j\to \mu $ weakly. Moreover, there are an index $j_0$ and, for every real $p\geq 1$, a constant $D_{n,p}>0$ depending only on $n$ and $p$, such that the following hold for all $j\geq j_0$ and $p\geq 1$.

  1. The measure $\mu _ j$ has a bounded density and compact support in $B$, and $\mu _ j(B)=m$.

  2. If $Q\in \mathcal Q_ j$ is a cube which meets $K$ and $z\in Q$, then $P(z,4\sqrt2\, h_ j)\subset B$, and every plurisubharmonic function $v\le 0$ on $P(z,4\sqrt2\, h_ j)$ satisfies

    CC0546ABBCE7 7.11.6
    \begin{equation} \frac1{|Q|}\int _ Q(-v)^ p\, \mathrm dV \leq D_{n,p}(-v(z))^ p, \end{equation}

    where the right side is $+\infty $ if $v(z)=-\infty $.

  3. Every $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$ satisfies

    00E17178E4CB 7.11.7
    \begin{equation} \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu _ j \leq D_{n,p}A_ p e_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}. \end{equation}

  4. The bounded plurisubharmonic solution $U_ j$ of $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ j)^ n=\mu _ j$ with zero boundary values, given by theorem 7.10.2, belongs to $\mathcal E_0(B)$ and satisfies $e_ p(U_ j)\leq (D_{n,p}A_ p)^{(n+p)/n}$.

Finally, the sequence $(U_ j)_{j\geq j_0}$ is relatively compact in $L^1(B)$, and the limit of every subsequence converging in $L^1(B)$ has a nonpositive plurisubharmonic representative.

Proof. If $m=0$, then $\mu _ j=0$ and $U_ j=0$ for all $j$, and all assertions hold. Assume that $m>0$, put $d=\operatorname {dist}(K,\mathbb C^ n\setminus B)>0$, and choose $j_0$ such that $h_ j<d/(6\sqrt{2n})$ for $j\geq j_0$; in the rest of the proof we consider only $j\geq j_0$. Since $\mu $ is a finite Borel measure, it is a Radon measure (proposition 2.2.2), so $\mu $ vanishes outside its support $K$. Hence a cube $Q$ with $\mu (Q)>0$ meets $K$, and as its diameter is $\sqrt{2n}\, h_ j<d/6$, it lies in the $(d/6)$-neighborhood of $K$, which is contained in $B$. These cubes are disjoint, each has volume $h_ j^{2n}$, and they lie in a bounded set; so there are only finitely many of them. Therefore $\mu _ j$ is a finite sum of measures with constant densities on cubes contained in $B$, so it has a bounded density and compact support in $B$, and $\mu _ j(B)=\sum _ Q\mu (Q)=m$. This proves (1).

For the weak convergence, let $g$ be a continuous function on $\mathbb C^ n$. Then

\[ \int g\, \mathrm d\mu _ j-\int g\, \mathrm d\mu =\sum _{Q\in \mathcal Q_ j}\int _ Q\left(\frac1{|Q|}\int _ Qg\, \mathrm dV-g\right)\mathrm d\mu , \]

and on every cube with $\mu (Q)>0$ the integrand is bounded by the modulus of continuity of $g$ at $\sqrt{2n}\, h_ j$ on the closed $(d/6)$-neighborhood of $K$. Hence the difference tends to zero.

To prove (2), fix a cube $Q\in \mathcal Q_ j$ which meets $K$ and a point $z\in Q$, and put $\varrho =4\sqrt2\, h_ j$. Every point of $Q$ differs from $z$ by less than $\sqrt2\, h_ j$ in each complex coordinate, so $Q\subset P(z,\varrho /2)$. Moreover $P(z,\varrho )$ lies in the Euclidean ball of radius $4\sqrt{2n}\, h_ j$ about $z$, and $\operatorname {dist}(z,K)\le \sqrt{2n}\, h_ j$, so this polydisc lies in the $(5d/6)$-neighborhood of $K$ and hence in $B$. Let $v\le 0$ be a plurisubharmonic function on $P(z,\varrho )$; if $v(z)=-\infty $, there is nothing to prove, so we assume that $v(z)>-\infty $. The function $\zeta \mapsto v(z+\varrho \zeta )$ is plurisubharmonic and nonpositive on the unit polydisc (proposition 5.9.3), with the finite value $v(z)$ at the origin. The first estimate of proposition 5.10.8, with its constant $C_{n,p}$, and the change of variables $\zeta \mapsto z+\varrho \zeta $, whose Jacobian is $\varrho ^{2n}$, give

\[ \int _ Q(-v)^ p\, \mathrm dV\le \int _{P(z,\varrho /2)}(-v)^ p\, \mathrm dV\le \varrho ^{2n}C_{n,p}^ p(-v(z))^ p. \]

Since $|Q|=h_ j^{2n}$ and $(\varrho /h_ j)^{2n}=32^ n$, we obtain (7.11.6) with $D_{n,p}=32^ nC_{n,p}^ p$.

For (3), let $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$. Since $\varphi \le 0$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$, (7.11.6) applies to $v=\varphi $ at every point $z$ of every cube with $\mu (Q)>0$. Integrating over $z\in Q$ with respect to $\mu $ and summing over the cubes, we obtain

\[ \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu _ j =\sum _{Q\in \mathcal Q_ j}\frac{\mu (Q)}{|Q|}\int _ Q(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm dV \leq D_{n,p}\int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu , \]

and (7.11.7) follows from (7.11.4).

For (4), note that $\mu _ j$ equals $f_ j\eta ^ n$ for a bounded density $f_ j\ge 0$, so the solution $U_ j$ exists by theorem 7.10.2, applied with zero boundary values. Since $U_ j$ is subharmonic (theorem 5.9.1) with boundary limits $0$, the maximum principle (proposition 3.4.7) gives $U_ j\le 0$, and its Monge–Ampère measure has finite mass $\mu _ j(B)=m$; hence $U_ j\in \mathcal E_0(B)$. Taking $\varphi =U_ j$ in (7.11.7), we get

\[ e_ p(U_ j)=\int _ B(-U_ j)^ p\, \mathrm d\mu _ j\le D_{n,p}A_ pe_ p(U_ j)^{p/(n+p)}. \]

If $e_ p(U_ j)>0$, dividing by $e_ p(U_ j)^{p/(n+p)}$ gives the asserted bound; if $e_ p(U_ j)=0$, there is nothing to prove.

Finally write $B=B(a,R)$ and $\rho (z)=|z-a|^2-R^2$. The function $\rho $ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $B$, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^ n=\eta ^ n=n!\pi ^{-n}\, \mathrm dV$. Applying theorem 7.11.2 to the finite measure $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^ n$ on $B$, with $p=2$, and using the uniform bound for $e_2(U_ j)$, we obtain $S=\sup _ j\int _ BU_ j^2\, \mathrm dV<\infty $. Since $U_ j\le 0$, theorem 5.10.4 shows that every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)$ to a plurisubharmonic function $u$; convergence to $-\infty $ is excluded by the $L^2$ bound. Exhausting $B$ by compact sets and passing to a diagonal subsequence, we may assume that $U_ j\to u$ almost everywhere on $B$, so Fatou's lemma gives $\int _ Bu^2\, \mathrm dV\le S$. For a compact set $L\subset B$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\[ \int _{B\setminus L}|U_ j-u|\, \mathrm dV\le 2S^{1/2}|B\setminus L|^{1/2}, \]

which is small when $|B\setminus L|$ is small. Since $U_ j\to u$ in $L^1(L)$, it follows that $U_ j\to u$ in $L^1(B)$ along this subsequence. In particular, if a subsequence of $(U_ j)$ converges in $L^1(B)$, its limit is represented by such a function $u$. Finally $u\le 0$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, by the uniqueness assertion of theorem 5.9.1 applied to $\max \{ u,0\} $ and $0$. $\square$

7.11.8 Convergence of energy integrals6293A9FE1CA2

We shall identify the limit $u$ of a convergent subsequence of canonical approximants as a solution of $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=\mu $. For this we must pass to the limit in integrals such as $\int U_ j\, \mathrm d\mu _ j$, in which both the integrand and the measure depend on $j$, and the energy bounds of proposition 7.11.5 control only the large negative values of the integrand. We first prove that $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergence of uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions implies $L^1$ convergence against any fixed finite measure that does not charge sets of outer capacity zero (lemma 7.11.9). Writing integrals against $\mu _ j$ as integrals of cube averages against $\mu $, we then transfer this continuity to the varying measures $\mu _ j$ (theorem 7.11.11).

Lemma 7.11.9.504F4EDE59EF Let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a bounded open set, and let $\nu $ be a finite positive Borel measure on $D$ such that $\nu (E)=0$ for every Borel set $E\subset D$ satisfying

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_ D^*(E\cap K)=0\qquad \text{for every compact }K\subset D. \]

Let $L\geq 0$, let $v_ j$ be plurisubharmonic functions on $D$ with $|v_ j|\leq L$, and suppose that $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, where $v$ is the plurisubharmonic representative of the limit (theorem 5.10.4, applied on each connected component of $D$). Then

\[ \int _ D|v_ j-v|\, \mathrm d\nu \longrightarrow 0. \]

The hypothesis on $\nu $ holds if $\nu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ for a bounded plurisubharmonic function $w$ on $D$.

Proof. We show that $\varlimsup _ jv_ j=v$ $\nu $-almost everywhere (Step 1) and that $\int v_ j\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int v\, \mathrm d\nu $ (Step 2), and combine the two facts (Step 3). First, the bounds $-L\le v\le L$ hold almost everywhere: for every compact set $K\subset D$ we have $(|v|-L)_+\le |v-v_ j|$, so $\int _ K(|v|-L)_+\, \mathrm dV\le \| v_ j-v\| _{L^1(K)}\to 0$. They hold everywhere by the uniqueness assertion of theorem 5.9.1, applied on each connected component of $D$ to the plurisubharmonic functions $\max \{ v,-L\} $ and $v$, and to $\max \{ v,L\} $ and the constant $L$.

Step 1. The upper limit of $v_ j$. We show that

0B2BC6EDF748 7.11.10
\begin{equation} \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }v_ j=v\qquad \nu \text{-almost everywhere}. \end{equation}

For $\ell \ge 1$ let $b_\ell =(\sup _{j\geq \ell }v_ j)^*$ be the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum. By theorem 5.10.2, these are plurisubharmonic functions with values in $[-L,L]$, and they decrease as $\ell $ increases. We claim that $b_\ell \downarrow v$ everywhere. Let $z\in D$ and $\varepsilon >0$. By upper semicontinuity, $v<v(z)+\varepsilon $ on a neighborhood of a closed ball $\overline{B'}\subset D$ centered at $z$, and Hartogs' lemma (theorem 5.10.4), applied on the connected component of $D$ containing $z$ with the constant majorant $v(z)+\varepsilon $, gives $v_ j<v(z)+\varepsilon $ on $\overline{B'}$ for all large $j$. Hence $\sup _{j\ge \ell }v_ j\le v(z)+\varepsilon $ on $B'$ for all large $\ell $, so $b_\ell (z)\le v(z)+\varepsilon $, and the decreasing limit of $b_\ell $ is at most $v$.

Conversely, $b_\ell \ge v_ j$ for all $j\ge \ell $, so $(v-b_\ell )_+\le |v-v_ j|$ for $j\ge \ell $; integrating over a compact set $K\subset D$ and letting $j\to \infty $, we find that $b_\ell \ge v$ almost everywhere on $K$, hence almost everywhere on $D$. The decreasing limit of the $b_\ell $ is bounded below by $-L$, so it is plurisubharmonic by theorem 5.10.1; it is at most $v$ and equals $v$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere by the uniqueness of plurisubharmonic representatives, applied on each connected component of $D$.

The sets $N_\ell =\{ \sup _{j\ge \ell }v_ j<b_\ell \} $ are Borel, and by corollary 7.6.2 they satisfy $\operatorname {Cap}_ D^*(N_\ell \cap K)=0$ for every compact $K\subset D$. By countable subadditivity of outer capacity (proposition 7.4.3), the union $N=\bigcup _\ell N_\ell $ has the same property, so $\nu (N)=0$ by hypothesis. For $z\notin N$ we have $\sup _{j\ge \ell }v_ j(z)=b_\ell (z)$ for every $\ell $, and therefore $\varlimsup _ jv_ j(z)=\lim _\ell b_\ell (z)=v(z)$. This proves (7.11.10). The argument applies to every sequence of plurisubharmonic functions with values in $[-L,L]$ that converges to $v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$.

Step 2. Convergence of $\int v_ j\, \mathrm d\nu $. We first construct convex combinations of the $v_ j$ whose integrals converge to $\int v\, \mathrm d\nu $. Let $\mathcal C_\ell $ be the set of finite convex combinations of $v_\ell ,v_{\ell +1},\ldots $; its elements are plurisubharmonic functions with values in $[-L,L]$ (proposition 5.9.5). Let

\[ d_\ell =\inf _{r\in \mathcal C_\ell }\| r\| _{L^2(\nu )}. \]

Since $\mathcal C_{\ell +1}\subset \mathcal C_\ell $, the numbers $d_\ell $ increase, and they are bounded by $L\, \nu (D)^{1/2}$; let $d$ be their limit. Choose $r_\ell \in \mathcal C_\ell $ with

\[ \| r_\ell \| _{L^2(\nu )}^2\leq d_\ell ^2+\ell ^{-1}. \]

For $k\geq \ell $ the midpoint $(r_ k+r_\ell )/2$ belongs to the convex set $\mathcal C_\ell $, so its norm is at least $d_\ell $, and the parallelogram identity gives

\[ \| r_ k-r_\ell \| _{L^2(\nu )}^2 =2\| r_ k\| _{L^2(\nu )}^2+2\| r_\ell \| _{L^2(\nu )}^2-4\Bigl \| \frac{r_ k+r_\ell }2\Bigr \| _{L^2(\nu )}^2 \leq 2(d^2-d_\ell ^2)+4\ell ^{-1}. \]

The right-hand side tends to zero as $\ell \to \infty $, so we can choose $\ell _1<\ell _2<\cdots $ with $\| r_{\ell _{i+1}}-r_{\ell _ i}\| _{L^2(\nu )}\le 2^{-i}$. By the triangle inequality in $L^2(\nu )$ and the monotone convergence theorem, the function $g=\sum _ i|r_{\ell _{i+1}}-r_{\ell _ i}|$ satisfies $\| g\| _{L^2(\nu )}\le 1$. Hence $g<\infty $ $\nu $-almost everywhere, and at every point where $g$ is finite the sequence $(r_{\ell _ i})$ converges.

On the other hand, the $r_{\ell _ i}$ are plurisubharmonic with values in $[-L,L]$, and $r_\ell \to v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, since $\| r_\ell -v\| _{L^1(K)}\le \sup _{j\ge \ell }\| v_ j-v\| _{L^1(K)}$ for every compact $K\subset D$. By Step 1, applied to the sequence $(r_{\ell _ i})$, we have $\varlimsup _ ir_{\ell _ i}=v$ $\nu $-almost everywhere. Therefore $r_{\ell _ i}\to v$ $\nu $-almost everywhere, and dominated convergence, with the constant bound $L$ and the finite measure $\nu $, gives $\int r_{\ell _ i}\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int v\, \mathrm d\nu $.

We now show that $\int v_ j\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int v\, \mathrm d\nu $. Otherwise there are $c>0$ and a subsequence $(v_{j_ k})$ such that either $\int v_{j_ k}\, \mathrm d\nu \ge \int v\, \mathrm d\nu +c$ for all $k$, or $\int v_{j_ k}\, \mathrm d\nu \le \int v\, \mathrm d\nu -c$ for all $k$. In the middle integral, $\mathrm d\chi _ k=k\theta '(k\rho )\, \mathrm d\rho $ vanishes outside the set where $-2\leq k\rho \leq -1$. Let $\Lambda $ be the maximum of $|\nabla b_0|$ on $\overline B$. If $z\in B$ and $\zeta \in \partial B$ is a nearest boundary point, then $b_0(\zeta )=0$ and $|z-\zeta |=R-|z-a|$, so the mean value theorem on the segment from $z$ to $\zeta $ gives

\[ |b_0(z)|\leq \Lambda (R-|z-a|)\leq \frac{\Lambda }{R}(R^2-|z-a|^2)=\frac{\Lambda }{R}|\rho (z)|. \]

Hence $|b_0\, k\theta '(k\rho )|\leq (\Lambda /R)|k\rho |\, |\theta '(k\rho )|\leq 2\Lambda \sup |\theta '|/R$ on $B$, uniformly in $k$, and $b_0\, k\theta '(k\rho )\to 0$ pointwise on $B$. Since $\mathrm d\rho \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b_1\wedge S$ has bounded coefficients on $B$, dominated convergence shows that the middle integral tends to zero. This proves (7.11.35).

As observed at the beginning of section 7.3, $\mathrm db\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}c\wedge S=\mathrm dc\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge S$ for smooth real $b,c$. Moreover, $\mathrm db\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b=(\mathrm i/2\pi )\partial b\wedge \bar\partial b$ is a positive $(1,1)$-form, and so are the forms $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ l$, $l\geq 2$, since the complex Hessian of a smooth plurisubharmonic function is positive semidefinite (proposition 5.9.2). Positive $(1,1)$-forms are strongly positive (section 5.3), so by proposition 5.3.2 the $(n,n)$-form $\mathrm db\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}b\wedge S$ is a nonnegative multiple of the volume form. Consequently the real bilinear form

\[ Q(b,c)=\int _ B\mathrm db\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}c\wedge S, \]

defined for smooth real functions $b,c$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ vanishing on $\partial B$, is symmetric and positive semidefinite, and by (7.11.35) we have $Q(b,c)=I(b,c,b_2,\ldots ,b_ n)$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for $Q$, which follows from the nonnegativity of the quadratic polynomial $t\mapsto Q(b+tc,b+tc)$, therefore gives

9F29C0C8E20E 7.11.36
\begin{equation} I(b,c,b_2,\ldots ,b_ n)^2 \leq I(b,b,b_2,\ldots ,b_ n)I(c,c,b_2,\ldots ,b_ n). \end{equation}

The symmetry of $Q$ allows us to interchange the first two arguments of $I$, and the symmetry of the products allows us to permute the last $n$ arguments. Hence $I$ is symmetric in all $n+1$ arguments when they are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ vanishing on $\partial B$.

Step 2. Quadratic collars. Let $u_0,\ldots ,u_ n$ have quadratic collars, say $u_ l=c_ l\rho $ near $\partial B$. We first show that the assertions of Step 1 persist for them. Extend each $u_ l$ by $c_ l\rho $ to a plurisubharmonic function on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Let $\vartheta _\varepsilon $ be the radial mollifiers of section 2.5, denoted $\rho _\varepsilon $ there; we change the letter because $\rho $ is now the quadratic function above. Because $\rho $ is a quadratic polynomial and $\vartheta _\varepsilon $ is radial, $\rho *\vartheta _\varepsilon =\rho +\gamma \varepsilon ^2$ with the constant $\gamma =\int |y|^2\vartheta _1(y)\, \mathrm dV(y)$. Hence

\[ u_{l,\varepsilon }=u_ l*\vartheta _\varepsilon -c_ l\gamma \varepsilon ^2 \]

equals $c_ l\rho $ on a fixed neighborhood of $\partial B$ for all small $\varepsilon $. By proposition 5.9.2, the convolutions $u_ l*\vartheta _\varepsilon $ are smooth and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ for small $\varepsilon $, and they decrease to $u_ l$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Hence each $u_{l,\varepsilon }$, which differs from $u_ l*\vartheta _\varepsilon $ by a constant, is smooth and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and vanishes on $\partial B$, although the $u_{l,\varepsilon }$ need not decrease. By theorem 7.3.7,

\[ (-u_0*\vartheta _\varepsilon )\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{1,\varepsilon }\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n,\varepsilon } \longrightarrow (-u_0)\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n \]

weakly, and the constant $c_0\gamma \varepsilon ^2$ changes the integral against a fixed test function only by an amount tending to zero, by the local mass bounds of theorem 7.2.5. Near $\partial B$, all the $u_{l,\varepsilon }$ and $u_ l$ coincide with $c_ l\rho $. Splitting the integrals over $B$ by a cutoff in $C_ c(B)$ which equals one outside this neighborhood of $\partial B$, we conclude that $I(u_{0,\varepsilon },\ldots ,u_{n,\varepsilon })\to I(u_0,\ldots ,u_ n)$, and likewise for every rearrangement of the potentials. Passing to the limit in the smooth case proves that $I$ is symmetric and satisfies (7.11.36) for potentials with quadratic collars.

We now prove the inequality with constant one for these potentials. Since $u_ l=c_ l\rho $ near $\partial B$ with $c_ l>0$, the energy $e_1(u_ l)$ is positive: already its integral over a neighborhood of $\partial B$ is positive. Since $I$ is positively homogeneous of degree one in each argument and $e_1(tv)=t^{n+1}e_1(v)$ for $t>0$, both sides of the inequality are multiplied by the same factor when each $u_ l$ is replaced by $e_1(u_ l)^{-1/(n+1)}u_ l$. We may therefore assume that $e_1(u_ l)=1$ for every $l$. Consider the finitely many ordered $(n+1)$-tuples whose entries are taken from the functions $u_0,\ldots ,u_ n$, and let $F$ be the largest value of $I$ on these tuples. Since $I(u_ l,\ldots ,u_ l)=e_1(u_ l)=1$, we have $F\geq 1$. Among the tuples on which $I$ equals $F$, choose one for which the sum of the squares of the multiplicities of its distinct entries is largest.

Suppose that this tuple contains two distinct functions $b$ and $c$, with multiplicities $r$ and $s$. By symmetry we may place them in the first two slots. In (7.11.36), the left side is $F^2$, while the two factors on the right are nonnegative values of $I$ on tuples of our family, hence at most $F$. Therefore both factors equal $F$. Replacing one entry $c$ by $b$ changes the sum of the squares of the multiplicities by $(r+1)^2+(s-1)^2-r^2-s^2=2(r-s)+2$, and replacing one entry $b$ by $c$ changes it by $2(s-r)+2$. One of these numbers is positive, which contradicts the choice of the tuple. Hence the chosen tuple consists of a single function repeated $n+1$ times, and $F=1$. In particular $I(u_0,\ldots ,u_ n)\leq 1$, which is the inequality with constant one.

Step 3. Approximation with controlled energy. Let $u\in \mathcal E_0(B)$, put $m_\rho =\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^ n$, and for $j\geq 1$ let

\[ \delta _ j=j^{-n-1},\qquad v_ j=\max \{ u-\delta _ j,j\rho \} . \]

Since $u-\delta _ j\leq -\delta _ j$ while $j\rho >-\delta _ j$ near $\partial B$, the function $v_ j$ equals $j\rho $ on a neighborhood of $\partial B$ in $B$. Since $v_ j$ and $j\rho $ coincide outside a compact subset of $B$, lemma 7.11.18 gives $\int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ n=j^ nm_\rho $, and hence $v_ j\in \mathcal E_0(B)$. Moreover, $v_ j\to u$ uniformly on $B$. Indeed, let $s>0$, and choose a neighborhood $V$ of $\partial B$ with $u>-s$ on $V\cap B$. Since $u-\delta _ j\leq v_ j\leq 0$, we have $|v_ j-u|\leq s+\delta _ j$ on $V\cap B$. On the set $B\setminus V$, whose closure is a compact subset of $B$, the function $\rho $ is bounded away from zero and $u$ is bounded, so $j\rho <u-\delta _ j$, and hence $v_ j=u-\delta _ j$, there for all large $j$.

Next we estimate $e_1(v_ j)$. Let $t>4\delta _ j$. Since $u\leq v_ j+\delta _ j$ and $v_ j\leq 0$,

\[ \{ v_ j<-t\} \subset \{ u<v_ j/2-t/4\} \subset \{ u<-t/4\} ; \]

for the first inclusion note that on $\{ v_ j<-t\} $ we have $v_ j/2<-t/2<-t/4-\delta _ j$, hence $u\leq v_ j+\delta _ j<v_ j/2-t/4$. As $u$ tends to $0$ and $v_ j/2-t/4$ tends to $-t/4$ at $\partial B$, the comparison theorem (theorem 7.3.11) applies to $u$ and $v_ j/2-t/4$ and gives

\[ \int _{\{ v_ j<-t\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ n \leq 2^ n\int _{\{ u<v_ j/2-t/4\} }\bigl (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v_ j/2)\bigr )^ n \leq 2^ n\int _{\{ u<-t/4\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n. \]

For $0<t\leq 4\delta _ j$ we use the total mass $j^ nm_\rho $ instead. Integrating over $t$ by the layer-cake formula $e_1(v)=\int _0^\infty (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n(\{ v<-t\} )\, \mathrm dt$, we obtain

\begin{equation} e_1(v_ j)\leq 4\delta _ j j^ n m_\rho +2^{n+2}e_1(u) =\frac{4m_\rho }j+C_ ne_1(u). \end{equation}

Step 4. The general case. Apply the construction of Step 3 to each $u_ l$, obtaining functions $v_{l,j}\in \mathcal E_0(B)$ which equal $j\rho $ near $\partial B$. They are uniformly bounded, since $u_ l-1\leq v_{l,j}\leq 0$, and they converge uniformly, hence locally in capacity, to $u_ l$. Let $\chi \in C_ c(B)$ with $0\leq \chi \leq 1$. The weight $-u_0$ is bounded and quasi-continuous (theorem 7.5.2), so the weighted form of theorem 7.5.8 and Step 2 give

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ B\chi (-u_0)\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n & =\lim _{j\to \infty }\int _ B\chi (-v_{0,j})\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{n,j}\\ & \leq \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\prod _{l=0}^ ne_1(v_{l,j})^{1/(n+1)}. \end{aligned} \]

By (7.11.37), the right side is at most

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty }\prod _{l=0}^ n\left(\frac{4m_\rho }j+C_ ne_1(u_ l)\right)^{1/(n+1)} =C_ n\prod _{l=0}^ ne_1(u_ l)^{1/(n+1)}. \]

Letting $\chi $ increase to one proves (7.11.34). Vanishing energies need no separate treatment, since the energies of the $v_{l,j}$ are positive. $\square$

Corollary 7.11.38.11945FC061EC Let $E\geq 0$, and for $1\leq l\leq n$ and $j\geq 1$ let $u_{l,j}\in \mathcal E_0(B)$ with $e_1(u_{l,j})\leq E$. Suppose that, for each $l$, $u_{l,j}\to u_ l$ locally in capacity as $j\to \infty $, where $u_ l$ is a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $B$. Then

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n,j} \longrightarrow \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n \]

weakly on $B$.

Proof. We compare the products with those of the truncations at a level $-T$: the latter converge by theorem 7.5.8, and by theorem 7.11.33 the two differ by a term of order $1/T$. For $T>0$ put $a_{l,j}=\max \{ u_{l,j},-T\} $, and write

\[ R_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n,j},\qquad R_{j,T}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_{n,j}. \]

By lemma 7.11.19, $a_{l,j}\in \mathcal E_0(B)$ and $e_1(a_{l,j})\leq E$. For each $l$, theorem 7.11.33 with $u_0=u_{l,j}$, respectively $u_0=a_{l,j}$, gives

\[ \int _ B(-u_{l,j})\, R_ j\leq C_ nE,\qquad \int _ B(-a_{l,j})\, R_{j,T}\leq C_ nE. \]

Let $D_{j,T}=\bigcup _{l=1}^ n\{ u_{l,j}\leq -T\} $. On $\{ u_{l,j}\leq -T\} $ both $-u_{l,j}$ and $-a_{l,j}$ are at least $T$, so Chebyshev's inequality gives

\[ R_ j(D_{j,T})\leq nC_ nE/T,\qquad R_{j,T}(D_{j,T})\leq nC_ nE/T. \]

On the complement of $D_{j,T}$, every $u_{l,j}$ is greater than $-T$. For $0\leq k\leq n$ put

\[ P_ k=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_{k,j}\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{k+1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n,j}, \]

so that $P_0=R_ j$ and $P_ n=R_{j,T}$. By the symmetry of the products, $P_ k=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_{k,j}\wedge \Theta _ k$ and $P_{k-1}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{k,j}\wedge \Theta _ k$ with the same closed positive current $\Theta _ k$ formed by the other $n-1$ factors, and maximum locality (proposition 7.3.10 with $p=1$, $u=u_{k,j}$, $v=-T$ and base current $\Theta _ k$) gives $P_ k=P_{k-1}$ on $\{ u_{k,j}>-T\} $. Hence $R_ j=R_{j,T}$ on the complement of $D_{j,T}$, and for every $\chi \in C_ c(B)$,

\[ \left|\int _ B\chi \, (R_ j-R_{j,T})\right| \leq 2nC_ nE\| \chi \| _\infty /T. \]

Now fix $\chi \in C_ c(B)$, choose an open set $B'\Subset B$ containing $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $, and let $T>\max _ l\sup _{B'}|u_ l|$. By (7.11.17), the uniformly bounded functions $a_{l,j}$ converge to the locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\max \{ u_ l,-T\} $ locally in capacity, and theorem 7.5.8 shows that $R_{j,T}\to \bigwedge _ l\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max \{ u_ l,-T\} $ weakly on $B$. On $B'$ we have $\max \{ u_ l,-T\} =u_ l$, so by locality (proposition 7.2.2) this limit equals $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n$ there. Consequently

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\left|\int _ B\chi \, R_ j-\int _ B\chi \, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n\right|\leq 2nC_ nE\| \chi \| _\infty /T \]

for all large $T$, and letting $T\to \infty $ completes the proof. $\square$
