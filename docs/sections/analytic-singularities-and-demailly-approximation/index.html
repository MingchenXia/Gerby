6.3 Analytic singularities and Demailly approximation

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

13 Analytic singularities and Demailly approximation

From Foundations of pluripotential theory

Let $u$ be a plurisubharmonic function on a domain in $\mathbb C^ n$. Convolution with a smoothing kernel approximates $u$ by smooth plurisubharmonic functions, but these approximants have lost all singularities: their Lelong numbers vanish and their multiplier ideals are trivial. Many problems require approximants whose singularities are still visible but as simple as possible, namely functions locally of the form $c\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ with holomorphic $g_ i$ and rational $c>0$; such functions are said to have analytic singularities. On a compact Kähler manifold a second difficulty appears: convolution is defined only in charts, and gluing local approximations costs positivity. This chapter presents Demailly's answer to both problems, in which convolution is replaced by weighted Bergman kernels.

For $u$ plurisubharmonic on a bounded pseudoconvex domain $\Omega $ and an integer $m\geq 1$, the holomorphic functions $F$ on $\Omega $ with $\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}<\infty $ form a Hilbert space, which generates the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(mu)$ of chapter 12. If $K_ m(z)$ denotes the supremum of $|F(z)|^2$ over its unit ball, then $u_ m=m^{-1}\log K_ m$ is plurisubharmonic and has analytic singularities described by $\mathcal I(mu)$. The Ohsawa–Takegoshi estimate of chapter 11 bounds $u_ m$ below by $u-O(1/m)$, and the mean-value inequality bounds it above by the supremum of $u$ over a small ball, up to an error that tends to zero as $m\to \infty $. Hence $u_ m\to u$, and the Lelong numbers of $u_ m$ differ from those of $u$ by at most $n/m$ (theorem 13.2.3).

On a compact Kähler manifold $(X,\omega )$ we apply this construction in finitely many coordinate balls to a function $\varphi $ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq 0$, where $\theta $ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form. A $\bar\partial $-estimate with a logarithmic pole shows that the kernels of two overlapping balls are comparable up to a factor that depends only on the charts (lemma 13.3.2). Gluing the local potentials by a maximum gives the quantitative regularization of theorem 13.3.3, with a curvature loss of order $1/m$ and a lower bound by the generators of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$. Gluing them instead by a sum with smooth cutoffs gives approximants $G_ m$ with gentle analytic singularities (lemma 13.4.1): $\exp (mG_ m)$ is smooth, and $G_ m$ has a smooth remainder on every log resolution of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$. Comparing the kernels at the scales $m$ and $2m$ by extension from the diagonal, we show that the dyadic sequence $G_{2^ j}$, corrected by small constants, decreases to $\varphi $ and that its multiplier ideals approximate those of $\varphi $, so that it is a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ in the sense of definition 13.4.4 (theorem 13.4.5). Any two quasi-equisingular approximations of $\varphi $ dominate each other up to an arbitrarily small loss in the coefficient (proposition 13.5.2).

The first section collects the elementary properties of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, introduces $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions and analytic singularities, and shows, using metrics on line bundles, that blowups with smooth centers preserve the Kähler property. The last section studies the ideal of holomorphic germs $f$ with $|f|^2\leq C\mathrm e^ v$: for $v$ with analytic singularities it is coherent, and on a compact manifold the ideal attached to $kv$ contains $\mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon )v)$ for all large $k$.

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in section B.13.

13.1 Smooth backgrounds and analytic singularities

From Foundations of pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 0$. Recall from section 7.5 that a Hermitian form on $X$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is strictly positive at every point, that is, the form associated with a Hermitian metric. It is a Kähler form if it is closed, and $X$ is a Kähler manifold if it carries a Kähler form. A Hermitian form $\omega $ defines a Riemannian metric on $X$ and the positive volume form $\omega ^ n/n!$.

Continuous real $(1,1)$-forms are controlled by a Hermitian form on compact sets.

Lemma 13.1.1. Let $\omega $ be a Hermitian form on a complex manifold $X$, let $K\subset X$ be compact, and let $\alpha $ be a continuous real $(1,1)$-form on a neighborhood of $K$. Then there is a constant $C\geq 0$ with $\alpha \geq -C\omega $ at every point of $K$.

Proof. If $K=\varnothing $ we take $C=0$. Otherwise cover $K$ by finitely many compact sets $K_\iota $, each contained in a chart and in the domain of $\alpha $, and write $\alpha =\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}a_{jk}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$ and $\omega =\mathrm i\sum _{j,k}w_{jk}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$ there, with Hermitian matrices $(a_{jk})$ and $(w_{jk})$. On $K_\iota $ the smallest eigenvalue of $(w_{jk})$, a continuous positive function, is at least some $c_\iota >0$, and the largest eigenvalue of $-(a_{jk})$ is at most some $M_\iota \geq 0$. Hence $(a_{jk})\geq -M_\iota I\geq -(M_\iota /c_\iota )(w_{jk})$ on $K_\iota $. By the matrix criterion for positivity (section 7.3), it follows that $\alpha \geq -C\omega $ on $K$ with $C=\max _\iota M_\iota /c_\iota $. $\square$

The following lemma provides smooth local potentials for smooth closed forms on whole coordinate balls, not only on small polydiscs as in lemma 7.3.1.

Lemma 13.1.2. Let $B$ be a coordinate ball and let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form defined on a neighborhood of $\overline B$. Then there is a smooth real function $h$ on $B$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $. Moreover, every real pluriharmonic function on a coordinate ball is, up to an additive constant, the real part of a holomorphic function on that ball.

Proof. In dimension zero there is nothing to prove, so let $n\geq 1$. Choose concentric balls $B\Subset B'\Subset B''$ such that $\theta $ is defined on a neighborhood of $\overline{B''}$. By the last assertion of lemma 7.3.1, applied to the closed real $2$-form $\theta $ on the ball $B''$, there is a smooth real one-form $\alpha $ on $B''$ with $\mathrm d\alpha =\theta $. Since $\theta $ has type $(1,1)$, the $(0,2)$-part $\bar\partial \alpha ^{0,1}$ of $\mathrm d\alpha $ vanishes. The ball $B'$ is pseudoconvex by corollary 11.2.5, and $\alpha ^{0,1}$ is bounded on $B'$. Hence theorem 11.3.4, applied on $B'$ with $p=0$ and $q=|z|^2$, whose complex Hessian is the identity, gives a function $f\in L^2(B')$ with $\bar\partial f=\alpha ^{0,1}$.

The function $f$ is smooth. Indeed, let $y\in B'$ and choose polydiscs $P'\Subset P\Subset B'$ centered at $y$. By lemma 7.3.1, applied to the smooth $\bar\partial $-closed form $\alpha ^{0,1}$ on $P$, there is a smooth function $f_0$ on $P'$ with $\bar\partial f_0=\alpha ^{0,1}$. Then $\bar\partial (f-f_0)=0$ on $P'$ in the sense of distributions, so $\Delta (f-f_0)=4\sum _ j\partial _{z_ j}\partial _{\bar z_ j}(f-f_0)=0$ there, and by theorem 2.1.4 the locally integrable function $f-f_0$ agrees almost everywhere on $P'$ with a smooth function. Thus $f$ agrees almost everywhere near each point with a smooth function; two such smooth functions coincide on the overlap of their domains, being continuous and equal almost everywhere, so we may take $f$ smooth on $B'$. Since $\alpha $ is real, $\alpha ^{1,0}=\overline{\alpha ^{0,1}}=\partial \bar f$, and the $(1,1)$-part of $\mathrm d\alpha =\theta $ gives

\[ \theta =\partial \alpha ^{0,1}+\bar\partial \alpha ^{1,0}=\partial \bar\partial f+\bar\partial \partial \bar f=\partial \bar\partial (f-\bar f)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(4\pi \operatorname{Im}f). \]

Thus $h=4\pi \operatorname{Im}f$ has the required property on $B\subset B'$.

For the last assertion, let $h$ be real and pluriharmonic on a coordinate ball. Then $\partial h$ is a $(1,0)$-form with holomorphic coefficients, since $\bar\partial \partial h=0$, and it is closed. By the last assertion of lemma 7.3.1, applied to the closed one-form $2\partial h$ on this ball, there is a smooth function $F$ on the whole ball with $\mathrm dF=2\partial h$. As $\mathrm dF$ has type $(1,0)$, $F$ is holomorphic, and $\mathrm d(\operatorname{Re}F)=\partial h+\bar\partial h=\mathrm dh$. Hence $h-\operatorname{Re}F$ is constant. $\square$

Definition 13.1.3. Let $X$ be a complex manifold. A function $\varphi \colon X\to [-\infty ,\infty )$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic if it is quasi-plurisubharmonic near every point of $X$ in the sense of corollary 12.3.2, that is, if every point of $X$ has a neighborhood $V$ on which $\varphi =u+f$ pointwise, with $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(V)$ and $f$ a smooth real function on $V$. For a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$ we put

\[ \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )=\{ \varphi \text{ quasi-plurisubharmonic on }X:\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq 0\} , \]

and call its elements $\theta $-plurisubharmonic ($\theta $-psh) functions.

We collect the elementary properties of these classes.

Lemma 13.1.4. Let $X$ be a complex manifold and let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$.

  1. Every quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ is locally integrable, hence finite almost everywhere. If $X$ has positive dimension, a quasi-plurisubharmonic function is, at every point, the limit of its averages over small coordinate balls centered at that point. Consequently, if $\varphi $ and $\psi $ are quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on an open set $V\subset X$ with $\varphi \leq \psi $ almost everywhere on $V$, then $\varphi \leq \psi $ everywhere on $V$; in particular, two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $V$ that agree almost everywhere on $V$ agree everywhere on $V$.

  2. Let $V\subset X$ be open and let $h$ be a smooth real function on $V$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $. Then a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi $ on $V$ satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq 0$ on $V$ if and only if $\varphi +h$ is plurisubharmonic on $V$.

  3. If $\varphi _1,\ldots ,\varphi _ k\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $k\geq 1$, then $\max _ i\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

  4. If $X$ is compact and $\omega $ is a Hermitian form on $X$, then for every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi $ on $X$ there is a constant $C\geq 0$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -C\omega $.

  5. Let $\omega $ be a Hermitian form on $X$, and let $\varphi \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)$ satisfy $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -C\omega $ in the sense of currents for a constant $C$. Then $\varphi $ agrees almost everywhere with a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$.

Proof. At points where $X$ has dimension zero, which are isolated, all functions are smooth and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ vanishes, so every assertion is trivial there; we may therefore assume that $X$ has positive dimension.

(1) Near each point, $\varphi =u+f$ with $u$ plurisubharmonic and $f$ smooth, and $u$ is locally integrable by theorem 8.2.1. Moreover $u$ is subharmonic, by the same theorem, so at every point it is the limit of its averages over small balls, by (3.3); the continuous function $f$ has the same property, and hence so has $\varphi $. Now let $\varphi $ and $\psi $ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on $V$ with $\varphi \leq \psi $ almost everywhere, and let $x\in V$. Since both functions are locally integrable, their averages over small coordinate balls centered at $x$, in a chart at $x$, are finite, and those of $\varphi $ are at most those of $\psi $; letting the radius tend to zero gives $\varphi (x)\leq \psi (x)$. Applying this in both directions, we see that two quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere.

(2) Let $\varphi $ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on $V$. If $\varphi +h$ is plurisubharmonic, then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi +h)\geq 0$ by theorem 8.2.1. Conversely, suppose that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi +h)\geq 0$ on $V$. On a coordinate ball $B\subset V$, theorem 8.2.1 gives a plurisubharmonic function on $B$ that agrees almost everywhere with $\varphi +h$; both are quasi-plurisubharmonic on $B$, so they agree everywhere on $B$ by (1). Hence $\varphi +h$ is plurisubharmonic on $V$.

(3) Let $B$ be a coordinate ball and $h$ a smooth real function on $B$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $ (lemma 13.1.2). By (2) the functions $\varphi _ i+h$ are plurisubharmonic on $B$, hence so is $\max _ i\varphi _ i+h=\max _ i(\varphi _ i+h)$, by proposition 8.2.5. Thus $\max _ i\varphi _ i$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic on $B$, and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max _ i\varphi _ i\geq 0$ there by (2). Such balls cover $X$.

(4) By compactness there are finitely many open sets $V_\kappa \Subset V_\kappa ^+$ such that the $V_\kappa $ cover $X$ and $\varphi =u_\kappa +f_\kappa $ on $V_\kappa ^+$, with $u_\kappa $ plurisubharmonic and $f_\kappa $ smooth. By lemma 13.1.1, applied to the compact set $\overline{V_\kappa }$ and the form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_\kappa $, there are constants $C_\kappa \geq 0$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_\kappa \geq -C_\kappa \omega $ on $V_\kappa $, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\kappa \geq 0$ by theorem 8.2.1. Hence $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -C\omega $ with $C=\max _\kappa C_\kappa $.

(5) Replacing $C$ by $\max \{ C,0\} $, we may assume that $C\geq 0$. Let $B$ be a coordinate ball whose closure lies in a chart. By lemma 13.1.1, applied in the chart to the compact set $\overline B$, the Hermitian form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and the form $-\omega $, there is $C'$ with $\omega \leq C'\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ on $B$, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi +A|z|^2)\geq 0$ on $B$ for $A=CC'$. By theorem 8.2.1, $\varphi +A|z|^2$ agrees almost everywhere on $B$ with a plurisubharmonic function, and subtracting $A|z|^2$ gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi _ B$ on $B$ that agrees almost everywhere with $\varphi $. If $B'$ is a second such ball, then $\varphi _ B$ and $\varphi _{B'}$ agree almost everywhere on $B\cap B'$, and both are quasi-plurisubharmonic, so they agree everywhere on $B\cap B'$ by (1). Since such balls cover $X$, the functions $\varphi _ B$ define a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ that agrees almost everywhere with $\varphi $. $\square$

Multiplier ideals of a quasi-plurisubharmonic weight are defined as in definition 12.2.5. Adding a smooth function to the weight multiplies the integrand by a locally bounded positive factor with locally bounded inverse, so the multiplier ideal of $\varphi =u+f$ near a point is that of $u$; in particular it is coherent, and the local results of chapter 12 apply to it. Lelong numbers of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are defined as in corollary 12.3.2, by $\nu (\varphi ,x)=\nu (u,x)$.

Definition 13.1.5. A quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi $ on $X$ has analytic singularities if every point of $X$ has a connected coordinate neighborhood $V$ on which

13.1
\begin{equation} \varphi =c\log \sum _{i=1}^ r|g_ i|^2+b, \qquad c\in \mathbb Q_{>0},\quad b\text{ bounded}, \end{equation}

where $r\geq 1$ is an integer and $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r\in \mathcal O(V)$ are not all identically zero. It has neat analytic singularities if such presentations exist with $b$ smooth. Let $c\in \mathbb Q_{>0}$ and let $\mathfrak a\subset \mathcal O_ X$ be a coherent ideal sheaf. We say that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ if presentations (13.1) exist with this coefficient $c$, where on each $V$ the functions $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r$ generate $\mathfrak a$.

The type condition does not depend on the choice of local generators: if $(g_ i)$ and $(g'_ k)$ both generate $\mathfrak a$ on $V$, then near each point of $V$ each tuple is a holomorphic matrix times the other, and both matrices are bounded on smaller neighborhoods, so $\log \sum |g_ i|^2-\log \sum |g'_ k|^2$ is locally bounded. A type is additional data and is not unique; for example, a function of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ also has type $(c/2,\mathfrak a^2)$.

The Lelong numbers of a function with analytic singularities are determined by its type.

Lemma 13.1.6. Let $x$ be a point of a complex manifold $X$ at which $X$ has positive dimension, let $\mathfrak a\subset \mathcal O_ X$ be a coherent ideal sheaf with $\mathfrak a_ x\neq 0$, generated near $x$ by $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s$, and let $c>0$. If $w$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic near $x$ and $w=c\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ near $x$, then $\nu (w,x)=c\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathfrak a$.

Proof. Choose a connected coordinate neighborhood $V$ of $x$ on which the $g_ i$ are defined and generate $\mathfrak a$, on which $w=v+f$ with $v$ plurisubharmonic and $f$ smooth and bounded, and on which $|w-c\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2|\leq M$ for a constant $M$. Let $I$ be the set of indices $i$ for which the germ of $g_ i$ at $x$ is not zero. It is not empty, since these germs generate $\mathfrak a_ x\neq 0$, and for $i\notin I$ the function $g_ i$ vanishes identically on $V$, by the identity theorem. Since $0\leq \log \sum _{i\in I}|g_ i|^2-\max _{i\in I}\log |g_ i|^2\leq \log s$, the function $v$ differs from $c\max _{i\in I}\log |g_ i|^2$ by a bounded function on $V$. The latter function is plurisubharmonic by proposition 8.3.3, proposition 8.2.5, so proposition 12.1.1 and proposition 12.1.4 give

\[ \nu (w,x)=\nu (v,x)=c\, \nu \Bigl (\max _{i\in I}\log |g_ i|^2,x\Bigr )=c\min _{i\in I}\operatorname {ord}_ xg_ i=c\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathfrak a, \]

where the first equality is the definition of Lelong numbers of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions (corollary 12.3.2) and the last one is the description of $\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathfrak a$ by generators in section 12.3. $\square$

For a holomorphic map $\pi \colon Y\to X$ and an ideal sheaf $\mathfrak a\subset \mathcal O_ X$, we write $\pi ^*\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a\cdot \mathcal O_ Y$ for the ideal generated by the pullbacks of local generators of $\mathfrak a$. Let $\mathfrak a$ be a coherent ideal on $X$, nonzero on each component. Since $X$ is smooth, the resolution step in the construction of log resolutions at the end of section A.5 is not needed, and theorem A.5.5, applied with the empty divisor, produces log resolutions of $\mathfrak a$ by blowups with smooth centers. From now on a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ always means a map of this form: a composite $\pi \colon Y\to X$ of blowups along closed submanifolds of positive codimension, finite over each relatively compact open subset of $X$, such that $\pi ^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$ for an effective divisor $D$ whose support has simple normal crossings. We also call $\pi $ a principalization of $\mathfrak a$, and we write $s_ D$ for a local holomorphic equation of $D$.

Proposition 13.1.7. Analytic singularities are preserved by multiplication by positive rational numbers, by sums and finite maxima, and by pullback under a holomorphic map $f\colon Y\to X$ of complex manifolds whenever the composition is not identically $-\infty $ on any component of $Y$. If $\varphi $ has type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ and $\pi \colon Y\to X$ is a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ with $\pi ^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$, then locally on $Y$

\[ \varphi \circ \pi =c\log |s_ D|^2+O(1), \]

and the remainder is smooth if the presentations of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ are neat.

Proof. Multiplication by $q\in \mathbb Q_{>0}$ replaces $c$ by $qc$. Sums and finite maxima of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are quasi-plurisubharmonic. Indeed, let $\varphi _ i=u_ i+\sigma _ i$ near a point, with $u_ i$ plurisubharmonic and $\sigma _ i$ smooth. On a small coordinate ball about the point, the smooth functions $\sigma _ i+N|z|^2$ have positive semidefinite complex Hessians for a large constant $N$, hence are plurisubharmonic by proposition 8.2.2(1). By proposition 8.2.5, $\varphi _1+\varphi _2$ is a plurisubharmonic function minus $2N|z|^2$, and $\max _ i\varphi _ i$ is a plurisubharmonic function minus $N|z|^2$. For a sum or a maximum of two functions with analytic singularities, choose presentations about a given point, with coefficients $c$ and $d$, and a connected coordinate neighborhood $V$ of the point contained in both domains; by the identity theorem, neither tuple vanishes identically on $V$. Since $c$ and $d$ are rational, we can choose $c_0\in \mathbb Q_{>0}$ such that $c/c_0$ and $d/c_0$ are positive integers. For an integer $k\geq 1$ the multinomial formula gives

\[ \Bigl (\sum _ i|g_ i|^2\Bigr )^ k=\sum _{|\alpha |=k}\Bigl |\sqrt{k!/\alpha !}\, g^\alpha \Bigr |^2, \]

again a sum of squared moduli of holomorphic functions, not all identically zero on $V$. We may therefore assume that both presentations have the coefficient $c_0$, say $c_0\log A+b$ and $c_0\log B+b'$ with $A=\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$ and $B=\sum _ l|g'_ l|^2$. The sum is $c_0\log (AB)+b+b'$, and $AB=\sum _{i,l}|g_ ig'_ l|^2$, where some product $g_ ig'_ l$ is not identically zero on the connected set $V$. For the maximum we use

\[ \max (\log A,\log B)\leq \log (A+B)\leq \max (\log A,\log B)+\log 2, \]

which shows that $\max (c_0\log A+b,c_0\log B+b')$ differs from $c_0\log (A+B)$ by a bounded function.

For the pullback, let $f\colon Y\to X$ be holomorphic, and suppose that $\varphi \circ f$ is not identically $-\infty $ on any connected component of $Y$. Near each point of $Y$, write $\varphi \circ f=u\circ f+\sigma \circ f$ on a connected coordinate neighborhood $W$, where $\varphi =u+\sigma $ near the image point with $u$ plurisubharmonic and $\sigma $ smooth; by proposition 8.2.3, $u\circ f$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $ on $W$. Let $S$ be the open set of points of $Y$ near which $\varphi \circ f$ is identically $-\infty $. If $y$ lies in the closure of $S$ and $W$ is as above for $y$, then $u\circ f=-\infty $ on the nonempty open set $W\cap S$; since plurisubharmonic functions are locally integrable, $u\circ f$ is identically $-\infty $ on $W$, so $y\in S$. Thus $S$ is open and closed, and by hypothesis it contains no component of $Y$; hence $S=\varnothing $, and $\varphi \circ f$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic. If $\varphi =c\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+b$ on $V$, then on every connected coordinate neighborhood $W\subset f^{-1}(V)$ we have $\varphi \circ f=c\log \sum _ i|g_ i\circ f|^2+b\circ f$, with $b\circ f$ bounded, and the functions $g_ i\circ f$ do not all vanish identically on $W$, because $S=\varnothing $.

Now let $\pi $ be a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ as in the statement. If $g_1,\ldots ,g_ r$ generate $\mathfrak a$, their pullbacks generate $\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$, so locally $g_ i\circ \pi =s_ Dh_ i$ with holomorphic $h_ i$, and $s_ D=\sum _ ik_ i(g_ i\circ \pi )$ for holomorphic $k_ i$. Since $s_ D$ is not a zero divisor, $\sum _ ik_ ih_ i=1$, so the $h_ i$ have no common zero and $\log \sum _ i|h_ i|^2$ is smooth. Hence

\[ \varphi \circ \pi =c\log |s_ D|^2+c\log \sum _ i|h_ i|^2+b\circ \pi , \]

which proves both assertions about the remainder. $\square$

If $\varphi $ has analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, we also call a log resolution of $\mathfrak a$ a log resolution of $\varphi $; since types are not unique, this refers to the chosen type. On such a resolution $\varphi \circ \pi =c\log |s_ D|^2+O(1)$ locally, by proposition 13.1.7. A merely bounded remainder need not become smooth on a log resolution. For instance, on the unit disc in $\mathbb C$ the function $\log |z|^2+|\operatorname{Re}z|$ is subharmonic, since $|\operatorname{Re}z|=\max (\operatorname{Re}z,-\operatorname{Re}z)$ is a maximum of harmonic functions (proposition 8.2.5), and it has analytic singularities of type $(1,(z))$. In dimension one a closed submanifold of positive codimension is a hypersurface, so every blowup in a log resolution is the identity by proposition A.5.3. The remainder $|\operatorname{Re}z|$ therefore stays as it is, and it is not smooth on the imaginary axis. This is why neat singularities, and the gentle singularities defined below on compact Kähler manifolds, are separate notions.

When $X$ is compact, a log resolution consists of finitely many blowups with smooth centers, and if $X$ is moreover Kähler, so is the resolved manifold (proposition 13.1.10). The proof uses metrics on line bundles and their curvature, which we now introduce.

Definition 13.1.8. A holomorphic line bundle $L$ on $X$ is given by an open cover $(V_ i)$ of $X$ and local frames $e_ i$ over $V_ i$ with transition functions $e_ i=g_{ij}e_ j$ on $V_ i\cap V_ j$, where $g_{ij}$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero and $g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}$ on triple overlaps. A smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on $L$ assigns to each frame a smooth positive squared norm $|e_ i|^2$ on $V_ i$, such that $|e_ i|^2=|g_{ij}|^2|e_ j|^2$ on $V_ i\cap V_ j$. Its curvature is the closed real $(1,1)$-form equal to $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |e_ i|^2$ on $V_ i$.

When several metrics occur, we write $|e_ i|_ h^2$ for the squared norms of a metric $h$. Smooth metrics always exist, and any two of them differ by a smooth positive factor.

Lemma 13.1.9. Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle on a complex manifold $X$, given by frames $e_ i$ over an open cover $(V_ i)$ with transition functions $g_{ij}$ as in definition 13.1.8.

  1. $L$ carries a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric.

  2. The curvature of a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on $L$ is well defined, and it is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$.

  3. Let $h_0$ be a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on $L$ with curvature $\eta _0$. For every smooth real function $f$ on $X$, the squared norms $|e_ i|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-f}$ define a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric $h_0\mathrm e^{-f}$ on $L$, whose curvature is $\eta _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$. Conversely, every smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on $L$ equals $h_0\mathrm e^{-f}$ for exactly one smooth real function $f$ on $X$.

Proof. (1) By proposition 7.1.3 there is a smooth partition of unity $(\chi _ a)$ subordinate to the cover $(V_ i)$; for each $a$ choose an index $k(a)$ with $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ a\subset V_{k(a)}$, and put $\rho _ k=\sum _{k(a)=k}\chi _ a$. Since the family $(\chi _ a)$ is locally finite, each $\rho _ k$ is smooth, the family $(\rho _ k)$ is locally finite, $\sum _ k\rho _ k=1$, and $\operatorname{Supp}\rho _ k\subset V_ k$, because a locally finite union of closed sets is closed. For each $i$ put

\[ |e_ i|^2=\sum _ k\rho _ k|g_{ik}|^2\qquad \text{on }V_ i, \]

where the $k$th term, defined on $V_ i\cap V_ k$, is extended by zero to the rest of $V_ i$. Each term is smooth on $V_ i$, since it is smooth on $V_ i\cap V_ k$ and vanishes on the open set $V_ i\setminus \operatorname{Supp}\rho _ k$, and these two open sets cover $V_ i$. By local finiteness, $|e_ i|^2$ is smooth on $V_ i$, and it is positive, because the $\rho _ k$ are nonnegative with sum one and every $g_{ik}$ is nowhere zero. On $V_ i\cap V_ j$, the cocycle relation $g_{ik}=g_{ij}g_{jk}$ holds on $V_ i\cap V_ j\cap V_ k$, hence wherever $\rho _ k\neq 0$, so

\[ |e_ i|^2=\sum _ k\rho _ k|g_{ij}|^2|g_{jk}|^2=|g_{ij}|^2|e_ j|^2. \]

Thus these squared norms form a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on $L$.

(2) On $V_ i\cap V_ j$ we have $\log |e_ i|^2-\log |e_ j|^2=\log |g_{ij}|^2$, which is pluriharmonic, since $g_{ij}$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero (section 7.3). Hence the forms $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |e_ i|^2$ on $V_ i$ agree on the overlaps and define a $(1,1)$-form $\eta $ on $X$. It is smooth, because each $\log |e_ i|^2$ is a smooth real function. Moreover $\eta =-\mathrm d(\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\log |e_ i|^2)$ on $V_ i$, by (7.6), and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ maps real forms to real forms; hence $\eta $ is real, and it is closed because $\mathrm d^2=0$.

(3) The functions $|e_ i|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-f}$ are smooth and positive on $V_ i$, and they satisfy the compatibility condition of definition 13.1.8 because the functions $|e_ i|_{h_0}^2$ do. The curvature of $h_0\mathrm e^{-f}$ is $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \bigl (|e_ i|_{h_0}^2\mathrm e^{-f}\bigr )=-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |e_ i|_{h_0}^2+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\eta _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ on $V_ i$. Conversely, let $h_1$ be a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on $L$. On $V_ i$ the function $|e_ i|_{h_1}^2/|e_ i|_{h_0}^2$ is smooth and positive, and on $V_ i\cap V_ j$ it equals $|e_ j|_{h_1}^2/|e_ j|_{h_0}^2$, since both numerator and denominator are multiplied by $|g_{ij}|^2$. Hence these quotients define a smooth positive function on $X$, which we write as $\mathrm e^{-f}$ with $f$ smooth and real; then $h_1=h_0\mathrm e^{-f}$. The function $f$ is unique, since $\mathrm e^{-f}$ must equal these quotients. $\square$

Proposition 13.1.10. Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold with Kähler form $\omega $, let $W\subset X$ be a closed submanifold of positive codimension, and let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be the blowup of $X$ along $W$, with exceptional divisor $E=\pi ^{-1}(W)$. Then $Y$ is a compact complex manifold, and there are a smooth function $\rho \colon X\to [0,\infty )$ with $\rho ^{-1}(0)=W$ and a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\eta $ on $Y$ with $\eta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (\rho \circ \pi )$ on $Y\setminus E$, such that $\pi ^*\omega +\varepsilon \eta $ is a Kähler form on $Y$ for every sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$. The form $\eta $ is the curvature of a smooth quadratic Hermitian metric on the line bundle $\mathcal O_ Y(-E)$, whose local frames are the function $1$ on $Y\setminus E$ and the local equations of $E$ near $E$. In particular $Y$ is a compact Kähler manifold. Consequently every log resolution of a coherent ideal on $X$, as above, is a compact Kähler manifold.

Proof. By proposition A.5.3, $Y$ is a complex manifold and $E$ is a smooth hypersurface with $\mathcal I_ W\mathcal O_ Y=\mathcal O_ Y(-E)$; moreover $Y$ is compact, since $\pi $ is proper (proposition A.5.2), and $\pi $ restricts to a biholomorphism $Y\setminus E\to X\setminus W$. The last assertion follows from the preceding ones by induction: since $X$ is compact, a log resolution is a composite of finitely many blowups along closed submanifolds of positive codimension, and each intermediate manifold is compact Kähler by what precedes.

We construct $\eta $ as the curvature of a metric on $\mathcal O_ Y(-E)$ and show that it is positive definite along the fibers of $E\to W$ (Step 1). Since $\pi ^*\omega $ is positive in the remaining directions, $\pi ^*\omega +\varepsilon \eta $ is then positive definite near $E$ for small $\varepsilon >0$ (Step 2), and also away from $E$ (Step 3). We use the charts of proposition A.5.3. Near a point of $W$ at which $W$ has codimension $r$, choose coordinates $(z,w)$ on $X$, $w=(w_1,\ldots ,w_ r)$, with $W=\{ w=0\} $. For each pivot $i$ the blowup has a chart with coordinates $(z,t,v)$, $v=(v_ j)_{j\neq i}$, in which

\[ \pi (z,t,v)=(z,t\tilde v),\qquad \tilde v_ i=1,\quad \tilde v_ j=v_ j\ (j\neq i); \]

in it $E=\{ t=0\} $, and the fibers of $E\to W$ are the sets $\{ z=\text{const},\ t=0\} $. If $r=1$ there is no variable $v$, and $\pi $ is the identity near the point.

Step 1: a closed $(1,1)$-form that is positive along the fibers of $E$. Choose finitely many charts $V_1,\ldots ,V_ N$ of $X$ covering $W$, with coordinates $(z^\alpha ,w^\alpha )$ such that $W\cap V_\alpha =\{ w^\alpha =0\} $, and put $V_0=X\setminus W$. Grouping the members of a partition of unity subordinate to this cover (proposition 7.1.3) according to a set $V_\alpha $ containing their supports, we obtain a smooth partition of unity $(\lambda _\alpha )_{0\leq \alpha \leq N}$ on $X$ with $\operatorname{Supp}\lambda _\alpha \subset V_\alpha $. Put

\[ \rho =\lambda _0+\sum _{\alpha =1}^ N\lambda _\alpha |w^\alpha |^2, \]

each term extended by zero outside $V_\alpha $. This is a smooth nonnegative function on $X$ that vanishes on $W$, because $\lambda _0=0$ there, and is positive on $X\setminus W$: at a point $x\notin W$ some $\lambda _\alpha (x)$ is positive, and $w^\alpha (x)\neq 0$ if $\alpha \geq 1$.

We describe $\rho $ near a point $x_0\in W$, in coordinates $(z,w)$ as above on a polydisc $\Delta $ centered at $x_0$. Shrinking $\Delta $, we may assume that $\Delta $ does not meet $\operatorname{Supp}\lambda _0$, nor $\operatorname{Supp}\lambda _\alpha $ for the indices $\alpha $ with $x_0\notin V_\alpha $, and that $\Delta \subset V_\alpha $ for the others. For such an $\alpha $, each component $w^\alpha _ k$ vanishes on $W=\{ w=0\} $, so

\[ w^\alpha _ k(z,w)=\sum _{l=1}^ rw_ l\int _0^1\frac{\partial w^\alpha _ k}{\partial w_ l}(z,sw)\, \mathrm ds, \]

that is, $w^\alpha =A_\alpha w$ with a holomorphic $r\times r$ matrix $A_\alpha $ on $\Delta $. At points of $W$ the matrix $A_\alpha (z,0)=(\partial w^\alpha _ k/\partial w_ l)(z,0)$ is invertible: since $w^\alpha $ vanishes on $W$, its derivatives in the $z$-directions vanish there, and the Jacobian matrix of the coordinate change $(z,w)\mapsto (z^\alpha ,w^\alpha )$ is invertible. Hence, on $\Delta $,

\[ \rho =\sum _\alpha \lambda _\alpha |A_\alpha w|^2=w^*\mathcal Q\, w,\qquad \mathcal Q=\sum _\alpha \lambda _\alpha A_\alpha ^*A_\alpha , \]

where the sums run over the indices $\alpha \geq 1$ with $x_0\in V_\alpha $, and $w^*$ is the conjugate transpose of the column vector $w$. The Hermitian matrix $\mathcal Q$ is smooth and positive semidefinite, and it is positive definite at $x_0$, because the coefficients $\lambda _\alpha (x_0)$ of the invertible matrices $A_\alpha (x_0)$ add up to one; shrinking $\Delta $, we may assume that $\mathcal Q$ is positive definite on $\Delta $. In a chart $(z,t,v)$ over $\Delta $ we obtain

\[ \rho \circ \pi =|t|^2h_ E,\qquad h_ E(z,t,v)=\tilde v^*\mathcal Q(z,t\tilde v)\, \tilde v, \]

and $h_ E$ is smooth and positive, since $\tilde v\neq 0$.

Define a real $(1,1)$-form $\eta $ on $Y$ by $\eta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (\rho \circ \pi )$ on $Y\setminus E$ and $\eta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log h_ E$ on each chart $(z,t,v)$ as above. These definitions agree. Off $E$ we have $\log (\rho \circ \pi )=\log |t|^2+\log h_ E$, and $\log |t|^2$ is pluriharmonic there. On the overlap of two charts, the local equations $t$ and $t'$ of $E$ generate the same ideal $\mathcal O_ Y(-E)$, so $t'=ut$ with $u$ holomorphic and nowhere zero. Off $E$ the corresponding functions $h_ E$ and $h'_ E$ satisfy $\log h_ E-\log h'_ E=\log |u|^2$. Both sides are continuous on the overlap and $E$ is nowhere dense, so this identity holds on the whole overlap, and $\log |u|^2$ is pluriharmonic. Thus $\eta $ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $Y$. It is the curvature, in the sense of definition 13.1.8, of the metric on the line bundle $\mathcal O_ Y(-E)$ with the frame $1$ on $Y\setminus E$, the frames $t$ on the charts and the transition functions $t$ and $t'/t$, for which $|1|^2=1/(\rho \circ \pi )$ and $|t|^2=1/h_ E$. These squared norms are smooth and positive, and they satisfy the compatibility condition of that definition because $\rho \circ \pi =|t|^2h_ E$ and $h'_ E=|t/t'|^2h_ E$.

We show that $\eta $ is positive definite along the fibers of $E\to W$. Fix a point $(z_0,0,v_0)$ of $E$ in a chart, and put $\mathcal Q_0=\mathcal Q(z_0,0)$. On the fiber $\{ z=z_0,\ t=0\} $ we have $h_ E=\tilde v^*\mathcal Q_0\tilde v$. Write $\mathcal Q_0=\Gamma ^*\Gamma $ with an invertible matrix $\Gamma $, for instance the positive square root of $\mathcal Q_0$, and let $e_1,\ldots ,e_ r$ be the standard basis of $\mathbb C^ r$. Then $h_ E=|F(v)|^2$ on the fiber, where $F(v)=\Gamma \tilde v=\Gamma e_ i+\sum _{j\neq i}v_ j\Gamma e_ j$ is an affine map whose linear part $\Lambda \xi =\sum _{j\neq i}\xi _ j\Gamma e_ j$ is injective, and $F(v)$ never lies in the image of $\Lambda $, because the columns $\Gamma e_1,\ldots ,\Gamma e_ r$ are linearly independent. Writing $\langle a,b\rangle =\sum _ ka_ k\bar b_ k$ on $\mathbb C^ r$, we have $\partial _{\bar v_ l}|F|^2=\langle F,\Gamma e_ l\rangle $ and $\partial _{v_ k}|F|^2=\langle \Gamma e_ k,F\rangle $, so for $\xi \in \mathbb C^{r-1}$

\[ \sum _{k,l\neq i}\frac{\partial ^2\log |F|^2}{\partial v_ k\partial \bar v_ l}\, \xi _ k\bar\xi _ l =\frac{|\Lambda \xi |^2}{|F|^2}-\frac{|\langle \Lambda \xi ,F\rangle |^2}{|F|^4} =\frac{|F|^2|\Lambda \xi |^2-|\langle \Lambda \xi ,F\rangle |^2}{|F|^4}. \]

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality this is nonnegative, and it vanishes only if $\Lambda \xi =\mu F$ for some $\mu \in \mathbb C$. Since $F$ does not lie in the image of $\Lambda $, this forces $\mu =0$, so $\Lambda \xi =0$ and $\xi =0$. By the matrix criterion, the restriction of $\eta $ to each fiber of $E\to W$ is positive definite.

Step 2: positivity near $E$. The form $P=\pi ^*\omega $ is a smooth closed semipositive $(1,1)$-form on $Y$. We regard the real $(1,1)$-forms $P$ and $\eta $ in a chart $(z,t,v)$ as Hermitian forms on holomorphic tangent vectors, given by their coefficient matrices, so that $P(\tau ,\tau )=\omega (\mathrm d\pi \, \tau ,\mathrm d\pi \, \tau )$ by the chain rule; norms of tangent vectors are taken in the coordinates of the chart. Let $V$ be spanned by the vectors $\partial /\partial v_ k$ and $H$ by the vectors $\partial /\partial z_ l$ and $\partial /\partial t$, and write $P$ and $\eta $ in block form with respect to the decomposition of the tangent space into $V$ and $H$. At points of $E$ we have $\mathrm d\pi (\partial /\partial v_ k)=t\, \partial /\partial w_ k=0$, while $\mathrm d\pi (\partial /\partial z_ l)=\partial /\partial z_ l$ and $\mathrm d\pi (\partial /\partial t)=\sum _ j\tilde v_ j\, \partial /\partial w_ j$ are linearly independent. Since $\omega $ is positive definite, $P_{HV}=0$ and $P_{HH}$ is positive definite at points of $E$, while $\eta _{VV}$ is positive definite there by Step 1.

Choose finitely many compact sets $K_\iota $ whose interiors cover $E$, each contained in a chart $(z,t,v)$. The smallest eigenvalues of $P_{HH}$ and of $\eta _{VV}$ are continuous and positive on the compact sets $E\cap K_\iota $, so there are $a,c>0$ such that they exceed $2c$ and $2a$, respectively, on $E\cap K_\iota $ for every $\iota $. Let $M>0$ bound the operator norm of $\eta $ on every $K_\iota $, so that $|\eta (\tau ,\tau ')|\leq M|\tau ||\tau '|$, and choose $\ell \in (0,1]$ with $2M\ell +M\ell ^2\leq a/2$. Where $P_{HH}$ is invertible, the linear map $L=P_{HH}^{-1}P_{HV}\colon V\to H$ depends continuously on the point, and it vanishes on $E$. Hence every point of $E\cap K_\iota $ has a neighborhood on whose intersection with $K_\iota $ we have $P_{HH}\geq c$, $\eta _{VV}\geq a$ and $\| L\| \leq \ell $. Let $O_\iota $ be the union of these neighborhoods. Then

\[ \mathcal N=\operatorname{Int}\Bigl (\bigcup _\iota K_\iota \Bigr )\cap \bigcap _\iota \bigl (O_\iota \cup (Y\setminus K_\iota )\bigr ) \]

is an open neighborhood of $E$ with $\mathcal N\cap K_\iota \subset O_\iota $ for every $\iota $. On $\mathcal N\cap K_\iota $ we have, for $\xi \in V$,

\[ \eta (\xi -L\xi ,\xi -L\xi )\geq \bigl (a-2M\| L\| -M\| L\| ^2\bigr )|\xi |^2\geq \frac a2|\xi |^2,\qquad |\xi -L\xi |\leq 2|\xi |. \]

Write a tangent vector at a point of $\mathcal N\cap K_\iota $ as $\tau =\xi +\zeta $ with $\xi \in V$ and $\zeta \in H$, and put $\sigma =\zeta +L\xi \in H$. Completing the square,

\[ P(\tau ,\tau )=P_{HH}(\sigma ,\sigma )+S(\xi ,\xi ),\qquad S=P_{VV}-P_{VH}P_{HH}^{-1}P_{HV}, \]

and $S\geq 0$ because $P\geq 0$ (take $\zeta =-L\xi $, so that $\sigma =0$). Hence $P(\tau ,\tau )\geq c|\sigma |^2$. Since $\tau =(\xi -L\xi )+\sigma $,

\[ \eta (\tau ,\tau )\geq \frac a2|\xi |^2-4M|\xi ||\sigma |-M|\sigma |^2. \]

Therefore, for $\varepsilon >0$,

\[ (P+\varepsilon \eta )(\tau ,\tau )\geq (c-\varepsilon M)|\sigma |^2+\frac{\varepsilon a}2|\xi |^2-4\varepsilon M|\xi ||\sigma |. \]

The right-hand side is a quadratic form in $(|\sigma |,|\xi |)$, which is positive definite as soon as $\varepsilon M<c$ and $(c-\varepsilon M)a>8\varepsilon M^2$; and $(\sigma ,\xi )\neq 0$ for $\tau \neq 0$. Thus $\pi ^*\omega +\varepsilon \eta $ is positive definite on the fixed neighborhood $\mathcal N$ of $E$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$.

Step 3: positivity away from $E$. On the compact set $Y\setminus \mathcal N\subset Y\setminus E$ the map $\pi $ is a local biholomorphism, so $\pi ^*\omega $ is positive definite at every point. Cover $Y\setminus \mathcal N$ by finitely many compact sets contained in charts. On each of them the smallest eigenvalue of the coefficient matrix of $\pi ^*\omega $ is bounded below by a positive constant $c'$, and the operator norm of the coefficient matrix of $\eta $ is bounded by a constant $M'$, with $c'$ and $M'$ independent of the set. Hence $\pi ^*\omega +\varepsilon \eta $ is positive definite on $Y\setminus \mathcal N$ if $\varepsilon M'<c'$. Together with Step 2, this shows that $\pi ^*\omega +\varepsilon \eta $ is positive definite on all of $Y$ for every sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$. It is closed, since both terms are, so it is a Kähler form on $Y$. $\square$

Definition 13.1.11. Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold. A quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi $ on $X$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ if it has analytic singularities of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$, the function $\exp (\varphi /c)$ is smooth on $X$, including at its zeros, and on every log resolution $\pi $ of $\mathfrak a$, with $\pi ^*\mathfrak a=\mathcal O(-D)$, one has locally

\[ \varphi \circ \pi =c\log |s_ D|^2+h,\qquad h\text{ smooth}. \]

The log resolutions in this definition are compact Kähler manifolds by proposition 13.1.10. The smoothness of $\exp (\varphi /c)$ on $X$ and that of the remainder on the log resolutions are separate requirements. We require the local form on every log resolution, not only on one, because later proofs use it on a resolution chosen in the course of the argument. Both requirements hold if $\varphi $ has presentations (13.1) of type $(c,\mathfrak a)$ with smooth remainders $b$, since then $\exp (\varphi /c)=\mathrm e^{b/c}\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$ locally and proposition 13.1.7 applies; they also hold for the cutoff sums of lemma 13.4.1.

13.2 Local Bergman kernels and ideal generators

From Foundations of pluripotential theory

In this section $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, is a bounded pseudoconvex open set, $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(\Omega )$, and integrals are taken with respect to Lebesgue measure unless another measure is specified. For an integer $m\geq 1$ we put

\[ H_ m=\Bigl \{ F\in \mathcal O(\Omega ):\| F\| _ m^2 =\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}<\infty \Bigr \} ,\qquad K_ m(z)=\sup _{\| F\| _ m\leq 1}|F(z)|^2. \]

Thus $H_ m$ is the space $\mathcal H_{mu}(\Omega )$ of theorem 12.2.7, for the complex manifold $\Omega $ and the weight $mu$, and the function $K_ m$ is the Bergman kernel of $H_ m$ on the diagonal.

We need that $H_ m$ is a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions that generates the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(mu)$, with bounded coefficients near each point. These facts were proved in chapter 12 on bounded pseudoconvex open sets, also for measures $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ comparable with Lebesgue measure (theorem 12.2.7, lemma 12.2.8), and the next lemma restates them in the notation of this section. Throughout this section, $\varrho $ denotes a measurable function on $\Omega $ with $c_\varrho \leq \varrho \leq C_\varrho $ for positive constants $c_\varrho $ and $C_\varrho $. Such measures arise in section 13.3, where the kernels are computed with the volume form of a Kähler form.

Lemma 13.2.1. Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, be a bounded pseudoconvex open set, let $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(\Omega )$, and let $\varrho $ be a measurable function on $\Omega $ with $c_\varrho \leq \varrho \leq C_\varrho $ for positive constants $c_\varrho $ and $C_\varrho $. Put

\[ \mathcal H=\Bigl \{ F\in \mathcal O(\Omega ):\| F\| ^2=\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi }\varrho \, \mathrm dV<\infty \Bigr \} ,\qquad \langle F,G\rangle =\int _\Omega F\bar G\, \mathrm e^{-\psi }\varrho \, \mathrm dV. \]

  1. For every compact set $K\subset \Omega $ there is a constant $C_ K$ such that $|F(z)|\leq C_ K\| F\| $ for all $F\in \mathcal H$ and $z\in K$. The space $\mathcal H$ is a separable Hilbert space, and it has an orthonormal basis, that is, an orthonormal family $(e_\ell )$, finite or countable, whose linear span is dense. For every such family and every $F\in \mathcal H$, the partial sums of $\sum _\ell \langle F,e_\ell \rangle e_\ell $ converge to $F$ in norm and locally uniformly on $\Omega $, and $\sum _\ell |\langle F,e_\ell \rangle |^2=\| F\| ^2$.

  2. The germs of the elements of $\mathcal H$ generate $\mathcal I(\psi )$ at every point of $\Omega $, and near each point finitely many elements of $\mathcal H$ already generate $\mathcal I(\psi )$.

  3. Let $x\in \Omega $, and let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s\in \mathcal H$ generate $\mathcal I(\psi )$ on a neighborhood of $x$. Then there are a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ and a constant $C$ such that every $F\in \mathcal H$ can be written as $F=\sum _ ig_ ih_ i$ on $V$ with $h_ i\in \mathcal O(V)$ and $\sup _ V|h_ i|\leq C\| F\| $.

Proof. We regard $\Omega $ as a complex manifold, with the single chart given by the identity map. Then $\psi $ is a weight on $\Omega $ in the sense of section 12.2, the set $\Omega $ satisfies the hypotheses of theorem 12.2.7, and $\mathcal H$ is the space $\mathcal H_\psi (\Omega ,\varrho )$ of that theorem, with the same inner product. Hence (1) is contained in theorem 12.2.7(1) and (2), and (2) follows from theorem 12.2.7(3), because an initial segment of an enumerated orthonormal basis is a finite family of elements of $\mathcal H$. Finally, (3) is the first assertion of lemma 12.2.8, applied to the weight $\psi $ on $\Omega $. $\square$

By lemma 13.2.1, applied with $\psi =mu$ and $\varrho =1$, the space $H_ m$ is a Hilbert space on which evaluation at each point is a bounded linear functional; in particular $K_ m$ is finite. Let $(e_\ell )$ be an orthonormal basis of $H_ m$. Then

13.2
\begin{equation} K_ m=\sum _\ell |e_\ell |^2. \end{equation}

Indeed, let $F$ lie in the unit ball of $H_ m$ and put $c_\ell =\langle F,e_\ell \rangle $. By lemma 13.2.1(1), $F(z)=\sum _\ell c_\ell e_\ell (z)$ and $\sum _\ell |c_\ell |^2=\| F\| _ m^2\leq 1$, so the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $|F(z)|^2\leq \sum _\ell |e_\ell (z)|^2$. Conversely, for each $N$ the function $F_ N=\sum _{\ell \leq N}\overline{e_\ell (z)}e_\ell $ satisfies $F_ N(z)=\| F_ N\| _ m^2=\sum _{\ell \leq N}|e_\ell (z)|^2$; if this number is positive, then $F_ N/\| F_ N\| _ m$ lies in the unit ball, and $\sum _{\ell \leq N}|e_\ell (z)|^2=|F_ N(z)|^2/\| F_ N\| _ m^2\leq K_ m(z)$. The same argument applies to the kernel of the space defined with $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ in place of Lebesgue measure, for an orthonormal basis of that space.

Lemma 13.2.2. The function $K_ m$ is real analytic on $\Omega $. Near each point of $\Omega $ it is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of $\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$, where $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s$ are finitely many generators of $\mathcal I(mu)$ near that point. If $\pi \colon Y\to \Omega $ is a holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$ such that $\pi ^*\mathcal I(mu)=\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$ for an effective divisor $D$ on $Y$, for instance a log resolution of $\mathcal I(mu)$, then locally on $Y$

\[ K_ m\circ \pi =|s_ D|^2b_ m,\qquad b_ m\text{ smooth and strictly positive}, \]

where $s_ D$ is a local equation of $D$. The same conclusions hold if Lebesgue measure is replaced by $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ in the definition of $H_ m$.

Proof. We give the proof for the measure $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$; Lebesgue measure is the case $\varrho =1$. By lemma 13.2.1, applied with $\psi =mu$, the space $H_ m$ is a Hilbert space with an orthonormal basis $(e_\ell )$, evaluation is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $\Omega $, and (13.2) holds.

We show that the map $z\mapsto (e_\ell (z))_\ell $ into $\ell ^2$ is given locally by a convergent power series with coefficients in $\ell ^2$. Fix a point $z_0$ and a polydisc $P$ of multiradius $\rho $ about $z_0$ with $\overline P\subset \Omega $, and let $C$ be the evaluation bound on $\overline P$. For a finitely supported sequence $(c_\ell )$ with $\sum _\ell |c_\ell |^2\leq 1$, the function $F=\sum _\ell c_\ell e_\ell $ has norm at most one, so $|F|\leq C$ on $P$, and the Cauchy estimates give $|\partial ^\alpha F(z_0)/\alpha !|\leq C\rho ^{-\alpha }$. Since $\partial ^\alpha F(z_0)/\alpha !=\sum _\ell c_\ell A_{\alpha ,\ell }$ with $A_{\alpha ,\ell }=\partial ^\alpha e_\ell (z_0)/\alpha !$, taking the supremum over such sequences shows that the sequence $A_\alpha =(A_{\alpha ,\ell })_\ell $ has $\ell ^2$ norm $\| A_\alpha \| \leq C\rho ^{-\alpha }$. Let $0<\lambda <1$, and let $P_\lambda $ be the concentric polydisc of multiradius $\lambda \rho $. For $z\in P_\lambda $ we have $\sum _\alpha \| A_\alpha \| |(z-z_0)^\alpha |\leq C\sum _\alpha \lambda ^{|\alpha |}<\infty $, so the $\ell ^2$-valued series $\sum _\alpha A_\alpha (z-z_0)^\alpha $ converges absolutely, and its $\ell $th component is the Taylor series of $e_\ell $, which converges to $e_\ell (z)$. By (13.2),

\[ K_ m(z)=\Bigl \| \sum _\alpha A_\alpha (z-z_0)^\alpha \Bigr \| ^2=\sum _{\alpha ,\beta }\langle A_\alpha ,A_\beta \rangle (z-z_0)^\alpha \overline{(z-z_0)^\beta },\qquad z\in P_\lambda , \]

where $|\langle A_\alpha ,A_\beta \rangle |\leq C^2\rho ^{-\alpha -\beta }$. Hence this double power series, and each series obtained from it by differentiating term by term, converge uniformly on $P_{\lambda '}$ for every $\lambda '<\lambda $, since a derivative of order $k$ only introduces factors bounded by a polynomial of degree $k$ in $|\alpha |+|\beta |$. Therefore $K_ m$ is real analytic, in particular smooth.

Next choose members $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s$ of $H_ m$ generating $\mathcal I(mu)$ near a given point, by lemma 13.2.1(2); by the remark following definition 13.1.5, any other finite system of generators gives a comparable sum of squares. By the definition of $K_ m$, $|g_ i|^2\leq \| g_ i\| _ m^2K_ m$, so $\sum _ i|g_ i|^2\leq \bigl (\sum _ i\| g_ i\| _ m^2\bigr )K_ m$. Conversely, lemma 13.2.1(3) gives, on a fixed smaller neighborhood, a representation $F=\sum _ ig_ ih_ i$ with $|h_ i|\leq C'$ for every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_ m$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $|F|^2\leq sC'^2\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$, and hence $K_ m\leq sC'^2\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$ there. These constants may depend on $m$, $u$ and $\varrho $.

Let $\pi $ and $D$ be as in the statement, let $y\in Y$, let $V$ be a neighborhood of $\pi (y)$ on which the bounded representation of the previous paragraph holds, and let $s_ D$ be a local equation of $D$ on a neighborhood $W\subset \pi ^{-1}(V)$ of $y$. Since $\pi ^*\mathcal I(mu)$ is generated by $s_ D$ on $W$, we may write $g_ i\circ \pi =s_ Dk_ i$ and $s_ D=\sum _ il_ i\, (g_ i\circ \pi )$ with holomorphic $k_ i$ and $l_ i$, after shrinking $W$; since $s_ D$ does not vanish identically on any open set, $\sum _ il_ ik_ i=1$. For $F\in H_ m$, the representation $F=\sum _ ig_ ih_ i$ on $V$ gives $F\circ \pi =s_ D\sum _ i(h_ i\circ \pi )k_ i$ on $W$, so

\[ \frac{F\circ \pi }{s_ D}=\sum _ i(h_ i\circ \pi )\, k_ i,\qquad \Bigl |\frac{F\circ \pi }{s_ D}\Bigr |\leq C'\| F\| _ m\sum _ i|k_ i|. \]

Hence evaluation of $(F\circ \pi )/s_ D$ is bounded on each compact subset of $W$, uniformly for $F$ in the unit ball of $H_ m$. The power-series argument above therefore applies, in local coordinates on $Y$, to the holomorphic functions $(e_\ell \circ \pi )/s_ D$, and shows that $b_ m=\sum _\ell |(e_\ell \circ \pi )/s_ D|^2$ is smooth; by (13.2), $K_ m\circ \pi =|s_ D|^2b_ m$.

To see that $b_ m$ is strictly positive, let $F$ lie in the unit ball of $H_ m$, with coefficients $c_\ell =\langle F,e_\ell \rangle $ and partial sums $S_ N$. The estimate in the last display, applied to $F-S_ N$, shows that $(S_ N\circ \pi )/s_ D\to (F\circ \pi )/s_ D$ pointwise on $W$; hence $(F\circ \pi )/s_ D=\sum _\ell c_\ell (e_\ell \circ \pi )/s_ D$, and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $|(F\circ \pi )/s_ D|^2\leq b_ m$. Taking $F=g_ i/\| g_ i\| _ m$ for the indices with $g_ i\neq 0$, we obtain $b_ m\geq |k_ i|^2/\| g_ i\| _ m^2$, and the functions $k_ i$ have no common zero. $\square$

Theorem 13.2.3. Let $\Omega $ have diameter at most $L$, and put $u_ m=m^{-1}\log K_ m$. Then $u_ m$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega $ and has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$. There are constants $C_1,C_2>0$, depending only on $n$ and $L$, such that

13.3
\begin{equation} u(z)-\frac{\log C_1}{m}\leq u_ m(z) \leq \sup _{B(z,r)}u+\frac1m\log \frac{C_2}{r^{2n}} \end{equation}

13.4
\begin{equation} \nu (u,z)-\frac nm\leq \nu (u_ m,z)\leq \nu (u,z). \end{equation}

Proof. Let $\Omega _0$ be a connected component of $\Omega $, and choose $a\in \Omega _0$ with $u(a)>-\infty $. By corollary 11.4.5, applied on $\Omega $ to the weight $mu$, some element of $H_ m$ takes the value $1$ at $a$, so $H_ m$ contains a function that does not vanish identically on $\Omega _0$. For every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_ m$ that is not identically zero on $\Omega _0$, $\log |F|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega _0$ by proposition 8.3.3, and $\log K_ m$ is the supremum of these functions on $\Omega _0$. Since $K_ m$ is continuous by lemma 13.2.2, this family is locally uniformly bounded above, and $\log K_ m$ is upper semicontinuous with values in $[-\infty ,\infty )$, so it equals its upper semicontinuous regularization. Hence theorem 8.3.2 shows that $u_ m$ is plurisubharmonic. The comparison in lemma 13.2.2 gives $u_ m=m^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ locally, with generators $g_ i$ of $\mathcal I(mu)$; this is the asserted type.

For the lower bound in (13.3), let $u(z)>-\infty $; at points of the pole set of $u$ there is nothing to prove. By corollary 11.4.5, applied on $\Omega $ to the weight $mu$, there is $F\in H_ m$ with $F(z)=1$ and $\| F\| _ m^2\leq C_1\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}$, where $C_1$ depends only on $n$ and $L$. \begin{align*} |F(z)|^2& \leq C_2r^{-2n}\int _{B(z,r)}|F|^2\\ & \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp \Bigl (m\sup _{B(z,r)}u\Bigr )\int _{B(z,r)}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu} \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp \Bigl (m\sup _{B(z,r)}u\Bigr ), \end{align*}

with $C_2=n!/\pi ^ n$, and taking the supremum over $F$ proves the upper bound.

Letting $r\downarrow 0$ and using the upper semicontinuity of $u$ gives $u_ m(z)\to u(z)$, also at points of the pole set. The stalk $\mathcal I(mu)_ z$ is not zero: by the first paragraph, applied to the component of $\Omega $ containing $z$, it contains the germ of an element of $H_ m$ that does not vanish identically on that component, and this germ is not zero by the identity theorem. Since $u_ m=m^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ near $z$, lemma 13.1.6 gives $\nu (u_ m,z)=m^{-1}\operatorname {ord}_ z\mathcal I(mu)$, and (13.4) follows from lemma 12.3.1.

The space of holomorphic functions is the same, and the two norms satisfy $c_\varrho \| F\| _ m^2\leq \| F\| _{m,\varrho }^2\leq C_\varrho \| F\| _ m^2$. \[ C_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m\leq K^\varrho _ m\leq c_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m. \]

The proof of plurisubharmonicity applies verbatim to $m^{-1}\log K^\varrho _ m$, with the unit ball taken for $\varrho $ and the continuity of $K^\varrho _ m$ given by lemma 13.2.2. The lower and upper bounds follow from the last display, with the constants $C_\varrho C_1$ and $C_2/c_\varrho $. The convergence and the Lelong numbers then follow as before, since lemma 13.2.2 applies to $K^\varrho _ m$. Finally, $K^\varrho _ m\mathrm e^{-mu}\leq c_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable. $\square$

The kernels at the scales $m$ and $2m$ are compared by extending a function from the diagonal of $\Omega \times \Omega $.

Lemma 13.2.4. Suppose that the spaces $H_ m$ are defined with respect to $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$; for instance $\varrho =1$. There is a constant $C_\Omega >0$, depending only on $n$, on a bound $L$ for the diameter of $\Omega $ and on $c_\varrho $ and $C_\varrho $, and in particular independent of $m$ and $u$, such that

\[ K_{2m}\leq C_\Omega K_ m^2\qquad \text{on }\Omega . \]

Proof. We write $\| \cdot \| _ m$ for the norm of $H_ m$. By the definition of $K_ m$, every $h\in H_ m$ satisfies $|h(\zeta )|^2\leq \| h\| _ m^2K_ m(\zeta )$ for all $\zeta \in \Omega $; if $\| h\| _ m=0$, then $h$ vanishes almost everywhere, hence identically.

Let $F\in H_{2m}$. We first extend $F$ from the diagonal to $\Omega \times \Omega $. By corollary 11.2.5, $\Omega \times \Omega $ is a bounded pseudoconvex open set in $\mathbb C^{2n}$, of diameter at most $\sqrt2L$, and the function $p(z,w)=mu(z)+mu(w)$ is plurisubharmonic on it (proposition 8.2.3, proposition 8.2.5). The diagonal $\Lambda =\{ (z,w)\in \mathbb C^{2n}:z=w\} $ is a complex linear subspace of codimension $n$, and the map $z\mapsto (z,z)$ multiplies Euclidean lengths by $\sqrt2$, so Lebesgue measure on $\Lambda $ corresponds to $2^ n$ times Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb C^ n$. On $\Lambda \cap (\Omega \times \Omega )$, identified with $\Omega $, the weight $p$ restricts to $2mu$, so

\[ \int _{\Lambda \cap (\Omega \times \Omega )}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\, \mathrm dV_{2n}=2^ n\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-2mu}\, \mathrm dV\leq 2^ nc_\varrho ^{-1}\| F\| _{2m}^2. \]

The extension statement of corollary 11.4.5, applied on $\Omega \times \Omega $ to the weight $p$ and the subspace $\Lambda $, gives $G\in \mathcal O(\Omega \times \Omega )$ with $G(z,z)=F(z)$ and $\int _{\Omega \times \Omega }|G|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\, \mathrm dV_{4n}\leq C^ n(\sqrt2L)^{2n}2^ nc_\varrho ^{-1}\| F\| _{2m}^2$, where $C$ is the constant of theorem 11.4.4. Hence

\[ \| G\| ^2=\int _{\Omega \times \Omega }|G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(z)-mu(w)}\varrho (z)\varrho (w)\, \mathrm dV(z)\, \mathrm dV(w)\leq C_\Omega \| F\| _{2m}^2 \]

with $C_\Omega =4^ nC^ nL^{2n}C_\varrho ^2/c_\varrho $.

We now bound $G$ on the diagonal by $K_ m$. For $w\in \Omega $ put

\[ N(w)=\int _\Omega |G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}\varrho (z)\, \mathrm dV(z)\in [0,\infty ]. \]

By Tonelli's theorem, $N$ is measurable and $\int _\Omega N(w)\mathrm e^{-mu(w)}\varrho (w)\, \mathrm dV(w)=\| G\| ^2<\infty $; since $\mathrm e^{-mu}\varrho >0$ almost everywhere, $N(w)<\infty $ for almost every $w$. For such $w$, the function $G(\cdot ,w)$ belongs to $H_ m$ with $\| G(\cdot ,w)\| _ m^2=N(w)$, so $|G(z,w)|^2\leq N(w)K_ m(z)$ for every $z$. Fix $z\in \Omega $. Integrating this inequality in $w$, we obtain

\[ \int _\Omega |G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(w)}\varrho (w)\, \mathrm dV(w)\leq K_ m(z)\| G\| ^2, \]

so $G(z,\cdot )\in H_ m$ with $\| G(z,\cdot )\| _ m^2\leq K_ m(z)\| G\| ^2$, and therefore $|G(z,w)|^2\leq K_ m(z)K_ m(w)\| G\| ^2$ for every $w$. Taking $w=z$,

\[ |F(z)|^2=|G(z,z)|^2\leq K_ m(z)^2\| G\| ^2\leq C_\Omega \| F\| _{2m}^2K_ m(z)^2. \]

Taking the supremum over the unit ball of $H_{2m}$ proves the lemma. $\square$

13.3 Overlap interpolation and quantitative patching

From Foundations of pluripotential theory

From now on $(X,\omega )$ is a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$. If $n=0$, then $X$ is a finite set, every quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ is real-valued and hence smooth, and every multiplier ideal is the unit ideal; the constant sequence $\Phi _ j=\varphi $ then has all the approximation properties proved below. We therefore assume $n\geq 1$.

We fix, once and for all, finitely many concentric coordinate balls

\[ U_ j\Subset U'_ j\Subset B_ j\Subset B_ j^+, \qquad j=1,\ldots ,J,\qquad X=\bigcup _ jU_ j, \]

and smooth functions $\chi _ j\colon X\to [0,1]$ with compact support in $U'_ j$ and $\chi _ j=1$ on $U_ j$. We require in addition that whenever $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}\neq \varnothing $, the union $B_ j^+\cup B_ l^+$ is contained in a coordinate ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$ whose closure lies in a coordinate chart. By lemma 13.1.1, applied to the forms $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ j$ on $X$, there is a constant $C_\chi \geq 0$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ j\geq -C_\chi \omega $ for all $j$.

Such balls and cutoff functions exist. To see this, we first construct a metric $d$ defining the topology of $X$. Choose finitely many charts $\kappa _ k\colon V_ k\to \mathbb C^ n$, $1\leq k\leq N$, and compact sets $L_ k\subset V_ k$ whose interiors cover $X$, and, by proposition 7.1.3, smooth functions $\psi _ k\colon X\to [0,1]$ with compact support in $V_ k$ and $\psi _ k=1$ on $L_ k$. The map $\Psi =(\psi _ k,\psi _ k\kappa _ k)_{1\leq k\leq N}$ from $X$ to $\mathbb R^ N\times \mathbb C^{nN}$, where $\psi _ k\kappa _ k$ is extended by zero outside $V_ k$, is continuous and injective: if $\Psi (x)=\Psi (y)$ and $x\in L_ k$, then $\psi _ k(y)=\psi _ k(x)=1$, so $y\in V_ k$ and $\kappa _ k(y)=\kappa _ k(x)$, whence $y=x$. A continuous injective map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its image, so $d(x,y)=|\Psi (x)-\Psi (y)|$ is a metric defining the topology of $X$.

Now cover $X$ by finitely many coordinate balls whose closures lie in coordinate charts, and let $\delta >0$ be a Lebesgue number of this cover for $d$, so that every subset of $X$ of $d$-diameter less than $\delta $ lies in one of these balls. About every point of $X$ choose concentric coordinate balls $U\Subset U'\Subset B\Subset B^+$, with $\overline{B^+}$ contained in a coordinate chart and of $d$-diameter less than $\delta /2$. By compactness finitely many of the balls $U$ cover $X$; these give the $U_ j$, and proposition 7.1.3 gives the cutoff functions $\chi _ j$. If $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}\neq \varnothing $, then $B_ j^+\cup B_ l^+$ is a union of two intersecting sets of diameter less than $\delta /2$, so it has diameter less than $\delta $ and lies in one ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$ of the cover.

All the quantities attached to these choices are now fixed: the bounds for the densities of $\omega ^ n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure in the charts, the coordinate changes, the diameters of the balls, the distances from $\overline{U'_ j}$ to $\partial B_ j$, and the derivatives of the cutoffs. We say that a constant depends only on the fixed geometry if it depends only on these data, hence only on $(X,\omega )$ once the choices have been made.

Lemma 13.3.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $a\geq 0$, and let $v_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(B_ j,\theta )$ for $j=1,\ldots ,J$ satisfy $v_ j\leq v_ l+a$ on $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}$ for all $j,l$. Then the function

\[ v(x)=\max _{j:\, x\in \overline{U'_ j}}\bigl (v_ j(x)+a\chi _ j(x)\bigr ) \]

is quasi-plurisubharmonic on $X$ and satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq -aC_\chi \omega $ and $v_ j\leq v\leq v_ j+2a$ on $U_ j$.

Proof. Since the $U_ i$ cover $X$, the maximum is taken over a nonempty set at every point. Fix $x_0\in X$ and choose $i$ with $x_0\in U_ i$. Indices $j$ with $x_0\notin \overline{U'_ j}$ do not occur near $x_0$. If $x_0\in \partial U'_ j$, then $\chi _ j=0$ near $x_0$, because $\chi _ j$ has compact support in $U'_ j$; so near $x_0$ the $j$th term, where it is defined, equals $v_ j\leq v_ i+a=v_ i+a\chi _ i$, since $\chi _ i=1$ on the neighborhood $U_ i$ of $x_0$. Such a term never exceeds the $i$th term and can be omitted. Hence on a small neighborhood of $x_0$, $v$ is the maximum of $v_ j+a\chi _ j$ over the fixed set of indices $j$ with $x_0\in U'_ j$. Each of these functions satisfies $\theta +aC_\chi \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v_ j+a\chi _ j)\geq 0$. Adding a local potential of the closed form $\theta +aC_\chi \omega $ on a coordinate ball about $x_0$ (lemma 13.1.2), which makes these functions plurisubharmonic by lemma 13.1.4(2), and applying proposition 8.2.5, we obtain that $v$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic near $x_0$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq -aC_\chi \omega $.

On $U_ j$ the $j$th term is $v_ j+a\geq v_ j$, and every term with index $l$ is at most $v_ l+a\leq v_ j+2a$. This proves the two bounds, also at points of the pole sets. $\square$

Now fix a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$. By lemma 13.1.2 we can choose smooth real functions $h_ j$ on $B_ j^+$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ j=\theta $. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and let $m\geq 1$ be an integer. Then $\varphi +h_ j$ is plurisubharmonic on $B_ j$ by lemma 13.1.4(2), and we let $K_{j,m}$ be the Bergman kernel of section 13.2 on the ball $B_ j$ for the weight $m(\varphi +h_ j)$ and the volume form $\omega ^ n/n!$:

\[ K_{j,m}(x)=\sup \Bigl \{ |F(x)|^2:F\in \mathcal O(B_ j),\ \int _{B_ j}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-m(\varphi +h_ j)}\frac{\omega ^ n}{n!}\leq 1\Bigr \} . \]

We put

\[ a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mh_ j}K_{j,m},\qquad v_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log a_{j,m}=m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}-h_ j. \]

The closure of $B_ j$ lies in a chart, so in the coordinates of $B_ j$ the density of $\omega ^ n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure is bounded above and below by positive constants on $B_ j$. Hence theorem 13.2.3, in the version with the measure $\varrho \, \mathrm dV=\omega ^ n/n!$, applies on $B_ j$: $m^{-1}\log K_{j,m}$ is plurisubharmonic, and $v_{j,m}\in \mathrm{PSH}(B_ j,\theta )$.

Lemma 13.3.2. There is a constant $C_ B\geq 0$, depending only on the fixed geometry and in particular independent of $\theta $, $\varphi $ and $m$, such that

\[ a_{j,m}\leq \mathrm e^{C_ B}a_{l,m}\qquad \text{on }\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l} \]

for all $j,l$. In particular $a_{j,m}$ and $a_{l,m}$ have the same zeros on $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}$, and $|v_{j,m}-v_{l,m}|\leq C_ B/m$ there outside these common zeros.

Proof. Fix $j,l$ with $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}\neq \varnothing $, and work in the coordinates of the ball $\mathcal B_{jl}$. By lemma 13.1.2 choose a smooth function $g$ on $\mathcal B_{jl}$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=\theta $. The functions $h_ j-g$ and $h_ l-g$ are pluriharmonic on the coordinate balls $B_ j^+$ and $B_ l^+$, so by the same lemma $h_ j-g=\operatorname{Re}b_ j$ and $h_ l-g=\operatorname{Re}b_ l$ with $b_ j,b_ l$ holomorphic on these balls (the constants are absorbed into $b_ j,b_ l$). Put $u=\varphi +g$, a plurisubharmonic function on $\mathcal B_{jl}$. Since $|\mathrm e^{-mb_ j/2}|^2=\mathrm e^{-m\operatorname{Re}b_ j}$, multiplication by $\mathrm e^{-mb_ j/2}$ is an isometry from the weighted space defining $K_{j,m}$ onto the space of holomorphic functions on $B_ j$ with weight $mu$ and volume form $\omega ^ n/n!$. If $K^{mu}_{B_ j}$ denotes the Bergman kernel of the latter space, then $K^{mu}_{B_ j}=\mathrm e^{-m\operatorname{Re}b_ j}K_{j,m}$, and therefore

\[ a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}K^{mu}_{B_ j},\qquad a_{l,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}K^{mu}_{B_ l}. \]

Hence it suffices to prove $K^{mu}_{B_ j}\leq C_0^2K^{mu}_{B_ l}$ on $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}$ with $C_0\geq 1$ depending only on the fixed geometry.

Choose a smooth function $\rho \colon X\to [0,1]$ with compact support in $B_ j\cap B_ l$, equal to one on a neighborhood of the compact set $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}$ (proposition 7.1.3); this choice belongs to the fixed geometry. Let $r>0$ be the distance, in the coordinates of $\mathcal B_{jl}$, from $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}$ to the support of $\bar\partial \rho $, and let $c>0$ and $C''$ be lower and upper bounds for the density of $\omega ^ n/n!$ with respect to Lebesgue measure on $B_ j\cup B_ l$ in these coordinates. The ball $B_ l$ is pseudoconvex also in the coordinates of $\mathcal B_{jl}$, since a biholomorphism transports a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function (proposition 8.2.3).

To compare the kernels at a point $x\in \overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}$, we correct $\rho f$, for $f$ holomorphic on $B_ j$, to a holomorphic function on $B_ l$ with the same value at $x$. Let $f$ be holomorphic on $B_ j$ with norm at most one in the weighted space, and extend $\rho f$ by zero to a smooth function on $B_ l$. We apply theorem 11.3.4 on $B_ l$ to the smooth $\bar\partial $-closed form $f\bar\partial \rho $, with the plurisubharmonic function $p=mu+n\log |z-x|^2$ and the smooth function $q=|z|^2$, whose complex Hessian matrix is the identity. On the support of $\bar\partial \rho $ we have $|z-x|^{-2n}\leq r^{-2n}$, so

\[ \int _{B_ l}|f\bar\partial \rho |^2\mathrm e^{-p-q}\, \mathrm dV\leq r^{-2n}\sup |\bar\partial \rho |^2\int _{B_ j}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}\, \mathrm dV\leq \frac{\sup |\bar\partial \rho |^2}{cr^{2n}}. \]

Hence there is a solution of $\bar\partial w=f\bar\partial \rho $ on $B_ l$ with

\[ \int _{B_ l}|w|^2|z-x|^{-2n}\mathrm e^{-mu-|z|^2}\, \mathrm dV\leq C=\frac{\sup |\bar\partial \rho |^2}{cr^{2n}}, \]

and $C$ depends only on the fixed geometry. The function $|z-x|^{2n}\mathrm e^{|z|^2}$ is bounded on the bounded set $B_ l$ by a constant of the same kind, and the density of $\omega ^ n/n!$ is at most $C''$. Hence the squared weighted norm of $w$, for the weight $mu$ and the volume form $\omega ^ n/n!$, is at most a constant $C'$ depending only on the fixed geometry.

Near $x$ we have $\bar\partial w=f\bar\partial \rho =0$, because $\rho =1$ there; hence $\Delta w=0$ near $x$, and by theorem 2.1.4 $w$ is smooth, hence holomorphic, near $x$. Suppose that $w(x)\neq 0$. Then $|w|^2\geq |w(x)|^2/4$ on a ball $B(x,\delta )$ on which $u\leq M$ for some constant $M$, since $u$ is upper semicontinuous, and the last integral is at least

\[ \frac{|w(x)|^2}4\, \mathrm e^{-mM-\sup _{B_ l}|z|^2}\int _{B(x,\delta )}|z-x|^{-2n}\, \mathrm dV=\infty , \]

because $\int _{B(x,\delta )}|z-x|^{-2n}\, \mathrm dV=s_{2n-1}\int _0^\delta t^{-1}\, \mathrm dt$ in polar coordinates (2.1). Hence $w(x)=0$. The function $F=\rho f-w$ satisfies $\bar\partial F=0$ on $B_ l$, so it is holomorphic by theorem 2.1.4, and $F(x)=f(x)$. Since $0\leq \rho \leq 1$ and $\rho $ vanishes outside $B_ j$, the weighted norm of $F$ on $B_ l$ is at most $1+C'^{1/2}=C_0$. By the definition of the kernel, $|f(x)|^2=|F(x)|^2\leq C_0^2K^{mu}_{B_ l}(x)$, and taking the supremum over $f$ gives $K^{mu}_{B_ j}(x)\leq C_0^2K^{mu}_{B_ l}(x)$.

No bound on $u$, $\theta $ or $m$ enters these constants. Taking $C_ B$ to be the maximum of $2\log C_0$ over the finitely many pairs $(j,l)$, with the roles of $j$ and $l$ also exchanged, proves the lemma. $\square$

Theorem 13.3.3. There is a constant $c>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega )$ through the fixed geometry, with the following property. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and let $m\geq 1$ be an integer. Then there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi _ m$ on $X$ such that

where the $g_ i$ are local generators of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$. The bounded term may depend on $m$, $\theta $, $\varphi $ and the point.

Proof. By lemma 13.3.2, the functions $v_{j,m}\in \mathrm{PSH}(B_ j,\theta )$ satisfy $v_{j,m}\leq v_{l,m}+C_ B/m$ on $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ l}$, also at their common zeros. 13.4 Smooth cutoff sums and decreasing approximation

From Foundations of pluripotential theory

The maximum in theorem 13.3.3 destroys smoothness, and that theorem does not compare the functions $\varphi _ m$ for different values of $m$. To obtain the regularity required of gentle singularities and, later, a decreasing sequence, we glue the local kernels instead by a sum with smooth cutoffs and control the curvature of its logarithm by a direct computation. We keep the notation of the previous section.

Lemma 13.4.1.

  1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, let $m\geq 1$ be an integer, and put

    \[ R_ m=\sum _ j\chi _ j^2a_{j,m},\qquad G_ m=m^{-1}\log R_ m, \]

    each summand being extended by zero outside the support of $\chi _ j$. There is a constant $c'>0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G_ m\geq -(c'/m)\omega $. Moreover $R_ m$ is smooth on $X$, and $G_ m$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi ))$.

  2. Let $W$ be an open subset of a complex manifold, let $N\geq 1$, and let $w_1,\ldots ,w_ N$ and $\chi _1,\ldots ,\chi _ N$ be smooth real functions on $W$ such that $S=\sum _{j=1}^ N\chi _ j^2\mathrm e^{w_ j}$ is positive on $W$. Put $\alpha _ j=\chi _ j\partial w_ j+2\partial \chi _ j$. Then, on $W$,

    13.5
    \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log S={}& \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi S^2} \sum _{j<l}\mathrm e^{w_ j+w_ l} (\chi _ j\alpha _ l-\chi _ l\alpha _ j) \wedge \overline{(\chi _ j\alpha _ l-\chi _ l\alpha _ j)}\\ & +\frac{\sum _ j\chi _ j^2\mathrm e^{w_ j}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j}{S} +2\frac{\sum _ j\mathrm e^{w_ j} \bigl (\chi _ j\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ j-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi } \partial \chi _ j\wedge \bar\partial \chi _ j\bigr )}{S}, \end{aligned} \end{equation}

    and the first term on the right of (13.5) is a semipositive $(1,1)$-form.

Proof. Step 1: proof of (2). Let $w_ j$, $\chi _ j$, $S$ and $\alpha _ j$ be as in (2), and put $f_ j=\chi _ j^2\mathrm e^{w_ j}$, so that $S=\sum _ jf_ j$. Then $\partial f_ j=\chi _ j\mathrm e^{w_ j}(\chi _ j\partial w_ j+2\partial \chi _ j)=\chi _ j\mathrm e^{w_ j}\alpha _ j$, so that $\partial S=\sum _ j\chi _ j\mathrm e^{w_ j}\alpha _ j$. Since $\chi _ j$ and $w_ j$ are real, $\bar\partial f_ j=\overline{\partial f_ j}=\chi _ j\mathrm e^{w_ j}\bar\alpha _ j$ with $\bar\alpha _ j=\chi _ j\bar\partial w_ j+2\bar\partial \chi _ j$, and

\[ \begin{aligned} \partial \bar\partial f_ j& =\partial (\chi _ j\mathrm e^{w_ j})\wedge \bar\alpha _ j+\chi _ j\mathrm e^{w_ j}\, \partial \bar\alpha _ j\\ & =\mathrm e^{w_ j}\bigl ((\partial \chi _ j+\chi _ j\partial w_ j)\wedge (\chi _ j\bar\partial w_ j+2\bar\partial \chi _ j)\\ & \qquad +\chi _ j\partial \chi _ j\wedge \bar\partial w_ j+\chi _ j^2\partial \bar\partial w_ j+2\chi _ j\partial \bar\partial \chi _ j\bigr )\\ & =\mathrm e^{w_ j}\bigl (\alpha _ j\wedge \bar\alpha _ j+\chi _ j^2\partial \bar\partial w_ j+2\chi _ j\partial \bar\partial \chi _ j-2\partial \chi _ j\wedge \bar\partial \chi _ j\bigr ), \end{aligned} \]

where the last line follows by expanding $\alpha _ j\wedge \bar\alpha _ j=\chi _ j^2\partial w_ j\wedge \bar\partial w_ j+2\chi _ j\partial w_ j\wedge \bar\partial \chi _ j+2\chi _ j\partial \chi _ j\wedge \bar\partial w_ j+4\partial \chi _ j\wedge \bar\partial \chi _ j$. Next, $\partial \bar\partial \log S=\partial \bar\partial S/S-\partial S\wedge \bar\partial S/S^2$, and since $\sum _ l\chi _ l^2\mathrm e^{w_ l}=S$,

\[ \sum _ j\frac{\mathrm e^{w_ j}\alpha _ j\wedge \bar\alpha _ j}S-\frac{\partial S\wedge \bar\partial S}{S^2} =\frac1{S^2}\sum _{j,l}\mathrm e^{w_ j+w_ l}\bigl (\chi _ l^2\, \alpha _ j\wedge \bar\alpha _ j-\chi _ j\chi _ l\, \alpha _ j\wedge \bar\alpha _ l\bigr ). \]

In the last sum the terms with $j=l$ vanish, and for $j<l$ the terms with indices $(j,l)$ and $(l,j)$ add up to $\mathrm e^{w_ j+w_ l}(\chi _ j\alpha _ l-\chi _ l\alpha _ j)\wedge \overline{(\chi _ j\alpha _ l-\chi _ l\alpha _ j)}$. Multiplying by $\mathrm i/2\pi $ and using $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial $, we obtain (13.5). The first term on the right of (13.5) is semipositive, because the coefficients $\mathrm e^{w_ j+w_ l}/(2\pi S^2)$ are positive and, for a $(1,0)$-form $\gamma =\sum _ k\gamma _ k\, \mathrm dz_ k$, the coefficient matrix $(\gamma _ k\bar\gamma _{k'})$ of $\mathrm i\gamma \wedge \bar\gamma $ is positive semidefinite.

We now prove (1) in three steps.

Step 2: a curvature estimate. By lemma 13.1.1, applied on the compact manifold $X$ to the finitely many smooth forms below, there is a constant $A>0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that $\chi _ j\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ j-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \chi _ j\wedge \bar\partial \chi _ j\geq -A\omega $ for every $j$. Let $W\subset X$ be open, and for the indices $j$ with $W\cap \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j\neq \varnothing $ let $w_ j$ be smooth real functions on $W$ such that

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\geq -m\theta \quad \text{on }W,\qquad \mathrm e^{w_ j}\leq \mathrm e^{C_ B}\mathrm e^{w_ i}\quad \text{on }W\cap U_ i\cap \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j \]

for all such $i,j$. We claim that $S=\sum _ j\chi _ j^2\mathrm e^{w_ j}$ is positive on $W$ and that

\[ \theta +\frac1m\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log S\geq -\frac{2JA\mathrm e^{C_ B}}m\omega \qquad \text{on }W. \]

Let $x\in W$ and choose $i$ with $x\in U_ i$. Then $\chi _ i(x)=1$, so $S(x)\geq \mathrm e^{w_ i(x)}>0$, and $\mathrm e^{w_ j}/S\leq \mathrm e^{C_ B}$ at $x$ for every $j$ with $\chi _ j(x)\neq 0$. Since $S>0$ on $W$, the identity (13.5) of (2) holds on $W$. If $\chi _ j(x)=0$, the $j$th summand of the last term in (13.5) vanishes at $x$, because the nonnegative function $\chi _ j$ attains its minimum at $x$, so that $\partial \chi _ j(x)=0$. Hence the last term is at least $-2JA\mathrm e^{C_ B}\omega $ at $x$. The coefficients $\chi _ j^2\mathrm e^{w_ j}/S$ are nonnegative with sum one, so the middle term is at least $-m\theta $, and the first term is semipositive. This proves the claim.

Step 3: $G_ m$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies the curvature bound. Since the kernels $a_{j,m}$ may vanish, we apply Step 2 to smooth approximations of $\log a_{j,m}$ and pass to the limit. Fix $x_0\in X$ and a small coordinate ball $W$ about $x_0$ such that $W\subset U'_ j$ whenever $x_0\in \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j$, and $W\cap \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j=\varnothing $ otherwise; then the indices $j$ with $W\cap \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j\neq \varnothing $ are those with $x_0\in \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j$. Choose a smooth function $g$ on $W$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=\theta $, and for each such $j$ write $h_ j-g=\operatorname{Re}b_ j$ on $W$ with $b_ j$ holomorphic, by lemma 13.1.2. Since $h_ j=g+\operatorname{Re}b_ j$ on $W$, we have $a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mh_ j}K_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}k_ j$ on $W$, where, by (13.2) and since $|\mathrm e^{-mb_ j/2}|^2=\mathrm e^{-m\operatorname{Re}b_ j}$, $k_ j=\mathrm e^{-m\operatorname{Re}b_ j}K_{j,m}=\sum _\ell |\mathrm e^{-mb_ j/2}e_\ell |^2$ for an orthonormal basis $(e_\ell )$ of the space defining $K_{j,m}$.

For $\delta >0$ the function $\log (k_ j+\delta )$ is smooth, by lemma 13.2.2, and plurisubharmonic: it is the increasing limit of the logarithms of the finite partial sums of these squared moduli together with $|\sqrt\delta |^2$, which are plurisubharmonic by proposition 8.3.3, and since the limit is continuous, theorem 8.3.2 applies. Put $w_ j^\delta =\log (k_ j+\delta )-mg$, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j^\delta \geq -m\theta $. On $W\cap U_ i\cap \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j$, which lies in $\overline{U'_ j}\cap \overline{U'_ i}$, lemma 13.3.2 and the identities $a_{j,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}k_ j$, $a_{i,m}=\mathrm e^{-mg}k_ i$ give $k_ j\leq \mathrm e^{C_ B}k_ i$, and since $\mathrm e^{C_ B}\geq 1$ also $k_ j+\delta \leq \mathrm e^{C_ B}(k_ i+\delta )$. Thus the functions $w_ j^\delta $ satisfy the hypotheses of Step 2 on $W$, and the function

\[ \frac1m\log \sum _ j\chi _ j^2\mathrm e^{w_ j^\delta }+g' \]

is plurisubharmonic on $W$, where $g'$ is a smooth function on $W$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g'=\theta +(2JA\mathrm e^{C_ B}/m)\omega $, given by lemma 13.1.2. Indeed, by Step 2 this function is smooth with positive semidefinite complex Hessian, so proposition 8.2.2(1) applies.

As $\delta \downarrow 0$, the sums $\sum _ j\chi _ j^2\mathrm e^{w_ j^\delta }=\mathrm e^{-mg}\sum _ j\chi _ j^2(k_ j+\delta )$ decrease to $R_ m$, so these functions decrease to $G_ m+g'$, which is not identically $-\infty $, since $G_ m\geq v_{i,m}$ on $U_ i$. By theorem 8.3.1, $G_ m+g'$ is plurisubharmonic on $W$. Hence $G_ m$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, and the curvature bound holds with $c'=2JA\mathrm e^{C_ B}$, which depends only on the fixed geometry.

Step 4: the singularities of $G_ m$ are gentle. Each summand of $R_ m$ is smooth by lemma 13.2.2. On $U_ i$ we have $a_{i,m}\leq R_ m\leq J\mathrm e^{C_ B}a_{i,m}$ by lemma 13.3.2, and $a_{i,m}$ is comparable with $\sum _ k|g_ k|^2$ for local generators $g_ k$ of $\mathcal I(m(\varphi +h_ i))=\mathcal I(m\varphi )$. Thus $G_ m$ has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(m\varphi ))$, and $\exp (mG_ m)=R_ m$ is smooth on $X$.

Now let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be any log resolution of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$, with $\pi ^*\mathcal I(m\varphi )=\mathcal O(-D)$. Its restriction $\pi ^{-1}(B_ j)\to B_ j$ satisfies $\pi ^*\mathcal I(m(\varphi +h_ j))=\mathcal O(-D)$ on $\pi ^{-1}(B_ j)$, so by lemma 13.2.2, applied with the volume form $\omega ^ n/n!$, the pulled-back kernel $K_{j,m}\circ \pi $ is locally $|s_ D|^2$ times a smooth strictly positive function on $\pi ^{-1}(B_ j)$. The factors $\mathrm e^{-mh_ j}\circ \pi $ and $\chi _ j^2\circ \pi $ are smooth, and the latter vanishes outside the closed set $\pi ^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j)\subset \pi ^{-1}(B_ j)$; hence $(R_ m\circ \pi )/|s_ D|^2$ is smooth on $Y$, and it is strictly positive because at every point some $\chi _ i\circ \pi $ equals one. Therefore $G_ m\circ \pi $ is locally the sum of $m^{-1}\log |s_ D|^2$ and a smooth function. These are exactly the requirements of definition 13.1.11. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.2. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. As $m\to \infty $, $G_ m\to \varphi $ pointwise on $X$ and in $L^1(X)$. For every integer $m\geq 1$ and every $x\in X$,

\[ \nu (\varphi ,x)-\frac nm\leq \nu (G_ m,x)\leq \nu (\varphi ,x), \qquad \mathrm e^{m(G_ m-\varphi )}\in L^1(X). \]

Moreover, there is a constant $D\geq 0$, depending only on the fixed geometry, such that $G_{2m}\leq G_ m+D/m$ for every integer $m\geq 1$.

Proof. We first bound $G_ m$ from below. Let $x\in U_ i$. Then $R_ m(x)\geq a_{i,m}(x)$, and the lower bound in (13.3), in the version of theorem 13.2.3 with the measure $\omega ^ n/n!$, gives $m^{-1}\log K_{i,m}(x)\geq \varphi (x)+h_ i(x)-C/m$. Since $h_ i$ cancels, $G_ m\geq v_{i,m}\geq \varphi -C/m$ on $U_ i$, where $C\geq 0$ depends only on the fixed geometry.

For an upper bound, let $r>0$ be smaller than the distance, in the coordinates of $B_ j$, from $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j$ to $\partial B_ j$ for every $j$, and for $x\in \operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j$ let $B_ j(x,r)\subset B_ j$ denote the ball of radius $r$ about $x$ in these coordinates. If $\chi _ j(x)\neq 0$, the upper bound in (13.3) on $B_ j$, again in the version with the measure $\omega ^ n/n!$, gives

\[ v_{j,m}(x)=\frac1m\log K_{j,m}(x)-h_ j(x)\leq \sup _{B_ j(x,r)}\varphi +\Bigl (\sup _{B_ j(x,r)}h_ j-h_ j(x)\Bigr )+\frac1m\log \frac{C_2}{r^{2n}}, \]

where $C_2$ depends only on the fixed geometry. By the mean value theorem, $\sup _{B_ j(x,r)}h_ j-h_ j(x)\leq C_ hr$, where $C_ h$ bounds the gradients of the finitely many functions $h_ j$ on the compact sets $\overline{B_ j}$ and therefore depends on $\theta $. Since $R_ m(x)$ is a sum of at most $J$ terms, each at most $\max _ ja_{j,m}(x)$ over the indices with $\chi _ j(x)\neq 0$,

\[ G_ m(x)\leq \max _{j:\, \chi _ j(x)\neq 0}\sup _{B_ j(x,r)}\varphi +C_ hr+\frac1m\log \frac{JC_2}{r^{2n}}. \]

Fix $x$. Letting first $m\to \infty $ and then $r\downarrow 0$, and using that the balls $B_ j(x,r)$ shrink to $x$, the upper semicontinuity of $\varphi $ and the lower bound, we obtain $G_ m(x)\to \varphi (x)$, also at points of the pole set. For a fixed $r$, the upper bound shows that $G_ m\leq \sup _ X\varphi +C_ hr+|\log (JC_2r^{-2n})|$ on $X$ for every $m$, where $\sup _ X\varphi <\infty $ because $\varphi $ is upper semicontinuous on the compact space $X$. Since moreover $\varphi -C\leq G_ m$ and $\varphi \in L^1(X)$, dominated convergence gives $G_ m\to \varphi $ in $L^1(X)$.

By lemma 13.4.1(1), $G_ m=m^{-1}\log \sum _ k|g_ k|^2+O(1)$ near $x$ with generators $g_ k$ of $\mathcal I(m\varphi )$. Since $G_ m\geq \varphi -C/m$ and $\varphi $ is locally integrable (lemma 13.1.4(1)), $G_ m$ is not identically $-\infty $ near $x$, so the germ of some $g_ k$ at $x$ is not zero and $\mathcal I(m\varphi )_ x\neq 0$. Hence lemma 13.1.6 gives $\nu (G_ m,x)=m^{-1}\operatorname {ord}_ x\mathcal I(m\varphi )$. Write $\varphi =u+f$ on a connected neighborhood of $x$, with $u$ plurisubharmonic and $f$ smooth; then $\mathcal I(m\varphi )=\mathcal I(mu)$ and $\nu (\varphi ,x)=\nu (u,x)$ near $x$, and the Lelong bounds follow from lemma 12.3.1, applied to the weight $u$.

For the integrability, note that $\mathrm e^{m(G_ m-\varphi )}=R_ m\mathrm e^{-m\varphi }=\sum _ j\chi _ j^2K_{j,m}\mathrm e^{-m(\varphi +h_ j)}$. Cover the compact set $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _ j$ by finitely many open sets on which $K_{j,m}\leq C_ m\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$ with members $g_ i$ of the weighted space on $B_ j$, by lemma 13.2.2. Since these $g_ i$ have finite weighted norm, every summand is integrable.

For the doubling inequality, lemma 13.2.4, applied on each $B_ j$ with $u=\varphi +h_ j$ and the measure $\omega ^ n/n!$, gives $K_{j,2m}\leq C_*K_{j,m}^2$, hence $a_{j,2m}\leq C_*a_{j,m}^2$, with $C_*$ depending only on the fixed geometry. Let $x\in U_ i$. For every $j$ with $\chi _ j(x)\neq 0$, lemma 13.3.2 gives $a_{j,m}(x)\leq \mathrm e^{C_ B}a_{i,m}(x)$, and $R_ m(x)\geq a_{i,m}(x)$. Consequently

\[ R_{2m}\leq C_*\sum _ j\chi _ j^2a_{j,m}^2 \leq C_*J\mathrm e^{2C_ B}R_ m^2 \]

at $x$, also when both sides vanish. Taking logarithms and dividing by $2m$ gives $G_{2m}\leq G_ m+D/m$ with $D=\frac12\log \max (1,C_*J\mathrm e^{2C_ B})$. $\square$

To obtain inclusions of multiplier ideals from an integrability estimate, we use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 13.4.3. Let $u,v$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic near a point $x$ of a complex manifold, and let $m>0$ be such that $\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$ is integrable near $x$. If $0<\lambda <\lambda '$ and $m\geq \lambda \lambda '/(\lambda '-\lambda )$, then

\[ \mathcal I(\lambda 'v)_ x\subseteq \mathcal I(\lambda u)_ x. \]

Proof. After shrinking the neighborhood we may subtract a constant from $v$ and assume $v\leq 0$; this changes neither the ideals nor the integrability of $\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$. Put $p=\lambda '/\lambda >1$, and note that $m\geq \lambda p/(p-1)>\lambda $. Let $f\in \mathcal I(\lambda 'v)_ x$, defined on a small neighborhood on which $|f|^2\leq C$ for a constant $C$. All the following inequalities are taken outside the pole sets of $u$ and $v$, which have measure zero. Where $u\geq pv$, we have $\lambda u\geq \lambda 'v$ and hence $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda u}\leq |f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda 'v}$. Where $u<pv$, since $m-\lambda >0$, $v\leq 0$ and $(m-\lambda )p-m=m(p-1)-\lambda p\geq 0$,

\[ (m-\lambda )u-mv\leq ((m-\lambda )p-m)v\leq 0, \]

that is, $-\lambda u\leq m(v-u)$; hence $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-\lambda u}\leq C\mathrm e^{m(v-u)}$. Both bounds are integrable near $x$, so $f\in \mathcal I(\lambda u)_ x$. $\square$

Definition 13.4.4. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. A quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ is a sequence $(\Phi _ j)_{j\geq 1}$ of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions with analytic singularities, decreasing pointwise to $\varphi $, such that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ j\geq -\varepsilon _ j\omega $ for some numbers $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, and such that for all $0<\lambda <\lambda '$

\[ \mathcal I(\lambda '\Phi _ j)\subseteq \mathcal I(\lambda \varphi ) \quad \text{for every sufficiently large }j. \]

Theorem 13.4.5. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Every $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ admits a quasi-equisingular approximation $(\Phi _ j)$ in which each $\Phi _ j$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^ j\varphi ))$, and which can be chosen with $\varepsilon _ j=c'2^{-j}$, where $c'$ is the constant of lemma 13.4.1(1). Moreover $\Phi _ j\to \varphi $ in $L^1(X)$, and the Lelong numbers of $\Phi _ j$ satisfy the bounds of lemma 13.4.2 with $m=2^ j$.

Proof. Put $\Phi _ j=G_{2^ j}+2D/2^ j$, with $D$ as in lemma 13.4.2. The doubling inequality gives

\[ \Phi _{j+1}=G_{2\cdot 2^ j}+\frac D{2^ j}\leq G_{2^ j}+\frac D{2^ j}+\frac D{2^ j}=\Phi _ j, \]

so the sequence is decreasing, and by lemma 13.4.2 its pointwise and $L^1$ limit is $\varphi $. Adding a constant changes neither the curvature, nor the analytic type, nor the Lelong numbers. Moreover $\exp (2^ j\Phi _ j)=\mathrm e^{2D}R_{2^ j}$ is smooth, and the remainder on a log resolution changes only by a constant. Hence $\Phi _ j$ has gentle analytic singularities of type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^ j\varphi ))$ by lemma 13.4.1(1), and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ j\geq -c'2^{-j}\omega $.

It remains to prove the inclusions of multiplier ideals. Let $0<\lambda <\lambda '$. The function $\exp (2^ j(\Phi _ j-\varphi ))=\mathrm e^{2D}\mathrm e^{2^ j(G_{2^ j}-\varphi )}$ is integrable on $X$ by lemma 13.4.2. If $j$ is so large that $2^ j\geq \lambda \lambda '/(\lambda '-\lambda )$, then lemma 13.4.3, with $u=\varphi $, $v=\Phi _ j$ and $m=2^ j$, gives $\mathcal I(\lambda '\Phi _ j)_ x\subseteq \mathcal I(\lambda \varphi )_ x$ at every point $x\in X$. Since this threshold for $j$ depends only on $\lambda $ and $\lambda '$, the inclusion holds as an inclusion of sheaves on $X$ for all such $j$. $\square$

13.5 Comparison of analytic singularities

From Foundations of pluripotential theory

For functions $u,v\colon X\to [-\infty ,\infty )$ we write $u\preceq v$, and say that $u$ is more singular than $v$, if $u\leq v+C$ on $X$ for some constant $C$. Since $X$ is compact, it suffices that every point have a neighborhood on which such an inequality holds. We now show that two quasi-equisingular approximations of the same function are comparable for $\preceq $, up to an arbitrarily small loss in the coefficient. The key is a growth estimate for the members of the multiplier ideals of a function with analytic singularities.

Lemma 13.5.1. Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities on a compact complex manifold $X$, and let $0<\eta <1$. There is $t_0>0$ such that for every real $t\geq t_0$ and every germ $f\in \mathcal I(tv)_ x$, $x\in X$,

\[ \log |f|^2\leq t(1-\eta )v+O(1) \]

near $x$, where the bounded term may depend on $f$. Moreover, for every quasi-plurisubharmonic function $u$ and every $t>0$, local generators $g_ i$ of $\mathcal I(tu)$ satisfy

\[ u\leq t^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1). \]

Proof. For the first assertion we pull $v$ back to log resolutions of its local presentations, where theorem 12.6.5 bounds the vanishing orders of $f$ along the components of the divisor from below. Cover $X$ by finitely many open sets $V_\kappa ^+$ on which $v$ has a presentation (13.1), and choose open sets $V_\kappa \Subset V_\kappa ^+$ that still cover $X$. By theorem A.5.5, the ideal generated by the presentation on $V_\kappa ^+$ has a log resolution $\pi _\kappa \colon Y_\kappa \to V_\kappa ^+$, a sequence of blowups that is finite over $V_\kappa $; it is proper and surjective, so $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(\overline{V_\kappa })$ and the fibers of $\pi _\kappa $ are compact. On $Y_\kappa $ we have locally $v\circ \pi _\kappa =\sum _ Ea_ E\log |s_ E|^2+O(1)$, where $\pi _\kappa ^*(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)=\mathcal O(-\sum _ Ed_ EE)$, $E$ runs over the components of this divisor, $s_ E$ is a local equation of $E$, and $a_ E=cd_ E>0$. Let $A_ E$ be the log discrepancy of $E$ (definition 12.6.3). Only finitely many components $E$ meet the compact set $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(\overline{V_\kappa })$, so altogether there are finitely many pairs $(a_ E,A_ E)$ with $E$ meeting one of these sets. Choose $t_0>0$ with $t_0\eta a_ E\geq A_ E$ for all of them.

Now let $x\in V_\kappa $, let $t\geq t_0$, and let $f\in \mathcal I(tv)_ x$ be defined on an open neighborhood $V'\subset V_\kappa $ of $x$. Near $x$ the function $tv$ differs by a smooth function from a plurisubharmonic function with the same multiplier ideal and of the form required in theorem 12.6.5, with coefficient $tc$. We apply that theorem over $V'$, shrunk if necessary, to the log resolution obtained by restricting $\pi _\kappa $. Each component $E'$ of the restricted divisor is a connected component of $E\cap \pi _\kappa ^{-1}(V')$ for a component $E$ as above, and it has the same coefficient $d_ E$ and the same log discrepancy $A_ E$, as we show below. Since $tc\, d_ E=ta_ E$, the theorem gives

\[ \operatorname {ord}_{E'}f>ta_ E-A_ E\geq t(1-\eta )a_ E \]

for every component $E'$ of the restricted divisor that meets $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(x)$; the second inequality holds because $t\geq t_0$ and $E$ meets $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(\overline{V_\kappa })$.

The coefficient and the log discrepancy of $E'$ are those of $E$ because the vanishing orders along $E$ of a local generator of $\pi _\kappa ^*(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)$ and of a local Jacobian determinant of $\pi _\kappa $ do not depend on the point of $E$ at which they are computed. Indeed, two local generators of this invertible ideal, like two local Jacobian determinants, differ by a unit, which has order zero along $E$; since orders add under products, they have the same order along $E$ at every point of $E$ where both are defined (lemma 12.6.1(2),(4)). Moreover, the order along $E$ of a holomorphic function is locally constant on $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, by the last assertion of lemma 12.6.1(2), and $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is connected (corollary A.2.13); here $E_{\mathrm{reg}}=E$, since the components of a simple normal crossings divisor are smooth.

If $f=0$ there is nothing to prove, so let $f\neq 0$. Shrinking $V'$, we may assume that $V'$ is connected; then $f$ does not vanish identically on any nonempty open subset of $V'$. Hence $f\circ \pi _\kappa $ does not vanish identically on any nonempty open subset of $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(V')$, because $\pi _\kappa $ is a biholomorphism off the nowhere dense set $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(V(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r))$; in particular, the orders of $f$ along the components of the restricted divisor are finite. Let $p\in \pi _\kappa ^{-1}(x)$, and choose coordinates $w$ on a neighborhood of $p$ in $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(V')$ in which the components of the divisor through $p$ are $\{ w_ k=0\} $, $k=1,\ldots ,\ell $, with coefficients $a_ k=a_ E$ for the corresponding components $E$. Then $v\circ \pi _\kappa =\sum _ ka_ k\log |w_ k|^2+O(1)$ near $p$. For each $k$, let $E'_ k$ be the component of the restricted divisor that contains $\{ w_ k=0\} $ near $p$, and put $o_ k=\operatorname {ord}_{E'_ k}f$, an integer with $o_ k>t(1-\eta )a_ k$ by the inequality above. Since $f\circ \pi _\kappa $ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $E'_ k$, its order along $E'_ k$ is the same at all points of the connected submanifold $E'_ k$, by lemma 12.6.1(3); hence $f\circ \pi _\kappa $ is divisible by $w_ k^{o_ k}$ near $p$, by lemma 12.6.1(1),(2).

By the last assertion of lemma 12.6.2, divisibility by each $w_ k^{o_ k}$ separately implies divisibility by their product: $f\circ \pi _\kappa =\prod _ kw_ k^{o_ k}\cdot h$ with $h$ holomorphic near $p$, hence bounded on a smaller neighborhood of $p$. Since $|w_ k|\leq 1$ there, we obtain $|f\circ \pi _\kappa |^2\leq C\prod _ k|w_ k|^{2t(1-\eta )a_ k}\leq C'\exp (t(1-\eta )v\circ \pi _\kappa )$. Finitely many such neighborhoods cover the compact fiber $\pi _\kappa ^{-1}(x)$, and since $\pi _\kappa $ is proper, their union contains the inverse image of a neighborhood of $x$. As $\pi _\kappa $ is surjective, the inequality $|f|^2\leq C'\exp (t(1-\eta )v)$ holds near $x$.

For the second assertion, fix a point and write $u=u_0-\sigma $ near it, with $u_0$ plurisubharmonic and $\sigma $ smooth. Then $\mathcal I(tu)=\mathcal I(tu_0)$ near the point. On a bounded polydisc $\Omega $ about the point on which $u_0$ is bounded above, theorem 12.2.7(3) provides finitely many elements of $\mathcal H_{tu_0}(\Omega )$ that generate $\mathcal I(tu_0)$ near the point, and (12.11) of lemma 12.2.8, applied with $m=t$ to the weight $u_0$, gives $u_0\leq t^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ for these generators. Since $\sigma $ is bounded near the point, and any two finite systems of local generators give comparable sums of squares (the remark following definition 13.1.5), the assertion follows. $\square$

Proposition 13.5.2. Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold, let $\varphi $ and $\Phi $ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ with $\varphi \leq \Phi $, where $\Phi $ has analytic singularities, and let $(\Psi _ k)_{k\geq 1}$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$ such that for all $0<\lambda <\lambda '$

\[ \mathcal I(\lambda '\Psi _ k)\subseteq \mathcal I(\lambda \varphi ) \quad \text{for every sufficiently large }k. \]

Then for each $0<\varepsilon <1$,

\[ \Psi _ k\preceq (1-\varepsilon )\Phi \quad \text{for every sufficiently large }k. \]

In particular, if $(X,\omega )$ is a compact Kähler manifold, $\theta $ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $(\Phi _ j)$ and $(\Psi _ k)$ are quasi-equisingular approximations of $\varphi $, then for each $j$ and each $0<\varepsilon <1$ we have $\Psi _ k\preceq (1-\varepsilon )\Phi _ j$ for every sufficiently large $k$.

Proof. Choose $0<\eta <\varepsilon $ and $\delta >0$ so small that $(1-\eta )/(1+\delta )\geq 1-\varepsilon $. Fix $t\geq t_0$, where $t_0$ is given by lemma 13.5.1 for $v=\Phi $ and $\eta $. By the hypothesis on $(\Psi _ k)$, applied with $\lambda =t$ and $\lambda '=t(1+\delta )$, and since $\varphi \leq \Phi $,

\[ \mathcal I(t(1+\delta )\Psi _ k) \subseteq \mathcal I(t\varphi )\subseteq \mathcal I(t\Phi ) \]

for every sufficiently large $k$. Fix such a $k$ and choose local generators $g_ i$ of $\mathcal I(t(1+\delta )\Psi _ k)$ near a point of $X$. They belong to $\mathcal I(t\Phi )$, so the first part of lemma 13.5.1 gives $\log |g_ i|^2\leq t(1-\eta )\Phi +O(1)$ near the point, and hence $\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2\leq t(1-\eta )\Phi +O(1)$. Its second part, applied to $u=\Psi _ k$ and $t(1+\delta )$, then gives

\[ \Psi _ k\leq \frac1{t(1+\delta )}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1) \leq \frac{1-\eta }{1+\delta }\Phi +O(1). \]

The upper semicontinuous function $\Phi $ is bounded above on the compact space $X$, say by $M$. Since $\Phi -M\leq 0$ and $(1-\eta )/(1+\delta )\geq 1-\varepsilon $, we have $\frac{1-\eta }{1+\delta }(\Phi -M)\leq (1-\varepsilon )(\Phi -M)$, and therefore $\Psi _ k\leq (1-\varepsilon )\Phi +O(1)$ near every point. By compactness $\Psi _ k\preceq (1-\varepsilon )\Phi $; the constant may depend on $k$.

For the last assertion, recall from definition 13.4.4 that each $\Phi _ j$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic with analytic singularities and that $\varphi \leq \Phi _ j$, since the sequence decreases to $\varphi $; and $(\Psi _ k)$ satisfies the hypothesis on the inclusions of multiplier ideals. Hence the first part applies with $\Phi =\Phi _ j$. $\square$

Example 13.5.3. Let $u=c\log |z|^2$ on a disc about $0\in \mathbb C$, with $c>0$. A germ $z^ kh$ with $h(0)\neq 0$ lies in $\mathcal I(mu)_0$ if and only if $|z|^{2k-2mc}$ is integrable near $0$, that is, $k>mc-1$; hence $\mathcal I(mu)$ is generated by $z^{\lfloor mc\rfloor }$ (example 12.5.5). A potential of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$, such as the Bergman potential $u_ m$ of theorem 13.2.3, is therefore $(\lfloor mc\rfloor /m)\log |z|^2+O(1)$ near $0$. Its coefficient $\lfloor mc\rfloor /m$ need not equal $c$ for any $m$, and then such an approximation does not reproduce the multiplier ideals of $u$ at a fixed scale. For instance, let $c$ be irrational, so that $\lfloor mc\rfloor /m<c$ for every $m$, and take the scale $1/c$. Since $u_ m/c$ differs by a bounded function from $c_ m\log |z|^2$ with $c_ m=\lfloor mc\rfloor /(mc)<1$, the same computation gives $\mathcal I(u_ m/c)_0=\mathcal O_0$ for every $m$, whereas $\mathcal I(u/c)_0=\mathcal I(\log |z|^2)_0$ is generated by $z$. This is why definition 13.4.4 compares two different scales $\lambda '>\lambda $ instead of requiring equality of multiplier ideals.

13.6 Bounded growth ideals

From Foundations of pluripotential theory

A germ $f$ belongs to the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(v)$ when $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-v}$ is integrable, a condition that allows $f$ to vanish to a slightly lower order than the growth of $\mathrm e^ v$ suggests. The stricter condition $|f|^2\leq C\mathrm e^ v$ defines a smaller ideal. For a weight $v$ with analytic singularities we prove that this ideal sheaf is coherent (theorem 13.6.2) and that, on a compact manifold, the ideal attached to $kv$ contains $\mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon )v)$ for all large $k$ (corollary 13.6.3). In the proof of coherence, the condition $|f|\leq C|g|^{p/q}$ for a tuple $g$ of holomorphic functions becomes a holomorphic one: on the normalization of a root cover of a cone over the blowup of the ideal $(g)$, it is the holomorphy of a function homogeneous of weight $p$.

Definition 13.6.1. Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on a complex manifold $X$. The bounded growth ideal $\mathcal I_\infty (v)$ is the sheaf of holomorphic germs $f$ satisfying

\[ \log |f|^2\leq v+O(1), \quad \text{equivalently}\quad |f|^2\leq C\exp (v), \]

on a neighborhood of their base point, for some finite constant $C$.

The zero germ belongs to $\mathcal I_\infty (v)$, and $\mathcal I_\infty (v)$ does not change if $v$ is changed by a bounded function. It is a sheaf of ideals, because $|f+g|^2\leq 2|f|^2+2|g|^2$ and holomorphic coefficients are locally bounded. It is contained in $\mathcal I(v)$, since $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-v}$ is locally bounded for $f\in \mathcal I_\infty (v)$.

Theorem 13.6.2. Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities on a complex manifold $X$. Then $\mathcal I_\infty (v)$ is a coherent ideal sheaf. More precisely, let $g=(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)$, with $r\geq 1$, be a tuple of holomorphic functions on a coordinate neighborhood $U$, put $|g|=(\sum _ j|g_ j|^2)^{1/2}$, and let $p,q$ be positive integers. Then the ideal sheaf

\[ \mathcal J_{p/q}(g)=\{ f\in \mathcal O_ U: |f|\leq C|g|^{p/q}\text{ near the base point of $f$, for some }C\} \]

is coherent.

Proof. Coherence is a local property. Fix $x\in U$ and shrink $U$ to a connected neighborhood of $x$. If all $g_ j$ vanish identically there, then $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ is the zero ideal near $x$. Otherwise choose an index $i$ with $g_ i$ not identically zero, and put $U_ i=\{ g_ i\ne 0\} $. By the identity theorem $U_ i$ is dense in $U$, and by lemma A.1.7 it is connected. Steps 1 and 2 construct the normal space described at the beginning of this section, Steps 3 to 5 show that its functions of weight $p$ are finitely generated over the holomorphic functions of $z$, and Step 6 identifies their coefficients with the germs of $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$.

Step 1: a cone over the blowup. Let $B\to U$ be the blowup of the ideal $(g_1,\ldots ,g_ r)$, constructed in proposition A.5.2. Thus $B\subset U\times \mathbb P^{r-1}$, and a point $(z,[\xi ])$ of $B$ determines the line $\mathbb C\xi \subset \mathbb C^ r$. Let $\Lambda =\{ (z,[\xi ],a)\in B\times \mathbb C^ r:a\in \mathbb C\xi \} $ be the total space of this line bundle, and consider the map $\Lambda \to U\times \mathbb C^ r$, $(z,[\xi ],a)\mapsto (z,a)$. It is proper: the inverse image of a compact set is closed and contained in the product of a compact subset of $B$, namely the inverse image of its projection to $U$, with a bounded set of vectors. By theorem A.6.2, its image $C\subset U\times \mathbb C^ r$ is analytic, and we regard it as a reduced complex space. It is invariant under the scalar multiplications $a\mapsto \lambda a$. Over $U_ i$ the blowup is the graph of $z\mapsto [g(z)]$, so the part of $C$ over $U_ i$ is the smooth family of lines

\[ a_ j=\tau g_ j(z),\qquad z\in U_ i,\quad \tau \in \mathbb C. \]

This family is dense in $C$. Indeed, the graph over $U_ i$ is dense in $B$, since $U_ i$ is dense in $U\setminus V(g)$ and the graph over $U\setminus V(g)$ is dense in $B$. Hence its part of $\Lambda $ is dense in $\Lambda $: if $(z,[\xi ],\lambda \xi )\in \Lambda $, choose graph points $(z_ k,[\xi _ k])\to (z,[\xi ])$ over $U_ i$ with representatives $\xi _ k\to \xi $, which is possible in the affine charts of $\mathbb P^{r-1}$; then $(z_ k,[\xi _ k],\lambda \xi _ k)\to (z,[\xi ],\lambda \xi )$. The image of a dense set under the continuous surjection $\Lambda \to C$ is dense.

This family is the part of $C$ over the open set $U_ i$, hence open in $C$, and it is the graph of the holomorphic map $(z,a_ i)\mapsto (a_ ig_ l(z)/g_ i(z))_{l\neq i}$ on the connected manifold $U_ i\times \mathbb C$, hence a connected complex manifold. Therefore $C$ is irreducible. Indeed, suppose that $C$ is the union of two analytic subsets different from $C$. Their traces on this connected manifold are analytic subsets covering it, so one of them has nonempty interior in it and hence, by the identity theorem, contains all of it. Since this manifold is dense in $C$ and analytic subsets are closed, that analytic subset contains $C$, a contradiction. Taking $\tau =0$, we see that $C$ contains $U_ i\times \{ 0\} $, hence $U\times \{ 0\} $, since $C$ is closed and $U_ i$ is dense in $U$.

Step 2: a root cover and its normalization. In $C\times \mathbb C_ b$ consider the analytic set defined by

\[ b^ q=a_ ig_ i^{q-1}, \]

and let $C'$ be the closure of its part over $U_ i$. The set $C'$ is analytic by lemma A.2.5, applied in $U\times \mathbb C^ r\times \mathbb C_ b$ to this analytic set and to the analytic set $\{ g_ i(z)=0\} $; near each of its points it is the union of the branches of that set not contained in $\{ g_ i(z)=0\} $. The projection $C'\to C$ is finite: the monic equation bounds $b$ over compact subsets of $C$ and allows at most $q$ values of $b$. Over $U_ i$ we have $a=\tau g(z)$ and $b^ q=\tau g_ i(z)^ q$, so $b=tg_ i(z)$ with $t^ q=\tau $. Hence the part of $C'$ over $U_ i$ is the image of the injective immersion

\[ U_ i\times \mathbb C\to C',\qquad (z,t)\longmapsto (z,\ a=t^ qg(z),\ b=tg_ i(z)), \]

whose inverse is $t=b/g_ i(z)$; it is a dense open connected submanifold of $C'$, so $C'$ is irreducible by the argument just given for $C$.

Let $Y\to C'$ be the normalization (theorem A.3.6), and let $\nu \colon Y\to C$ be its composition with the projection; $\nu $ is finite. Over $U_ i$ the normalization is an isomorphism, and we identify the corresponding open subset of $Y$ with $U_ i\times \mathbb C_ t$; we call it the dense open family. Its complement in $Y$ is the zero set of $g_ i$ composed with $\nu $, an analytic subset, and it is nowhere dense. Indeed, by theorem A.3.6 the map $Y\to C'$ is bimeromorphic: it restricts to a homeomorphism from the complement of a nowhere dense analytic subset of $Y$ onto the complement of a nowhere dense analytic subset of $C'$. The part of $C'$ over $U_ i$ is open and dense in $C'$, so its part in the second complement is dense there, and the inverse image of this part is dense in the first complement, hence in $Y$.

Step 3: finite generation over $C$. For an open set $W\subset C$ let $\mathcal F(W)$ be the ring of holomorphic functions on $\nu ^{-1}(W)$; holomorphic functions on $W$ act on it by multiplication after composition with $\nu $. We show that every point $w_0\in C$ has a neighborhood $W$ in $C$ with the following property: there are finitely many $s_1,\ldots ,s_ N\in \mathcal F(W)$ such that for every $w\in W$ and every holomorphic function $s$ on a neighborhood of $\nu ^{-1}(w)$ there are holomorphic functions $k_1,\ldots ,k_ N$ of $(z,a)$ on a neighborhood of $w$ in $U\times \mathbb C^ r$ with $s=\sum _ j(k_ j\circ \nu )s_ j$ near $\nu ^{-1}(w)$.

The tool is division by monic polynomials whose roots need not coincide. Let $\Phi (\xi ,u)=u^\mu +c_1(\xi )u^{\mu -1}+\cdots +c_\mu (\xi )$, with coefficients holomorphic on an open subset of a complex vector space, let $\xi _1$ be a point of this set, let $\Gamma $ be the union of small disjoint circles about the distinct roots of $\Phi (\xi _1,\cdot )$, and let $\Delta $ be the union of the open discs they bound. Choose a relatively compact neighborhood $A_1$ of $\xi _1$ such that $\Phi \neq 0$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{A_1}\times \Gamma $. Let $H$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{A_1}\times \overline\Delta $. The two integrals (A.2) of proposition A.1.1, with $f$, $P$ and $w$ replaced by $H$, $\Phi $ and $u$, and taken over $\Gamma $ instead of a single circle,

\begin{align*} Q(\xi ,u)& =\frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\int _\Gamma \frac{H(\xi ,\zeta )\, \mathrm d\zeta }{\Phi (\xi ,\zeta )(\zeta -u)},\\ R(\xi ,u)& =\frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\int _\Gamma \frac{H(\xi ,\zeta )}{\Phi (\xi ,\zeta )}\, \frac{\Phi (\xi ,\zeta )-\Phi (\xi ,u)}{\zeta -u}\, \mathrm d\zeta , \end{align*}

define, by differentiation under the integral sign, a holomorphic function $Q$ on $A_1\times \Delta $ and, since $(\Phi (\xi ,\zeta )-\Phi (\xi ,u))/(\zeta -u)$ is a polynomial in $u$ of degree less than $\mu $ whose coefficients are polynomials in $\zeta $ and the $c_ k(\xi )$, a polynomial $R=\sum _{k<\mu }r_ k(\xi )u^ k$ with coefficients holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{A_1}$. They satisfy $H=\Phi Q+R$ on $A_1\times \Delta $: for $u\in \Delta $, Cauchy's formula on the disc containing $u$ gives $H(\xi ,u)=\frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\int _\Gamma H(\xi ,\zeta )(\zeta -u)^{-1}\, \mathrm d\zeta $, since the circles that do not surround $u$ contribute nothing, and writing $1=\bigl (\Phi (\xi ,u)+(\Phi (\xi ,\zeta )-\Phi (\xi ,u))\bigr )/\Phi (\xi ,\zeta )$ in this integral gives $\Phi (\xi ,u)Q(\xi ,u)+R(\xi ,u)$. If $H$ depends holomorphically on further variables, so do $Q$ and the $r_ k$.

Applying this successively to variables $u_1,\ldots ,u_ m$ and monic polynomials $\Phi _1(\xi ,u_1),\ldots ,\Phi _ m(\xi ,u_ m)$, with the corresponding sets $\Delta _1,\ldots ,\Delta _ m$ and a common $A_1$, we find: a function holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline{A_1}\times \overline{\Delta _1}\times \cdots \times \overline{\Delta _ m}$ agrees, at the points of $A_1\times \Delta _1\times \cdots \times \Delta _ m$ where all $\Phi _ l(\xi ,u_ l)$ vanish, with a polynomial $\sum _\beta r_\beta (\xi )u^\beta $, $\beta _ l<\deg \Phi _ l$, whose coefficients are holomorphic on $A_1$.

Next we embed $Y$ locally by functions that are integral over the functions of $(z,a)$. Let $y\in Y$, let $y'$ be its image in $C'$, and let $w=\nu (y)$. By corollary A.3.7, applied to the analytic subset $C'$ of the domain $U\times \mathbb C^ r\times \mathbb C_ b$ and to the point $y'$, a neighborhood of $y$ in $Y$ is identified, compatibly with the maps to $C'$, with the closure of the graph of finitely many weakly holomorphic functions $f_1,\ldots ,f_ m$ over the regular part of the branch of $(C',y')$ that corresponds to $y$. This closure is an analytic subset of $\Omega '\times \mathbb C^ m$ for a neighborhood $\Omega '$ of $y'$ in $U\times \mathbb C^ r\times \mathbb C_ b$, embedded by the coordinates $(z,a,b)$ and by the coordinates $f_1,\ldots ,f_ m$ of $\mathbb C^ m$. Each $f_ j$ satisfies on it a monic equation whose coefficients are holomorphic functions of $(z,a,b)$ on $\Omega '$, and every holomorphic function on $Y$ near a point of this neighborhood of $y$ is the restriction of a holomorphic function of $(z,a,b,f_1,\ldots ,f_ m)$.

We first reduce the coefficients of the monic equations of the $f_ j$ to polynomials in $b$. Divide each of these coefficients, extended by zero near the roots of $b^ q-a_ ig_ i(z)^{q-1}$ different from the $b$-coordinate of $y'$, by this monic polynomial in $b$. Since the polynomial vanishes on $C'$, each coefficient agrees on $Y$ near $y$ with a polynomial in $b$ of degree less than $q$ whose coefficients are holomorphic functions of $(z,a)$ near $w$.

Let $\mathcal O_ w$ be the ring of germs at $w$ of holomorphic functions of $(z,a)$, acting through $\nu $. Reducing powers of $b$ by $b^ q=a_ ig_ i^{q-1}$ and the top power of $f_ j$ by its equation, we see that the $\mathcal O_ w$-module spanned by the germs at $y$ of the products $b^ kf_ j^ l$, with $k<q$ and $l$ less than the degree of the equation of $f_ j$, contains $1$ and is stable under multiplication by $f_ j$. Thus, if $v_1,\ldots ,v_ M$ are these products, then $f_ jv_\kappa =\sum _\lambda e_{\kappa \lambda }v_\lambda $ with $e_{\kappa \lambda }\in \mathcal O_ w$; in other words, the column vector $v=(v_\lambda )$ satisfies $(f_ jI-(e_{\kappa \lambda }))v=0$. Multiplying by the adjugate matrix of $f_ jI-(e_{\kappa \lambda })$, whose product with this matrix is $\det (f_ jI-(e_{\kappa \lambda }))\, I$ over every commutative ring, we find that $\det (f_ jI-(e_{\kappa \lambda }))$ annihilates every $v_\kappa $, in particular $1$. Expanding this determinant gives a monic equation for $f_ j$ with coefficients in $\mathcal O_ w$. Consequently, after shrinking the neighborhood of $y$, each coordinate of $u=(b,f_1,\ldots ,f_ m)$ satisfies a monic equation $\Phi _ l(z,a;u_ l)=0$ whose coefficients are holomorphic near $w$ in $U\times \mathbb C^ r$.

Now fix $w_0\in C$, and let $y_1,\ldots ,y_\ell $ be the points of the finite fiber $\nu ^{-1}(w_0)$. Choose embeddings as above on pairwise disjoint neighborhoods $N_1,\ldots ,N_\ell $ of these points, with coordinates $u^{(\iota )}$ on $N_\iota $ and monic equations $\Phi _{\iota ,l}$. Since $\nu $ is proper, there is a neighborhood $W$ of $w_0$ in $C$ with $\nu ^{-1}(W)\subset N_1\cup \cdots \cup N_\ell $, and we may assume that $W=C\cap A$ for an open set $A\subset U\times \mathbb C^ r$ on which the coefficients of all the $\Phi _{\iota ,l}$ are holomorphic. For each $\iota $ and each multi-index $\beta $ with $\beta _ l<\deg \Phi _{\iota ,l}$, let $s_{\iota ,\beta }\in \mathcal F(W)$ be the function equal to the monomial $(u^{(\iota )})^\beta $ on $N_\iota \cap \nu ^{-1}(W)$ and to zero on the other sets $N_{\iota '}\cap \nu ^{-1}(W)$.

We show that these functions have the required property. Let $w\in W$, let $s$ be holomorphic near $\nu ^{-1}(w)$, and fix $\iota $. The coordinates $u^{(\iota )}$ of the points of $\nu ^{-1}(w)\cap N_\iota $ are distinct points of the finite product of the sets of roots of the polynomials $\Phi _{\iota ,l}(w,\cdot )$, and near each of them $s$ is the restriction of a holomorphic function of $(z,a,u^{(\iota )})$. Let $H$ be the function, defined on a neighborhood of $\{ w\} $ times this finite product, that equals such an extension near the coordinates of the points of $\nu ^{-1}(w)\cap N_\iota $ and zero near the other points of the product. By the iterated division, $H$ agrees with a polynomial $\sum _\beta r_{\iota ,\beta }(z,a)(u^{(\iota )})^\beta $, with coefficients holomorphic near $w$, on the common zero set of the $\Phi _{\iota ,l}$ near these points. This zero set contains $Y\cap N_\iota $ near $\nu ^{-1}(w)\cap N_\iota $, so $s=\sum _\beta (r_{\iota ,\beta }\circ \nu )s_{\iota ,\beta }$ there. Summing over $\iota $, we obtain the claim with the finitely many functions $s_{\iota ,\beta }$.

Step 4: homogeneous components. For $x'\in U$ and $\varepsilon >0$ with $\{ |z-x'|\leq \varepsilon \} \subset U$ put

\[ W_\varepsilon (x')=\{ (z,a)\in C:|z-x'|<\varepsilon ,\ |a_ l|<\varepsilon \text{ for }l=1,\ldots ,r\} . \]

These sets form a basis of neighborhoods of $(x',0)$ in $C$, and since $C$ is closed in $U\times \mathbb C^ r$, the closure of $W_{\varepsilon '}(x')$ is a compact subset of $W_\varepsilon (x')$ for $\varepsilon '<\varepsilon $. The part $G$ of $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x'))$ in the dense open family consists of the points $(z,t)$ with $z\in U_ i$, $|z-x'|<\varepsilon $ and $|t^ qg_ l(z)|<\varepsilon $ for all $l$. For fixed $z$ these $t$ form a disc about $0$, and $G$ is invariant under the rotations $t\mapsto \mathrm e^{\mathrm i\vartheta }t$. Let $s\in \mathcal F(W_\varepsilon (x'))$. On $G$ we expand $s(z,t)=\sum _{e\geq 0}c_ e(z)t^ e$, where $c_ e(z)=\partial _ t^ es(z,0)/e!$ is holomorphic on $U_ i\cap \{ |z-x'|<\varepsilon \} $, and for an integer $d$ we define

\[ (P_ ds)(z,t)=\frac1{2\pi }\int _0^{2\pi } \mathrm e^{-\mathrm id\vartheta } s(z,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\vartheta }t)\, \mathrm d\vartheta =\begin{cases} c_ d(z)t^ d,& d\geq 0,\\ 0,& d<0,\end{cases} \]

integrating the power series term by term. For $\varepsilon '<\varepsilon $ the circle through a point of $G\cap \nu ^{-1}(W_{\varepsilon '}(x'))$ stays in this set, so $|P_ ds|$ is bounded there by the maximum of $|s|$ on the compact set $\nu ^{-1}(\overline{W_{\varepsilon '}(x')})$. Since every point of $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x'))$ lies in such a set $\nu ^{-1}(W_{\varepsilon '}(x'))$, the holomorphic function $P_ ds$ on $G$ is locally bounded near every point of $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x'))$. Since $Y$ is normal and the complement of $G$ in $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x'))$ is a nowhere dense analytic subset, lemma A.3.4, applied on each of the open sets $\nu ^{-1}(W_{\varepsilon '}(x'))$, extends $P_ ds$ holomorphically to $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x'))$; the extensions on these sets agree on their overlaps by density of $G$. We denote the extension again by $P_ ds$.

We say that $s$ is homogeneous of weight $d$ if $s(z,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\vartheta }t)=\mathrm e^{\mathrm id\vartheta }s(z,t)$ on $G$, that is, comparing the power series in $t$, if $c_ e=0$ for every $e\neq d$; then $P_ ds=s$ on $G$, hence on $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x'))$ by density of $G$. In particular, $P_ ds$ is homogeneous of weight $d$, a function homogeneous of negative weight vanishes, and since $a=t^ qg(z)$ on $G$, the function $a^\alpha $ is homogeneous of weight $q|\alpha |$. The operators $P_ d$ commute with restriction to smaller sets $W_{\varepsilon _1}(x'')\subset W_\varepsilon (x')$.

Let $k$ be the restriction to $W_\varepsilon (x')$ of a holomorphic function $K$ on $\{ |z-x'|<\varepsilon \} \times \{ |a_ l|<\varepsilon \text{ for all }l\} $, with Taylor expansion $K(z,a)=\sum _\alpha K_\alpha (z)a^\alpha $ in $a$. At a point of $G$ we have $k(\nu (z,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\vartheta }t))=\sum _\alpha K_\alpha (z)\mathrm e^{\mathrm iq|\alpha |\vartheta }a^\alpha $ with $a=t^ qg(z)$, and $\sum _\alpha |K_\alpha (z)a^\alpha |<\infty $, while $s$ is bounded on the circle. Integrating term by term, we obtain, first on $G$ and then on $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x'))$ by density,

\[ P_ p(ks)=\sum _{q|\alpha |\leq p}K_\alpha (z)\, a^\alpha \, P_{p-q|\alpha |}s \]

for every integer $p$, where $K_\alpha (z)$ and $a^\alpha $ are composed with $\nu $; the terms with $q|\alpha |>p$ vanish because $P_ es=0$ for $e<0$.

Step 5: functions of weight $p$. Let $W$ and $s_1,\ldots ,s_ N$ be as in Step 3 for $w_0=(x,0)$, and choose $\varepsilon >0$ with $\{ |z-x|\leq \varepsilon \} \subset U$ and $W_\varepsilon (x)\subset W$. Let $|x'-x|<\varepsilon /2$, let $0<\varepsilon _1\leq \varepsilon /2$, and let $h\in \mathcal F(W_{\varepsilon _1}(x'))$ be homogeneous of weight $p$. Then $W_{\varepsilon _1}(x')\subset W_\varepsilon (x)$. By Step 3, $h=\sum _ j(k_ j\circ \nu )s_ j$ near $\nu ^{-1}(x',0)$, with $k_ j$ holomorphic near $(x',0)$ in $U\times \mathbb C^ r$. Since $\nu $ is proper, after shrinking $\varepsilon _1$ this identity holds on $\nu ^{-1}(W_{\varepsilon _1}(x'))$, and each $k_ j$ is holomorphic on $\{ |z-x'|<\varepsilon _1\} \times \{ |a_ l|<\varepsilon _1\text{ for all }l\} $. We apply $P_ p$ to the identity. Since $P_ ph=h$, Step 4 gives

\[ h=\sum _{j=1}^ N\sum _{q|\alpha |\leq p}k_{j,\alpha }(z)\, a^\alpha \, P_{p-q|\alpha |}s_ j \qquad \text{on }\nu ^{-1}(W_{\varepsilon _1}(x')), \]

where the $k_{j,\alpha }$ are the Taylor coefficients of $k_ j$ in $a$. Thus every function of weight $p$ near $\nu ^{-1}(x',0)$ is a combination, with coefficients holomorphic in $z$ near $x'$, of the finitely many functions

\[ a^\alpha P_ es_ j,\qquad 1\leq j\leq N,\quad e\geq 0,\quad q|\alpha |+e=p, \]

which are holomorphic on $\nu ^{-1}(W_\varepsilon (x))$ and homogeneous of weight $p$.

Step 6: identification with $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$. By Step 4, each function $a^\alpha P_ es_ j$ of Step 5 equals $f_{j,\alpha }(z)t^ p$ on the dense open family, with $f_{j,\alpha }$ holomorphic on $U_ i\cap \{ |z-x|<\varepsilon \} $. Let $M$ be the maximum of the moduli of these finitely many functions on the compact set $\nu ^{-1}(L)$, where $L=\{ (z,a)\in C:|z-x|\leq \varepsilon /2,\ |a_ l|\leq \varepsilon /2\text{ for all }l\} \subset W_\varepsilon (x)$. For $z$ with $|z-x|<\varepsilon /2$ and $g_ i(z)\neq 0$, choose $t$ with

\[ |t|^ q|g(z)|=\varepsilon /2. \]

Then $|t^ qg_ l(z)|\leq \varepsilon /2$ for all $l$, so $(z,t)\in \nu ^{-1}(L)$, and $|f_{j,\alpha }(z)||t|^ p\leq M$, that is,

\[ |f_{j,\alpha }(z)|\leq M(\varepsilon /2)^{-p/q}|g(z)|^{p/q}. \]

In particular $f_{j,\alpha }$ is bounded near $\{ g_ i=0\} $, so it extends holomorphically to the ball $\{ |z-x|<\varepsilon /2\} $ by lemma A.1.7, and the inequality persists on $\{ g_ i=0\} $, since both sides are continuous and $U_ i$ is dense. Hence the finitely many functions $f_{j,\alpha }$ belong to $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ on this ball.

Conversely, let $f$ be a germ of $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ at a point $x'$ with $|x'-x|<\varepsilon /2$, say $|f|\leq C_ f|g|^{p/q}$ on $\{ |z-x'|<\rho \} $, and let $0<\varepsilon _1\leq \min (\rho ,\varepsilon /2)$. On the part of $\nu ^{-1}(W_{\varepsilon _1}(x'))$ in the dense open family, the function $f(z)t^ p$ satisfies

\[ |f(z)t^ p|\leq C_ f|t^ qg(z)|^{p/q}=C_ f|a|^{p/q}. \]

It is therefore bounded, and since $Y$ is normal it extends holomorphically to $\nu ^{-1}(W_{\varepsilon _1}(x'))$ by lemma A.3.4; the extension $h$ is homogeneous of weight $p$. By Step 5, after shrinking $\varepsilon _1$, $h$ is a combination of the functions $a^\alpha P_ es_ j$ with coefficients $k_{j,\alpha }(z)$ holomorphic near $x'$. Comparing the coefficients of $t^ p$ on the dense open family gives $f=\sum k_{j,\alpha }f_{j,\alpha }$ on $U_ i$ near $x'$, hence near $x'$, since both sides are continuous and $U_ i$ is dense. Thus the functions $f_{j,\alpha }$ generate $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ on $\{ |z-x|<\varepsilon /2\} $, and $\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ is coherent by theorem A.1.5, lemma A.1.4.

Finally, if $v=(p/q)\log \sum _ j|g_ j|^2+O(1)$ is a local presentation of $v$, then $\mathcal I_\infty (v)=\mathcal J_{p/q}(g)$ on its domain. Since coherence is a local property, $\mathcal I_\infty (v)$ is coherent. (If $g(x)\neq 0$, both ideals are the unit ideal near $x$; in dimension zero every finite weight gives the unit ideal.) $\square$

Corollary 13.6.3. Let $v$ be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities on a compact complex manifold $X$. For every $\varepsilon >0$ there is $k_0>0$ such that for every real $k\geq k_0$

\[ \mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon )v)\subseteq \mathcal I_\infty (kv). \]

Proof. Apply lemma 13.5.1 with $\eta =\varepsilon /(1+\varepsilon )$ and $t=k(1+\varepsilon )$, so that $t(1-\eta )=k$. The lemma provides one threshold $t_0$ for all germs on $X$; put $k_0=t_0/(1+\varepsilon )$. For $k\geq k_0$, every germ $f\in \mathcal I(k(1+\varepsilon )v)$ satisfies $\log |f|^2\leq kv+O(1)$ near its base point, that is, $f\in \mathcal I_\infty (kv)$. In dimension zero all these ideals are the unit ideal. $\square$
