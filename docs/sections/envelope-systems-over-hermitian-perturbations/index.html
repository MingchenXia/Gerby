13.5 Envelope systems over Hermitian perturbations

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

25 Envelope systems over Hermitian perturbations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and fix real numbers $a<c$.

For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the multiplier-ideal envelope $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ of definition 16.2.1 is the regularized supremum of the nonpositive $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions with the multiplier ideals of $u$. It depends on the background $\theta $. When $\theta $ is perturbed by Hermitian forms, the envelopes of one multiplier-ideal class in the perturbed backgrounds form a compatible family, and as the perturbation tends to zero these normalized representatives decrease to a potential in the original background. Projecting the limit back to the perturbed backgrounds recovers the family exactly when the limit keeps the multiplier ideals of the family; otherwise it gives the greatest family below it that comes from the original background.

We apply this to test curves that are constant for parameters $\tau \leq a$ and have cutoff $c$. Recovery can fail even in a Kähler background: on a ruled surface, at zero volume, the limit acquires an additional divisorial pole. On the other hand, for big backgrounds on manifolds with the bounded mass property, recovery holds by theorem 30.5.6 when the lower volumes $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}$ of the initial slices (definition 13.3.1) have a positive limit. On the Hopf threefold, scalar invariance along the elliptic fibers instead allows recovery from positive lower volume on the projective base, by theorem 25.6.3, even though ambient upper mass is unbounded. Smooth changes of the background commute with recovery, whereas the limiting Monge–Ampère mass can change when the background is not closed.

We also use the ordinary singularity envelope $P_\theta [u]$ of definition 1.19.4, the ordinary and multiplier-ideal singularity orders of section 1.12, and the extended Monge–Ampère measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}$ of theorem 6.1.2. A potential $u$ with $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}=u$ is called $P_\theta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed.

25.1 Curves constant on a left half-line

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We restrict the envelope test curves of definition 18.1.1 by requiring that each slice be fixed by the multiplier-ideal envelope and that the curve be constant for $\tau \leq a$. Constancy identifies the initial potential explicitly as $\Gamma _ a$, and it confines the associated ray between the translates of this potential by $at$ and by $ct$.

Definition 25.1.1. Let $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$ be the set of families $\Gamma =(\Gamma _\tau )_{\tau <c}$ such that

  1. $\Gamma _\tau \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $P_\theta [\Gamma _\tau ]_{\mathcal I}=\Gamma _\tau $ for every $\tau <c$;

  2. the family is pointwise concave and decreasing in $\tau $;

  3. $\Gamma _\tau =\Gamma _ a$ for $\tau \leq a$.

The cutoff of $\Gamma $ is $c$, and we extend $\Gamma $ by $\Gamma _ c=\lim _{\tau \uparrow c}\Gamma _\tau $ and $\Gamma _\tau =-\infty $ for $\tau >c$. Such curves are ordered by the pointwise order of their slices $\Gamma _\tau $, $\tau <c$.

Every slice has supremum zero by theorem 16.2.2. Hence, by theorem 1.4.2, corollary 1.4.3, the decreasing limit $\Gamma _ c$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$; in particular, it is not identically $-\infty $. It is also $P_\theta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed. Indeed, $\Gamma _ c\leq 0$ is a competitor for its own envelope, and $\Gamma _ c\leq \Gamma _\tau $, so the order preservation of proposition 16.2.6 gives

\[ \Gamma _ c\leq P_\theta [\Gamma _ c]_{\mathcal I}\leq \Gamma _\tau \quad (\tau <c). \]

Taking the infimum over $\tau $ proves equality.

Proposition 25.1.2. A curve $\Gamma \in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$ is an envelope test curve with initial potential $\varphi =\Gamma _ a$. Its Legendre transform

\[ U_ t=\Bigl (\sup _{\tau <c}(\Gamma _\tau +t\tau )\Bigr )^*\qquad (t>0) \]

is an envelope ray with initial potential $\varphi $ and slope $c$, in the sense of definition 18.1.2, and satisfies

\[ \varphi +at\leq U_ t\leq \varphi +ct\qquad (t>0). \]

In particular $U_ t\sim \varphi $ for every $t>0$.

Proof. By corollary 16.2.4, a $P_\theta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed potential is also fixed by the ordinary envelope $P_\theta $. The regularized supremum of the curve is $\Gamma _ a$, since the curve is decreasing and constant for $\tau \leq a$. Hence all the conditions of definition 18.1.1 hold, and theorem 18.2.1 applies. The term $\tau =a$ in the transform gives the lower bound, and the inequalities $\Gamma _\tau \leq \varphi $ for $\tau <c$ give the upper bound. $\square$

25.2 Transition maps and envelope systems

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Curves over different backgrounds are related by projecting each slice to the ideal envelope in the larger background. For these projections to form a consistent system, projecting in two steps must agree with projecting directly. This follows because each projection preserves multiplier ideals, while in each background a multiplier-ideal class has a unique representative fixed by the ideal envelope. The same fact gives injectivity: equal projected slices have equal multiplier ideals, hence come from equal fixed representatives.

Proposition 25.2.1. Let $\beta \geq \theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Slicewise projection, $\Gamma \mapsto (P_\beta [\Gamma _\tau ]_{\mathcal I})_{\tau <c}$, defines an injective and order-preserving map

\[ P_\beta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}:\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )\longrightarrow \mathscr T_{a,c}(\beta ). \]

For every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta '$ with $\beta \geq \beta '\geq \theta $, these maps satisfy

\[ P_\beta \bigl [P_{\beta '}[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal I}\bigr ]_{\mathcal I}=P_\beta [\Gamma ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

Proof. Since $\beta \geq \theta $, every slice is $\beta $-plurisubharmonic, and by theorem 16.2.2 its projection is a $\beta $-plurisubharmonic function fixed by $P_\beta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$. Decreasingness, constancy for $\tau \leq a$, and order preservation follow from proposition 16.2.6.

For concavity, let $r,t<c$ and $0<s<1$, and put $\tau =(1-s)r+s t$. By the same proposition and the concavity of the original curve,

\[ \begin{split} (1-s)P_\beta [\Gamma _ r]_{\mathcal I}+sP_\beta [\Gamma _ t]_{\mathcal I} & \leq P_\beta [(1-s)\Gamma _ r+s\Gamma _ t]_{\mathcal I}\\ & \leq P_\beta [\Gamma _\tau ]_{\mathcal I}. \end{split} \]

Here the first inequality follows by combining the scaling and addition formulas of that proposition, with backgrounds $(1-s)\beta $ and $s\beta $.

Each projection preserves the multiplier ideals at every weight. Hence the two sides of the transition identity are $P_\beta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed representatives of the same multiplier-ideal class, and they are equal. If two projected curves agree, their original slices have the same multiplier ideals; since those slices are $P_\theta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed, they agree as well. This proves injectivity. $\square$

Let $\mathscr H(X)$ denote the set of Hermitian forms on $X$; for $\eta \in \mathscr H(X)$ we also write $\eta >0$.

Definition 25.2.2. An envelope system over $\theta $, with parameters $a<c$, is a family

\[ \boldsymbol \Gamma =(\Gamma ^\eta )_{\eta \in \mathscr H(X)}, \qquad \Gamma ^\eta \in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta +\eta ), \]

such that

25.3
\begin{equation} \Gamma ^\eta =P_{\theta +\eta }[\Gamma ^{\eta '}]_{\mathcal I} \quad \hbox{whenever }\eta \geq \eta '. \end{equation}

Write $\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta )$ for the set of these systems, ordered componentwise. For $\Gamma \in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$, set

\[ \mathsf E(\Gamma )^\eta =P_{\theta +\eta }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal I} \qquad (\eta \in \mathscr H(X)). \]

A system is realized in the background $\theta $ if it equals $\mathsf E(\Gamma )$ for some $\Gamma \in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$.

By the transition proposition, $\mathsf E(\Gamma )$ is an envelope system, and the map $\mathsf E$ is injective. All components of an envelope system have the same multiplier ideals at each $\tau <c$: compare two components $\Gamma ^\eta $ and $\Gamma ^{\eta '}$ with the component indexed by $\eta +\eta '$.

If $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the constant family $\Gamma _\tau =P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$, $\tau <c$, with cutoff $c$, lies in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$, and its projections form the envelope system $\mathsf E(\Gamma )$.

25.3 The limiting curve and recovery

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Every Hermitian form dominates all sufficiently small multiples of a fixed Hermitian form. A single decreasing sequence of components therefore determines the limit of the whole system. Projecting the resulting curve back gives the greatest realized system below the original one, and strict inequality means that recovery fails.

Theorem 25.3.1. Fix a Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, and let $j$ range over the positive integers. For every $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta )$ and every $\tau <c$, the sequence $\Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau $ decreases, and its limit

\[ (\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau =\lim _{j\to \infty }\Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau \]

is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, in particular not identically $-\infty $. These limits define an element $\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma $ of $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$, which does not depend on $h$. The maps $\mathsf E$ and $\mathsf R$ satisfy

\[ \mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}, \qquad \mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol \Gamma ) \leq \boldsymbol \Gamma . \]

Both maps preserve order. More precisely,

25.4
\begin{equation} \mathsf E(\Gamma )\leq \boldsymbol \Gamma \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \Gamma \leq \mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma . \end{equation}

Thus $\mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol \Gamma )$ is the greatest system below $\boldsymbol \Gamma $ that is realized in the original background.

Proof. Step 1: the limit. Fix $\tau <c$ and put $q_ j=\Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau $. By compatibility, $q_ j=P_{\theta +h/j}[q_{j+1}]_{\mathcal I}$, and $q_{j+1}\leq 0$ is a competitor for this envelope; hence $q_ j\geq q_{j+1}$. The functions $q_ j$ have supremum zero and a common smooth lower curvature bound. By theorem 1.4.2, corollary 1.4.3, their decreasing limit $q$ is not identically $-\infty $ and has supremum zero. Passing to the limit in the distributional inequalities

\[ \theta +h/j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_ j\geq 0 \]

gives $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

The defining family of an ideal envelope grows when the background increases. Together with $q\leq q_ j$ and order preservation, this gives

\[ q\leq P_\theta [q]_{\mathcal I} \leq P_{\theta +h/j}[q]_{\mathcal I} \leq P_{\theta +h/j}[q_ j]_{\mathcal I}=q_ j. \]

Taking the infimum over $j$ proves $P_\theta [q]_{\mathcal I}=q$. Concavity, decreasingness and constancy on the left half-line pass to the pointwise limits, so these limits form a curve in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$.

Step 2: independence of $h$. For every $\eta >0$ there is $j$ with $h/j\leq \eta $, and compatibility then gives $\Gamma ^\eta _\tau \geq \Gamma ^{h/j}_\tau $. Consequently

\[ (\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau =\inf _{\eta \in \mathscr H(X)}\Gamma ^\eta _\tau , \]

which proves that $\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma $ does not depend on $h$. This formula also shows that $\mathsf R$ preserves order.

Step 3: the two compositions. Write $q=(\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau $. Since $q\leq \Gamma ^\eta _\tau $ and $\Gamma ^\eta _\tau $ is fixed by its envelope, order preservation gives $P_{\theta +\eta }[q]_{\mathcal I}\leq \Gamma ^\eta _\tau $. This proves $\mathsf E\mathsf R\leq \mathrm{id}$.

Now start with a curve $\Gamma \in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$. Its slice $\Gamma _\tau $ is a competitor in every larger background, so

\[ \Gamma _\tau \leq (\mathsf R\mathsf E\Gamma )_\tau \leq P_{\theta +\eta }[\Gamma _\tau ]_{\mathcal I} \quad (\eta >0). \]

The two outer terms have the same multiplier ideals, so the two ideal inclusions given by these inequalities show that the middle term has those ideals as well. Since both the middle term and $\Gamma _\tau $ are $P_\theta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed, they are equal. This proves $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$.

Step 4: the adjunction. By the transition proposition, $\mathsf E$ preserves order. If $\mathsf E(\Gamma )\leq \boldsymbol \Gamma $, applying $\mathsf R$ gives $\Gamma =\mathsf R\mathsf E(\Gamma )\leq \mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma $. Conversely, if $\Gamma \leq \mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma $, applying $\mathsf E$ and using $\mathsf E\mathsf R\leq \mathrm{id}$ gives $\mathsf E(\Gamma )\leq \boldsymbol \Gamma $. This proves (25.4), and the final maximality assertion follows from it. $\square$

Proposition 25.3.2. A system $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta )$ is realized in $\theta $ if and only if, for every $\tau <c$, its limiting slice $(\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau $ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to one, and hence every, slice $\Gamma ^\eta _\tau $.

Proof. For a realized system, this follows from $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$ and the preservation of multiplier ideals by each projection. Conversely, the assumed equivalence and the fact that $\Gamma ^\eta _\tau $ is fixed by its envelope in the larger background give

\[ P_{\theta +\eta }[(\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau ]_{\mathcal I} =\Gamma ^\eta _\tau . \]

Thus the system equals $\mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol \Gamma )$. $\square$

By theorem 1.16.3, multiplier-ideal equivalence means equality of all Lelong numbers on all local modifications. Recovery therefore holds exactly when the limiting slices keep these Lelong numbers. They are the same for all components of a system, whereas the limiting slice can have larger values: in the ruled-surface example of section 25.5, one of them increases from zero to one. For big backgrounds on manifolds with the bounded mass property, theorem 30.5.6 excludes such an increase when the initial slices have positive limiting lower volume.

When $X$ is Kähler, the Kähler forms are cofinal among the Hermitian forms: a sufficiently small positive multiple of a fixed Kähler form lies below any given Hermitian form. The compatibility condition therefore determines a system by its components indexed by Kähler forms. Conversely, every compatible family indexed by Kähler forms extends uniquely to a system: for $\eta >0$, choose a Kähler form $\kappa \leq \eta $ and set $\Gamma ^\eta =P_{\theta +\eta }[\Gamma ^\kappa ]_{\mathcal I}$. Two choices of $\kappa $ give the same result, as one sees by passing to a common smaller Kähler form and using the transition identity.

25.4 Changes of representative and limiting mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Subtracting a smooth function transports a curve to a new background, and projection restores the envelope normalization. The gauge estimate is uniform in the perturbation, so this transport also respects the limiting curve. The limiting Monge–Ampère mass behaves differently: in a nonclosed background, it can change even for the system all of whose slices are zero.

Proposition 25.4.1. Let $f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$ and $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$. For $\Gamma \in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )$ and $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta )$, the formulas

\[ (\mathsf G_ f\Gamma )_\tau =P_{\theta '}[\Gamma _\tau -f]_{\mathcal I}, \qquad (\mathsf G_ f\boldsymbol \Gamma )^\eta _\tau =P_{\theta '+\eta }[\Gamma ^\eta _\tau -f]_{\mathcal I} \]

define bijections $\mathsf G_ f:\mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta )\to \mathscr T_{a,c}(\theta ')$ and $\mathsf G_ f:\mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta )\to \mathscr S_{a,c}(\theta ')$. They satisfy the cocycle law $\mathsf G_ g\mathsf G_ f=\mathsf G_{f+g}$, where $\mathsf G_ g$ is formed in the background $\theta '$, and they commute with $\mathsf E$ and $\mathsf R$.

Proof. Subtracting $f$ changes the background from $\theta $ to $\theta '$ and preserves concavity, decreasingness and constancy on the left half-line. Slicewise projection preserves these properties by the argument of proposition 25.2.1. Since $f$ is smooth, every new slice has the multiplier ideals of the original one. Applying the construction with $-f$ therefore gives a $P_\theta [\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed slice with the same ideals as the original slice, hence the original slice itself. This proves that $\mathsf G_{-f}$ is the inverse of $\mathsf G_ f$; the same uniqueness of fixed representatives proves the cocycle law.

Compatibility of the transformed system and commutation with $\mathsf E$ follow by comparing multiplier ideals in the common final background. For commutation with $\mathsf R$, the gauge estimate of proposition 16.3.1, applied in each background $\theta +\eta $, gives

\[ \Gamma ^\eta _\tau +\min _ Xf \leq (\mathsf G_ f\boldsymbol \Gamma )^\eta _\tau +f \leq \Gamma ^\eta _\tau +\max _ Xf, \]

with constants independent of $\eta $. Pass to the decreasing limits along a cofinal sequence. The new limit has the same ordinary singularity type as the old limit minus $f$, and hence the same multiplier ideals. It is $P_{\theta '}[\cdot ]_{\mathcal I}$-fixed by theorem 25.3.1, so it equals $P_{\theta '}[(\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau -f]_{\mathcal I}$. This proves commutation with $\mathsf R$. $\square$

For closed backgrounds on a compact Kähler manifold, [chapter “Non-Archimedean pluripotential theory”, section “The definition of non-Archimedean metrics”, SGPT] indexes such systems by Kähler perturbations, as above, and defines their volume as a limit of the masses of the initial potentials. Its components are general test curves fixed by the ideal envelope, with initial potentials of positive mass, whereas our curves are constant on a prescribed left half-line and our system spaces impose no mass condition. For nonclosed positive backgrounds, the corresponding limit of masses is not invariant under the gauge bijections.

Proposition 25.4.2. There are a compact complex surface $X$, Hermitian forms $\omega ,h$ on $X$, and a smooth real function $f$ with $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f>0$, with the following property. For every $a<c$ there is a system $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\omega )$ such that the limits

\[ \lim _{\epsilon \downarrow 0} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\omega +\epsilon h} (\Gamma ^{\epsilon h}_ a), \qquad \lim _{\epsilon \downarrow 0} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\omega '+\epsilon h} ((\mathsf G_ f\boldsymbol \Gamma )^{\epsilon h}_ a) \]

both exist and are different.

Proof. Take the torus $X$ and the smooth perturbation of proposition 16.3.2, so that $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ is Hermitian and $\int _ X(\omega ')^2\neq \int _ X\omega ^2$, and fix any Hermitian form $h$. In every background $\omega +\eta $, let every slice with $\tau <c$ be zero. This is an envelope system for every $a<c$, since zero is the multiplier-ideal envelope of the unit-ideal class in a positive background. Its first limit is

\[ \lim _{\epsilon \downarrow 0}\int _ X(\omega +\epsilon h)^2 =\int _ X\omega ^2. \]

The gauge bijection sends every slice to $P_{\omega '+\eta }[-f]_{\mathcal I}=0$, since $f$ is smooth and the new background is positive. The second limit is therefore $\int _ X(\omega ')^2$, which is different. $\square$

25.5 Failure of recovery in a positive background

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We construct a Kähler background on a ruled surface over an elliptic curve and an envelope system that is not realized in this background. The obstruction is a section $D$ of the ruling whose class is nef but contains only one closed positive current, namely $[D]$. Prescribing a logarithmic pole along one fiber in the Kähler background leaves the class of $D$ as the residual class. In every positive perturbation of the background, this class has smooth positive representatives, so the perturbed envelopes have only the fiber pole. In the limit, however, the only positive representative left is $[D]$, so the limiting envelope acquires $D$ as an additional pole, and the residual volume tends to zero.

In this section, $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$, and $X$ is the compact ruled surface

\[ X=(\mathbb C_ z\times \mathbb P^1_ w)/\sim , \qquad (z,w)\sim (z+1,w),\qquad (z,w)\sim (z+\mathrm i,w+1). \]

These commuting transformations define a holomorphic $\mathbb P^1$-bundle $\pi :X\to E$. Let $D$ be its section $w=\infty $. The function $v=w-\operatorname {Im}z$ is invariant under both transformations and descends to a smooth function on $X\setminus D$.

Lemma 25.5.1. Let $\sigma _0=(\mathrm i/2)\, \mathrm{d}z\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z$ be the flat probability form on $E$. There is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta \in c_1(\mathcal O_ X(D))$ whose class is nef and for which

\[ \kappa =\theta +\pi ^*\sigma _0 \]

is a Kähler form. The only closed positive current of the form $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is $[D]$.

Proof. Let $s_ D$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal O_ X(D)$, with zero divisor $D$. For a real parameter $t>0$, define a smooth Hermitian metric on this line bundle by prescribing the norm of $s_ D$:

\[ |s_ D|_ t^2=(1+t|v|^2)^{-1}\quad \hbox{on }X\setminus D. \]

To check that it extends smoothly, use the coordinate $\xi =1/w$ near $D$. The squared norm of a local nonvanishing frame is

\[ \bigl (|\xi |^2+t|1-(\operatorname {Im}z)\xi |^2\bigr )^{-1}, \]

which is smooth and positive. The prescribed norm is invariant under the transformations on the complement of $D$, so these local extensions define a global metric. Its curvature form is

\[ \theta _ t=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+t|v|^2) \quad \hbox{on }X\setminus D. \]

Set $\theta =\theta _1$, and let $\sigma =\sigma _0/\pi $ on $E$, where the denominator is the number $\pi $.

We now estimate the curvature from below. Put $r=|v|^2$ and let $H$ be the complex Hessian of $\log (1+tr)$ in the coordinates $(z,w)$. Direct differentiation gives

\[ \begin{split} H_{w\bar w}& =\frac{t}{(1+tr)^2},\\ H_{z\bar w}& =\frac{\mathrm i t(1-tv^2)}{2(1+tr)^2},\\ H_{z\bar z}& =\frac{t}{2(1+tr)} -\frac{t^2(\operatorname {Re}v)^2}{(1+tr)^2}. \end{split} \]

Consequently

\[ H_{z\bar z}-\frac{|H_{z\bar w}|^2}{H_{w\bar w}} =\frac{t(1-tr)}{4(1+tr)}\geq -\frac t4. \]

Completing the square gives $\theta _ t\geq -(t/4)\pi ^*\sigma $ on $X\setminus D$, and by continuity this inequality extends across $D$. The restriction of $\theta _ t$ to every fiber is a positive Fubini–Study form, including at infinity. Thus $\theta _ t+A\pi ^*\sigma $ is strictly positive off $D$ whenever $A>t/4$. At $\xi =0$, its coefficient matrix, with the common factor $\mathrm i/(2\pi )$ omitted, is

\[ \begin{pmatrix} A & \mathrm i/2 \\ -\mathrm i/2 & 1/t \end{pmatrix}. \]

Its vertical coefficient is positive and its Schur complement is $A-t/4>0$, so the form is strictly positive along $D$ as well. In particular, $\kappa =\theta +\pi ^*\sigma _0$ is Kähler.

The function

\[ f_ t=\log \frac{1+t|v|^2}{1+|v|^2} \]

extends smoothly across $D$ and satisfies $\theta _ t=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ t$. Letting $t\downarrow 0$ in the curvature lower bound shows that the class of $\theta $ is nef in the sense of section 23.6.

Write $\psi _ D=\log |s_ D|_1^2$. By the Poincaré–Lelong formula, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ D=[D]$. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then $u-\psi _ D$ is plurisubharmonic on $X\setminus D$. Its lift $\Phi (z,w)$ to $\mathbb C^2$ is invariant under the two deck transformations and, since $u$ is bounded above on the compact surface $X$, satisfies

\[ \Phi (z,w)\leq C+\log \bigl (1+|w-\operatorname {Im}z|^2\bigr ) \]

for some real constant $C$. For each fixed $w$, the function $z\mapsto \Phi (z,w)$ is either identically $-\infty $ or subharmonic. It is $1$-periodic and has logarithmic upper growth, so lemma 1.3.6 makes it constant. Thus $\Phi (z,w)=\phi (w)$, where $\phi $ is a subharmonic function of $w$, not identically $-\infty $. The second deck transformation gives $\phi (w+1)=\phi (w)$, and the bound at $z=0$ gives logarithmic growth, so the same lemma makes $\phi $ constant. Therefore $u=\psi _ D+C$ off $D$, and lemma 1.3.3 extends this equality across $D$. Hence $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=[D]$. $\square$

Fix a point $p\in E$ and let $F=\pi ^{-1}(p)$. The Green-kernel construction in the proof of theorem 19.3.1 gives a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $g$ on $E$ with $\sigma _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=\delta _ p$. Its pullback $\psi _ F=g\circ \pi $ has a logarithmic pole of coefficient one along $F$, and

\[ \pi ^*\sigma _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ F=[F]. \]

Theorem 25.5.2. For the Kähler background $\kappa $ of lemma 25.5.1 and every $a<c$, there is a system $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\kappa )$ for which

\[ \mathsf E\mathsf R(\boldsymbol \Gamma ) \neq \boldsymbol \Gamma . \]

Every slice $\Gamma ^\eta _\tau $, $\tau <c$, of every component has multiplier ideals

\[ \mathcal I(t\Gamma ^\eta _\tau ) =\mathcal O_ X(-\lfloor t\rfloor F)\qquad (t>0), \]

whereas its limiting slices have multiplier ideals

\[ \mathcal I\bigl (t(\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )_\tau \bigr ) =\mathcal O_ X(-\lfloor t\rfloor (F+D)). \]

In particular, on the system space $\mathscr S_{a,c}(\kappa )$, which carries no mass condition, $\mathsf E$ is not surjective and $\mathsf R$ is not injective.

Proof. For each Hermitian form $\eta $, the nef approximation of lemma 25.5.1 gives a smooth function $f_\eta $ such that $\theta +\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_\eta \geq 0$. Then

\[ u_\eta =\psi _ F+f_\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa +\eta ). \]

Let every slice $\Gamma ^\eta _\tau $, $\tau <c$, of the component $\Gamma ^\eta $ be $q_\eta =P_{\kappa +\eta }[u_\eta ]_{\mathcal I}$, with cutoff $c$. The multiplier ideals of $q_\eta $ are those of $\psi _ F$ and do not depend on the smooth function $f_\eta $. Hence the compatibility condition follows by comparing these ideals in the larger background, and the constant curves form a system with the required parameters.

Let $q$ be its limiting slice under $\mathsf R$, and let $\nu (q,F)$ be its generic Lelong number along $F$. Each $q_\eta $ has generic Lelong number one along $F$, so lemma 1.17.6, applied to a decreasing cofinal sequence, gives $\nu (q,F)\geq 1$. By lemma 1.17.1,

\[ \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q-[F]\geq 0. \]

By the Poincaré–Lelong identity for $\psi _ F$, this current equals $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(q-\psi _ F)$, where the difference $q-\psi _ F$ is first taken as a locally integrable function. Since the current is positive, this difference has a canonical $\theta $-plurisubharmonic representative, and by lemma 25.5.1 it differs from $\psi _ D$ by a constant. Hence

\[ q=\psi _ F+\psi _ D+C. \]

The divisors $F$ and $D$ are smooth and meet transversely, since one is a fiber and the other a section. A direct integrability calculation in local product coordinates therefore gives the stated ideals for every real $t>0$. In particular, the Lelong number at a generic point of $D$ has increased from zero to one, and proposition 25.3.2 shows that recovery fails.

Finally, since $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$, the system $\boldsymbol \Gamma $ and the realized system $\mathsf E\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma \neq \boldsymbol \Gamma $ have the same image under $\mathsf R$. Thus $\mathsf R$ is not injective, and $\boldsymbol \Gamma $ is not in the image of $\mathsf E$. $\square$

Proposition 25.5.3. Let $q_\eta $ be the common slice of the component $\Gamma ^\eta $ constructed in the preceding proof. Along the closed perturbations $\eta =\epsilon \kappa $, with real $\epsilon >0$,

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\kappa +\epsilon \kappa }(q_{\epsilon \kappa }) =2\epsilon \int _ X\theta \wedge \kappa +\epsilon ^2\int _ X\kappa ^2\longrightarrow 0. \]

The limiting slice has zero non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure.

Proof. The potential $u_\eta =\psi _ F+f_\eta $ has analytic singularities, so proposition 16.2.7 gives $q_\eta \sim u_\eta \sim \psi _ F$. Removing the divisorial component $[F]$ gives a positive current in the background $\theta +\eta $, whose global potential $w_\eta =q_\eta -\psi _ F$ is bounded by this ordinary equivalence.

Off $F$, plurifine locality identifies the Monge–Ampère measure of $q_\eta $ with $(\theta +\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\eta )^2$. This bounded product gives no mass to $F$ by proposition 1.7.1. For closed $\eta =\epsilon \kappa $, Stokes' theorem gives its total mass as $\int _ X(\theta +\epsilon \kappa )^2$.

The normal bundle of $D$ is trivial. Indeed, the transformation $w\mapsto w+1$ becomes $\xi \mapsto \xi /(1+\xi )$, whose derivative at $\xi =0$ is one, and the other transformation fixes $w$. Hence $\int _ X\theta ^2=D^2=0$, and expanding the square gives the formula. The limiting potential has curvature current $[F]+[D]$, which vanishes away from the pluripolar set $F\cup D$; its non-pluripolar measure is therefore zero. $\square$

25.6 Recovery through the Hopf fibration

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Positive lower volume on a closed base can force recovery even when the ambient manifold has unbounded upper mass. We now take

\[ X=(\mathbb C^3\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto \mathrm e^{-1}z\rangle , \qquad B=\mathbb P^2,\qquad \pi :X\to B, \]

and use the geometry of chapter 8. Write $\kappa =\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$, with $\int _ B\kappa ^2=1$, and put

\[ s=\log |z|^2,\qquad \alpha =\pi ^*\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s,\qquad \beta =\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s,\qquad h=\alpha +\beta . \]

The derivatives of $s$ descend to $X$. Scalar multiplication gives an action of the elliptic group $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+2\pi \mathrm i\mathbb Z)$, transitive on each fiber of $\pi $ and preserving $h$. By theorem 8.0.1, $h$ has unbounded upper mass. Invariant potentials nevertheless reduce to potentials on $B$ because the horizontal part of $h$ is the pullback of $\kappa $.

Proposition 25.6.1. For every $b>0$ and $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B,b\kappa )$,

\[ P_{bh}[\pi ^*v]_{\mathcal I} =\pi ^*P_{b\kappa }[v]_{\mathcal I}. \]

The envelope on the left uses all ambient competitors.

Proof. First, an $E$-invariant $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,bh)$ descends to an upper semicontinuous function $w$ on $B$, since $\pi $ is proper and $q$ is constant on its fibers. In a product chart, Fubini gives $w\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, and

\[ bh+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q=\pi ^*(b\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)+b\beta . \]

Lift a smooth base vector to a vector annihilated by $\partial s$. Its pairing with $\beta $ is zero. Testing the positive current above against these lifts and a nonnegative smooth fiber density of integral one proves $b\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\geq 0$ distributionally. A local potential of $b\kappa $ gives its canonical plurisubharmonic representative. Its pullback agrees almost everywhere with $q$, hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Thus $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(B,b\kappa )$. Conversely, every such $w$ lifts to a $bh$-plurisubharmonic function by the displayed identity.

Now set $q=P_{bh}[\pi ^*v]_{\mathcal I}$. Translation by any $g\in E$ preserves its complete competitor family: it preserves the background, the obstacle zero, and the ideals of the invariant input. The envelope is their greatest member, so $q\circ g=q$. Descend $q=\pi ^*w$ as above. By lemma 1.16.6, its ideal equivalence to $\pi ^*v$ implies $w\sim _{\mathcal I}v$: a germ pulled back from the base is weighted-integrable in a product chart if and only if the base germ is, by Fubini. Hence $w$ is a base competitor. Every base competitor also lifts to an ambient competitor by the same lemma and the current identity. Taking the greatest members gives equality. $\square$

Fix real numbers $a<c$. For an envelope system $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(h)$, scalar invariance alone supplies the whole transverse system, including its components over nonclosed base perturbations.

Lemma 25.6.2. Suppose $\Gamma _\tau ^{\epsilon h}$ is $E$-invariant for every $\epsilon >0$ and $\tau <c$. There is a unique envelope system $\boldsymbol \Psi \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(\kappa )$ such that

\[ \Gamma _\tau ^{\epsilon h}=\pi ^*\Psi _\tau ^{\epsilon \kappa } \quad (\epsilon >0,\ \tau <c). \]

Writing $\Gamma =\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma $ and $\psi =\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Psi $, we have $\Gamma _\tau =\pi ^*\psi _\tau $ for every $\tau <c$.

Proof. The descent in proposition 25.6.1 gives $\Gamma _\tau ^{\epsilon h}=\pi ^*\phi _\tau ^\epsilon $ with $\phi _\tau ^\epsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}(B,(1+\epsilon )\kappa )$. Normalization, decrease, concavity and constancy for $\tau \leq a$ descend pointwise. The fiber-envelope identity descends both ideal-model fixedness and scalar compatibility:

\[ \phi _\tau ^{\epsilon _2} =P_{(1+\epsilon _2)\kappa } [\phi _\tau ^{\epsilon _1}]_{\mathcal I} \quad (\epsilon _2\geq \epsilon _1>0). \]

In particular, the positive scalar slices have the same ideals at every weight.

For a Hermitian form $\xi >0$ on $B$, choose $\delta >0$ with $\delta \kappa \leq \xi $, fixed for the whole parameter interval, and define

\[ \Psi _\tau ^\xi =P_{\kappa +\xi } [\phi _\tau ^\delta ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

The definition is independent of $\delta $ because the scalar inputs have the same ideals. It reproduces the scalar components, and proposition 25.2.1 proves that each component is a curve in $\mathscr T_{a,c}(\kappa +\xi )$. Projection preserves the ideals, so comparing the complete competitor families proves compatibility for $\xi \geq \xi '$. Thus $\boldsymbol \Psi $ is a full envelope system. Every possible extension is forced by this formula, which proves uniqueness.

The retraction theorem applies to both systems. Taking their decreasing limits along $h/j$ and $\kappa /j$ in the scalar identity gives $\Gamma _\tau =\pi ^*\psi _\tau $. In particular the limits, including their values at poles, agree. For any other Hermitian metric $k$ on $B$, constants $0<l\leq r$ with $l\kappa \leq k\leq r\kappa $ give

\[ \phi _\tau ^{l/j}\leq \Psi _\tau ^{k/j}\leq \phi _\tau ^{r/j}. \]

Both outer sequences decrease to $\psi _\tau $. This also identifies the transverse limit directly, without assuming that it keeps the ideals of the positive scalar slices. $\square$

Theorem 25.6.3. Let $\boldsymbol \Gamma \in \mathscr S_{a,c}(h)$ have $E$-invariant scalar components as in lemma 25.6.2. Let $\psi =\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Psi $ be the transverse limiting curve. If

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\kappa (\psi _ a)>0, \]

then for every $\tau <c$ and every Hermitian form $\eta >0$ on $X$,

\[ \Gamma _\tau ^\eta =P_{h+\eta }[\pi ^*\psi _\tau ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

Thus $\boldsymbol \Gamma =\mathsf E(\mathsf R\boldsymbol \Gamma )$.

Proof. The closed Kähler background $\kappa $ is big and $B$ has the bounded mass property. Apply theorem 30.5.6 to the full transverse system. Its initial-volume hypothesis is exactly the displayed one, and it gives

\[ \Psi _\tau ^{\epsilon \kappa } =P_{(1+\epsilon )\kappa }[\psi _\tau ]_{\mathcal I} \quad (\epsilon >0,\ \tau <c). \]

The fiber-envelope identity yields $\Gamma _\tau ^{\epsilon h}=P_{(1+\epsilon )h}[\pi ^*\psi _\tau ]_{\mathcal I}$. For a general $\eta >0$, choose $\epsilon h\leq \eta $. Compatibility and the nested-background identity of proposition 25.2.1 then give

\[ \Gamma _\tau ^\eta =P_{h+\eta }[\Gamma _\tau ^{\epsilon h}]_{\mathcal I} =P_{h+\eta }[\pi ^*\psi _\tau ]_{\mathcal I}. \]
$\square$

The positivity condition is a lower ordinary-type volume on the closed base. It does not use an ambient upper-volume deficit or identify the ideals of the limit in advance. The conclusion holds at every finite $\tau <c$; it gives no uniform positive-volume bound as $\tau $ approaches $c$.

25.6.1 A nonanalytic family

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The countable hyperplane series of 30.8.5, 30.8.6 supplies an explicit family satisfying the transverse condition. Choose infinitely many distinct hyperplanes $H_ j\subset B$, positive coefficients $q_ j$ with $\sum _ jq_ j=1$, and their standard logarithms $\ell _ j\leq 0$. Put

\[ u_0=\sum _{j\geq 1}q_ j\ell _ j -\sup _ B\sum _{j\geq 1}q_ j\ell _ j. \]

Then $u_0\leq 0$ is $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic and $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_0=\sum _ jq_ j[H_ j]$.

Example 25.6.4. Fix $0<\lambda _0<\lambda _*<1$ and $r>0$ with $r(c-a)<\lambda _*-\lambda _0$. For $\tau <c$, set

\[ \lambda (\tau )=\lambda _0+r\max (\tau -a,0),\qquad u_\tau =\lambda (\tau )u_0, \]

and, for every Hermitian form $\eta >0$ on $X$, define

\[ \Gamma _\tau ^\eta =P_{h+\eta }[\pi ^*u_\tau ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

These components form an envelope system with $E$-invariant scalar slices. Its transverse limiting curve is $\psi _\tau =P_\kappa [u_\tau ]_{\mathcal I}$, and

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\kappa (\psi _\tau ) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\kappa (\psi _\tau ) =(1-\lambda (\tau ))^2>0. \]

The system is recovered from its limit. Its ideal data cannot be represented by analytic singularities.

Proof. The function $\lambda $ is positive, increasing and convex, and $u_0\leq 0$, so $u_\tau $ decreases, is pointwise concave and is constant for $\tau \leq a$. Moreover,

\[ \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\tau =(1-\lambda (\tau ))\kappa +\lambda (\tau )\sum _ jq_ j[H_ j]>0, \qquad h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\pi ^*u_\tau \geq (1-\lambda _*)h. \]

Set $q_\tau =P_\kappa [u_\tau ]_{\mathcal I}$. The order, scaling and addition inequalities of proposition 16.2.6 show that $q_\tau $ remains decreasing and concave; it is constant on the same left half-line. Thus $q\in \mathscr T_{a,c}(\kappa )$. Apply the same argument upstairs to $Q_\tau =P_ h[\pi ^*u_\tau ]_{\mathcal I}$. The fiber-envelope identity gives $Q_\tau =\pi ^*q_\tau $, and the nested-background identity identifies the displayed family with $\mathsf E(Q)$. It is therefore an envelope system, its limit is $Q$ by $\mathsf R\mathsf E=\mathrm{id}$, and its scalar slices are invariant.

Apply 30.8.6 with background $\kappa $, coefficients $a_ j=\lambda (\tau )q_ j$ and residual $(1-\lambda (\tau ))\kappa $. It gives the asserted exact lower and upper volumes of $q_\tau $. The transverse recovery theorem applies, also confirming recovery directly.

For every $j$, the divisorial coefficient of $u_\tau $ along $H_ j$ is positive. Projection preserves all ideals and hence these coefficients. Analytic singularities on a compact manifold have only finitely many divisorial components, since their defining coherent ideals have this property. Thus no analytic singularity type has the same all-weight ideals. The same conclusion holds upstairs along the distinct divisors $\pi ^{-1}(H_ j)$, by submersion pullback. $\square$

25.6.2 Exact pullback and smooth changes of background

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $\mu :Y\to X$ be a modification and let $\boldsymbol \Gamma $ satisfy theorem 25.6.3. By proposition 16.2.3, its recovery identity pulls back to

\[ \Gamma _\tau ^\eta \circ \mu =P_{\mu ^*(h+\eta )}[\Gamma _\tau \circ \mu ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

This concerns the exact pulled backgrounds. The form $\mu ^*\eta $ may be degenerate, so these identities do not define a full system indexed by every Hermitian form on $Y$.

For a modification $\nu :\widetilde B\to B$, proposition 13.3.2 gives

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\nu ^*\kappa } (\psi _ a\circ \nu ) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\kappa (\psi _ a)>0, \]

so the transverse positivity test survives exact pullback. The same proposition also preserves ambient singularity volumes in an exact pulled background; it makes no comparison with an independent positive background upstairs.

Finally, the smooth transport $\mathsf G_ f$ of proposition 25.4.1 commutes with both $\mathsf E$ and $\mathsf R$. Therefore recovery survives the change from $h$ to $h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ for every smooth real $f$. The transported scalar slices need not remain invariant under $E$, and no invariance of the raw masses of independently normalized nonclosed-background representatives is inferred. The general recovery problem without the scalar symmetry or transverse positive-volume condition remains open.
