22.1 Non-Archimedean pluripotential theory1E9C5D0261FD

1E9C5D0261FD

This chapter develops a non-Archimedean pluripotential theory for compact Kähler manifolds. In the projective setting, Boucksom and Jonsson constructed a space of non-Archimedean psh metrics on the Berkovich analytification of a polarized variety. Our approach starts instead from the analytic objects developed in the previous chapters: $\mathcal I$-model test curves and their operations. The resulting theory agrees with the Boucksom–Jonsson theory in the projective case.

The guiding idea is the Ross–Witt Nyström correspondence. A geodesic ray can be encoded by a test curve, and the $\mathcal I$-model version of this construction retains precisely the multiplier-ideal information relevant to algebraic approximation. For a pseudo-effective class $\{ \theta \} $, we define a non-Archimedean potential as an inverse system of $\mathcal I$-model test curves in the big classes $\{ \theta +\omega \} $, where $\omega $ ranges over Kähler forms. The transition maps are given by the $\mathcal I$-envelope.

After the definition, we develop the basic operations on non-Archimedean potentials. Sums, translations, maxima, suprema, infima and rescalings are induced from the corresponding operations on test curves. We also define the non-Archimedean volume and prove its basic monotonicity and continuity properties.

The next part of the chapter constructs the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of a non-Archimedean potential based on the theory of Okounkov bodies developed in section 19.1. It is defined through Okounkov test curves and is shown to be independent of the chosen flag.

The final part compares this construction with the Boucksom–Jonsson theory. In the projective case, we define the analytification of our non-Archimedean potentials and prove that the resulting space is naturally identified with the Boucksom–Jonsson space of psh non-Archimedean metrics.

The present chapter is logically based on the test-curve formalism of section 17.11, especially the $\mathcal I$-model theory of section 17.14 and the operations of section 17.15. It is largely independent of section 21.13, section 18.28, while the comparison with Boucksom–Jonsson uses the non-Archimedean theory recalled in subsection 22.1.64.

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$. Let $\textup{Käh}(X)$ be the set of Kähler forms on $X$ with the partial order given as follows: We say $\omega \preceq \omega '$ if $\omega \geq \omega '$. Note that the partially ordered set $\textup{Käh}(X)$ is a directed set.

Let $\theta $ be a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form representing a pseudo-effective cohomology class.

Lemma 22.1.9.587EEDE88F4F Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, let $\omega $ be a Kähler form on $X$, and let $0<t\leq 1$. Then for every $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _ X \left(\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n\\ \leq {}& \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Proof. For $\sigma \in \{ -\infty ,\tau \} $, put

\[ S_ t^\sigma =\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\sigma }. \]

By the transition compatibility and proposition 22.1.5, example 18.1.11, proposition 16.3.10, projecting $\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\sigma }$ to the class $\theta +\omega $ does not change the total mass computed in the larger class. Thus

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\sigma }\right)^ n = \int _ X \left(S_ t^\sigma +(1-t)\omega \right)^ n. \]

Expanding the right-hand side and subtracting the two identities for $\sigma =-\infty $ and $\sigma =\tau $, we get

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n\\ ={}& \sum _{a=0}^ n \binom {n}{a}(1-t)^{n-a} \left(\int _ X (S_ t^{-\infty })^ a\wedge \omega ^{n-a}-\int _ X (S_ t^\tau )^ a\wedge \omega ^{n-a}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Since $\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{-\infty }\geq \Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\tau }$, each summand is non-negative by the monotonicity theorem for mixed non-pluripolar products. Keeping only the term $a=n$ gives the desired inequality. $\square$

Let $X$ be an irreducible reduced variety over $\mathbb {C}$ of dimension $n$. We recall the Berkovich analytification $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ of $X$ with respect to the trivial valuation on $\mathbb {C}$.

Definition 22.1.67.433FF564E9A3 A (real-valued) valuation on $X$ (or a valuation of $\mathbb {C}(X)$) is a map $v\colon \mathbb {C}(X)\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ satisfying the following conditions:

  1. 8EDEF9CF138B

    For $f\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(f)=\infty $ if and only if $f=0$;

  2. 3ACA653FD4D8

    For $f,g\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)$;

  3. 4C1AC87714F3

    For $f,g\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(f+g)\geq v(f)\land v(g)$;

  4. C9D9521A044A

    For $c\in \mathbb {C}^{\times }$, $v(c)=0$.

The set of valuations on $X$ is denoted by $X^{\mathrm{val}}$. The center of a valuation $v$ is the scheme-theoretic point $c=c(v)$ of $X$ such that $v\geq 0$ on $\mathcal{O}_{X,c}$ and $v>0$ on the maximal ideal $\mathfrak {m}_{X,c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_{X,c}$. The center is unique if it exists, and it exists if $X$ is proper.

In the remainder of this section, we assume that $X$ is projective.

As a set, $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ is the set of semi-valuations on $X$, in other words, real-valued valuations $v$ on irreducible reduced subvarieties $Y$ of $X$ that are trivial on $\mathbb {C}$. We call $Y$ the support of the semi-valuation $v$. In other words,

\[ X^{\mathrm{An}}=\coprod _{Y} Y^{\mathrm{val}}. \]

We will write $v_{\mathrm{triv}}\in X^{\mathrm{An}}$ for the trivial valuation on $X$: $v_{\mathrm{triv}}(f)=0$ for any $f\in \mathbb {C}(X)^{\times }$.

We endow $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ with the coarsest topology such that

  1. 3E9AB9B11483

    for any Zariski open subset $U\subseteq X$, the subset $U^{\mathrm{An}}$ of $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ consisting of semi-valuations whose supports meet $U$ is open;

  2. 16DC97C2CD0A

    for each Zariski open subset $U\subseteq X$ and each $f\in \mathrm{H}^0(U,\mathcal{O}_ X)$ (here $\mathcal{O}_ X$ is the sheaf of regular functions), the map $|f|\colon U^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ sending $v$ to $\exp (-v(f))$ is continuous.

We will be most interested in divisorial valuations. Recall that a divisorial valuation on $X$ is a valuation of the form $t\operatorname{ord}_ E$, where $t\in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}$ and $E$ is a prime divisor over $X$. The set of divisorial valuations on $X$ is denoted by $X^{\operatorname{div}}$. When $\mathbb {Q}_{>0}$ is replaced by $\mathbb {R}_{>0}$, we can similarly define a space $X^{\operatorname{div}}_{\mathbb {R}}$.

Given any coherent ideal $\mathfrak {a}$ on $X$ and any $v\in X^{\mathrm{An}}$, we define

18B5288E3A41 22.1.68
\begin{equation} v(\mathfrak {a})\coloneqq \min \{ v(f):f\in \mathfrak {a}_{c(v)}\} \in [0,\infty ], \end{equation}

where $c(v)$ is the center of the valuation $v$ on $X$.

Given any valuation $v$ on $X$, the Gauss extension of $v$ is a valuation $\sigma (v)$ on $X\times \mathbb {A}^1$:

3B8E87503101 22.1.69
\begin{equation} \sigma (v)\left(\sum _ i f_ i t^ i\right)\coloneqq \min _ i (v(f_ i)+i). \end{equation}

Here $t$ is the standard coordinate on $\mathbb {A}^1=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb {C}[t]$. The key property is that when $v$ is a divisorial valuation, then so is $\sigma (v)$. See [Lemma 4.2, BHJ17].

22.1.70 Non-Archimedean plurisubharmonic functions3CE1C3E79114

Let $X$ be an irreducible complex projective variety of dimension $n$ and $L$ be a holomorphic pseudo-effective $\mathbb {Q}$-line bundle on $X$. Through the GAGA morphism $X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow X$ of ringed spaces, $L$ can be pulled back to an analytic line bundle $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ on $X$. The purpose of this section is to study the psh metrics on $L^{\mathrm{An}}$. This description avoids the direct treatment of $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ itself.

We define $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$, the set of (rational) generically finite Fubini–Study functions $\phi \colon X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ that are of the following form:

13.37
\begin{equation} \phi =\frac{1}{m}\max _ j \{ \log |s_ j|+\lambda _ j\} . \end{equation}

Here $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ is an integer such that $L^ m$ is a line bundle, the $s_ j$'s are a finite collection of non-zero sections in $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ m)$, and $\lambda _ j\in \mathbb {Q}$. We follow the convention of Boucksom–Jonsson by writing $\log |s_ j|(v)=-v(s_ j)$.

Definition 22.1.71 .EEADED50AAC3 A plurisubharmonic metric (or psh metric for short) on $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ is a function $\phi \colon X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ that is not identically $-\infty $, and is the pointwise limit of a decreasing net $(\phi _ i)_{i\in I}$, where $\phi _ i\in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}(L_ i^{\mathrm{An}})$ for some $\mathbb {Q}$-line bundles $L_ i$ on $X$ satisfying $c_1(L_ i)\to c_1(L)$ in $\mathrm{NS}^1(X)_{\mathbb {R}}$.

The set of psh metrics on $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ is denoted by $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. We endow $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ with the topology of pointwise convergence on $X^{\operatorname{div}}$. This topology is Hausdorff as functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ are completely determined by their restriction on $X^{\operatorname{div}}$:

Theorem 22.1.72 ([Theorem 4.22, BJ22]).0DAB63894803 Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ and $\psi :X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ be an usc function. Assume that $\phi \leq \psi $ on $X^{\operatorname{div}}$. Then the same holds on $X^{\mathrm{An}}$.

Proposition 22.1.73 ([Theorem 4.7, BJ22]).8E1FC46DBC87 Let $\phi ,\phi '\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. Then so is their pointwise maximum $\phi \lor \phi '$.

Proposition 22.1.74.85809FFB162F Let $H$ be an ample line bundle on $X$. Consider $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}((L+H)^{\mathrm{An}})$. Assume that for each $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we have $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}((L+m^{-1}H)^{\mathrm{An}})$. Then $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

This is a special case of [(PSH2), BJ22] on Page 45. Next we note that we may use sequences instead of nets in the definition of $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$:

Theorem 22.1.75 ([Corollary 12.18, BJ22]).6B2CF0C2744A Let $S$ be an ample line bundle on $X$. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. Then there is a sequence of rational numbers $\varepsilon _ i\searrow 0$ and a decreasing sequence $\phi _ i\in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+\varepsilon _ i S)^{\mathrm{An}})$ such that $\phi $ is the pointwise limit of $\phi _ i$, as $i\to \infty $.

The space $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ inherits most of the expected properties of (Archimedean) psh functions ([Theorem 4.7, BJ22]). However, the following compactness result is not known:

Conjecture 22.1.76 .95739E70234B Assume that $X$ is unibranch. Then every bounded from above increasing net of elements in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ converges in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

This prediction is equivalent to the so-called envelope conjecture [Conjecture 5.14, BJ22]: the regularized supremum of a bounded from above family of functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ lies in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. See [Theorem 5.11, BJ22] for the proof of the equivalence. This conjecture is proved when $X$ is smooth and $L$ is nef in [BJ22]. More recently, in [BJ22b], Boucksom–Jonsson further established the case when $X$ is smooth and $L$ is pseudo-effective.
