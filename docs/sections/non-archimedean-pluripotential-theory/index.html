19.1 Non-Archimedean pluripotential theory

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

13 Non-Archimedean pluripotential theory

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

This chapter develops a non-Archimedean pluripotential theory for compact Kähler manifolds. In the projective setting, Boucksom and Jonsson constructed a space of non-Archimedean psh metrics on the Berkovich analytification of a polarized variety. Our approach starts instead from the analytic objects developed in the previous chapters: $\mathcal I$-model test curves and their operations. The resulting theory agrees with the Boucksom–Jonsson theory in the projective case.

The guiding idea is the Ross–Witt Nyström correspondence. A geodesic ray can be encoded by a test curve, and the $\mathcal I$-model version of this construction retains precisely the multiplier-ideal information relevant to algebraic approximation. For a pseudo-effective class $\{ \theta \} $, we define a non-Archimedean potential as an inverse system of $\mathcal I$-model test curves in the big classes $\{ \theta +\omega \} $, where $\omega $ ranges over Kähler forms. The transition maps are given by the $\mathcal I$-envelope.

After the definition, we develop the basic operations on non-Archimedean potentials. Sums, translations, maxima, suprema, infima and rescalings are induced from the corresponding operations on test curves. We also define the non-Archimedean volume and prove its basic monotonicity and continuity properties.

The next part of the chapter constructs the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of a non-Archimedean potential based on the theory of Okounkov bodies developed in chapter 10. It is defined through Okounkov test curves and is shown to be independent of the chosen flag.

The final part compares this construction with the Boucksom–Jonsson theory. In the projective case, we define the analytification of our non-Archimedean potentials and prove that the resulting space is naturally identified with the Boucksom–Jonsson space of psh non-Archimedean metrics.

A related approach has been developed independently by Mesquita-Piccione [MP24], where a Berkovich-like analytification of a compact Kähler manifold is constructed. The present chapter is logically based on the test-curve formalism of chapter 9, especially the $\mathcal I$-model theory of section 9.3 and the operations of section 9.4. It is largely independent of chapter 12, chapter 14, while the comparison with Boucksom–Jonsson uses the non-Archimedean theory recalled in section 13.4.

13.1 The definition of non-Archimedean metrics

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$. Let $\textup{Käh}(X)$ be the set of Kähler forms on $X$ with the partial order given as follows: We say $\omega \preceq \omega '$ if $\omega \geq \omega '$. Note that the partially ordered set $\textup{Käh}(X)$ is a directed set.

Let $\theta $ be a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form representing a pseudo-effective cohomology class.

Definition 13.1.1. We define

\[ \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )=\varprojlim _{\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)} \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega )_{>0}\footnotemark \]
Note. The cumbersome projective limit can be avoided if instead of relying on the language of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, we use that of augmented nef b-divisors developed in definition 11.1.17 instead. But given the fact that these two formulations are completely equivalent to each other by corollary 11.1.19, we just stick to the slightly more traditional language here.
in the category of sets, where the transition maps are given as follows: Suppose that $\omega ,\omega '\in \textup{Käh}$ and $\omega \geq \omega '$. Then the transition map is defined in proposition 9.3.6:
13.1
\begin{equation} P_{\theta +\omega }[\bullet ]_{\mathcal{I}}\colon \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega ')_{>0}\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega )_{>0}. \end{equation}

Note that the transition maps are compatible:

\[ P_{\theta +\omega ''}[P_{\theta +\omega '}[\varphi ]_\mathcal {I}]_\mathcal {I}=P_{\theta +\omega ''}[\varphi ]_\mathcal {I},\quad \forall \, \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega )_{>0} \]

for $\omega \leq \omega '\leq \omega ''$, since both sides are $\mathcal{I}$-model and are $\mathcal{I}$-equivalent to $\varphi $.

Recall that $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ is defined in definition 9.3.1.

In general, when we denote an element in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ by $\Gamma $, its component in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega )_{>0}$ ($\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$) will be written either as $\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }$ or as $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$.

Note that $\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\max }$ is independent of the choice of $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$. We denote this common value by $\Gamma _{\max }$.

Remark 13.1.2. Thanks to proposition 9.3.3, for any other $\theta '$ representing $\{ \theta \} $, we have a canonical bijection

\[ \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )\xrightarrow {\sim }\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta '). \]

Moreover, these bijections satisfy the cocycle condition. If we view the set of closed real smooth $(1,1)$-forms representing $\{ \theta \} $ as a category with a unique morphism between any two objects, then we can define

\[ \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\{ \theta \} )=\varprojlim _{\theta } \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ). \]

This definition is independent of the choice of the explicit representative of the cohomology class $\{ \theta \} $.

However, given the fact that our notation is already quite heavy, we stick to the set $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. The constructions below are independent of the choice of $\theta $ within its cohomology class.

Proposition 13.1.3. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Then given $\omega ,\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)$ with $\omega \geq \omega '$, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}_{-\infty } \right]=P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}_{-\infty } \right]_{\mathcal{I}} = \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }. \]

Proof. Since for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, the potential $\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}_{\tau }$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good by example 7.1.7, it follows that

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}_{\tau } \right]=P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}_{\tau } \right]_{\mathcal{I}}=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau } \]

for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. Our assertion follows from proposition 3.1.28 and proposition 3.2.19. $\square$

Proposition 13.1.4. There is a natural injective map

\[ \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}\hookrightarrow \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ),\quad \Gamma \mapsto \left(P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}\right)_{\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)}. \]
In the sequel, we do not distinguish an element of $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ from its image in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Note that given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, the value of $\Gamma _{\max }$ does not depend on whether we view it as an element of $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ or of $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. The map is well-defined by the compatibility of the transition maps. It suffices to argue its injectivity. Suppose that $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}} \]

for some Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. Then $\Gamma _{\max }=\Gamma '_{\max }$ and for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }\sim _{\mathcal{I}}\Gamma '_{\tau } \]

by proposition 6.1.12. It follows again from proposition 6.1.12 that

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=\Gamma '_{\tau }. \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Definition 13.1.5. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. We define its volume as follows:

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma \coloneqq \lim _{\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)} \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n\in [0,\infty ). \]
Observe that the net is decreasing, so the limit exists.

Proposition 13.1.6. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma =\int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{-\infty }\right)^ n. \]

Proof. This follows from proposition 3.1.26, corollary 3.1.27 and proposition 9.1.11. $\square$

Lemma 13.1.7. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, let $\omega $ be a Kähler form on $X$, and let $0<t\leq 1$. Then for every $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _ X \left(\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n\\ \leq {}& \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Proof. For $\sigma \in \{ -\infty ,\tau \} $, put

\[ S_ t^\sigma =\theta +t\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\sigma }. \]

By the transition compatibility and proposition 13.1.3, example 7.1.7, proposition 3.1.6, projecting $\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\sigma }$ to the class $\theta +\omega $ does not change the total mass computed in the larger class. Thus

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\sigma }\right)^ n = \int _ X \left(S_ t^\sigma +(1-t)\omega \right)^ n. \]

Expanding the right-hand side and subtracting the two identities for $\sigma =-\infty $ and $\sigma =\tau $, we get

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n\\ ={}& \sum _{a=0}^ n \binom {n}{a}(1-t)^{n-a} \left(\int _ X (S_ t^{-\infty })^ a\wedge \omega ^{n-a}-\int _ X (S_ t^\tau )^ a\wedge \omega ^{n-a}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Since $\Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{-\infty }\geq \Gamma ^{\theta +t\omega }_{\tau }$, each summand is non-negative by the monotonicity theorem for mixed non-pluripolar products. Keeping only the term $a=n$ gives the desired inequality. $\square$

Lemma 13.1.8. The image of the canonical injection

\[ \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}\hookrightarrow \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ) \]

is given by the set of $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ with positive volume.

Proof. By proposition 13.1.6, every element in the image has positive volume.

Conversely, take $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ with positive volume. We want to construct $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ representing $\Gamma $.

Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. Define

13.6
\begin{equation} \Gamma '_{\tau }\coloneqq \lim _{k\to \infty }\Gamma ^{\theta +k^{-1}\omega }_{\tau },\quad \tau <\Gamma _{\max }. \end{equation}

We claim that it suffices to show

13.7
\begin{equation} \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\int _ X \left(\theta +k^{-1}\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +k^{-1}\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n>0 \end{equation}

for some $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. If this holds, then the argument of 9.1.3 implies that the same holds for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. Then proposition 3.1.26, proposition 3.2.18 guarantee that $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and represents $\Gamma $.

It remains to argue (13.7). Let $\epsilon =\operatorname{vol}\Gamma >0$. Take $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$ so that

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n<\epsilon /2. \]

Therefore, by 13.1.7, for any $k\geq 1$,

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +k^{-1}\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +k^{-1}\omega }_{-\infty }\right)^ n-\int _ X \left(\theta +k^{-1}\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +k^{-1}\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n<\epsilon /2. \]

By the definition of $\operatorname{vol}\Gamma $, the first integral in the preceding line tends to $\epsilon $ as $k\to \infty $. Hence

\[ \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\int _ X \left(\theta +k^{-1}\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\theta +k^{-1}\omega }_{\tau }\right)^ n\geq \epsilon /2>0, \]

which proves (13.7). $\square$

Example 13.1.9. Fix $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We can define an associated non-Archimedean metric

\[ \Gamma ^{\varphi }\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ) \]

as follows:

  1. $\Gamma ^{\varphi }_{\max }=0$;

  2. for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$ and any $\tau <0$, we set

    \[ \Gamma ^{\varphi ,\theta +\omega }_{\tau }=P_{\theta +\omega }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Such non-Archimedean metrics are called homogeneous non-Archimedean metrics.

Observe that

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\varphi }=\operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \]

See proposition 7.3.4 and the footnote there.

Definition 13.1.10. Let $\omega $ be a closed real smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. We define the map

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\bullet ]_{\mathcal{I}}\colon \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega ) \]

as follows: Given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, we define $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ as the element such that for any $\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}^{\theta +\omega +\omega '}=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega +\omega '}. \]
It is straightforward to check that under the identification of proposition 13.1.4, the map $P_{\theta +\omega }[\bullet ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ extends the map (13.1).

Proposition 13.1.11. The maps $P_{\theta +\omega }[\bullet ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ in definition 13.1.10 together induce a bijection

Proof. It is a tautology that the maps $P_{\theta +\omega }[\bullet ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ in definition 13.1.10 are compatible with the transition maps. So the map (13.8) is well-defined. It is injective by the same argument as proposition 13.1.4. We argue the surjectivity.

By unfolding the definitions, an object in the target of (13.8) is an assignment: With each $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we associate a family $(\Gamma ^{\omega ,\omega '})_{\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)}$ satisfying:

  1. $\Gamma ^{\omega ,\omega '}\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega +\omega ')_{>0}$ for each $\omega ,\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)$;

  2. for each $\omega ,\omega ',\omega ''\in \textup{Käh}(X)$ satisfying $\omega ''\geq \omega '$, we have

    \[ P_{\theta +\omega +\omega ''}\left[\Gamma ^{\omega ,\omega '}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}=\Gamma ^{\omega ,\omega ''}; \]

The preimage of such an object is given by the family $(\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega })_{\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)}$ given by

\[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }=\Gamma ^{\omega /2,\omega /2}. \]

The fact that the image of $\Gamma $ is as expected follows directly from the definitions. $\square$

13.2 Operations on non-Archimedean metrics

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta ,\theta ',\theta ''$ be closed real smooth $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ representing big cohomology classes.

This section relies heavily on section 9.4. We shall use the notions introduced in that section without further explanations.

Definition 13.2.1. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$. We say $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$ if for some $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega } \leq \Gamma '{}^{\theta '+\omega }. \]
This notion is independent of the choice of $\omega $ thanks to 9.4.2. Moreover, we have the following:

Proposition 13.2.2. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\omega $ be a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$;

  2. $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}$.

Proof. This follows immediately from 9.4.2. $\square$

Observe that this definition coincides with the corresponding definition in definition 9.4.1 when $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Proposition 13.2.3. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma \leq \operatorname{vol}\Gamma '. \]

Proof. It suffices to show that for any Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }\leq \operatorname{vol}\Gamma '{}^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }, \]

which is an immediate consequence of theorem 2.4.8. $\square$

Definition 13.2.4. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$. Then we define $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\theta ')$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \Gamma +\Gamma ' \right)^{\theta +\theta '+2\omega }=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }+\Gamma '^{\theta '+\omega }. \]
This definition yields an element in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\theta ')$ by 9.4.6 and it extends the definition in definition 9.4.3 by 9.4.6 as well.

Proposition 13.2.5. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$. Suppose that $\omega ,\omega '$ are two smooth closed positive $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega +\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma +\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}+P_{\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of 9.4.6. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.6. The operation $+$ is commutative and associative: For any $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$ and $\Gamma ''\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta '')$, we have

\[ \Gamma +\Gamma '=\Gamma '+\Gamma ,\quad (\Gamma +\Gamma ')+\Gamma ''=\Gamma +(\Gamma '+\Gamma ''). \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of proposition 9.4.5. $\square$

Definition 13.2.7. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $C\in \mathbb {R}$. We define $\Gamma +C\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \Gamma +C \right)^{\theta +\omega }=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }+C. \]
It is obvious from definition 9.4.7 that $\Gamma +C\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. It is also obvious that this definition extends definition 9.4.7.

Proposition 13.2.8. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Suppose that $\omega $ is a smooth closed positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}+C=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma +C]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is clear by definition. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.9. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')$ and $C,C'\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

  1. $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')+C=\Gamma +(\Gamma '+C)=(\Gamma +C)+\Gamma '$;

  2. $\Gamma +(C+C')=(\Gamma +C)+C'$.

Proof. This is a direct consequence of proposition 9.4.8. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.10. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma =\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma +C). \]

Proof. It suffices to show that for each Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }=\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma +C)^{\theta +\omega }_{-\infty }, \]

which is obvious. $\square$

Definition 13.2.11. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, we define $\Gamma \lor \Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \Gamma \lor \Gamma ' \right)^{\theta +\omega }=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega }\lor \Gamma '^{\theta +\omega }. \]
It follows from 9.4.12 that $\Gamma \lor \Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and this definition extends the corresponding definition in definition 9.4.9.

Proposition 13.2.12. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\omega $ be a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma \lor \Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}\lor P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of 9.4.12. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.13. The operation $\lor $ is commutative and associative.

In particular, given a finite non-empty family $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, we then define $\bigvee _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i$ in the obvious way.

Proof. This is a direct consequence of corollary 9.4.11. $\square$

Definition 13.2.14. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Assume that

13.10
\begin{equation} \sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }<\infty . \end{equation}

Then we define $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ as the unique element such that for any $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$, we have

\[ \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i \right)^{\theta +\omega }=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^{i,\theta +\omega }. \]
It follows immediately from 9.4.18 that $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and this definition extends definition 9.4.16. Moreover, this definition clearly extends definition 13.2.11 as well.

Proposition 13.2.15. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.10). Assume that $\omega $ is a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}= \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of 9.4.18. $\square$

We also have a non-Archimedean version of Choquet's lemma.

Proposition 13.2.16. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ satisfying (13.10). Then there exists a countable subfamily $I'\subseteq I$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i. \]

Valuations

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be an irreducible reduced variety over $\mathbb {C}$ of dimension $n$. We recall the Berkovich analytification $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ of $X$ with respect to the trivial valuation on $\mathbb {C}$.

Definition 13.4.1. A (real-valued) valuation on $X$ (or a valuation of $\mathbb {C}(X)$) is a map $v\colon \mathbb {C}(X)\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ satisfying the following conditions:

  1. For $f\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(f)=\infty $ if and only if $f=0$;

  2. For $f,g\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)$;

  3. For $f,g\in \mathbb {C}(X)$, $v(f+g)\geq v(f)\land v(g)$;

  4. For $c\in \mathbb {C}^{\times }$, $v(c)=0$.

The set of valuations on $X$ is denoted by $X^{\mathrm{val}}$. The center of a valuation $v$ is the scheme-theoretic point $c=c(v)$ of $X$ such that $v\geq 0$ on $\mathcal{O}_{X,c}$ and $v>0$ on the maximal ideal $\mathfrak {m}_{X,c}$ of $\mathcal{O}_{X,c}$. The center is unique if it exists, and it exists if $X$ is proper.

In the remainder of this section, we assume that $X$ is projective.

As a set, $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ is the set of semi-valuations on $X$, in other words, real-valued valuations $v$ on irreducible reduced subvarieties $Y$ of $X$ that are trivial on $\mathbb {C}$. We call $Y$ the support of the semi-valuation $v$. In other words,

\[ X^{\mathrm{An}}=\coprod _{Y} Y^{\mathrm{val}}. \]

We will write $v_{\mathrm{triv}}\in X^{\mathrm{An}}$ for the trivial valuation on $X$: $v_{\mathrm{triv}}(f)=0$ for any $f\in \mathbb {C}(X)^{\times }$.

We endow $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ with the coarsest topology such that

  1. for any Zariski open subset $U\subseteq X$, the subset $U^{\mathrm{An}}$ of $X^{\mathrm{An}}$ consisting of semi-valuations whose supports meet $U$ is open;

  2. for each Zariski open subset $U\subseteq X$ and each $f\in \mathrm{H}^0(U,\mathcal{O}_ X)$ (here $\mathcal{O}_ X$ is the sheaf of regular functions), the map $|f|\colon U^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ sending $v$ to $\exp (-v(f))$ is continuous.

See [Berk93] for more details.

We will be most interested in divisorial valuations. Recall that a divisorial valuation on $X$ is a valuation of the form $t\operatorname{ord}_ E$, where $t\in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}$ and $E$ is a prime divisor over $X$. The set of divisorial valuations on $X$ is denoted by $X^{\operatorname{div}}$. When $\mathbb {Q}_{>0}$ is replaced by $\mathbb {R}_{>0}$, we can similarly define a space $X^{\operatorname{div}}_{\mathbb {R}}$.

Given any coherent ideal $\mathfrak {a}$ on $X$ and any $v\in X^{\mathrm{An}}$, we define

13.35
\begin{equation} v(\mathfrak {a})\coloneqq \min \{ v(f):f\in \mathfrak {a}_{c(v)}\} \in [0,\infty ], \end{equation}

where $c(v)$ is the center of the valuation $v$ on $X$.

Given any valuation $v$ on $X$, the Gauss extension of $v$ is a valuation $\sigma (v)$ on $X\times \mathbb {A}^1$:

13.36
\begin{equation} \sigma (v)\left(\sum _ i f_ i t^ i\right)\coloneqq \min _ i (v(f_ i)+i). \end{equation}

Here $t$ is the standard coordinate on $\mathbb {A}^1=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb {C}[t]$. The key property is that when $v$ is a divisorial valuation, then so is $\sigma (v)$. See [Lemma 4.2, BHJ17].

Non-Archimedean plurisubharmonic functions

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be an irreducible complex projective variety of dimension $n$ and $L$ be a holomorphic pseudo-effective $\mathbb {Q}$-line bundle on $X$. Through the GAGA morphism $X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow X$ of ringed spaces, $L$ can be pulled back to an analytic line bundle $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ on $X$. The purpose of this section is to study the psh metrics on $L^{\mathrm{An}}$. We will follow the approach of [BJ22], which avoids the direct treatment of $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ itself.

Following [Definition 2.18, BJ22], we define $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$, the set of (rational) generically finite Fubini–Study functions $\phi \colon X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ that are of the following form:

13.37
\begin{equation} \phi =\frac{1}{m}\max _ j \{ \log |s_ j|+\lambda _ j\} . \end{equation}

Here $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ is an integer such that $L^ m$ is a line bundle, the $s_ j$'s are a finite collection of non-zero sections in $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ m)$, and $\lambda _ j\in \mathbb {Q}$. We follow the convention of Boucksom–Jonsson by writing $\log |s_ j|(v)=-v(s_ j)$.

Definition 13.4.2 ([Definition 4.1, BJ22]). A plurisubharmonic metric (or psh metric for short) on $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ is a function $\phi \colon X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ that is not identically $-\infty $, and is the pointwise limit of a decreasing net $(\phi _ i)_{i\in I}$, where $\phi _ i\in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}(L_ i^{\mathrm{An}})$ for some $\mathbb {Q}$-line bundles $L_ i$ on $X$ satisfying $c_1(L_ i)\to c_1(L)$ in $\mathrm{NS}^1(X)_{\mathbb {R}}$.

The set of psh metrics on $L^{\mathrm{An}}$ is denoted by $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. We endow $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ with the topology of pointwise convergence on $X^{\operatorname{div}}$. This topology is Hausdorff as functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ are completely determined by their restriction on $X^{\operatorname{div}}$:

Theorem 13.4.3 ([Theorem 4.22, BJ22]). Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ and $\psi :X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ be an usc function. Assume that $\phi \leq \psi $ on $X^{\operatorname{div}}$. Then the same holds on $X^{\mathrm{An}}$.

Proposition 13.4.4 ([Theorem 4.7, BJ22]). Let $\phi ,\phi '\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. Then so is their pointwise maximum $\phi \lor \phi '$.

Proposition 13.4.5. Let $H$ be an ample line bundle on $X$. Consider $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}((L+H)^{\mathrm{An}})$. Assume that for each $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we have $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}((L+m^{-1}H)^{\mathrm{An}})$. Then $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

This is a special case of [(PSH2), BJ22] on Page 45. Next we note that we may use sequences instead of nets in the definition of $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$:

Theorem 13.4.6 ([Corollary 12.18, BJ22]). Let $S$ be an ample line bundle on $X$. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. Then there is a sequence of rational numbers $\varepsilon _ i\searrow 0$ and a decreasing sequence $\phi _ i\in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+\varepsilon _ i S)^{\mathrm{An}})$ such that $\phi $ is the pointwise limit of $\phi _ i$, as $i\to \infty $.

The space $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ inherits most of the expected properties of (Archimedean) psh functions ([Theorem 4.7, BJ22]). However, the following compactness result is not known:

Conjecture 13.4.7 ([§5, BJ22]). Assume that $X$ is unibranch. Then every bounded from above increasing net of elements in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ converges in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$.

This prediction is equivalent to the so-called envelope conjecture [Conjecture 5.14, BJ22]: the regularized supremum of a bounded from above family of functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ lies in $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. See [Theorem 5.11, BJ22] for the proof of the equivalence. This conjecture is proved when $X$ is smooth and $L$ is nef in [BJ22]. More recently, in [BJ22b], Boucksom–Jonsson further established the case when $X$ is smooth and $L$ is pseudo-effective.

Definition 13.4.11. Let $N\in \mathbb {N}$, and $A_{0},\ldots ,A_ N$ be a finite collection of elements in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $\tau _0>\tau _1>\cdots >\tau _ N$ be finitely many real numbers. Then the piecewise linear curve $A=(A_{\tau })_{\tau \in \mathbb {R}}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $ associated with these data is the affine interpolation of these data:

  1. $A_{\tau _ i}=A_ i$ for $i=0,\ldots ,N$;

  2. $A_{\tau }=A_{\tau _ N}$ for $\tau \leq \tau _ N$;

  3. for $t\in (0,1)$ and $i=0,\ldots ,N-1$, we have

    \[ A_{(1-t)\tau _ i+t\tau _{i+1}}=(1-t)A_{\tau _ i}+tA_{\tau _{i+1}}; \]

  4. $A_{\tau }\equiv -\infty $ for $\tau >\tau _0$.

The analytification of $A$ is the function $A^{\mathrm{An}}\colon X^{\mathrm{An}}\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ defined as follows:

13.42
\begin{equation} A^{\mathrm{An}}(v)\coloneqq \sup _{\tau \leq \tau _0} (A_\tau ^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau )=\max _{i=0,\ldots ,N} \left(A_{\tau _ i}^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau _ i\right)\quad \forall v\in X^{\mathrm{An}}. \end{equation}
We also say $A=(A_{\tau })_{\tau \leq \tau _0}$ is a piecewise linear curve in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Remark 13.4.12. Note that $\tau \mapsto A_{\tau }$ is upper semicontinuous, but not necessarily concave. Let $(A'_{\tau })_{\tau \in \mathbb {R}}$ be the upper concave envelope of $\tau \mapsto A_{\tau }$. Then it can be inductively constructed as follows:

  1. For $\tau \in (\tau _0,\infty )$, we let $A'_{\tau }\equiv -\infty $;

  2. we set $A'_{\tau _0}=A_{\tau _0}$;

  3. define inductively for $j=0,\ldots ,N-1$ the following: For $\tau \in [\tau _{j+1},\tau _ j)$, we set

    \[ A'_{\tau }=\max _{i=j+1,\ldots ,N}\left( \frac{\tau _ j-\tau }{\tau _ j-\tau _ i}A_{\tau _ i}+\frac{\tau -\tau _ i}{\tau _ j-\tau _ i}A'_{\tau _ j} \right)\lor A'_{\tau _ j}; \]

  4. for $\tau \in (-\infty ,\tau _ N)$, we set $A'_{\tau }=A_{\tau _ N}$.

This construction is just a reformulation of the general formula proposition A.1.11.

In particular, $A'_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for all $\tau \leq \tau _0$.

Note that $A'$ is not necessarily piecewise linear.

Lemma 13.4.13. Let $A$ be a piecewise linear curve in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let $(A'_{\tau })_{\tau \in \mathbb {R}}$ be the upper concave envelope of $\tau \mapsto A_{\tau }$. Define $\tilde A$ by

\[ \tilde{A}^{\theta +\omega }_{\tau }\coloneqq P_{\theta +\omega }[A'_{\tau }]_{\mathcal I}, \quad \omega \in \textup{Käh}(X),\ \tau <\tau _0. \]

Then $\tilde A\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$, and

13.43
\begin{equation} A^{\mathrm{An}}=\tilde{A}^{\mathrm{An}}\quad \text{on }X^{\operatorname{div}}. \end{equation}
Here $\tau _0$ is as in definition 13.4.11.

Proof. We continue to use the notations in definition 13.4.11. The fact that $\tilde{A}\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ follows from 13.4.12 and the cocycle property of the $\mathcal{I}$-projection. In order to prove (13.43), we fix $v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}$ and $\omega \in \textup{Käh}(X)$. The inductive construction in 13.4.12 uses only finite maxima and positive affine combinations of the potentials $A_{\tau _ i}$. On divisorial valuations, analytification commutes with both operations. Moreover, $P_{\theta +\omega }[\bullet ]_{\mathcal I}$ does not change divisorial valuations. Hence

\[ \tau \mapsto \left(P_{\theta +\omega }[A'_\tau ]_{\mathcal I}\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(v)= (A'_\tau )^{\mathrm{An}}(v) \]

is the ordinary upper concave envelope of $\tau \mapsto A_\tau ^{\mathrm{An}}(v)$. The Legendre-type supremum over $\tau $ is therefore unchanged by passing from $A$ to $A'$, which proves (13.43). $\square$

Fix $v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}$. By theorem 13.4.3, it suffices to prove that

13.51
\begin{equation} \sup _{\tau \in \mathbb {R}} \left( \left(\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _{i,\tau }\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right)= \adjustlimits \inf _{i\in I}\sup _{\tau \in \mathbb {R}} \left( \Gamma _{i,\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right). \end{equation}

Write $\Gamma =\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _ i$. We first compare the two sides inside the Legendre-type supremum. If $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, then by the definition of the infimum test curve,

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _{i,\tau }. \]

The net $(\Gamma _{i,\tau })_ i$ decreases to $\Gamma _{\tau }$. By 9.4.27, $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is stable under $P_{\theta }[\bullet ]$. Hence proposition 3.1.26 gives convergence of the masses, and corollary 6.2.11 yields

\[ \Gamma _{i,\tau }\xrightarrow {d_ S}\Gamma _{\tau }. \]

Hence theorem 6.2.29 gives

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma _{i,\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v),\quad \tau <\Gamma _{\max }. \]

If $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$, then $\Gamma _{\tau }\equiv -\infty $, and also $\inf _ i\Gamma _{i,\tau }\equiv -\infty $, since $\Gamma _{\max }=\inf _ i(\Gamma _ i)_{\max }$. Thus the same equality holds for $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$ as well, with value $-\infty $. The possible endpoint $\tau =\Gamma _{\max }$ does not affect the supremum: for any test curve $\Lambda $ and any $v\in X^{\operatorname{div}}$,

\[ \sup _{\tau \leq \Lambda _{\max }}\left(\Lambda _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right) =\sup _{\tau < \Lambda _{\max }}\left(\Lambda _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\tau \right), \]

because $\Lambda _{\Lambda _{\max }}\leq \Lambda _{\tau }$ for $\tau <\Lambda _{\max }$, hence

\[ \Lambda _{\Lambda _{\max }}^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\Lambda _{\max } \leq \Lambda _{\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)+\Lambda _{\max } \]

and we let $\tau \nearrow \Lambda _{\max }$.

Consequently, in (13.51) we may replace $\left(\inf _ i\Gamma _{i,\tau }\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(v)$ by $\inf _ i\Gamma _{i,\tau }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)$ inside the supremum. The equality then follows from proposition A.2.10 applied to the closed convex functions $-\Gamma _{i,\bullet }^{\mathrm{An}}(v)$. $\square$

Theorem 13.4.24. The map (13.46) is an order preserving bijection.

Proof. The map (13.46) is an order preserving injection by 13.4.21. It remains to prove that it is surjective. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. We want to construct $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ with $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi $.

Let $H$ be an ample line bundle and $(\epsilon _ i)_ i$ be a decreasing sequence of rational numbers with limit $0$, $\phi _ i\in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+\epsilon _ i H)^{\mathrm{An}})$ such that

\[ \phi =\inf _{i>0}\phi _ i. \]

The existence of these data is guaranteed by theorem 13.4.6. Fix a Kähler form $\omega \in c_1(H)$.

Thanks to 13.4.22, we can find $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\epsilon _ i \omega )$ with $(\Gamma ^ i)^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi _ i$. It follows from 13.4.21 that

\[ \Gamma ^ i\geq P_{\theta +\epsilon _ i\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i+1} \right]_{\mathcal{I}} \geq \Gamma ^{i+1}. \]

Therefore, for any $\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)$, after discarding finitely many $i$ so that $\omega '\geq \epsilon _ i\omega $, the sequence $(P_{\theta +\omega '}\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}})_ i$ is decreasing. This does not change its infimum. We let

\[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}=\inf _{i\gg 1}P_{\theta +\omega '}\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]_{\mathcal{I}}\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega '). \]

Note that the infimum is defined thanks to (13.48). It follows from proposition 13.4.23 that

\[ \left(\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '} \right)^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi . \]

From this, we conclude that for $\omega ',\omega ''\in \textup{Käh}(X)$ with $\omega '\leq \omega ''$, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\omega ''}\left[ \Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}=\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega ''}. \]

Indeed, both sides have analytification $\phi $, by (13.47) and (13.46). The injectivity part of 13.4.21 gives our assertion.

It follows that $(\Gamma ^{\theta +\omega '})_{\omega '\in \textup{Käh}(X)}$ defines an element $\Gamma $ in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi $. $\square$

Theorem 13.4.25. Under the bijection theorem 13.4.24, the operations on the space $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ defined in section 13.2 all correspond to the corresponding operations on $\mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ in Boucksom–Jonsson's theory.

The meaning is immediate for all operations except for the trace operator, and the proofs are elementary, as we have seen in proposition 13.4.23 for the infimum operator. We shall only restate and prove the case of trace operators; the remaining arguments are recorded in [Xia23Operations].

Theorem 13.4.26. Let $Y\subseteq X$ be an irreducible analytic subset. Consider an element $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ with well-defined restriction to $Y$. Then

13.52
\begin{equation} \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\mathrm{An}}|_{Y^{\operatorname{div}}}=\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}|_{Y^{\operatorname{div}}}. \end{equation}
Observe that there is a canonical identification $Y^{\operatorname{div}}=\widetilde{Y}^{\operatorname{div}}$. Recall that a generalized Fubini–Study metric is defined in definition 1.8.11.

Proof. We first reduce to the positive mass case. Choose a Kähler form $\omega _0$ on $X$ and set $\widehat{\Gamma }=P_{\theta +\omega _0}[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$. Replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\omega _0$ and $\Gamma $ by $\widehat{\Gamma }$ does not change $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}$, by (13.47); it also only replaces $\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )$ by the corresponding $\mathcal{I}$-projection on $\widetilde{Y}$, whose analytification is unchanged. Hence we may assume that $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Let $\phi =\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}\in \mathrm{PSH}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$. By 13.4.27, $\phi (v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}})\neq -\infty $.

Take an ample line bundle $S$ on $X$ and a Kähler form $\omega $ in $c_1(S)$. Write $\phi $ as the decreasing limit of a sequence $(\phi ^{i})_ i$ of elements in $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}((L+i^{-1}S)^{\mathrm{An}})$ as in theorem 13.4.6.

Take $\Gamma ^{i}\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +i^{-1}\omega )$ such that $\Gamma ^{i,\mathrm{An}}=\phi ^{i}$. Note that by 13.1.8, $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +i^{-1}\omega )_{>0}$.

Write $\widehat{\Gamma }^ i\coloneqq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ^ i]_{\mathcal{I}}$ and $\widehat{\Gamma }\coloneqq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$. Applying proposition 13.4.23 to the decreasing sequence $(\widehat{\Gamma }^ i)_ i$ and then using the injectivity in 13.4.21, we get $\inf _ i\widehat{\Gamma }^ i=\widehat{\Gamma }$. Hence, by the componentwise definition of this infimum, for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \inf _{i\in \mathbb {N}}\widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau }=\widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau }. \]

Now proposition 3.1.26 and corollary 6.2.11 give

\[ \widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau }\xrightarrow {d_ S} \widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau }. \]

for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$.

By 13.4.27 again, each $\widehat{\Gamma }^{i}$ has non-trivial restriction to $Y$. By proposition 8.2.2, for any Kähler form $\omega '$ on $\widetilde{Y}$ satisfying $\omega '\geq \omega |_{\widetilde{Y}}$ we have

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau } \right)\xrightarrow {d_ S} \operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau }\right) \]

for any $\tau <(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma ))_{\max }$. Thanks to theorem 6.2.29, for every $c\operatorname{ord}_ F\in Y^{\operatorname{div}}$ and every such $\tau $,

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }_{\tau } \right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=\inf _{i\geq 1}\operatorname{Tr}_ Y \left( \widehat{\Gamma }^{i}_{\tau } \right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \]

Applying proposition A.2.10 to the Legendre transforms in $\tau $, and using again that $\mathcal{I}$-projection does not change analytification, we get

\[ \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=\inf _{i\geq 1}\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma ^ i)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \]

Since also $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\inf _ i \Gamma ^{i,\mathrm{An}}$, it suffices to prove (13.52) with $\Gamma ^ i$ in place of $\Gamma $.

In other words, we have reduced to the case where $\phi \in \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}_{\mathbb {Q}}(L^{\mathrm{An}})$ and $L$ is big.

Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ with $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}=\phi $. By 13.4.22, we can find a concave curve $(\Gamma '_{\tau })_{\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }}$ such that $\Gamma '_{\tau }$ is a generalized Fubini–Study metric for each $\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }$ and such that

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=P_{\theta }[\Gamma '_{\tau }]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\Gamma '_{\tau }]. \]

Here the second equality follows from example 7.1.6, since generalized Fubini–Study metrics have analytic singularities. With the convention that $\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(-\infty )=-\infty $ and $(-\infty )^{\mathrm{An}}\equiv -\infty $, it follows that for any $c\operatorname{ord}_ F\in \widetilde{Y}^{\operatorname{div}}$,

\[ \begin{aligned} \phi |_{Y^{\mathrm{An}}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\Gamma '_{\tau }{}^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\left(\Gamma '_{\tau }|_{\widetilde{Y}}\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma '_{\tau })^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma _{\tau })^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)+\tau \right)\\ =& \operatorname{Tr}_ Y(\Gamma )^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \end{aligned} \]

The third equality follows from example 8.1.10, since $\Gamma '_{\tau }$ has analytic singularities. The fourth equality follows from $\Gamma _{\tau }=P_{\theta }[\Gamma '_{\tau }]$, proposition 8.2.1 and corollary 6.1.9. It remains to justify the second line. Namely, we want to show that for any generalized Fubini–Study metric $\varphi $ whose restriction to $\widetilde{Y}$ is not identically $-\infty $, we have

13.58
\begin{equation} \varphi ^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F)=\left(\varphi |_{\widetilde{Y}}\right)^{\mathrm{An}}(c\operatorname{ord}_ F). \end{equation}

We can immediately reduce to the case where $\varphi $ is a Fubini–Study metric, and then to the case

\[ \varphi =\log |s|_{h_0}^2, \]

where $s$ is a holomorphic section of $L$, not vanishing identically on $Y$, in which case (13.58) is obvious. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.27. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and $Y\subseteq X$ be an irreducible analytic subset. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}(v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}})\neq -\infty $;

  2. $\Gamma ^{\mathrm{An}}|_{Y^{\mathrm{An}}}\not\equiv -\infty $;

  3. $\Gamma $ has a well-defined restriction to $Y$.

Here $v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}}$ denotes the trivial valuation of $\mathbb {C}(Y)$.

Proof. The equivalence between 1 and 2 is a simple consequence of the maximum principle [Lemma 1.4(i), BJ22].

To see the equivalence between 1 and 3, it suffices to observe that for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$,

\[ \varphi ^{\mathrm{An}}(v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}})= \begin{cases} -\infty ,& \textup{if }\nu (\varphi ,Y)>0;\\ 0,& \textup{if }\nu (\varphi ,Y)=0. \end{cases} \]

This completes the proof. $\square$
