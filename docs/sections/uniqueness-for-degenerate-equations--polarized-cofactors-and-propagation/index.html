14.22 Polarized cofactors and propagation9769545E981A

9769545E981A

Uniform ellipticity is sufficient for the propagation of an extremum, but it is not necessary: the directions in which the cofactor is positive may already connect enough points to force constancy. For $C^2$ potentials, the quadratic form $Q$ in (14.21.7) is continuous, and its polarization defines $Q^\sharp :T^*X\to TX$ by $\eta (Q^\sharp \xi )=Q(\xi ,\eta )$. The reachable set of a point consists of the endpoints of absolutely continuous curves starting there whose velocity lies in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $ almost everywhere. The control orbit of a point consists of the endpoints of finite concatenations of flow lines, starting there, of locally Lipschitz vector fields with values in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $.

A positive weight $h$ satisfying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(hT)=0$ gives an energy identity that controls $\mathrm{d}w$ everywhere. Without such a weight, a degenerate maximum principle gives propagation only from the extremal sets. This distinction accounts for the different density assumptions on reachable sets and on orbits in the following theorem.

Theorem 14.22.1.919310C73DBE Let $u,v\in C^2(X)$ satisfy $A,B\geq 0$ and $A^ n=B^ n$, and put $w=u-v$.

  1. If there is a continuous $h>0$ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(hT)=0$ in distributions, then

    24A83D889133 14.22.2
    \begin{equation} \mathrm{i}\, \partial w\wedge \bar\partial w\wedge T\equiv 0. \end{equation}

    It follows that $w$ is constant if the set where $T$ is strictly positive is dense, or if the reachable set of one point is dense.

  2. Without the weight hypothesis, the sets where $w$ attains its maximum and its minimum are invariant under the flow of every locally Lipschitz vector field with values in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $. If every control orbit is dense, then $w$ is constant. For smooth data it is enough that smooth vector fields with values in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $, for example the columns of the symbol $a$ in (14.22.10) below, together with their iterated Lie brackets, span $TX$ at every point.

14.22.3 Weighted energy1961884C0812

Testing the weighted cofactor against $w^2$ produces a nonnegative gradient integral. Only distributional $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closedness of $hT$ is needed: for a continuous $h>0$, the condition is

271FE8625215 14.22.4
\begin{equation} \int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \wedge hT=0\qquad (\varphi \in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)). \end{equation}

of 14.22.1(1). The product rule gives

A2BD70AF14EC 14.22.5
\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w^2)=2w\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w+2\mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w =2w\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi }\partial w\wedge \bar\partial w. \end{equation}

Approximate $w^2$ in $C^2$ by smooth functions $\varphi $ in (14.22.4). Since $hT$ is continuous and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$ by (14.20.1), this gives

A832621F5643 14.22.6
\begin{equation} 0=\frac1\pi \int _ X h\, \mathrm{i}\partial w\wedge \bar\partial w\wedge T. \end{equation}

The continuous integrand is nonnegative, so it vanishes identically, which is (14.22.2).

By (14.21.7), (14.22.2) says that $Q(\mathrm{d}w)=0$. For the nonnegative quadratic form $Q$, this implies $Q(\mathrm{d}w,\eta )=0$ for every $\eta $. Therefore

A81FFABB4E8D 14.22.7
\begin{equation} \mathrm{d}w_ x\bigl (Q_ x^\sharp \eta \bigr )=0\qquad (x\in X,\ \eta \in T_ x^*X). \end{equation}

By the chain rule and (14.22.7), $w$ is constant along every absolutely continuous curve whose velocity lies in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $ almost everywhere. Thus $w$ is constant on each reachable set, and by continuity one dense reachable set suffices. If instead $Q$ is positive definite on a dense set, (14.22.6) gives $\mathrm{d}w=0$ there, and hence everywhere. $\square$

The argument uses only the test function $w^2$. For general test functions, integration by parts introduces derivatives of $hT$; the condition $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(hT)=0$ alone does not make $L\varphi =(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \wedge T)/\mathrm{d}V$ a symmetric operator in divergence form.

14.22.8 The real symbol and exterior-ball propagation1FF2DD5C252C

To propagate an extremum without a weight, we write the linearized operator in real coordinates. Its symbol is continuous and semipositive. At a point where a ball outside the maximum set touches it, the normal direction must lie in the kernel of the symbol; this forces the maximum set to be invariant under the flows of vector fields with values in the range of the symbol.

In a holomorphic chart $z_ j=x_ j+\mathrm{i} y_ j$, let $C_{\rm cf}$ be the Hermitian matrix characterized by

04DC9E828E52 14.22.9
\begin{equation} \frac{\bigl (\mathrm{i}\sum H_{j\bar k}\mathrm{d}z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_ k\bigr )\wedge T}{\mathrm{d}V} =\operatorname {Tr}(C_{\rm cf}H) \end{equation}

for every Hermitian matrix $H=(H_{j\bar k})$. Positivity of mixed products makes $C_{\rm cf}$ positive semidefinite. Write $C_{\rm cf}=P_{\rm cf}+\mathrm{i} Q_{\rm cf}$, where $P_{\rm cf}$ is real symmetric and $Q_{\rm cf}$ is real skew-symmetric. In the real coordinate order $(x_1,\ldots ,x_ n,y_1,\ldots ,y_ n)$,

7A7369E544A8 14.22.10
\begin{equation} L\varphi :=\frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \wedge T}{\mathrm{d}V} =\operatorname {Tr}(aD^2\varphi ),\qquad a=\frac1{8\pi } \begin{pmatrix} P_{\rm cf} & Q_{\rm cf} \\ -Q_{\rm cf} & P_{\rm cf} \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation}

Indeed $\varphi _{j\bar k}=(\varphi _{x_ jx_ k}+\varphi _{y_ jy_ k} +\mathrm{i}\varphi _{x_ jy_ k}-\mathrm{i}\varphi _{y_ jx_ k})/4$; inserting this expression in (14.22.9) proves (14.22.10). For real coordinate vectors $\xi ,\eta \in \mathbb R^ n$, moreover,

\[ (\xi ,\eta )^ Ta(\xi ,\eta ) =\frac1{8\pi }(\xi -\mathrm{i}\eta )^*C_{\rm cf}(\xi -\mathrm{i}\eta )\geq 0. \]

Thus $a$ is a continuous positive semidefinite principal symbol, the operator $L$ has no first-order term in holomorphic coordinates, and the range of $a$ is that of $Q^\sharp $.

An exterior-ball argument propagates the maximum for this operator. Let $W\in C^2$ satisfy $LW\geq 0$ in a chart, suppose $W\leq M$, and put $K=\{ W=M\} $. If $z\in K$ is a point of $K$ nearest to a point $y\notin K$ of the chart, then

2692BD3333CA 14.22.11
\begin{equation} (y-z)^ Ta(z)(y-z)=0. \end{equation}

To prove this, suppose the left side is positive, set $r_0=|y-z|$, and shrink the tangent ball toward $z$: $y'=z+\theta (y-z)$, $R=\theta r_0$, with $0<\theta <1$ small. The closed ball $\overline{B(y',R)}$ lies in the chart and meets $K$ only at $z$. This follows from the triangle inequality; equality in $|k-y|\leq |k-y'|+|y'-y|\leq r_0$, for $k\in K$, forces $k=z$. For large $\alpha >0$, the function

\[ \psi (x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha R^2}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha |x-y'|^2} \]

satisfies

\[ L\psi =2\alpha \mathrm{e}^{-\alpha |x-y'|^2} \bigl (\operatorname {tr}a-2\alpha (x-y')^ Ta(x)(x-y')\bigr )<0 \]

in a neighborhood of $z$, because $z-y'$ is a nonzero multiple of $y-z$, so that $(z-y')^ Ta(z)(z-y')>0$ by assumption. Choose a sufficiently small $r<R$, and consider the lens $D=B(z,r)\cap B(y',R)$. On the part of its boundary lying on the sphere $\partial B(y',R)$, we have $\psi =0$ and $W-M\leq 0$. On the remaining compact boundary portion, $W-M<0$, since the tangent ball meets $K$ only at $z$. Hence, for sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$, $W-M\leq \varepsilon \psi $ on $\partial D$. At a positive interior maximum of $W-M-\varepsilon \psi $, the Hessian would be nonpositive, whereas $L(W-M-\varepsilon \psi )>0$; this is impossible since $a\geq 0$. Hence the inequality $W-M\leq \varepsilon \psi $ holds in $D$. Outside the ball $B(y',R)$ we have $\psi \geq 0$, so this inequality holds in a full neighborhood of $z$. Since equality holds at $z$, the function $W-M-\varepsilon \psi $ has a local maximum at $z$, and therefore

\[ 0\leq LW(z)\leq \varepsilon L\psi (z)<0, \]

proving (14.22.11).

Since $a\geq 0$, its range is the orthogonal complement of its kernel, and (14.22.11) implies that $y-z$ lies in this kernel. Consequently $(y-z)\cdot Z(z)=0$ for every vector field $Z$ with values in the range of $a$. If $Z$ is locally Lipschitz and $\gamma $ is one of its flow curves starting in $K$, let $d(t)$ denote the squared distance from $\gamma (t)$ to $K$ in a sufficiently small compact chart neighborhood. Shortening the time interval ensures that nearest points $z_ t$ lie in the chart interior. Write $D^+d(t)=\limsup _{h\downarrow 0}(d(t+h)-d(t))/h$ for the upper right derivative. Holding such a nearest point fixed when differentiating the distance from above gives

\begin{align*} D^+d(t)& \leq 2(\gamma (t)-z_ t)\cdot Z(\gamma (t))\\ & =2(\gamma (t)-z_ t)\cdot \bigl (Z(\gamma (t))-Z(z_ t)\bigr ) \leq 2\operatorname {Lip}(Z)d(t). \end{align*}

As $d(0)=0$, Gronwall's inequality gives $d(t)=0$. The same argument for $-Z$, followed by continuation, proves invariance under both signs of time and under finite concatenations of such flows.

of 14.22.1(2). Apply the preceding argument to $W=w$ and to $W=-w$ in each chart; both satisfy $LW=0$ by (14.20.1). The maximum set of $w$ is nonempty, closed, and contains every control orbit starting in it. If every orbit is dense, this maximum set is all of $X$, proving constancy. The argument for the minimum set is identical.

It remains to prove the bracket criterion. Suppose that a family of smooth vector fields with values in $\operatorname {im}Q^\sharp $ is bracket generating. At $p$, let $\mathcal D_ p$ be the span of the values of all original fields pushed forward by finite compositions of their local flows and inverse flows. This distribution contains the original fields and is invariant under their flows. Differentiation of pushed-forward fields along a flow shows that it contains their nested Lie brackets; finite-dimensional subspaces are closed, so the derivative remains in $\mathcal D_ p$. Induction and the Jacobi identity give every iterated bracket. The assumed rank condition therefore gives $\mathcal D_ p=T_ pX$.

Choose $2n$ such pushed-forward fields independent at $p$. The flow of each is a conjugate of an original flow and hence preserves its orbit. The map obtained by successively flowing for $2n$ small times has invertible derivative at zero. The inverse function theorem puts a neighborhood of $p$ in its orbit. All orbits are open, and their partition of connected $X$ has only one member. This proves the Hörmander-type sufficient condition; it requires neither a constant-rank hypothesis nor a smooth matrix square root of $a$. In particular, the columns of a smooth symbol $a$ may be used as the original vector fields. $\square$

For $C^{2,1}$ potentials, the columns of $a$ are locally Lipschitz, so the orbit criterion still applies to them. The bracket criterion requires smooth vector fields.

14.22.12 The unweighted energy defectB7138A0B3C69

Integration against $w^2$ also measures the obstruction to using the unweighted cofactor, that is, the case $h=1$. For smooth potentials, the error is exactly the pairing of $w^2$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T$. On a surface, the Gauduchon condition makes this error vanish for every pair of potentials; in dimension three, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T$ contains an additional term depending on the potentials.

Proposition 14.22.13.F563CACF5D7D Let $n\geq 2$, and let $u,v$ be smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions with $A^ n=B^ n=f\, \mathrm{d}V$ for a smooth function $f\geq 0$. Then

475D4376A313 14.22.14
\begin{equation} \int _ X \mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge T =\frac12\int _ X w^2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T. \end{equation}

If $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$ and $\{ f>0\} $ is dense in $X$, then $u-v$ is constant.

Proof. By (14.20.1), $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$. Integrating (14.22.5) against $T$, and applying Stokes' theorem twice to the left-hand side, yields

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w^2)\wedge T=\int _ Xw^2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T. \]

Substitution proves (14.22.14).

For the uniqueness assertion, apply 14.22.1(1) with $h=1$. At every point where $f>0$, the form $B$ is positive definite, so the term $B^{n-1}$ makes the cofactor $T$ strictly positive. The set where $T$ is strictly positive is therefore dense, as required. $\square$

Corollary 14.22.15.EAC3CF41BD99 Assume $n=2$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$. Let $f\geq 0$ be smooth, with a zero set of empty interior, and let $c>0$. Then any two smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic solutions of

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^2=cf\, \mathrm{d}V \]

normalized by $\sup _ Xu=0$ coincide.

Proof. Here $T=A+B$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$. The set $\{ f>0\} $ is dense, so the proposition, applied to the density $cf$, makes the difference of the two solutions constant, and the normalization makes this constant zero. $\square$

In dimension three, direct expansion gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=3\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)+3\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u+v). \]

The Gauduchon condition cancels the first term but need not cancel the second. For instance, take both potentials equal to the perturbation $sv$ in example 11.23.5. The common form $A=B$ is positive and satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(A^2)\ne 0$, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=3\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(A^2)\ne 0$. Thus even for two equal positive solution forms, the cofactor need not be $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed.
