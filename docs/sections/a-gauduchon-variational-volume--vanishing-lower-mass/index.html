12.19 Vanishing lower mass108F40B2DFAA

108F40B2DFAA

By (12.15.5), the lower mass $v_-(h)$ vanishes as soon as there are Gauduchon metrics $\eta $ whose degrees $\int _ Xh\wedge \eta ^{n-1}$ stay bounded while their volumes $\int _ X\eta ^ n$ tend to infinity. We construct such metrics on a compact threefold. Writing $\Gamma =\eta ^2$, the volume of $\eta $ is a determinant integral of the coefficient matrix of the positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed form $\Gamma $. The construction first concentrates the trace of a normalized form $\Gamma $ in one direction near a hypersurface, and then adds an average of compact rational curves that fills the two complementary directions. The degree stays bounded, while the determinant, and hence the volume of the positive root, becomes large. The smooth equation then converts these metrics into potentials whose masses tend to zero.

Theorem 12.19.1.38D9206B1A2F There is a connected compact complex threefold $X$ such that, for every Hermitian form $h$ on $X$, there are smooth real functions $u_ j$ satisfying

\[ h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j>0,\qquad \sup _ Xu_ j=0,\qquad \int _ X(h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^3\longrightarrow 0. \]

In particular, $v_-(h)=0$ for every Hermitian form $h$ on this manifold.

12.19.2 The elliptic quotient1DD06E8B2862

Write points of $\mathbb P^3$ as $[z:w]$, with $z,w\in \mathbb C^2$, and set

\[ \Omega =\{ [z:w]:z\ne 0,\ w\ne 0\} ,\qquad q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi },\qquad g[z:w]=[z:qw]. \]

Thus $g$ is an automorphism of $\Omega $; let $\langle g\rangle $ be the cyclic group it generates. This group acts freely. Indeed, if $g^ k$ fixed a point, the same scalar would have to equal $1$ on the nonzero block $z$ and $q^ k$ on the nonzero block $w$, which forces $k=0$.

The function

\[ t=\log \frac{\| w\| ^2}{\| z\| ^2} \]

is well defined on $\Omega $ and satisfies $t\circ g=t+4\pi $. Consequently the action is properly discontinuous: $t$ is bounded on compact subsets, so only finitely many translates of one compact set can meet another. Moreover, the strip $\{ 0\leq t\leq 4\pi \} $ is compact. In homogeneous coordinates of total norm one, its defining inequalities bound both blocks away from zero, so it is a closed subset of $\mathbb P^3$ contained in $\Omega $. Since the strip meets every orbit,

\[ X=\Omega /\langle g\rangle \]

is a connected compact complex threefold.

The map $[z:w]\mapsto ([z],[w])$ induces a holomorphic submersion $X\to \mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$. Using a logarithm of the scalar relating two points with the same image, one sees that its fibers are the elliptic curves $E=\mathbb C/(2\pi \mathbb Z+2\pi \mathrm{i}\mathbb Z)$. Let $\alpha ,\beta $ be the pullbacks of the Fubini–Study forms of the two factors, each normalized to have integral one. Although $t$ does not descend to $X$, its differentials $\mathrm dt$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t$ do. The form

444A78EADE47 12.19.3
\begin{equation} \omega =\alpha +\beta +\mathrm{d}t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t \end{equation}

is Hermitian: $\alpha +\beta $ is semipositive with kernel the vertical line, while $\mathrm{d}t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t$ is semipositive and strictly positive on the vertical line.

On the open set $\{ z_0w_0\ne 0\} $ we use the coordinates

\[ x=\frac{z_1}{z_0},\qquad y=\frac{w_1}{w_0},\qquad \zeta =\log \frac{w_0}{z_0}\in E. \]

Near $0\in E$, the last coordinate is represented by an ordinary complex number. For any one of these coordinates, denoted by $v$, put $\sigma _ v=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar v$. Choose a smooth probability density $p$ relative to $\sigma _ v$ that is positive on $\{ |v|<1\} $ and vanishes on $\{ |v|\geq 1\} $. Choose also a smooth function $\chi _0$ with values in $[0,1]$, supported in $\{ |v|<1\} $ and equal to one on $\{ |v|\leq 1/2\} $, and write $\chi (x,y)=\chi _0(x)\chi _0(y)$. For $0<\delta <1/100$ define

\[ B_\delta =\chi (x,y)\delta ^{-2}p(\zeta /\delta )\sigma _\zeta , \]

extended by zero. This is a smooth semipositive $(1,1)$-form on $X$, because the chosen functions are smooth with compact support in their coordinate domains. As $\delta \downarrow 0$ it concentrates on the hypersurface $\{ \zeta =0\} $.

The first step shows that normalized positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(2,2)$-forms can pair arbitrarily strongly with $B_\delta $.

Lemma 12.19.4.669B72559DF9 For $\delta >0$ put

\[ C_\delta =\sup \left\{ \int _ XB_\delta \wedge \Theta : \Theta >0,\ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Theta =0,\ \int _ X\omega \wedge \Theta =1\right\} , \]

the supremum being taken over smooth real $(2,2)$-forms $\Theta $. These numbers are finite and tend to infinity as $\delta \downarrow 0$.

Proof. For fixed $\delta $, choose a constant $M_\delta >0$ with $B_\delta \leq M_\delta \omega $; this shows that $C_\delta \leq M_\delta $. Suppose instead that $C_{\delta _ l}\leq C$ along a sequence $\delta _ l\to 0$. Then $\int _ X(C\omega -B_{\delta _ l})\wedge \Theta \geq 0$ for every admissible $\Theta $, and in particular for the squares of Gauduchon metrics. By theorem 11.6.1, there are quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $v_ l$ such that

\[ C\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ l\geq B_{\delta _ l},\qquad \sup _ Xv_ l=0. \]

By theorem 5.25.2, a subsequence converges in $L^1$ to a $C\omega $-plurisubharmonic function $v$, not identically $-\infty $. Since $\delta ^{-2}p(\zeta /\delta )\sigma _\zeta $ is an approximate identity in the variable $\zeta $,

\[ B_{\delta _ l}\rightharpoonup \chi [\zeta =0],\qquad C\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq \chi [\zeta =0]. \]

On a coordinate ball where $\chi =1$, add to $v$ a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function whose complex Hessian dominates $C\omega $. The resulting plurisubharmonic function has a current that dominates $[\zeta =0]$, so its Lelong number is at least one at every point of an open subset of this hypersurface. Smooth corrections do not change Lelong numbers, so the same holds for $v$. Siu's analyticity theorem, recalled in 5.34.2, shows that $Z=\{ \nu (v,\cdot )\geq 1\} $ is an analytic subset of $X$. It is a proper subset because $v$ is not identically $-\infty $.

Let $\widetilde Z$ be the inverse image of $Z$ in $\Omega $. It contains an open subset of $H_0\cap \Omega $, where $H_0=\{ w_0=z_0\} $. This surface is a projective plane with two points removed, so it is connected and irreducible, and the identity principle gives $H_0\cap \Omega \subset \widetilde Z$. Since $\widetilde Z$ is invariant under $g$, also

\[ H_ k\cap \Omega \subset \widetilde Z,\qquad H_ k=\{ w_0=q^ kz_0\} , \qquad k\in \mathbb Z. \]

Now use the chart $z_1=1$ near the point $[0:1:1:0]\in \Omega $. There $H_ k$ is the graph $z_0=q^{-k}w_0$. For each fixed $(w_0,w_1)$ near $(1,0)$, every local holomorphic defining function of $\widetilde Z$ vanishes at the distinct values $z_0=q^{-k}w_0$, which tend to zero as $k\to +\infty $. The one-variable identity theorem makes each defining function vanish identically on a polydisk. Thus $\widetilde Z$ contains a nonempty open subset of the connected manifold $\Omega $, contradicting properness. Hence no subsequence of $C_\delta $ stays bounded as $\delta \downarrow 0$. $\square$

By the lemma we can choose $\delta _ j\downarrow 0$, each smaller than half its predecessor, and then, by the definition of the supremum, smooth strictly positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(2,2)$-forms $\Theta _ j$ with

388DD0213040 12.19.5
\begin{equation} \int _ X\omega \wedge \Theta _ j=1,\qquad J_ j=\int _ XB_{\delta _ j}\wedge \Theta _ j>j^4. \end{equation}

12.19.6 Averaging the trace and compact curves57556869C08A

A large pairing with $B_{\delta _ j}$ means that $\Theta _ j$ has a large coefficient in one direction, but possibly only on a small set. Averaging by translations spreads this coefficient over a whole product region of size comparable to $\delta _ j$ in the vertical direction. A second average, this time of compact rational curves, fills the two complementary directions, which is needed for a lower bound on a determinant. Fix $j$ and write $\delta =\delta _ j$.

For complex parameters $a,b,\tau $, the automorphisms

\[ T_{a,b,\tau }[z_0:z_1:w_0:w_1] =[z_0:z_1+az_0:\mathrm{e}^{\tau }w_0: \mathrm{e}^{\tau }(w_1+bw_0)] \]

of $\Omega $ commute with $g$, and in the product chart they act by $(x,y,\zeta )\mapsto (x+a,y+b,\zeta +\tau )$. Average $T_{a,b,\tau }^*\Theta _ j$ against the probability density

\[ 3^{-4}p(a/3)p(b/3)(3\delta )^{-2}p(\tau /(3\delta )) \sigma _ a\wedge \sigma _ b\wedge \sigma _\tau , \]

and denote the result by $\widehat\Theta _ j$. It is smooth, strictly positive and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed. The parameters range in a fixed compact set of automorphisms, so $(T_{a,b,\tau }^{-1})^*\omega \leq C\omega $ with one constant $C$, and a change of variables gives

E994975CCA57 12.19.7
\begin{equation} \int _ X\omega \wedge \widehat\Theta _ j\leq C. \end{equation}

Let $F_ j$ be the coefficient of $\sigma _ x\wedge \sigma _ y$ in the restriction of $\Theta _ j$ to the slices $\{ \zeta =\text{constant}\} $, and let $\widehat F_ j$ be the corresponding coefficient of $\widehat\Theta _ j$. Positivity gives $F_ j\geq 0$, and since the translations preserve $\sigma _ x\wedge \sigma _ y$, $\widehat F_ j$ is an average of translates of $F_ j$. At a point of

\[ V_ j=\{ |x|,|y|<1/4,\ |\zeta |<\delta /4\} , \]

every translation carrying it into $\operatorname {supp}B_\delta $ has $|a|,|b|\leq 5/4$ and $|\tau |\leq 5\delta /4$. On this range the first two averaging factors are bounded below by a positive constant, and the third by a positive constant times $\delta ^{-2}$. On the other hand, $\chi \leq 1$ and $p$ is bounded, so the number $J_ j$ of (12.19.5) is at most a constant times $\delta ^{-2}$ times the integral of $F_ j$ over $\operatorname {supp}B_\delta $. Comparing the two integrals gives

79DFF8AF8DCA 12.19.8
\begin{equation} \widehat F_ j\geq cJ_ j\quad \hbox{on }V_ j, \end{equation}

with $c>0$ independent of $j$.

The second average fills the two horizontal directions. Put

\[ f(a,b)=p(4(a-1))p(b/2),\qquad \rho _ j(\tau )=4\delta ^{-2}p(2\tau /\delta ). \]

For parameters in the supports of these functions and $|u|\leq 1/16$, consider the holomorphic maps $\iota _{\tau ,u,a,b}:\mathbb P^1\to X$ defined by

BE92C16DAFFD 12.19.9
\begin{equation} \iota _{\tau ,u,a,b}[z_0:z_1] =[z_0:z_1:\mathrm{e}^{\tau }(z_0+uz_1): \mathrm{e}^{\tau }(az_1+bz_0)]\in X. \end{equation}

For $0<\varepsilon \leq 1/16$, average their integration currents against

\[ \rho _ j(\tau )f(a,b)\varepsilon ^{-2}p(u/\varepsilon ) \sigma _\tau \wedge \sigma _ a\wedge \sigma _ b\wedge \sigma _ u, \]

and call the result $T_{j,\varepsilon }$. Define $T_{j,0}$ in the same way, but with $u=0$ and without the integral over $u$.

Lemma 12.19.10.40DB573E7C1E For $\varepsilon >0$, the form $T_{j,\varepsilon }$ is smooth on $X$, semipositive and closed, and

\[ \int _ X\omega \wedge T_{j,\varepsilon }\leq C' \]

with $C'$ independent of $j$ and $\varepsilon $. For each fixed $j$, $T_{j,\varepsilon }$ converges smoothly to $T_{j,0}$ on compact subsets of the product chart as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. In this chart

3DC335D33654 12.19.11
\begin{equation} T_{j,0}=\rho _ j(\zeta )\sigma _\zeta \wedge K_ f,\qquad K_ f=\int _\mathbb Cf(a,y-ax)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi } (\mathrm{d}y-a\, \mathrm{d}x)\wedge (\mathrm{d}\bar y-\bar a\, \mathrm{d}\bar x)\, \sigma _ a, \end{equation}

and on $\{ |x|,|y|\leq 1/4\} $ the form $K_ f$ is bounded below by a positive multiple of $\sigma _ x+\sigma _ y$.

Proof. On the parameter supports, $|a-bu|\geq 3/4-2/16=5/8$. Hence the matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1& u\\ b& a\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is uniformly invertible, and the projective line (12.19.9) lies in $\Omega $. Distinct deck translates of this line are disjoint: equality at a point fixes $[z_0:z_1]$, and invertibility of the matrix then forces the deck scalar to be one. Thus each map is an embedding onto a compact rational curve in $X$.

The evaluation map from $\mathbb P^1$ times the open parameter domain to $X$ is a holomorphic submersion. Indeed, after fixing a projective gauge, variations of $u$ and $a$ span the two $w$-coordinates at points with $z_1\ne 0$, variations of $\tau $ and $b$ span them at points with $z_1=0$, and the coordinate on $\mathbb P^1$ supplies the remaining direction. For $\varepsilon >0$, the average is the integral along the fibers of this submersion of a smooth compactly supported parameter density, which proves smoothness. As an average of closed positive integration currents, $T_{j,\varepsilon }$ is closed and semipositive.

All curve parameters lie in one compact subset of the domain where the matrix is invertible, so the $\omega $-areas of the curves are uniformly bounded. The weights in $\tau $ and $u$ have mass one, and the weight in $(a,b)$ has a fixed finite mass. This proves the degree bound.

When $u=0$, the curve is given in the product chart by $\zeta =\tau $ and $y=ax+b$. Integrating in $b$ and $\tau $ yields (12.19.11); the $(1,1)$-factor there is the integration current of the graph of $y=ax+b$. On $\{ |x|,|y|\leq 1/4\} $, the density $f(a,y-ax)$ is bounded below by a positive constant for $a$ in a fixed small disk about $1$. The covectors $\mathrm{d}y-a\, \mathrm{d}x$, for $a$ in that disk, have no common nonzero annihilator, so integrating their squared absolute values and using compactness of the unit sphere in the $(x,y)$-directions gives the asserted lower bound.

For general $u$, the curve is given by

\[ \zeta =\tau +\log (1+ux),\qquad y=\frac{ax+b}{1+ux}. \]

On a fixed compact subset of the chart, $1+ux$ stays away from zero for all sufficiently small $u$. Solving for the parameters gives $\tau =\zeta -\log (1+ux)$ and $b=(1+ux)y-ax$. Regarding $\rho _ j$ as a smooth function on $E$, this change of variables also applies near the boundary of its support. The Jacobians, the density coefficients and any fixed number of their derivatives depend smoothly on $u$. Averaging against the approximate identity in $u$ therefore gives local $C^\infty $ convergence. This convergence is needed only for each fixed $j$; it is not uniform in $j$. $\square$

12.19.12 The volume of the positive root92006A543CD2

The two averages contribute complementary blocks of the coefficient matrix of a $(2,2)$-form: the translated forms give a large vertical entry, and the curves give a positive horizontal block. A Schur-complement estimate combines them into a lower bound for the volume of the positive root. In the coframe $(x,y,\zeta )$ put $\mathrm{d}V_0=\sigma _ x\wedge \sigma _ y\wedge \sigma _\zeta $. For a real $(1,1)$-form $\xi $, let $H_\xi $ be its coefficient matrix in this normalization. A real $(2,2)$-form $\Theta $ corresponds to a Hermitian matrix $Q_\Theta $, which we call its cofactor matrix, by

\[ \xi \wedge \Theta =\operatorname {tr}(H_\xi Q_\Theta )\, \mathrm{d}V_0. \]

Strict positivity of $\Theta $ is equivalent to $Q_\Theta >0$.

If $\eta ^2=\Theta $ and $H=H_\eta $, expanding the wedge products gives

\[ Q_\Theta =2(\det H)H^{-1},\qquad \eta ^3=\frac{3}{\sqrt2}(\det Q_\Theta )^{1/2}\, \mathrm{d}V_0. \]

Conversely, these formulas uniquely recover a smooth positive $H$ from $Q_\Theta >0$; this is the three-dimensional case of the root construction used in the proof of theorem 11.6.1. We write $\Theta ^{3/2}=\eta ^3$.

Lemma 12.19.13.BD9C00A32F59 There are smooth strictly positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(2,2)$-forms $\Gamma _ j$ such that

\[ \int _ X\omega \wedge \Gamma _ j\leq C_2,\qquad R_ j=\int _ X\Gamma _ j^{3/2}\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

Proof. Write the cofactor matrix of $\widehat\Theta _ j$ in block form, with a $2\times 2$ Hermitian block $A_ j$ in the $(x,y)$-directions and a column $\kappa _ j\in \mathbb C^2$:

\[ Q_ j=\begin{pmatrix} A_ j & \kappa _ j \\ \kappa _ j^* & \widehat F_ j \end{pmatrix}. \]

The cofactor matrix of $T_{j,0}$ has the form $\operatorname {diag}(B_ j,0)$. Let $I_2$ denote the $2\times 2$ identity matrix. The lower bound for $K_ f$ and the definition of $\rho _ j$ give a constant $c_0>0$ such that $B_ j\geq c_0\delta _ j^{-2}I_2$ on $V_ j$. Since $Q_ j>0$, the Schur complement $A_ j-\kappa _ j\kappa _ j^*/\widehat F_ j$ is positive definite, and

\[ \det \bigl (Q_ j+\operatorname {diag}(B_ j,0)\bigr ) =\widehat F_ j\det \left(B_ j+A_ j- \frac{\kappa _ j\kappa _ j^*}{\widehat F_ j}\right) \geq \widehat F_ j\det B_ j, \]

by monotonicity of the determinant on positive Hermitian matrices. Combining the root formula with (12.19.8), we obtain

\[ (\widehat\Theta _ j+T_{j,0})^{3/2} \geq c\delta _ j^{-2}\sqrt{J_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V_0 \quad \hbox{on }V_ j. \]

The smaller region $W_ j=\{ |x|,|y|<1/8,\ |\zeta |<\delta _ j/8\} $ has $\mathrm{d}V_0$-volume equal to a fixed positive constant times $\delta _ j^2$. Consequently

\[ I_ j=\int _{W_ j}(\widehat\Theta _ j+T_{j,0})^{3/2} \geq c_1\sqrt{J_ j}>c_1j^2. \]

For this fixed $j$, lemma 12.19.10 gives smooth convergence of $T_{j,\varepsilon }$ to $T_{j,0}$ on the closure of $W_ j$. The cofactor matrix of $\widehat\Theta _ j+T_{j,0}$ is positive definite there, so the integrand of the root volume depends continuously on these smooth coefficients. We can therefore choose $0<\varepsilon _ j\leq 1/16$ so small that

\[ \int _{W_ j}(\widehat\Theta _ j+T_{j,\varepsilon _ j})^{3/2} \geq I_ j/2. \]

Set $\Gamma _ j=\widehat\Theta _ j+T_{j,\varepsilon _ j}$. It is smooth, strictly positive and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed. Its degree is bounded by (12.19.7) and lemma 12.19.10, whereas its root volume is at least $c_1j^2/2$. $\square$

The potentials $u_ j$ are smooth, hence bounded, so $v_-(h)=0$. $\square$

12.19.14 Consequences for dimension and equations8E3D46779F5F

The example occurs in the smallest possible dimension: on a surface, comparison with a Gauduchon metric gives positive lower mass. Products with projective spaces carry the threefold degeneration to every higher dimension, and a product with the Hopf threefold produces, in addition, unbounded upper mass.

Corollary 12.19.15.435BD74C27A9 Every Hermitian form on a compact complex manifold of dimension at most two has positive lower mass. In every dimension $n\geq 3$ there is a connected compact complex manifold on which every Hermitian form has zero lower mass.

Proof. In dimension one the mass is $\int _ Xh>0$, and the surface case is corollary 12.18.1. In higher dimensions we use the monotonicity and the homogeneity of degree $n$ of $v_-$, which follow directly from (12.15.5), to pass from a split metric to every Hermitian metric.

For $n=3$ this is theorem 12.19.1. For $n>3$, take the product of the threefold $X$ with $\mathbb P^{n-3}$, let $\kappa $ be a Fubini–Study form on the projective factor, and consider the split metric $h_0=\omega +\kappa $, with pullbacks understood. For the potentials $u_ j$ given by the theorem for the background $\omega $,

\[ \int _{X\times \mathbb P^{n-3}}(h_0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^ n =\binom n3\left(\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^3\right) \left(\int _{\mathbb P^{n-3}}\kappa ^{n-3}\right) \longrightarrow 0. \]

For another Hermitian form $h$, choose $A$ with $h\leq Ah_0$. Monotonicity and homogeneity give $v_-(h)\leq A^ n v_-(h_0)=0$. $\square$

Corollary 12.19.16.940F7BB9270C In every dimension $n\geq 6$ there is a connected compact complex manifold with

\[ v_-(h)=0,\qquad v_+(h)=+\infty \]

for every Hermitian form $h$.

Proof. Take the product of the threefold above with the Hopf threefold of section 23.1, with a split metric. Potentials pulled back from the first factor make the masses tend to zero, while the smooth potentials of theorem 23.1.1, pulled back from the second factor, make them tend to infinity. In each case the product integral is $\binom 63$ times the product of the two factor masses. The metric comparison in the preceding proof handles the lower mass for every Hermitian form, and the metric independence in proposition 13.14.15 handles the upper mass. Products with projective spaces give every higher dimension by the same expansion of wedge powers. $\square$

Finally, fix a smooth positive probability measure $\mu $ on the threefold $X$. The smooth equation still gives $\mathcal V_\mu (h)>0$ for each Hermitian form $h$. With $\mathscr M$ and $\lambda _*$ as in subsection 12.15.3, theorem 12.15.4 gives

\[ \inf _{\nu \in \mathscr M}\mathcal V_\nu (h)=0, \qquad \lambda _*(h)=0. \]

Thus even a smooth strictly positive background can have vanishing infimum over densities. For the metrics $\eta _ j$ and numbers $R_ j,P_ j$ in the proof of theorem 12.19.1, the measures $\mu _ j=\eta _ j^3/R_ j$ satisfy $\mathcal V_{\mu _ j}(h)\leq P_ j^3/R_ j^2\to 0$. Hence the densities $\mu _ j/\mu $ cannot be uniformly bounded above: a bound $\mu _ j\leq M\mu $ would give $\mathcal V_{\mu _ j}(h)\geq M^{-1}\mathcal V_\mu (h)>0$, by the comparison of denominators in the proof of proposition 12.15.1.

The example also shows that the endpoint estimate in theorem 12.9.2 fails for the full family of normalized Gauduchon currents: the supremum of the $L^{3/2}$ norms of their determinant densities is infinite, although every subcritical $L^ q$ norm, $1<q<3/2$, remains uniformly bounded by theorem 12.9.1. For the same fixed background, theorem 12.9.7, corollary 12.9.10 give uniform $L^\infty $ bounds for the exact maximizers and their adjoint probabilities in a small $C^{2,\alpha }$ family of smooth densities. A larger sufficient family is given by theorem 12.9.18: uniformly comparable smooth solution forms yield uniform bounds for both densities, without derivative bounds on the varying Hessian. Endpoint estimates for arbitrary bounded families of continuous densities remain open.

12.19.17 A balanced metric and a finite upper massDEA97C6D08EB

Although every Hermitian form on the threefold of theorem 12.19.1 has zero lower mass, the metric $\omega $ of (12.19.3) is balanced and has finite upper mass. A direct calculation expresses the loss of mass under a potential as a vertical Dirichlet energy along the elliptic fibers. The same calculation shows that the degeneration cannot be realized by potentials that are the sum of a periodic function of $t$ and a function on the base: their masses stay above $8$. Put $D=\mathrm{d}t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t$, so that $\omega =\alpha +\beta +D$.

Proposition 12.19.18.94417AD14985 On the threefold $X$ of theorem 12.19.1, the Hermitian form $\omega =\alpha +\beta +D$ satisfies

\[ \mathrm{d}(\omega ^2)=0,\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =2\alpha \wedge \beta \geq 0, \qquad \int _ X\omega ^3=24\pi . \]

For every smooth real function $u$,

9E38044EBFA0 12.19.19
\begin{equation} \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3 =24\pi -6\int _ X\mathrm{d}u\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u\wedge \alpha \wedge \beta . \end{equation}

Consequently $v_+(\omega )=24\pi $. In particular, $X$ has the bounded mass property, while $v_-(h)=0$ for every Hermitian form $h$ on $X$.

Proof. Although $t$ is defined only on the covering space $\Omega $, both $\mathrm{d}t$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t$ descend to $X$. The Fubini–Study potentials $\log \| z\| ^2$ and $\log \| w\| ^2$ give $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}t=\beta -\alpha $. The forms $\alpha ,\beta $ are closed, and $\alpha ^2=\beta ^2=0$ since each is pulled back from a curve. As $D^2=0$, we obtain

\[ \mathrm{d}D=-\mathrm{d}t\wedge (\beta -\alpha ),\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}D=-(\beta -\alpha )^2=2\alpha \wedge \beta , \]
\[ \mathrm{d}(\omega ^2) =-2(\alpha +\beta +D)\wedge \mathrm{d}t\wedge (\beta -\alpha )=0. \]

In the last identity, the term involving $D$ contains $\mathrm{d}t$ twice, and $(\alpha +\beta )\wedge (\beta -\alpha )=0$. Thus $\omega $ is balanced, hence Gauduchon, and the formula for $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $ follows from that for $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}D$.

On a fiber, write $\zeta =a+\mathrm{i}b$, where both real periods are $2\pi $. There $t=2a$ plus a constant, so

\[ D|_ E=\frac1\pi \, \mathrm{d}a\wedge \mathrm{d}b, \qquad \int _ ED=4\pi . \]

Since $\omega ^3=6\alpha \wedge \beta \wedge D$, fiber integration and the normalization of the Fubini–Study forms give $\int _ X\omega ^3=24\pi $.

The identity (12.16.4) for Gauduchon metrics on threefolds, with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =2\alpha \wedge \beta $, gives (12.19.19). Its last integral is nonnegative. Thus every smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic potential has mass at most $24\pi $, and the constant potential attains this value. Regularization of bounded potentials, as in the proof of theorem 12.15.4, gives the same supremum over bounded potentials, so $v_+(\omega )=24\pi $. The metric independence in proposition 13.14.15 then gives the bounded mass property of $X$. The assertion about the lower mass is theorem 12.19.1. $\square$

For an admissible smooth $u$, equality in the upper-mass bound holds precisely when $u$ is constant along every elliptic fiber. Indeed, $\alpha \wedge \beta $ is a positive volume form in the base directions, so the nonnegative integral in (12.19.19) vanishes exactly when the derivative of $u$ along the fibers vanishes. The fibers are connected, and smooth local sections then show that $u$ is the pullback of a smooth function on $\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$. For the potentials with masses tending to zero in theorem 12.19.1, the same integral instead tends to $4\pi $.

A function $f(t)$ descends to $X$ when $f$ is $4\pi $-periodic, since $t\circ g=t+4\pi $. Even after adding an arbitrary potential pulled back from the base, such functions leave a positive mass gap. The sharp gap follows from the bounds on the first two derivatives of $f$ imposed by positivity.

Proposition 12.19.20.3290D6E85075 Let $\pi :X\to B=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$ be the elliptic fibration above, and put $L=4\pi $. Consider the smooth potentials

\[ u=f(t)+\pi ^*v,\qquad \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0, \]

where $f:\mathbb R\to \mathbb R$ is smooth and $L$-periodic and $v\in C^\infty (B,\mathbb R)$. The infimum of $\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3$ over these potentials is $8$. Every such mass is strictly greater than $8$, and the infimum is approached already with $v=0$.

Proof. Write $p=f'$, an $L$-periodic function with mean zero. The chain rule gives

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u =(1-p)\alpha +(1+p)\beta +\pi ^*\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v+(1+p')D. \]

Positivity on the vertical line implies $p'>-1$. On the complex plane $\ker \partial t$, the form $D$ vanishes and the projection to the base is an isomorphism. For each value of $t$ modulo $L$, every base point has a point above it with that value. Hence, writing $\alpha _ B,\beta _ B$ for the two Fubini–Study forms on $B$, the form $(1-p(s))\alpha _ B+(1+p(s))\beta _ B+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ is positive on $B$ for every $s$. Integration over the two rulings gives $1-p>0$ and $1+p>0$. Thus $|p|<1$ and $p'>-1$; when $v=0$, these inequalities also suffice for strict positivity.

In a logarithmic fiber coordinate $x+\mathrm{i}y$, both real periods are $2\pi $, $t=2x$ plus a constant, and $D|_ E=\pi ^{-1}\mathrm{d}x\wedge \mathrm{d}y$. Hence $\int _ E F(t)D=\int _0^ L F(s)\, \mathrm{d}s$ for every $L$-periodic function $F$. Only the derivative of $u$ along the fibers contributes to (12.19.19), and it equals $p(t)$ times the derivative of $t$. Fiber integration therefore gives

87EE58E6037C 12.19.21
\begin{equation} \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3 =6\left(L-\int _0^ L p(s)^2\, \mathrm{d}s\right). \end{equation}

We now bound $\int _0^ Lp^2$. Suppose first that $p\not\equiv 0$, and put $m_+=\max p>0$, $m_-=-\min p>0$ and $r=m_++m_-<2$. The function $G(s)=(m_+-s)(s+m_-)$ is nonnegative on $[-m_-,m_+]$. Choose an interval $I$ of length at most $L$ that runs from a maximum point of $p$ forward to the next minimum point. Since $p'\geq -1$, we have $G(p)\geq -G(p)p'$. Using that $p$ has mean zero, we obtain

\[ \begin{split} m_+m_-L-\int _0^ L p^2\, \mathrm{d}s & =\int _0^ L G(p)\, \mathrm{d}s \geq -\int _ I G(p)p'\, \mathrm{d}s\\ & =\int _{-m_-}^{m_+}G(s)\, \mathrm{d}s=\frac{r^3}{6}. \end{split} \]

As $m_+m_-\leq r^2/4$, it follows that

\[ \int _0^ L p^2\, \mathrm{d}s \leq \frac{Lr^2}{4}-\frac{r^3}{6}<L-\frac43, \]

where the last inequality holds because the middle expression is strictly increasing in $r\in (0,2]$, as $L>2$. The inequality $\int _0^ Lp^2<L-4/3$ holds trivially when $p=0$. Equation (12.19.21) now gives the strict lower bound $8$.

To prove sharpness, put $a=(L-2)/2$ and define an $L$-periodic function $p_0$ by

\[ p_0(s)= \begin{cases} 1,& 0\leq s<a,\\ 1-(s-a),& a\leq s<a+2,\\ -1,& a+2\leq s<L. \end{cases} \]

It has mean zero, $|p_0|\leq 1$ and $\int _0^ L p_0^2\, \mathrm{d}s=L-4/3$. Its distributional derivative is $-1$ on the descending interval and zero on the two constant intervals, and it has a positive atom where the period closes up. Thus $p_0'\geq -1$ as distributions on the circle. Let $\rho _\varepsilon $ be a nonnegative smooth periodic approximate identity of integral one, and set $p_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )(p_0*\rho _\varepsilon )$ for $0<\varepsilon <1$. Then $p_\varepsilon $ is smooth, has mean zero, and

\[ |p_\varepsilon |\leq 1-\varepsilon , \qquad p_\varepsilon '\geq -1+\varepsilon , \qquad p_\varepsilon \longrightarrow p_0\quad \hbox{in }L^2. \]

Its primitive $f_\varepsilon $ is smooth and periodic, so $u_\varepsilon =f_\varepsilon (t)$ is admissible. By (12.19.21), its mass tends to $8$. $\square$

Proposition 12.19.22.69BB70EA26D9 The threefold $X$ admits neither a pluriclosed Hermitian metric nor a closed Kähler current.

Proof. By proposition 12.19.18, the nonzero smooth semipositive $(2,2)$-form $\alpha \wedge \beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega /2)$ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact. If a Hermitian metric $\eta $ satisfied $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta =0$, Stokes' theorem would give

\[ 0<\int _ X\eta \wedge \alpha \wedge \beta =\frac12\int _ X\eta \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =\frac12\int _ X\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta =0, \]

a contradiction. The first inequality holds because $\eta $ is positive in the vertical direction at every point.

Similarly, if a closed positive current $T$ satisfied $T\geq \varepsilon \omega $ for some $\varepsilon >0$, positivity and Stokes' theorem for currents would give

\[ 0<\varepsilon \int _ X\omega \wedge \alpha \wedge \beta \leq \int _ XT\wedge \alpha \wedge \beta =\frac12\int _ X\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0. \]

Hence there is no closed Kähler current. $\square$

Since a compact complex manifold carries a closed Kähler current exactly when it belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$, as recalled in section 24.4, the threefold $X$ lies outside this class. The example shows that a balanced metric does not guarantee positive lower mass, even together with bounded upper mass. The Hopf threefold, in contrast, has positive lower mass and infinite upper mass, so the two properties are logically independent, and the products in corollary 12.19.16 show that both can fail at once. In particular, in a theorem asserting solvability for every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure, bounded mass alone cannot replace the hypothesis of positive lower mass.
