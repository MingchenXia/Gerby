11.2 Non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measures

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

6 Non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Throughout this chapter, $\omega $ is a Hermitian form on $X$ and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive probability volume form used for $L^1$ norms and integrals. As in section 1.7, we write $\theta _ v=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$, and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$ when $v$ is bounded; convergence in capacity refers to the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ of section 1.8.

For an unbounded $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function $u$, the Bedford–Taylor products of proposition 1.7.1 are not defined. On the set $\{ u>-t\} $, however, $u$ coincides with its bounded truncation $\max (u,-t)$, and since bounded products are plurifinely local, the restricted measures are compatible as $t$ increases. Their limit is a measure on the complement of the pole set, the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of $u$. For a general smooth background $\theta $, the truncation need not be $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, so positivity of the restricted products requires a local argument. The total mass of the limit may also be infinite. For a Hermitian background, finiteness of this mass for every potential is equivalent to a uniform bound on the Monge–Ampère masses of bounded potentials, the bounded mass property. When the property fails, we construct a potential of infinite mass whose multiplier ideals are all trivial; its complex Hessian even has integrable coefficients, although its determinant is not integrable.

6.1 Truncation and non-pluripolar measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For $t>0$, put

\[ u_ t=\max (u,-t),\qquad D_ t=\{ u>-t\} . \]

Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta $. Then $u_ t$ is bounded and $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic, so proposition 1.7.1 defines the product $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n$ as a signed measure. It need not be positive on $X$, but its restriction to the plurifinely open set $D_ t$ is positive. The proof replaces $u_ t$ near each point by a local truncation that preserves positivity up to an arbitrarily small smooth error.

Lemma 6.1.1. With the notation above,

\[ 0\leq \mathbf1_{D_ t}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n \leq \mathbf1_{D_ t}(\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n. \]

More generally, let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms, let $u^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$, and let $D$ be the set where $u^ i>-t$ for every $i$. Then the mixed product of the currents $\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (u^ i,-t)$ is positive on $D$.

Proof. Work in a coordinate ball with a fixed positive constant form $\kappa $, and fix $\epsilon >0$. Every point has a smaller neighborhood carrying a constant real form $\theta '$ such that

\[ -\epsilon \kappa \leq \theta -\theta '\leq \epsilon \kappa . \]

Take a smooth local potential $r$ for $\theta '+\epsilon \kappa $. Then $u+r$ is plurisubharmonic. On a relatively compact smaller neighborhood choose $k>t+\sup |r|$ and set

\[ h_ k=\max (u+r,-k)-r. \]

This function is locally bounded and equals $u_ t=u$ on $D_ t$. Moreover,

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ k+2\epsilon \kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (u+r,-k)+(\theta -\theta '+\epsilon \kappa ) \]

is the sum of a positive closed current with bounded potential and a smooth positive form, so its bounded products are positive. By plurifine locality, the restriction of $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t+2\epsilon \kappa )^ n$ to $D_ t$ is positive on this neighborhood.

These neighborhoods cover the coordinate ball. Now let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. The product with the fixed bounded potential $u_ t$ is a polynomial in $\epsilon $ whose coefficients have locally finite variation, so it converges in local total variation, also after restriction to $D_ t$. This proves the first inequality. The same construction, applied to each factor, gives the positivity of mixed products.

For the second inequality, expand the difference of the two powers by the telescoping identity, using the smooth positive form $\Omega -\theta $. Every summand is a mixed product of factors that are positive on $D_ t$, as just proved. The difference is therefore nonnegative there. $\square$

Theorem 6.1.2 (Extended non-pluripolar measure). Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The measures $\mathbf1_{D_ t}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n$ increase with $t$. Their limit

6.1
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) :=\lim _{t\to +\infty }\mathbf1_{\{ u>-t\} } (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (u,-t))^ n \end{equation}

is a positive Borel measure that puts no mass on pluripolar sets; it is finite on each $D_ t$ and $\sigma $-finite on $X$. It depends only on the current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$; in particular, it is unchanged when a constant is added to $u$. If $\Omega $ is a Hermitian form with $\theta \leq \Omega $, then

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Omega (u). \]

The total mass of $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$ may be infinite.

Proof. For $s>t$, plurifine locality on $D_ t\subset D_ s$ gives

\[ \mathbf1_{D_ t}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ s)^ n =\mathbf1_{D_ t}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n. \]

Together with lemma 6.1.1, this proves that the measures increase. Their increasing limit is countably additive by monotone convergence, and it vanishes on pluripolar sets because each bounded product does. The comparison with $\Omega $ passes to the limit. By the compatibility just proved,

\[ \mathbf1_{D_ t}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) =\mathbf1_{D_ t}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n, \]

and the mass of this measure is bounded by that of the bounded product $(\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n$. The sets $D_ t$ exhaust the complement of $\{ u=-\infty \} $, which has zero mass, so the limit is $\sigma $-finite.

For a smooth real function $f$, consider $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ and $u'=u-f$. On a common superlevel set of $u$ and $u'$, sufficiently deep truncations of $u$ and $u'$ define the same current with bounded potentials, so their restricted products agree. These sets exhaust the complement of the common pole set, which proves invariance under this change of decomposition. By the elliptic-regularity argument in the proof of proposition 1.7.1, any two smooth decompositions of the same current differ in this way, and adding a constant is a special case. $\square$

We call $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$ the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of $u$; the word extended indicates that its total mass may be infinite. On compact $X$ it defines a current of order zero exactly when its total mass is finite. In that case it is the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure in the usual sense, namely the extension by zero across $\{ u=-\infty \} $ of the plurifinely local bounded products. If $u$ is bounded, then $D_ t=X$ for large $t$, and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$. In accordance with the usual notation for non-pluripolar products, we also write $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n\rangle $ for $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$; lower powers $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ k\rangle $ are defined by the same truncation where they are used.

6.2 Contact sets

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The bounded contact inequality of lemma 1.7.2 extends to unbounded potentials on common finite levels. As in the previous section, local positive corrections are needed, because the global truncations need not remain $\theta $-plurisubharmonic.

Proposition 6.2.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $u\leq v$. Then

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u=v\} }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq \mathbf1_{\{ u=v\} }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v). \]

Proof. Fix a coordinate ball with a constant positive form $\kappa $, a level $t>0$, and $\epsilon >0$. On smaller neighborhoods, freeze $\theta $ to a constant form $\theta '$ with $-\epsilon \kappa \leq \theta -\theta '\leq \epsilon \kappa $, and choose a quadratic function $r$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r=\theta '+\epsilon \kappa $. Then $u+r$ and $v+r$ are plurisubharmonic. For a large integer $k$ put

\[ a_ k=\max (u+r,-k)-r,\qquad b_ k=\max (v+r,-k)-r. \]

These functions are bounded on relatively compact neighborhoods, satisfy $a_ k\leq b_ k$, and

\[ \theta +2\epsilon \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ k =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (u+r,-k)+(\theta -\theta '+\epsilon \kappa )\geq 0, \]

with the same inequality for $b_ k$. We may therefore apply the top-degree case of lemma 1.7.2 with background $\theta +2\epsilon \kappa $.

On $D_ t=\{ u>-t\} $, both $u$ and $v$ are larger than $-t$. Taking $k>t+\sup |r|$ makes $a_ k=u$ and $b_ k=v$ on $D_ t$. Restricting the bounded inequality to $\{ u=v\} \cap D_ t$ and using locality, we obtain the contact inequality for the background $\theta +2\epsilon \kappa $ on that set. The smaller neighborhoods cover the original ball, so the restricted inequality holds throughout it.

Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta $. The truncations $u_ t=\max (u,-t)$ and $v_ t=\max (v,-t)$ are bounded and $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic, so their signed products with background $\theta $ are defined. On $D_ t$, the products used above are the restrictions of $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t+2\epsilon \kappa )^ n$ and $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ t+2\epsilon \kappa )^ n$. These are polynomials in $\epsilon $ whose coefficients have locally finite variation, so they converge in local total variation as $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, also after restriction to $\{ u=v\} \cap D_ t$. This proves the inequality for the background $\theta $ on each finite level. Finally let $t\to +\infty $: the common pole set has zero extended mass, and the sets $\{ u=v\} \cap D_ t$ exhaust its complement in the contact set. $\square$

6.3 Weighted lower semicontinuity

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Products of uniformly bounded potentials are continuous under convergence in capacity. Multiplying the extended measure by a cutoff in the potential that vanishes below a fixed level reduces it to such bounded products, and letting the level tend to $-\infty $ gives lower semicontinuity of the extended measures, even when their masses are infinite. The weights are allowed to vary with the potentials.

Lemma 6.3.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Suppose $u_ j,u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ have a common upper bound and $u_ j\to u$ in capacity. If $b_ j,b$ are nonnegative uniformly bounded quasi-continuous functions and $b_ j\to b$ in capacity, then

\[ \int _ Xb\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq \liminf _ j\int _ Xb_ j\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j). \]

Both sides may be infinite.

Proof. Subtracting a common constant, we may assume that all the potentials are nonpositive. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta $. For $T>0$, let $g_ T:[-\infty ,0]\to [0,1]$ be a continuous nondecreasing cutoff, equal to zero on $[-\infty ,-2T]$ and to one on $[-T,0]$, chosen so that $g_ T$ increases pointwise to one on $(-\infty ,0]$ as $T\to \infty $. Put $v_ j=\max (u_ j,-2T)$ and $v=\max (u,-2T)$. These are uniformly bounded $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic functions, and they converge in capacity.

Fix $T$. Expanding $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ n$ by means of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j=(\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)+(\theta -\Omega )$, and bounding each smooth factor by a constant multiple of $\Omega $, we see that the total variations of the signed terms are controlled by positive bounded mixed measures. By lemma 1.8.9, these have a common capacity bound. Bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity gives weak convergence term by term. It also allows the weights $b_ jg_ T(v_ j)$: their differences from the limiting weight tend to zero in capacity, so the common capacity bound makes the corresponding integrals tend to zero. For the fixed limiting weight, remove an open set of arbitrarily small capacity, extend the weight continuously from its complement, and control the discarded part by the same bound. Since the cutoff vanishes at the truncation level $-2T$, locality gives

\[ \begin{split} \int _ Xb_ jg_ T(u_ j)\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j) & =\int _ Xb_ jg_ T(v_ j)(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ n\\ & \longrightarrow \int _ Xbg_ T(u)\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \end{split} \]

The first integral is nonnegative and at most the corresponding integral without the cutoff, although the bounded product $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ n$ may be signed. Hence the limit is bounded by the lower limit in the assertion. Letting $T\to \infty $ and using monotone convergence completes the proof. $\square$

6.4 Density and trace inequalities

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The mixed density inequality of lemma 1.7.6 passes to unbounded potentials on common finite levels. It gives a lower bound for the extended measure of a convex combination of potentials and, by comparison with a smooth metric, a lower bound for the full trace measure.

Lemma 6.4.1. Let $\theta $ be a smooth semipositive $(1,1)$-form and let $f,g\geq 0$ be measurable functions on $X$. Suppose $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)\geq f\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v)\geq g\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Then, for $0\leq t\leq 1$,

6.4
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (tu+(1-t)v) \geq \bigl (tf^{1/n}+(1-t)g^{1/n}\bigr )^ n\, \mathrm{d}V. \end{equation}

Proof. The cases $t=0$ and $t=1$ are the hypotheses. Let $0<t<1$, translate $u,v$ to be nonpositive, and put $u_ k=\max (u,-k)$ and $v_ k=\max (v,-k)$. Since $\theta $ is semipositive, these are bounded $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions, and by locality

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ k)\geq \mathbf1_{\{ u>-k\} }f\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (v_ k)\geq \mathbf1_{\{ v>-k\} }g\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Apply lemma 1.7.6 to each mixed term in the positive expansion of $\theta _{tu_ k+(1-t)v_ k}^{n}$. On $E_ k=\{ u>-k\} \cap \{ v>-k\} $ this gives the density on the right of (6.4). On the same set, plurifine locality identifies the left-hand side with the extended measure of $tu+(1-t)v$. As $k\to \infty $, the sets $E_ k$ exhaust the complement of the two pole sets, which have zero smooth volume. Monotone convergence on Borel sets proves the inequality. $\square$

The same mixed inequality gives a lower bound for the full trace measure of an unbounded potential. Unlike the extended measure, the full current $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ keeps its mass on the pole set; the bound below concerns only its absolutely continuous part.

Lemma 6.4.2. Let $\gamma $ be a Hermitian form. If $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ and $0\leq g\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ satisfy $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq g\, \mathrm{d}V$, then

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1} \geq (g\, \mathrm{d}V/\gamma ^ n)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n. \]

Proof. For bounded $u$, lemma 1.7.6, applied in local coordinates with $T_1=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$, $T_2=\gamma $ and $k=1$, gives the asserted inequality.

In general, subtract a constant so that $u\leq 0$, and put $u_ k=\max (u,-k)$. Positivity and locality give

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\omega (u_ k)\geq \mathbf1_{\{ u>-k\} }g\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

so the bounded case applies to $u_ k$ with the density $\mathbf1_{\{ u>-k\} }g$. Since $u_ k\to u$ in $L^1$, the positive measures $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ k)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ converge weakly to $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$. The densities on the right converge in $L^1$, because $g^{1/n}$ is integrable and the pole set has zero smooth volume. Passing to the limit proves the assertion. $\square$

6.5 The bounded mass property

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

When $\omega $ is a Kähler form, all bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions have the same Monge–Ampère mass $\int _ X\omega ^ n$. For a general Hermitian form, the mass can depend on the potential, as in example 2.3.1, and the following two numbers measure its range.

Definition 6.5.1. Let $\omega $ be a Hermitian form on $X$. The lower mass and the upper mass of $\omega $ are

\[ \begin{split} v_-(\omega )& = \inf _{v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)} \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n,\\ v_+(\omega )& = \sup _{v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)} \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n. \end{split} \]

The manifold $X$ has the bounded mass property, abbreviated BMP, if $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $. The form $\omega $ has positive lower mass if $v_-(\omega )>0$.

Testing with $v=0$ gives $v_-(\omega )\leq \int _ X\omega ^ n\leq v_+(\omega )$. By the next proposition, the bounded mass property does not depend on the choice of $\omega $. Since the extended measure (6.1) is built from products of bounded potentials, the upper mass also controls the extended measures of unbounded potentials (proposition 6.5.3).

Proposition 6.5.2. The bounded mass property is independent of the Hermitian form. Moreover, the supremum in definition 6.5.1 is unchanged if only smooth potentials are used.

Proof. For another Hermitian form $\eta $, choose $C>0$ such that $\eta \leq C\omega $. If $v$ is a bounded $\eta $-plurisubharmonic function, then $v/C$ is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic and

\[ 0\leq \eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v \leq C\bigl (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v/C)\bigr ). \]

By positivity of mixed bounded products, $\int _ X(\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\leq C^ n v_+(\omega )$. Interchanging the two forms proves metric independence.

For the last assertion, approximate a bounded potential by theorem 1.11.1. The smooth approximants decrease and remain uniformly bounded, so the continuity of bounded products along decreasing sequences gives convergence of their total masses. The reverse inequality between the two suprema is immediate. $\square$

Proposition 6.5.3. Assume $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $. If $\theta $ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form with $\theta \leq C\omega $ for some $C>0$, and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, then

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)(X)\leq C^ n v_+(\omega ). \]

Proof. The comparison in theorem 6.1.2, applied with the Hermitian form $C\omega $, gives

\[ \mathbf1_{D_ t}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq \mathbf1_{D_ t}(C\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t)^ n. \]

Since $u_ t/C$ is bounded and $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, the right-hand side has mass at most $C^ n v_+(\omega )$. Let $t\to \infty $. $\square$

6.6 Infinite mass with trivial multiplier ideals

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

If the bounded mass property fails, there are smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u_ j$ whose masses $M_ j$ tend to infinity. In a convex combination $U=\sum _ ja_ ju_ j$, positivity of mixed products shows that each summand contributes at least $a_ j^ nM_ j$ to the extended measure of $U$. Choosing the masses $M_ j$ to outgrow $a_ j^{-n}$ therefore forces infinite mass. On the other hand, each tail $\sum _{j>N}a_ ju_ j$ is a small multiple of a convex combination of normalized potentials, and the uniform exponential integrability of lemma 1.5.1 applies to it at every exponent.

Theorem 6.6.1. If $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $, there is $U\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, $U\leq 0$, such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)(X)=+\infty , \qquad \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-pU}\, \mathrm{d}V<+\infty \quad \hbox{for every }p>0. \]

In particular, $\mathcal I(pU)=\mathcal O_ X$ for every $p>0$.

Proof. Set $a_ j=2^{-j}$ for $j\geq 1$. By proposition 6.5.2, we can choose smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u_ j$ with $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$ and

\[ M_ j:=\int _ X\omega _{u_ j}^ n\geq 2^{j(n+1)}. \]

The partial sums $U_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ ju_ j$ decrease and satisfy

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N =\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j\omega _{u_ j} +\left(1-\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j\right)\omega \geq 0. \]

Their $L^1$ norms are bounded by (1.1), so their limit $U$ is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic and nonpositive.

We first prove that the mass is infinite. Fix $j$ and write $v_ j=U-a_ ju_ j$. Passing to the limit in the remaining partial sums gives $(1-a_ j)\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j\geq 0$. For $t>0$, put

\[ v_{j,t}=\max (v_ j,-t),\qquad W_{j,t}=a_ ju_ j+v_{j,t}. \]

The function $v_{j,t}$ is a bounded potential for the positive background $(1-a_ j)\omega $, so $W_{j,t}$ is bounded and $\omega $-plurisubharmonic.

On $D_ t=\{ U>-t\} $, the inequality $u_ j\leq 0$ gives $v_ j=U-a_ ju_ j\geq U>-t$, and hence $W_{j,t}=U=\max (U,-t)$ there. Plurifine locality and positivity of bounded mixed products yield

\begin{align*} \mathbf1_{D_ t}\omega _{\max (U,-t)}^ n & =\mathbf1_{D_ t} \bigl (a_ j\omega _{u_ j} +(1-a_ j)\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{j,t}\bigr )^ n\\ & \geq a_ j^ n\mathbf1_{D_ t}\omega _{u_ j}^ n. \end{align*}

The sets $D_ t$ exhaust $X\setminus \{ U=-\infty \} $. This pole set is pluripolar, hence has zero smooth volume, so the smooth measure $\omega _{u_ j}^ n$ gives it no mass. Letting $t\to +\infty $, we obtain

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)(X) \geq a_ j^ nM_ j\geq 2^ j. \]

Since $j$ is arbitrary, the mass is infinite.

For the integrability, fix $p>0$, let $\alpha $ be the exponent of lemma 1.5.1, and take $N$ so large that $p\delta \leq \alpha $, where $\delta =\sum _{j>N}a_ j=2^{-N}$. Write $U=A_ N+V_ N$ with $A_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ ju_ j$ smooth. For a finite tail ending at $m>N$, Jensen's inequality for the convex exponential function, with the unused weight assigned to the value zero, gives

\begin{align*} \exp \! \left(-p\sum _{N<j\leq m}a_ ju_ j\right) & \leq \sum _{N<j\leq m}\frac{a_ j}{\delta } \mathrm{e}^{-p\delta u_ j} +1-\sum _{N<j\leq m}\frac{a_ j}{\delta }. \end{align*}

Because $u_ j\leq 0$ and $p\delta \leq \alpha $, the integrals of the exponentials on the right are uniformly bounded. Integrating and letting $m\to +\infty $ by monotone convergence, we obtain $\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-pV_ N}\mathrm{d}V<+\infty $. Multiplication by the bounded smooth function $\mathrm{e}^{-pA_ N}$ proves $\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-pU}\mathrm{d}V<+\infty $.

Consequently, the constant germ $1$ is locally integrable against each weight $\mathrm{e}^{-pU}$, and every multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(pU)$ is the whole structure sheaf. $\square$

Corollary 6.6.2. The bounded mass property holds if and only if $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)(X)<+\infty $ for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. If the bounded mass property fails, there are potentials with infinite extended mass and trivial multiplier ideals $\mathcal I(pu)=\mathcal O_ X$, $p>0$, arbitrarily close to zero in $L^1$.

Proof. The first assertion follows from proposition 6.5.3, theorem 6.6.1. For the second, take $\varepsilon U$, $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$, with $U$ as in theorem 6.6.1. It is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic and has the required exponential integrability. On $D_ t=\{ U>-t\} $, its truncation at level $\varepsilon t$ is $\varepsilon \max (U,-t)$, and

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl (\varepsilon \max (U,-t)\bigr ) =\varepsilon \omega _{\max (U,-t)}+(1-\varepsilon )\omega . \]

Positivity of mixed products bounds the restricted measure of $\varepsilon U$ below by $\varepsilon ^ n$ times that of $U$. Its limiting mass is therefore infinite. Finally, $\| \varepsilon U\| _{L^1}\to 0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$. $\square$

6.7 Integrable Hessians and infinite mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In the construction of theorem 6.6.1, the Gauduchon identity of proposition 4.2.1 fixes the trace integral of every positive summand $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$. The series defining $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$ therefore converges in the coefficient $L^1$ norm, although its determinant is not integrable. The trace measures converge in total variation, yet the Monge–Ampère mass is infinite. In chapter 7, weights that decay on deep sublevel sets will control these masses.

Fix a Gauduchon form $\gamma $ and set $M=\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$. For a real $(1,1)$-form $T$ with measurable coefficients, let $A_ T$ be its Hermitian matrix relative to $\gamma $, and let $|A_ T|=(A_ T^2)^{1/2}$ be its positive matrix absolute value. We use the norm

\[ \| T\| _{L^1(\gamma )} =\frac1n\int _ X\operatorname {tr}|A_ T|\, \gamma ^ n. \]

For $T\geq 0$ this equals $\int _ XT\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$. On compact $X$, any other smooth Hermitian form gives an equivalent norm and hence the same space of integrable coefficients. When the coefficients of $T$ are integrable, we write $\det _\gamma T=\det A_ T$; this function is defined almost everywhere, and $T^ n=(\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n$ when $T$ is smooth.

Proposition 6.7.1. Suppose $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $. For the potential $U$ and its smooth decreasing approximants $U_ N$ constructed in the proof of theorem 6.6.1, the current $T=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$ has integrable coefficients and

\[ \| \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N-T\| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq 2^{1-N}M. \]

Its pointwise determinant satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U) \geq (\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n, \qquad \int _ X(\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n=+\infty . \]

Moreover,

\[ \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N)^ n\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

Proof. Write $T_ j=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$ for the smooth positive forms in that construction, and put

\[ S_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ jT_ j,\qquad \delta _ N=\sum _{j>N}a_ j=2^{-N}. \]

The smooth positive forms $S_ N$ increase pointwise, and the Gauduchon identity gives

\[ \int _ XS_ N\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=(1-\delta _ N)M. \]

By monotone convergence of their traces, the series of matrices has a finite positive limit $S$ almost everywhere. The coefficients of $S$ are integrable, because in a $\gamma $-unitary frame the absolute value of every coefficient of a positive matrix is bounded by its trace. Positivity and the same trace identity give

\[ \| S-S_ N\| _{L^1(\gamma )}=\delta _ N M. \]

In particular, $S_ N\to S$ in the sense of distributions. Since

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N=S_ N+\delta _ N\omega \longrightarrow \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U \]

in the sense of distributions, by $L^1$ convergence of the potentials, we obtain $T=S$. The triangle inequality now gives

\[ \| \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N-T\| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq \| S_ N-S\| _{L^1(\gamma )} +\delta _ N\| \omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )} =2\delta _ N M. \]

To compare $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)$ with the determinant, fix $N$ and set $V_ N=U-U_ N$. The remaining partial sums give $\delta _ N\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_ N\geq 0$ and $V_ N\leq 0$. For $t>0$, the function $W_{N,t}=U_ N+\max (V_ N,-t)$ is bounded, and its current is the sum of two positive terms:

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W_{N,t} =S_ N+\bigl (\delta _ N\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\max (V_ N,-t)\bigr ). \]

On $D_ t=\{ U>-t\} $, the inequality $U_ N\leq 0$ gives $V_ N\geq U>-t$, and consequently $W_{N,t}=U=\max (U,-t)$ there. Locality and positivity of bounded products imply

\[ \mathbf1_{D_ t}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U) \geq \mathbf1_{D_ t}S_ N^ n. \]

The smooth measure $S_ N^ n$ gives no mass to the pluripolar pole set of $U$, so letting $t\to \infty $ proves $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)\geq S_ N^ n$. The positive matrices $S_ N$ increase to $T$ almost everywhere, so their determinants increase to $\det _\gamma T$. Monotone convergence on every Borel set gives the asserted inequality between $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)$ and the determinant measure.

Finally, for every $N\geq j$, positivity gives $S_ N\geq a_ jT_ j$. Hence both the measurable limit and the smooth approximating forms satisfy

\[ \int _ X(\det _\gamma T)\gamma ^ n \geq a_ j^ n\int _ XT_ j^ n\geq 2^ j, \qquad \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ N)^ n \geq a_ j^ n\int _ XT_ j^ n\geq 2^ j. \]

The first bound holds for every $j$, and the second for every $N\geq j$; together they prove the two assertions about infinite mass. $\square$

Corollary 6.7.2. If the bounded mass property fails, there are nonpositive potentials $w_\varepsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, $0<\varepsilon <1$, such that

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon \geq (1-\varepsilon )\omega , \qquad \mathcal I(pw_\varepsilon )=\mathcal O_ X\quad (p>0), \]

and such that both the integral of the pointwise determinant $\det _\gamma (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon )$ against $\gamma ^ n$ and the extended Monge–Ampère mass of $w_\varepsilon $ are infinite. As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, the potentials converge to zero in $L^1$, their currents converge to $\omega $ in the coefficient $L^1$ norm, and their full trace measures $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ converge to $\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ in total variation.

Proof. Take $w_\varepsilon =\varepsilon U$, with $U$ and $T=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$ as in proposition 6.7.1. Its current is

\[ T_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )\omega +\varepsilon T. \]

The lower bound and the two $L^1$ convergence assertions follow immediately. The full trace measures have integrable densities, and their distance from $\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ in total variation is at most

\[ \varepsilon \| T-\omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )}. \]

Every multiplier ideal is trivial by the exponential integrability of $U$ proved in theorem 6.6.1. Since $T_\varepsilon \geq \varepsilon T$ almost everywhere, the determinant integral of $T_\varepsilon $ is at least $\varepsilon ^ n$ times that of $T$, hence infinite. The extended mass of $w_\varepsilon $ is infinite by the proof of corollary 6.6.2. $\square$

For smooth potentials, the total masses can be prescribed in advance while both the currents and the exponential weights converge.

Corollary 6.7.3. Suppose $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $. Let $(\varepsilon _ j)_{j\geq 1}$ and $(b_ j)_{j\geq 1}$ be real sequences with $0<\varepsilon _ j<1$, $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$, and $b_ j>\int _ X\omega ^ n$. There are smooth potentials $w_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\sup _ Xw_ j=0$ such that

\[ \begin{gathered} \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\geq (1-\varepsilon _ j)\omega , \qquad \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^ n=b_ j, \\ \| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq 2M\varepsilon _ j. \end{gathered} \]

Moreover, $w_ j\to 0$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ and $\mathrm{e}^{-pw_ j}\to 1$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ for every fixed $p>0$. The full Gauduchon trace measures converge to $\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ in total variation.

Proof. By proposition 6.5.2, we can choose a smooth $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$ and

\[ \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^ n>\varepsilon _ j^{-n}b_ j. \]

Put $T_ j=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$. The polynomial

\[ F_ j(s)=\int _ X\bigl ((1-s)\omega +sT_ j\bigr )^ n, \qquad 0\leq s\leq \varepsilon _ j, \]

satisfies $F_ j(0)<b_ j$, and also $F_ j(\varepsilon _ j)\geq \varepsilon _ j^ n\int _ XT_ j^ n>b_ j$ by positivity of the mixed terms. The intermediate value theorem gives $s_ j\in (0,\varepsilon _ j)$ with $F_ j(s_ j)=b_ j$. Set $w_ j=s_ ju_ j$. Then $\sup _ Xw_ j=0$, $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j=(1-s_ j)\omega +s_ jT_ j\geq (1-\varepsilon _ j)\omega $, and $w_ j$ has the prescribed mass $b_ j$.

The Gauduchon identity gives $\| T_ j\| _{L^1(\gamma )}=\| \omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )}=M$. Hence

\[ \| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\| _{L^1(\gamma )} =s_ j\| T_ j-\omega \| _{L^1(\gamma )} \leq 2M\varepsilon _ j. \]

Since the absolute value of the trace of a Hermitian matrix is bounded by the trace of its absolute value, the same bound holds for the total variation of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$. With $K_\omega $ as in (1.1), we also have $\| w_ j\| _{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq K_\omega \varepsilon _ j$.

Finally, let $\alpha ,C$ be the constants of lemma 1.5.1 for $(X,\omega ,\mathrm{d}V)$, and fix $p>0$. For all sufficiently large $j$, one has $ps_ j\leq \alpha $. Convexity of the exponential function, applied with weight $ps_ j/\alpha $, gives

\[ 0\leq \mathrm{e}^{-pw_ j}-1 \leq \frac{ps_ j}{\alpha }\bigl (\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha u_ j}-1\bigr ). \]

Integrating, we bound the $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ norm of $\mathrm{e}^{-pw_ j}-1$ by $pC\varepsilon _ j/\alpha $, which proves the last convergence. $\square$
