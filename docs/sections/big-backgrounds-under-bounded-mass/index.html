10.31 Big backgrounds under bounded massC2AE04961A4E

C2AE04961A4E

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, let $\theta $ be a smooth big real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, not necessarily closed or semipositive, and let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. As in section 15.37, we solve the equations $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $, $\lambda >0$, and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $, $c>0$, for an arbitrary nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu $, with $u$ in the relative class of $\varphi $. We replace the smaller-background model condition used there by upper and positive lower mass bounds.

Throughout this chapter we make two standing assumptions. First, $X$ has the bounded mass property of definition 13.14.14: $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $ for one, and hence every, Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$ (proposition 13.14.15). Second, $\varphi $ satisfies (13.20.5): it is an ordinary model, $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $ (definition 5.40.4), and its lower singularity volume is positive. Recall from definition 16.28.58 that the lower and upper volumes $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ and $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ are the infimum and the supremum of $\int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)$ over all $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $v\sim u$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega $ for capacity and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$.

The two assumptions give the two bounds needed for approximation: every member of the relative class of $\varphi $ has Monge–Ampère mass at least $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )>0$ and at most $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )<\infty $. With these bounds, first moments relative to a reference prevent collapse and keep limits in the relative class, while higher moments control convergence against a smoothed measure. This first gives solutions in the class of the potential $V_\theta $ with minimal singularities.

To pass to the reference $\varphi $, we project a solution in the relative class of $V_\theta $ onto the relative class of $\varphi $. The projection keeps only part of the exponentially weighted measure, but weighted addition can fill any remaining deficit; maximizing the weighted mass therefore gives the required equation. The same contact estimates keep limits in the relative class when the model reference varies in the volume comparison of section 13.22.

For general finite non-pluripolar data the solutions lie in the relative full-mass class; for $L^ p$ densities, a stronger moment estimate shows that they differ from $\varphi $ by a bounded function. A positive exponent determines the solution uniquely; at exponent zero only the scalar is unique.

For $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, write

\[ \mathcal E_\varphi =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ) =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ), \qquad \theta _ a=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a,\qquad \mu _ a=\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a). \]

Here $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta ,\varphi )$ is the relative full-mass class of definition 13.18.4, consisting of the $u\preceq \varphi $ with $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )$; $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi )$ is the relative envelope class of definition 16.28.61; and the two classes coincide by theorem 13.18.5. As in definition 16.7.1, $u\preceq v$ means $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$, and $u\sim v$ means $u\preceq v\preceq u$. The measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a)$ is the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 13.14.3; it is finite by proposition 13.14.16. Put

\[ \ell =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )>0, \qquad M=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )<\infty . \]

By theorem 13.18.5, every $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ satisfies

A6F2ACEED83F 10.31.1
\begin{equation} \ell \leq \mu _ u(X)\leq M. \end{equation}

We use the stretching envelopes $Q_ b(u,w)=P_\theta (bu-(b-1)w)$ introduced before definition 16.28.61, the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta (u_1,\ldots ,u_ N)=P_\theta (\min _ iu_ i)$, and $V_\theta =P_\theta (0)$ (section 5.40). For $a\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, proposition 16.28.65 gives $P_\theta [a]=\varphi $, and hence, as in (15.37.2),

\[ a-\sup _ Xa\leq \varphi . \]

We refer to this inequality as the envelope identity. Finally, zero domination refers to theorem 13.19.1 with coefficient zero: if $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $v\preceq \varphi $ and $\mu _ u(\{ u<v\} )=0$, then $u\geq v$.

A positive exponent determines a unique solution in the relative class. At exponent zero, the equation determines its positive scalar, but normalized potentials may still differ, as in corollary 15.37.34.

Theorem 10.31.3.2568C6F1B2F7 For every nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu $ on $X$:

  1. There are $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and a unique $c>0$ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $.

  2. For each $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ such that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $.

If $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$ with $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$ for some $p>1$, the solutions in both parts may be taken with $u\sim \varphi $.

Part (1) is theorem 10.31.45, part (2) is theorem 10.31.44, and the assertion for $L^ p$ densities is corollary 10.31.56.

The standing assumptions also give $v_-(\omega )>0$. Indeed, choose $C>0$ with $\theta \leq C\omega $. Since $\varphi \preceq 0$, theorem 13.17.1 gives

\[ 0<\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi ) \leq \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{C\omega }(0) =C^ n v_-(\omega ). \]

10.31.4 Convergence under a fixed measureD176FBCC507C

As in the semipositive case, a fixed dominating measure gives a common lower envelope after extraction, and then convergence in capacity. The proof follows that of lemma 15.37.13, theorem 15.37.19: the mass interval (10.31.1) replaces the lower bound $\kappa $ used there, and weighted lower semicontinuity holds in the present background by lemma 13.14.8.

Theorem 10.31.5.6AC45579AB2E Suppose $u_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $u_ j\to u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu $ for one finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu $. Then there is a subsequence with

\[ h=P_\theta \left(\inf _ j u_ j\right)\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

Moreover, for the original sequence,

\[ u\in \mathcal E_\varphi ,\qquad u_ j\longrightarrow u\text{ in capacity},\qquad \mu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mu _ u\leq \nu . \]

Proof. We follow the proofs of lemma 15.37.13, theorem 15.37.19 and check their ingredients in the present background. The envelope identity and Hartogs' lemma give $u_ j\leq \varphi +C$. Finite rooftops and stretching envelopes of members of $\mathcal E_\varphi $ stay in $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by proposition 16.28.64, lemma 16.28.63, and their masses are at least $\ell $ by (10.31.1). These facts replace the corresponding ones for semipositive backgrounds.

After a common translation, assume $u_ j,u\leq \varphi \leq 0$. Using lemma 5.44.2, extract once and for all a subsequence along which $u_ j\to u$ almost everywhere for smooth volume and

\[ I_ j:=\int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _{u_ j}\leq 2^{-j}. \]

For $p_{j,N}=P_\theta (u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N)$, partitioning the contact set according to the first obstacle touched gives

C0EFA39C2570 10.31.6
\begin{equation} \mu _{p_{j,N}}\leq \nu ,\qquad \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _{p_{j,N}}\leq 2^{1-j}. \end{equation}

In the proof of the extraction lemma, replace $\kappa $ by $\ell $. For each fixed $B\geq 1$, the stretched rooftops $r_{j,N}=Q_ B(p_{j,N},\varphi )$ satisfy

\[ \ell \leq \mu _{r_{j,N}}(X),\qquad \mu _{r_{j,N}}\leq B^ n\nu . \]

The exponential test (15.37.15) therefore bounds $\sup _ Xr_{j,N}$ from below, independently of $N$, once $j$ is sufficiently large. The construction combining the tail with the finitely many earlier terms, with $B=2b$, uses only convex combinations and finite rooftops that are not identically $-\infty $. It provides every defining competitor for

\[ h=P_\theta (\inf _ j u_ j)\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

Since $h\leq u\leq \varphi $, upward stability gives $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

For the continuity statements, put $p_ j=P_\theta (\inf _{k\geq j}u_ k)$ and $p=(\lim _ jp_ j)^*$. The monotone limits $p_{j,N}\downarrow p_ j$ and $p_ j\uparrow p$ have the common lower bound $h\not\equiv -\infty $, so they hold in capacity by lemma 5.29.19. All the limits lie in $\mathcal E_\varphi $, and $p\leq u$. Applying lemma 13.14.8 twice to (10.31.6) gives

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^ p-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _ p=0. \]

Hence $\mu _ p(\{ p<u\} )=0$, and zero domination (theorem 13.19.1 with coefficient zero) gives $p=u$. The upper envelopes $(\sup _{k\geq j}u_ k)^*$ decrease to $u$ by Hartogs' lemma, and squeezing the $u_ j$ between them and $p_ j$ proves convergence in capacity.

Finally, the fixed finite dominating measure gives

\[ \mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j\leq -T\} )\leq \nu (\{ h\leq -T\} )\longrightarrow 0 \]

uniformly in $j$, and theorem 13.15.23 proves weak convergence to $\mu _ u\leq \nu $. Starting with an arbitrary subsequence, we obtain a further subsequence with both convergences and the same limit. Hence the conclusions hold for the original sequence. $\square$

10.31.7 Relative moments985DB0A9A096

For $w\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $u\leq w$, the relative first moment $\int _ X(w-u)\, \mu _ u$ measures how far $u$ lies below $w$, weighted by the measure of $u$. A uniform bound on these moments prevents a sequence from collapsing and keeps its limits in $\mathcal E_\varphi $. The proof compares rooftop masses with the fixed measure of a potential of the same type as $w$ whose mass is close to the upper volume $M$. For a real number $t$, write $t_+=\max (t,0)$.

Proposition 10.31.8.56A4FC216C27 Fix $w\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Suppose $u_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $u_ j\leq w$, and

\[ K:=\sup _ j\int _ X(w-u_ j)\, \mu _{u_ j}<+\infty . \]

Then $\sup _ Xu_ j$ is bounded below. If $u_ j\to u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1(X)$, then $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

The same conclusions hold if $u_ j\leq w$ is replaced by a common upper bound $u_ j\leq \varphi +C$ and the integral is replaced by $\int _ X(w-u_ j)_+\, \mu _{u_ j}$.

Proof. We first treat the case $u_ j\leq w$. Since $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (w)=M$, we may choose $w'\sim w$ with $\mu _{w'}(X)>M-\ell /2$. Translate $w'$ so that $w'\leq w$, and put $p_ j=P_\theta (u_ j,w')$. These rooftops belong to $\mathcal E_\varphi $. Contact support and ordered contact imply

C1417B0EF5E7 10.31.9
\begin{equation} \int _ X(w'-p_ j)\, \mu _{p_ j} \leq \int _ X(w-u_ j)\, \mu _{u_ j}\leq K. \end{equation}

Indeed, the integrand vanishes on the contact part $\{ p_ j=w'\} $, and on the remaining contact part $p_ j=u_ j<w'\leq w$.

We shall use the following estimate for maxima. If $a\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $a\leq w'$, and $t>0$, then

525AD3E9DFE4 10.31.10
\begin{equation} \mu _ a(\{ a>w'-t\} ) \leq \mu _{w'}(\{ a>w'-t\} )+M-\mu _{w'}(X). \end{equation}

To see this, put $m=\max (a,w'-t)$. It has the ordinary singularity type of $w'$, so $\mu _ m(X)\leq M$. On $\{ a>w'-t\} $, locality gives $\mu _ m=\mu _ a$. On the complementary set, where $m=w'-t$, ordered contact gives $\mu _ m\geq \mu _{w'}$. Integrating over the two sets proves (10.31.10).

Suppose that a subsequence had $\sup _ Xu_ j\to -\infty $; then also $\sup _ Xp_ j\to -\infty $. Splitting the mass of $p_ j$ at the level $w'-s$, $s>0$, and using (10.31.9) and (10.31.10), we obtain

\[ \ell \leq \mu _{p_ j}(X) \leq \frac Ks+\mu _{w'}(\{ p_ j>w'-s\} )+M-\mu _{w'}(X). \]

For fixed $s$, the middle term tends to zero as $j\to \infty $, since $w'$ is finite off a pluripolar set and $\mu _{w'}$ is finite and non-pluripolar. Letting $j\to \infty $ and then $s\to \infty $ gives $\ell \leq M-\mu _{w'}(X)<\ell /2$, a contradiction. This proves the first assertion.

Now suppose $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1$. Then $u\leq w\preceq \varphi $. Fix $b>1$ and suppose that $Q_ b(u,w)\equiv -\infty $. Since $w-w'$ is bounded, translating competitors by a constant shows that $Q_ b(u,w')\equiv -\infty $ as well. Keep the same $p_ j$ and set

\[ h_ j=Q_ b(p_ j,w')\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

We claim that $\sup _ Xh_ j\to -\infty $. Otherwise, since $h_ j\leq p_ j\leq w'$, theorem 5.25.2 would give a subsequence converging in $L^1$ to some $h\not\equiv -\infty $. Passing to the limit in

\[ b^{-1}h_ j+(1-b^{-1})w'\leq p_ j\leq u_ j, \]

first almost everywhere and then everywhere by lemma 5.24.3, would give a competitor for $Q_ b(u,w')$, a contradiction.

On the contact set of $h_ j$ one has $p_ j=b^{-1}h_ j+(1-b^{-1})w'$. Thus $h_ j\leq w'-bs$ implies $p_ j\leq w'-s$ there. Contact support and (10.31.9) therefore imply

\[ \mu _{h_ j}(\{ h_ j\leq w'-bs\} )\leq b^ nK/s. \]

Applying (10.31.10) with $a=h_ j$ and $t=bs$ on the complementary set, we obtain

\[ \ell \leq b^ nK/s+ \mu _{w'}(\{ h_ j>w'-bs\} )+M-\mu _{w'}(X). \]

Let first $j\to \infty $ and then $s\to \infty $. The choice of $w'$ again gives $\ell <\ell /2$, a contradiction. Therefore $Q_ b(u,w)\not\equiv -\infty $ for every $b>1$. The resulting convex inequalities and theorem 13.17.1, proposition 13.17.2 give

\[ (1-b^{-1})^ n M\leq \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)\leq M. \]

Letting $b\to \infty $ shows that the upper volumes of $u$ and $\varphi $ are equal; hence $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

For the last assertion put $q_ j=P_\theta (u_ j,w)$. Contact gives

\[ \int _ X(w-q_ j)\, \mu _{q_ j} \leq \int _ X(w-u_ j)_+\, \mu _{u_ j}\leq K. \]

The case already treated bounds $\sup _ Xq_ j$ from below, and therefore also $\sup _ Xu_ j\geq \sup _ Xq_ j$. If $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1$, then, since $q_ j\leq w$, a subsequence of the $q_ j$ converges in $L^1$ to some $q\not\equiv -\infty $, and the case already treated gives $q\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Since $q\leq u\leq \varphi +C$, upward stability gives $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ as well. $\square$

When the first moment of the difference from the limit tends to zero, rooftops of the tails identify the limit in capacity.

Theorem 10.31.11.4AC0DDA39172 Let $u_ j,u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, with $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$. If

\[ \int _ X|u_ j-u|\, \mu _{u_ j}\longrightarrow 0, \]

then $u_ j\to u$ in capacity. Moreover, after passing to a subsequence, $P_\theta (\inf _{k\geq j}u_ k)$ belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ for every $j$.

Proof. Start with any subsequence and extract a further subsequence along which the integrals in the hypothesis are at most $2^{-j}$ and $u_ j\to u$ almost everywhere for smooth volume. The envelope identity and Hartogs' lemma give a uniform bound $u_ j,u\leq \varphi +C$. For $N\geq j$ let $p_{j,N}=P_\theta (u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N)$. Partitioning the contact set according to the first obstacle touched gives

706A43A92BE8 10.31.12
\begin{equation} \int _ X|p_{j,N}-u|\, \mu _{p_{j,N}}\leq 2^{1-j}. \end{equation}

Apply proposition 10.31.8 with $w=u$, in its one-sided form. It bounds $\sup _ Xp_{j,N}$ from below, independently of $N$. Consequently the decreasing sequence has a limit

\[ p_ j=P_\theta \left(\inf _{k\geq j}u_ k\right) \]

that is not identically $-\infty $, and the same proposition puts $p_ j$ in $\mathcal E_\varphi $. The increasing sequence $p_ j$ has a regularized limit $p$, which belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by upward stability, since $p_1\leq p\leq u\preceq \varphi $. The inequality $p\leq u$ follows from the almost-everywhere convergence of the extracted sequence.

Both $p_{j,N}\downarrow p_ j$ and $p_ j\uparrow p$ hold in capacity by lemma 5.29.19, since $p_1\not\equiv -\infty $ is a common lower bound. Apply lemma 13.14.8 to (10.31.12), with the bounded weights $\min (1,|p_{j,N}-u|)$ and then $\min (1,|p_ j-u|)$. These weights converge in capacity: discard the deep sublevel sets of $p_1$ and $u$, and use that $\min (1,|x-y|)$ is Lipschitz on finite values. The result is

\[ \int _ X\min (1,|p-u|)\, \mu _ p=0. \]

Hence $\mu _ p(\{ p<u\} )=0$, and zero domination gives $p=u$. The upper envelopes $(\sup _{k\geq j}u_ k)^*$ decrease to $u$. Squeezing the extracted $u_ j$ between these envelopes and $p_ j$ proves convergence in capacity. Since the initial subsequence was arbitrary, the conclusion holds for the original sequence. $\square$

10.31.13 Higher moments in the minimal singularity type811851E56701

In this subsection the reference is the potential $V=V_\theta =P_\theta (0)$ with minimal singularities. It is an ordinary model, and we write $\mathcal E_ V=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,V)$ and

\[ \ell _ V=\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (V)\geq \ell >0, \qquad M_ V=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (V)<\infty . \]

The first inequality follows from volume monotonicity, since $\varphi \preceq V$, and the last from bounded mass. Thus $V$ satisfies the standing assumptions on $\varphi $, and the results above apply with $\varphi $ replaced by $V$. For a non-pluripolar probability measure $\nu $, recall from 5.44.9 that

\[ A_\theta (\nu )=\sup \left\{ \int _ X(-w)\, \mathrm{d}\nu : w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\ \sup _ Xw=0\right\} . \]

We show that a first moment of $V-u$ against the measure $\mu _ u$ of $u$ itself controls a higher moment of $V-u$ against every fixed $\nu $ with $A_\theta (\nu )<\infty $. An envelope with a concave obstacle converts the distribution of $V-u$ under $\mu _ u$ into this stronger integrability estimate.

Lemma 10.31.14.E0503BA0806A Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $u\sim V$ and $u\leq V$. Let $\chi :\mathbb R_{\leq 0}\to \mathbb R_{\leq 0}$ be increasing and concave, with $\chi (0)=0$ and $\chi '\geq 1$, and assume that $\chi $ is $C^{1,1}$ on compact intervals. Set

\[ b=V+\chi (u-V),\qquad p=P_\theta (b), \]

where the obstacle $b$ is defined off the pole set of $V$ and is set equal to $-\infty $ on that set. Then $p\sim V$ and

\[ \mu _ p\leq \mathbf1_{\{ p=b\} }\chi '(u-V)^ n\mu _ u. \]

The measures and contact sets in this inequality may be restricted to the complement of the pole set of any fixed strict analytic barrier for $\theta $, that is, of any $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with analytic singularities such that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq a\omega $ for some $a>0$.

Proof. The difference $u-V$ is bounded, so $V-C\leq b\leq u\leq V$ for some constant $C$. Hence $p\sim V$. The obstacle is quasi-continuous: away from sets of arbitrarily small capacity, $u$ and $V$ are finite and continuous, and their difference has a fixed bounded range. The general contact theorem theorem 15.8.6 therefore applies.

Choose a strict analytic barrier $\rho $ and translate it so that $\rho \leq V$; put $\Omega =X\setminus \{ \rho =-\infty \} $. On this open set $u$, $V$ and $p$ are locally bounded. Let $\tau =\chi ^{-1}$; then $\tau $ is convex and $0<\tau '\leq 1$. By lemma 5.28.13, the function

\[ w=V+\tau (p-V) \]

is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic on $\Omega $ and satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (w)\geq \tau '(p-V)^ n\mu _ p. \]

Since $p\leq w\leq V$ on $\Omega $, the removability lemma lemma 5.24.7 extends $w$ across the analytic set $X\setminus \Omega $, preserving its smooth lower curvature bound.

The inequality $p\leq b$ implies $w\leq u$, with equality on $D=\{ p=b\} $. Ordered contact gives

\[ \mathbf1_ D\tau '(p-V)^ n\mu _ p \leq \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (w) \leq \mathbf1_ D\mu _ u. \]

On $D$ the derivative $\tau '(p-V)$ equals $1/\chi '(u-V)$. Together with contact support for $p$, this proves the claim on $\Omega $. The analytic set $X\setminus \Omega $ is not charged by the non-pluripolar measures involved. $\square$

Proposition 10.31.15.3464170481EE Set $N=2n+1$ and $r=1+(4N)^{-1}$. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim V$ and $\sup _ Xu=0$, put $B=\int _ X(V-u)\, \mu _ u$. There is a constant $C$, depending only on $n,\ell _ V,M_ V$, such that, for every non-pluripolar probability measure $\nu $ with $A=A_\theta (\nu )<\infty $,

\[ \int _ X(V-u)^ r\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq C(1+A)(1+B)^{1+1/N} . \]

In particular, normalized potentials $u\sim V$ with uniformly bounded $B$ are uniformly integrable with respect to $\nu $.

Proof. From the distribution of $V-u$ under $\mu _ u$ we build a concave weight adapted to $u$, apply the preceding lemma, and use $A$ to bound the resulting envelope against $\nu $. Throughout, $c$ and $C$ denote positive constants depending only on $n,\ell _ V,M_ V$.

The normalization gives $u\leq V$. Put $x=V-u\geq 0$ and

\[ T=\sup \{ t\geq 0:\mu _ u(\{ x>t\} )>0\} , \]

with $T=0$ if this set is empty. Since $u\sim V$, $T$ is finite. Zero domination, applied to $u$ and $V-t$ for $t>T$, shows that $u\geq V-T$; thus $x\leq T$ off the pole set. If $T\leq 2$ the claim is immediate. We may therefore suppose $T>2$; in particular $B>0$.

Fix a real number $s$ with $0<s<T-2$. For $t\geq 0$, define

  1. For a fixed $\lambda >0$, the solutions

    \[ u_ j\in \mathcal E_{\varphi _ j},\qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\mu _ j \]

    converge in $L^1(X)$ and in capacity to the solution $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ with datum $\mu $. Their Monge–Ampère measures converge to $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$ in total variation.

  2. Let $u_ j\in \mathcal E_{\varphi _ j}$ satisfy $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)=c_ j\mu _ j$. Then $c_ j\to c$, where $c$ is the scalar for the reference $\varphi $ and the datum $\mu $, and

    \[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_ j)\longrightarrow c\mu \quad \text{in total variation}. \]

    The potentials form a relatively compact family in $L^1$. Every $L^1$ cluster limit $u$ lies in $\mathcal E_\varphi $, has supremum zero and solves $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $. Each subsequence converging in $L^1$ converges to its limit in capacity.

Proof. Write $M_0=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (V_\theta )$. Every solution above has mass between $\delta $ and $M_0$. In the first case let $s_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j$. Then

\[ s_ j\geq \lambda ^{-1}\log \frac{\delta }{\mu _ j(X)}, \]

which is a uniform lower bound since $\mu _ j(X)\to \mu (X)>0$. To bound $s_ j$ from above, suppose that $s_ j\to +\infty $ along a subsequence. By normalized compactness, after a further extraction, $v_ j=u_ j-s_ j\to v$ in $L^1$ with $\sup _ Xv=0$. The fixed-measure lemma lemma 5.44.2 and $f_ j\to f$ in $L^1(\nu )$ imply

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_ j}f_ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu \longrightarrow \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\, \mathrm{d}\nu >0. \]

The limit is positive because $f\nu $ is nonzero and does not charge the poles of $v$. But the left-hand side equals $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s_ j}\mu _{u_ j}(X)\leq M_0\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s_ j}$, which tends to zero, a contradiction. Thus $s_ j$ is bounded, and the $u_ j$ form a relatively compact family in $L^1$ whose limits are not identically $-\infty $.

Start with any subsequence and extract $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1$. Extract again so that $\sum _ j\| f_ j-f\| _{L^1(\nu )}<\infty $. The function

\[ g=f+\sum _ j|f_ j-f|\in L^1(\nu ) \]

dominates all retained $f_ j$. If $C$ is a common upper bound for the $u_ j$, then $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda C}g\nu $. By theorem 10.31.30, $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, the potentials converge in capacity, and the measures converge weakly to $\mu _ u$. On the other hand,

\[ \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}f_ j\longrightarrow \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\quad \text{in }L^1(\nu ). \]

Indeed, the error coming from $f_ j-f$ is at most $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda C}\| f_ j-f\| _{L^1(\nu )}$, and lemma 5.44.2 for the fixed measure $f\nu $ controls the remaining term. Hence $\mu _ u=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $, with convergence in total variation. By the uniqueness in theorem 10.31.44, every cluster limit is this solution, and the subsequence criterion gives all three convergences for the original sequence.

In the second case, integrating the equation gives

\[ \frac{\delta }{\mu _ j(X)}\leq c_ j\leq \frac{M_0}{\mu _ j(X)}. \]

Since $\mu _ j(X)\to \mu (X)>0$, these scalars stay in a compact subinterval of $(0,+\infty )$. After extraction, normalized compactness gives $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1$ with $\sup _ Xu=0$, and $c_ j\to c'>0$. Extract a summable density error as above. Then $\mu _{u_ j}\leq Cg\nu $ for a fixed $C$, so theorem 10.31.30 gives $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, convergence in capacity and weak convergence to $\mu _ u$. Since $c_ jf_ j\to c'f$ in $L^1(\nu )$, the limiting equation is $\mu _ u=c'\mu $, and the uniqueness of the scalar in theorem 10.31.45 gives $c'=c$. Hence the whole sequence of scalars converges to $c$, and $c_ j\mu _ j\to c\mu $ in total variation. Applying the same argument within any $L^1$-convergent subsequence proves its convergence in capacity and the equation for its limit. $\square$

10.31.48 Arbitrary weighted sums9E6F276DB6E3

With existence for general measures available, weighted addition extends to arbitrary $u,v\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Truncating the exponential weights gives finite data, which theorem 10.31.44 solves. A first-moment estimate prevents collapse of these solutions, and a rooftop involving their limit localizes the equation before the limit can be compared with the inputs.

Theorem 10.31.49.9A2FDB1A65B5 For every $u,v\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $\lambda >0$, there is a unique $a\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ satisfying

\[ \mu _ a=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (a-u)}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (a-v)}\mu _ v. \]

Moreover $a\leq \min (u,v)$ and $\mu _ a\leq \mu _ u+\mu _ v$.

Proof. By the scaling used in theorem 10.31.38, it suffices to take $\lambda =1$. For positive integers $j$, let

\[ \tau _ j=\mathrm{e}^{-\max (u,-j)}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{-\max (v,-j)}\mu _ v. \]

These are nonzero finite non-pluripolar measures, and they increase with $j$. Only the weights are truncated; the functions $\max (u,-j)$ and $\max (v,-j)$ need not be $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. Theorem 10.31.44 gives $a_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ with $\mu _{a_ j}=\mathrm{e}^{a_ j}\tau _ j$. Since $\tau _ j$ increases, exponential order implies that $a_ j$ decreases.

Set $r=P_\theta (u,v)\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. On $\{ a_ j<r\} $, one has $r\leq u,v$ and

\[ (r-a_ j)\mathrm{e}^{a_ j-\max (u,-j)}\leq (r-a_ j)\mathrm{e}^{a_ j-r}\leq \mathrm{e}^{-1}, \]

with the same inequality for $v$. Consequently

\[ \int _ X(r-a_ j)_+\, \mu _{a_ j} \leq \mathrm{e}^{-1}\bigl (\mu _ u(X)+\mu _ v(X)\bigr ). \]

Also $a_ j\leq a_1\leq \varphi +\sup _ Xa_1$. The one-sided first-moment criterion of proposition 10.31.8, with fixed reference $r$, bounds $\sup _ Xa_ j$ from below. Thus the decreasing sequence has an $L^1$ limit $a\not\equiv -\infty $, and the same criterion puts $a$ in $\mathcal E_\varphi $.

We identify the equation before comparing $a$ with the inputs. The rooftop

\[ q=P_\theta (a,u,v)\in \mathcal E_\varphi \]

lies below $a_ j$, $a$, $u$ and $v$.

For $T>0$, let $\chi _ T=g_ T(q)$, with $g_ T$ as in the proof of lemma 10.31.35: a continuous nondecreasing cutoff that vanishes where $q\leq -2T$, equals one where $q\geq -T$, and increases to one off the pole set of $q$ as $T\to \infty $. On $\{ \chi _ T>0\} $ both inputs exceed $-2T$. Hence, for $j>2T$,

9B190A9E8A47 10.31.50
\begin{equation} \chi _ T\mu _{a_ j} =\chi _ T \mathrm{e}^{a_ j}(\mathrm{e}^{-u}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{-v}\mu _ v). \end{equation}

The factors $\chi _ T\mathrm{e}^{-u}$ and $\chi _ T\mathrm{e}^{-v}$ are bounded by $\mathrm{e}^{2T}$. Since $a_ j\downarrow a$ and $a_ j\leq \sup _ Xa_1$, dominated convergence against the fixed measure $\mu _ u+\mu _ v$ gives convergence in total variation of the right-hand sides to the corresponding expression with $a$.

For the left-hand side, $a_ j\to a$ in capacity by lemma 5.29.19. Choose a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta $, and replace $a_ j$ by $b_ j=\max (a_ j,-2T)$ before forming bounded products. The $b_ j$ are uniformly bounded $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic functions converging in capacity. The expansion

\[ (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ j)^ n =\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ j)^ k \wedge (\theta -\Omega )^{n-k} \]

and bounded capacity continuity apply against the fixed quasi-continuous weight $\chi _ T$. Where $\chi _ T\ne 0$ no potential is truncated, so locality gives convergence to $\chi _ T\mu _ a$. In this way the possibly signed bounded product is handled before the cutoff is inserted.

Thus (10.31.50) holds in the limit. Let $T\to \infty $. Both sides ignore the poles of $q$, so monotone convergence gives

\[ \mu _ a=\mathrm{e}^{a-u}\mu _ u+\mathrm{e}^{a-v}\mu _ v. \]

Since $a\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, this also shows that the weighted right-hand side is finite.

For $\varepsilon >0$, the equation implies

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u<a-\varepsilon \} }\mu _ u \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon }\mathbf1_{\{ u<a-\varepsilon \} }\mu _ a. \]

Strict domination, theorem 13.19.1, gives $u\geq a-\varepsilon $, and letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $u\geq a$. Similarly $v\geq a$, so $\mu _ a\leq \mu _ u+\mu _ v$. Two solutions have the same exponentially weighted measure, so corollary 13.19.3, applied in both directions, gives uniqueness. $\square$

Corollary 10.31.51.DE6440832625 For fixed $\lambda >0$, write $u\star _\lambda v$ for the solution $a$ in the theorem. This operation on $\mathcal E_\varphi $ is commutative and associative, and, for $c\in \mathbb R$,

\[ (u+c)\star _\lambda (v+c)=(u\star _\lambda v)+c, \qquad u\star _\lambda u=u-\lambda ^{-1}\log 2. \]

Proof. Put $F_\lambda (u)=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\mu _ u$, with zero mass on the poles. The theorem says that $F_\lambda (u\star _\lambda v)=F_\lambda (u)+F_\lambda (v)$. By corollary 13.19.3, the map $F_\lambda $ is injective on $\mathcal E_\varphi $. Commutativity and associativity therefore follow from those of addition of positive measures, including measures of infinite total mass. Finally, $F_\lambda (u+c)=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda c}F_\lambda (u)$, which together with injectivity gives both identities. $\square$

10.31.52 Relative boundedness for $L^ p$ data2CD3B93A6226

If the datum has finite moments of sufficiently high order against normalized $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions, every normalized $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ whose measure is dominated by the datum differs from $\varphi $ by a bounded function. We work directly with $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $: truncating $u$ against $\varphi -j$ would introduce a contact measure that need not be controlled by the datum. Envelopes with concave obstacles, as in lemma 10.31.14, keep this domination and give a differential inequality for the distribution of $\varphi -u$.

Lemma 10.31.53.F816A9EF0F9D Let $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ with $u\leq \varphi $, and let $\chi :\mathbb R_{\leq 0}\to \mathbb R_{\leq 0}$ be increasing and concave, with $\chi (0)=0$. Suppose that $\chi $ is $C^{1,1}$ on compact intervals and that, for a finite constant $L\geq 1$,

\[ 1\leq \chi '\leq L. \]

Then the envelope

\[ p=P_\theta (\varphi +\chi (u-\varphi )) \]

belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $, and

\[ \mu _ p\leq \mathbf1_{\{ p=\varphi +\chi (u-\varphi )\} } \chi '(u-\varphi )^ n\mu _ u. \]

Here the obstacle and the contact set are defined off the pluripolar set where $u-\varphi $ is undefined, which all these measures ignore.

Proof. For $t\leq 0$, the bounds on $\chi '$ give $Lt\leq \chi (t)\leq t$. Thus

\[ Q_ L(u,\varphi )\leq p\leq u\leq \varphi . \]

The lower potential belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by lemma 16.28.63, so $p$ is not identically $-\infty $ and belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by upward stability. The obstacle is finite and quasi-continuous off the poles, so the contact theorem theorem 15.8.6 applies.

Let $\tau =\chi ^{-1}$. It is convex, with $L^{-1}\leq \tau '\leq 1$. We justify the transformation

\[ w=\varphi +\tau (p-\varphi ) \]

without assuming that $\varphi $ is locally bounded on some open set. On a coordinate ball choose a smooth function $q$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq 0$. The functions

\[ p_ m=\max (p,q-m),\qquad \varphi _ m=\max (\varphi ,q-m) \]

are locally bounded $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions on the ball, and $p_ m\leq \varphi _ m$. Lemma 5.28.13 applies to $w_ m=\varphi _ m+\tau (p_ m-\varphi _ m)$ and gives

\[ \theta _{w_ m}\geq 0,\qquad \mu _{w_ m}\geq \tau '(p_ m-\varphi _ m)^ n\mu _{p_ m}. \]

The function $(s,t)\mapsto t+\tau (s-t)$ on $\{ s\leq t\} $ is nondecreasing in each variable. Consequently $w_ m$ decreases as $m$ increases. Since $p\leq p_ m\leq w_ m\leq \varphi _ m$, the limit is not identically $-\infty $ on the ball. Off the poles it equals $w$. The local limits therefore agree on overlaps after regularization and define a global $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function.

On $D_ m=\{ p>q-m\} $, neither $p$ nor $\varphi $ is truncated. Thus $w_ m=w$ there, and plurifine locality transfers the product inequality to $D_ m$. These sets exhaust the complement of the poles of $p$ in the ball. Since all products ignore the discarded pluripolar set,

\[ \mu _ w\geq \tau '(p-\varphi )^ n\mu _ p. \]

The obstacle bound $p\leq \varphi +\chi (u-\varphi )$ gives $w\leq u$, with equality on the contact set of $p$. Ordered contact, the identity $\tau '(p-\varphi )=1/\chi '(u-\varphi )$ on that set, and contact support of $\mu _ p$ prove the assertion. $\square$

Proposition 10.31.54.36BCEF7C4255 Let $\mu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure. Suppose that, for some real $m>2n+1$,

\[ A_ m:=\sup _{\substack {v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\\ \sup _ Xv=0}} \int _ X(-v)^ m\, \mathrm{d}\mu <\infty . \]

There is a finite constant $C$, depending only on $n,\ell ,M,m,\mu (X)$ and $A_ m$, such that every $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ with $\sup _ Xu=0$ and $\mu _ u\leq \mu $ satisfies

\[ \varphi -C\leq u\leq \varphi . \]

Proof. As in proposition 10.31.15, we build a concave weight from the distribution of $\varphi -u$ and apply the preceding lemma. The moment bound on $\mu $ now yields a differential inequality for this weight, which bounds the depth of $\varphi -u$ under $\mu _ u$.

The envelope identity gives $u\leq \varphi \leq 0$. Put $x=\varphi -u$, defined off the poles, and $N=2n+1$. Since $x\leq -u$ and $\mu _ u\leq \mu $, Hölder's inequality gives the uniform first-moment bound

\[ B:=\int _ Xx\, \mu _ u \leq \int _ X(-u)\, \mathrm{d}\mu \leq \mu (X)^{1-1/m}A_ m^{1/m}=:A_1. \]

Let $H(t)=\mu _ u(\{ x>t\} )$ and $T=\sup \{ t\geq 0:H(t)>0\} $, with $T=0$ if this set is empty; at this point $T$ may be infinite. If $T<\infty $, zero domination applied to $u$ and $\varphi -t$ for $t>T$ gives $u\geq \varphi -T$. It is therefore enough to bound $T$. We may assume $T>1$, and we choose any $S$ with $1<S<T$.

Define, for $t\geq 0$,

\[ g(t)= \begin{cases} ((1+t)^{3/2}H(t))^{-1},& 0\leq t\leq S,\\ (1+t)^{-3/2},& t>S, \end{cases} \qquad F(t)=1+\int _0^ t g(s)\, \mathrm{d}s, \]

and set $W(t)=\int _0^ t F(s)^{1/N}\, \mathrm{d}s$ and $\chi (-t)=-W(t)$. Since $H(S)>0$, $F$ is bounded on the whole half-line, although its bound may depend on $S$ and $u$. It is locally Lipschitz and nondecreasing. Hence $\chi $ has bounded derivative, is increasing and concave, and satisfies the hypotheses of the preceding lemma. Also $W(t)\geq t$ and $W(t)\leq tF(t)^{1/N}$.

Since $H(t)\leq M$, Tonelli's theorem gives a bound independent of $S$ and $u$:

\[ \int _ X F(x)\, \mu _ u =\mu _ u(X)+\int _0^\infty g(t)H(t)\, \mathrm{d}t \leq 3\max (1,M)=:K. \]

Let $p=P_\theta (\varphi -W(x))\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, the envelope of the preceding lemma for this $\chi $. On its contact set the lemma bounds its measure by $F(x)^{n/N}\mu _ u$. Hence, using $N=2n+1$ and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\[ \begin{split} \int _ X(\varphi -p)^{1/2}\, \mu _ p & \leq \int _ X x^{1/2}F(x)^{(n+1/2)/N}\, \mu _ u\\ & \leq \left(\int _ Xx\, \mu _ u\right)^{1/2} \left(\int _ XF(x)\, \mu _ u\right)^{1/2} \leq \sqrt{KB}. \end{split} \]

The identity $P_\theta [p]=\varphi $ implies $p-\sup _ Xp\leq \varphi $. Since $p\leq \varphi \leq 0$ and $\mu _ p(X)\geq \ell $, the last integral is at least $\ell \sqrt{-\sup _ Xp}$. Consequently

\[ 0\leq -\sup _ Xp\leq KA_1/\ell ^2=:D. \]

Using $W(x)\leq \varphi -p\leq -p$, and applying the moment hypothesis to $p-\sup _ Xp$, we obtain

\begin{equation} \int _ XW(x)^ m\, \mathrm{d}\mu \leq 2^{m-1}\bigl (A_ m+D^ m\mu (X)\bigr )<C_0, \end{equation}

where we choose $C_0=1+2^{m-1}(A_ m+D^ m\mu (X))$. This constant is independent of $S$ and $u$.

For $0<t\leq S$, Chebyshev's inequality and $\mu _ u\leq \mu $ give

\[ H(t)\leq C_0W(t)^{-m}, \qquad F'(t)\geq \frac{W(t)^ m}{C_0(1+t)^{3/2}} \quad \text{for almost every }t. \]

Since $W'=F^{1/N}$, the derivative of $(W')^{N+1}$ is at least

\[ \frac{N+1}{NC_0}\, \frac{W^ mW'}{(1+t)^{3/2}}. \]

All functions being differentiated are absolutely continuous on compact intervals. Integrating from $0$ to $t$ and using $W(0)=0$ gives

\[ (W'(t))^{N+1} \geq \frac{N+1}{NC_0(m+1)} \frac{W(t)^{m+1}}{(1+t)^{3/2}}. \]

In particular

\[ W'(t)\geq c_0(1+t)^{-\gamma }W(t)^\kappa , \quad \gamma =\frac{3}{2(N+1)}<1,\quad \kappa =\frac{m+1}{N+1}>1, \]

with $c_0>0$ independent of $S$ and $u$. Integrating the derivative of $W^{1-\kappa }$ over $[1,S]$ yields

\[ (\kappa -1)c_0\int _1^ S(1+t)^{-\gamma }\, \mathrm{d}t \leq W(1)^{1-\kappa }-W(S)^{1-\kappa }\leq 1, \]

because $W(1)\geq 1$. Since the integral on the left diverges as $S\to \infty $, every $S<T$ is bounded by a constant independent of $S$ and $u$, and hence so is $T$. The zero-domination argument at the beginning of the proof now gives $u\geq \varphi -C$. The case $T\leq 1$, which includes $B=0$, was covered there as well. $\square$
