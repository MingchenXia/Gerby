15.9 Compactness with a common lower barrierD6040BDF0454

D6040BDF0454

We return to a semipositive big background $\theta $. Convergence of potentials in $L^1$ does not by itself imply convergence of their Monge–Ampère measures. We give a criterion for convergence in capacity when the potentials stay within a bounded distance of a reference admitting a smaller-background envelope. This common lower barrier ensures that all finite and infinite rooftop envelopes of the sequence are $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. A small error integral of each potential against its own Monge–Ampère measure bounds the corresponding error of the rooftops by a summable tail. Weighted lower semicontinuity passes this bound to the limit, and singular domination identifies the limit of the lower envelopes with the $L^1$ limit. Squeezing the sequence between lower and upper envelopes then gives convergence in capacity, and a domination of the measures also controls their mass on deep sublevels.

Theorem 15.9.1.A15C1A7CB4F8 Let $\theta \geq 0$ be big. Suppose $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies $P_{\delta \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty $ for some $0<\delta <1$. Let $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy, for a fixed $C$,

\[ \varphi -C\leq u_ j\leq \varphi +C,\qquad u_ j\longrightarrow u\text{ in }L^1(X). \]

If

B172EB5C34CB 15.9.2
\begin{equation} I_ j:=\int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)\longrightarrow 0, \end{equation}

then $u_ j\to u$ in capacity.

Proof. The canonical representative of the limit also lies between $\varphi -C$ and $\varphi +C$. It suffices to show that every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in capacity. We may therefore assume that $I_ j\leq 2^{-j}$ and that $u_ j\to u$ almost everywhere. For $N\geq j$ define

\[ p_{j,N}=P_\theta (\min \{ u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N\} ),\qquad p_ j=P_\theta \left(\inf _{\ell \geq j}u_\ell \right). \]

All these envelopes lie between $\varphi -C$ and $\varphi +C$. The contact theorem and ordered contact give an inequality for the finite rooftops. Partition the contact set of $p_{j,N}$ according to the first index $\ell $ for which $p_{j,N}=u_\ell $. On the corresponding piece, the measure of $p_{j,N}$ is bounded by that of $u_\ell $, and $\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}=\mathrm{e}^{u_\ell }$. Therefore

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p_{j,N}) \leq \sum _{\ell =j}^ N I_\ell \leq 2^{1-j}. \]

Only positive measures enter this argument, so it applies also when their total masses are infinite.

As $N\to \infty $, $p_{j,N}$ decreases to $p_ j$; the limit is the envelope of the infimum because every competitor lies below every finite rooftop. The sequence $p_ j$ increases, and its canonical limit $p$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. By lemma 5.29.19, both monotone limits hold in capacity. Applying lemma 13.14.8 to the bounded nonnegative weights $|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|$, first in $N$ and then in $j$, we obtain

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^ p-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p)=0. \]

The inequalities $p_ j\leq u_\ell $ for all $\ell \geq j$ imply $p\leq u$, first almost everywhere and then for the canonical representatives. Also $p\sim u\sim \varphi $. Off the pole sets, $\mathrm{e}^ p<\mathrm{e}^ u$ exactly on $\{ p<u\} $, so $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p)(\{ p<u\} )=0$. Since $P_{\delta \theta }(\varphi )-C$ is a competitor for $P_{\delta \theta }(p)$, the latter is not identically $-\infty $. Theorem 15.2.2, applied with coefficient zero, gives $p\geq u$, and hence $p=u$.

Finally, by Hartogs' lemma and the almost-everywhere convergence, $U_ j=(\sup _{\ell \geq j}u_\ell )^*$ decreases to $u$. The inequalities $p_ j\leq u_ j\leq U_ j$, together with the convergence in capacity of both monotone sequences, prove the claim. $\square$

Proposition 15.9.3.EFAF50966FDF In theorem 15.9.1, the two bounds on $u_ j$ may be replaced by

\[ \varphi -C\leq u_ j\leq C, \]

provided that the $L^1$ limit satisfies $u\preceq \varphi $. In particular, the approximants may be bounded while $\varphi $ is unbounded.

Proof. The $L^1$ limit satisfies $u\geq \varphi -C$, so the additional hypothesis gives $u\sim \varphi $. We repeat the construction of the finite rooftops in the theorem. Every $p_{j,N}$ is still $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and lies between $\varphi -C$ and $C$, which is enough for contact support and for the boundedness of the exponential weights. The resulting inequality is again

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p_{j,N})\leq 2^{1-j}. \]

The limits $p_ j$ exist because of the common lower bound, and $p_ j\leq u$ follows from $p_ j\leq u_\ell $ for every $\ell \geq j$ and almost-everywhere convergence. Thus $\varphi -C\leq p_ j\leq u$, so all $p_ j$ and their increasing limit $p$ have the same ordinary singularity type as $\varphi $. Monotone convergence in capacity and weighted lower semicontinuity give $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p)(\{ p<u\} )=0$, and singular domination applies to $(p,u)$ exactly as before, so $p=u$. The upper envelopes of the tails decrease to $u$, and the same squeezing argument gives convergence in capacity. Neither the finite rooftops nor the individual approximants needed to have the singularity type of $\varphi $. $\square$

Corollary 15.9.4.F856D22ADF4C In the setting of theorem 15.9.1, with the bounds $\varphi -C\leq u_ j\leq \varphi +C$, suppose either

  1. $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)\leq \nu $ for a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu $; or

  2. $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)\leq A\omega _{w_ j}^ n$, where $A$ is fixed and $w_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ are uniformly bounded and converge in capacity to a bounded $w$.

Then $u_ j\to u$ in capacity, all the measures $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)$ and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$ have finite mass, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j) \rightharpoonup \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \]

Proof. In the first case, lemma 5.44.2, applied to the exponentials $\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}$, proves (15.9.2). In the second case we use lemma 5.44.7 instead, since these exponentials are bounded and have a common lower bound for their curvature. In both cases the criterion gives convergence in capacity.

The masses are uniformly bounded by those of the dominating measures. For the deep sublevels we use $u_ j\geq \varphi -C$, which gives $\{ u_ j<-T\} \subset \{ \varphi <C-T\} $. In the first case the masses of these sublevels are at most $\nu (\{ \varphi <C-T\} )$, which tends to zero as $T\to \infty $ because $\nu $ does not charge the pole set of $\varphi $. In the second case lemma 5.29.16 bounds them by $C'\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ \varphi <C-T\} )$, which also tends to zero. The tail criterion theorem 13.15.13, in the general-background form of theorem 13.15.23, now gives finiteness of the limiting measure and weak convergence. $\square$

The reference $\varphi $ in these results need not be an envelope fixed point; the smaller-background condition suffices. Envelope fixed points enter when the two barriers must be derived from an a priori estimate, as in theorem 15.12.3. When no common lower barrier is available, one has to be constructed in a relative envelope class; this problem is treated in subsection 15.37.30.
