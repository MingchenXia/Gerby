12.18 Geometric bounds and determinant concentrationAA652CC345D8

AA652CC345D8

By theorem 12.9.2, the determinant densities of all normalized Gauduchon currents are bounded in the critical space $L^{n/(n-1)}$ exactly when the lower mass is positive. Together with the mass-invariance criterion of theorem 11.1.7, this gives geometric sufficient conditions for the critical bound. The critical exponent cannot be raised, even for currents that are powers of Kähler forms in one fixed class. The same concentrating family shows that currents approaching the maximum of the determinant functional for a fixed smooth density need not satisfy any uniform higher integrability, although the maximizer itself is smooth.

Throughout this section $n\geq 2$ and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive probability volume form. For a Hermitian form $h$, $k_ T$ denotes the determinant density relative to $\mathrm{d}V$ of a current $T\in \mathscr G_ h$, as in section 12.9. Put $q=n/(n-1)$.

Corollary 12.18.1.40D45A12B192 Suppose $g$ is a Hermitian form with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g^2)=0$, and put $V_ g=\int _ Xg^ n$. For every Hermitian form $h\geq cg$, $c>0$,

\[ v_-(h)\geq c^ nV_ g,\qquad \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_ h}\| k_ T\| _{L^ q(\mathrm{d}V)} \leq c^{-1}V_ g^{-1/n}. \]

Equality holds in the determinant bound when $h=g$ and $c=1$. In particular, on a compact Kähler manifold or a compact complex surface, the critical norm is uniformly bounded over the normalized Gauduchon currents of each fixed Hermitian form.

Proof. The identity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g^2)=2g\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g+2\mathrm{d}g\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g$ shows that $\mathrm{d}g\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g=0$, so theorem 11.1.7 gives $v_-(g)=V_ g$. The quotient in (12.15.5) is monotone and homogeneous in its numerator, so $h\geq cg$ implies $v_-(h)^{1/n}\geq cV_ g^{1/n}$. Theorem 12.9.2 turns these lower-mass bounds into the asserted determinant bounds, with equality when $h=g$.

A Kähler form satisfies both differential identities. On a surface, theorem 11.4.2 gives a metric $g$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=0$, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g^2)=0$ for degree reasons. By compactness, each fixed $h$ satisfies $h\geq cg$ for some $c>0$. $\square$

For a fixed smooth $F$, theorem 12.7.3 gives a unique maximizing current for the determinant functional $\mathfrak D_ F$ of (12.7.1), and this current is smooth and strictly positive, so its determinant density is bounded. The following construction shows that currents approaching this maximum satisfy no uniform bound on higher moments of their determinant densities.

Proposition 12.18.2.89AE8B534538 Let $X=E^ n$, where $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z)$ carries its flat area form of total area one, and write $\omega _ i$ for the pullback of this form from the $i$th factor. Set

\[ h=\sum _{i=1}^ n\omega _ i,\qquad \mathrm{d}V=\omega _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \omega _ n,\qquad V=n!,\qquad \alpha =\frac{n-1}{n}. \]

For $F=0$, the unique maximizing current is $T_*=h^{n-1}/V$, with $k_{T_*}=V^{-1/n}$ and $\mathfrak D_0(T_*)=V^{-1/n}$. There are smooth strictly positive closed forms $S_\varepsilon \in \mathscr G_ h$, $0<\varepsilon <\min \{ 1/4,\mathrm e^{-1}\} $, such that

\[ \| k_{S_\varepsilon }\| _{L^ q}=V^{-1/n},\qquad \mathfrak D_0(S_\varepsilon )\longrightarrow V^{-1/n}, \]

while for every fixed finite $p>q$,

827DBF9602F3 12.18.3
\begin{equation} \int _ X k_{S_\varepsilon }^{p}\, \mathrm{d}V \geq C_{n,p}[\log (1/\varepsilon )]^{-\alpha p} \varepsilon ^{2-2\alpha p}\longrightarrow +\infty . \end{equation}

Their $L^\infty $ norms also diverge. Each $S_\varepsilon $ is the $(n-1)$st power of a Kähler form in the fixed class $V^{-1/(n-1)}[h]$, and has the same Bott–Chern, de Rham and Aeppli classes as $T_*$. No $S_\varepsilon $ attains the maximum of $\mathfrak D_0$.

Proof. Put $H_ i=\bigwedge _{j\ne i}\omega _ j$. Expanding the wedge powers gives $h^{n-1}=(n-1)!\sum _ iH_ i$ and $h\wedge H_ i=\mathrm{d}V$. Thus $T_*=(1/n)\sum _ iH_ i$ is normalized, and the determinant formula gives $k_{T_*}=V^{-1/n}$. The smooth equation for $F=0$ is solved by the zero potential, since $h^ n=V\, \mathrm{d}V$; theorem 12.7.3 therefore gives the maximal value and the uniqueness of the maximizer.

To construct $S_\varepsilon $, choose a nonnegative smooth function $\beta $ compactly supported in the unit disk in $\mathbb C$, of integral one, with $\beta \geq \beta _0>0$ on a disk of radius $r_0\in (0,1)$. On an embedded disk in the first elliptic factor put $b_\varepsilon (z)=\varepsilon ^{-2}\beta (z/\varepsilon )$, extended by zero; it has integral one. For $0<t<1$, set

\[ \begin{gathered} f_{\varepsilon ,t}=1-t+tb_\varepsilon ,\qquad a=V^{-1/(n-1)},\\ \gamma _{\varepsilon ,t}=a(f_{\varepsilon ,t}\omega _1+\omega _2+\cdots +\omega _ n), \qquad T_{\varepsilon ,t}=\gamma _{\varepsilon ,t}^{n-1}. \end{gathered} \]

The form $\gamma _{\varepsilon ,t}$ is strictly positive, and it is closed because $f_{\varepsilon ,t}$ depends only on the first factor, which has real dimension two. Direct expansion gives

A89800F03DB4 12.18.4
\begin{equation} T_{\varepsilon ,t}=\frac1n\left(H_1+f_{\varepsilon ,t}\sum _{i=2}^ nH_ i\right), \qquad k_{T_{\varepsilon ,t}}=V^{-1/n}f_{\varepsilon ,t}^{\alpha }. \end{equation}

Since $\int _ Xf_{\varepsilon ,t}\, \mathrm{d}V=1$, the trace integral is one, so $T_{\varepsilon ,t}\in \mathscr G_ h$, and $\| k_{T_{\varepsilon ,t}}\| _{L^ q}=V^{-1/n}$ because $\alpha q=1$.

The mean-zero function $f_{\varepsilon ,t}-1$ has a smooth real potential $\psi $ on $E$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =(f_{\varepsilon ,t}-1)\omega _1$; for example, the mean-zero Fourier series solving $\Delta \psi =4\pi (f_{\varepsilon ,t}-1)$ in the area-one coordinates. Consequently $\gamma _{\varepsilon ,t}-ah=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a\psi )$. Since $T_*=(ah)^{n-1}$, the closed form $R=\sum _{j=0}^{n-2}\gamma _{\varepsilon ,t}^{n-2-j}\wedge (ah)^ j$ satisfies

\[ T_{\varepsilon ,t}-T_*=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a\psi R). \]

This proves all the assertions about classes, including the case $n=2$, where $R=1$.

Jensen's inequality and $f_{\varepsilon ,t}\geq 1-t$ give

\[ V^{-1/n}(1-t)^\alpha \leq \mathfrak D_0(T_{\varepsilon ,t}) =V^{-1/n}\int _ Xf_{\varepsilon ,t}^{\alpha }\, \mathrm{d}V <V^{-1/n}, \]

where the inequality is strict because $f_{\varepsilon ,t}$ is nonconstant and $0<\alpha <1$. On the product of the disk of radius $r_0\varepsilon $ with the other factors, we have $f_{\varepsilon ,t}\geq t\beta _0\varepsilon ^{-2}$, and this set has volume $\pi r_0^2\varepsilon ^2$. Hence

\[ \begin{split} \int _ Xk_{T_{\varepsilon ,t}}^ p\, \mathrm{d}V & \geq V^{-p/n}\pi r_0^2\beta _0^{\alpha p}t^{\alpha p} \varepsilon ^{2-2\alpha p},\\ \| k_{T_{\varepsilon ,t}}\| _\infty & \geq V^{-1/n}\beta _0^\alpha t^\alpha \varepsilon ^{-2\alpha }. \end{split} \]

Now take $t=1/\log (1/\varepsilon )$, which lies in $(0,1)$ by the restriction on $\varepsilon $, and $S_\varepsilon =T_{\varepsilon ,t}$. Then $\mathfrak D_0(S_\varepsilon )$ converges to its maximum, while both lower bounds diverge for $p>q$ because $\alpha p>1$. All constants are independent of $\varepsilon $. $\square$

If instead $t\in (0,1)$ is kept fixed in the same construction, then $\int _ Xk_{T_{\varepsilon ,t}}^ p\, \mathrm{d}V\geq C_{n,p,t}\varepsilon ^{2-2\alpha p}$ and $\| k_{T_{\varepsilon ,t}}\| _\infty \geq C_{n,t}\varepsilon ^{-2\alpha }$. Thus there are no uniform bounds on higher moments, either over the full normalized cone or along a maximizing sequence for one smooth density. Every member of these families attains the supremum $V^{-1/n}$ of the critical norm in corollary 12.18.1, whereas the maximum of $\mathfrak D_0$ is attained only by $T_*$.

12.18.5 Intrinsic determinant measures and the complete profile5613B7E2253B

Keep the smooth positive probability volume $\mathrm dV$ fixed. For a positive $(n-1,n-1)$-current $T$ of finite mass, let $\Gamma _{0,h}(T)$ denote the determinant measure of (12.7.1) computed with a positive auxiliary metric $h$. The current need not be smooth or strictly positive. The following coordinate calculation identifies the measure itself, including on singular coefficient measures.

Lemma 12.18.6.5771D42397D7 For a fixed positive current $T$ and fixed $\mathrm dV$, the measure $\Gamma _{0,h}(T)$ is independent of the positive auxiliary metric $h$. This statement includes singular and degenerate currents.

Proof. In a holomorphic frame put $\lambda =\bigwedge _{j=1}^ n(i\, dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j)$, $h=i\sum H_{ij}\, dz_ i\wedge d\bar z_ j$ and $\mathrm dV=v\lambda $. Positive currents have measure coefficients. Choose a positive Radon measure $\nu $ dominating them. For a smooth real form $\xi =i\sum \Xi _{ij}\, dz_ i\wedge d\bar z_ j$, let $Q$ be the positive semidefinite Hermitian coefficient matrix characterized by $\xi \wedge T=\operatorname {tr}(\Xi Q)\nu $. Then

\[ \sigma _ h=\operatorname {tr}(HQ)\nu ,\qquad P_ h=\frac{nH^{1/2}QH^{1/2}}{\operatorname {tr}(HQ)}. \]

The formula holds on $Q\ne 0$; zero is assigned when $Q=0$. Since $h^ n=n!\det (H)\lambda $, determinant algebra gives

DCAEAF642D39 12.18.7
\begin{equation} \Gamma _{0,h}(T) =n(n!)^{-1/n}v^{1/n}(\det Q)^{1/n}\nu . \end{equation}

This expression has no $H$. Replacing $\nu $ by $L\nu $, where $L>0$ almost everywhere, replaces $Q$ by $Q/L$, leaving the measure unchanged; a common dominating measure compares any two choices. The original geometric definition ensures compatibility under frame changes. Matrix AM–GM bounds it locally by a constant times a reference trace measure, proving local finiteness. Compactness gives a finite Radon measure. $\square$

Write $\Gamma _0(T)$ for this intrinsic measure.

Theorem 12.18.8.3EA495FF9D1A Let $\beta $ be smooth positive Hermitian with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\beta ^ j)=0$ for $1\leq j\leq n-1$, $V=\int _ X\beta ^ n$, and let $h=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$ for a smooth global real function $u$. Then

\[ \mathscr G_ h=\mathscr G_\beta ,\qquad \{ \Gamma _{0,h}(T):T\in \mathscr G_ h\} =\{ \Gamma _{0,\beta }(T):T\in \mathscr G_\beta \} . \]

Every measure in this common family has a density $k_ T$ satisfying

\[ k_ T\geq 0,\qquad \| k_ T\| _{L^{n/(n-1)}(\mathrm dV)}\leq V^{-1/n}. \]

For every real or complex $p\in L^ n(\mathrm dV)$,

290803738499 12.18.9
\begin{equation} \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_ h}\int _ X|p|\, \Gamma _0(T) =V^{-1/n}\| p\| _{L^ n(\mathrm dV)}. \end{equation}

All suprema retain singular and degenerate tests.

Proof. Distributional integration by parts gives $\int h\wedge T=\int \beta \wedge T+\int u\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T =\int \beta \wedge T$. This proves equality of the actual current sets; Lemma 12.18.6 proves equality of their measure sets.

Take a smooth strictly positive real $r$ and put $M=\int r^ n \mathrm dV$. By theorem 14.2.1 there are a smooth potential $w$, a positive form $A=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ and a positive coefficient in its prescribed-volume equation. The mass-invariance hypothesis and theorem 11.1.7 determine that coefficient, giving

AF0F557495C3 12.18.10
\begin{equation} A^ n=\frac{Vr^ n}{M}\, \mathrm dV. \end{equation}

In particular, the total mass of $A$ is $V$. No estimates uniform in a degenerating smooth density are required.

For every $T\in \mathscr G_\beta $, integration by parts gives $\int A\wedge T=1$. In an $A$-unitary frame, matrix AM–GM gives $\det (P_ A)^{1/n}\leq 1$, including singular coefficient densities. Thus as Radon measures

\[ \Gamma _0(T)\leq (\mathrm dV/A^ n)^{1/n}(A\wedge T). \]

Multiplying by $r$ and using (12.18.10) yields

B462DE2DBA4C 12.18.11
\begin{equation} \int r\, \Gamma _0(T)\leq V^{-1/n}\| r\| _{L^ n(\mathrm dV)}. \end{equation}

Replacing a smooth nonnegative $r$ by $r+\eta $ and letting $\eta \downarrow 0$ extends this bound to nonnegative smooth tests. For smooth real or complex $s$, smooth positive approximations $\sqrt{|s|^2+\eta ^2}$ imply $|\int s\, \Gamma _0(T)|\leq V^{-1/n}\| s\| _ n$. Consequently integration against $\Gamma _0(T)$ extends continuously from smooth functions to $L^ n$. Its positive dual representative is $k_ T\in L^{n/(n-1)}$ with the stated bound. Equality on smooth tests identifies the two Radon measures, proving absolute continuity. The integrals of arbitrary $L^ n$ roots are therefore well-defined.

For the reverse inequality with $r>0$ smooth, let $T_ r=A^{n-1}/V$. It is smooth and positive. Write $E=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$. For $j\leq n-1$, expansion and closedness of $E$ give

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(A^ j)=\sum _{l=0}^ j\binom jl\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\beta ^{j-l})\wedge E^ l=0. \]

The term with $j=l$ differentiates the constant one. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ r=0$, and smooth integration by parts gives

\[ \int _ X\beta \wedge A^{n-1} =\int _ X A^ n-\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge A^{n-1}=V. \]

Consequently $T_ r\in \mathscr G_\beta $. Its positive root is $\gamma =V^{-1/(n-1)}A$, so

\[ k_{T_ r}=(\gamma ^ n/\mathrm dV)^{(n-1)/n} =V^{-1/n}\frac{r^{n-1}}{\| r\| _ n^{n-1}}. \]

It attains equality in (12.18.11). For a nonnegative $p\in L^ n$, take smooth positive $r_ j\to p$ in $L^ n$. The common dual bound controls $\int (p-r_ j)k_{T_{r_ j}}\mathrm dV$, proving the lower bound in (12.18.9); the upper bound already holds for every current. Apply the same argument to $|p|$ for complex roots. $\square$

12.18.12 Fixed determinant data with degenerating ellipticity95D23527B325

Here $v_-(h)$ and $v_+(h)$ denote the infimum and supremum of $\int _ X(h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ over bounded $h$-psh potentials $w$.

Theorem 12.18.13.BDB7ADF7845B On $X=(\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z))^ n$, $n\geq 2$, there are positive metrics $\omega _\varepsilon $ in a fixed Kähler class, $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$, with fixed complete current and determinant-measure families and fixed endpoint (12.18.9), while their least relative eigenvalue equals $\varepsilon $. They have uniformly bounded smooth coefficients and global potentials, and

\[ v_-(\omega _\varepsilon )=v_+(\omega _\varepsilon )=n!>0. \]

The manifold has a global holomorphic volume form and $c_1=0$. Every displayed metric is already balanced and pluriclosed.

Proof. Write $z_ j=x_ j+iy_ j$, $\beta _ j=(i/2)dz_ j\wedge d\bar z_ j=dx_ j\wedge dy_ j$, $\beta =\sum _ j\beta _ j$, $V=n!$, and $\mathrm dV=\beta ^ n/V$. Set

\[ u_\varepsilon =\frac{1-\varepsilon }{4\pi }\cos (4\pi x_1), \qquad a_\varepsilon =1-(1-\varepsilon )\cos (4\pi x_1). \]

For a function of $x_1$, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=(u''/(4\pi ))\beta _1$. Hence

\[ \omega _\varepsilon =\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon =a_\varepsilon \beta _1+\sum _{j=2}^ n\beta _ j. \]

These are globally periodic smooth positive closed forms with $\varepsilon \leq a_\varepsilon \leq 2-\varepsilon $ and $\omega _\varepsilon \leq 2\beta $. Their minimum relative eigenvalue is $\varepsilon $. They converge smoothly to a semipositive form losing rank on $\{ x_1\in \tfrac 12\mathbb Z\} $. All fixed-order smooth norms of the coefficients and potentials are uniformly bounded. Theorem 12.18.8 proves equality of the entire current data and the exact norm at every $\varepsilon $.

Since $\omega _\varepsilon $ is closed, theorem 11.1.7 gives mass $V$ for every bounded $\omega _\varepsilon $-plurisubharmonic potential. The explicit formula also gives $\int _ X\omega _\varepsilon ^ n=V$, since $a_\varepsilon $ has mean one. Thus both extrema over bounded potentials equal $V$. The global form $dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dz_ n$ trivializes the canonical bundle; closed positive metrics are balanced and pluriclosed. $\square$

12.18.14 The same obstruction for nonclosed pluriclosed metrics10AA5741C871

Theorem 12.18.15.17E4315391CC On the fixed torus above, fix $0<\tau <1/2$. There is a positive nonclosed pluriclosed metric $h_\tau $ and a family $\Omega _\varepsilon =h_\tau +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon >0$, $0<\varepsilon \leq 1/2$, with identical actual current sets and intrinsic determinant-measure families. For any fixed smooth positive probability volume their entire-family endpoint is

\[ \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_{\Omega _\varepsilon }} \int _ X|p|\, \Gamma _0(T) =V_\tau ^{-1/n}\| p\| _{L^ n(\mathrm dV)},\qquad V_\tau =n!(1-\tau ^2/2). \]

Both bounded-potential masses equal $V_\tau $. All fixed-order smooth coefficient and potential norms are uniformly bounded, while

\[ \min _ X\lambda _{\min }(\Omega _\varepsilon \mid \beta )=\varepsilon , \qquad d\Omega _\varepsilon =dh_\tau \ne 0. \]

The flat $\beta $ is a fixed balanced metric on this same manifold with $c_1=0$; each $\Omega _\varepsilon $ is pluriclosed.

Proof. With the torus coordinates above set $s=\sin (2\pi x_1)$ and

\[ \xi =\frac i2(dz_1\wedge d\bar z_2+dz_2\wedge d\bar z_1) =dx_1\wedge dy_2+dx_2\wedge dy_1, \qquad h_\tau =\beta +\tau s\xi . \]

The first relative Hermitian block is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1& \tau s\\ \tau s& 1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so positivity follows from $|\tau s|<1$. Moreover

\[ dh_\tau =2\pi \tau \cos (2\pi x_1) dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dy_1\ne 0. \]

Because $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s$ is proportional to $dz_1\wedge d\bar z_1$, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(s\xi )=0$. The first two-coordinate metric is thus pluriclosed, and its square is top degree on that complex surface. Expanding with the remaining closed curve factors proves

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(h_\tau ^ j)=0\quad (1\leq j\leq n-1),\qquad \int _ X h_\tau ^ n=n!\int _0^1(1-\tau ^2\sin ^2(2\pi x_1))dx_1 =V_\tau . \]

Use the same $u_\varepsilon $ and $a_\varepsilon $ as in Theorem 12.18.13, now with $\varepsilon \leq 1/2$. Then

\[ \Omega _\varepsilon =a_\varepsilon \beta _1+ \sum _{j\geq 2}\beta _ j+\tau s\xi , \qquad a_\varepsilon =\varepsilon +2(1-\varepsilon )s^2. \]

The first block of $\Omega _\varepsilon -\varepsilon \beta $ has diagonal entries $2(1-\varepsilon )s^2$ and $1-\varepsilon $, and determinant

\[ \bigl [2(1-\varepsilon )^2-\tau ^2\bigr ]s^2\geq 0. \]

It is positive semidefinite; the remaining diagonal entries are $1-\varepsilon $. Hence $\Omega _\varepsilon \geq \varepsilon \beta $, with equality in the least eigenvalue at $s=0$. Also $\Omega _\varepsilon \leq 3\beta $, all fixed-order smooth norms are uniformly bounded, and the smooth semipositive limit loses rank at $s=0$. Since $d\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon =0$, the exterior derivative is the displayed fixed nonzero $dh_\tau $.

The lower-power expansion in the proof of theorem 12.18.8 applies to $\Omega _\varepsilon =h_\tau +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon $; it gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega _\varepsilon ^ j)=0$ for $j\leq n-1$, in particular pluriclosedness. Its volume is $V_\tau $, either by integrating the displayed determinant or by theorem 11.1.7. That theorem also gives mass $V_\tau $ for every bounded $\Omega _\varepsilon $-plurisubharmonic potential, so both mass extrema equal $V_\tau $. Theorem 12.18.8 applied to $h_\tau $ gives equality of the entire current and measure sets and their exact endpoint for every $p\in L^ n$. The balanced trace is fixed as well:

\[ \int _ X\Omega _\varepsilon \wedge \beta ^{n-1}=n!. \]

The off-diagonal term has zero trace and the mean of $a_\varepsilon $ equals one. The global holomorphic volume form gives $c_1=0$. The manifold itself is already Kähler, so these nonclosed metrics show that the complete determinant profile and positive bounded-potential mass extrema can stay fixed while the least relative eigenvalue tends to zero. The coexistence problem of 24.4.22 remains open. $\square$

12.18.16 Concentration at exact maximizers for varying rootsA4F04193B5B9

Sharp determinant bounds also need not give uniform integrability of the determinant powers or preserve maximizers under weak current limits.

Theorem 12.18.17.6072DDDAB57F On $X=(\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm i\mathbb Z))^ n$, let $\beta _ j=\mathrm dx_ j\wedge \mathrm dy_ j$, $\beta =\sum _ j\beta _ j$, $V=n!$ and $\mathrm dV=\beta ^ n/V$. There are smooth positive closed $A_\varepsilon =\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon $, $0<\varepsilon <1$, and $T_\varepsilon =A_\varepsilon ^{n-1}/V \in \mathscr G_\beta $ satisfying

\[ \int _ X k_{T_\varepsilon }^{n/(n-1)}\mathrm dV=V^{-1/(n-1)}. \]

Each $T_\varepsilon $ is an actual dual maximizer for a specified unit $L^ n$ root. The potentials $w_\varepsilon $ have uniformly bounded oscillation and converge uniformly to a continuous canonical $\beta $-psh $w_0$. The currents converge weakly to $T_0=(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_0)^{n-1}/V\in \mathscr G_\beta $, but $\Gamma _0(T_0)=0$. The power densities above are not uniformly integrable. Nevertheless every $w_\varepsilon $, including $w_0$, has bounded-potential Monge–Ampère mass $V$.

Proof. Put $r=1-\varepsilon $ and

\[ P_ r(x)=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos (2\pi x)+r^2},\qquad f_\varepsilon =\varepsilon +(1-\varepsilon )P_ r. \]

The positive periodic Poisson kernel has mean one and Fourier series

\[ P_ r=1+2\sum _{m\geq 1}r^ m\cos (2\pi mx). \]

Define

\[ w_\varepsilon =-\frac{2(1-\varepsilon )}{\pi } \sum _{m\geq 1}\frac{r^ m\cos (2\pi mx_1)}{m^2}. \]

For fixed $\varepsilon >0$ this is smooth. Differentiation gives $w_\varepsilon ''=4\pi (f_\varepsilon -1)$, and hence

\[ A_\varepsilon =f_\varepsilon (x_1)\beta _1+ \sum _{j=2}^ n\beta _ j=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\varepsilon >0. \]

It is closed, and $\int A_\varepsilon ^ n=V$. Theorem 12.18.8 and the smooth-root formula give

\[ k_{T_\varepsilon }=V^{-1/n}f_\varepsilon ^{(n-1)/n}, \qquad k_{T_\varepsilon }^{n/(n-1)} =V^{-1/(n-1)}f_\varepsilon . \]

Thus the displayed power integral attains the upper bound over all currents, not a bound for a selected family. More precisely, $p_\varepsilon =f_\varepsilon ^{1/n}$ has $L^ n$ norm one, and $\int p_\varepsilon k_{T_\varepsilon }\mathrm dV=V^{-1/n}$, so $T_\varepsilon $ attains the original entire-family dual supremum for this varying root. No common fixed density is asserted.

The Fourier series bounds $|w_\varepsilon |\leq \pi /3$ and implies uniform convergence to

\[ w_0=-\frac2\pi \sum _{m\geq 1}\frac{\cos (2\pi mx_1)}{m^2}. \]

This limit is continuous and canonically $\beta $-psh by distributional positivity of $A_0=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_0$. The probability measures $P_ r(x_1)dx_1$ converge weakly to the periodic Dirac mass at $x_1=0$, as follows from their Fourier coefficients $r^{|m|}$. Thus

\[ A_0=\delta _{x_1=0}\beta _1+\sum _{j=2}^ n\beta _ j, \]

where the Dirac measure is tensored with Lebesgue measure in the remaining real coordinates. Its bounded local products are genuine Bedford–Taylor products. Locally the first-coordinate current $\beta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_0$ is pulled back from one complex coordinate, so its powers of degree at least two vanish in the bounded-product recursion. The remaining factors are smooth and closed. Multilinear expansion therefore contains at most one first-coordinate factor; the same expansion for $A_\varepsilon $ passes directly to the distributional limit. Consequently $T_\varepsilon \to T_0=A_0^{n-1}/V$ weakly. This current is positive, closed and normalized by $\int \beta \wedge T_0=1$.

In its positive coefficient matrix the absolutely continuous part is proportional to the first diagonal matrix below; the singular part on $x_1=0$ is proportional to the second:

\[ \operatorname {diag}(1,0,\ldots ,0),\qquad \operatorname {diag}(0,1,\ldots ,1). \]

Their ranks are respectively one and $n-1$, both less than $n$. With a measure dominating these mutually singular parts, formula (12.18.7) therefore gives $\Gamma _0(T_0)=0$. This is not the Monge–Ampère measure of $w_0$, which has total mass $V$ by theorem 11.1.7.

Away from $x_1=0$, $f_\varepsilon \to 0$ pointwise; its integral against $\mathrm dV$ remains one. Thus the positive power densities are not uniformly integrable: almost-everywhere convergence to zero and uniform integrability would force their fixed positive integrals to vanish. In particular $T\mapsto \int k_ T^{n/(n-1)}\mathrm dV$ is not continuous under these weak current limits, even with uniformly convergent continuous potentials. The intrinsic measures themselves satisfy $\Gamma _0(T_\varepsilon )\to 0$ in total mass; this follows from their uniform $L^{n/(n-1)}$ bound by splitting off arbitrarily small neighborhoods of $x_1=0$. The discontinuity concerns their power integrals, not that last measure limit. $\square$

The maximizing root varies with $\varepsilon $. This construction concerns the complete dual profile and the critical power integral; it does not give a sequence of exact maximizers for one fixed smooth density.
