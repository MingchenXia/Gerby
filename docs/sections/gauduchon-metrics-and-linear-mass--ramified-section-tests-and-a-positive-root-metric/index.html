11.30 Ramified section tests and a positive root metricEF54BD838F8D

EF54BD838F8D

The untwisted test of corollary 11.29.10 can fail even for a line bundle with a positively curved metric, because the evaluation map of a globally generated bundle may fail to be an immersion where a holomorphic map ramifies. We give an explicit example on the product of a hyperelliptic curve with a projective space, where the complete spaces of sections can be computed. The same example gives a family of weighted tests, built from a root of the Fubini–Study weight of a very ample power instead of the twists of the preceding section, whose first successful level is explicit and can be made arbitrarily large.

Fix integers $n\geq 2$ and $q\geq 2$. Choose a section $\beta \in H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(2q))$ with $2q$ distinct zeros. Let $\pi :C\to \mathbb P^1$ be the double cover defined in the total space of $\mathcal O(q)$ by the equation $w^2=\beta $, where $w$ is the tautological coordinate, and put

\[ \begin{gathered} X=C\times \mathbb P^{n-1},\qquad L_ C=\pi ^*\mathcal O(1),\\ L=L_ C\boxtimes \mathcal O(1),\qquad A=L^ q,\qquad F=L^{-1}. \end{gathered} \]

Lemma 11.30.1.8B6FA358A78D The manifold $X$ is smooth compact projective. The line bundle $L$ is globally generated, $A$ is very ample, and for every integer $k\geq 0$,

\[ \begin{aligned} H^0(X,L^ k)& = \bigl (H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(k)) \oplus H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(k-q))\bigr ) \\ & \qquad \otimes H^0(\mathbb P^{n-1},\mathcal O(k)). \end{aligned} \]

In the second summand, the sections are multiplied by the coordinate $w$ of the cover. In particular,

\[ h^0(X,L^ k)=\bigl (k+1+\max (k-q+1,0)\bigr ) \binom {n-1+k}{k}. \]

For a basis $\sigma _1,\ldots ,\sigma _ N$ of $H^0(X,A)$, the Fubini–Study weight and the corresponding root weight, in a local frame of $L$ and the induced frame of $A=L^ q$, are

\[ \phi _ A=\log \sum _{i=1}^ N|\sigma _ i|^2, \qquad \phi =\phi _ A/q. \]

The metric $\mathrm e^{-\phi }$ on $L$ has strictly positive curvature. Thus it is strictly Griffiths-positive, and $G(v)=\mathrm e^{\phi }|v|^2$ is strictly plurisubharmonic on $F$ off its zero section.

Proof. The direct image of the structure sheaf of $C$ is $\mathcal O\oplus \mathcal O(-q)\, w$, with multiplication of the second summand given by $w^2=\beta $. Tensoring by $\mathcal O(k)$, the local basis $1,w$ gives the stated decomposition of sections on $C$. Simple zeros of $\beta $ make the cover smooth: near a branch point the equation gives a coordinate $w$ with the base coordinate a nonzero multiple of $w^2$ to leading order. The cover is connected because the rational function representing $\beta $ has a simple zero and therefore is not a square. Finiteness over compact $\mathbb P^1$ makes $C$ compact.

On $C$, the complete sections of $L_ C^ q$ are the degree-$q$ base monomials together with $w$. The map to projective space defined by these sections separates the base points by the Veronese coordinates, separates the two sheets by $w$, and has injective differential. At the ramification points the coordinate $w$ gives the missing derivative, and elsewhere the base coordinates do. Compactness makes this map an embedding. This shows that $C$ is projective and that $L_ C^ q$ is very ample.

For the decomposition of sections on the product, expand a section on each $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ fiber in its complete degree-$k$ monomial basis. The coefficients depend holomorphically on $C$ and transform as sections of $L_ C^ k$. This proves the displayed tensor identity without a cohomological vanishing assumption. The dimensions follow from the monomial counts, since line bundles of negative degree on $\mathbb P^1$ have no nonzero sections. The degree-one base sections generate $L_ C$, hence $L$. The product of the curve embedding and the degree-$q$ Veronese embedding, followed by the Segre embedding, shows that $A$ is very ample.

Its complete linear system therefore defines an immersion, so the Fubini–Study metric on $A$ has strictly positive curvature. Dividing its weight by $q$ defines a metric on $L$, since the transition functions of $A=L^ q$ are the $q$th powers of those of $L$, and its curvature is $1/q$ times that positive form. The final assertion follows from lemma 11.29.1. $\square$

11.30.2 The weighted family and its first successful level5B78AD45B21A

Keep the root metric $\mathrm e^{-\phi }$ on $L$, and choose the sphere bundle $S$ and the compact set $K$ for $F$ as in section 11.29. For each integer $j\geq 0$, use the complete space

\[ \widetilde V_ j=H^0(X,L^{j+1}) \]

and pair its basis with $v\in F_ x$ to get a local column $p_ j(v)$, written in a local frame of $L^ j$. For every Hermitian coefficient matrix $B$, define

\[ \widetilde G_{j,B}(v)=\mathrm e^{-j\phi (x)}p_ j(v)^*B p_ j(v). \]

These are globally defined functions, quadratic on each fiber. Their Levi matrices are

\[ \begin{gathered} \widetilde\Psi _ j(v,\xi )=\mathrm e^{-j\phi } (r_ jr_ j^*-j\kappa p_ jp_ j^*),\\ r_ j=\mathrm dp_ j(\xi )-j\phi _ u p_ j,\qquad \kappa =\phi _{u\bar u}, \end{gathered} \]

where $u$ is the projection of $\xi $ to the base. As in (11.29.3), direct differentiation gives $\mathcal L\widetilde G_{j,B}(\xi )=\operatorname {tr}(B\widetilde\Psi _ j)$, and $\widetilde\Psi _ j$ transforms in the same way under changes of frames.

Proposition 11.30.3.A8FAF022D4CA The first integer $j\geq 0$ for which some $\widetilde G_{j,B}$, with $B$ an arbitrary Hermitian matrix, is strictly plurisubharmonic off the zero section is $j=q-1$. At every lower level, zero belongs to $\operatorname {conv}(\widetilde\Psi _ j(K))$; one atom witnesses this at $j=0$, and two atoms suffice at each $1\leq j<q-1$.

Proof. At $j=q-1$, choose the basis $\sigma _ i$ that defines $\phi _ A$ and take $B=\operatorname {Id}$. Since $p_ j(v)=v(\sigma _ i(x))_ i$ in the chosen frames,

\[ \widetilde G_{q-1,\operatorname {Id}}(v) =\mathrm e^{-(q-1)\phi }|v|^2\mathrm e^{q\phi } =\mathrm e^\phi |v|^2. \]

This function is strictly plurisubharmonic by lemma 11.30.1.

Now suppose that $0\leq j<q-1$. Then $j+1<q$, so by lemma 11.30.1 every section in $\widetilde V_ j$ comes from the first summand of the decomposition, which does not involve $w$. At a ramification point of $C$, choose a local coordinate $t$ on $C$, and use frames of the bundles pulled back from $\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^{n-1}$. Writing $\zeta $ for the coordinates of the factor $\mathbb P^{n-1}$, the column has the form $p_ j(v)=vU_ j(\pi (t),\zeta )$. It is nonzero for $v\ne 0$, since $L^{j+1}$ is globally generated, and since $\pi $ ramifies at the point, its derivative in the $t$ direction vanishes there when $v$ is held fixed.

Choose such a fiber vector $v$ on the sphere bundle $S$. Write tangent vectors to the total space of $F$ by their components in the $t$ direction, in the $\zeta $ directions and in the fiber direction. Let $\xi _ v=(0,0,v)$ be the vertical radial vector, let $u=\partial /\partial t$, and let $\xi _ h=(u,0,j\phi _ u v)$. At this point $\mathrm dp_ j(\xi _ v)=p_ j$ and $r_ j(\xi _ h)=0$. Thus

\[ \begin{aligned} \widetilde\Psi _ j(v,\xi _ v)& =\mathrm e^{-j\phi }p_ jp_ j^*,\\ \widetilde\Psi _ j(v,\xi _ h)& =-j\kappa \mathrm e^{-j\phi }p_ jp_ j^*, \qquad \kappa >0. \end{aligned} \]

Let $a_ v$ and $a_ h$ be the squared norms of $\xi _ v$ and $\xi _ h$ for the form $b$, and divide each tangent vector by its norm to obtain points of $K$. At $j=0$ the second matrix is zero, which gives the witness with one atom. For $j>0$, the matrix at the second normalized point is $-c$ times the matrix at the first, with $c=j\kappa a_ v/a_ h>0$. The probability measure giving these two points the weights $c/(1+c)$ and $1/(1+c)$ has zero matrix integral, which excludes strict positivity for every Hermitian $B$. This proves failure at all lower levels, so the first successful level is $j=q-1$. $\square$

11.30.4 Relation to the fixed very ample criterion32E68898DD56

Corollary 11.30.5.9F9E99FDE392 For the data $E=L$ and $A=L^ q$, with the Fubini–Study metric of a basis of $H^0(X,A)$, the criterion of theorem 11.29.9 applies with $m_0=0$. Its untwisted level fails, although $E$ carries a strictly Griffiths-positive metric, and a higher level succeeds. The atom witnessing the failure at level zero annihilates only the Levi forms of the level-zero test space, and does not give a measure with $D^*T=0$.

Proof. By lemma 11.30.1, $L$ is globally generated and $A$ is very ample, as the criterion requires. At a ramification point, the complete untwisted evaluation map has a nonzero tangent vector in its kernel, in the direction of the cover, as in the preceding proof; hence corollary 11.29.10 gives failure at level zero. The positive root metric and theorem 11.29.9 give success at some higher level. Moreover, the Levi form of the dual of this positive metric pairs strictly positively with every nonzero positive matrix-valued measure on $W\to S$, so the atom at level zero cannot annihilate the Levi forms of all smooth Hermitian forms. $\square$

The level $m$ of the criterion in theorem 11.29.9 corresponds to $j=qm$ in the root-weight family, since $E\otimes A^ m=L^{qm+1}$ and $m\phi _ A=qm\phi $. The first success at $j=q-1$ therefore gives no bound on the first successful level $m$ of that criterion. The root-weight family is nested in steps of $q$, because multiplication by the sections of $A$ uses $\sum _ i|\sigma _ i|^2=\mathrm e^{q\phi }$; we do not claim that it is nested in steps of one. As $q$ grows, the root-weight family has arbitrarily many failed initial levels, although the bundle carries a strictly positive metric.
