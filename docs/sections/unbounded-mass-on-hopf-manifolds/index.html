20.1 Unbounded mass on Hopf manifolds

8 Unbounded mass on Hopf manifolds

By proposition 7.1.1, every compact complex curve or surface has the bounded mass property. The property can fail in dimension three even when the metric is Gauduchon. We prove this on a Hopf threefold, then extend the construction to every scalar Hopf manifold of dimension at least three.

The threefold mass formula provides the starting point. Its linear term vanishes for a Gauduchon metric, and for the standard Hopf metric the quadratic term is a positive multiple of the Dirichlet energy along the elliptic fibers over $\mathbb P^2$. We therefore seek smooth potentials with large vertical energy whose perturbed forms stay positive. A fiber heat kernel supplies the concentration. Varying its time across the base controls the mixed derivatives; a function of the base then restores positivity without changing the vertical energy. The higher-dimensional extension follows by comparing the angular densities.

We next use the fiber structure to study minimum mass, damping and weak limits. Fiber averaging identifies the bounded potentials of minimum mass. For normalized potentials invariant under unitary rotations of the last two coordinates, a positive fiber flux yields the sharp square-root damping threshold; concentration at the endpoint separates individual finiteness from uniform tails and capacity continuity. Finally, we classify the excess measures produced by shrinking smooth potentials. They are exactly the lifts of base measures by the normalized area measures on the elliptic fibers, for the standard metric and for every other Hermitian metric on this threefold.

Theorem 8.0.1 (Xia–Zhang). The compact threefold

\[ X=(\mathbb C^3\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto \mathrm{e}^{-1}z\rangle \]

admits a Gauduchon form $\omega $ and smooth real functions $V_ j$ such that

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_ j>0,\qquad \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_ j)^3\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

In particular, $X$ does not have the bounded mass property.

8.1 The Hopf metric and its mass

The projection to $\mathbb P^2$ separates the horizontal and vertical parts of the metric. We normalize the fiber measure to have mass one; this fixes the constants in the mass formula.

On the covering space $\mathbb C^3\setminus \{ 0\} $, with coordinates $z=(z_0,z_1,z_2)$, put

\[ s=\log |z|^2,\qquad \alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s,\qquad \beta =\mathrm{d}s\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s =\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial s\wedge \bar\partial s, \qquad \omega =\alpha +\beta . \]

The contraction $z\mapsto \mathrm{e}^{-1}z$ changes $s$ by the constant $-2$, so these forms descend to $X$. A closed spherical shell between two successive radii is a compact fundamental domain; this also proves that the quotient is compact. Let $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^2$, given by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+|\xi |^2)$ in affine coordinates $\xi =(\xi _1,\xi _2)$. Projectivization gives a holomorphic map

\[ \pi :X\longrightarrow \mathbb P^2, \qquad \alpha =\pi ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}},\qquad \int _{\mathbb P^2}\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2=1. \]

The form $\alpha $ has rank two and is positive transversally to the fibers, while the rank-one form $\beta $ is positive on the vertical line. Their kernels intersect only in zero, and therefore $\omega >0$.

We shall use the identities

8.1
\begin{equation} \alpha ^3=0,\quad \beta ^2=0,\quad \mathrm{d}\alpha =0, \quad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\beta =-\alpha ^2, \quad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)=0. \end{equation}

The first three follow from the definitions and the dimension of the base. For the fourth, differentiating $\beta =\mathrm i\partial s\wedge \bar\partial s/(2\pi )$ gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\beta =(\partial \bar\partial s)^2/(4\pi ^2)=-\alpha ^2$. Finally, $\omega ^2=\alpha ^2+2\alpha \wedge \beta $, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)=-2\alpha ^3=0$. Thus $\omega $ is Gauduchon, although $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =-\alpha ^2\ne 0$.

On the affine base chart $U_0=\{ [z_0:z_1:z_2]\in \mathbb P^2:z_0\ne 0\} \simeq \mathbb C^2$ write

\[ \xi _ j=z_ j/z_0\quad (j=1,2),\qquad w=\log z_0. \]

The choice of logarithm and the contraction change $w$ by elements of $\Lambda =\mathbb Z+2\pi \mathrm i\mathbb Z$. Hence

\[ \pi ^{-1}(U_0)\simeq U_0\times E,\qquad E=\mathbb C/\Lambda ,\qquad \mathrm{d}A_ E=\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi }\mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar w, \quad \int _ E\mathrm{d}A_ E=1. \]

On another base chart the fiber coordinate changes by a holomorphic function of the base. Consequently, the vertical derivative $\partial _ w$ and the fiber integral below are globally defined. For a smooth function $V$ on $X$, write $V_ w=\partial V/\partial w$.

Proposition 8.1.1. For every $V\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$,

8.2
\begin{equation} \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V)^3 =6+6\int _{\mathbb P^2} \left(\int _{\pi ^{-1}(y)}|V_ w|^2\, \mathrm{d}A_ E\right) \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2(y). \end{equation}

In particular, positivity of $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V$ is not required.

Proof. Since $\beta $ restricts to $2\mathrm{d}A_ E$ on each fiber,

\[ \int _ X\omega ^3=3\int _ X\alpha ^2\wedge \beta =6. \]

By (2.1) and (8.1), followed by an integration by parts against the closed form $\alpha ^2$,

\[ \begin{split} \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V)^3-\int _ X\omega ^3 & =3\int _ X V\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V\\ & =-3\int _ X V\alpha ^2\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V =3\int _ X\mathrm{d}V\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}V\wedge \alpha ^2. \end{split} \]

Wedging with $\alpha ^2$ removes every component of $\mathrm{d}V\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}V$ except the vertical one, which is $\mathrm i|V_ w|^2\mathrm{d}w\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar w/(2\pi )=2|V_ w|^2\mathrm{d}A_ E$. Integration over the fibers proves the formula. $\square$

8.2 The radial–vertical block

We look for potentials that depend on the base point only through its distance from the origin of the affine chart. In a suitable frame, the matrix of $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V$ then consists of one tangential horizontal coefficient and a two-by-two radial–vertical block, whose Schur complement isolates the mixed derivative that must be controlled. Subscripts denote partial derivatives in the indicated variables.

For $\xi \ne 0$ introduce

\[ x=\log |\xi |^2,\qquad \mu =\frac{\mathrm{e}^ x}{1+\mathrm{e}^ x},\qquad \mu _ x=\mu (1-\mu ). \]

We use smooth real functions $V:\mathbb R\times E\to \mathbb R$, written $V=V(x,w)$, that are independent of $x$ for $x$ near $-\infty $ and constant in both variables for $x$ near $+\infty $. These conditions allow $V$ to be extended smoothly across $\xi =0$, and over the complement of the affine chart by the same constant. The region of the base where $V$ is nonconstant may depend on $V$.

Lemma 8.2.1. For such a function, at $\xi \ne 0$ the inequality $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V>0$ is equivalent to

8.5
\begin{equation} 1+V_{w\bar w}>0,\qquad \mu +V_ x>0,\qquad Q[V]:=V_{xx}+\mu - \frac{|\mu +V_{x\bar w}|^2}{1+V_{w\bar w}}>0. \end{equation}

At $\xi =0$, the inequality $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V>0$ is equivalent to $1+V_{w\bar w}>0$.

Proof. In these coordinates,

\[ s=w+\bar w+\log (1+\mathrm{e}^ x),\qquad \partial s=\mathrm{d}w+\mu \partial x, \]

and

\[ 2\pi \alpha =\mathrm i\mu \partial \bar\partial x +\mathrm i\mu (1-\mu )\partial x\wedge \bar\partial x. \]

Choose horizontal vectors of unit length for $2\pi \alpha $, one orthogonal to the radial direction and the other

\[ e_ r=\frac{\xi _1\partial _{\xi _1}+\xi _2\partial _{\xi _2}}{\sqrt{\mu (1-\mu )}}. \]

Adjoin the vertical vector $\partial _ w$. In this frame the matrix of $2\pi (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V)$ is

\[ \begin{pmatrix} (\mu +V_ x)/\mu & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & (\mu +V_{xx})/[\mu (1-\mu )] & (\mu +V_{x\bar w})/\sqrt{\mu (1-\mu )} \\ 0 & (\mu +V_{wx})/\sqrt{\mu (1-\mu )} & 1+V_{w\bar w} \end{pmatrix}. \]

Taking the Schur complement of the last diagonal entry gives (8.5). At $\xi =0$, the derivatives of $V$ in the base directions vanish and $\partial s=\mathrm{d}w$, which gives the last assertion. $\square$

A rapid cutoff of a concentrated fiber potential can make the mixed derivative $V_{x\bar w}$ too large for this criterion. We therefore spread the concentration by the heat equation before cutting off, so that the fiber derivatives are already exponentially small in the cutoff region.

8.3 Heat potentials and the mixed derivative

The fiber potential will be built from the heat kernel of $E$. It has large vertical energy at small times and decays at large times, where it can be cut off, and a lower bound for its logarithmic Hessian controls vertical positivity. To control the radial–vertical block when the time varies, we also need an estimate relating the kernel to its logarithmic gradient.

For $t>0$ define

8.6
\begin{equation} k_ t(w)=\frac2t\sum _{\lambda \in \Lambda } \exp \left(-\frac{|w-\lambda |^2}{t}\right). \end{equation}

This is a smooth positive $\Lambda $-periodic function of integral one: unfolding the integral reduces it to the Gaussian integral on $\mathbb C$, since $\mathrm{d}A_ E=\mathrm{d}a\, \mathrm{d}b/(2\pi )$ for $w=a+\mathrm ib$. For $(p,q)\in \mathbb Z^2$ set

\[ \nu _{p,q}=p+\frac{\mathrm iq}{2\pi },\qquad \chi _{p,q}(w)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi \mathrm ip\operatorname {Re}w+ \mathrm iq\operatorname {Im}w}. \]

These characters form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(E,\mathrm{d}A_ E)$. Computing the Fourier coefficients by the same unfolding gives

8.7
\begin{equation} k_ t=\sum _{p,q}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi ^2|\nu _{p,q}|^2t}\chi _{p,q}, \qquad \partial _ tk_ t=\partial _ w\partial _{\bar w}k_ t. \end{equation}

All differentiated series converge locally uniformly for $t>0$.

Lemma 8.3.1. Let $0<\eta <1/10$ and define

\[ F_\eta (t,w)=-\eta \int _ t^\infty (k_ r(w)-1)\, \mathrm{d}r. \]

Then $F_\eta $ is smooth and real, and

8.8
\begin{align} (F_\eta )_ t=(F_\eta )_{w\bar w} & =\eta (k_ t-1),\\ (\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}& \geq -1/t,\\ \int _ E|(F_\eta )_ w(t,w)|^2\mathrm{d}A_ E & =2\eta ^2\log (1/t)+O(\eta ^2)\quad (0<t\leq 1). \end{align}

The error term in (8.10) is uniform in $t$ and $\eta $. For fixed nonnegative integers $a,m$ there is also a uniform estimate

8.11
\begin{equation} \| \partial _ t^ aF_\eta (t,\cdot )\| _{C^ m(E)} \leq C_{a,m}\eta \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\quad (t\geq 1). \end{equation}

Proof. Integrating the nonconstant Fourier modes gives

\[ F_\eta (t,w)=-\frac{\eta }{\pi ^2} \sum _{(p,q)\ne (0,0)} \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\pi ^2|\nu _{p,q}|^2t}}{|\nu _{p,q}|^2}\chi _{p,q}(w). \]

This proves smoothness, reality and (8.8). The least nonzero value of $\pi ^2|\nu _{p,q}|^2$ is $1/4$, and any fixed number of differentiations introduces coefficients of polynomial growth in $|\nu _{p,q}|$. Factoring out $\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ for $t\geq 1$ therefore leaves a convergent Gaussian series, which proves (8.11).

To prove (8.9), fix a lift of $w$ and give $\Lambda $ the probability weights

\[ P_\lambda = \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-|w-\lambda |^2/t}}{\sum _{\lambda '}\mathrm{e}^{-|w-\lambda '|^2/t}}. \]

Logarithmic differentiation of the Gaussian sum yields

\[ t\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t=-\sum _\lambda (w-\lambda )P_\lambda , \]

and one more derivative gives

\[ (\log k_ t)_{w\bar w} =-\frac1t+\frac1{t^2} \left(\sum _\lambda |w-\lambda |^2P_\lambda -\left|\sum _\lambda (w-\lambda )P_\lambda \right|^2\right). \]

The expression in parentheses is a variance and is nonnegative.

Finally, Parseval's identity gives, with $\mathcal E_\eta (t)$ denoting the energy in (8.10),

\[ \mathcal E_\eta (t)=\frac{\eta ^2}{\pi ^2} \sum _{(p,q)\ne (0,0)} \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi ^2|\nu _{p,q}|^2t}}{|\nu _{p,q}|^2}, \qquad \mathcal E_\eta '(t)=-2\eta ^2(k_{2t}(0)-1). \]

The Gaussian formula at the origin implies

\[ k_{2t}(0)=\frac1t+ O\left(t^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-1/(4t)}\right)\quad (0<t\leq 1). \]

To see this, isolate the zero lattice vector and use $\mathrm{e}^{-|\lambda |^2/(2t)}\leq \mathrm{e}^{-1/(4t)}\mathrm{e}^{-|\lambda |^2/4}$ for $\lambda \ne 0$. The error term is integrable near $t=0$, and $\mathcal E_\eta (1)=O(\eta ^2)$. Integrating the derivative between $t$ and $1$ proves (8.10). $\square$

The Schur complement subtracts a square involving the mixed derivative. When the time parameter is allowed to vary, the heat equation produces a compensating positive square, and the following bound relating the heat kernel to its logarithmic gradient controls the region where that square is small.

Lemma 8.3.2. For a constant $C_0\geq 1$ depending only on $E$,

8.12
\begin{equation} tk_ t(w)\leq C_0\exp \left( -\frac{|t\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t(w)|^2}{C_0t}\right) \quad (0<t\leq 1). \end{equation}

Proof. Choose a nearest lattice point $\lambda _0$, and put $d=|w-\lambda _0|$ and $s_\lambda =|w-\lambda |^2-d^2\geq 0$. The distance $d$ is bounded by the diameter of a fundamental rectangle. By the triangle inequality, $s_\lambda \geq |\lambda -\lambda _0|^2/4-C$, and outside a fixed finite set of $\lambda $ we have $s_\lambda \geq |\lambda -\lambda _0|^2/8$. Consequently,

\[ \sum _\lambda (1+|w-\lambda |)\mathrm{e}^{-s_\lambda /t}\leq C, \qquad tk_ t(w)\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-d^2/t}. \]

The probability formula in the previous proof gives, with $G=|t\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t|$,

\[ G\leq d+\sum _{\lambda \ne \lambda _0}|w-\lambda |\mathrm{e}^{-s_\lambda /t}. \]

If $d\geq 1/4$, this is at most $Cd$. If $d<1/4$, the nonzero lattice distances are at least one and

\[ s_\lambda \geq |\lambda -\lambda _0| (|\lambda -\lambda _0|-2d) \geq \tfrac 12|\lambda -\lambda _0|^2. \]

Summing the resulting Gaussian bound gives $G\leq C(d+\mathrm{e}^{-c/t})$ in all cases. When $G>2C\mathrm{e}^{-c/t}$, we have $d\geq G/(2C)$, and the bound $tk_ t\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-d^2/t}$ gives (8.12). In the remaining case $G^2/t$ is bounded, and (8.12) follows from $tk_ t\leq C$ after increasing $C_0$. $\square$

Lemma 8.3.3. There is a constant $C$ depending only on $E$ with the following property. For $0<\kappa <1/(200C_0)$, $0<\eta <1/10$, $0<t\leq 1$ and $1/2\leq \mu \leq 1$, define $\sigma >0$ and the functions $h,\zeta $ of $w\in E$ by

\[ \sigma ^2=\frac{2\kappa }{\eta }\log \frac1{\eta t},\qquad h=\eta k_ t,\qquad \zeta =\frac{\sigma }{\mu }\, t\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t. \]

Then

8.13
\begin{equation} \kappa tk_ t- \frac{\mu ^2(1-\eta )h}{1-\eta +h} \left|\zeta -\frac1{1-\eta }\right|^2 \leq C\kappa \exp \left(-\frac1{C\sigma ^2t}\right). \end{equation}

Proof. If $\sigma ^2t\geq 1/100$, the bound $tk_ t\leq C_0$ gives (8.13) after enlarging $C$. Assume therefore that $\sigma ^2t<1/100$. If $|\zeta -(1-\eta )^{-1}|\geq 1/4$, the subtracted expression is at least $h/[100(1+h)]$. Since $\log (1/(\eta t))>2$, the definition of $\sigma $ gives

\[ \kappa t(1+h)\leq \frac{\eta }{400}+C_0\kappa \eta <\frac{\eta }{100}. \]

Multiplication by $k_ t/(1+h)$ shows that the left side of (8.13) is nonpositive.

Otherwise $|\zeta |\geq 3/4$, so

\[ |t\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t|=\frac{\mu |\zeta |}{\sigma } \geq \frac3{8\sigma }. \]

Discarding the nonnegative square and applying lemma 8.3.2 gives (8.13) in this case as well. $\square$

8.9
\[\begin{aligned}\begin{align} (\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}& \geq -1/t,\\ \end{align}\end{aligned}\]
8.10
\[\begin{aligned}\begin{align} \int _ E|(F_\eta )_ w(t,w)|^2\mathrm{d}A_ E & =2\eta ^2\log (1/t)+O(\eta ^2)\quad (0<t\leq 1). \end{align}\end{aligned}\]

8.4 Variation of the heat time

The heat time will remain small on a fixed base ball and then increase with the radial variable until the fiber potential can be cut off. We choose its rate of increase so that the negative part of the Schur complement has small integral in the radial variable. Fix $\kappa $ as in lemma 8.3.3.

All estimates below concern the limit $\eta \downarrow 0$, with $\kappa $ fixed. Write

\[ L=\log (1/\eta ),\quad \varepsilon =\eta ^{1/\eta ^2},\quad q_0=\sqrt{\log \frac1{\eta \varepsilon }},\quad a=\sqrt{\frac\kappa {2\eta }}. \]

Fix a smooth nondecreasing $\chi :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$, independent of $\eta $, equal to zero on $(-\infty ,0]$ and one on $[1,+\infty )$, with $\int _0^1\chi =1/2$. Define

8.14
\begin{equation} q(x)=q_0-\int _0^{ax}\chi (s)\, \mathrm{d}s, \qquad t(x)=\eta ^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-q(x)^2}. \end{equation}

Thus $t=\varepsilon $ on $x\leq 0$, and $q=q_0+1/2-ax$ on $x\geq a^{-1}$. Put

\[ b=\frac{q_0-1/2}{a},\qquad x_* =\frac{q_0+1/2}{a}. \]

For small $\eta $, we have $0<a^{-1}<b<x_*$, with

8.15
\begin{equation} q(b)=1,\quad q(x_*)=0,\quad b,x_*=O\bigl (\sqrt{L/\eta }\bigr ),\quad \eta b=o(1). \end{equation}

Fix also a smooth cutoff $\vartheta :\mathbb R\to [0,1]$, independent of $\eta $, zero on $(-\infty ,0]$ and one on $[1,+\infty )$, and define

\[ W(x,w)=\vartheta (q(x))F_\eta (t(x),w). \]

This function equals $F_\eta (\varepsilon ,w)$ for $x\leq 0$ and vanishes for $x\geq x_*$. Moreover, by (8.8),

8.16
\begin{equation} 1+W_{w\bar w}=1+\vartheta (q)\eta (k_ t-1)>1-\eta . \end{equation}

Lemma 8.4.1. For the functions just constructed, let

\[ d_\eta (x)=\max _{w\in E}\max \{ -Q[W](x,w),0\} . \]

For all sufficiently small $\eta $, the function $d_\eta $ is continuous, supported in $[0,b]$, and

8.17
\begin{equation} \int _\mathbb Rd_\eta (x)\, \mathrm{d}x=o(1),\qquad \sup _{\mathbb R\times E}\max \{ -W_ x,0\} =o(1). \end{equation}

On $[-2,-1]\times E$, one also has $Q[W]\geq 2/27$.

Proof. We first derive an exact formula for $Q[W]$ on $[0,b]$, then bound its negative part separately on $[0,a^{-1}]$ and on $[a^{-1},b]$, integrate these bounds, and finally examine the ends of the radial line.

Step 1: the Schur complement on $[0,b]$. Write $F=F_\eta $ for the heat potential. On $[0,b]$ we have $q\geq 1$, hence $\vartheta (q)=1$ and $W=F(t(x),w)$. Set

\[ h=\eta k_ t,\qquad \sigma =(\log t)_ x,\qquad \zeta =\frac{\sigma t}{\mu }\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t. \]

The heat equation gives $F_ t=h-\eta $, and the chain rule gives

\[ W_ x=\sigma tF_ t,\quad W_{xx}=(\sigma _ x+\sigma ^2)tF_ t+\sigma ^2t^2F_{tt}, \quad W_{x\bar w}=\sigma tF_{t\bar w}. \]

Substituting these in $Q[W]$, using $h_ t=h((\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}+|\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t|^2)$, and completing the square, we obtain the exact identity

8.18
\begin{equation} \begin{split} Q[W]={}& \sigma ^2t\{ h[1+t(\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}]-\eta \} +\sigma _ xtF_ t+\mu (1-\mu )-\frac{\mu ^2\eta }{1-\eta }\\ & +\frac{\mu ^2(1-\eta )h}{1-\eta +h} \left|\zeta -\frac1{1-\eta }\right|^2. \end{split} \end{equation}

By (8.9), the part $\sigma ^2th[1+t(\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}]$ is nonnegative.

Step 2: the initial interval $[0,a^{-1}]$. Here

\[ \sigma =2aq\chi (ax),\qquad \sigma _ x=-\frac\kappa \eta \chi (ax)^2 +\frac\kappa \eta q\chi '(ax). \]

Moreover $q_0-1/2\leq q\leq q_0$ and $t\leq \varepsilon \mathrm{e}^{q_0}$, and the latter tends to zero faster than any power of $\eta $. In particular,

\[ \kappa q\chi '(ax)t\leq \eta ,\qquad \sigma ^2t\leq \eta ^2. \]

At each point where $\chi (ax)>0$, apply lemma 8.3.3 with $\kappa \chi (ax)^2$ in place of $\kappa $; its constant is uniform in $x$. The positive square in (8.18) then absorbs the negative term $-\kappa \chi ^2tk_ t$ coming from $\sigma _ xtF_ t$. The remaining part of $\sigma _ xtF_ t$, which involves $\chi '$, is at least $-\eta $, because $F_ t\geq -\eta $. The other negative terms in (8.18) are $O(\eta )$, and so is the exponential error of lemma 8.3.3, since $\sigma ^2t\leq \eta ^2$. Thus $d_\eta \leq C\eta $ on this interval. At points where $\chi (ax)=0$, the smooth nonnegative function $\chi $ has a minimum, so $\chi '(ax)=0$ and directly

\[ Q[W]=\mu -\frac{\mu ^2}{1-\eta +h}>0, \]

since $\mu \leq \mu (a^{-1})<1-\eta $ for small $\eta $.

Step 3: the interval $[a^{-1},b]$. Here the formulas simplify to

8.21
\begin{equation} \sigma =2aq,\qquad \sigma _ x=-\kappa /\eta ,\qquad \mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{a}=\sqrt{\frac\eta {2\kappa }}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{tq}. \end{equation}

For $t\leq 1$, lemma 8.3.3 and (8.18) give

8.22
\begin{equation} d_\eta (x)\leq 2\eta +2\kappa q^2t +C\kappa \exp \left(-\frac\eta {C\kappa q^2t}\right). \end{equation}

Indeed, $-\eta \sigma ^2t=-2\kappa q^2t$ and $\sigma _ xtF_ t=-\kappa tk_ t+\kappa t$; these are the two terms for which the scale was chosen. When $t\geq 1$, we insert the exponential derivative estimates (8.11) directly in the definition of $Q$. The denominator stays above $1-\eta $, and the chain rule gives

8.23
\begin{equation} d_\eta (x)\leq C\kappa q^2t^2\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}. \end{equation}

The perturbing derivatives are bounded by constants times $\eta \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$, $\eta \sigma t \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ and $\eta [(|\sigma _ x|+\sigma ^2)t+\sigma ^2t^2]\mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$. Since $q,t\geq 1$ here, each is bounded by a constant times the right side of (8.23). Since $1+W_{w\bar w}\geq 1-\eta $, subtracting the two quotients gives

\[ |Q[W]-Q[0]|\leq |W_{xx}|+ C\bigl (|W_{x\bar w}|+|W_{x\bar w}|^2+|W_{w\bar w}|\bigr ). \]

The square of the mixed derivative is at most $C\kappa \eta q^2t^2\mathrm{e}^{-t/2}$, so it satisfies the same bound. Since $Q[0]>0$, this proves (8.23).

Step 4: integration of the negative part. The constant $2\eta $ and the initial interval contribute $O(\eta b)=o(1)$, by (8.15). In the small-time region, $q\geq \sqrt L$ and $t=\eta ^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-q^2}$. Therefore

\[ \int 2\kappa q^2t\, \mathrm{d}x \leq \frac{2\kappa }{a\eta } \int _{\sqrt L}^{\infty }q^2\mathrm{e}^{-q^2}\, \mathrm{d}q \leq C\sqrt{\eta L}=o(1). \]

For the exponential term the integral is at most

\[ \frac{C\kappa }{a} \int _{\sqrt L}^{q_0} \exp \left(-\frac{\eta ^2\mathrm{e}^{q^2}}{C\kappa q^2}\right)\, \mathrm{d}q. \]

The interval $[\sqrt L,2\sqrt L]$ has length $O(\sqrt L)$. For $q\geq 2\sqrt L$ and sufficiently small $\eta $, $\eta ^2\mathrm{e}^{q^2}/q^2\geq \mathrm{e}^{q^2/4}$. The integrand on this remaining interval is bounded by $\exp (-\mathrm{e}^{q^2/4}/(C\kappa ))$, which is integrable on $[1,+\infty )$. The prefactor is $O(\sqrt\eta )$, and the initial interval contributes $O(\sqrt{\eta L})$, so the full expression is $o(1)$. In the large-time region, (8.21) gives

\[ \int d_\eta (x)\, \mathrm{d}x \leq C\sqrt\eta \int _1^{1/(\mathrm{e}\eta )}qt \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\, \mathrm{d}t \leq C\sqrt{\eta L}\int _1^\infty t \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}\, \mathrm{d}t=o(1). \]

This proves the integral assertion on $[0,b]$.

Step 5: the radial slope. The negative part of $W_ x$ is uniformly small as well. For $t\leq 1$, we have $F_ t\geq -\eta $ and $0\leq \sigma \leq 2aq$, so

\[ \max \{ -W_ x,0\} \leq \sqrt{2\kappa \eta }\, t\sqrt{\log \frac1{\eta t}}\leq C\sqrt{\eta L}. \]

The last inequality follows because $t\sqrt{L+\log (1/t)}$ is increasing on $(0,1]$ when $L>1$. For $t\geq 1$ and $q\geq 1$, use $|W_ x|\leq C\sqrt\eta \, qt \mathrm{e}^{-t/4}$ and $q\leq \sqrt L$.

Step 6: the ends. It remains to consider the two ends, including the cutoff region. If $b\leq x\leq x_*$, then $0\leq q\leq 1$ and $1/(\mathrm{e}\eta )\leq t\leq 1/\eta $. The derivatives of the cutoffs and scale have at most polynomial growth in $\eta ^{-1}$. Thus (8.11) implies

\[ \sup _ w\bigl (|W_ x|+|Q[W]-Q[0]|\bigr ) \leq C\mathrm{e}^{-1/(5\mathrm{e}\eta )}. \]

But $Q[0]=\mu (1-\mu )\geq \mathrm{e}^{-x_*}/4$ on this interval, and (8.15) shows that the error is $o(\mathrm{e}^{-x_*})$. Hence $Q[W]>0$ there. For $x\geq x_*$, $W=0$ and positivity is immediate. For $x\leq 0$, $W_ x=0$ and

\[ Q[W]\geq \mu \left(1-\frac\mu {1-\eta }\right)>0, \]

since $\mu \leq 1/2$. On $[-2,-1]$, the bounds $1/9\leq \mu \leq 3/10$ and $1-\eta \geq 9/10$ give $Q[W]\geq 2/27$. Finally, $Q[W]$ is continuous, with positive denominator, so the maximum of its negative part over the compact fiber $E$ is continuous in $x$. This proves the assertions about support and continuity, and completes the proof. $\square$

Adding a function of $x$ alone changes $Q[W]$ by its second derivative and leaves the vertical energy unchanged. The integral bound for $d_\eta $ lets us choose this second derivative larger than $d_\eta $ and compensate its integral on an interval where $Q[W]$ is uniformly positive. The correction has small first derivative, so the remaining horizontal coefficient stays positive as well.

8.6 Higher-dimensional Hopf manifolds

The threefold mass formula locates the source of unbounded mass, but it does not by itself give a construction in higher dimension. The same heat potentials do work there: the radial–vertical block is unchanged, and each extra angular direction contributes a factor whose lower bound is uniform where the threefold mass diverges.

For $n\geq 3$, let

\[ H_ n=(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )/ \langle z\mapsto \mathrm e^{-1}z\rangle , \qquad p_ n:H_ n\longrightarrow \mathbb P^{n-1}. \]

Put $s=\log |z|^2$, $\alpha _ n=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s=p_ n^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$, $\beta _ n=\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s$ and $\omega _ n=\alpha _ n+\beta _ n$, where $\int _{\mathbb P^{n-1}}\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^{n-1}=1$. The forms descend and $\omega _ n$ is positive for the same reasons as in dimension three. The fiber is the same elliptic curve $E$, with $\int _ E\beta _ n=2$. Since $\alpha _ n$ has rank $n-1$ and $\beta _ n^2=0$,

\[ \int _{H_ n}\omega _ n^ n=2n,\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _ n^{n-1})=-(n-1)\alpha _ n^ n=0. \]

Thus $\omega _ n$ is Gauduchon.

On the affine chart write $\xi \in \mathbb C^{n-1}$, $x=\log |\xi |^2$, $w=\log z_0\pmod\Lambda $ and $\mu =\mathrm e^ x/(1+\mathrm e^ x)$ as before. For a smooth real function $V(x,w)$ with the endpoint properties of lemma 8.2.1, let $J_ n(V;x,w)$ be the density obtained by integrating $(\omega _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V)^ n$ over the angular variables, with angular probability measure. Thus

\[ \int _{H_ n}(\omega _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V)^ n =\int _{\mathbb R\times E}J_ n(V;x,w)\, \mathrm dx\, \mathrm dA_ E. \]

The endpoints and the complement of the affine chart have zero smooth volume.

Lemma 8.6.1. The positivity criterion (8.5) holds in every dimension $n\geq 3$, and

\begin{equation} J_ n(V;x,w)=\frac{n(n-1)}6 (\mu +V_ x)^{n-3}J_3(V;x,w). \end{equation}

Here $J_3$ is evaluated for the same function $V$ on the threefold.

Proof. Choose $n-2$ horizontal angular vectors, one horizontal radial vector and the vertical vector $\partial _ w$. In the normalization of the proof of lemma 8.2.1, each angular entry is $A=(\mu +V_ x)/\mu $, and the radial–vertical block is

\[ K_ V=\begin{pmatrix} (\mu +V_{xx})/[\mu (1-\mu )] & (\mu +V_{x\bar w})/\sqrt{\mu (1-\mu )} \\ (\mu +V_{wx})/\sqrt{\mu (1-\mu )} & 1+V_{w\bar w} \end{pmatrix}. \]

This follows by differentiating $s=w+\bar w+\log (1+\mathrm e^ x)$: the radial part of $\partial \bar\partial x$ is zero, whereas its angular part contributes $V_ x/\mu $. The Schur complement of the last entry gives the stated positivity criterion. At $\xi =0$ the same endpoint argument as in dimension three applies.

Set $d=n-1$. The radial Fubini–Study probability density is $d\mu ^{d-1}\mathrm d\mu =d\mu ^ d(1-\mu )\mathrm dx$; it follows from the polar-coordinate determinant of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+|\xi |^2)$ and its integral is one. The background block $K_0$ has determinant one. Using the fiber mass two, therefore,

\[ J_ n(V)=2nd\mu ^ d(1-\mu )A^{n-2}\det K_ V. \]

For $n=3$ this is $12\mu ^2(1-\mu )A\det K_ V$. Comparing the two expressions proves (8.24). $\square$

Proof. Use exactly the functions $V_\eta =W+g_\eta $ constructed in the proof of theorem 8.0.1, including the heat scale $\varepsilon =\eta ^{1/\eta ^2}$. \[ \mu +(V_\eta )_ x\geq \tfrac 14 \qquad (x\geq 0) \]

\[ \int _ E J_3(V_\eta ;x,w)\, \mathrm dA_ E =12(\mu +g_\eta ') \bigl (g_\eta ''+\mu (1-\mu )\bigr ). \]

\[ \int _{x<0}\! J_3(V_\eta )\, \mathrm dx\, \mathrm dA_ E \leq 12\int _{-\infty }^0\mu ^2(1-\mu )\, \mathrm dx +12\int _{\mathbb R}|g_\eta ''|\, \mathrm dx \leq \tfrac 32+24m_\eta . \]

\[ \int _{H_ n}(\omega _ n+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\eta )^ n \geq \frac{n(n-1)}6\, 4^{-(n-3)} \int _{x\geq 0}J_3(V_\eta )\, \mathrm dx\, \mathrm dA_ E \longrightarrow +\infty . \]

Proof. For a Hermitian form $h$, compactness gives $h\geq c\omega _ n$ for some $c>0$. 8.7 Minimum mass and fiber averaging

We return to the threefold $X=H_3$ and its form $\omega $.

Averaging along the elliptic fibers removes the variation measured by (8.2). The Poincaré inequality on the fibers bounds the distance from a potential to its average by the excess mass. Bounded regularization preserves this inequality, which therefore identifies all bounded potentials of minimal mass and shows that potentials of almost minimal mass are close to them.

Let $E$ act on $X$ by scalar multiplication: $\tau _ a[z]=[\mathrm{e}^ a z]$ for $a\in E$. The action preserves $\alpha $, $\beta $ and $\omega $, and $\mathrm{d}A_ E$ is the Haar probability measure of $E$. For a bounded Borel function $u:X\to \mathbb R$, set

\[ \mathrm{d}\nu =\frac{\omega ^3}{6}=\frac12\alpha ^2\wedge \beta , \qquad Au(p)=\int _ Eu(\tau _ a(p))\, \mathrm{d}A_ E(a) \quad (p\in X). \]

Thus $Au$ is constant on each fiber, and $\nu $ is the probability measure whose disintegration along $\pi $ has fiber measures $\mathrm{d}A_ E$ and base measure $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2$.

Proposition 8.7.1. For every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$, the average $Au$ is the pullback of a bounded $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^2$, and

8.25
\begin{equation} \| u-Au\| _{L^2(X,\nu )}^2 \leq \frac23\left(\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3-6\right). \end{equation}

The coefficient $2/3$ is optimal, even for smooth $u$ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$. Moreover,

\[ \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^3=6 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad u=\pi ^*v\quad \text{for some } v\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^2,\omega _{\mathrm{FS}})\cap L^\infty (\mathbb P^2). \]

Proof. First suppose that $u$ is smooth and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$. Averaging the positive forms $\tau _ a^*(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)$ gives $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}Au>0$. Since $Au$ is smooth and constant on the fibers, $Au=\pi ^*v$ for a smooth function $v$ on $\mathbb P^2$. At each point, the kernel of $\partial s$ maps isomorphically onto the tangent space of the base, and $\beta $ vanishes on it. Restricting $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}Au=\pi ^*(\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)+\beta $ to this kernel therefore shows that $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v>0$.

For a smooth function $f:E\to \mathbb R$, the nonconstant Fourier characters in (8.7), indexed by $(p,q)\in \mathbb Z^2\setminus \{ (0,0)\} $, have eigenvalues $\pi ^2p^2+q^2/4\geq 1/4$ for $-\partial _ w\partial _{\bar w}$. Parseval's identity therefore gives

\[ \int _ E\left|f-\int _ Ef\, \mathrm{d}A_ E\right|^2\mathrm{d}A_ E \leq 4\int _ E|f_ w|^2\, \mathrm{d}A_ E. \]

Applying this inequality on every fiber, integrating against $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2$ and using the mass identity (8.2), we obtain (8.25).

For bounded $u$, choose uniformly bounded smooth functions $u_ j\downarrow u$ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j>0$ by theorem 1.11.1. Their averages decrease pointwise to $Au$ by dominated convergence. The corresponding smooth base potentials $v_ j$ decrease to a bounded $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic function $v$, so $Au=\pi ^*v$. Dominated convergence gives $u_ j\to u$ and $Au_ j\to Au$ in $L^2(X,\nu )$, while bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity gives convergence of the total masses. Thus (8.25) passes to $u$.

If the mass is $6$, the inequality gives $u=Au$ almost everywhere. Since both functions are bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic, lemma 1.3.4 gives equality everywhere. Conversely, if $u=\pi ^*v$ with bounded $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^2,\omega _{\mathrm{FS}})$, choose smooth $v_ j\downarrow v$ with $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j>0$ by theorem 1.11.1. The pullbacks decrease to $u$ and are uniformly bounded. They are strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic because the base forms are positive and $\beta $ is positive on the vertical line. Their vertical derivatives vanish, so (8.2) gives mass $6$ for each of them. Bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity gives the same mass for $u$.

It remains to prove that the constant $2/3$ is optimal. Choose a nonzero real $\rho \in C_ c^\infty (U_0)$. For $\delta \in \mathbb R$, the function

\[ u(\xi ,w)=\delta \rho (\xi )\cos (\operatorname {Im}w) \]

extends by zero to a smooth function on $X$. For sufficiently small nonzero real $\delta $, it satisfies $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$. Its fiber average is zero, and its only fiber frequencies are $(p,q)=(0,\pm 1)$, with eigenvalue $1/4$. Hence equality holds in the fiber inequality, and therefore in (8.25), with nonzero left-hand side. No smaller coefficient can hold. $\square$

Corollary 8.7.2. The Hopf metric satisfies

\[ v_-(\omega )=6,\qquad v_+(\omega )=+\infty . \]

Proof. The lower bound $v_-(\omega )\geq 6$ follows from (8.25), and it is attained by constants. Theorem 8.0.1 gives $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $. $\square$

In particular, for any sequence of bounded potentials whose masses tend to $6$, the differences from their fiber averages tend to zero in $L^2(X,\nu )$. This controls only the variation along the fibers; the averaged potentials can still be arbitrary bounded potentials on the base.

For nonpositive $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ on this threefold, proposition 7.3.1 gives finite damped measures for the weight $(1-u)^{-1}[\log (\mathrm{e}-u)]^{-a}$, $a>1$, at the threshold $r_3=1$, with uniform tails and capacity continuity. The optimal damping rate for all normalized potentials on this threefold remains open. Under unitary rotations of the last two coordinates, theorem 8.8.9 gives the exact square-root threshold.

8.8 Sharp damping under unitary rotations

The general damping estimate controls mass by the depth of a potential. On the standard Hopf threefold, unitary symmetry gives a stronger estimate: after integration along the elliptic fibers, a positive radial flux has total mass one. A semiconvexity estimate bounds the remaining radial coefficient by the square root of the depth. The resulting damping threshold is sharp, and at its endpoint finite measures can still concentrate on a pole divisor.

We retain the standard threefold $X$, form $\omega $, projection $\pi :X\to \mathbb P^2$, fiber $E$ and probability area $\mathrm dA_ E$ of this chapter. Let $\mathcal R$ be the class of $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\sup _ Xu=0$ invariant under

\[ (z_0,z')\longmapsto (z_0,Az'),\qquad A\in U(2),\quad z'=(z_1,z_2). \]

This holomorphic action preserves $\omega $. The class includes bounded and unbounded potentials. For a positive $C^1$ nonincreasing function $W:[0,+\infty )\to (0,+\infty )$ tending to zero, write

\begin{gather*} \mu _ u=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u),\qquad \nu _{W,u}=W(-u)\mu _ u,\\ Q_ T(W)=\sup _{t\geq T}(1+\sqrt{2t})W(t),\qquad Q(W)=Q_0(W). \end{gather*}

The weighted measure gives zero mass to the pole set, as does the extended non-pluripolar measure. Capacity always means $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ of section 1.8.

Lemma 8.8.1. For every smooth invariant $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function,

\[ \int _ E\Delta _ V\, \mathrm dA_ E =m''+\mu (1-\mu )-\tfrac 12e''. \]

Thus $f(x)=\log (1+\mathrm e^ x)+m(x)-e(x)/2$ is convex.

Both $m$ and $e$ are smooth global functions on the projective base: local fiber coordinates change by holomorphic translations, preserving fiber Haar area, the vertical derivative and the Dirichlet integral. At the positive end use a normal coordinate $z_0/z_1$ to the hyperplane $\{ z_0=0\} $ on the base. This does not require that the pointwise potential be constant on its elliptic fibers at that end. Consequently $f'(-\infty )=0$ and $f'(+\infty )=1$. \[ V(x+h,w)\geq V(x,w)+hV_ x(x,w)-h^2/2. \]

8.8.2 Heat concentration at large depth

The flux estimate suggests the depth scale at which a counterexample must place its mass. A faster initial heat time than in the unbounded-mass construction gives mass of order $\eta ^{-1}$ at depth of order $\eta ^{-2}$. Its integrated radial defect remains small after a fixed parameter is chosen, but no longer tends to zero. We therefore need a correction with a small fixed total slope.

We use the heat kernel $k_ t$ of (8.6) and its potential $F_\eta $ of lemma 8.3.1. The following value estimates complement the derivative bounds already proved.

Lemma 8.8.4. Uniformly in $\eta $ and $w$, $F_\eta (t,w)\leq C\eta $ for $t>0$, and $F_\eta (t,w)\geq -2\eta \log (1/t)-C\eta $ for $0<t\leq 1$. Uniformly on $\sqrt t\leq |w|\leq 2\sqrt t$ near the origin of $E$, as $t\to 0$,

Proof. For the upper bound use $k_ r\geq 0$ on $(0,1]$ and (8.11). The nonnegative Fourier coefficients imply $k_ r(w)\leq k_ r(0)$, and Gaussian summation gives $k_ r(0)\leq 2/r+C r^{-1}\mathrm e^{-1/(2r)}$ for $r\leq 1$. Its integrable error proves the lower bound. On the indicated annulus the nonzero-lattice terms and their derivatives are exponentially small relative to the zero-lattice term, proving the last two identities. For the first, split the defining integral at a fixed small $r_0$. Theorem 8.8.5. There are $A,B,D,\eta _0>0$ such that for every $0<\eta <\eta _0$ there exist $u_\eta \in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)\cap \mathcal R$ and a measurable $K_\eta \subset X$ satisfying

\begin{gather*} \sup _ Xu_\eta =0,\qquad \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\eta >0, \qquad 0\leq -u_\eta \leq B\eta ^{-2}\quad \text{on }X,\\ A\eta ^{-2}\leq -u_\eta \leq B\eta ^{-2}\quad \text{on }K_\eta , \qquad \int _{K_\eta }(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\eta )^3\geq D\eta ^{-1}. \end{gather*}

Proof. Fix $\kappa $ as in lemma 8.3.3, also with $\kappa <1/100$. Choose a small fixed $c>0$ later and set

\begin{gather*} \varepsilon =\mathrm e^{-c\eta ^{-3}},\qquad q_0=\sqrt{c\eta ^{-3}+\log (1/\eta )},\qquad a=\sqrt{\kappa /(2\eta )},\\ q(x)=q_0-\int _0^{ax}\chi (s)\, \mathrm ds,\qquad t(x)=\eta ^{-1}\mathrm e^{-q(x)^2},\\ b=(q_0-1/2)/a,\qquad x_*=(q_0+1/2)/a,\qquad Z_\eta (x,w)=\vartheta (q(x))F_\eta (t(x),w). \end{gather*}

Use the cutoffs $\chi $ and $\vartheta $ from the earlier heat-time construction. Then $Z_\eta =F_\eta (\varepsilon ,\cdot )$ for $x\leq 0$, $Z_\eta =0$ for $x\geq x_*$, and

\[ \eta b,\eta x_*\longrightarrow \ell :=\sqrt{2c/\kappa },\qquad 1+(Z_\eta )_{w\bar w}>1-\eta . \]

The larger value of $q_0$ changes the integrated positivity defect; we estimate it at this scale. Write $r_-=\max (-r,0)$ for the negative part of a real number $r$.

On $[0,b]$ we have $\vartheta =1$. Write $F=F_\eta $ and put $\sigma =(\log t)_ x$, $h=\eta k_ t$ and $\zeta =\sigma t\partial _{\bar w}\log k_ t/\mu $. The identity (8.18) applies to $Z_\eta $ on this interval. The contribution involving $h[1+t(\log k_ t)_{w\bar w}]$ is nonnegative. On $[0,a^{-1}]$,

\[ \sigma =2aq\chi (ax),\quad \sigma _ x=-\frac\kappa \eta \chi (ax)^2+\frac\kappa \eta q\chi '(ax), \quad t\leq \varepsilon \mathrm e^{q_0}. \]

This upper bound for $t$ is smaller than every power of $\eta $. Thus $\kappa q\chi 't\leq \eta $ and $\sigma ^2t\leq \eta ^2$. Use (8.13) with $\kappa \chi ^2$ where $\chi >0$, and $F_ t\geq -\eta $ for the $\chi '$ term. It follows that $[Q[Z_\eta ]]_-\leq C\eta $. Where $\chi =0$ also $\chi '=0$, and $Q[Z_\eta ]=\mu -\mu ^2/(1-\eta +h)>0$, since $x\leq a^{-1}=o(1)$.

On $[a^{-1},b]$, $\sigma =2aq$, $\sigma _ x=-\kappa /\eta $, $\mathrm dx=-\mathrm dq/a$. For $t\leq 1$, (8.18) and (8.13) give

\[ [Q[Z_\eta ]]_-\leq 2\eta +2\kappa q^2t+C\kappa \exp \left(-\frac\eta {C\kappa q^2t}\right). \]

For $t\geq 1$, (8.11) gives instead $[Q[Z_\eta ]]_-\leq C\kappa q^2t^2\mathrm e^{-t/4}$. Put $L=\log (1/\eta )$. Integrating the $q^2t$ term uses $\int _{\sqrt L}^\infty q^2\mathrm e^{-q^2}\, \mathrm dq\leq C\sqrt L\eta $. For the exponential term split at $2\sqrt L$: the initial interval has length $O(\sqrt L)$, and on the rest $\eta ^2\mathrm e^{q^2}/q^2\geq \mathrm e^{q^2/4}$ for small $\eta $. Its remaining integral is uniformly bounded even with upper endpoint infinity. For large time use $\mathrm dx=\sqrt{\eta /(2\kappa )}\, \mathrm dt/(tq)$, $q\leq \sqrt L$, and the integrability of $t\mathrm e^{-t/4}$. Including the constant term and initial interval yields

8.34
\begin{equation} \int _0^ b\max _{w\in E}[Q[Z_\eta ](x,w)]_-\, \mathrm dx \leq C\eta b+C\sqrt{\eta L}\leq C\ell +o(1). \end{equation}

All constants here are independent of $q_0,c,\eta $ with fixed $\kappa $. The bound is small after choosing $c$, although it need not tend to zero.

Also $\sup [(Z_\eta )_ x]_-=O(\sqrt{\eta L})$: for $t\leq 1$ use $F_ t\geq -\eta $ and $t\sqrt{L+\log (1/t)}\leq \sqrt L$; for $t\geq 1$ use (8.11). On $[b,x_*]$, $0\leq q\leq 1$ and $(\mathrm e\eta )^{-1}\leq t\leq \eta ^{-1}$, so differentiated cutoffs and (8.11) give

\[ \sup _ w\bigl (|(Z_\eta )_ x|+|Q[Z_\eta ]-Q[0]|\bigr ) \leq C\mathrm e^{-1/(5\mathrm e\eta )}. \]

Choose $c$ so that $\ell <1/(10\mathrm e)$. Since $Q[0]=\mu (1-\mu )\geq \mathrm e^{-x_*}/4$ here, this implies $Q[Z_\eta ]>0$ on the cutoff interval. For $x\leq 0$ use $Q[Z_\eta ]\geq \mu (1-\mu /(1-\eta ))>0$, and for $x\geq x_*$ use $Z_\eta =0$. On $[-2,-1]$ the same formula gives $Q[Z_\eta ]\geq 2/27$.

The continuous defect $d_\eta (x)=\max _ w[Q[Z_\eta ]]_-$ is therefore supported in $[0,b]$. Choose a smooth nonnegative $r_\eta $, supported in $(-1/2,b+1/2)$, with $r_\eta >d_\eta $ on $[0,b]$ and $m_\eta :=\int r_\eta \leq \int d_\eta +\eta $. Uniform approximation and a positive bump give this choice for each fixed $\eta $; no uniform derivative bound is needed. Fix a smooth probability bump $\psi $ supported in $(-2,-1)$ and put

\[ g_\eta (x)=\int _{-\infty }^ x(x-s)(r_\eta (s)-m_\eta \psi (s))\, \mathrm ds, \qquad V_\eta =Z_\eta +g_\eta . \]

The support order implies $-m_\eta \leq g_\eta '\leq 0$; $g_\eta =0$ at the negative end and is constant at the positive end. Furthermore $g_\eta \leq 0$ and $|g_\eta |\leq m_\eta (b+3)=O_ c(\eta ^{-1})$. Shrink the fixed $c$ so that (8.34) ensures

\[ m_\eta <\min \{ \mu (-2)/2,\, 1/8,\, 1/(27\| \psi \| _\infty )\} \]

for all sufficiently small $\eta $. Now $Q[V_\eta ]=Q[Z_\eta ]+r_\eta -m_\eta \psi >0$ everywhere: use the majorant on $[0,b]$, the margin $2/27$ on $(-2,-1)$, and the established positivity elsewhere. The vertical coefficient is above $1-\eta $. For the horizontal coefficient, derivatives vanish for $x\leq -2$; on $[-2,0]$ use $\mu \geq \mu (-2)>m_\eta $; on $[0,\infty )$ use $\mu \geq 1/2$ and $(Z_\eta )_ x\geq -o(1)$. Thus (8.5) gives a smooth globally strictly positive potential.

On $x<-3$, $V_\eta =F_\eta (\varepsilon ,\cdot )$ has fiber mean zero, so $0\leq \sup _ XV_\eta \leq C\eta $ by lemma 8.8.4 and $g_\eta \leq 0$. Set $u_\eta =V_\eta -\sup _ XV_\eta $. Where the cutoff is nonzero $t\geq \varepsilon $, giving

8.35
\begin{equation} 0\leq -u_\eta \leq 2c\eta ^{-2}+O_ c(\eta ^{-1})+O(\eta ). \end{equation}

Multiplication by a cutoff between zero and one preserves this lower bound for $Z_\eta $, including where $F_\eta $ is positive.

Mass and depth on the concentration region. Let $K_\eta $ consist of all radial angular directions and

\[ q_0/2\leq q(x)\leq 3q_0/4,\qquad \sqrt{t(x)}\leq |w|\leq 2\sqrt{t(x)}, \]

using the small lift near zero in $E$. This lies in the linear transport region, with $\vartheta =1$, $x\asymp _ c\eta ^{-1}\to \infty $, $\sigma =2aq\asymp _ c\eta ^{-2}$, and superexponentially small $t$. Lemma 8.8.4 gives, uniformly,

\[ 0<c_1\leq tk_ t\leq 3,\quad h\to \infty ,\quad \zeta \to 0,\quad \eta \sigma ^2t\to 0,\quad \mu \to 1. \]

In (8.18) the first $h$ contribution is nonnegative, $\sigma _ xtF_ t=-\kappa t(k_ t-1)\geq -3\kappa $, and the completed square tends to one. Its other negative terms tend to zero. Thus $Q[Z_\eta ]\geq 1/4$ for small $\eta $. On the core $\psi =0$ and $g_\eta ''=r_\eta \geq 0$, so $Q[V_\eta ]\geq 1/4$. Also

\[ \mu +(V_\eta )_ x\geq c_2\eta \sigma ,\qquad 1+(V_\eta )_{w\bar w}\geq c_1\eta /t. \]

Indeed $(Z_\eta )_ x=\eta \sigma (tk_ t-t)$ and $\mu +g_\eta '>0$ here; the vertical inequality follows from $1-\eta +\eta k_ t$.

In (8.26), $\Delta _{V_\eta }=(1+(V_\eta )_{w\bar w})Q[V_\eta ]$. The fiber annulus has area $3t/2$. Integrating the preceding lower bounds and using $\sigma \, \mathrm dx=-2q\, \mathrm dq$ yields

\[ \int _{K_\eta }(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\eta )^3 \geq c_3\eta ^2\int _{q_0/2}^{3q_0/4}2q\, \mathrm dq =c_4\eta ^2q_0^2\geq c_5\eta ^{-1}. \]

The annular value estimate also gives

\[ -u_\eta =2\eta q^2+O_ c(\eta ^{-1})+O(\eta \log (1/\eta )). \]

As $\eta q_0^2=c\eta ^{-2}+\eta \log (1/\eta )$, this is bounded above and below by fixed positive multiples of $\eta ^{-2}$ on the core. Together with (8.35), this proves the asserted mass and depth bounds. All these potentials are bounded: truncations beyond their individual depth equal them everywhere, so the defining extended product equals (8.26). $\square$

Corollary 8.8.6. Let $W:[0,\infty )\to (0,\infty )$ be nonincreasing. A uniform bound for the weighted masses

\[ \int _ X W(-u)\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u) \]

over all normalized $\omega $-psh potentials requires

\[ \sup _{T\gg 1}\sqrt T\, W(T)<\infty . \]

Uniform deep tails over that class require $\sqrt T\, W(T)\to 0$.

Proof. On $K_\eta $, monotonicity of $W$ gives

\[ \int _ XW(-u_\eta )\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u_\eta ) \geq D\eta ^{-1}W(B\eta ^{-2}). \]

Every sufficiently large $T$ equals $B\eta ^{-2}$ for some sufficiently small $\eta $, proving the necessary bound. Also $K_\eta \subset \{ -u_\eta >A\eta ^{-2}/2\} $, so uniform deep tails force this lower bound to tend to zero. $\square$

Theorem 8.8.7. Suppose $W$ is positive and nonincreasing, finite on compact intervals, and

\[ \sup _{T\gg 1}\sqrt T W(T)=\infty . \]

Then there is a single $U\in \mathcal R$ such that

\[ \int _ XW(-U)\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)=\infty . \]

Proof. First select the correction with additional local control. Put

\[ x_{\rm pre}=(q_0+1/2-2\sqrt L)/a. \]

Then $t(x_{\rm pre})=\eta ^3$, $x_{\rm pre}\to \infty $, and $b-x_{\rm pre}=O(\sqrt{\eta L})\to 0$. For a fixed large $K$ the preceding defect estimates give $d_\eta \leq (K/2)\eta $ on $[0,x_{\rm pre}]$: on the linear portion $q^2t\leq 4L\eta ^3$, and the exponential error is $O(\exp (-1/(C\kappa \eta ^2L)))=o(\eta )$. On the initial portion use the previously proved $C\eta $ bound. The integral of $d_\eta $ on $[x_{\rm pre},b]$ tends to zero by the same integrated estimates and this interval's length.

Choose a fixed left cutoff, zero for $x\leq -1/2$ and one for $x\geq -1/4$, and a translated right cutoff, one for $x\leq x_{\rm pre}$ and zero for $x\geq x_{\rm pre}+1/4$. Let $r^0_\eta $ be $K\eta $ times their product. It strictly exceeds $d_\eta $ on $[0,x_{\rm pre}]$. The continuous function $e_\eta =\max (d_\eta -r^0_\eta ,0)$ is supported in $[x_{\rm pre},b]$. Add a smooth nonnegative majorant $s_\eta >e_\eta $ on that interval, supported in $(x_{\rm pre}-1/4,b+1/2)$, with $\int s_\eta \leq \int e_\eta +\eta $. Set $r_\eta =r^0_\eta +s_\eta $. Then

\[ r_\eta >d_\eta \quad \text{on }[0,b],\qquad m_\eta =\int r_\eta \leq K\eta b+O(\sqrt{\eta L})+O(\eta ). \]

After shrinking the fixed $c$, this is bounded by a fixed $m_0$ within all the positivity tolerances above. The conclusions of theorem 8.8.5 therefore hold for this choice, with fixed $A,B,D$. On every fixed compact interval $I\Subset (0,\infty )$, for sufficiently small $\eta $ we have $r_\eta =K\eta $ and exactly

8.36
\begin{equation} g_\eta (x)=-m_\eta (x-\mu _\psi ) +K\eta (x^2/2+b_0x+b_1),\qquad \mu _\psi =\int s\psi (s)\, \mathrm ds. \end{equation}

Here $b_0,b_1$ depend only on the fixed left cutoff. Globally $0\leq -g_\eta (x)\leq m_0(x+2)$ for $x\geq 0$.

Let $G(w)=-\int _0^\infty (k_ r(w)-1)\, \mathrm dr$ for $w\ne 0$ on $E$. Gaussian integration gives the uniform bound

8.37
\begin{equation} -F_\eta (t,w)\leq C\eta \bigl (1+\log ^+(1/\operatorname {dist}_ E(w,0)^2)\bigr ),\quad t>0. \end{equation}

Indeed the nearest Gaussian has integral bounded by $C+2\log ^+(1/\operatorname {dist}_ E(w,0)^2)$ on $(0,1]$; the other lattice terms have bounded integral and large times decay. The same bound applies after the cutoff. On a fixed $I\Subset (0,\infty )$, $\vartheta =1$ for small $\eta $ and $t_\eta (x)\leq \exp (-c/(2\eta ^3))$. Every fixed $x$ derivative of $t_\eta $ has this bound times a polynomial in $\eta ^{-1}$. On compact fiber sets away from zero,

\[ F_\eta (t_\eta (x),w)/\eta \longrightarrow G(w) \quad \text{in }C^\ell \text{ for every fixed }\ell . \]

To verify derivatives, subtract $G$ from $G_ t=F_\eta (t,\cdot )/\eta $: $G_ t-G=\int _0^ t(k_ r-1)\, \mathrm dr=-t+O(\exp (-d^2/(Ct)))$ when $d$ stays positive. Differentiated Gaussian errors have only polynomial factors in $t^{-1}$, and repeated $x$ differentiation preserves their decay. Together with (8.36) and $0\leq \sup V_\eta \leq C\eta $, this gives uniform $C^\ell $ bounds for $u_\eta $ on each such fixed compact product set, for all sufficiently small $\eta $. The threshold may depend on the set and derivative order.

Fix $a_ j=2^{-j-2}$, $j\geq 1$, so $\sum a_ j=1/4$. Choose scales $\eta _ j\downarrow 0$ recursively and write $u_ j=u_{\eta _ j}$, $K_ j=K_{\eta _ j}$. Require the minimum $x$ on $K_ j$ to exceed every earlier $x_{*,i}$ by two. Choose a relatively compact product neighborhood $O_ j$ of $K_ j$, with positive $x$ interval and fiber closure away from zero, on which the earlier $u_ i$ are constant. The $K_ j$ are pairwise disjoint. With $E_ j=\sum _{i<j}a_ i\| u_ i\| _\infty $, impose

\begin{gather*} E_ j\leq a_ j\eta _ j^{-2},\qquad \eta _ j\leq a_ j,\\ a_ j\eta _ j\leq 2^{-j}a_ i\eta _ i\quad (i<j). \end{gather*}

For each earlier $O_ i$, require the preceding uniform derivative bounds to hold for $u_ j$ through order $j$. All are finite constraints at stage $j$, achievable by making $\eta _ j$ smaller. They ensure that the future series $\sum _{k>i}a_ ku_ k$ converges in $C^\infty $ on compact subsets of $O_ i$: for every fixed derivative order the sufficiently late terms have a uniform bound there, and $\sum a_ k<\infty $.

These constraints also give $\sum _{k>j}a_ k\eta _ k\leq a_ j\eta _ j$. On $K_ j$ the coordinate and distance estimates are

\[ x\leq C_ c\eta _ j^{-1},\qquad \log ^+(1/\operatorname {dist}_ E(w,0)^2)\leq C_ c\eta _ j^{-3}. \]

Using (8.37), the global slope bound on $g_ k$, and the supremum constants, the future depth is at most

\[ C_ c\eta _ j^{-3}\sum _{k>j}a_ k\eta _ k +C_ c\eta _ j^{-1}\sum _{k>j}a_ k \leq C_ c a_ j\eta _ j^{-2}+C_ c\eta _ j^{-1}/4. \]

The last term is at most a fixed multiple of $a_ j\eta _ j^{-2}$ since $\eta _ j\leq a_ j$. Including the earlier and current terms, there is a fixed $C_*>0$, independent of the scales, such that

8.38
\begin{equation} -\sum _ i a_ iu_ i\leq C_*a_ j\eta _ j^{-2}\quad \text{on }K_ j. \end{equation}

Fix this constant before the scale choice. Since $\sqrt T W(T)$ is unbounded, at each stage choose arbitrarily large $T_ j$ such that

\[ \frac D{\sqrt{C_*}}a_ j^{5/2}\sqrt{T_ j}W(T_ j)\geq 1, \qquad \eta _ j=\sqrt{C_*a_ j/T_ j}, \]

and so small a scale that all finite preceding constraints hold. Such values occur arbitrarily far out because $W$ is finite on compact intervals. Thus the recursive choice is possible.

The smooth nonpositive partial sums $v_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}a_ ju_ j$ decrease, and

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ N =(1-\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j)\omega +\sum _{j\leq N}a_ j(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)\geq \tfrac 34\omega . \]

By theorem 1.4.2, $\int _ X(-v_ N)\, \mathrm dV\leq C\sum _ j a_ j<+\infty $. Thus corollary 1.4.3 gives a $\omega $-plurisubharmonic limit $v$ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq 3\omega /4$. Its supremum is finite and nonpositive.

On $O_ j$ the earlier terms are constant and the future series converges smoothly. Thus $v$ is smooth there and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq a_ j(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)$ pointwise. The determinant inequality for positive Hermitian matrices gives on $K_ j$

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (v)\geq a_ j^3(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^3. \]

On these sets the extended product is the smooth product: on a compact neighborhood of $K_ j$ all sufficiently deep canonical truncations equal $v$. Set $U=v-\sup _ Xv$. Since $\sup v\leq 0$, $-U\leq -v$, and its local currents are unchanged. Monotonicity of $W$ and (8.38) give

\[ \int _{K_ j}W(-U)\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U) \geq D a_ j^3\eta _ j^{-1}W(C_*a_ j\eta _ j^{-2}) =\frac D{\sqrt{C_*}}a_ j^{5/2}\sqrt{T_ j}W(T_ j)\geq 1. \]

The disjoint $K_ j$ prove infinite total mass for this single potential. They lie in its smooth locus, so no mass is assigned to poles. $\square$

8.8.3 Concentration at the critical weight

Lemma 8.8.8. Suppose $\sqrt t\, W(t)$ is bounded but does not tend to zero. There are smooth $u_ j\in \mathcal R$ converging in capacity to $g\in \mathcal R$ such that a weak cluster measure of $\nu _{W,u_ j}$ gives positive mass to the pole divisor

\[ H=\pi ^{-1}\{ [z_0:z_1:z_2]:z_0=0\} , \]

whereas $\nu _{W,g}(H)=0$.

Proof. Use the correction chosen in the proof of theorem 8.8.7. Its plateau and its late supplement satisfy

\[ \int r^0_\eta =K\eta x_{\rm pre}+O(\eta ),\qquad \int s_\eta =O(\sqrt{\eta L})+O(\eta ),\qquad \eta x_{\rm pre}\to \ell . \]

Therefore $m_\eta \to m:=K\ell >0$. Shrink the fixed $c$ so that $m$ satisfies all the established positivity margins. On each fixed finite $x$ interval, $r_\eta $ and all its derivatives tend to zero; the late supplement escapes the interval. Thus $g_\eta $ converges smoothly there to

\[ g(x)=-m\int _{-\infty }^ x(x-s)\psi (s)\, \mathrm ds. \]

It is zero for $x\leq -2$ and equals $-m(x-\mu _\psi )$ for $x\geq -1$. The same margins give $\mu +g'>0$ and $g''+\mu (1-\mu )\geq 0$ at finite $x$. Across $H$, its extension has logarithmic singularity of coefficient $m$: a local defining function $\zeta $ gives $g=m\log |\zeta |^2+O(1)$. Its upper semicontinuous extension is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, has supremum zero and belongs to $\mathcal R$.

The bound (8.37) and $F_\eta \leq C\eta $ imply that, on compact base sets away from $H$, each fixed-depth exceptional set lies in an exponentially shrinking tube about the local holomorphic zero section $w=0$. These tubes have capacity tending to zero. Indeed, on a relatively compact coordinate chart, $\log |w|^2$ is locally integrable and plurisubharmonic; the weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate of section 1.6 bounds the relative capacity of $\{ \log |w|^2<-T\} $ by $C/T$. A finite cover and the local capacity comparison transfer this estimate to $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $.

The corrections converge uniformly on compact base sets away from $H$, including $\xi =0$, and the supremum normalization is $O(\eta )$. Since $g$ has its logarithmic pole on $H$, the estimate (1.26) gives neighborhoods of $H$ with arbitrarily small capacity. Removing one such neighborhood proves $u_\eta \to g$ in capacity.

Assume $\sqrt t\, W(t)$ is bounded but does not tend to zero. Choose the preceding sequence of cores with weighted mass bounded below by $c_0>0$. The proved upper bound makes their full weighted measures uniformly finite, so a subsequence has a weak cluster measure $\nu $. For each fixed closed neighborhood $H_ r$ of $H$, all sufficiently late cores lie in $H_ r$. Portmanteau gives $\nu (H_ r)\geq c_0$; decreasing continuity of the finite measure gives $\nu (H)\geq c_0$. But $\nu _{W,g}(H)=0$ by its defining non-pluripolar truncation. $\square$

Theorem 8.8.9. Within $\mathcal R$ the following assertions hold.

  1. Every individual $\nu _{W,u}$ is finite if and only if $\sup _{t\gg 1}\sqrt t\, W(t)<\infty $. This is also equivalent to a uniform total-mass bound. In this case

    \[ \sup _{u\in \mathcal R}\nu _{W,u}(X)\leq 12Q(W). \]

    \[ \nu _{W,u}(\{ -u>T\} )\leq 12Q_ T(W). \]

    The inequality is understood in the extended sense.

When $\sqrt t\, W(t)$ is bounded but does not tend to zero, there are smooth $u_ j\in \mathcal R$ converging in capacity to $g\in \mathcal R$, whose weighted measures have a weak cluster measure charging the pole divisor of $g$.

Proof. The sufficient conditions are proposition 8.8.3. The necessary uniform mass and tail conditions follow from corollary 8.8.6, whose smooth potentials are invariant. If $\sqrt t\, W(t)$ is unbounded, theorem 8.8.7 disproves universal individual finiteness and prevents a map into finite measures.

For uniform absolute continuity, the concentration regions of theorem 8.8.5 lie in $\{ x\geq c_1/\eta \} $ for a fixed $c_1>0$, hence in shrinking neighborhoods of $H$. The globally defined $h_ H=\log (|z_0|^2/|z|^2)$ has supremum zero and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ H=\beta +[H]\geq 0$. If $\limsup _{t\to +\infty }\sqrt t\, W(t)>0$, choose the scales so that $D\eta ^{-1}W(B\eta ^{-2})$ stays bounded below. Corollary 8.8.10. For a weight $W$ satisfying the assumptions of this section and agreeing for large $t$ with

8.9 Limits of shrinking smooth potentials

When the bounded mass property fails, corollary 7.5.2 produces smooth potentials converging to zero whose Monge–Ampère measures keep an excess mass in the limit. On the Hopf threefold we can determine exactly which excess measures occur: they are the lifts of arbitrary base measures by the fiber probability measures. The proof combines the intermediate currents of proposition 7.5.3 with averaging over the automorphism group.

On a general Hermitian manifold, the restrictions on the intermediate currents in proposition 7.5.3 are still necessary; we do not know whether they are sufficient, or what the support and pluripolar behavior of shrinking defects can be in general.

We keep the Hopf threefold $X$, its projection $\pi :X\to \mathbb P^2$, and the forms $\omega =\alpha +\beta $ of the previous sections. For $p\in \mathbb P^2$, let $h_ p$ be the Haar probability measure on the elliptic fiber $\pi ^{-1}(p)$, that is, the measure $\mathrm{d}A_ E$ transported by the action of $E$. For a finite positive measure $\mu $ on the base, define its fiberwise lift $\mathcal L\mu $ by

\[ \int _ X\varphi \, \mathrm{d}\mathcal L\mu =\int _{\mathbb P^2}\left(\int _{\pi ^{-1}(p)}\varphi \, \mathrm{d}h_ p\right)\mathrm{d}\mu (p), \qquad \varphi \in C(X). \]

Since fiber integrals of continuous functions are continuous in $p$, the map $\mathcal L$ is weakly continuous. The normalization $\int _{\pi ^{-1}(p)}\beta =2$ gives

8.39
\begin{equation} \mathcal L\mu =\tfrac 12\beta \wedge \pi ^*\mu , \qquad \mathcal L(\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2)=\tfrac 16\omega ^3. \end{equation}

Here $\pi ^*\mu $ denotes the pullback of a top-degree current by the submersion $\pi $; locally it is the base measure with constant coefficients in the fiber directions.

For a Hermitian form $\Omega $ on $X$, put

\[ M_\Omega (f)=\int _ X(\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^3,\qquad V_\Omega =\int _ X\Omega ^3 \]

for smooth real $f$, and let $b$ be a real number. A positive measure $\sigma $ on $X$ is called a shrinking defect for $(\Omega ,b)$ if, for every sequence $0<\varepsilon _ j<1$ tending to zero, there are smooth $U_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\Omega )$ and numbers $0<r_ j<\varepsilon _ j$ such that

8.40
\begin{equation} \sup _ XU_ j=0,\qquad w_ j=r_ jU_ j,\qquad M_\Omega (w_ j)=b,\qquad (\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^3\rightharpoonup \Omega ^3+\sigma . \end{equation}

Here weak convergence means convergence against every continuous function on $X$. We first classify the shrinking defects for the metric $\omega $, for which $V_\omega =6$; we then write $m=b-6>0$. Afterwards we pass to an arbitrary Hermitian form.

8.9.1 Positive quadratic measures

The mass formula of proposition 8.1.1 gives

\[ M_\omega (\lambda f)-6=\lambda ^2\bigl (M_\omega (f)-6\bigr ) \]

for every smooth real $f$ and real $\lambda $. To use this scalar identity in a weak limit, we also need a positive measure that represents the quadratic term.

Lemma 8.9.1. Let $f$ be smooth, $0<t<1$, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\geq -t\omega $. Set $H=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f+t\omega $, $d=M_\omega (f)-6$, and $\nu =3\omega \wedge H^2$. Then $d\geq 0$ and

\[ \begin{gathered} \int _ X\omega ^2\wedge H=6t,\qquad \int _ X\omega \wedge H^2=d/3+6t^2,\qquad \int _ XH^3=td+6t^3,\\ \bigl \| (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^3-\omega ^3-\nu \bigr \| _{\mathrm{TV}} \leq 36t+2td+24t^3. \end{gathered} \]

Proof. The Gauduchon identity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega ^2=0$ gives $\int _ X\omega ^2\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=0$, and Stokes' formula gives $\int _ X(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^3=0$. Expanding the three integrals with these identities proves the asserted values, and proposition 8.1.1 gives $d\geq 0$. The identity of measures

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^3 =(1-t)^3\omega ^3+3(1-t)^2\omega ^2\wedge H +(1-t)\nu +H^3 \]

has four positive terms. After subtracting $\omega ^3+\nu $, the total masses of the remaining terms bound the total variation by $18t+18t+t(d+18t^2)+td+6t^3$, as asserted. $\square$

8.9.2 Orbital mixtures

Averaging a shrinking defect over automorphisms of $X$ produces new shrinking defects. The compact group $G=U(3)\times (\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)$ acts transitively on $X$ by the holomorphic maps $g[z]=[\mathrm{e}^ tAz]$, $g=(A,t)$, and preserves $\omega $. Let $h_ G$ be its Haar probability measure. For a probability measure $\rho $ on $G$ and a finite measure $\sigma $ on $X$, write

\[ \mathcal A_\rho \sigma =\int _ Gg^*\sigma \, \mathrm{d}\rho (g). \]

Here the pullback of a measure is defined by $\int _ X\varphi \, \mathrm{d}(g^*\sigma )=\int _ X\varphi \circ g^{-1}\, \mathrm{d}\sigma $. In particular, transitivity gives

\[ \int _ Gg^*\zeta \, \mathrm{d}h_ G(g) =\frac{\zeta (X)}6\omega ^3 \]

for every finite measure $\zeta $ on $X$.

Proposition 8.9.2. If $\sigma $ is a shrinking defect for $(\omega ,6+m)$, then so is $\mathcal A_\rho \sigma $ for every probability measure $\rho $ on $G$.

Proof. Fix a sequence $\varepsilon _ j$ as in the definition. Take a sequence $f_ k=t_ ku_ k$ realizing $\sigma $ as in (8.40), so that $f_ k$ has mass $6+m$, and put $H_ k=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ k+t_ k\omega $. By lemma 8.9.1, the positive measures $\nu _ k=3\omega \wedge H_ k^2$ have masses $m+18t_ k^2$ and converge weakly to $\sigma $.

The idea is to replace $f_ k$ by a normalized sum of $N$ random translates. First suppose that $\rho $ has density at most $L$ relative to $h_ G$. Choose $N$ independent random group elements $g_1,\ldots ,g_ N$ with law $\rho $, and set

\[ W=N^{-1/2}\sum _{a=1}^ Nf_ k\circ g_ a,\qquad \tau =t_ k\sqrt N,\qquad K=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W+\tau \omega =N^{-1/2}\sum _{a=1}^ Ng_ a^*H_ k\geq 0. \]

The quadratic measure of $W$ is

\[ 3\omega \wedge K^2=D+C,\qquad D=N^{-1}\sum _{a=1}^ Ng_ a^*\nu _ k,\qquad C=\frac6N\sum _{a<c}\omega \wedge g_ a^*H_ k\wedge g_ c^*H_ k\geq 0. \]

Averaging $u_ k\circ g$ against $h_ G$ gives a constant, hence $\int _ Gg^*H_ k\, \mathrm{d}h_ G=t_ k\omega $. Consequently $B=\int _ Gg^*H_ k\, \mathrm{d}\rho \leq Lt_ k\omega $, and independence gives

\[ \mathbb E[C(X)]=3(N-1)\int _ X\omega \wedge B^2 \leq 18(N-1)L^2t_ k^2. \]

For any real $\varphi \in C(X)$ with $\| \varphi \| _\infty \leq 1$, the variance of $D(\varphi )$ is at most $(m+18t_ k^2)^2/N$.

For general $\rho $, convolve it with the normalized restrictions of $h_ G$ to shrinking neighborhoods of the identity, which have positive Haar measure. This gives probability measures $\rho _ j\rightharpoonup \rho $ with bounded densities $L_ j$. Fix a countable dense set $(\varphi _ l)$ in the real unit ball of $C(X)$ and numbers $0<\delta _ j<\min (1,m/4)$ tending to zero. Choose $N_ j$ large enough that

\[ \frac{j(m+18)^2}{N_ j\delta _ j^2}<\frac14. \]

Next choose $k_ j$ large enough that $t_{k_ j}<1$, $\tau _ j=t_{k_ j}\sqrt{N_ j}<\min (\varepsilon _ j/4,1/j,1/4)$, $18N_ jt_{k_ j}^2<m/4$, and the expected cross-term mass is less than $\delta _ j/4$. Chebyshev's inequality for the first $j$ test functions and Markov's inequality for $C(X)$ show that some choice of $N_ j$ group elements satisfies

\[ |D_ j(\varphi _ l)-(\mathcal A_{\rho _ j}\nu _{k_ j})(\varphi _ l)|<\delta _ j \quad (l\leq j),\qquad C_ j(X)<\delta _ j. \]

We may also arrange $k_ j\to \infty $. For each fixed test function $\varphi $, the family $\{ \varphi \circ g^{-1}:g\in G\} $ is compact in $C(X)$, so a finite uniform net and the bounded masses of $\nu _ k$ show that $\int \varphi \circ g^{-1}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ k\to \int \varphi \circ g^{-1}\, \mathrm{d}\sigma $ uniformly in $g$. It follows that $\mathcal A_{\rho _ j}\nu _{k_ j}\rightharpoonup \mathcal A_\rho \sigma $. The dense test functions and the bounded masses now imply $D_ j\rightharpoonup \mathcal A_\rho \sigma $, while $C_ j\to 0$ in total variation.

By the integral identity of lemma 8.9.1,

\[ d_ j=M_\omega (W_ j)-6 =m+C_ j(X)-18(N_ j-1)t_{k_ j}^2\longrightarrow m, \qquad d_ j>3m/4. \]

Put $\lambda _ j=\sqrt{m/d_ j}$, so $\lambda _ j\to 1$ and $\lambda _ j<2$. The average $a_ j=N_ j^{-1}\sum _ a u_{k_ j}\circ g_ a$ is smooth and $\omega $-plurisubharmonic. With

\[ U_ j=a_ j-\sup _ Xa_ j,\qquad r_ j=\lambda _ j\tau _ j<\varepsilon _ j, \]

the potential $r_ jU_ j$ differs from $\lambda _ jW_ j$ by a constant and has exact mass $6+m$. Its positive quadratic measure is $\lambda _ j^2(D_ j+C_ j)$. The total-variation estimate of lemma 8.9.1, with parameter $r_ j$, proves the required weak limit. $\square$

Since $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $, corollary 7.5.2 gives a sequence with some defect of mass $m$. Passing to a further subsequence meets any prescribed bounds $\varepsilon _ j$ without changing the defect, so a shrinking defect of mass $m$ exists. Applying proposition 8.9.2 with $\rho =h_ G$ shows that $(m/6)\omega ^3$ is a shrinking defect. In particular, the excess mass can persist as a smooth limiting density.

8.9.3 Fiber measures

The intermediate currents force every shrinking defect to be a lift of a base measure, and the automorphisms of $X$ realize all such lifts.

Lemma 8.9.3. Let $f_ j=t_ ju_ j$, where $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ are smooth, $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$ and $0<t_ j\to 0$. Suppose that $M_\omega (f_ j)=6+m$ and $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j)^3\rightharpoonup \omega ^3+\sigma $. Then there is a unique positive measure $\mu $ on $\mathbb P^2$, of mass $m$, such that

\[ \sigma =\mathcal L\mu ,\qquad (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j+t_ j\omega )^2\rightharpoonup \tfrac 16\pi ^*\mu . \]

Proof. Put $H_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j+t_ j\omega $. By proposition 7.5.3, applied with $h=\omega $ and $n=3$, every convergent subsequence of $H_ j^2$ has a positive closed limit $K$ with $\sigma =3\omega \wedge K$; moreover $\alpha \wedge K=0$, because $\alpha $ is closed and semipositive, so that $\alpha \wedge K$ is a positive measure with $\int _ X\alpha \wedge K=0$. In holomorphic coordinates $(\xi _1,\xi _2,w)$ on a local trivialization of the elliptic bundle, the coefficient matrix of $K$ on the three exterior pairs is positive semidefinite as a matrix of measures. To see this directly, note that positivity of the matrices of $H_ j^2$ passes to the limit when they are tested against constant vectors, and take Radon–Nikodym derivatives with respect to the diagonal trace. Since $\alpha $ is strictly positive in the horizontal directions, the identity $\alpha \wedge K=0$ makes both diagonal entries containing $\mathrm{d}w$ vanish. Positivity then makes their rows and columns vanish, so only the horizontal top-degree coefficient remains.

Since $K$ is closed, both real fiber derivatives of that coefficient vanish in the sense of distributions. Testing against a compactly supported base function times a nonconstant Fourier mode on $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+2\pi \mathrm{i}\mathbb Z)$ shows that every such mode vanishes. By density of trigonometric polynomials in $C(E)$, the coefficient is a base measure times the constant fiber area. Changes of bundle coordinates translate $w$ by a holomorphic function of the base and preserve that area. Thus $K=\pi ^*\tau $ for a positive measure $\tau $ on $\mathbb P^2$.

Since $\alpha \wedge K=0$, (8.39) gives $\sigma =3\beta \wedge \pi ^*\tau =\mathcal L(6\tau )$. Therefore $\mu =6\tau =\pi _*\sigma $ is uniquely determined by $\sigma $, and it has mass $m$. In particular, every subsequential limit of $H_ j^2$ is the same, which proves convergence of the entire sequence. $\square$

Lemma 8.9.4. For every $p\in \mathbb P^2$, the measure $mh_ p$ is a shrinking defect for $(\omega ,6+m)$.

Proof. Start with smooth $f_ j=t_ ju_ j$ realizing the shrinking defect $(m/6)\omega ^3=\mathcal L(m\, \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2)$, and put $H_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j+t_ j\omega $. By lemma 8.9.3, $H_ j^2\rightharpoonup (m/6)\alpha ^2$, while $H_ j$ has trace mass $O(t_ j)$. Fix $A\in GL(3,\mathbb C)$, regarded as an automorphism of $X$, and let $a$ be the induced projective transformation of $\mathbb P^2$. Choose $C_ A\geq 1$ with $A^*\omega \leq C_ A\omega $, and put

\[ R_ A=C_ A\omega -A^*\omega \geq 0,\qquad H_ j^ A=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(f_ j\circ A)+C_ At_ j\omega =A^*H_ j+t_ jR_ A. \]

For this fixed $A$, the trace mass of $A^*H_ j$ is $O_ A(t_ j)$, and the positive difference $(H_ j^ A)^2-A^*(H_ j^2)$ has trace mass $O_ A(t_ j^2)$. Since $\alpha =\pi ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$, it follows that

\[ (H_ j^ A)^2\rightharpoonup \frac m6\pi ^*(a^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2), \qquad 3\omega \wedge (H_ j^ A)^2\rightharpoonup \mathcal L(m a^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2). \]

The limiting measure has mass $m$, so the integral identity of lemma 8.9.1, applied with the parameter $C_ At_ j$, gives $d_ j^ A=M_\omega (f_ j\circ A)-6\to m$. For large $j$, multiply $f_ j\circ A$ by $\sqrt{m/d_ j^ A}\to 1$. The mass is then exactly $6+m$, and the weak limit is unchanged. The underlying normalized potential is $u_ j\circ A/C_ A\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, with parameter $C_ At_ j\sqrt{m/d_ j^ A}\to 0$. The total-variation estimate of lemma 8.9.1 therefore shows that $\mathcal L(m\, a^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2)$ is a shrinking defect; any prescribed bounds $\varepsilon _ j$ are met by passing to a sufficiently late subsequence.

Now take $A_ l=\operatorname {diag}(R_ l^{-1},1,1)$, $R_ l\to +\infty $. Since

\[ a_ l^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2=(a_ l^{-1})_*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2, \]

and $a_ l^{-1}[q_0:q_1:q_2]=[R_ lq_0:q_1:q_2]\to [1:0:0]$ outside the hyperplane $\{ q_0=0\} $, which has zero volume, dominated convergence gives $a_ l^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2\rightharpoonup \delta _{[1:0:0]}$. By weak continuity of $\mathcal L$, the shrinking defects $\mathcal L(m\, a_ l^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^2)$ converge to $mh_{[1:0:0]}$. For each $l$, choose one corrected potential for $A_ l$, sufficiently late in its sequence that its parameter is less than $\varepsilon _ l$ and its measure differs from the corresponding limit by less than $1/l$ on the first $l$ functions of a fixed dense sequence of test functions. This diagonal sequence has the exact mass $6+m$ and the limit $\omega ^3+mh_{[1:0:0]}$. Unitary automorphisms move this fiber to any prescribed fiber. $\square$

Theorem 8.9.5. For $b>6$, the shrinking defects for $(\omega ,b)$ are exactly $\mathcal L\mu $, where $\mu $ ranges over all positive measures on $\mathbb P^2$ of mass $b-6$. Each such defect has support $\pi ^{-1}(\operatorname {supp}\mu )$ and has no point atoms. It charges no pluripolar sets if and only if $\mu $ charges no pluripolar sets on $\mathbb P^2$.

Proof. Put $m=b-6$. Necessity follows from lemma 8.9.3. Finite convex combinations of the measures $mh_ p$ are shrinking defects by lemma 8.9.4, proposition 8.9.2, the latter applied to an atomic measure $\rho $ supported on unitary maps that move a fixed fiber to the desired fibers. Approximate an arbitrary base measure of mass $m$ by finite atomic measures of the same mass, using finite partitions of diameter tending to zero. Weak continuity of $\mathcal L$ and the diagonal choice on dense test functions used in the preceding proof give a realizing sequence for every prescribed sequence $\varepsilon _ j$.

Every nonempty open subset of a fiber has positive Haar measure. Local trivializations therefore show that $\mathcal L\mu $ has support $\pi ^{-1}(\operatorname {supp}\mu )$, and the fiber measures have no point atoms. If $B$ is a pluripolar subset of the base, local plurisubharmonic functions whose pole sets contain $B$ pull back by the submersion to such functions on $X$. Hence $\pi ^{-1}(B)$ is pluripolar, and $\mathcal L\mu (\pi ^{-1}(B))=\mu (B)$; thus $\mathcal L\mu $ charges a pluripolar set whenever $\mu $ does.

Conversely, suppose that $\mu $ charges no pluripolar subset of the base. Cover a pluripolar set in $X$ by countably many local product charts, each carrying a plurisubharmonic function $q(\xi ,w)\not\equiv -\infty $ whose pole set contains the part of the set in that chart. For a relatively compact disk $D$ in the fiber direction, the function

\[ F(\xi )=\frac1{|D|}\int _ Dq(\xi ,w)\, \mathrm{d}A(w) \]

is plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty $ on a smaller base chart. Indeed, local integrability of $q$ and Fubini's theorem show that $F$ is locally integrable, and in particular finite almost everywhere; a common upper bound and the reverse Fatou lemma give upper semicontinuity; and integrating the submean inequalities in the base directions gives the submean property. The restriction of $q$ to a fiber is either subharmonic with a pole set of zero area or identically $-\infty $. The fibers of the second kind lie over the pluripolar set $\{ F=-\infty \} $, which has zero $\mu $-measure. Integrating the Haar measures of the fibers against $\mu $ therefore shows that each local pole set has zero $\mathcal L\mu $-measure, and the countable cover proves the assertion for this pluripolar set. For arbitrary pluripolar subsets, the same argument applies to Borel pole sets containing them, with outer measure. Similarly, for the first implication, a Borel $\mathcal L\mu $-null set containing $\pi ^{-1}(B)$ has a measurable fiber-mass function equal to one on $B$, which gives the corresponding outer-measure statement. $\square$

For every nonempty closed set $F\subseteq \mathbb P^2$, a measure $m\sum _{l\geq 1}2^{-l}\delta _{p_ l}$ carried by a countable dense subset $\{ p_ l\} $ of $F$ has support $F$. Thus every closed set of the form $\pi ^{-1}(F)$ is the support of a shrinking defect. The fibers themselves are pluripolar in a global sense: for the fiber over $[1:0:0]$, the function

\[ \psi =\log \frac{|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2}{|z|^2},\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi \geq -\alpha ,\qquad \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi \geq \beta \geq 0. \]

is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, and its pole set is exactly that fiber; unitary maps give the other fibers. In particular, for $0\leq \lambda \leq 1$ and $p\in \mathbb P^2$, the measure

\[ m\lambda h_ p+\frac{m(1-\lambda )}6\omega ^3 \]

is a shrinking defect. It gives mass $m\lambda $ to the pluripolar fiber $\pi ^{-1}(p)$, while every pluripolar set has mass at most $m\lambda $. For $0<\lambda <1$ its support is all of $X$, despite this positive mass on a pluripolar set.

8.9.4 Changing the Hermitian metric

For a general Hermitian form, the fiber Haar measures are replaced by the normalized area measures of the form. Let $\Omega $ be any Hermitian form on the Hopf threefold $X$. Its restriction to each elliptic fiber has positive area

\[ A_\Omega (p)=\int _{\pi ^{-1}(p)}\Omega . \]

These areas depend smoothly on $p$ and are bounded above and below by positive constants. Define the probability measures $h_ p^\Omega =\Omega |_{\pi ^{-1}(p)}/A_\Omega (p)$ on the fibers, and define the lift $\mathcal L_\Omega \mu $ of a finite positive measure $\mu $ on $\mathbb P^2$ by the formula defining $\mathcal L\mu $, with $h_ p^\Omega $ in place of $h_ p$.

Theorem 8.9.6. For every Hermitian form $\Omega $ on $X$ and every $b>V_\Omega $, the shrinking defects for $(\Omega ,b)$ are exactly

\[ \mathcal L_\Omega \mu ,\qquad \mu \geq 0,\qquad \mu (\mathbb P^2)=b-V_\Omega . \]

Their supports are $\pi ^{-1}(\operatorname {supp}\mu )$, they have no point atoms, and they charge no pluripolar sets exactly when $\mu $ charges no base pluripolar sets. Moreover, $v_+(\Omega )=+\infty $.

Proof. Put $m=b-V_\Omega >0$. For necessity, let $\sigma $ be a shrinking defect for $(\Omega ,b)$. Proposition 7.5.3, applied with $h=\Omega $, gives a positive closed limit $K$ of the squares of the intermediate forms with $\sigma =3\Omega \wedge K$, and $\alpha \wedge K=0$ as in the proof of lemma 8.9.3. The local coefficient and Fourier argument of that proof gives $K=\pi ^*\tau $ for a positive measure $\tau $ on $\mathbb P^2$. Consequently

\[ \sigma =\mathcal L_\Omega \mu ,\qquad \mu =3A_\Omega \tau =\pi _*\sigma , \]

and $\mu $ has mass $m$.

Conversely, fix such a $\mu $ and set $\tau =\mu /(3A_\Omega )$. By theorem 8.9.5, choose a sequence $f_ j=t_ ju_ j$ realizing the shrinking defect $\mathcal L(6\tau )$ for $\omega $. The positive forms $H_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j+t_ j\omega $ have trace mass $O(t_ j)$, and $H_ j^2\rightharpoonup \pi ^*\tau $ by lemma 8.9.3. Choose $C\geq 1$ with $\omega \leq C\Omega $, and put

\[ R=C\Omega -\omega \geq 0,\qquad G_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j+Ct_ j\Omega =H_ j+t_ jR\geq 0. \]

The trace mass of $G_ j$ is $O(t_ j)$, and $G_ j^2-H_ j^2$ has trace mass $O(t_ j^2)$. Thus $G_ j^2\rightharpoonup \pi ^*\tau $ and $3\Omega \wedge G_ j^2\rightharpoonup \mathcal L_\Omega \mu $.

Unlike $\omega $, the form $\Omega $ need not be Gauduchon, so the mass polynomial may have a linear term. Define

\[ \ell _ j=3\int _ X\Omega ^2\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j=O(t_ j),\qquad q_ j=3\int _ X\Omega \wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j)^2\longrightarrow m. \]

These estimates follow by expanding $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j=G_ j-Ct_ j\Omega $ and using the preceding trace bounds. Stokes' formula removes the cubic integral, so

\[ M_\Omega (\lambda f_ j)=V_\Omega +\lambda \ell _ j+\lambda ^2q_ j. \]

For large $j$, $q_ j>0$. The positive root

8.41
\begin{equation} \lambda _ j=\frac{-\ell _ j+\sqrt{\ell _ j^2+4mq_ j}}{2q_ j} \longrightarrow 1 \end{equation}

gives $M_\Omega (\lambda _ jf_ j)=b$. The normalized function $U_ j=u_ j/C$ is $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic, and $w_ j=\lambda _ j f_ j=r_ jU_ j$ with $r_ j=\lambda _ jCt_ j\to 0$.

To identify the full weak limit, first note that expanding $G_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j+Ct_ j\Omega $ gives

\[ \int _ XG_ j^3=Ct_ jq_ j+(Ct_ j)^2\ell _ j+(Ct_ j)^3V_\Omega =O(t_ j). \]

Hence $G_ j^3\to 0$ in total variation. The positive expansion

\[ (\Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)^3 =(1-r_ j)^3\Omega ^3 +3(1-r_ j)^2\lambda _ j\Omega ^2\wedge G_ j +3(1-r_ j)\lambda _ j^2\Omega \wedge G_ j^2 +\lambda _ j^3G_ j^3 \]

converges weakly to $\Omega ^3+\mathcal L_\Omega \mu $, since the second and fourth terms tend to zero in total variation. Given prescribed bounds $\varepsilon _ j$, pass to a sufficiently late subsequence with $r_ j<\varepsilon _ j$ at every index. This preserves the normalization, the exact mass and the weak limit.

The fiber probabilities $h_ p^\Omega $ have smooth densities relative to $h_ p$ that are bounded above and below by positive constants, uniformly in $p$. Thus $\mathcal L_\Omega \mu $ and $\mathcal L\mu $ are bounded by fixed positive multiples of each other, and the assertions about support, point atoms and pluripolar sets follow from theorem 8.9.5. The global functions with fiber poles also remain available: if $\Omega \geq c\omega $, then $c\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\Omega )$.

Finally, for smooth $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ and $c>0$ with $\Omega \geq c\omega $,

\[ \Omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(cu)\geq c(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u),\qquad M_\Omega (cu)\geq c^3M_\omega (u). \]

Since $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $ by corollary 8.7.2 and the supremum may be computed over smooth potentials (proposition 6.5.2), it follows that $v_+(\Omega )=+\infty $. $\square$
