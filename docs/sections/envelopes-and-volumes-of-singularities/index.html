13.4 Envelopes and volumes of singularities

13 Envelopes and volumes of singularities

Throughout this chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, with an auxiliary Hermitian metric $\omega $ and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$, and $\theta $ denotes a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ whose positivity is specified in each section.

In a closed background, the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère mass of a potential depends only on its singularity type; this is what makes mass a tool for comparing singularities. In a nonclosed background, a bounded change of the potential can change the mass, and a singularity type may contain potentials of different, even infinite, masses. This chapter opens the part of the book on relative singularities and the equations they govern, and introduces two kinds of invariants that take this into account.

The first records the variation of mass: the lower and upper volumes $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ and $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ of a potential $u$ are the infimum and the supremum of the masses of all potentials of the same singularity type. The second replaces a mass condition by a condition on envelopes. Fix a reference potential $\varphi $. Stretching a potential $u$ away from $\varphi $ by a factor $b\geq 1$ gives the obstacle $bu-(b-1)\varphi $, which is more singular than $u$ where $u$ lies below $\varphi $. The relative envelope class $\mathcal E_\varphi $ consists of the potentials $u$ at least as singular as $\varphi $ for which every such stretched obstacle lies above some $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function. This class is convex and stable under finite rooftops, its members have the same singularity envelope as $\varphi $, and its definition involves no mass.

The main tool is the contact property of envelopes: the Monge–Ampère measure of an envelope is carried by the set where it touches its obstacle. Together with ordered contact, it gives the domination principle for bounded potentials in a semipositive background and bounds the measures of rooftop envelopes. For envelopes of logarithmic growth on $\mathbb C^ n$, which correspond to envelopes on $\mathbb P^ n$, we also examine how the contact of the regularized envelope with its obstacle depends on the growth allowance.

In a semipositive big background, the contact property also produces positive mass. Let $\varphi $ be a model reference, that is, a fixed point of the singularity envelope that admits a competitor in a smaller background. Every member $u$ of $\mathcal E_\varphi $ then has a competitor in a background $a\theta $ with $0<a<1$, and an envelope in the remaining big background $(1-a)\theta $ has positive mass, which it transfers to $u$ on the contact set. The argument uses only that each bounded potential has positive mass, so it applies even when the infimum of these masses is zero. Conversely, if the Hermitian metric $\omega $ does not have the bounded mass property of definition 6.5.1, the relative envelope class of the zero potential contains potentials of infinite mass. Envelope conditions and mass bounds are therefore different requirements, and this distinction is essential for the equations with prescribed singularities in the next chapter.

We use the following notation from chapter 1. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$, we write $u\preceq v$ if $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$, and $u\sim v$ if $u\preceq v$ and $v\preceq u$; the equivalence class of $u$ is its singularity type (definition 1.12.1). For an obstacle $f$, the envelope $P_\theta (f)$ is the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of all $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $v\leq f$ quasi-everywhere; it may be identically $-\infty $, and then it does not belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the rooftop envelope is $P_\theta (u,v)=P_\theta (\min (u,v))$, and $V_\theta =P_\theta (0)$ is the potential with minimal singularities; see section 1.19. The singularity envelope $P_\theta [u]$, its variant $P_{\theta ,F}[u]$ with a smooth obstacle $F$, and envelope fixed points, that is, potentials with $P_\theta [u]=u$, are defined in definition 1.19.4. We write $\theta _ u=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and $\omega _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$ denotes the extended Monge–Ampère measure $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)$ of theorem 6.1.2. It is the Bedford–Taylor measure $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ when $u$ is bounded, it puts no mass on pluripolar sets, and its total mass, the extended mass of $u$, may be infinite.

13.1 Contact sets

The Monge–Ampère measure of an envelope is concentrated on the contact set, where the envelope meets its obstacle. For a smooth obstacle and a Hermitian background, the envelope is the uniform limit of solutions of exponential equations whose densities tend to zero wherever the envelope stays strictly below the obstacle. Continuity of bounded products then carries this property to quasi-continuous obstacles, first bounded ones, through monotone approximation, and then general ones, through truncation.

Proposition 13.1.1. Let $f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. For $\beta >0$, denote by $u_\beta $ the smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic solution of

\[ \omega _{u_\beta }^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\beta (u_\beta -f)}\omega ^ n. \]

As $\beta \to +\infty $, these solutions converge uniformly to $p=P_\omega (f)$. Moreover,

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ p<f\} }\omega _ p^ n=0. \]

Proof. The solutions exist by theorem 9.6.2. Choose $A\geq 0$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\leq A\omega $. At a maximum point of $u_\beta -f$, the second derivative test gives

\[ \omega _{u_\beta }\leq \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\leq (1+A)\omega . \]

Evaluating the equation at this point, we obtain

\[ u_\beta \leq f+\frac{C_ f}{\beta },\qquad C_ f=n\log (1+A). \]

Thus $u_\beta -C_ f/\beta $ is a competitor for $P_\omega (f)$, and $u_\beta \leq p+C_ f/\beta $.

For the lower bound, note that $p$ is continuous by proposition 1.19.3, and choose smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $v_ j\downarrow p$ by theorem 1.11.1; by Dini's theorem the convergence is uniform. For any $\delta >0$, choose $j$ so that

\[ p\leq v_ j\leq p+\delta /2. \]

Then $v_ j-\delta \leq f$, since $p\leq f$. Since $\omega _{v_ j}>0$ and $X$ is compact, there is $c_ j>0$ such that $\omega _{v_ j}^ n\geq c_ j\omega ^ n$. Put $C_ j=\max (0,-\log c_ j)$. The function

\[ w_{j,\beta }=v_ j-\delta -C_ j/\beta \]

satisfies

\[ \omega _{w_{j,\beta }}^ n=\omega _{v_ j}^ n \geq \mathrm{e}^{-C_ j}\omega ^ n \geq \mathrm{e}^{\beta (w_{j,\beta }-f)}\omega ^ n. \]

Exponential comparison, corollary 10.2.4 with $\lambda =\beta $, gives $w_{j,\beta }\leq u_\beta $. Consequently

\[ p-\delta -C_ j/\beta \leq u_\beta \leq p+C_ f/\beta . \]

Letting first $\beta \to \infty $ with $j$ fixed, so that no lower bound for $c_ j$ uniform in $j$ is needed, and then $\delta \downarrow 0$, we obtain uniform convergence. Bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity then gives weak convergence of the measures.

On a compact subset of the open set $\{ p<f\} $, uniform convergence gives $u_\beta -f\leq -\epsilon $ for some $\epsilon >0$ and all large $\beta $. There the measures $\omega _{u_\beta }^ n$ have densities at most $\mathrm{e}^{-\beta \epsilon }$ with respect to $\omega ^ n$. Testing against continuous functions with compact support in $\{ p<f\} $ and passing to the limit gives $\omega _ p^ n=0$ on this open set. $\square$

Proposition 13.1.2. If $f$ is bounded and lower semicontinuous, then $P_\omega (f)$ is bounded, lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, and its Monge–Ampère measure is concentrated on $\{ P_\omega (f)=f\} $.

Proof. Suppose first that $f$ is continuous. Choose smooth functions $f_ j\to f$ uniformly. The Lipschitz bound for envelopes in proposition 1.19.3 gives $p_ j=P_\omega (f_ j)\to p=P_\omega (f)$ uniformly. Every compact subset of the open set $\{ p<f\} $ has a neighborhood on which $p_ j<f_ j$ for all large $j$. The preceding proposition and convergence of bounded products then give $\omega _ p^ n=0$ on $\{ p<f\} $.

For lower semicontinuous $f$, choose continuous functions $f_ j\uparrow f$ with common upper and lower bounds. For example, for a fixed distance $d$ inducing the topology of $X$, one may take

\[ f_ j(x)=\inf _{y\in X}\bigl (f(y)+j d(x,y)\bigr ). \]

These functions are Lipschitz and increase to $f$: if convergence failed at $x$, approximate minimizers $y$ would approach $x$, contradicting lower semicontinuity of $f$ at $x$.

The functions $p_ j=P_\omega (f_ j)$ increase and stay bounded, so the regularized limit $p_\infty =(\lim _ jp_ j)^*$ is a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function. It agrees with $\lim _ jp_ j$ outside a pluripolar set, and since each $p_ j$ lies below $f_ j\leq f$ outside a pluripolar set, we obtain $p_\infty \leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Thus $p_\infty \leq P_\omega (f)$.

For fixed $k$, the set $\{ p_\infty <f_ k\} $ is open, since $p_\infty $ is upper semicontinuous and $f_ k$ is continuous. If $j\geq k$, then $p_ j\leq p_\infty <f_ k\leq f_ j$ there, so the continuous case gives $\omega _{p_ j}^ n=0$ on that open set. Bedford–Taylor continuity along increasing sequences applies after adding a fixed smooth local function whose Hessian dominates $\omega $ and expanding with respect to the resulting smooth form. Together with the lower semicontinuity of the mass of open sets under weak convergence, it yields

\[ \omega _{p_\infty }^ n(\{ p_\infty <f_ k\} )=0. \]

Taking the union over $k$ gives zero measure on $\{ p_\infty <f\} $, hence also on $\{ p_\infty <P_\omega (f)\} $ outside a pluripolar set, since $P_\omega (f)\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Bounded measures of this kind do not charge pluripolar sets, so the domination principle, theorem 10.2.2, gives $p_\infty \geq P_\omega (f)$. The two functions are therefore equal, and the assertion follows. $\square$

Theorem 13.1.3. Let $\theta \geq 0$ be smooth and let $f$ be bounded and quasi-continuous. Then $p=P_\theta (f)$ is bounded, $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere, and

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ p<f\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)=0. \]

Proof. First suppose that $\theta =\omega $ is Hermitian. Choose bounded lower semicontinuous functions $f_ j\downarrow f$ quasi-everywhere, with common bounds, as in lemma 1.8.8. The bounded functions $p_ j=P_\omega (f_ j)$ decrease to $p$. Indeed, their limit is a competitor for $p$, since it lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, and the reverse inequality follows from monotonicity.

For fixed $k$, put $H_ k=\min \{ 1,(f-p_ k)_+\} $, a bounded quasi-continuous function. Where $H_ k>0$ and $j\geq k$, we have $p_ j\leq p_ k<f\leq f_ j$ outside a pluripolar set. The lower semicontinuous case therefore gives

\[ \int _ X H_ k\, \omega _{p_ j}^ n=0. \]

The convergence of bounded products against quasi-continuous weights in section 1.8 gives $\int _ XH_ k\, \omega _ p^ n=0$. Since the sets $\{ p_ k<f\} $ increase to $\{ p<f\} $, this proves the assertion for Hermitian $\theta $.

For general semipositive $\theta $, set $\theta _ j=\theta +j^{-1}\omega $. The functions $p_ j=P_{\theta _ j}(f)$ have the common bounds $\inf _ Xf\leq p_ j\leq \sup _ Xf$ and decrease. Their limit is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic by distributional convergence and lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, so it is at most $p=P_\theta (f)$; since $p\leq p_ j$ for all $j$, it equals $p$. For each fixed $k$, the same coefficient $H_ k=\min \{ 1,(f-p_ k)_+\} $ gives

\[ \int _ XH_ k\, \mathrm{MA}_{\theta _ j}(p_ j)=0\qquad (j\geq k). \]

All $p_ j$ are bounded potentials for the fixed Hermitian form $\theta +\omega $. Expanding the products with background $\theta _ j$ with respect to this fixed background, we may use convergence of bounded products and uniform capacity bounds to pass the last identity to the limit, with the quasi-continuous weight $H_ k$; this gives $\int _ XH_ k\, \mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)=0$. Exhausting $\{ p<f\} $ as before completes the proof. $\square$

Theorem 13.1.4. Let $\theta $ be smooth and semipositive, and let $f$ be a quasi-continuous obstacle. If $p=P_\theta (f)$ is not identically $-\infty $, then $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ p<f\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)=0. \]

Here the total extended mass of $p$ may be infinite.

Proof. The local envelope theorem, applied to a countable family realizing the supremum (Choquet's lemma), gives $p\leq f$ outside a pluripolar set. Choose $M>\sup _ Xp$ and replace $f$ by $\min (f,M)$. This does not change the envelope, since $p$ itself is a competitor for the smaller obstacle. Since $p<M$, the contact set is also unchanged, so it suffices to prove the theorem for the smaller obstacle; that is, we may assume that $f$ is bounded above.

Set $f_ j=\max (f,-j)$ and $p_ j=P_\theta (f_ j)$. The constants $-j$ are competitors, so $p_ j$ is bounded. Theorem 13.1.3 gives $p_ j\leq f_ j$ quasi-everywhere and

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ p_ j<f_ j\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p_ j)=0. \]

The sequence $p_ j$ decreases and stays above $p$. Its limit is therefore $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, it lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, and so it is a competitor for $p$. Hence $p_ j\downarrow p$.

Fix an integer $k\geq 1$ and $C>0$, and define the bounded nonnegative quasi-continuous function

\[ H_{k,C}=\min \{ 1,(f-p_ k)_+\} \, \min \{ 1,(p+C)_+\} . \]

Its values on pluripolar sets are immaterial. Where this coefficient is positive, $p_ k<f$ and $p>-C$. For $j\geq k$, we then have $p_ j\leq p_ k<f\leq f_ j$ and $p_ j\geq p>-C$ there. Plurifine locality and theorem 13.1.3 therefore imply

\[ \int _ X H_{k,C}\, \mathrm{MA}_\theta (\max (p_ j,-C))=0. \]

The truncated potentials are uniformly bounded and decrease to $\max (p,-C)$, so their measures converge weakly by bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity. The uniform capacity bounds and the weighted convergence in section 1.8 also allow testing against $H_{k,C}$. Hence the last identity holds with $p$ in place of $p_ j$. By plurifine locality, this says that the extended measure of $p$ vanishes on $\{ p_ k<f\} \cap \{ p>-C\} $. Off the pole set of $p$, these sets increase to $\{ p<f\} $ as first $k$ and then $C$ tend to infinity. Continuity of positive measures along increasing sequences proves the assertion, even if the total extended mass is infinite. $\square$

For a smooth big background, constants need not be competitors, and a strictly $\theta $-plurisubharmonic barrier with poles replaces the constant lower truncations. The same contact property for quasi-continuous obstacles finite almost everywhere is proved in this setting in theorem 14.7.4. Combined with ordered contact, either contact theorem controls the measures of rooftop envelopes. In the following bound, the strict inequality in the first term assigns the common contact set $\{ p=u=v\} $ to the second term.

Proposition 13.1.5. Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is semipositive or big, and let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. If $p=P_\theta (u,v)$ is not identically $-\infty $, then

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (p) \leq \mathbf1_{\{ p=u<v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u) +\mathbf1_{\{ p=v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v). \]

Proof. The envelope lies below both obstacles quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, since the obstacles are upper semicontinuous. By the contact theorem (theorem 13.1.4 or theorem 14.7.4), its measure is carried by $\{ p=\min (u,v)\} $. Partition this set into $\{ p=u<v\} $ and $\{ p=v\} $, and apply proposition 6.2.1 on each part. All measures vanish on the pluripolar pole sets, which can therefore be discarded throughout. $\square$

Proposition 13.1.6. Let $\theta $ be smooth and semipositive. Every envelope fixed point $\varphi $ satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (\varphi ) \leq \mathbf1_{\{ \varphi =0\} }\theta ^ n. \]

In particular, its extended mass is finite.

Proof. Since $\varphi =P_\theta [\varphi ]\leq 0$, the function $\varphi $ is a competitor for $P_\theta (\varphi +1,0)$. This rooftop is also a competitor for $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $, being at most $\varphi +1$ and at most zero, so it equals $\varphi $. Now apply proposition 13.1.5 with $u=\varphi +1$ and $v=0$. The contact set $\{ \varphi =\varphi +1<0\} $ is contained in the pole set of $\varphi $, which the extended measure does not charge. Only contact with zero contributes, and this gives the asserted bound. $\square$

13.1.1 Affine growth and projective envelopes

The contact property concerns the regularized envelope. For the classical weighted extremal functions on $\mathbb C^ n$, the obstacle is imposed at every point of a compact set, and one may ask when the regularized envelope still touches the obstacle there. Compactifying $\mathbb C^ n$ to $\mathbb P^ n$ turns the logarithmic growth allowance into a semipositive background. This correspondence distinguishes contact of the raw supremum with the obstacle from contact of its upper semicontinuous regularization, and it shows how both depend on the growth allowance.

In the rest of this section we work on $\mathbb C^ n$ and on $\mathbb P^ n$ rather than on $X$. Write $\chi (z)=\log (1+\lVert z\rVert ^2)$ on $\mathbb C^ n$, and let $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ n$ with this local potential, so that $\int _{\mathbb P^ n}\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^ n=1$ with our normalization of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$. For $\delta >0$ put

\[ \theta _\delta =\tfrac \delta 2\omega _{\mathrm{FS}},\qquad \mathcal L_\delta = \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb C^ n): u(z)\leq \delta \log ^+\lVert z\rVert +C_ u\} . \]

Here $\log ^+=\max (\log ,0)$, and $C_ u$ is a constant depending on $u$. Thus $\mathcal L_\delta $ is the class of plurisubharmonic functions of logarithmic growth at most $\delta $. Let $\mathcal L_\delta ^+$ consist of the members of $\mathcal L_\delta $ that are also bounded below by $\delta \log ^+\lVert z\rVert -C$ for some constant $C$.

Lemma 13.1.7. The map $u\mapsto u-\delta \chi /2$, followed by the canonical extension across the hyperplane at infinity, is a bijection from $\mathcal L_\delta $ onto $\mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^ n,\theta _\delta )$.

Proof. Use homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots :Z_ n]$ and the affine chart $Z_0\ne 0$. On a chart $Z_ i\ne 0$ meeting $H=\{ Z_0=0\} $, the local plurisubharmonic representative of $p=u-\delta \chi /2$ is

\[ p+\frac\delta 2\log \frac{\sum _{j=0}^ n|Z_ j|^2}{|Z_ i|^2} =u+\delta \log |Z_0/Z_ i|. \]

This function is locally bounded above near $H$, by the growth bound on $u$, because the norm of the affine coordinates is at most a constant, depending on the chart, divided by $|Z_0/Z_ i|$. By lemma 1.3.7, it therefore extends canonically across $H$. Since the local potentials of $\theta _\delta $ differ by smooth functions on overlaps, the extensions agree there.

Conversely, every $p\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^ n,\theta _\delta )$ is bounded above, since $\mathbb P^ n$ is compact. The function $u=p+\delta \chi /2$ is plurisubharmonic on the affine chart, and

\[ 0\leq \tfrac 12\chi -\log ^+\lVert z\rVert \leq \tfrac 12\log 2 \]

gives the required growth bound. Neither function is identically $-\infty $ on the affine chart. Uniqueness of the canonical extension shows that the two maps are inverse to each other. $\square$

Fix a non-pluripolar compact set $K\subset \mathbb C^ n$ and a bounded real function $\psi $ on $K$. Define the raw and the regularized envelope

\[ V_\delta =\sup \{ u\in \mathcal L_\delta :u|_ K\leq \psi \} , \qquad F_\delta =V_\delta ^*, \]

where the obstacle condition is imposed at every point of $K$. On $\mathbb P^ n$ put

\[ G_\delta =\sup \{ p\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^ n,\theta _\delta ): p|_ K\leq \psi -\delta \chi /2\} . \]

Proposition 13.1.8. The regularized envelope $G_\delta ^*$ on $\mathbb P^ n$ is bounded and $\theta _\delta $-plurisubharmonic. On the affine chart,

13.1
\begin{equation} V_\delta =\tfrac \delta 2\chi +G_\delta ,\qquad F_\delta =\tfrac \delta 2\chi +G_\delta ^*\in \mathcal L_\delta ^+. \end{equation}

Moreover, $V_\delta =F_\delta $ almost everywhere.

Proof. By lemma 13.1.7, the map $u\mapsto u-\delta \chi /2$ is a bijection between the two families of competitors, which proves the identity for $V_\delta $. Constants below $\inf _ K(\psi -\delta \chi /2)$ are competitors for $G_\delta $.

Since all competitors for $G_\delta $ lie below the constant $\sup _ K(\psi -\delta \chi /2)$ on $K$, lemma 1.19.1 gives a common upper bound for them. Together with a constant competitor, the upper-envelope theorem shows that $G_\delta ^*$ is bounded and $\theta _\delta $-plurisubharmonic. Addition of the smooth function $\delta \chi /2$ in (13.1) commutes with regularization, and the two-sided bounds for $G_\delta ^*$ give $F_\delta \in \mathcal L_\delta ^+$.

Locally, adding a potential of $\theta _\delta $ makes the competitors plurisubharmonic. The upper-envelope theorem recalled in chapter 1 shows that regularization changes their supremum only on a pluripolar set. Thus $G_\delta =G_\delta ^*$ almost everywhere, and adding $\delta \chi /2$ gives the same assertion for $V_\delta $. $\square$

The regularized envelope does not change if the obstacle is imposed only outside a pluripolar subset of $K$. Within the growth class this can be seen directly: a function in $\mathcal L_\delta $ with poles on the exceptional set repairs each competitor.

Lemma 13.1.9. For every pluripolar set $E\subseteq \mathbb C^ n$ and every $\delta >0$, there is $b\in \mathcal L_\delta $ with $b=-\infty $ on $E$. Given a non-pluripolar compact set $K$, it can be chosen with $b\leq 0$ on $K$.

Proof. Put $E_ j=E\cap \overline B(0,j)$. Each $E_ j$ is pluripolar as a subset of $\mathbb P^ n$: it is locally pluripolar near every affine point, and a neighborhood of the hyperplane at infinity does not meet this bounded set. By Josefson's theorem on compact manifolds, [Theorem 1.1.5, SGPT], there is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^ n$, not identically $-\infty $, that equals $-\infty $ on $E_ j$. Rescaling it so that it becomes $\theta _1$-plurisubharmonic and subtracting its supremum gives

\[ p_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^ n,\theta _1),\qquad p_ j\leq 0,\qquad p_ j|_{E_ j}=-\infty . \]

The $L^1$ norms of these normalized potentials are uniformly bounded by theorem 1.4.2. The partial sums

\[ p_ N=\sum _{j=1}^ N2^{-j}p_ j \]

decrease, have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms, and remain $\theta _1$-plurisubharmonic because $\theta _1\geq 0$ and the coefficients sum to at most one. By corollary 1.4.3, their limit $p$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^ n,\theta _1)$. Every point of $E$ is a pole of one of the summands, hence $p=-\infty $ on $E$. By lemma 13.1.7,

\[ b=\delta (p+\chi /2)\in \mathcal L_\delta \]

has the required properties. Its supremum on $K$ is finite, since $b$ is bounded above on the compact set $K$ and is not identically $-\infty $ on $K$, which is non-pluripolar. Subtracting this supremum gives $b\leq 0$ on $K$. $\square$

Corollary 13.1.10. The envelope $F_\delta $ is unchanged if $u|_ K\leq \psi $ is imposed only quasi-everywhere, or only on $K\setminus E$ for any pluripolar $E\subseteq K$.

Proof. Let $u\in \mathcal L_\delta $ satisfy $u\leq \psi $ on $K\setminus E$, where $E$ is pluripolar. Choose $b$ as in the lemma, with $b=-\infty $ on $E$ and $b\leq 0$ on $K$, and put $m=\inf _ K\psi $. For $\epsilon >0$ define

\[ u_\epsilon =\frac{u+\epsilon b+\epsilon m}{1+\epsilon }. \]

This function belongs to $\mathcal L_\delta $. On $K\setminus E$, the inequalities $u\leq \psi $, $b\leq 0$ and $m\leq \psi $ give $u_\epsilon \leq \psi $, and on $E$ the pole of $b$ gives $u_\epsilon =-\infty $. Thus $u_\epsilon $ is a competitor for $V_\delta $, so $u_\epsilon \leq F_\delta $; moreover, $u_\epsilon \to u$ almost everywhere as $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. By lemma 1.3.3, $u\leq F_\delta $ everywhere. Taking the supremum over $u$ and regularizing proves the asserted equalities.

Alternatively, apply proposition 1.19.6 on $\mathbb P^ n$ with the big background $\theta _\delta $ and the bounded obstacle $\psi -\delta \chi /2$, and then use (13.1). $\square$

These equalities concern regularized envelopes. The raw envelope $V_\delta $ is still defined with the obstacle imposed at every point of $K$.

Changing $\delta $ changes both the background $\theta _\delta $ and the obstacle $\psi -\delta \chi /2$. Nevertheless, the set of parameters for which contact holds has a simple structure. Put

\[ \Lambda =\{ \delta >0:V_\delta |_ K=\psi \} ,\qquad \Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)= \{ \delta \in \Lambda :F_\delta (a)=\psi (a)\} \quad (a\in K). \]

The parameters $\delta \in \Lambda $ are those for which the raw envelope touches the obstacle at every point of $K$; we say that raw contact holds for them. For $a\in K$, the parameters in $\Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$ are those for which, in addition, the regularized envelope touches the obstacle at $a$; we say that regular contact holds at $a$.

Theorem 13.1.11. The set $\Lambda $ is empty, $(0,\infty )$, or $[\lambda ,\infty )$ for some $\lambda >0$. If $\Lambda =[\lambda ,\infty )$, each $\Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$ is empty, $\{ \lambda \} $, or $\Lambda $. If $\Lambda =(0,\infty )$, each $\Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$ is empty or $\Lambda $. The same alternatives hold for $\bigcap _{a\in K}\Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$.

Proof. Let $m=\inf _ K\psi $. Inclusion of the growth classes and scaling of competitors give, for $0<\alpha <\beta $,

13.2
\begin{equation} 0\leq V_\beta -V_\alpha \leq \frac{\beta -\alpha }{\alpha }(V_\alpha -m). \end{equation}

Indeed, if $u$ is a competitor for $V_\beta $, then $(\alpha /\beta )(u-m)+m$ is a competitor for $V_\alpha $, since $\psi \geq m$. The constant $m$ is also a competitor, so all terms are finite and the right-hand side is nonnegative. The estimate shows that $V_\delta (z)$ depends continuously on $\delta >0$ at every point $z$. Since $V_\delta \leq \psi $ on $K$ and $V_\delta $ increases with $\delta $, the set $\Lambda $ is closed upwards, and by continuity it is relatively closed in $(0,\infty )$. This proves the three possible forms of $\Lambda $.

For $0\leq t\leq 1$, convex combinations of competitors give

\[ V_{(1-t)\alpha +t\beta }\geq (1-t)V_\alpha +tV_\beta . \]

By the preceding proposition, the same inequality holds for the regularized envelopes almost everywhere, and hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Thus $F_\delta (z)$ is a concave nondecreasing function of $\delta $ at every point $z$.

For $a\in K$ and $\delta \in \Lambda $, the defect $d_\delta =F_\delta (a)-\psi (a)$ is nonnegative, concave and nondecreasing in $\delta $. Suppose $d_\alpha =0$, and that there is $\eta \in \Lambda $ with $\eta <\alpha $. For every $\beta >\alpha $, write $\alpha $ as a convex combination of $\eta $ and $\beta $ with positive weights. Concavity then forces both nonnegative defects $d_\eta $ and $d_\beta $ to vanish, and monotonicity gives $d_\delta =0$ for all $\delta \in \Lambda $ with $\delta <\alpha $. Hence a zero of the defect at a parameter other than the smallest element of $\Lambda $ forces every defect to vanish. This proves the alternatives for each $\Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$, and intersecting them gives the alternatives for $\bigcap _{a\in K}\Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$. $\square$

Thus raw contact persists as the growth allowance increases, whereas regular contact at a point may hold only at the smallest contact parameter. The correspondence with $\mathbb P^ n$ keeps the background and the obstacle matched throughout this variation.

Growth slopes and supporting probabilities

By concavity, the rate at which $F_\delta $ grows when the growth allowance $\delta $ is increased is at least its rate of growth for large $\delta $, and the latter is the classical extremal function of $K$, without weight. For continuous weights, supporting probabilities express the initial rate of growth through tests by functions of the fixed class $\mathcal L_1$.

When the weight varies, we write $V_{\delta ,q}$ and $F_{\delta ,q}=V_{\delta ,q}^*$ for the raw and regularized envelopes defined as above, with a bounded obstacle $q$ on the fixed compact set $K$ in place of $\psi $. Thus $F_\delta =F_{\delta ,\psi }$. Set

\[ V_ K=V_{1,0},\qquad V_ K^*=F_{1,0},\qquad m=\inf _ K\psi ,\qquad M=\sup _ K\psi . \]

Thus $V_ K^*$ is the extremal function of $K$. Since the constant $0$ is a competitor for $V_ K$, and since competitors can be scaled and translated by constants, we have $V_ K^*\geq 0$ and

13.3
\begin{equation} \delta V_ K^*+m\leq F_\delta \leq \delta V_ K^*+M. \end{equation}

Lemma 13.1.12. For $0<\alpha <\beta $, one has on $\mathbb C^ n$

13.4
\begin{equation} (\beta -\alpha )V_ K^*\leq F_\beta -F_\alpha \leq \frac{\beta -\alpha }{\alpha }(F_\alpha -m). \end{equation}

Proof. If $u$ is a competitor for $V_\alpha =V_{\alpha ,\psi }$ and $v$ is a competitor for $V_ K=V_{1,0}$, then $u+(\beta -\alpha )v$ is a competitor for $V_\beta $. Taking the suprema over $u$ and $v$ independently gives

\[ V_\beta \geq V_\alpha +(\beta -\alpha )V_ K. \]

By proposition 13.1.8, the raw envelopes agree almost everywhere with their regularizations, so this inequality holds almost everywhere for the regularizations, and hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. This is the first inequality. The same argument applied to (13.2) gives the second. $\square$

Proposition 13.1.13. For every $a\in \mathbb C^ n$ and $\tau >0$, the right derivative

\[ D_\tau (a)=\lim _{h\downarrow 0}\frac{F_{\tau +h}(a)-F_\tau (a)}{h} \]

exists and is finite. The difference quotients decrease as $h>0$ increases, and

13.5
\begin{equation} V_ K^*(a)\leq \frac{F_{\tau +h}(a)-F_\tau (a)}h \leq D_\tau (a)\leq \frac{F_\tau (a)-m}{\tau }. \end{equation}

As $h\to \infty $, they converge to $V_ K^*(a)$, with the error bound

13.6
\begin{equation} 0\leq \frac{F_{\tau +h}(a)-F_\tau (a)}h-V_ K^*(a) \leq \frac{M-m}h. \end{equation}

Moreover, $D_\tau (a)=V_ K^*(a)$ if and only if

13.7
\begin{equation} F_\delta (a)=F_\tau (a)+(\delta -\tau )V_ K^*(a) \qquad (\delta \geq \tau ). \end{equation}

Proof. For $0<h_1<h_2$, concavity of $F_\delta (a)$ gives

\[ F_{\tau +h_1}(a)-F_\tau (a) \geq \frac{h_1}{h_2}\bigl (F_{\tau +h_2}(a)-F_\tau (a)\bigr ). \]

Thus the difference quotients decrease with $h$. Lemma 13.1.12 bounds them between $V_ K^*(a)$ and $(F_\tau (a)-m)/\tau $, which proves the existence and finiteness of $D_\tau (a)$ and (13.5). Combining the lower bound for the increments with (13.3) gives (13.6), with a bound independent of $a$. If $D_\tau (a)=V_ K^*(a)$, then by (13.5) every difference quotient equals $V_ K^*(a)$, which gives (13.7). Conversely, that formula gives $D_\tau (a)=V_ K^*(a)$. $\square$

Corollary 13.1.14. Let $a\in K$ and $\tau \in \Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$. Regular contact at $a$ holds for every $\delta >\tau $ if and only if $D_\tau (a)=0$. If $D_\tau (a)>0$, regular contact at $a$ fails for every $\delta >\tau $, and $\tau $ is the smallest element of $\Lambda $.

Proof. At $\tau $ one has $F_\tau (a)=\psi (a)$, and raw contact holds for all $\delta \geq \tau $ by theorem 13.1.11. If $D_\tau (a)=0$, then by (13.5) every difference quotient at $\tau $ vanishes, so $F_\delta (a)=\psi (a)$ for all $\delta >\tau $. If $D_\tau (a)>0$, then $F_\delta (a)>\psi (a)$ for all $\delta $ slightly larger than $\tau $, and by monotonicity for all $\delta >\tau $. In this case $\Lambda _{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$ contains $\tau $ but no larger parameter, so by theorem 13.1.11 it equals $\{ \lambda \} $, where $\lambda =\tau $ is the smallest element of $\Lambda $. $\square$

Assume now that the weights are continuous. If the obstacle is imposed only quasi-everywhere on $K$, the regularized envelope itself becomes a competitor, so the supremum needs no regularization; a constraint imposed at every point would miss the values created by regularization. Indeed, for $q\in C(K,\mathbb R)$,

13.8
\begin{equation} F_{\delta ,q}(z)=\sup \{ u(z):u\in \mathcal L_\delta , \ u\leq q\text{ q.e. on }K\} . \end{equation}

The envelope $F_{\delta ,q}$ itself is a competitor on the right, since it agrees with the raw envelope off a pluripolar set by proposition 13.1.8. Conversely, the argument of corollary 13.1.10 shows that every competitor on the right lies below $F_{\delta ,q}$ everywhere.

For $a\in \mathbb C^ n$, let $\mathcal J_ a(K)$ be the set of supporting pairs $(\mu ,c)$, where $\mu $ is a Borel probability measure on $K$ and $c\geq 0$, such that

13.9
\begin{equation} u(a)\leq \int _ K f\, \mathrm{d}\mu +c \end{equation}

whenever $u\in \mathcal L_1$, $f\in C(K,\mathbb R)$ and $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. We call $\mu $ a supporting probability at $a$.

Theorem 13.1.15. For every $a\in \mathbb C^ n$, $q\in C(K,\mathbb R)$ and $\delta >0$,

13.10
\begin{equation} F_{\delta ,q}(a)=\min _{(\mu ,c)\in \mathcal J_ a(K)} \left(\int _ K q\, \mathrm{d}\mu +\delta c\right). \end{equation}

In particular, $\mathcal J_ a(K)$ is nonempty and

\[ \min _{(\mu ,c)\in \mathcal J_ a(K)}c=V_ K^*(a). \]

Proof. On the real Banach space $C(K)$, put $T(f)=F_{1,f}(a)$. By (13.8), $T$ is concave, since convex combinations of competitors are competitors. Comparison of obstacles and translation by constants show that $T$ is nondecreasing and satisfies

\[ T(f+t)=T(f)+t,\qquad |T(f)-T(g)|\leq \| f-g\| _\infty . \]

Fix $f_0\in C(K)$. The directional derivative of the convex Lipschitz function $-T$ at $f_0$ is finite and sublinear. By the Hahn–Banach theorem, there is a continuous linear functional dominated by it, and since difference quotients of a convex function are monotone, this functional supports $-T$ at $f_0$. Changing the sign, we obtain a continuous linear functional $\ell $ with

\[ T(f)\leq T(f_0)+\ell (f-f_0)\qquad (f\in C(K)). \]

Testing $f=f_0+th$ with $h\geq 0$ and $t>0$, and using monotonicity, gives $\ell (h)\geq 0$. Testing with $f=f_0\pm t$ gives $\ell (1)=1$. By the Riesz representation theorem, $\ell (f)=\int _ K f\, \mathrm{d}\mu $ for a Borel probability measure $\mu $ on $K$. Set $c=T(f_0)-\int _ K f_0\, \mathrm{d}\mu $. The supporting inequality at $f=0$ gives $c\geq T(0)=V_ K^*(a)\geq 0$. Together with (13.8), it shows that $(\mu ,c)\in \mathcal J_ a(K)$ and

\[ T(f_0)=\int _ K f_0\, \mathrm{d}\mu +c. \]

Conversely, applying (13.9) to $u=F_{1,f_0}$, which lies below $f_0$ quasi-everywhere on $K$, shows that $T(f_0)\leq \int _ Kf_0\, \mathrm{d}\mu +c$ for every pair in $\mathcal J_ a(K)$. This proves (13.10) for $\delta =1$, with the minimum attained. The identity $F_{\delta ,q}=\delta F_{1,q/\delta }$ gives the general case, and the zero obstacle gives the last assertion. $\square$

Theorem 13.1.16. If $\psi \in C(K,\mathbb R)$, then for every $a\in \mathbb C^ n$ and $\tau >0$,

13.11
\begin{equation} D_\tau (a)=\min \left\{ c:(\mu ,c)\in \mathcal J_ a(K), \ \int _ K\psi \, \mathrm{d}\mu +\tau c=F_\tau (a)\right\} . \end{equation}

Consequently, $D_\tau (a)=V_ K^*(a)$ if and only if there is a Borel probability measure $\mu $ on $K$ such that

\[ (\mu ,V_ K^*(a))\in \mathcal J_ a(K),\qquad \int _ K\psi \, \mathrm{d}\mu =F_\tau (a)-\tau V_ K^*(a). \]

Proof. Each defining inequality of $\mathcal J_ a(K)$ is preserved under weak convergence of the probability measures and convergence of the constants, because the test function $f$ is continuous. Since the Borel probability measures on the compact metric space $K$ form a weakly compact set, the pairs in $\mathcal J_ a(K)$ with $c\leq C$ form a compact set for each finite $C$.

Call a pair in $\mathcal J_ a(K)$ active at $\tau $ if it satisfies the equality in (13.11). Such pairs exist by theorem 13.1.15, and since $\int _ K\psi \, \mathrm{d}\mu \geq m=\inf _ K\psi $, they satisfy

\[ 0\leq c\leq \frac{F_\tau (a)-m}{\tau }. \]

The set of active pairs is therefore compact, and the minimum of $c$ over it is attained. By theorem 13.1.15 at $\tau +h$, each active pair gives $F_{\tau +h}(a)\leq F_\tau (a)+hc$, hence $D_\tau (a)\leq c$.

Conversely, for $h>0$ choose a pair $(\mu _ h,c_ h)$ attaining the minimum in (13.10) at $\tau +h$, with $q=\psi $. The representation at $\tau $ gives

\[ F_\tau (a)\leq \int _ K\psi \, \mathrm{d}\mu _ h+\tau c_ h =F_{\tau +h}(a)-hc_ h. \]

Thus $0\leq c_ h\leq (F_{\tau +h}(a)-F_\tau (a))/h$, and these constants are bounded by (13.5). Along a sequence $h\downarrow 0$, compactness gives a limiting pair $(\mu ,c)\in \mathcal J_ a(K)$. The same bound makes $F_{\tau +h}(a)\to F_\tau (a)$ and $hc_ h\to 0$, and continuity of $\psi $ then gives $\int _ K\psi \, \mathrm{d}\mu +\tau c=F_\tau (a)$. Thus the limit pair is active, and it satisfies $c\leq D_\tau (a)$. This proves (13.11). Finally, every pair has $c\geq V_ K^*(a)$ by theorem 13.1.15, so the attainment of the minimum gives the stated criterion. $\square$

At a point $a$ of regular contact, $F_\tau (a)=\psi (a)$, so the criterion involves only the original weight and tests by functions in $\mathcal L_1$. If the defining tests instead required $u\leq f$ at every point of $K$, the pair $(\delta _ a,0)$, where $\delta _ a$ is the unit point mass at $a\in K$, would always be allowed; the resulting infimum would be at most $\psi (a)$ and would not detect a defect created by regularization.

Variation of continuous weights

Supporting probabilities also describe how the envelope responds to changes of the weight, jointly with changes of the growth allowance. Keep the non-pluripolar compact set $K\subset \mathbb C^ n$ fixed. For $a\in \mathbb C^ n$, $\delta >0$ and $q\in C(K,\mathbb R)$, let

13.12
\begin{equation} \mathcal J^{\mathrm{act}}_ a(\delta ,q)= \left\{ (\mu ,c)\in \mathcal J_ a(K): \int _ Kq\, \mathrm{d}\mu +\delta c=F_{\delta ,q}(a)\right\} . \end{equation}

be the set of pairs that are active at $(\delta ,q)$. We give the weights the uniform norm, the probability measures the weak topology, and the constants the usual topology of $\mathbb R$.

Lemma 13.1.17. The set $\mathcal J^{\mathrm{act}}_ a(\delta ,q)$ is nonempty, convex and compact. For $\delta ,\eta \geq \epsilon >0$ and $p,q\in C(K,\mathbb R)$,

13.13
\begin{equation} |F_{\eta ,p}(a)-F_{\delta ,q}(a)| \leq \| p-q\| _\infty + |\eta -\delta |\left(V_ K^*(a)+ \frac{\operatorname {osc}_ Kq}{\epsilon }\right). \end{equation}

If $\delta _ j\to \delta >0$, $q_ j\to q$ uniformly and $(\mu _ j,c_ j)\in \mathcal J^{\mathrm{act}}_ a(\delta _ j,q_ j)$, then these pairs have a convergent subsequence, and every subsequential limit belongs to $\mathcal J^{\mathrm{act}}_ a(\delta ,q)$.

Proof. Nonemptiness follows from theorem 13.1.15. Since the test functions are continuous, $\mathcal J_ a(K)$ is closed and convex, and the equality defining active pairs is a closed affine condition. For an active pair, (13.3), applied with the weight $q$, gives

\[ 0\leq c\leq \frac{F_{\delta ,q}(a)-\inf _ Kq}{\delta } \leq V_ K^*(a)+\frac{\operatorname {osc}_ Kq}{\delta }. \]

The pairs in $\mathcal J_ a(K)$ with bounded constants form a compact set, as in the proof of theorem 13.1.16; hence the set of active pairs is compact.

With $q$ fixed, lemma 13.1.12 and (13.3), applied with the weight $q$, bound $|F_{\eta ,q}(a)-F_{\delta ,q}(a)|$ by $|\eta -\delta |(V_ K^*(a)+\operatorname {osc}_ Kq/\epsilon )$. Comparison of obstacles and translation by constants bound $|F_{\eta ,p}(a)-F_{\eta ,q}(a)|$ by $\| p-q\| _\infty $. Adding these bounds gives (13.13).

For the sequence in the statement, the constants $c_ j$ have a common bound, by the estimate above, because the weights $q_ j$ are uniformly bounded and the parameters $\delta _ j$ are bounded below by a positive constant. Weak compactness of probability measures gives a convergent subsequence of pairs, and its limit belongs to $\mathcal J_ a(K)$, which is closed. The estimate (13.13) gives $F_{\delta _ j,q_ j}(a)\to F_{\delta ,q}(a)$, while uniform convergence of the $q_ j$ and weak convergence of the $\mu _ j$ give $\int _ Kq_ j\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ j\to \int _ Kq\, \mathrm{d}\mu $. Passing to the limit in the equality defining active pairs proves the last assertion. $\square$

Proposition 13.1.18. For $r\in C(K,\mathbb R)$ and $s\in \mathbb R$, one has

13.14
\begin{equation} \lim _{h\downarrow 0} \frac{F_{\delta +hs,q+hr}(a)-F_{\delta ,q}(a)}h =\min _{(\mu ,c)\in \mathcal J^{\mathrm{act}}_ a(\delta ,q)} \left(\int _ Kr\, \mathrm{d}\mu +sc\right). \end{equation}

The same limit holds along any sequence $h_ j\downarrow 0$ if $s,r$ in the difference quotient are replaced by $s_ j,r_ j$, where $s_ j\to s$ and $r_ j\to r$ uniformly. Thus the one-sided directional derivative is stable under convergence of the directions.

Proof. By compactness of the set of active pairs, the minimum on the right is attained. For a fixed pair $(\mu ,c)$ active at $(\delta ,q)$, the representation (13.10) at the perturbed parameters gives

\[ F_{\delta +hs,q+hr}(a) \leq F_{\delta ,q}(a)+h\left(\int _ Kr\, \mathrm{d}\mu +sc\right). \]

Choosing an active pair that attains the minimum on the right, we see that the upper limit of the difference quotients is at most this minimum. Conversely, choose a pair $(\mu _ h,c_ h)$ active at $(\delta +hs,q+hr)$. The representation at $(\delta ,q)$ gives

\[ \frac{F_{\delta +hs,q+hr}(a)-F_{\delta ,q}(a)}h \geq \int _ Kr\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ h+sc_ h. \]

The estimate (13.13) bounds the difference quotients. Along a sequence of values of $h$ realizing their lower limit, lemma 13.1.17 gives a subsequence of pairs converging to a pair active at $(\delta ,q)$. For this pair, $\int _ Kr\, \mathrm{d}\mu +sc$ is at least the minimum on the right. This proves (13.14).

For convergent directions, the same two inequalities hold. The fixed active pair attaining the minimum gives an upper bound with the right limit. The active pairs at the perturbed parameters still have uniformly bounded constants, and their subsequential limits are active at $(\delta ,q)$. Uniform convergence of the $r_ j$ controls the integrals $\int _ Kr_ j\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{h_ j}$, and $s_ j\to s$ controls the terms $s_ jc_{h_ j}$. The lower bound therefore has the same limit. Note that all perturbed growth allowances $\delta +h_ js_ j$ are positive for small $h_ j$. $\square$

Corollary 13.1.19. For fixed $a$ and $q$, the function $\delta \mapsto F_{\delta ,q}(a)$ has finite left and right derivatives $D^-_{\delta ,q}(a)$ and $D^+_{\delta ,q}(a)$ at every positive parameter, with

13.15
\begin{equation} D^-_{\delta ,q}(a)=\max _{\mathcal J^{\mathrm{act}}_ a(\delta ,q)}c, \qquad D^+_{\delta ,q}(a)=\min _{\mathcal J^{\mathrm{act}}_ a(\delta ,q)}c. \end{equation}

It is differentiable exactly when all active pairs have the same constant $c$. For each fixed $a,q$, the set of positive parameters where it fails to be differentiable is at most countable.

Proof. Apply (13.14) with $r=0$ and $s=\pm 1$. The direction $s=-1$ gives minus the left derivative, which proves (13.15). Concavity gives

\[ D^+_{\alpha ,q}(a)\geq D^-_{\beta ,q}(a) \qquad (0<\alpha <\beta ). \]

Hence the open intervals $(D^+_{\delta ,q}(a),D^-_{\delta ,q}(a))$, which are nonempty exactly at the parameters where differentiability fails, are pairwise disjoint. Choosing a rational number in each of them proves countability. $\square$

Corollary 13.1.20. For fixed $a$ and $\delta >0$, the map $q\mapsto F_{\delta ,q}(a)$ on $C(K,\mathbb R)$ is Gâteaux differentiable at $q$ if and only if all pairs active at $(\delta ,q)$ have the same probability measure $\mu $. In that case its derivative is

\[ r\longmapsto \int _ Kr\, \mathrm{d}\mu . \]

Proof. With $s=0$, (13.14) shows that the right derivative in the direction $r$ is the minimum of $\int _ Kr\, \mathrm{d}\mu $ over active pairs, and the direction $-r$ shows that the left derivative is the maximum. If all active pairs have the same probability measure, these values agree and define a continuous linear derivative. Conversely, if the map is Gâteaux differentiable, the minimum and the maximum agree for every continuous $r$. Continuous functions separate Borel probability measures on $K$, so all active pairs have the same probability measure; their constant is then determined by (13.12), since $\delta >0$. $\square$

Example 13.1.21. Let $D\subset \mathbb C$ be the open unit disk, let $K=\overline D$, and fix $\delta >0$. For $q_ t(z)=-t|z|^2$,

13.16
\begin{equation} F_{\delta ,q_ t}(0)= \begin{cases} -t,& t\geq 0,\\ 0,& -\delta /2\leq t\leq 0. \end{cases} \end{equation}

Thus the map $q\mapsto F_{\delta ,q}(0)$ is not Gâteaux differentiable at $q=0$, and there are active pairs at $(\delta ,0)$ with different probability measures.

Proof. For $t\geq 0$, each competitor is at most $-t$ on the unit circle, so the maximum principle for subharmonic functions bounds it by $-t$ on $D$. This bound persists after regularization on $D$, and the constant $-t$ is a competitor. Hence $F_{\delta ,q_ t}(0)=-t$.

For $t=-b$ with $0\leq b\leq \delta /2$, define

\[ u_ b(z)= \begin{cases} b|z|^2,& |z|\leq 1,\\ b+\delta \log |z|,& |z|>1. \end{cases} \]

This function is continuous and subharmonic: its Laplacian has density $4b$ inside the disk, density zero outside, and a nonnegative contribution on the circle coming from the jump $\delta -2b\geq 0$ of the radial derivative. It belongs to $\mathcal L_\delta $ and equals $q_{-b}$ on $K$. Hence $F_{\delta ,q_{-b}}(0)\geq u_ b(0)=0$. On $D$ the raw envelope is at most the continuous function $b|z|^2$, so its regularization at zero is at most zero. This proves (13.16). In particular, the right and left derivatives in $t$ at $t=0$ are $-1$ and $0$, and corollary 13.1.20, applied in the direction $r=-|z|^2$, gives the final assertion. $\square$

Local regularity and strict weights

Whether the regularized envelope touches the obstacle at a point $a\in K$ depends only on the parts of $K$ near $a$. Without weight, this local regularity is detected by the relative extremal function $h_{E,B}$ of definition 1.6.3, for compact pieces $E$ of $K$ near $a$ and small balls $B$ centered at $a$. If the weight extends to a global function whose complex Hessian has a strict lower bound near $a$, a failure of this regularity produces a quantitative defect of the weighted envelope.

For a non-pluripolar compact set $E\subset \mathbb C^ n$, write $V_ E$ and $V_ E^*$ for the raw and regularized envelopes of growth one with zero obstacle on $E$; thus $V_ K=V_{1,0}$ as before. For other weights and growth allowances, write $F_{\delta ,E,q}$ for the regularized envelope with growth allowance $\delta $ and obstacle $q$ imposed at every point of $E$.

Lemma 13.1.22. Let $a\in \mathbb C^ n$, $0<r<R$ and $B=B(a,R)$, and let $E\subseteq \overline B(a,r)$ be compact. If $E$ is pluripolar, then $h_{E,B}^*=0$ on $B$. If $E$ is non-pluripolar, then

\[ h_{E,B}^*(a)=-1\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad V_ E^*(a)=0. \]

Proof. In the pluripolar case, choose $b\in \mathcal L_1$ with $b=-\infty $ on $E$ by lemma 13.1.9, and a finite upper bound $C$ for $b$ on $B$. The functions

\[ u_\epsilon =\max \{ -1,\epsilon (b-C)\} \]

are competitors for $h_{E,B}$, and they tend to zero almost everywhere as $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. Hence $h_{E,B}^*\geq 0$ almost everywhere, and then everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Since $h_{E,B}^*\leq 0$, it vanishes.

Now suppose that $E$ is non-pluripolar. Proposition 13.1.8 gives a finite upper bound $M\geq 1$ for $V_ E^*$ on $B$. For each competitor $v$ for $V_ E$, the function $\max (-1,v/M-1)$ is a competitor for $h_{E,B}$. Taking suprema and regularizing gives

\[ h_{E,B}^*(a)\geq \max \{ -1,V_ E^*(a)/M-1\} . \]

As $V_ E^*\geq 0$, the equality $h_{E,B}^*(a)=-1$ forces $V_ E^*(a)=0$.

For the converse, choose $r<R_0<R$ and $k>0$ with $k\log (R_0/r)>1$. The plurisubharmonic function

\[ L(z)=k\log \bigl (\lVert z-a\rVert /r\bigr ) \]

belongs to $\mathcal L_ k$ and equals $-\infty $ at $a$. For a competitor $u$ for $h_{E,B}$, define $W=\max (u+1,L)$ on $B$ and $W=L$ outside $B$. On the collar $R_0<\lVert z-a\rVert <R$ we have $L>1\geq u+1$, so the two definitions agree there, and $W$ is plurisubharmonic by lemma 1.3.2. On $E$ one has $u=-1$ and $L\leq 0$, so $W=0$; on the ball $B(a,r)$ one has $L<0\leq u+1$, so $W=u+1$. Hence $W/k$ is a competitor for $V_ E$, with $k$ independent of $u$. Taking the supremum over $u$ and regularizing at $a$ gives

\[ V_ E^*(a)\geq (1+h_{E,B}^*(a))/k. \]

Thus $V_ E^*(a)=0$ forces $h_{E,B}^*(a)=-1$. $\square$

Let $K\subset \mathbb C^ n$ again be a non-pluripolar compact set. For $a\in K$ and $r>0$, put $K_ r=K\cap \overline B(a,r)$.

Definition 13.1.23. The compact set $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a\in K$ if every $K_ r$, $r>0$, is non-pluripolar and $V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$. For $\delta >0$ and a bounded function $q$ on $K$, it is locally $(\delta ,q)$-regular at $a$ if $F_{\delta ,K_ r,q}(a)=q(a)$ for every $r>0$.

It suffices to check arbitrarily small radii, since envelopes decrease as the compact set increases. By lemma 13.1.22, $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a$ if and only if $h_{K_ r,B(a,R)}^*(a)=-1$ for all small $0<r<R$.

Lemma 13.1.24. Let $q$ be bounded and upper semicontinuous on $K$, and suppose that raw contact holds for the parameter $\delta >0$ and the obstacle $q$ on $K$, that is, $V_{\delta ,q}=q$ on $K$. If $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a$, then $F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)=q(a)$ and $K$ is locally $(\delta ,q)$-regular at $a$. These equalities also hold with $\delta $ replaced by any larger parameter.

Proof. Put $M_ r=\sup _{K_ r}q$. Raw contact, monotonicity of envelopes with respect to the compact set, and (13.3) for $K_ r$ give

\[ q(a)\leq F_{\delta ,K,q}(a) \leq F_{\delta ,K_ r,q}(a) \leq M_ r+\delta V_{K_ r}^*(a)=M_ r. \]

Since $q$ is upper semicontinuous and $a\in K_ r$, we have $M_ r\downarrow q(a)$ as $r\downarrow 0$, which gives $F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)=q(a)$.

Every compact subset $E\subseteq K$ inherits raw contact: on $E$, the raw envelope for $E$ is at least the raw envelope for $K$, which equals $q$, and at most the obstacle $q$. For fixed $s>0$, apply the same bounds with $r<s$ and $K_ s$ in place of $K$, and let $r\downarrow 0$. This proves local $(\delta ,q)$-regularity. By theorem 13.1.11, raw contact also holds for every larger parameter, and applying what has been proved to each of them gives the last assertion. $\square$

Theorem 13.1.25. Let $a\in K$, $\tau >0$ and $\Psi \in \mathcal L_\tau \cap C(\mathbb C^ n)$, and put $q=\Psi |_ K$. Suppose that, for some $R,c>0$,

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi \geq c\omega _0\quad \text{on }B(a,R), \qquad \omega _0=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\lVert z\rVert ^2. \]

For $0<r<R$ and $\delta \geq \tau $,

13.21
\begin{equation} F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)-\Psi (a) \geq cr^2\bigl (1+h_{K_ r,B(a,R)}^*(a)\bigr ). \end{equation}

Regular contact at $a$, that is, $F_{\delta ,K,q}(a)=q(a)$, holds for some $\delta \geq \tau $ if and only if $K$ is locally pluriregular at $a$. In that case it holds for every $\delta \geq \tau $.

Proof. Set $B=B(a,R)$ and $E=K_ r$. For a competitor $u$ for $h_{E,B}$, the function

\[ H_ u(z)=\Psi (z)-c\lVert z-a\rVert ^2+cr^2(u(z)+1) \]

is plurisubharmonic on $B$, because the assumption on $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi $ makes $\Psi -c\lVert z-a\rVert ^2$ plurisubharmonic there. Since $u\leq 0$, we have $H_ u\leq \Psi $ where $\lVert z-a\rVert \geq r$. Define $W=\max (\Psi ,H_ u)$ on $B$ and $W=\Psi $ outside $B$. The two definitions agree near $\partial B$, so $W$ is plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb C^ n$, and it belongs to $\mathcal L_\tau $ because it equals $\Psi $ outside $B$. On $E$, the equality $u=-1$ gives $H_ u\leq \Psi $, and on $K\setminus E$ the distance bound gives the same inequality. Thus $W=q$ on $K$, so $W$ is a competitor for $F_{\delta ,K,q}$ for each $\delta \geq \tau $. Since $W\geq H_ u$ near $a$, taking the supremum over $u$, regularizing, and using the continuity of $\Psi $, we obtain (13.21).

The function $\Psi $ itself is a competitor, so raw contact holds for every $\delta \geq \tau $. If regular contact holds for one such $\delta $, the left-hand side of the estimate vanishes, so $h_{K_ r,B(a,R)}^*(a)=-1$ for all $0<r<R$. By lemma 13.1.22, every $K_ r$ with $r<R$ is then non-pluripolar and satisfies $V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$; for $r\geq R$ this follows by monotonicity. Conversely, local pluriregularity and lemma 13.1.24 give regular contact at $a$ for every $\delta \geq \tau $. $\square$

In this regular case, $V_ K^*(a)\leq V_{K_ r}^*(a)=0$ by monotonicity, and the right derivative $D_\tau (a)$ with respect to the growth allowance, for the weight $q$, vanishes by corollary 13.1.14, since regular contact persists.

Corollary 13.1.26. Let $\tau >0$, $\Psi \in \mathcal L_\tau \cap C^2(\mathbb C^ n)$ and $a\in K$. If $F_{\tau ,K,\Psi |_ K}(a)=\Psi (a)$, but $F_{\delta ,K,\Psi |_ K}(a)>\Psi (a)$ for some $\delta >\tau $, then

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi )^ n(a)=0. \]

Proof. The complex Hessian of $\Psi $ is semipositive. If its top power did not vanish at $a$, it would be positive definite at $a$, and by continuity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi \geq c\omega _0$ on a neighborhood of $a$ for some $c>0$. Theorem 13.1.25 would then give regular contact at $a$ for every $\delta \geq \tau $, a contradiction. $\square$

A regularization defect along a complex disk

Raw contact with a constant obstacle can hold for every positive growth parameter while regularization raises the value of the envelope at a point of the compact set. In the following example, a complex disk through the point, transverse to the planar direction, prevents any local plurisubharmonic function from separating the point from the rest of the compact set, but it does not remove this defect.

In the complex plane, let $D$ be the open unit disk and put

\[ p_ j=2+2^{-j},\qquad r_ j=\exp (-16\, 2^ j),\qquad E=\overline D\cup \{ 2\} \cup \bigcup _{j\geq 1}\overline D(p_ j,r_ j). \]

Lemma 13.1.27. The set $E$ is compact, and every neighborhood of each of its points contains an open disk contained in $E$. The series

\[ h(\zeta )=\sum _{j\geq 1}2^{-j}\log |\zeta -p_ j|+2\log 2 \]

defines a subharmonic function on $\mathbb C$ of logarithmic growth at most one; it satisfies $h(2)=0$ and is at most $-16+2\log 2$ on every small disk $\overline D(p_ j,r_ j)$. The function

\[ v(\zeta )=\frac{8\log ^+|\zeta |+h(\zeta )-4\log 2}{9} \]

is subharmonic of logarithmic growth at most one, is nonpositive on $E\setminus \{ 2\} $, and satisfies $v(2)=4\log 2/9$.

Proof. Since $r_ j<2^{-j-3}$, the small disks are pairwise disjoint and disjoint from $\overline D$, and they accumulate only at $2$. This proves compactness. Every neighborhood of $2$ contains one of the small disks, and every other point of $E$ lies in the closure of the interior of one of the disks.

To show that the series defines a subharmonic function, write $\chi (\zeta )=\log (1+|\zeta |^2)$ on $\mathbb C$ and $\theta =\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}/2$ on $\mathbb P^1$. By lemma 13.1.7, each

\[ q_ j=\log |\zeta -p_ j|-\chi /2 \]

extends to a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^1$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $q_ j\leq \log 4$, while the value of $q_ j$ at infinity is zero. Thus $0\leq \sup q_ j\leq \log 4$, and theorem 1.4.2 bounds $\| q_ j-\log 4\| _{L^1}$ uniformly. The partial sums

\[ w_ N=\sum _{j=1}^ N2^{-j}(q_ j-\log 4) \]

decrease, remain $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norms. By corollary 1.4.3, their limit $w$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^1,\theta )$. Since $\sum _ j2^{-j}=1$,

\[ h=w+\chi /2+\log 4+2\log 2 \]

on the affine chart. This proves subharmonicity and the growth bound, and $\sum _ j j2^{-j}=2$ gives $h(2)=0$.

On the $j$th small disk, all distances to the other centers are less than one, so the corresponding terms are negative, and

\[ h\leq 2^{-j}\log r_ j+2\log 2=-16+2\log 2. \]

On $\overline D$ all distances to the centers are less than four, hence $h\leq 4\log 2$ and $v\leq 0$. On each small disk $|\zeta |<4$, so $9v\leq 14\log 2-16<0$. The growth bound for $v$ and its value at $2$ follow directly from the formula. $\square$

Now let $n\geq 2$ and $K=E\times \overline D^{\, n-1}\subset \mathbb C^ n$. For a non-pluripolar compact set $L\subset \mathbb C^ n$, write $V_{\delta ,L}$ and $F_{\delta ,L}$ for the raw envelope with growth allowance $\delta $ and zero obstacle on $L$, and for its regularization.

Proposition 13.1.28. For every point of $K$ and every neighborhood $W$ of it, the set $K\cap W$ is non-pluripolar. For $\delta >0$ and $a=(2,w)$ with $w\in \overline D^{\, n-1}$,

13.22
\begin{equation} V_{\delta ,K}|_ K=0,\qquad F_{\delta ,K}(a)\geq \frac{4\delta }{9}\log 2>0. \end{equation}

For every $r>0$, the same lower bound holds for $F_{\delta ,K\cap \overline B(a,r)}(a)$. At $a_0=(2,0,\ldots ,0)$, every plurisubharmonic function $f$ defined near $a_0$ satisfies

13.23
\begin{equation} f(a_0)\leq \limsup _{K\setminus \{ a_0\} \ni z\to a_0}f(z). \end{equation}

Thus $K$ is not plurithin at $a_0$, although the regularized envelope does not touch the obstacle at $a_0$ for any positive growth parameter.

Proof. By the preceding lemma, every neighborhood of a point of $K$ contains a nonempty open subset of $K$, namely a product of open disks. This proves the first assertion, which applies in particular to each $K\cap \overline B(a,r)$, $r>0$.

Put $b(\zeta )=\log |\zeta -2|-\log 3$. Since $|\zeta -2|\leq 3$ on $E$, we have $b\leq 0$ there, and $b(2)=-\infty $. For $\varepsilon >0$, the plurisubharmonic function

\[ u_\varepsilon (z)=\frac{v(z_1)+\varepsilon b(z_1)}{1+\varepsilon } \]

belongs to $\mathcal L_1$ and satisfies $u_\varepsilon \leq 0$ at every point of $K$, including the points with $z_1=2$, where it equals $-\infty $. Thus $u_\varepsilon \leq F_{1,K}$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $v(z_1)\leq F_{1,K}(z)$ off the hyperplane $\{ z_1=2\} $, and lemma 1.3.3 extends this inequality everywhere. The value $v(2)=4\log 2/9$ gives the lower bound for $\delta =1$. Since $0$ is a competitor, $V_{\delta ,K}|_ K=0$, and scaling of competitors gives $F_{\delta ,K}=\delta F_{1,K}$. Replacing $K$ by a smaller compact set only increases the envelope, which proves the local assertion.

Finally, let $f$ be plurisubharmonic near $a_0$. If $f(a_0)=-\infty $, (13.23) is automatic. Otherwise, restrict $f$ to the complex line through $a_0$ in which only the second coordinate varies. Small circles in this line centered at $a_0$ lie in $K\setminus \{ a_0\} $, and the submean inequality on these circles gives (13.23). $\square$

13.2 Bounded domination in a semipositive background

We return to the compact manifold $X$.

The domination principle for Hermitian backgrounds, theorem 10.2.2, extends to a smooth semipositive background $\theta $ provided every bounded $\theta $-plurisubharmonic potential has positive mass. For bounded potentials $u,v$ satisfying the measure inequality below, contact support bounds $\sup _ XP_\theta (bu-(b-1)v)$ from below as the stretching parameter $b>1$ increases. A fixed gap between $u$ and $v$ would then force the $L^1$ norms of these envelopes, normalized by their suprema, to diverge, contradicting compactness.

We call a smooth semipositive form $\theta $ non-collapsing on bounded potentials if

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (w)>0 \quad \hbox{for every }w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cap L^\infty (X). \]

Every Hermitian form has this property by corollary 10.2.3. Bigness also implies it, by the following envelope argument.

Proposition 13.2.1. If a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ is big, every bounded $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has strictly positive Monge–Ampère mass.

Proof. Choose $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $a>0$ with $\theta _\rho \geq a\omega $, normalize $\rho \leq 0$, and put

\[ p=P_{a\omega }(u-\rho ). \]

The obstacle is finite almost everywhere and bounded below, so lemma 1.19.2 shows that $p$ is bounded. The obstacle is quasi-continuous off the pluripolar pole set of $\rho $. The contact theorem, theorem 13.1.4, applied in the Hermitian background $a\omega $, shows that $\mathrm{MA}_{a\omega }(p)$ is carried by $D=\{ p+\rho =u\} $, outside the pole set of $\rho $.

By lemma 1.3.3, $p+\rho \leq u$ everywhere, and

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p+\rho ) =(a\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)+(\theta _\rho -a\omega ) \geq a\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p\geq 0. \]

On common finite truncation levels, positivity of the mixed products and ordered contact give

\[ \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_{a\omega }(p) \leq \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p+\rho ) \leq \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u). \]

Exhausting by these levels proves the same inequalities for the extended measures. Since $p$ is bounded, its Monge–Ampère mass with respect to the Hermitian form $a\omega $ is positive by corollary 10.2.3. Integrating the inequalities proves the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 13.2.2 (Guedj–Lu). Let $\theta \geq 0$ be non-collapsing on bounded potentials. Suppose that $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cap L^\infty (X)$ and $0\leq c<1$. If

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u) \leq c\, \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v), \]

then $u\geq v$.

Proof. If the conclusion failed, we could choose $a>0$ such that $E=\{ u<v-a\} $ is nonempty. This set has positive volume by lemma 1.3.4. Fix $C$ with $|u|,|v|\leq C$.

For $b>1$ define the bounded envelope

\[ w_ b=P_\theta \bigl (bu-(b-1)v\bigr ),\qquad D_ b=\{ w_ b=bu-(b-1)v\} . \]

Since $\theta \geq 0$, constants below the obstacle are competitors. By the bounded contact theorem, theorem 13.1.3, the measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (w_ b)$ is concentrated on $D_ b$. The potential $b^{-1}w_ b+(1-b^{-1})v$ lies below $u$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3, and it equals $u$ on $D_ b$ outside a pluripolar set. Ordered contact and positivity of the mixed terms give

\[ \mathbf1_{D_ b}\left[b^{-n}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (w_ b) +(1-b^{-1})^ n\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)\right] \leq \mathbf1_{D_ b}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u). \]

Choose $b$ so large that $(1-b^{-1})^ n>c$. Then the hypothesis forces $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (w_ b)=0$ on $D_ b\cap \{ u<v\} $. Since $\theta $ is non-collapsing, $w_ b$ has positive total mass, so some of this mass is carried by $D_ b\cap \{ u\geq v\} $. At a point of this set outside the exceptional pluripolar set,

\[ w_ b=bu-(b-1)v=u+(b-1)(u-v)\geq -C. \]

Consequently $\sup _ Xw_ b\geq -C$ for all large $b$.

On the other hand, on the fixed set $E$ the obstacle bound gives $w_ b\leq C-(b-1)a$ quasi-everywhere. Hence

\[ \int _ X|w_ b-\sup _ Xw_ b|\, \mathrm{d}V \geq \bigl ((b-1)a-2C\bigr )\operatorname {vol}_{\mathrm{d}V}(E) \]

for all sufficiently large $b$. This contradicts the uniform $L^1$ bound for normalized potentials in theorem 1.4.2. $\square$

The exponential comparison principle follows as in corollary 10.2.4. For unbounded potentials, the stretched envelopes must also be kept in the required singularity class; this is the role of the relative classes introduced below.

13.3 Upper and lower volumes

Let $\theta $ be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form. Taking the infimum and the supremum of the mass over a singularity type gives two invariants with values in $[0,+\infty ]$. They can differ, because a bounded change of potential need not preserve the mass.

Definition 13.3.1. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, define

\[ \begin{split} \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u) & =\inf _{v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\ v\sim u} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v),\\ \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u) & =\sup _{v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\ v\sim u} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v). \end{split} \]

These are the lower and upper volumes of the singularity type of $u$ in the background $\theta $. When $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is nonempty, we write $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}(\theta )$ and $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}(\theta )$ for their values at the potential $V_\theta $ with minimal singularities.

For semipositive $\theta $, the singularity type of the zero potential consists of the bounded potentials. Extending the lower mass of definition 6.5.1 to this background, we put

\[ v_-(\theta )=\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (0) =\inf _{v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cap L^\infty (X)} \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v). \]

The condition $v_-(\theta )>0$ is stronger than the non-collapsing property used in bounded domination: it requires a single positive lower bound for the masses of all bounded potentials. Singularity volumes under a bounded mass hypothesis are compared in chapter 30.

Both volumes are invariant under bounded changes of $u$, by definition. They are also invariant under the smooth change of decomposition $(\theta ,u)\mapsto (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f,u-f)$, for $f\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$: the map $v\mapsto v-f$ identifies the competitors, their currents and their measures. Scaling gives

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{t\theta }(tu) =t^ n\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u),\qquad \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{t\theta }(tu) =t^ n\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)\quad (t>0). \]

Pullback by a modification also identifies the entire ordinary singularity type. The measure identity below gives preservation of both volumes in the exact pulled background.

Proposition 13.3.2. Let $\mu :Y\to X$ be a modification of connected compact complex manifolds, let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

\[ \mu _*\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\mu ^*\theta }(u\circ \mu ) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \]

Both singularity volumes satisfy

\[ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\mu ^*\theta }(u\circ \mu ) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u),\qquad \overline{\operatorname {vol}}_{\mu ^*\theta }(u\circ \mu ) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u), \]

including infinite values.

Proof. Choose a proper analytic set $S\subset X$ such that $\mu $ is an isomorphism outside $D=\mu ^{-1}(S)$. On these complements, bounded truncations commute with composition by $\mu $, and their products commute with the biholomorphic change of coordinates. Passing to the increasing limits gives the measure identity there. By theorem 6.1.2, the two measures give no mass to $S$ and $D$, which proves the global identity and equality of total masses.

Pullback is a bijection between the potentials in the two backgrounds by proposition 1.22.12. Surjectivity of $\mu $ preserves and reflects bounded differences, so this bijection identifies the ordinary-type competitor families. Their masses agree by the first assertion. Taking their infima and suprema proves the volume identities. $\square$

The background upstairs is the exact pullback $\mu ^*\theta $, which may be degenerate even when $\theta >0$. These identities do not compare volumes for an independently chosen Hermitian form on $Y$.

13.4 Relative envelope classes

Fix a reference potential $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For $b\geq 1$, the stretched obstacle $bu-(b-1)\varphi =\varphi +b(u-\varphi )$ magnifies the difference between $u$ and $\varphi $ by the factor $b$, and so becomes more singular where $u$ lies below $\varphi $. Requiring a competitor below every finite stretch defines a relative class that is stable under convex combinations and finite rooftops.

For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $b\geq 1$, define the family of competitors

\[ \mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi )= \left\{ h\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): b^{-1}h+(1-b^{-1})\varphi \leq u\right\} . \]

For $b=1$ the inequality means $h\leq u$. Away from the poles of $u$ and $\varphi $, the inequality means $h\leq bu-(b-1)\varphi $. The convex combination on the left is defined everywhere, so the definition does not require a value of $bu-(b-1)\varphi $ at common poles. When $\mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi )$ is nonempty, we write

\[ Q_ b(u,\varphi )=P_\theta (bu-(b-1)\varphi ) \]

for its upper envelope. Since the obstacle is finite almost everywhere, the competitors have a common upper bound by section 1.19, so $Q_ b(u,\varphi )$ is then a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function; in particular, it is not identically $-\infty $. The upper-envelope theorem gives its defining inequality almost everywhere, and lemma 1.3.3 makes it pointwise; thus $Q_ b(u,\varphi )$ is the largest member of $\mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi )$.

Definition 13.4.1. The relative envelope class of $\varphi $ is

\[ \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ) =\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\preceq \varphi , \ \mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi )\ne \varnothing \text{ for every }b\geq 1\} . \]

When the background $\theta $ is fixed, we abbreviate $\mathcal E_\varphi =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi )$.

Proposition 13.4.2. The class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi )$ is convex, contains all potentials of the singularity type of $\varphi $, and is stable under bounded changes of its members. If $u$ belongs to it, $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $u\leq v$ and $v\preceq \varphi $, then $v$ belongs to it. In particular, it is stable under maxima of two members. Replacing $\varphi $ by another member of $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ of the same singularity type does not change the class. For every smooth real function $f$,

\[ u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad u-f\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f,\varphi -f). \]

Proof. If $u\geq \varphi -C$, then $h=\varphi -bC$ belongs to $\mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi )$ for each $b$; hence every $u\sim \varphi $ belongs to the class. More generally, if $u'\geq u-C$ and $h\in \mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi )$, then $h-bC\in \mathcal A_ b(u',\varphi )$. Since the condition $u\preceq \varphi $ is also unchanged by bounded changes of $u$, the class is stable under such changes. A convex combination of two competitors is a competitor for the same convex combination of the two potentials, which proves convexity. Finally, a competitor for $u$ is a competitor for any larger $v$, and the assumption $v\preceq \varphi $ gives the remaining condition.

If $\varphi '\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi '\sim \varphi $, choose $C\geq 0$ with $|\varphi '-\varphi |\leq C$ off their common pole set. For $h\in \mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi )$, the function $h-(b-1)C$ belongs to $\mathcal A_ b(u,\varphi ')$: the required inequality holds off the poles, and it extends everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. The reverse implication follows by interchanging the references, and $u\preceq \varphi $ is equivalent to $u\preceq \varphi '$.

For the last assertion, note that $h\mapsto h-f$ is a bijection from $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ onto $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)$. It preserves the order $\preceq $ and transforms each stretching inequality into

\[ b^{-1}(h-f)+(1-b^{-1})(\varphi -f)\leq u-f. \]

This identity also holds at poles, because $f$ is smooth and finite. Thus the corresponding families of competitors are in bijection for every $b\geq 1$. $\square$

Lemma 13.4.3. If $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $u\leq \varphi $, then $Q_ b(u,\varphi )\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ for every $b\geq 1$. More generally, if $u,w\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $u\leq w$, then $Q_ b(u,w)\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

Proof. Fix $b,s\geq 1$ and put $h=Q_{bs}(u,\varphi )$. Its defining inequality gives, off the pole sets,

\[ s^{-1}h+(1-s^{-1})\varphi \leq bu-(b-1)\varphi . \]

The left-hand side is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, so it is at most $Q_ b(u,\varphi )$. Hence $h\in \mathcal A_ s(Q_ b(u,\varphi ),\varphi )$ for every $s$. Also $Q_ b(u,\varphi )\leq u\leq \varphi $, so $Q_ b(u,\varphi )\preceq \varphi $. The inequalities between convex combinations extend across the poles by lemma 1.3.3, which proves the first assertion.

For the second assertion, choose a constant $C$ such that $u-C\leq \varphi $ and $w-C\leq \varphi $. Since $Q_ b(u-C,w-C)=Q_ b(u,w)-C$ and class membership is unchanged by translation, we may assume $u,w\leq \varphi $. Because $u\leq w\leq \varphi $, the obstacle for $Q_ b(u,w)$ lies above that for $Q_ b(u,\varphi )$ and below $u$. Thus

\[ Q_ b(u,\varphi )\leq Q_ b(u,w)\leq u\leq \varphi . \]

Since $Q_ b(u,\varphi )\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ by the first assertion, the stability under increase proved in proposition 13.4.2 gives the conclusion. $\square$

Proposition 13.4.4. If $u_1,\ldots ,u_ N\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $N\geq 1$, then the rooftop envelope $P_\theta (\min _ iu_ i)$ is not identically $-\infty $ and belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $.

Proof. Adding a common constant to all $u_ i$, we may assume $u_ i\leq \varphi $. By lemma 13.4.3, proposition 13.4.2,

\[ h=\frac1N\sum _{i=1}^ N Q_ N(u_ i,\varphi )\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

Each summand is at most $\varphi $. For every $i$, the defining inequality of $Q_ N(u_ i,\varphi )$ therefore gives

\[ h\leq N^{-1}Q_ N(u_ i,\varphi )+(1-N^{-1})\varphi \leq u_ i. \]

These inequalities between convex combinations also hold at common poles. Thus $h$ is a competitor for $p=P_\theta (\min _ i u_ i)$, so $p$ is not identically $-\infty $, and $h\leq p\leq u_1\preceq \varphi $. Stability under increase gives $p\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, and undoing the translation preserves this conclusion. $\square$

The finiteness of the family is essential: every $\varphi -j$ belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $, whereas $P_\theta (\inf _{j\geq 1}(\varphi -j))\equiv -\infty $. The constructions of common envelopes for sequences in chapter 31 require additional estimates.

Proposition 13.4.5. If $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, then for every smooth real function $F$,

\[ P_{\theta ,F}[u]=P_{\theta ,F}[\varphi ]. \]

In particular, $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [\varphi ]$.

Proof. The relation $u\preceq \varphi $ gives $P_{\theta ,F}[u]\leq P_{\theta ,F}[\varphi ]$. For the reverse inequality, let $a\in (0,1)$ and choose $h_ a\in \mathcal A_{1/(1-a)}(u,\varphi )$, so that $a\varphi +(1-a)h_ a\leq u$. Combining competitors below $F$ gives

\[ aP_{\theta ,F}[\varphi ]+(1-a)P_{\theta ,F}[h_ a] \leq P_{\theta ,F}[u]. \]

Indeed, if $g_1$ and $g_2$ are competitors for $P_{\theta ,F}[\varphi ]$ and $P_{\theta ,F}[h_ a]$, then $ag_1+(1-a)g_2$ lies below $F$ and is at least as singular as $a\varphi +(1-a)h_ a\leq u$; taking the two upper envelopes preserves the inequality, by local regularization. Write $m=\min _ XF$ and $M=\max _ XF$. Translating competitors gives

\[ P_\theta [h_ a]+m\leq P_{\theta ,F}[h_ a]\leq P_\theta [h_ a]+M. \]

Every $P_\theta [h_ a]$ has supremum zero, since it lies above $h_ a-\sup _ Xh_ a$ and below zero. The uniform $L^1$ bound for normalized potentials in theorem 1.4.2 therefore bounds the $L^1$ norms of the $P_{\theta ,F}[h_ a]$ uniformly in $a$. Letting $a\uparrow 1$ in the first inequality gives $P_{\theta ,F}[\varphi ]\leq P_{\theta ,F}[u]$ almost everywhere, and by lemma 1.3.3 the inequality holds everywhere. $\square$

13.5 Model references and positive mass

In this section $\theta $ is semipositive and big. Suppose that a potential $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has a competitor in a smaller background $a\theta $, $0<a<1$, that is, $P_{a\theta }(u)\not\equiv -\infty $. Then the big form $(1-a)\theta $ remains available: an envelope in this background has positive mass, and the contact property transfers this mass to $u$. To obtain such competitors for all members of a relative class, we use a reference $\varphi $ satisfying

13.26
\begin{equation} P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi ,\qquad P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty \quad \text{for some }0<\varepsilon <1. \end{equation}

We call such a $\varphi $ a model reference and fix one such $\varepsilon $. Note that $\varphi =P_\theta [\varphi ]\leq 0$. For $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, the envelope of $u-\varphi $ belongs to the relative envelope class $\mathcal E_0=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,0)$ of the zero potential, and stretching realizes it in an arbitrarily small background. Under a bounded mass hypothesis, chapter 30 uses instead the condition (30.10), in which a positive lower volume replaces the condition on the smaller background; the two conditions are compared there.

Lemma 13.5.1. Let $\varphi $ be a model reference. If $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $u\leq \varphi $, then

\[ w=P_\theta (u-\varphi )\in \mathcal E_0, \qquad w\leq 0, \qquad w+\varphi \leq u. \]

Proof. Since $\varphi \leq 0$, the potential $u$ lies below the obstacle $u-\varphi $ away from the pole set of $\varphi $, so it is a competitor. Hence the envelope $w$ is not identically $-\infty $. It is at most zero, since $u-\varphi \leq 0$, and it satisfies $w+\varphi \leq u$ off the poles, hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. For $b\geq 1$, let $h_ b=Q_ b(u,\varphi )$. Then, off the poles,

\[ h_ b\leq bu-(b-1)\varphi \leq b(u-\varphi ). \]

Since $\theta \geq 0$, the function $b^{-1}h_ b$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and so it is a competitor for $w$. Hence $h_ b\leq bw$, so $\mathcal A_ b(w,0)$ is nonempty for every $b$. Since also $w\leq 0$, this means exactly that $w\in \mathcal E_0$. $\square$

Lemma 13.5.2. Let $0<a<1$ and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. If $q=P_{a\theta }(u)$ is not identically $-\infty $, then

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\geq (1-a)^ n v_-(\theta ), \]

and the mass on the left is strictly positive, even when $v_-(\theta )=0$.

Proof. The obstacle $u-q$ is nonnegative away from the poles, so by lemma 1.19.2 the envelope $p=P_{(1-a)\theta }(u-q)$ is bounded. By the contact theorem, its measure is carried by $D=\{ p+q=u\} $, and $p+q\leq u$ everywhere. Since $p+q$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, positivity of mixed products and ordered contact imply

\[ \mathrm{MA}_{(1-a)\theta }(p) \leq \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p+q) \leq \mathbf1_ D\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u), \]

where all assertions are first restricted to the complement of the poles. Writing $p=(1-a)\widetilde p$ with $\widetilde p\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cap L^\infty (X)$ and integrating, we obtain the lower bound from the definition of $v_-(\theta )$. Finally, the bounded potential $p$ has strictly positive mass by proposition 13.2.1, since $(1-a)\theta $ is big. $\square$

Theorem 13.5.3. Let $\varphi $ be a model reference, with $\varepsilon $ as in (13.26). For every $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and every $0<\delta <1-\varepsilon $, the envelope $P_{(\varepsilon +\delta )\theta }(u)$ is not identically $-\infty $. Consequently,

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\geq (1-\varepsilon )^ n v_-(\theta ), \qquad \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)>0. \]

The mass may be infinite.

Proof. After adding a constant to $u$, we may assume $u\leq \varphi $. Let $w\in \mathcal E_0$ be as in lemma 13.5.1. Since $w\in \mathcal E_0$, the envelope $P_\theta (w/\delta )$ is not identically $-\infty $, and $\delta P_\theta (w/\delta )\leq w$. The potential

\[ P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )+\delta P_\theta (w/\delta ) \leq \varphi +w\leq u \]

belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,(\varepsilon +\delta )\theta )$, so it is a competitor for $P_{(\varepsilon +\delta )\theta }(u)$. Applying lemma 13.5.2 with $a=\varepsilon +\delta $ and letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ gives the uniform estimate. The positivity follows from the same lemma with any one such $\delta $. $\square$

13.6 Infinite mass inside the envelope class

We now take the Hermitian background $\theta =\omega $ and the reference $\varphi =0$. If $\omega $ does not have the bounded mass property, that is, if the upper mass $v_+(\omega )$ of definition 6.5.1 is infinite, then the series constructed in theorem 6.6.1 has infinite mass. It splits into a smooth finite sum and a tail whose curvature is bounded below by an arbitrarily small multiple of $-\omega $. Multiplying a sufficiently late tail by the stretching factor gives a competitor for that stretch, so the series lies in the relative envelope class of zero.

Proposition 13.6.1. If $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $, the potential $U$ in theorem 6.6.1 can be chosen so that, in addition,

\[ U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega ,0),\qquad P_\omega [U]=0. \]

The same conclusions hold for $\varepsilon U$, $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$. Each of these potentials has infinite extended mass and trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight.

Proof. Use the smooth nonpositive $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u_ j$, $j\geq 1$, of theorem 6.6.1 and their convergent series $U=\sum _{j\geq 1}2^{-j}u_ j$. For each integer $N\geq 1$, write

\[ U=A_ N+V_ N,\qquad A_ N=\sum _{j\leq N}2^{-j}u_ j,\qquad V_ N=\sum _{j>N}2^{-j}u_ j,\qquad \delta _ N=2^{-N}. \]

The head $A_ N$ is smooth, and since the finite partial sums of the tail decrease, $V_ N\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\delta _ N\omega )$; the tail is not identically $-\infty $, because $U=A_ N+V_ N$ is not.

Fix $b>1$ and choose $N$ so large that $b\delta _ N\leq 1$. Then

\[ h_ b=bV_ N+\min _ X(bA_ N) \]

is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, because $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ b\geq -b\delta _ N\omega \geq -\omega $. Moreover, $h_ b\leq bU$. Thus $h_ b\in \mathcal A_ b(U,0)$ for every $b$, and since $U\leq 0$, this proves $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega ,0)$. For $\varepsilon U$, use the tails $\varepsilon V_ N$, and choose $N$ with $b\varepsilon \delta _ N\leq 1$. By proposition 13.4.5, the singularity envelope of each of these potentials is $P_\omega [0]=0$. Their infinite mass and trivial multiplier ideals are given by corollary 6.6.2. $\square$

The bound on the mass in proposition 13.1.6 concerns the envelope fixed point itself. This example shows that potentials with the same singularity envelope as a fixed point, even with trivial multiplier ideals at every positive weight, need not have finite mass.

Corollary 13.6.2. The following conditions on a Hermitian metric $\omega $ are equivalent:

  1. $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $;

  2. every member of $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega ,0)$ has finite extended mass;

  3. every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $P_\omega [u]=0$ has finite extended mass.

Proof. If $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $, all $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions have finite extended mass by proposition 6.5.3. If $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $, proposition 13.6.1 contradicts each of the other two conditions. $\square$
