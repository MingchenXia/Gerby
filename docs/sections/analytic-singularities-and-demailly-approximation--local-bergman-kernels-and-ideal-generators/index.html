6.15 Local Bergman kernels and ideal generators9C2A6446C90A

9C2A6446C90A

In this section $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, is a bounded pseudoconvex open set, $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(\Omega )$, and integrals are taken with respect to Lebesgue measure unless another measure is specified. For an integer $m\geq 1$ we put

\[ H_ m=\Bigl \{ F\in \mathcal O(\Omega ):\| F\| _ m^2 =\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}<\infty \Bigr \} ,\qquad K_ m(z)=\sup _{\| F\| _ m\leq 1}|F(z)|^2. \]

Thus $H_ m$ is the space $\mathcal H_{mu}(\Omega )$ of theorem 6.8.9, for the complex manifold $\Omega $ and the weight $mu$, and the function $K_ m$ is the Bergman kernel of $H_ m$ on the diagonal.

We need that $H_ m$ is a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions that generates the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(mu)$, with bounded coefficients near each point. These facts were proved in section 6.6 on bounded pseudoconvex open sets, also for measures $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ comparable with Lebesgue measure (theorem 6.8.9, lemma 6.8.10), and the next lemma restates them in the notation of this section. Throughout this section, $\varrho $ denotes a measurable function on $\Omega $ with $c_\varrho \leq \varrho \leq C_\varrho $ for positive constants $c_\varrho $ and $C_\varrho $. Such measures arise in section 6.16, where the kernels are computed with the volume form of a Kähler form.

Lemma 6.15.1.C2732E72E566 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, be a bounded pseudoconvex open set, let $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(\Omega )$, and let $\varrho $ be a measurable function on $\Omega $ with $c_\varrho \leq \varrho \leq C_\varrho $ for positive constants $c_\varrho $ and $C_\varrho $. Put

\[ \mathcal H=\Bigl \{ F\in \mathcal O(\Omega ):\| F\| ^2=\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi }\varrho \, \mathrm dV<\infty \Bigr \} ,\qquad \langle F,G\rangle =\int _\Omega F\bar G\, \mathrm e^{-\psi }\varrho \, \mathrm dV. \]

  1. For every compact set $K\subset \Omega $ there is a constant $C_ K$ such that $|F(z)|\leq C_ K\| F\| $ for all $F\in \mathcal H$ and $z\in K$. The space $\mathcal H$ is a separable Hilbert space, and it has an orthonormal basis, that is, an orthonormal family $(e_\ell )$, finite or countable, whose linear span is dense. For every such family and every $F\in \mathcal H$, the partial sums of $\sum _\ell \langle F,e_\ell \rangle e_\ell $ converge to $F$ in norm and locally uniformly on $\Omega $, and $\sum _\ell |\langle F,e_\ell \rangle |^2=\| F\| ^2$.

  2. The germs of the elements of $\mathcal H$ generate $\mathcal I(\psi )$ at every point of $\Omega $, and near each point finitely many elements of $\mathcal H$ already generate $\mathcal I(\psi )$.

  3. Let $x\in \Omega $, and let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s\in \mathcal H$ generate $\mathcal I(\psi )$ on a neighborhood of $x$. Then there are a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ and a constant $C$ such that every $F\in \mathcal H$ can be written as $F=\sum _ ig_ ih_ i$ on $V$ with $h_ i\in \mathcal O(V)$ and $\sup _ V|h_ i|\leq C\| F\| $.

Proof. We regard $\Omega $ as a complex manifold, with the single chart given by the identity map. Then $\psi $ is a weight on $\Omega $ in the sense of section 6.8, the set $\Omega $ satisfies the hypotheses of theorem 6.8.9, and $\mathcal H$ is the space $\mathcal H_\psi (\Omega ,\varrho )$ of that theorem, with the same inner product. Hence (1) is contained in theorem 6.8.9(1) and (2), and (2) follows from theorem 6.8.9(3), because an initial segment of an enumerated orthonormal basis is a finite family of elements of $\mathcal H$. Finally, (3) is the first assertion of lemma 6.8.10, applied to the weight $\psi $ on $\Omega $. $\square$

By lemma 6.15.1, applied with $\psi =mu$ and $\varrho =1$, the space $H_ m$ is a Hilbert space on which evaluation at each point is a bounded linear functional; in particular $K_ m$ is finite. Let $(e_\ell )$ be an orthonormal basis of $H_ m$. Then

2D48BBAB29E1 6.15.2
\begin{equation} K_ m=\sum _\ell |e_\ell |^2. \end{equation}

Indeed, let $F$ lie in the unit ball of $H_ m$ and put $c_\ell =\langle F,e_\ell \rangle $. By lemma 6.15.1(1), $F(z)=\sum _\ell c_\ell e_\ell (z)$ and $\sum _\ell |c_\ell |^2=\| F\| _ m^2\leq 1$, so the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $|F(z)|^2\leq \sum _\ell |e_\ell (z)|^2$. Conversely, for each $N$ the function $F_ N=\sum _{\ell \leq N}\overline{e_\ell (z)}e_\ell $ satisfies $F_ N(z)=\| F_ N\| _ m^2=\sum _{\ell \leq N}|e_\ell (z)|^2$; if this number is positive, then $F_ N/\| F_ N\| _ m$ lies in the unit ball, and $\sum _{\ell \leq N}|e_\ell (z)|^2=|F_ N(z)|^2/\| F_ N\| _ m^2\leq K_ m(z)$. The same argument applies to the kernel of the space defined with $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ in place of Lebesgue measure, for an orthonormal basis of that space.

Lemma 6.15.3.09CBB2B6BA0F The function $K_ m$ is real analytic on $\Omega $. Near each point of $\Omega $ it is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of $\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$, where $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s$ are finitely many generators of $\mathcal I(mu)$ near that point. If $\pi \colon Y\to \Omega $ is a holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$ such that $\pi ^*\mathcal I(mu)=\mathcal O_ Y(-D)$ for an effective divisor $D$ on $Y$, for instance a log resolution of $\mathcal I(mu)$, then locally on $Y$

\[ K_ m\circ \pi =|s_ D|^2b_ m,\qquad b_ m\text{ smooth and strictly positive}, \]

where $s_ D$ is a local equation of $D$. The same conclusions hold if Lebesgue measure is replaced by $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ in the definition of $H_ m$.

Proof. We give the proof for the measure $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$; Lebesgue measure is the case $\varrho =1$. By lemma 6.15.1, applied with $\psi =mu$, the space $H_ m$ is a Hilbert space with an orthonormal basis $(e_\ell )$, evaluation is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of $\Omega $, and (6.15.2) holds.

We show that the map $z\mapsto (e_\ell (z))_\ell $ into $\ell ^2$ is given locally by a convergent power series with coefficients in $\ell ^2$. Fix a point $z_0$ and a polydisc $P$ of multiradius $\rho $ about $z_0$ with $\overline P\subset \Omega $, and let $C$ be the evaluation bound on $\overline P$. For a finitely supported sequence $(c_\ell )$ with $\sum _\ell |c_\ell |^2\leq 1$, the function $F=\sum _\ell c_\ell e_\ell $ has norm at most one, so $|F|\leq C$ on $P$, and the Cauchy estimates give $|\partial ^\alpha F(z_0)/\alpha !|\leq C\rho ^{-\alpha }$. Since $\partial ^\alpha F(z_0)/\alpha !=\sum _\ell c_\ell A_{\alpha ,\ell }$ with $A_{\alpha ,\ell }=\partial ^\alpha e_\ell (z_0)/\alpha !$, taking the supremum over such sequences shows that the sequence $A_\alpha =(A_{\alpha ,\ell })_\ell $ has $\ell ^2$ norm $\| A_\alpha \| \leq C\rho ^{-\alpha }$. Let $0<\lambda <1$, and let $P_\lambda $ be the concentric polydisc of multiradius $\lambda \rho $. For $z\in P_\lambda $ we have $\sum _\alpha \| A_\alpha \| |(z-z_0)^\alpha |\leq C\sum _\alpha \lambda ^{|\alpha |}<\infty $, so the $\ell ^2$-valued series $\sum _\alpha A_\alpha (z-z_0)^\alpha $ converges absolutely, and its $\ell $th component is the Taylor series of $e_\ell $, which converges to $e_\ell (z)$. By (6.15.2),

\[ K_ m(z)=\Bigl \| \sum _\alpha A_\alpha (z-z_0)^\alpha \Bigr \| ^2=\sum _{\alpha ,\beta }\langle A_\alpha ,A_\beta \rangle (z-z_0)^\alpha \overline{(z-z_0)^\beta },\qquad z\in P_\lambda , \]

where $|\langle A_\alpha ,A_\beta \rangle |\leq C^2\rho ^{-\alpha -\beta }$. Hence this double power series, and each series obtained from it by differentiating term by term, converge uniformly on $P_{\lambda '}$ for every $\lambda '<\lambda $, since a derivative of order $k$ only introduces factors bounded by a polynomial of degree $k$ in $|\alpha |+|\beta |$. Therefore $K_ m$ is real analytic, in particular smooth.

Next choose members $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s$ of $H_ m$ generating $\mathcal I(mu)$ near a given point, by lemma 6.15.1(2); by the remark following definition 6.14.5, any other finite system of generators gives a comparable sum of squares. By the definition of $K_ m$, $|g_ i|^2\leq \| g_ i\| _ m^2K_ m$, so $\sum _ i|g_ i|^2\leq \bigl (\sum _ i\| g_ i\| _ m^2\bigr )K_ m$. Conversely, lemma 6.15.1(3) gives, on a fixed smaller neighborhood, a representation $F=\sum _ ig_ ih_ i$ with $|h_ i|\leq C'$ for every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_ m$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $|F|^2\leq sC'^2\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$, and hence $K_ m\leq sC'^2\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$ there. These constants may depend on $m$, $u$ and $\varrho $.

Let $\pi $ and $D$ be as in the statement, let $y\in Y$, let $V$ be a neighborhood of $\pi (y)$ on which the bounded representation of the previous paragraph holds, and let $s_ D$ be a local equation of $D$ on a neighborhood $W\subset \pi ^{-1}(V)$ of $y$. Since $\pi ^*\mathcal I(mu)$ is generated by $s_ D$ on $W$, we may write $g_ i\circ \pi =s_ Dk_ i$ and $s_ D=\sum _ il_ i\, (g_ i\circ \pi )$ with holomorphic $k_ i$ and $l_ i$, after shrinking $W$; since $s_ D$ does not vanish identically on any open set, $\sum _ il_ ik_ i=1$. For $F\in H_ m$, the representation $F=\sum _ ig_ ih_ i$ on $V$ gives $F\circ \pi =s_ D\sum _ i(h_ i\circ \pi )k_ i$ on $W$, so

\[ \frac{F\circ \pi }{s_ D}=\sum _ i(h_ i\circ \pi )\, k_ i,\qquad \Bigl |\frac{F\circ \pi }{s_ D}\Bigr |\leq C'\| F\| _ m\sum _ i|k_ i|. \]

Hence evaluation of $(F\circ \pi )/s_ D$ is bounded on each compact subset of $W$, uniformly for $F$ in the unit ball of $H_ m$. The power-series argument above therefore applies, in local coordinates on $Y$, to the holomorphic functions $(e_\ell \circ \pi )/s_ D$, and shows that $b_ m=\sum _\ell |(e_\ell \circ \pi )/s_ D|^2$ is smooth; by (6.15.2), $K_ m\circ \pi =|s_ D|^2b_ m$.

To see that $b_ m$ is strictly positive, let $F$ lie in the unit ball of $H_ m$, with coefficients $c_\ell =\langle F,e_\ell \rangle $ and partial sums $S_ N$. The estimate in the last display, applied to $F-S_ N$, shows that $(S_ N\circ \pi )/s_ D\to (F\circ \pi )/s_ D$ pointwise on $W$; hence $(F\circ \pi )/s_ D=\sum _\ell c_\ell (e_\ell \circ \pi )/s_ D$, and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $|(F\circ \pi )/s_ D|^2\leq b_ m$. Taking $F=g_ i/\| g_ i\| _ m$ for the indices with $g_ i\neq 0$, we obtain $b_ m\geq |k_ i|^2/\| g_ i\| _ m^2$, and the functions $k_ i$ have no common zero. $\square$

Theorem 6.15.4.5AEABFD276A6 Let $\Omega $ have diameter at most $L$, and put $u_ m=m^{-1}\log K_ m$. Then $u_ m$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega $ and has analytic singularities of type $(m^{-1},\mathcal I(mu))$. There are constants $C_1,C_2>0$, depending only on $n$ and $L$, such that

CDB3DBD2FFAA 6.15.5
\begin{equation} u(z)-\frac{\log C_1}{m}\leq u_ m(z) \leq \sup _{B(z,r)}u+\frac1m\log \frac{C_2}{r^{2n}} \end{equation}

for all $z\in \Omega $ and $r>0$ with $B(z,r)\Subset \Omega $. As $m\to \infty $, $u_ m\to u$ pointwise and in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega )$. For every $z\in \Omega $,

CD15DA06F0BB 6.15.6
\begin{equation} \nu (u,z)-\frac nm\leq \nu (u_ m,z)\leq \nu (u,z). \end{equation}

Finally, for each fixed $m$, the function $\exp (m(u_ m-u))$ is locally integrable on $\Omega $. All these assertions remain true if Lebesgue measure is replaced by $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$ in the definition of $H_ m$, provided $C_1$ and $C_2$ are replaced by $C_\varrho C_1$ and $C_2/c_\varrho $.

Proof. Let $\Omega _0$ be a connected component of $\Omega $, and choose $a\in \Omega _0$ with $u(a)>-\infty $. By corollary 6.5.8, applied on $\Omega $ to the weight $mu$, some element of $H_ m$ takes the value $1$ at $a$, so $H_ m$ contains a function that does not vanish identically on $\Omega _0$. For every $F$ in the unit ball of $H_ m$ that is not identically zero on $\Omega _0$, $\log |F|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega _0$ by proposition 5.10.3, and $\log K_ m$ is the supremum of these functions on $\Omega _0$. Since $K_ m$ is continuous by lemma 6.15.3, this family is locally uniformly bounded above, and $\log K_ m$ is upper semicontinuous with values in $[-\infty ,\infty )$, so it equals its upper semicontinuous regularization. Hence theorem 5.10.2 shows that $u_ m$ is plurisubharmonic. The comparison in lemma 6.15.3 gives $u_ m=m^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ locally, with generators $g_ i$ of $\mathcal I(mu)$; this is the asserted type.

For the lower bound in (6.15.5), let $u(z)>-\infty $; at points of the pole set of $u$ there is nothing to prove. By corollary 6.5.8, applied on $\Omega $ to the weight $mu$, there is $F\in H_ m$ with $F(z)=1$ and $\| F\| _ m^2\leq C_1\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}$, where $C_1$ depends only on $n$ and $L$. Hence $K_ m(z)\geq |F(z)|^2/\| F\| _ m^2\geq C_1^{-1}\mathrm e^{mu(z)}$. For the upper bound, let $\| F\| _ m\leq 1$. The mean-value inequality (6.8.7) of lemma 6.8.6 for $|F|^2$ on $B(z,r)$, where $|B(z,r)|=\pi ^ nr^{2n}/n!$, gives

\begin{align*} |F(z)|^2& \leq C_2r^{-2n}\int _{B(z,r)}|F|^2\\ & \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp \Bigl (m\sup _{B(z,r)}u\Bigr )\int _{B(z,r)}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-mu} \leq C_2r^{-2n}\exp \Bigl (m\sup _{B(z,r)}u\Bigr ), \end{align*}

with $C_2=n!/\pi ^ n$, and taking the supremum over $F$ proves the upper bound.

Now fix $z$ and $r$, and let $m\to \infty $ in (6.15.5): we obtain $u(z)\leq \varliminf _ mu_ m(z)\leq \varlimsup _ mu_ m(z)\leq \sup _{B(z,r)}u$. Letting $r\downarrow 0$ and using the upper semicontinuity of $u$ gives $u_ m(z)\to u(z)$, also at points of the pole set. On a compact set $K\subset \Omega $, a fixed $r<\operatorname {dist}(K,\partial \Omega )$ in the upper bound gives a uniform upper bound for all $u_ m$, while $u_ m\geq u-|\log C_1|$. Since $u\in L^1(K)$, dominated convergence gives $u_ m\to u$ in $L^1(K)$.

For the Lelong numbers, let $g_1,\ldots ,g_ s$ generate $\mathcal I(mu)$ near $z$. The stalk $\mathcal I(mu)_ z$ is not zero: by the first paragraph, applied to the component of $\Omega $ containing $z$, it contains the germ of an element of $H_ m$ that does not vanish identically on that component, and this germ is not zero by the identity theorem. Since $u_ m=m^{-1}\log \sum _ i|g_ i|^2+O(1)$ near $z$, lemma 6.14.7 gives $\nu (u_ m,z)=m^{-1}\operatorname {ord}_ z\mathcal I(mu)$, and (6.15.6) follows from lemma 6.9.1.

Finally, near each point $K_ m\leq C\sum _ i|g_ i|^2$ with $g_ i\in H_ m$, by lemma 6.15.3, and each $|g_ i|^2\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is integrable on $\Omega $. Hence $\exp (m(u_ m-u))=K_ m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable.

Now replace Lebesgue measure by $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$, and let $K^\varrho _ m$ be the corresponding kernel. The space of holomorphic functions is the same, and the two norms satisfy $c_\varrho \| F\| _ m^2\leq \| F\| _{m,\varrho }^2\leq C_\varrho \| F\| _ m^2$. If $\| F\| _ m\leq 1$, then $F/C_\varrho ^{1/2}$ lies in the unit ball for $\varrho $, and if $\| F\| _{m,\varrho }\leq 1$, then $c_\varrho ^{1/2}F$ lies in the unit ball for Lebesgue measure; hence

\[ C_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m\leq K^\varrho _ m\leq c_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m. \]

The proof of plurisubharmonicity applies verbatim to $m^{-1}\log K^\varrho _ m$, with the unit ball taken for $\varrho $ and the continuity of $K^\varrho _ m$ given by lemma 6.15.3. The lower and upper bounds follow from the last display, with the constants $C_\varrho C_1$ and $C_2/c_\varrho $. The convergence and the Lelong numbers then follow as before, since lemma 6.15.3 applies to $K^\varrho _ m$. Finally, $K^\varrho _ m\mathrm e^{-mu}\leq c_\varrho ^{-1}K_ m\mathrm e^{-mu}$ is locally integrable. $\square$

The kernels at the scales $m$ and $2m$ are compared by extending a function from the diagonal of $\Omega \times \Omega $.

Lemma 6.15.7.365648DE5857 Suppose that the spaces $H_ m$ are defined with respect to $\varrho \, \mathrm dV$; for instance $\varrho =1$. There is a constant $C_\Omega >0$, depending only on $n$, on a bound $L$ for the diameter of $\Omega $ and on $c_\varrho $ and $C_\varrho $, and in particular independent of $m$ and $u$, such that

\[ K_{2m}\leq C_\Omega K_ m^2\qquad \text{on }\Omega . \]

Proof. We write $\| \cdot \| _ m$ for the norm of $H_ m$. By the definition of $K_ m$, every $h\in H_ m$ satisfies $|h(\zeta )|^2\leq \| h\| _ m^2K_ m(\zeta )$ for all $\zeta \in \Omega $; if $\| h\| _ m=0$, then $h$ vanishes almost everywhere, hence identically.

Let $F\in H_{2m}$. We first extend $F$ from the diagonal to $\Omega \times \Omega $. By corollary 6.3.5, $\Omega \times \Omega $ is a bounded pseudoconvex open set in $\mathbb C^{2n}$, of diameter at most $\sqrt2L$, and the function $p(z,w)=mu(z)+mu(w)$ is plurisubharmonic on it (proposition 5.9.3, proposition 5.9.5). The diagonal $\Lambda =\{ (z,w)\in \mathbb C^{2n}:z=w\} $ is a complex linear subspace of codimension $n$, and the map $z\mapsto (z,z)$ multiplies Euclidean lengths by $\sqrt2$, so Lebesgue measure on $\Lambda $ corresponds to $2^ n$ times Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb C^ n$. On $\Lambda \cap (\Omega \times \Omega )$, identified with $\Omega $, the weight $p$ restricts to $2mu$, so

\[ \int _{\Lambda \cap (\Omega \times \Omega )}|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\, \mathrm dV_{2n}=2^ n\int _\Omega |F|^2\mathrm e^{-2mu}\, \mathrm dV\leq 2^ nc_\varrho ^{-1}\| F\| _{2m}^2. \]

The extension statement of corollary 6.5.8, applied on $\Omega \times \Omega $ to the weight $p$ and the subspace $\Lambda $, gives $G\in \mathcal O(\Omega \times \Omega )$ with $G(z,z)=F(z)$ and $\int _{\Omega \times \Omega }|G|^2\mathrm e^{-p}\, \mathrm dV_{4n}\leq C^ n(\sqrt2L)^{2n}2^ nc_\varrho ^{-1}\| F\| _{2m}^2$, where $C$ is the constant of theorem 6.5.4. Hence

\[ \| G\| ^2=\int _{\Omega \times \Omega }|G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(z)-mu(w)}\varrho (z)\varrho (w)\, \mathrm dV(z)\, \mathrm dV(w)\leq C_\Omega \| F\| _{2m}^2 \]

with $C_\Omega =4^ nC^ nL^{2n}C_\varrho ^2/c_\varrho $.

We now bound $G$ on the diagonal by $K_ m$. For $w\in \Omega $ put

\[ N(w)=\int _\Omega |G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(z)}\varrho (z)\, \mathrm dV(z)\in [0,\infty ]. \]

By Tonelli's theorem, $N$ is measurable and $\int _\Omega N(w)\mathrm e^{-mu(w)}\varrho (w)\, \mathrm dV(w)=\| G\| ^2<\infty $; since $\mathrm e^{-mu}\varrho >0$ almost everywhere, $N(w)<\infty $ for almost every $w$. For such $w$, the function $G(\cdot ,w)$ belongs to $H_ m$ with $\| G(\cdot ,w)\| _ m^2=N(w)$, so $|G(z,w)|^2\leq N(w)K_ m(z)$ for every $z$. Fix $z\in \Omega $. Integrating this inequality in $w$, we obtain

\[ \int _\Omega |G(z,w)|^2\mathrm e^{-mu(w)}\varrho (w)\, \mathrm dV(w)\leq K_ m(z)\| G\| ^2, \]

so $G(z,\cdot )\in H_ m$ with $\| G(z,\cdot )\| _ m^2\leq K_ m(z)\| G\| ^2$, and therefore $|G(z,w)|^2\leq K_ m(z)K_ m(w)\| G\| ^2$ for every $w$. Taking $w=z$,

\[ |F(z)|^2=|G(z,z)|^2\leq K_ m(z)^2\| G\| ^2\leq C_\Omega \| F\| _{2m}^2K_ m(z)^2. \]

Taking the supremum over the unit ball of $H_{2m}$ proves the lemma. $\square$
