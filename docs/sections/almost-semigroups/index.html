16.2 Almost semigroups

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

C Almost semigroups

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

This appendix develops the theory of almost semigroups in $\mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$ that underpins the partial Okounkov body construction of chapter 10. Almost semigroups generalize the integral semigroups arising in the classical Lazarsfeld–Mustaţă and Kaveh–Khovanskii constructions to the setting where the underlying graded family is no longer multiplicative. The main result is the construction of a convex body $\Delta (S)\subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n$ out of an almost semigroup $S$. The corresponding statements were stated without proof in chapter 10; the proofs are given here.

C.1 Convex bodies

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Fix $n\in \mathbb {N}$.

Definition C.1.1. A convex body in $\mathbb {R}^ n$ is a non-empty compact convex set.

We allow a convex body to have empty interior.

We write $\mathcal{K}_ n$ for the set of convex bodies in $\mathbb {R}^ n$.

Definition C.1.2. The Hausdorff metric between $K_1,K_2\in \mathcal{K}_ n$ is given by

\[ d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(K_1,K_2)\coloneqq \max \left\{ \adjustlimits \sup _{x_1\in K_1}\inf _{x_2\in K_2}|x_1-x_2|, \adjustlimits \sup _{x_2\in K_2}\inf _{x_1\in K_1}|x_1-x_2|\right\} . \]
It is well-known that the metric space $(\mathcal{K}_ n,d_{\mathrm{Haus}})$ is complete. We will need the following fundamental theorem:

Theorem C.1.3 (Blaschke selection theorem). The metric space $(\mathcal{K}_ n,d_{\mathrm{Haus}})$ is locally compact.

We refer to [Theorem 1.8.7, Sch14] for details.

Theorem C.1.4. The Lebesgue volume $\operatorname{vol}\colon \mathcal{K}_ n\rightarrow \mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}$ is continuous.

See [Theorem 1.8.20, Sch14].

Theorem C.1.5. Let $K_ i, K\in \mathcal{K}_ n$ ($i\in \mathbb {N}$). Then $K_ i\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}K$ if and only if the following conditions hold:

  1. each point $x\in K$ is the limit of a sequence $x_ i\in K_ i$, and

  2. the limit of any convergent sequence $(x_{i_ j})_{j\in \mathbb {N}}$ with $x_{i_ j}\in K_{i_ j}$ lies in $K$, where $i_ j$ is a strictly increasing sequence in $\mathbb {Z}_{>0}$.

See [Theorem 1.8.8, Sch14].

For a convex body $K\subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n$ and $\epsilon >0$, we write $K_\epsilon \coloneqq \{ x\in K: B(x,\epsilon )\subseteq K\} $ for the inner $\epsilon $-shrinking of $K$. (Compare with the outer thickening $K^\epsilon $ used in corollary 5.2.15; the two are different operations.)

Lemma C.1.6. Let $K\in \mathcal{K}_ n$ be a convex body with positive volume and $K'\in \mathcal{K}_ n$. Assume that, for some $k\in \mathbb Z_{>0}$, $K'$ contains $K\cap (k^{-1}\mathbb Z)^ n$. Then

\[ K'\supseteq K_{n^{1/2}/k}. \]

Proof. Let $x\in K_{n^{1/2}k^{-1}}$. By assumption, the closed ball $B$ with center $x$ and radius $n^{1/2}k^{-1}$ is contained in $K$. Observe that $x$ can be written as a convex combination of points in $B\cap (k^{-1}\mathbb {Z})^ n$, which are contained in $K'$ by assumption. It follows that $x\in K'$. $\square$

Given a sequence of convex bodies $K_ i$ ($i\in \mathbb {N}$), we set

\[ \varliminf _{i\to \infty }K_ i= \overline{\bigcup _{i=0}^{\infty }\bigcap _{j\geq i}K_ j}. \]

Suppose $K$ is the limit of a subsequence of $K_ i$, we have

C.1
\begin{equation} \varliminf _{i\to \infty }K_ i\subseteq K. \end{equation}

This is a simple consequence of theorem C.1.5.

Lemma C.1.7. Assume that $n>0$. Let $K\subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n$ be a convex body. Let

\[ t_{\min }\coloneqq \min \{ t\in \mathbb {R}:\{ x_1=t\} \cap K\neq \varnothing \} ,\quad t_{\max }\coloneqq \max \{ t\in \mathbb {R}:\{ x_1=t\} \cap K\neq \varnothing \} . \]

Then for $t\in [t_{\min },t_{\max }]$, the map

\[ t\mapsto \{ y\in \mathbb {R}^{n-1}:(t,y)\in K\} \]

is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric.

Here $x_1$ denotes the first coordinate in $\mathbb {R}^ n$.

Proof. We may assume that $t_{\min }<t_{\max }$ as otherwise there is nothing to prove.

For each $t\in [t_{\min },t_{\max }]$, we write $K_ t=\{ y\in \mathbb {R}^{n-1}:(t,y)\in K\} $. Let $t_ j\to t$ be a convergent sequence in $[t_{\min },t_{\max }]$, we want to show that $K_{t_ j}$ converges to $K_ t$ with respect to the Hausdorff metric. Recall that this amounts to the following two assertions:

  1. For each convergent sequence $y_ j\in K_{t_ j}$ with limit $y$, we have $y\in K_ t$;

  2. Given any $y\in K_ t$, up to replacing $t_ j$ by a subsequence, we can find $y_ j\in K_{t_ j}$ converging to $y$.

The first assertion is obvious. Let us prove the second. Take $y\in K_ t$. Up to replacing $t_ j$ by a subsequence and taking the symmetry into account, we may assume that $t_ j> t$ for all $j$. In particular, $t<t_{\max }$.

We can find a point $z=(z^1,z')\in K$ such that $z^1>t$ (for example, there is always such a point with $z^1=t_{\max }$). Replacing $t_ j$ by a subsequence, we may assume that $t_ j\in (t,z^1)$ for all $j$. Then it suffices to take

\[ y_ j=\frac{z^1-t_ j}{z^1-t}y+\frac{t_ j-t}{z^1-t}z'. \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Lemma C.1.8. Assume that $n>0$. The maps $\mathcal{K}_ n\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ given by

\[ K\mapsto \min _{x\in K}x_1,\qquad K\mapsto \max _{x\in K}x_1 \]

are continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric.

Proof. We prove the assertion for the maximum; the proof for the minimum is the same. Let $K,L\in \mathcal{K}_ n$ and set $\epsilon =d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(K,L)$. For every $x\in K$, there exists $y\in L$ with $|x-y|\leq \epsilon $, hence

\[ x_1\leq y_1+\epsilon \leq \max _{z\in L}z_1+\epsilon . \]

Taking the maximum over $x\in K$ gives

\[ \max _{x\in K}x_1\leq \max _{z\in L}z_1+\epsilon . \]

Interchanging $K$ and $L$, we get

\[ \left|\max _{x\in K}x_1-\max _{z\in L}z_1\right|\leq d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(K,L). \]

This proves the continuity. $\square$

Lemma C.1.9. Let $D_ j\subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n$ $(j\geq 1)$ be a decreasing sequence of bounded convex sets. Assume that $\operatorname{vol}\bigcap _ j D_ j>0$. Then

\[ \overline{\bigcap _{j=1}^{\infty }D_ j}=\bigcap _{j=1}^{\infty }\overline{D_ j}. \]

Proof. The $\subseteq $ direction is clear. By convexity, it suffices to show that both sides have the same positive volume: indeed, a proper inclusion of closed convex sets with positive volume would cut off a non-empty relatively open convex piece by a separating hyperplane, hence would strictly decrease the volume. As the boundary of convex sets has zero Lebesgue measure, it follows that the volumes of both sides are equal to $\lim _{j\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}D_ j$. $\square$

Definition C.1.10. Let $K,K'\in \mathcal{K}_ n$, their Minkowski sum is given by

\[ K+K'\coloneqq \{ x+x': x\in K,x'\in K'\} . \]

Proposition C.1.11.The Minkowski sum $\mathcal{K}_ n\times \mathcal{K}_ n\rightarrow \mathcal{K}_ n$ is continuous.

See [Page 139, Sch14].

Theorem C.1.12 (Brunn–Minkowski). Let $K,K'\in \mathcal{K}_ n$. Then for any $t\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ \operatorname{vol}\bigl ((1-t)K'+tK\bigr )\geq \bigl (\operatorname{vol}K'\bigr )^{1-t}\bigl (\operatorname{vol}K\bigr )^ t. \]
In other words, the volume is log concave. See [Page 372, Sch14].

C.2 The Okounkov bodies of almost semigroups

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Fix an integer $n\geq 0$. Fix a closed convex cone $C\subseteq \mathbb {R}^{n}\times \mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}$ such that $C\cap \{ x_{n+1}=0\} =\{ 0\} $. Here $x_{n+1}$ is the last coordinate of $\mathbb {R}^{n+1}$.

C.2.1 Generalities on semigroups

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Write $\hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$ for the set of subsets of $C\cap \mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$ and $\mathcal{S}(C)$ for the set of subsemigroups $S\subseteq C\cap \mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$. For each $k\in \mathbb {N}$ and $S\in \hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$, we write

\[ S_ k\coloneqq \left\{ x\in \mathbb {Z}^ n: (x,k)\in S \right\} . \]

Note that $S_ k$ is a finite set by our assumption on $C$.

We introduce a pseudometric on $\hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$ as follows:

C.2
\begin{equation} d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')\coloneqq \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } k^{-n} \left(|S_ k|+|S'_ k|-2|(S\cap S')_ k|\right). \end{equation}
Here $|\bullet |$ denotes the cardinality of a finite set.

Lemma C.2.1. The above defined $d_{\mathrm{sg}}$ is a pseudometric on $\hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$.

Proof. Only the triangle inequality needs to be argued. Take $S,S',S''\in \hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$. We claim that for any $k\in \mathbb {N}$,

\[ |S_ k|+|S'_ k|-2|S_ k\cap S'_ k|+|S''_ k|+|S'_ k|-2|S''_ k\cap S'_ k|\geq |S_ k|+|S''_ k|-2|S_ k\cap S''_ k|. \]

From this the triangle inequality follows. To argue the claim, we rearrange it to the following form:

\[ |S'_ k|-|S_ k\cap S'_ k| \geq |S'_ k\cap S''_ k|-|S_ k\cap S''_ k|, \]

which is obvious. $\square$

Given $S,S'\in \hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$, we say $S$ is equivalent to $S'$ and write $S\sim S'$ if $d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')=0$. This is an equivalence relation by C.2.1.

Lemma C.2.2. Given $S,S',S''\in \hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$, we have

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S\cap S'',S'\cap S'')\leq d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S'). \]

In particular, if $S^ i,S'^ i\in \hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$ ($i\in \mathbb {N}$) and $S^ i\to S$, $S'^ i\to S'$, then

\[ S^ i\cap S'^ i\to S\cap S'. \]

Proof. Observe that for any $k\in \mathbb {N}$,

\[ |S_ k\cap S_ k''|-|S_ k\cap S_ k'\cap S_ k''|\leq |S_ k|-|S_ k\cap S_ k'|. \]

The same holds if we interchange $S$ with $S'$. It follows that

\[ |S_ k\cap S_ k''|+|S_ k'\cap S_ k''|-2|S_ k\cap S_ k'\cap S_ k''|\leq |S_ k|+|S'_ k|-2|S_ k\cap S_ k'|. \]

The first assertion follows.

Next we compute

\[ \begin{split} d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i\cap S'^ i,S\cap S')\leq & d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i\cap S'^ i,S^ i\cap S')+d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i\cap S',S\cap S')\\ \leq & d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S'^ i,S')+d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i,S) \end{split} \]

and the second assertion follows. $\square$

The volume of $S\in \mathcal{S}(C)$ is defined as

\[ \operatorname{vol}S\coloneqq \lim _{k\to \infty } (ka)^{-n}|S_{ka}|=\varlimsup _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}|S_ k|, \]
where $a$ is a sufficiently divisible positive integer. The existence of the limit and its independence from $a$ both follow from the more precise result [Theorem 2, KK12], a special case of which can be stated as follows:

Theorem C.2.3 (Kaveh–Khovanskii). Let $S\in \mathcal{S}(C)$. Then, for every sufficiently divisible positive integer $a$, the limit

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty }(ka)^{-n}|S_{ka}| \]

exists, is independent of $a$, and is equal to $\varlimsup _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}|S_ k|$. If moreover $S$ generates $\mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$ as an Abelian group, and if $C(S)$ denotes the closed convex cone generated by $S\cup \{ 0\} $, then

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}|S_ k| = \operatorname{vol}\{ x\in \mathbb {R}^ n:(x,1)\in C(S)\} . \]

In particular, for every convex body $K\subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n$,

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } k^{-n}\# \left(kK\cap \mathbb {Z}^ n\right)=\operatorname{vol}K. \]

The same assertion holds with $\mathbb {Z}^ n$ replaced by any lattice, provided the Lebesgue measure is normalized by that lattice.

This is the part of [Theorem 2, KK12] used in this book.

Lemma C.2.4. Let $S,S'\in \mathcal{S}(C)$. Then

\[ |\operatorname{vol}S-\operatorname{vol}S'|\leq d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S'). \]

Proof. Choose a sufficiently divisible positive integer $a$. Taking the limsup in the definition of $d_{\mathrm{sg}}$ along $k=ma$ gives

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')\geq \operatorname{vol}S+\operatorname{vol}S'-2\operatorname{vol}(S\cap S'). \]

It follows that $\operatorname{vol}S-\operatorname{vol}S'\leq d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')$ and $\operatorname{vol}S'-\operatorname{vol}S\leq d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')$. $\square$

We define $\overline{\mathcal{S}}(C)$ as the closure of $\mathcal{S}(C)$ in $\hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$ with respect to the topology defined by the pseudometric $d_{\mathrm{sg}}$. By C.2.4, $\operatorname{vol}\colon \mathcal{S}(C)\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ admits a unique $1$-Lipschitz extension to

C.5
\begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}\colon \overline{\mathcal{S}}(C)\rightarrow \mathbb {R}. \end{equation}

Lemma C.2.5. Suppose that $S,S'\in \overline{\mathcal{S}}(C)$ and $S\subseteq S'$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}S\leq \operatorname{vol}S'. \]

Proof. Take sequences $S^ j,S'^ j$ in $\mathcal{S}(C)$ such that $S^ j\to S$, $S'^ j\to S'$. By C.2.2, after replacing $S^ j$ by $S^ j\cap S'^ j$, we may assume that $S^ j\subseteq S'^ j$ for each $j$. Then our assertion follows easily. $\square$

C.2.2 Okounkov bodies of semigroups

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Given $S\in \hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$, we will write $C(S)\subseteq C$ for the closed convex cone generated by $S\cup \{ 0\} $. Moreover, for each $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we define

\[ \Delta _ k(S)\coloneqq \mathrm{Conv}\left\{ k^{-1}x\in \mathbb {R}^ n:x\in S_ k \right\} \subseteq \mathbb {R}^ n. \]

Here $\mathrm{Conv}$ denotes the convex hull.

See Definition C.2.6.

Note that for any $S\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$, the cone $C(S)$ has full dimension (i.e., the topological interior is non-empty). Given a full-dimensional subcone $C'\subseteq C$, the semigroup $C'\cap \mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$ lies in $\mathcal{S}'(C)$.

This class behaves well under intersections:

Lemma C.2.7. Let $S,S'\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$. Assume that $\operatorname{vol}(S\cap S')>0$. Then $S\cap S'\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$, and

\[ C(S\cap S')=C(S)\cap C(S'). \]
The lemma obviously fails if $\operatorname{vol}(S\cap S')=0$.

Proof. We first observe that the cone $C(S)\cap C(S')$ has full dimension since otherwise $\operatorname{vol}(S\cap S')=0$. Take a full-dimensional subcone $C'$ in $C(S)\cap C(S')$ such that $C'$ intersects the boundary of $C(S)\cap C(S')$ only at $0$. It follows from [Theorem 1, KK12] that there is an integer $N>0$ such that any $x\in \mathbb {Z}^{n+1}\cap C'$ with Euclidean norm at least $N$ lies in $S\cap S'$. Therefore, $S\cap S'\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$. Since $C'$ can be chosen around any ray in the interior of $C(S)\cap C(S')$, the same argument also shows that

\[ C(S\cap S')=C(S)\cap C(S'). \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

We recall the following definition from [KK12].

Definition C.2.8. Given $S\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$, its Okounkov body is defined as follows

\[ \Delta (S)\coloneqq \left\{ x\in \mathbb {R}^ n:(x,1)\in C(S) \right\} . \]

See Theorem C.2.9.

This is essentially proved in [Lemma 4.8, WN14], which itself follows from a theorem of Khovanskii [Kho92].

Proof. The equalities (C.6) follow from theorem C.2.3.

It remains to prove (C.7). By the argument of [Lemma 4.8, WN14], for any compact set $K\subseteq \operatorname{Int}\Delta (S)$, there is $k_0>0$ such that for any $k\geq k_0$, $\alpha \in K\cap (k^{-1}\mathbb {Z})^ n$ implies that $\alpha \in \Delta _ k(S)$.

To conclude, fix $\epsilon >0$. Choose $\delta >0$ such that $K\coloneqq \Delta (S)_{\delta }$ has positive volume and $d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(K,\Delta (S))<\epsilon /2$. Applying C.1.6 to $K$, we find that $\Delta _ k(S)$ contains $K_{n^{1/2}k^{-1}}$ for all large $k$. After increasing $k$, we may also assume that $d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(K,K_{n^{1/2}k^{-1}})<\epsilon /2$. Since $\Delta _ k(S)\subseteq \Delta (S)$, this implies (C.7). $\square$

See Corollary C.2.10.

Proof. This is a direct consequence of C.2.7 and (C.6). $\square$

Lemma C.2.11. Given $S\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$, we have $S\sim \operatorname{Reg}(S)$.

Recall that the regularization $\operatorname{Reg}(S)$ of $S$ is defined as $C(S)\cap \mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$.

Proof. Since $S$ and $\operatorname{Reg}(S)$ have the same Okounkov body, we have $\operatorname{vol}S=\operatorname{vol}\operatorname{Reg}(S)$ by theorem C.2.9. By corollary C.2.10 again,

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(\operatorname{Reg}(S),S)=\operatorname{vol}\operatorname{Reg}(S)-\operatorname{vol}S=0. \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Lemma C.2.12. Let $S,S'\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$. Assume that $d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')=0$. Then $\Delta (S)=\Delta (S')$.

Proof. Observe that $\operatorname{vol}(S\cap S')>0$, as otherwise

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')\geq \operatorname{vol}S+\operatorname{vol}S'>0, \]

which is a contradiction.

It follows from C.2.7 that $S\cap S'\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$. It suffices to show that $\Delta (S)=\Delta (S\cap S')$. In fact, suppose that this holds, since $\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S')=\operatorname{vol}S'=\operatorname{vol}S=\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S)$, the inclusion $\Delta (S')\supseteq \Delta (S\cap S')=\Delta (S)$ is an equality.

After replacing $S'$ by $S\cap S'$, we still have $d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')=0$ by C.2.2, and now $S'\subseteq S$. Hence corollary C.2.10 gives

\[ 0=d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S')=\operatorname{vol}S-\operatorname{vol}S', \]

so $\operatorname{vol}S=\operatorname{vol}S'$. Therefore

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Delta (S)=\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S')>0 \]

by (C.6). Therefore, they are equal. $\square$

Lemma C.2.13. Suppose that $S^ i\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$ is a decreasing sequence such that

\[ \lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}S^ i>0. \]

Then there is $S\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$ such that $S^ i\to S$.

In general, one cannot simply take $S=\bigcap _ i S^ i$. For example, consider the sequence $S^ i=S^1\cap \{ x_{n+1}\geq i\} $.

Proof. By C.2.11, we may replace $S^ i$ by its regularization and assume that $S^ i=C(S^ i)\cap \mathbb {Z}^{n+1}$. We define

\[ S=\left( \bigcap _{i=1}^{\infty }C(S^ i) \right) \cap \mathbb {Z}^{n+1}. \]

The convex bodies $\Delta (S^ i)$ form a decreasing sequence and satisfy

\[ \lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S^ i)=\lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}S^ i>0. \]

By continuity from above for Lebesgue measure,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\bigcap _ i\Delta (S^ i)=\lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S^ i)>0. \]

Set

\[ K\coloneqq \bigcap _{i=1}^{\infty }C(S^ i). \]

The cone $K$ is full-dimensional. As noted above, $K\cap \mathbb Z^{n+1}$ generates $\mathbb Z^{n+1}$ as an Abelian group; hence $S=K\cap \mathbb Z^{n+1}$ lies in $\mathcal S'(C)$. Moreover, the closed convex cone generated by $S$ is exactly $K$. Therefore

\[ \Delta (S)=\{ x:(x,1)\in K\} =\bigcap _{i=1}^{\infty }\{ x:(x,1)\in C(S^ i)\} =\bigcap _{i=1}^{\infty }\Delta (S^ i). \]

By corollary C.2.10 and theorem C.2.9, we can compute the distance

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S,S^ i)=\operatorname{vol}S^ i-\operatorname{vol}S=\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S^ i)-\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S), \]

which tends to $0$ by construction. $\square$

C.2.3 Okounkov bodies of almost semigroups

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Definition C.2.14. We define $\overline{\mathcal{S}'(C)}_{>0}$ to be the set of elements in the closure of $\mathcal{S}'(C)$ in $\hat{\mathcal{S}}(C)$ with positive volume. An element in $\overline{\mathcal{S}'(C)}_{>0}$ is called an almost semigroup in $C$.

Recall that the volume here is defined in (C.5). Our goal is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem C.2.15. The Okounkov body map $\Delta \colon \mathcal{S}'(C) \rightarrow \mathcal{K}_ n$ as defined in definition C.2.8 admits a unique continuous extension

C.8
\begin{equation} \Delta \colon \overline{\mathcal{S}'(C)}_{>0}\rightarrow \mathcal{K}_ n. \end{equation}

Moreover, for any $S\in \overline{\mathcal{S}'(C)}_{>0}$, we have

C.9
\begin{equation} \operatorname{vol}S=\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S). \end{equation}

Proof. The uniqueness of the extension is clear once existence is known. Moreover, (C.9) follows easily from theorem C.2.9 and theorem C.1.4 by continuity. It remains to argue the existence of the continuous extension. We first construct an extension and prove its continuity.

Step 1. We construct the desired map (C.8). Let $S\in \overline{\mathcal{S}'(C)}_{>0}$. We wish to construct a convex body $\Delta (S)\in \mathcal{K}_ n$.

Let $S^ i\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$ be a sequence that converges to $S$ such that

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i,S^{i+1})\leq 2^{-i}. \]

For each $i,j\geq 0$, we introduce

\[ S^{i,j}=S^ i\cap S^{i+1}\cap \cdots \cap S^{i+j}. \]

Then by C.2.2,

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i,j},S^{i,j+1})\leq 2^{-i-j}. \]

Take $i_0>0$ large enough so that, for $i\geq i_0$, we have $\operatorname{vol}S^ i>2^{-1}\operatorname{vol}S$ and $2^{2-i}< \operatorname{vol}S$. Then

\[ \operatorname{vol}S^ i-\operatorname{vol}S^{i,j}\leq d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i,0},S^{i,1})+d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i,1},S^{i,2})+\cdots +d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i,j-1},S^{i,j})\leq 2^{1-i}. \]

It follows that $\operatorname{vol}S^{i,j}> 2^{-1}\operatorname{vol}S-2^{1-i}>0$ whenever $i\geq i_0$. In particular, by C.2.7, $S^{i,j}\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$ for $i\geq i_0$.

By C.2.13, for $i\geq i_0$, there exists $T^ i\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$ such that $S^{i,j}\to T^ i$ as $j\to \infty $. Moreover,

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(T^ i,S)=\lim _{j\to \infty } d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i,j},S)\leq \lim _{j\to \infty } d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i,j},S^ i)+d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i,S)\leq 2^{1-i}+d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i,S). \]

Therefore, $T^ i\to S$. We then define

\[ \Delta (S)\coloneqq \overline{\bigcup _{i=i_0}^{\infty } \Delta (T^ i)}. \]

In other words, we have defined

\[ \Delta (S)\coloneqq \varliminf _{i\to \infty }\Delta (S^ i). \]

Indeed, by the construction in the proof of C.2.13 and the cone equality in C.2.7,

\[ \Delta (T^ i)=\bigcap _{j\geq 0}\Delta (S^{i,j}) =\bigcap _{j\geq i}\Delta (S^ j). \]

Thus the bodies $\Delta (T^ i)$ are increasing in $i$ and are all contained in the compact slice $\{ x\in \mathbb {R}^ n:(x,1)\in C\} $. In particular, $\Delta (S)$ is a convex body and

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Delta (S)=\lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}\Delta (T^ i)=\lim _{i\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}T^ i=\operatorname{vol}S. \]

This is an honest limit: if $\Delta $ is the limit of a subsequence of $\Delta (S^ i)$, then $\Delta (S)\subseteq \Delta $ by (C.1). Moreover,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\Delta =\lim _{m\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}\Delta (S^{i_ m})=\lim _{m\to \infty }\operatorname{vol}S^{i_ m}=\operatorname{vol}S. \]

Comparing the volumes, we find that equality holds. So by theorem C.1.3,

C.10
\begin{equation} \Delta (S)=\lim _{i\to \infty }\Delta (S^ i). \end{equation}

Next we claim that $\Delta (S)$ as defined above does not depend on the choice of the sequence $S^ i$. In fact, suppose that $S'^ i\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$ is another sequence satisfying the same conditions as $S^ i$. By C.2.2, we have

\[ R^ i=S^{i+1}\cap S'^{i+1}\longrightarrow S\cap S=S. \]

Since $\operatorname{vol}S>0$ and the volume is continuous on $\overline{\mathcal S}(C)$, we have $\operatorname{vol}R^ i>0$ for all large $i$. Thus $R^ i\in \mathcal S'(C)$ by C.2.7. Moreover,

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(R^ i,R^{i+1}) \leq d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i+1},S^{i+2}) + d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S'^{i+1},S'^{i+2}). \]

Passing simultaneously to subsequences of $S^ i$ and $S'^ i$, we may assume

\[ d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^{i+1},S^{i+2})+d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S'^{i+1},S'^{i+2})\leq 2^{-i}. \]

Then the construction above applies to $R^ i=S^{i+1}\cap S'^{i+1}$. Since $R^ i\subseteq S^{i+1}$ and $R^ i\subseteq S'^{i+1}$, every Hausdorff limit of $\Delta (R^ i)$ is contained in the corresponding limits of $\Delta (S^ i)$ and $\Delta (S'^ i)$. The volumes of all three limits are $\operatorname{vol}S$, hence the inclusions are equalities. The same is true with $S'^ i$ in place of $S^ i$. So we conclude that $\Delta (S)$ as in (C.10) does not depend on the choices we made.

Step 2. It remains to prove the continuity of $\Delta $ defined in Step 1. Suppose that $S^ i\in \overline{\mathcal{S}'(C)}_{>0}$ is a sequence with limit $S\in \overline{\mathcal{S}'(C)}_{>0}$. We want to show that

C.11
\begin{equation} \Delta (S^ i)\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}\Delta (S). \end{equation}

We first reduce to the case where $S^ i\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$. By (C.10), for each $i$, we can choose $T^ i\in \mathcal{S}'(C)$ such that $d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i,T^ i)<2^{-i}$ and $d_{\mathrm{Haus}}(\Delta (S^ i),\Delta (T^ i))<2^{-i}$. If we have shown $\Delta (T^ i)\xrightarrow {d_{\mathrm{Haus}}}\Delta (S)$, then (C.11) follows immediately.

Next we reduce to the case where $d_{\mathrm{sg}}(S^ i,S^{i+1})\leq 2^{-i}$. Thanks to theorem C.1.3, in order to prove (C.11), it suffices to show that each subsequence of $\Delta (S^ i)$ admits a subsequence that converges to $\Delta (S)$. Hence, we may pass to a sufficiently fast subsequence and reduce to the required case.

After these reductions, (C.11) is nothing but (C.10). $\square$

Remark C.2.17. The construction of $\Delta $ is independent of the choice of $C$ in the following sense. If $C'$ is another cone satisfying the same assumptions as $C$ and $C'\supseteq C$, then the Okounkov body map $\Delta \colon \overline{\mathcal{S}'(C')}_{>0}\rightarrow \mathcal{K}_ n$ extends the corresponding map (C.8). We will use this fact without further comment.
