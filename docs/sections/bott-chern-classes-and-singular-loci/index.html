9.2 Bott–Chern classes and singular loci

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

28 Bott–Chern classes and singular loci

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

This part of the book returns to closed backgrounds. A closed real $(1,1)$-form has a Bott–Chern class, and positivity can then be asked of the class rather than of a chosen representative. On a compact Kähler manifold, the positivity of a class is described by its non-nef and non-Kähler loci, by intersection numbers and by volumes. The present chapter asks which parts of this description survive on an arbitrary compact complex manifold, where no Kähler metric controls masses, and how closed classes interact with the Hermitian equations of the preceding parts. In chapter 29, closed classes arise from the Lee form of a locally conformally Kähler metric.

Throughout the chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$ as in the Conventions, and $\omega $ is a fixed Hermitian form on $X$; sections that require a Kähler manifold or a special geometry say so at their beginning. Without a Kähler class, Gauduchon metrics provide the substitute mass control. Pairing a closed current with the $(n-1)$st power of a Gauduchon metric eliminates its potential, so the currents of a fixed class whose negative part is bounded by a multiple of $\omega $ have uniformly bounded mass. With this compactness, regularization defines the non-nef locus through Lelong numbers of minimal currents and the non-Kähler locus through polar sets of Kähler currents. These loci separate the singularities forced by the class from those of a particular current. Restricted volumes, which describe the same loci on Kähler manifolds, behave differently: computed with a Hermitian error, they can be infinite even for the zero class. When $X$ belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$, pulling a class back to a Kähler resolution of each subvariety gives instead a finite restricted mass; on a smooth subvariety, its positivity detects bigness of the restricted class. For a divisorial trace with bounded remainder, the non-pluripolar mass is the top intersection of a nef residual class; positive mass produces a Kähler current, but possibly with a larger singularity than the trace.

Closed classes also constrain the Hermitian Monge–Ampère equations of earlier chapters. A divisorial component of a positive current in a Hermitian background consumes Gauduchon trace but contributes no density to its non-pluripolar measure, so it can lower the largest coefficient of a subsolution. At the maximal coefficient, weak limits of maximizing Gauduchon tests annihilate the class of the divisor. On the elliptic threefold of section 26.18, closedness forces every positive current to descend to the base surface. No closed class is big there, although small Hermitian perturbations of the boundary classes have positive variational volume, whose degeneration can be computed explicitly.

Smooth solutions raise a metric question. For a big class on a compact Kähler manifold, the solution of the Monge–Ampère equation with a smooth positive density defines a Kähler metric on the ample locus, but its smoothness there does not determine the distance to the complement. Zeros of the density can come from ramification, from collapsed exceptional fibers or from a singular Hessian, and they give different normal lengths even when the determinant vanishes to the same order. For radial equations, boundedness of the potential and finiteness of the distance are governed by two different integrals: a continuous minimal remainder can have a complete end, whereas a Hölder remainder forces finite distance. For exponential equations with a divisorial pole, dividing the determinant by its tangential part gives a lower bound for normal length and a sharp logarithmic threshold for completeness. Explicit surfaces give exponential equations with unique finite-energy solutions and incomplete metrics. One solution has minimal singularities in a big non-nef class; another has a nef and big class but an unbounded cusp.

At the boundary of the nef cone, smooth semipositive representatives have a different metric behavior. On a complex torus, a constant semipositive form whose kernel leaves are dense is the only closed positive current in its class, and adding a small fixed Kähler form produces metrics whose diameters tend to zero. An explicit family of flat metrics collapses in the same way at a class with many semipositive representatives, so the collapse depends on how the class is approached. On the resolution of a Kummer quotient, an irrational nef boundary class has many smooth semipositive representatives. Finally, in a family of blown-up projective planes, degree-zero curves on the nearby fibers force a larger singularity along one component of the central curve, so a prescribed semipositive metric on the special fiber does not extend to the family.

28.1 Closed classes with Hermitian lower bounds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Testing a closed current against a Gauduchon form eliminates its potential. The resulting trace depends only on the class, and it bounds the mass of every almost positive representative.

Recall from section 23.4 that $H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ is the space of smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms modulo $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of smooth real functions, and that a class is pseudoeffective if it contains a closed positive current. It is nef if, for every $\varepsilon >0$, it has a smooth representative bounded below by $-\varepsilon \omega $ (section 23.6). Fix a class $\alpha \in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ and a smooth closed representative $\theta $. By lemma 23.4.1, the closed real $(1,1)$-currents in $\alpha $ are exactly the currents $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $u$ a real distribution, and the potential of a positive one is quasi-plurisubharmonic. A closed current $T$ with $T\geq \delta \omega $ for some $\delta >0$ is called a Kähler current; we also say that such a current is strict. The class $\alpha $ is big if it contains a Kähler current, that is, if $\theta $ is big in the sense of section 1.19. None of these properties depends on the choice of $\theta $, because replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ with $f$ smooth only changes the potentials by $-f$.

For a pseudoeffective class $\alpha $ and $\varepsilon >0$, let

\[ \alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ] =\{ T\in \alpha :T\geq -\varepsilon \omega \} \]
be the set of closed currents in $\alpha $ whose negative part is bounded by $\varepsilon \omega $.

Proposition 28.1.1. For each fixed $\varepsilon >0$, the currents in $\alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$ have uniformly bounded distributional mass.

Proof. Choose a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $. For $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ in $\alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$, the current $T+\varepsilon \omega $ is positive, and since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\gamma ^{n-1}=0$, Stokes' theorem for currents gives

\[ \int _ X(T+\varepsilon \omega )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =\int _ X(\theta +\varepsilon \omega )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}. \]

The right-hand side does not depend on $T$. Since $\gamma $ is comparable to any fixed reference metric, this trace bounds all coefficient measures of the positive current $T+\varepsilon \omega $. Subtracting the fixed smooth form $\varepsilon \omega $ gives the asserted bound for $T$. In dimension one the same argument applies with $\gamma ^{n-1}=1$ and the ordinary Stokes theorem. $\square$

Proposition 28.1.2. The pseudoeffective cone in $H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ is closed and convex and contains no nonzero line. For a fixed Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, the trace functional $\ell _\gamma $ of section 23.4 is strictly positive on its nonzero elements, and the slice of pseudoeffective classes with $\ell _\gamma =1$ is compact. This slice may be empty. Pullback and pushforward by a compact modification are continuous linear maps on Bott–Chern cohomology and preserve pseudoeffectivity.

Proof. The finite-dimensional space of smooth representatives in lemma 23.7.1 identifies Bott–Chern cohomology with a finite-dimensional vector space and gives it its usual topology; the trace functional is continuous for this topology. Its positivity and the absence of lines in the cone were proved in section 23.4, and convexity follows by adding positive currents.

Let pseudoeffective classes $\alpha _ j$ converge to $\alpha $, and choose positive closed representatives $T_ j$. Their masses $\ell _\gamma (\alpha _ j)$ are bounded, so by weak compactness a subsequence converges to a positive closed current $T$. Let $B_\gamma $ be the continuous smooth representative map of lemma 23.7.1. Along the subsequence, $B_\gamma (T_ j)$ converges both to $B_\gamma (T)$ and to the representative of $\alpha $, so $T$ represents $\alpha $. Thus the cone is closed. On its mass-one slice, (23.3) bounds $B_\gamma (T)$ in the finite-dimensional representative space. The slice is therefore closed and bounded there, hence compact.

The class maps of a modification are linear, and they are well-defined because classes can be represented equally by smooth forms and by currents. As linear maps between finite-dimensional spaces, they are continuous. Pullback preserves positive closed currents, since it can be computed on local plurisubharmonic potentials as in proposition 23.7.2, and proper pushforward preserves positivity and closedness. Applying these maps to positive representatives proves the assertions about the cones. $\square$

This compactness is linear. The functional $\ell _\gamma $ pairs a closed current with a fixed smooth form; it is not a top-degree volume of the class, and it gives no bound for the Monge–Ampère masses of potentials.

The same compactness shows that a nef class $\alpha $ is pseudoeffective. Indeed, choose smooth representatives $\theta _ j\in \alpha $ with $\theta _ j\geq -\varepsilon _ j\omega $, where $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$. They have the same Gauduchon trace and hence uniformly bounded mass. A weak cluster value is a closed current, positive because the negative parts tend to zero, and it belongs to $\alpha $ because the smooth representative map of lemma 23.7.1 is continuous.

28.2 Minimal multiplicities and the non-nef locus

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In this section $\alpha $ is pseudoeffective. A current in $\alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$ may have singularities that the class does not force. To isolate the forced ones, take in each family a current with the mildest singularities and let the allowed negative error tend to zero. The Lelong numbers that persist define the non-nef locus.

For $\varepsilon >0$ put

\[ V_\varepsilon =V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }, \qquad T_{\min ,\varepsilon }=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\varepsilon , \]

where $V_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }=P_{\theta +\varepsilon \omega }(0)$ is the normalized envelope of section 1.19. Since $\alpha $ is pseudoeffective, $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\varepsilon \omega )$ is nonempty, so $V_\varepsilon $ is $(\theta +\varepsilon \omega )$-plurisubharmonic and $T_{\min ,\varepsilon }\in \alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$. Moreover, $V_\varepsilon $ has minimal singularities in this family: $u-\sup _ Xu\leq V_\varepsilon $ for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\varepsilon \omega )$.

Definition 28.2.1. For $x\in X$, the minimal multiplicity of $\alpha $ at $x$ is

\[ \nu (\alpha ,x)=\sup _{\varepsilon >0}\nu (T_{\min ,\varepsilon },x). \]

The non-nef locus is

\[ E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )=\{ x\in X:\nu (\alpha ,x)>0\} . \]

As $\varepsilon $ decreases, the family $\alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$ shrinks, so the Lelong numbers of its minimal current can only increase; the supremum is therefore a limit as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Two Hermitian forms are comparable up to a constant factor, so the families defined by them are nested after rescaling $\varepsilon $; hence $\nu (\alpha ,x)$ does not depend on $\omega $. Replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ changes the minimal potentials by $-f$ up to a bounded normalization error, which does not affect Lelong numbers.

The smooth regularization of 1.15.1 turns vanishing Lelong numbers into an arbitrarily small loss of positivity. This gives the converse direction in the following characterization.

Proposition 28.2.2. For a pseudoeffective class $\alpha $, the set $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )$ is a countable union of analytic subsets. Moreover,

\[ \alpha \text{ is nef}\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )=\varnothing . \]

Proof. By the monotonicity just noted,

\[ E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha ) =\bigcup _{k,\ell \geq 1} \{ x:\nu (T_{\min ,1/\ell },x)\geq 1/k\} , \]

and each set in this union is analytic by Siu's analyticity theorem (1.13.1).

If $\alpha $ is nef, each family $\alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$ contains a smooth current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$. By minimality, $V_\varepsilon \geq f-\sup _ Xf$, so $V_\varepsilon $ is bounded and all Lelong numbers of $T_{\min ,\varepsilon }$ vanish. Conversely, if the non-nef locus is empty, every $T_{\min ,\varepsilon }$ has zero Lelong numbers. The regularization just recalled then gives a smooth representative of $\alpha $ bounded below by $-2\varepsilon \omega $. Since $\varepsilon >0$ is arbitrary, $\alpha $ is nef. $\square$

The approximating families cannot be replaced by the single family of positive currents in $\alpha $: even for a nef class, a positive current with minimal singularities in $\alpha $ itself may have nonzero Lelong numbers; see the example discussed in section 34.16.

28.3 The non-Kähler locus

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

A big class contains Kähler currents, but in general none of them is smooth. The points at which every Kähler current in the class is singular form its non-Kähler locus. Analytic regularization, maxima of potentials and the descending chain condition for analytic sets produce a single Kähler current whose singular set is exactly this locus.

For an almost positive closed $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$, write

\[ E_+(T)=\{ x\in X:\nu (T,x)>0\} \]

for its positive Lelong set, where $\nu (T,x)$ is the Lelong number of a local potential of $T$ at $x$.

Definition 28.3.1. The non-Kähler locus of a big class $\alpha $ is

\[ E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\bigcap _{T}E_+(T), \]

where $T$ ranges over the Kähler currents in $\alpha $. For a pseudoeffective class that is not big, we set $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=X$.

Theorem 28.3.2. Let $\alpha $ be big. There is a Kähler current in $\alpha $ with analytic singularities, smooth off its polar set, whose positive Lelong set is exactly $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. In particular this locus is a proper analytic subset, and

\[ E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha ), \qquad E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\varnothing \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \alpha \text{ is Kähler}. \]

Proof. Step 1: reduction to analytic singularities. By the analytic regularization of 1.15.1, a Kähler current $T\in \alpha $ can be approximated by currents in $\alpha $ with analytic singularities and smooth remainder away from their poles. After a sufficiently small loss of positivity these are still Kähler currents, and their positive Lelong sets are contained in $E_+(T)$. Hence the intersection defining $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$ may be taken over Kähler currents with analytic singularities and smooth remainder.

Step 2: intersections. Let $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1$ and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2$ be two such currents. By lemma 1.3.1, $\max (u_1,u_2)$ is the potential of a current dominating $\delta \omega $ for a common $\delta >0$, and its Lelong number at each point is the minimum of those of $u_1$ and $u_2$. Regularizing this current as in Step 1 gives a Kähler current with analytic singularities whose positive Lelong set is contained in the intersection of the two previous sets.

Step 3: realization. The positive Lelong sets of Step 1 are analytic subsets of $X$. By the descending chain condition for analytic subsets of a compact complex manifold, some finite intersection of them is minimal, and it then equals the full intersection $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. Repeated use of Step 2 gives a single Kähler current with analytic singularities whose polar set, which is also its positive Lelong set, is contained in this finite intersection. Since $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$ is contained in the positive Lelong set of every Kähler current in $\alpha $, equality holds.

Step 4: the two consequences. For a positive current $T\in \alpha $ and $\varepsilon >0$, we have $T\in \alpha [-\varepsilon \omega ]$, so minimality gives $\nu (T_{\min ,\varepsilon },x)\leq \nu (T,x)$. Taking the supremum over $\varepsilon $ and then the intersection over Kähler currents $T$ gives $E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )\subset E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. If the non-Kähler locus is empty, the current of Step 3 has no poles and is therefore a Kähler form in $\alpha $. Conversely, if $\alpha $ contains a Kähler form, using it in the defining intersection gives $E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\varnothing $. $\square$

Proposition 28.3.3. The non-Kähler locus of a big class has no isolated points.

Proof. Let $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi \geq \delta \omega $ be a current as in theorem 28.3.2, and suppose that $x$ is an isolated point of its polar set. Choose holomorphic coordinates $z=(z_1,\ldots ,z_ n)$ on a ball centered at $x$ that contains no other pole. For a sufficiently large constant $A>0$ and any real constant $C$, the local function $q(z)=A|z|^2-C$ satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \delta '\omega $ on a smaller ball, for some $\delta '>0$.

Since $\Phi $ is smooth on a closed annular collar near the boundary of this smaller ball, we can choose $C$ so that $q<\Phi $ on the collar. Since $\Phi \to -\infty $ at $x$, the function $\max (\Phi ,q)$ agrees with $q$ near $x$ and with $\Phi $ on the collar. By lemma 1.3.2, it glues with $\Phi $ outside the ball to the potential of a Kähler current in $\alpha $ that is smooth near $x$. Its Lelong number at $x$ is zero, contradicting $x\in E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. $\square$

Bigness of a closed class has a strong global consequence. A compact complex manifold carries a Kähler current if and only if it belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$; this is the classical modification criterion discussed in section 34.16. Hence a compact complex manifold outside class $\mathcal C$ has no big Bott–Chern class at all, whereas the strictly positive nonclosed backgrounds studied in this book exist on every compact complex manifold.

28.4 Null loci and numerical restricted volumes

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In this section $X$ is Kähler and $\omega $ is a Kähler form. For a closed class $\alpha $ and an irreducible analytic subvariety $V\subset X$ of positive dimension, the intersection number $\int _ V\alpha ^{\dim V}$ is computed by pulling back a smooth closed representative to a resolution of $V$; by Stokes' theorem it does not depend on the representative. For a nef class $\alpha $, the subvarieties on which this number vanishes form the classical null locus

\[ \operatorname {Null}(\alpha ) =\bigcup _{\substack {V\subset X\ \mathrm{irreducible},\ \dim V>0\\ \int _ V\alpha ^{\dim V}=0}}V. \]

For a nef and big class, the classical theorem discussed in section 34.16 gives

\[ E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )=\operatorname {Null}(\alpha ). \]

The same equality holds on compact manifolds in class $\mathcal C$.

For a pseudoeffective class that is not nef, intersection numbers are replaced by numerical restricted volumes. Let $V\subset X$ be an irreducible compact analytic subvariety of dimension $k>0$, with regular locus $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. If $V\subset E_{\mathrm{nn}}(\alpha )$, the numerical restricted volume of $\alpha $ along $V$ is defined to be zero. Otherwise, for real $\varepsilon >0$, let $T$ range over the closed currents in $\alpha $ with $T\geq -\varepsilon \omega $ that have analytic singularities with smooth remainder and whose polar set does not contain $V$, and set

28.1
\begin{equation} \langle \alpha ^ k\rangle _{X\mid V} =\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}\ \sup _ T \int _{V_{\mathrm{reg}}} \bigl ((T+\varepsilon \omega )|_{V_{\mathrm{reg}}}\bigr )_{\mathrm{ac}}^ k, \end{equation}

where the subscript $\mathrm{ac}$ denotes the absolutely continuous part of a measure on $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Because $T$ has smooth remainder, the integrand is the extension by zero of a smooth measure defined away from an analytic subset of $V$.

The numerical null locus of $\alpha $ is the union of the positive-dimensional irreducible subvarieties on which (28.1) vanishes. The trace theory of [Chapter 8, SGPT] expresses these restricted volumes as masses of trace currents on $V$; this is why singularity types and restrictions of multiplier ideals enter into the identification of this locus. Such a trace need not equal the pointwise restriction of a singular potential; see chapter 19.

28.5 Restricted volumes and Hermitian perturbations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We return to an arbitrary compact complex manifold $X$ with a Hermitian form $\omega $. In (28.1), the negative part of $T$ is measured by a closed Kähler form. If the closed perturbation is replaced by the Hermitian form $\omega $, the supremum can be infinite even for the zero Bott–Chern class $\{ 0\} $ and $V=X$. Unbounded Monge–Ampère masses of smooth potentials, such as those on the Hopf threefold, already produce this failure.

Proposition 28.5.1. Suppose there are smooth real functions $v_ j$ on $X$, for positive integers $j$, such that

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j>0, \qquad \int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ n\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

Then for every $\varepsilon >0$,

28.2
\begin{equation} \sup _{\substack {T\in \{ 0\} \ \mathrm{smooth,\ closed}\\ T\geq -\varepsilon \omega }} \int _ X(T+\varepsilon \omega )^ n=+\infty . \end{equation}

This occurs for the Gauduchon form on the Hopf threefold in theorem 8.0.1.

Proof. Fix $\varepsilon >0$ and take $T_ j=\varepsilon \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j$. Each $T_ j$ is smooth and closed and represents the zero Bott–Chern class. Moreover,

\[ T_ j+\varepsilon \omega =\varepsilon (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)>0, \]

and its top mass $\varepsilon ^ n\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^ n$ tends to $+\infty $. The functions $v_ j$ on the Hopf threefold are provided by theorem 8.0.1. $\square$

The currents $T_ j$ are smooth, so requiring analytic singularities with smooth remainder does not exclude them. Moreover, the supremum is infinite for each fixed $\varepsilon $, so letting $\varepsilon $ tend to zero afterwards cannot produce a finite limit. By contrast, if $\omega $ is Kähler and $T=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ is smooth, Stokes' theorem gives

\[ \int _ X(T+\varepsilon \omega )^ n =\varepsilon ^ n\int _ X\omega ^ n\longrightarrow 0. \]

On the other hand, the zero class is nef, its non-nef locus is empty, and its only positive representative is the zero current. Indeed, a positive representative $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$ would make $u$ a global plurisubharmonic function on a compact manifold, hence constant. Thus (28.2) measures the nonlinear mass of the Hermitian error family rather than positivity of the closed class.

A numerical theory of restricted volumes in a Hermitian background must therefore either control the approximating family or use a different invariant. Two qualitative loci avoid this supremum. The non-Hermitian locus of a big background is defined through its strict currents, whose exterior derivative is prescribed by the background; Dang's null locus tests failure of bigness after restriction to subvarieties. The singularities allowed in the first definition, and the equality of the two loci for nef and big backgrounds, are recalled in section 33.3.

28.6 Finite restricted masses of closed classes

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The nonclosed error in proposition 28.5.1 destroys the cohomological bound on masses. On a compact Kähler manifold, by contrast, the non-pluripolar mass of a positive current in a closed class is bounded by a top intersection number. Pulling a class back to a Kähler resolution of a subvariety therefore gives a finite invariant: the supremum of the non-pluripolar masses of positive currents in the pulled-back class. It measures positivity along every subvariety, including subvarieties along which a given modification of $X$ fails to be an isomorphism.

In this section and the next, $X$ belongs to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$. Then every irreducible reduced subvariety $Y\subset X$ has a resolution $\pi :Z\to Y$ with $Z$ a compact Kähler manifold: closed subspaces of a space in class $\mathcal C$ are again in class $\mathcal C$, and the Kähler modification theorem applies to $Y$. These classical facts are discussed in section 34.16. In particular, a Kähler model of $Y$ is obtained directly, not as a proper transform under one fixed modification of $X$.

Two such resolutions $Z_1,Z_2$ of $Y$ admit a common Kähler refinement. Indeed, apply embedded resolution to the closure in $Z_1\times Z_2$ of the graph of the induced bimeromorphic map. The resulting strict transform is smooth, and it is Kähler because the ambient resolution is a sequence of blowups of the compact Kähler product along smooth centers. A blowup of a compact Kähler manifold along a smooth center is again Kähler: add to the pulled-back Kähler form a small positive multiple of a curvature form of $\mathcal O(-E)$, which is positive in the vertical directions along the exceptional divisor $E$.

For a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $S$ on a compact Kähler manifold $Z$ of dimension $k>0$, write $\langle S^ k\rangle $ for its non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure, that is, $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\eta (u)$ for any decomposition $S=\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $\eta $ smooth and closed (theorem 6.1.2). For a class $b\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R)$, set

28.3
\begin{equation} \mathcal R_ Z(b)=\sup _{\substack {S\in b\\ S\geq 0}} \int _ Z\langle S^ k\rangle , \qquad \sup \varnothing =0. \end{equation}

For a class $a\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ and an irreducible reduced subvariety $Y\subset X$ of dimension $k>0$, let $j:Y\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion, write $f=j\circ \pi $, and define

28.4
\begin{equation} \mathcal R(a;Y)=\mathcal R_ Z(f^*a). \end{equation}

Proposition 28.6.1. The quantities in (28.3) and (28.4) are finite, and the latter is independent of the Kähler resolution. If $\eta \in b$ is smooth, $\kappa $ is Kähler and $C>0$ satisfies $C\kappa -\eta >0$, then

28.5
\begin{equation} \mathcal R_ Z(b+t\{ \kappa \} ) \leq (C+t)^ k\int _ Z\kappa ^ k \qquad (t\geq 0). \end{equation}

Let $\mu :X'\to X$ be a modification and $Y'$ the proper transform of a subvariety $Y$ that meets the locus where $\mu $ is an isomorphism. Then

\[ \mathcal R(\mu ^*a;Y')=\mathcal R(a;Y). \]

Proof. Step 1: Kähler forms. Let $\lambda $ be a Kähler form on $Z$ and $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(Z,\lambda )$ with $q\leq 0$. The truncations $q_ j=\max (q,-j)$ have mass $\int _ Z\lambda ^ k$ by theorem 2.2.2, or by Stokes' theorem when $k=1$. Restricting their Monge–Ampère measures to $\{ q>-j\} $ and passing to the increasing limit gives

\[ \int _ Z\langle (\lambda +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ k\rangle \leq \int _ Z\lambda ^ k. \]

The smooth current $\lambda $ attains equality.

Step 2: the mass bound. Put $A=C\kappa -\eta $. For a positive current $S$ in $b+t\{ \kappa \} $, the current $S+A$ is positive and belongs to $(C+t)\{ \kappa \} $. Since local non-pluripolar products are positive and multilinear, $\langle S^ k\rangle \leq \langle (S+A)^ k\rangle $, and Step 1 applied to the Kähler form $(C+t)\kappa $ yields (28.5). In particular $\mathcal R_ Z(b)$ is finite. The definition involves only the class, so the choice of its smooth representative or of the comparison form does not change the supremum.

Step 3: modifications. Let $\rho :W\to Z$ be a modification between compact Kähler manifolds. By proposition 1.22.12, every positive current in $\rho ^*b$ is the pullback of a unique positive current in $b$. Their non-pluripolar measures correspond on the locus where $\rho $ is an isomorphism, and neither charges the exceptional analytic sets. Hence their total masses agree and

\[ \mathcal R_ W(\rho ^*b)=\mathcal R_ Z(b). \]

Applying this identity on a common Kähler refinement shows that $\mathcal R(a;Y)$ is independent of the resolution. The same argument compares resolutions of $Y$ and $Y'$, since their maps to $X$ agree on a dense open set and therefore everywhere on a common refinement. $\square$

On a compact Kähler manifold, positivity of this mass has a classical interpretation. We use two facts from Kähler pluripotential theory, discussed in section 34.16: a class containing a positive current with positive non-pluripolar top mass is big, and in a big class a current with minimal singularities attains the volume, the largest non-pluripolar top mass; for a nef class this volume is the top intersection number. For a nef class, the Kähler current used here can also be obtained from the following normalized diagonal construction.

Proposition 28.6.2. Let $Z$ be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $k>0$ and $b\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Z,\mathbb R)$. Then

\[ \mathcal R_ Z(b)>0\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad b\text{ is big}. \]

If $b$ is nef, then

28.6
\begin{equation} \mathcal R_ Z(b)=\int _ Zb^ k. \end{equation}

Suppose that $b$ is nef with $I=\int _ Zb^ k>0$. Fix a Kähler form $\kappa $, let $p_1,p_2:Z\times Z\to Z$ be the projections and $\Delta \subset Z\times Z$ the diagonal, and put

\[ M=k\int _ Zb^{k-1}\wedge \{ \kappa \} . \]

Then there are $d>0$ and a closed positive $(k,k)$-current $\Theta $ in $(p_1^*b+p_2^*b)^ k$ with $\Theta \geq d[\Delta ]$ such that

28.7
\begin{equation} K_ b=M^{-1}(p_1)_*(\Theta \wedge p_2^*\kappa )\in b, \qquad K_ b\geq (d/M)\kappa . \end{equation}

Proof. If $\mathcal R_ Z(b)>0$, some positive representative has positive non-pluripolar top mass, so $b$ is big by the first fact above. Conversely, if $S\in b$ satisfies $S\geq \delta \kappa $ with $\delta >0$, then $S-\delta \kappa $ is positive and closed, and expanding the non-pluripolar product gives $\langle S^ k\rangle \geq \delta ^ k\kappa ^ k$. Hence $\mathcal R_ Z(b)>0$.

Suppose now that $b$ is nef. For $t>0$, the class $b+t\{ \kappa \} $ is Kähler. Adding $t\kappa $ to an arbitrary positive representative of $b$ and using the Kähler mass bound of Step 1 in the proof of proposition 28.6.1 yields

\[ \mathcal R_ Z(b)\leq \int _ Z(b+t\{ \kappa \} )^ k. \]

Letting $t$ decrease to zero gives the upper bound in (28.6). In particular, equality holds if $I=0$.

Let $I>0$, and choose $L>0$ such that $L\{ \kappa \} -b$ is Kähler. Since intersection numbers of nef classes are nonnegative,

\[ 0\leq \int _ Zb^{k-1}\wedge (L\{ \kappa \} -b) =LM/k-I, \]

so $M>0$. The class $B=p_1^*b+p_2^*b$ is nef and satisfies

\[ \int _{Z\times Z}B^{2k}={2k\choose k}I^2>0. \]

The classical mass concentration theorem along the diagonal gives $\Theta \in B^ k$ with $\Theta \geq d[\Delta ]$ for some $d>0$. When $B^ k\wedge p_2^*\{ \kappa \} $ is pushed forward by $p_1$, only the term containing exactly one factor $p_1^*b$ survives. Thus

\[ (p_1)_*(B^ k\wedge p_2^*\{ \kappa \} )=Mb, \qquad (p_1)_*([\Delta ]\wedge p_2^*\kappa )=\kappa . \]

Since $\Theta -d[\Delta ]$ is positive, these identities prove (28.7); the $\partial \bar\partial $-lemma for currents on the compact Kähler manifold $Z$ identifies the cohomology class computed in this way with the Bott–Chern class $b$. Therefore $b$ is big, and by the second fact above a current with minimal singularities in $b$ has non-pluripolar mass $\int _ Zb^ k$. This gives the remaining lower bound. $\square$

When $Y$ is smooth, the criterion descends to $Y$. Write $a_ Y=j^*a$ for the restricted class, let $\pi :Z\to Y$ be a Kähler modification with Kähler form $\kappa $, and choose a Hermitian metric $h$ on $Y$ and $C>0$ with $\pi ^*h\leq C\kappa $. If $K\in \pi ^*a_ Y$ satisfies $K\geq \delta \kappa $ with $\delta >0$, then descent of potentials (proposition 1.22.12) makes $\pi _*K$ a positive representative of $a_ Y$, and

28.8
\begin{equation} \pi _*K\geq (\delta /C)\pi _*\pi ^*h=(\delta /C)h. \end{equation}

Conversely, let $S$ be a strict positive representative of $a_ Y$. On a coordinate ball in the locus where $\pi $ is an isomorphism, $\pi ^*S$ dominates a positive multiple of the closed form $\kappa $, so the local expansion of the non-pluripolar product shows that $\pi ^*S$ has positive non-pluripolar mass on that ball. Consequently, $\mathcal R(a;Y)>0$ if and only if the restricted class $a_ Y$ is big. The Hermitian metric $h$ enters only through inequalities between currents; the products are compared with the closed form $\kappa $.

Proposition 28.6.3. Let $W$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $k>0$, let $g:W\to X$ be holomorphic, and suppose $\dim g(W)<k$. Then for every class $a\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$,

\[ \mathcal R_ W(g^*a)=0. \]

Proof. If the image is a point, then $g^*a=0$ and (28.6) gives the assertion. Otherwise, choose a Kähler resolution $r:Q\to g(W)$ and resolve the graph of the meromorphic lift of $g$ to $Q$. This gives a Kähler modification $\rho :\widehat W\to W$ and a holomorphic map $F:\widehat W\to Q$ with $g\circ \rho =r\circ F$, where $r$ is also viewed as a map into $X$.

Choose a smooth representative $\eta \in a$, a Kähler form $\kappa _ Q$ on $Q$, and $C>0$ such that $C\kappa _ Q-r^*\eta \geq 0$. Since $\rho ^*g^*\eta =F^*r^*\eta $, the forms

\[ P=CF^*\kappa _ Q, \qquad A=P-\rho ^*g^*\eta \]

are closed and semipositive. Since $\dim Q<k$, we have $P^ k=0$. For any positive current $S\in g^*a$, the current $\rho ^*S+A$ is positive and lies in the nef class $\{ P\} $. Hence

\[ \int _{\widehat W}\langle (\rho ^*S)^ k\rangle \leq \int _{\widehat W}\langle (\rho ^*S+A)^ k\rangle \leq \mathcal R_{\widehat W}(\{ P\} ) =\int _{\widehat W}P^ k=0. \]

Modification invariance (Step 3 in the proof of proposition 28.6.1) now gives the result. If the class has no positive representative, its invariant is zero by definition. $\square$

Corollary 28.6.4. Let $a$ be a nef class on $X$. For every positive-dimensional irreducible reduced subvariety $Y\subset X$,

\[ \mathcal R(a;Y)=\int _ Ya^{\dim Y}. \]

If $\int _ Xa^ n>0$, then

28.9
\begin{equation} \bigcup _{\substack {Y\subset X\ \mathrm{irreducible},\ \dim Y>0\\ \mathcal R(a;Y)=0}}Y =\operatorname {Null}(a)=E_{\mathrm{nK}}(a). \end{equation}

Proof. The pullback of $a$ to a Kähler resolution of $Y$ is nef: pull back smooth representatives with arbitrarily small negative part, and bound the pullback of $\omega $ by a multiple of a Kähler form on the resolution. Since the intersection number $\int _ Ya^{\dim Y}$ is defined through the same pullback, (28.6) gives the first assertion. The first equality in (28.9) then follows from the definition of the null locus, and the second is the classical theorem on nef and big classes recalled earlier in this chapter. $\square$

When $X$ is Kähler and $a$ is nef, the numerical restricted volume (28.1) along $Y$ is also $\int _ Ya^{\dim Y}$, so the two restricted invariants agree. Outside the nef case this comparison does not identify them: (28.4) is a supremum over all positive currents on a resolution of $Y$, and these currents need not extend to $X$.

28.7 Divisorial traces with bounded remainder

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The invariant $\mathcal R(a;Y)$ is a supremum over all positive currents in a class. The trace of a given potential on $Y$, on the other hand, carries a specified singularity, and its non-pluripolar mass can be smaller. Suppose that on a resolution the trace becomes a divisorial logarithmic term plus a bounded remainder. The non-pluripolar product then ignores the divisor and sees only the residual current, which has bounded potentials. The class of this residual current is nef, and its top intersection number computes the mass.

We keep the assumption that $X$ belongs to class $\mathcal C$. Let $Y\subset X$ be an irreducible reduced subvariety of dimension $k>0$, let $\alpha _0$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\alpha _0)$ be such that its pointwise restriction $v=u|_ Y$ is not identically $-\infty $. On a Kähler resolution $\pi :Z\to Y$, with $f=j\circ \pi :Z\to X$ as in section 28.6, put $\alpha _ Z=f^*\alpha _0$ and $v_\pi =u\circ f$. When $Y$ is smooth, one may instead start from a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha _ Y$ on $Y$ and $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\alpha _ Y)$, and put $\alpha _ Z=\pi ^*\alpha _ Y$ and $v_\pi =v\circ \pi $. In both cases $v_\pi $ is the composition of a given potential with a map; the intrinsic multiplier-ideal trace of chapter 19 is a different construction.

Suppose that on one such resolution

28.10
\begin{equation} v_\pi =\ell +w, \qquad \ell =\sum _{j=1}^ N c_ j\log |s_ j|_{h_ j}^2, \qquad w\text{ bounded}, \end{equation}

where $c_ j>0$, the $D_ j$ are prime divisors on $Z$, $s_ j$ is the canonical section of $\mathcal O(D_ j)$, and $h_ j$ is a smooth Hermitian metric on $\mathcal O(D_ j)$; the empty sum $\ell =0$ is allowed. Write $\Gamma =\sum _ jc_ jD_ j$ and $\gamma _ j=c_1(\mathcal O(D_ j),h_ j)$ for the curvature forms, and define the residual form and the residual class

28.11
\begin{equation} \eta _ v=\alpha _ Z-\sum _ jc_ j\gamma _ j, \qquad b_ v=\{ \eta _ v\} =\{ \alpha _ Z\} -\{ [\Gamma ]\} . \end{equation}

For singular $Y$, all assertions below are made on its resolutions.

Lemma 28.7.1. The current $R_ v=\eta _ v+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ is positive and has bounded local potentials, and its class $b_ v$ is nef. Moreover,

28.12
\begin{equation} \int _ Z\langle (\alpha _ Z+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\pi )^ k\rangle =\int _ ZR_ v^ k =\int _ Zb_ v^ k =\mathcal R_ Z(b_ v). \end{equation}

If $v_1,\ldots ,v_ k$ satisfy the same conditions on $Z$, possibly for different closed backgrounds, then

28.13
\begin{equation} \int _ ZR_{v_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge R_{v_ k} =\int _ Zb_{v_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge b_{v_ k}. \end{equation}

Proof. Off the support $|\Gamma |$, we have $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ell =-\sum _ jc_ j\gamma _ j$, so the current $\alpha _ Z+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\pi $ equals $\eta _ v+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ there and is positive. After the addition of a smooth local potential for $\eta _ v$, the bounded function $w$ is plurisubharmonic off $|\Gamma |$. By lemma 1.3.7, it extends across the divisor as a bounded plurisubharmonic function that agrees almost everywhere with the original one; we use this extension to represent $w$. Thus $R_ v\geq 0$ on $Z$, and the Poincaré–Lelong formula gives

\[ \alpha _ Z+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\pi =[\Gamma ]+R_ v. \]

Since $w$ is bounded, $b_ v$ is nef by lemma 23.6.1. The non-pluripolar measure on the left of (28.12) agrees with $R_ v^ k$ off the divisor, and neither measure charges the divisor. This gives the first equality.

Fix a Kähler form $\kappa $ on $Z$. For all sufficiently large real $t$, the smooth closed form $\eta _ v+t\kappa $ is Kähler, and $w$ is a bounded potential for it. Therefore

\[ \int _ Z(R_ v+t\kappa )^ k=\int _ Z(\eta _ v+t\kappa )^ k \]

by theorem 2.2.2, or by Stokes' theorem when $k=1$. Both sides are polynomials in $t$, so their constant coefficients agree; this gives $\int _ ZR_ v^ k=\int _ Zb_ v^ k$. The last equality in (28.12) is (28.6). Applying the same polynomial argument to positive linear combinations of the $R_{v_ i}$ and comparing coefficients gives (28.13). $\square$

We denote the common value in (28.12) by $\mathfrak r(v;Y)$ and call it the trace mass of $v$ on $Y$. When $Y$ is smooth, it is also the non-pluripolar mass of the trace current on $Y$ itself:

\[ \mathfrak r(v;Y)=\int _ Y\langle (\alpha _ Y+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ k\rangle , \]

where $\alpha _ Y=\alpha _0|_ Y$ when $v$ is a restriction. Indeed, the currents on $Z$ and on $Y$ correspond on the locus where $\pi $ is an isomorphism, and their non-pluripolar measures give no mass to its analytic complement. A change of variables therefore identifies the total masses.

Theorem 28.7.2. The number $\mathfrak r(v;Y)$ does not depend on the divisor metrics or on the Kähler resolution carrying (28.10). If two potentials in the same closed background have the same ordinary singularity type and each has a decomposition of the form (28.10), then their residual classes agree on a common refinement, and in particular their trace masses are equal. Residual classes add when the traces and their backgrounds are added, and their mixed masses are given by (28.13).

Proof. Changing a divisor metric changes $\ell $ by a smooth function, $w$ by its negative, and $\eta _ v$ by the corresponding $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-term. Thus $R_ v$ is unchanged. The same cancellation occurs when a smooth change of the background is accompanied by the opposite change of the potential.

Let $\rho :W\to Z$ be a modification with $W$ Kähler. Pulling back the decomposition, with the total divisor $\rho ^*\Gamma $ including all exceptional coefficients, gives a decomposition on $W$ with bounded remainder $w\circ \rho $, residual current $\rho ^*R_ v$ and nef residual class $\rho ^*b_ v$. Its mass is

\[ \int _ W(\rho ^*b_ v)^ k=\int _ Zb_ v^ k. \]

For two decompositions on different resolutions, pass to a common Kähler refinement and pull both back in this way. At a generic smooth point of a prime component of the union of the two divisors, the difference of the logarithmic terms is $(a_1-a_2)\log |t|^2+O(1)$, where $t$ is a local defining coordinate of the component and $a_1,a_2$ are its two coefficients. The two potentials have bounded difference, and the resulting pair of inequalities forces $a_1=a_2$. Hence the total divisors coincide, and so do the residual classes in the common background. Finally, adding two decompositions adds both the divisors and the bounded remainders. The residual class of the sum is therefore the sum of the nef residual classes, and (28.13) computes its mixed terms. $\square$

Proposition 28.7.3. Suppose that $\mathfrak r(v;Y)>0$. Then the class $\{ \alpha _ Z\} $ contains a Kähler current $\alpha _ Z+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p$ whose potential satisfies $p\leq v_\pi +A$ for some constant $A$. If $b_ v$ has a smooth positive representative on a resolution carrying (28.10), then on that resolution $p$ can be chosen with $p\sim v_\pi $, that is, with $p-v_\pi $ bounded. When $Y$ is smooth, the Kähler current and the corresponding inequalities between potentials descend to $Y$.

Proof. Positive trace mass means $\int _ Zb_ v^ k>0$. Since $b_ v$ is nef, (28.7) gives a current $K=\eta _ v+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\geq \delta \kappa $ in $b_ v$, where $\kappa $ is a Kähler form and $\delta >0$. The current

\[ [\Gamma ]+K=\alpha _ Z+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\ell +a) \]

is then a Kähler current. Since $a$ is bounded above on $Z$ and $w$ is bounded below, its potential $p=\ell +a$ satisfies $p\leq \ell +w+A=v_\pi +A$ for a constant $A$. If $b_ v$ has a smooth positive representative, $a$ can be chosen smooth, and then $p-v_\pi =a-w$ is bounded.

For smooth $Y$, take $\alpha _ Y=\alpha _0|_ Y$ when $v$ is a restriction. By proposition 1.22.12, $p=q\circ \pi $ for some $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\alpha _ Y)$. The current $\alpha _ Y+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$ is strict by (28.8), and since $\pi $ is surjective, each inequality between potentials descends. The hypothesis of a smooth positive representative refers to the specified resolution: the pullback of a Kähler class under a further blowup need not be Kähler. $\square$

The trace mass can vanish even when the restricted class is big, because the non-pluripolar product ignores the divisorial part of the trace.

Example 28.7.4. Let $k\geq 1$, let $Y\simeq \mathbb P^ k$ be a hyperplane in $X=\mathbb P^{k+1}$, and let another hyperplane $H_ X\subset X$ cut out $H=H_ X\cap Y$. Let $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form, normalized by $\int _ Y\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^ k=1$, and let $s_{H_ X}$ be a section of $\mathcal O(1)$ defining $H_ X$, measured with the Fubini–Study metric. For $c>0$ and $d\geq 0$, take $\alpha _0=(c+d)\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ on $X$ and $u=c\log |s_{H_ X}|^2$. The restriction $v=u|_ Y$ is not identically $-\infty $, and

\[ \alpha _0|_ Y+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v=c[H]+d\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}|_ Y, \qquad \mathfrak r(v;Y)=d^ k, \qquad \mathcal R(\{ \alpha _0\} ;Y)=(c+d)^ k. \]

Thus for $d=0$ the trace mass is zero, although the restricted invariant is positive. The divisor $c[H]$ is a nonzero part of the trace current, but the non-pluripolar top product discards it.

The next example shows that the inequality $p\leq v_\pi +A$ in proposition 28.7.3 cannot in general be improved to $p\sim v_\pi $, even for a bounded trace.

Example 28.7.5. Let $\mu :Y\to \mathbb P^2$ be the blowup of a point, with exceptional curve $E$, let $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^2$ with total volume one, and put $\alpha _ Y=\mu ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ and $v=0$. The decomposition (28.10) holds on $Z=Y$ with no divisor, the residual class $\{ \alpha _ Y\} $ is nef and big, and

\[ \mathfrak r(0;Y)=\mathcal R(\{ \alpha _ Y\} ;Y)=1. \]

Nevertheless, there is no bounded $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\alpha _ Y)$ for which $\alpha _ Y+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \delta \kappa $ with $\delta >0$ and $\kappa $ Kähler. Indeed, since $q$ is bounded, its restriction to $E$ is defined, and the disk criterion of lemma 1.22.1 gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(q|_ E)\geq \delta \kappa |_ E, \]

because $\alpha _ Y|_ E=0$. Integration over the compact curve $E$ contradicts Stokes' theorem. Thus positive trace mass gives the one-sided comparison of proposition 28.7.3, but a Kähler current in the class may need additional singularities.

A subvariety contained in the null locus can nevertheless have positive restricted invariant; the union in (28.9) is needed to recover the locus.

Example 28.7.6. Let $\mu :X\to \mathbb P^3$ be the blowup along a line $L$, and set $a=\mu ^*\{ \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}\} $, where $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ has total volume one; the class $a$ is nef and $\int _ Xa^3=1$. Since $N_{L/\mathbb P^3}\simeq \mathcal O(1)\oplus \mathcal O(1)$, the exceptional divisor is $E=\mathbb P(N_{L/\mathbb P^3})\simeq \mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, and on $E$ the map $\mu $ is the projection to $L$. Hence $\int _ Ea^2=0$, whereas a section $Y$ of $E\to L$ satisfies $\int _ Ya=1$. Every irreducible subvariety not contained in $E$ has positive intersection number, because $\mu ^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ is positive on its nonempty open part outside $E$. By corollary 28.6.4,

\[ E_{\mathrm{nK}}(a)=E, \qquad \mathcal R(a;E)=0, \qquad \mathcal R(a;Y)=1. \]

The union of the subvarieties with zero invariant is the whole null locus $E$, although the curve $Y\subset E$ has positive invariant. Positivity along one subvariety does not place it outside the null locus.

28.8 Divisorial components at the threshold

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

A divisorial component of a Hermitian current consumes part of its Gauduchon trace but contributes no density to its non-pluripolar measure. It can therefore lower the largest coefficient in a Monge–Ampère subsolution inequality. At the maximal coefficient, every weak limit of a maximizing sequence of Gauduchon tests annihilates the class of the divisor, and uniform integrability along the sequence makes the limit a maximizer.

In this section $X$ is again an arbitrary compact complex manifold, now of dimension $n\geq 2$, with the Hermitian form $\omega $. Fix a smooth strictly positive probability volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ and a nonzero function $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, and write $p=f^{1/n}$ for its root density. Let $b=b_\omega (f)$ be its subsolution threshold: by theorem 14.27.2, it is the largest $a\geq 0$ for which some $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq af\, \mathrm{d}V$, where $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega $ is the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 6.1.2. Such a $u$ with $a=b$ is a threshold subsolution in the sense of section 14.27. We also use the following notation from section 4.4, section 26.9. The set $\mathscr G_\omega $ consists of the positive $(n-1,n-1)$-currents $T$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$ and $\int _ X\omega \wedge T=1$, and $\mathscr C_\omega \subset \mathscr G_\omega $ is the weak closure of its smooth strictly positive members, the normalized powers $\gamma ^{n-1}$ of Gauduchon metrics $\gamma $. For $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, the determinant measure of $T$ has the form $\Gamma _0(T)=k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$ by theorem 26.8.1, and $D_ p(T)=\int _ Xp k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$. The root norm $\rho _\omega (p)=b^{-1/n}$ is the supremum of $D_ p$ over $\mathscr G_\omega $, and $\mathcal D_\omega ^0$ is the closure of the bounded functions in the space of roots with finite root norm. Finally, $\kappa _\omega (\beta )$ is the linear positivity threshold of a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\beta $ from corollary 4.3.2, the infimum over Gauduchon metrics $\gamma $ of $\int _ X\beta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}/\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$.

Proposition 28.8.1. Let $c>0$ be real and let $E$ be an effective real divisor on $X$. Suppose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq c f\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq [E]. \]

Let $\beta _ E$ be a smooth closed representative of the Bott–Chern class of $[E]$, and put $\kappa =\kappa _\omega (\beta _ E)$. Then $b>0$, $0\leq \kappa <1$ and

28.14
\begin{equation} c\leq b(1-\kappa )^ n. \end{equation}

Suppose that $c=b$, and let $(T_ j)$ be a smooth maximizing sequence for $D_ p$, that is, a sequence of smooth strictly positive forms $T_ j\in \mathscr G_\omega $ with $D_ p(T_ j)\to \rho _\omega (p)$. Then

\[ \int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T_ j=\int _ X[E]\wedge T_ j\longrightarrow 0. \]

Every weak cluster current $T$ therefore satisfies $\int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T=0$, and the class of $E$ is not big. If the densities $p k_{T_ j}$ of the chosen sequence are uniformly integrable, every such cluster current maximizes $D_ p$. In particular, this holds for every smooth maximizing sequence when $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$.

Proof. By theorem 14.27.2, $b\geq c>0$. Let $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ be smooth and strictly positive. By lemma 6.4.2, the measure

\[ R=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T-c^{1/n}p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \]

is positive, and its mass is $1-c^{1/n}D_ p(T)$. The absolutely continuous term gives no mass to the support of $E$, whereas $[E]\wedge T$ is concentrated there. Restricting $R$ to the support of $E$ and using $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq [E]$ therefore gives

\[ \int _ X[E]\wedge T\leq R(X)=1-c^{1/n}D_ p(T). \]

Let $\ell _ E$ be a global logarithmic potential, so that $[E]=\beta _ E+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ell _ E$ by the Poincaré–Lelong formula. Since $\ell _ E$ is locally integrable and $T$ is smooth with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$, Stokes' theorem for currents identifies the left side with $\int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T$. This integral is nonnegative for every such $T$, so $\kappa \geq 0$; since $D_ p(T)>0$, we also get $\kappa <1$. Taking the supremum of $D_ p(T)$, which is $\rho _\omega (p)=b^{-1/n}$, proves (28.14).

Now let $c=b$, and let $(T_ j)$ be a smooth maximizing sequence. Then the right side of the preceding bound tends to zero. Since pairing with the fixed smooth form $\beta _ E$ is continuous under weak convergence of currents, every cluster current satisfies $\int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T=0$. Moreover, (28.14) with $c=b$ forces $\kappa =0$, so the class of $E$ is not big by corollary 4.3.2. If the densities along the sequence are uniformly integrable, proposition 26.9.3 shows that every cluster current maximizes $D_ p$. When $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$, theorem 26.9.1 gives this uniform integrability for every sequence. $\square$

Geometrically, at the threshold $c=b$ with $E\ne 0$, the equation $\int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T=0$ defines a nonempty proper exposed face of $\mathscr C_\omega $. Indeed, $\mathscr C_\omega $ is compact and convex, and the pairing with $\beta _ E$ is continuous and nonnegative on it and strictly positive on every smooth strictly positive member. The proposition places every cluster current of a smooth maximizing sequence on this face. The statement concerns $\mathscr C_\omega $, which can be strictly smaller than $\mathscr G_\omega $, as the next example shows. Moreover, for a singular $T$ the smooth pairing $\int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T$ does not define a product $[E]\wedge T$.

The difference between $\mathscr C_\omega $ and $\mathscr G_\omega $ comes from positive currents that pair negatively with a class which is nonnegative on all smooth tests. Integration currents along negative curves give such examples.

Example 28.8.2. The weak closure $\mathscr C_\omega $ of the smooth strictly positive normalized Gauduchon forms can be smaller than $\mathscr G_\omega $, even on a Kähler surface. Let $X$ be the blowup of $\mathbb P^2$ at one point and $C$ its exceptional curve. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ and a smooth closed representative $\beta _ C$ of the class of $[C]$. Then

\[ T_ C=\frac{[C]}{\int _ C\omega }\in \mathscr G_\omega , \qquad \int _ X\beta _ C\wedge T_ C=\frac{-1}{\int _ C\omega }<0, \]

whereas every smooth strictly positive $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ satisfies $\int _ X\beta _ C\wedge T=\int _ C T>0$. In particular $T_ C\notin \mathscr C_\omega $.

Proof. The integration current $[C]$ is positive and closed, so its normalization belongs to $\mathscr G_\omega $. Its pairing with the smooth form $\beta _ C$ is the self-intersection $C^2=-1$, computed in example 26.5.2. For smooth $T$, the Poincaré–Lelong formula and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$ give the second identity. Since the pairing with the fixed smooth form $\beta _ C$ is continuous under weak convergence of currents, it is nonnegative on the weak closure of the smooth tests, and this closure does not contain $T_ C$. $\square$

Let $m_\omega (\beta )$ be the minimum of $\int _ X\beta \wedge T$ over all $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, as in section 26.20, whereas by (4.4) the threshold $\kappa _\omega (\beta )$ is the minimum over $\mathscr C_\omega $. Since $[C]$ is a positive representative of the class of $\beta _ C$, the two thresholds satisfy

\[ m_\omega (\beta _ C)\leq -\frac1{\int _ C\omega }<0 \leq \kappa _\omega (\beta _ C). \]

Thus extending the minimum in (4.4) to all normalized Gauduchon currents changes its value in this example.

We return to the general setting $(X,\omega ,f)$ of this section. The next two corollaries assume uniform integrability along one smooth maximizing sequence. The condition $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$ suffices, since it gives $b>0$ and uniform integrability over the whole of $\mathscr G_\omega $.

Corollary 28.8.3. Assume $b>0$ and that $D_ p$ has a smooth maximizing sequence $(T_ j)$ with uniformly integrable densities $p k_{T_ j}$. Let $E$ be an effective real divisor whose class has a smooth semipositive representative $\beta _ E$. If

\[ \int _{\{ \beta _ E=0\} }f\, \mathrm{d}V=0, \]

then no threshold subsolution $u$ for $f$ satisfies $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq [E]$.

Proof. Suppose such a subsolution exists. By proposition 28.8.1, there is a maximizing current $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ with $\int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T=0$. We use the matrix-density notation of section 26.6: $\sigma _ T=\omega \wedge T$ is the trace measure of $T$, $P_ T$ is its matrix density and $B$ is the coefficient matrix of $\beta _ E$. Since both matrices are positive semidefinite, the vanishing pairing gives $\operatorname {tr}(BP_ T)=0$ at $\sigma _ T$-almost every point. Wherever $B\ne 0$, this forces $P_ T$ to be singular: a positive definite $P_ T$ would have strictly positive trace against the nonzero positive semidefinite matrix $B$. The determinant measure $\Gamma _0(T)$ vanishes where $P_ T$ is singular, so it is concentrated on $\{ \beta _ E=0\} $. By theorem 26.8.1, $\Gamma _0(T)=k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$, and the hypothesis on $f$ gives $p k_ T=0$ almost everywhere. This contradicts $D_ p(T)=\rho _\omega (p)>0$. $\square$

Corollary 28.8.4. Assume $b>0$ and that $D_ p$ has a smooth maximizing sequence $(T_ j)$ with uniformly integrable densities $p k_{T_ j}$. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ be a threshold subsolution for $f$, and suppose that $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq [E]$ for an effective real divisor $E$ whose Bott–Chern class is nef. Let $\beta _ E$ be any smooth closed representative of that class. Then for every smooth strictly positive probability measure $\mu $, the Gauduchon root volume of definition 26.1.1 satisfies

\[ \lambda _\mu (\beta _ E)=0. \]

Proof. By proposition 28.8.1, there is a maximizing current $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ with $\int _ X\beta _ E\wedge T=0$. If $\lambda _\mu (\beta _ E)>0$, then proposition 26.20.2 gives $\Gamma _0(T)=0$, hence $D_{f^{1/n}}(T)=0$, contradicting the positivity of its maximal value. Since the nef class of $\beta _ E$ has $\lambda _\mu (\beta _ E)\geq 0$, the conclusion follows for each $\mu $. $\square$

The semipositive criterion and the nef-volume criterion detect different obstructions. On the Hopf threefold, the class of a hyperplane divisor has zero variational volume, so corollary 28.8.4 says nothing about it, but its nowhere vanishing semipositive representative excludes a divisorial component at the threshold.

To see this, let $X$ be the Hopf threefold with its Hopf metric $\omega =\alpha +\zeta $ (chapter 8, section 26.22), where $\alpha $ is the pullback of the Fubini–Study form under the elliptic fibration $X\to \mathbb P^2$, and let $H$ be the inverse image of a projective hyperplane. The class of $H$ is represented by $\alpha $, which is semipositive of rank two everywhere. Its variational volume vanishes for every smooth positive volume form $\mu $: the metrics $\gamma _ t=\alpha +t\zeta $, $t>0$, are Gauduchon by the proof of proposition 26.22.1, and since $\alpha ^3=\zeta ^2=0$ and $\omega ^3=3\alpha ^2\wedge \zeta $, one has $\int _ X\alpha \wedge \gamma _ t^2=\tfrac {2t}{3}\int _ X\omega ^3$ and $\gamma _ t^3=t\, \omega ^3$. Hence the quotient in definition 26.1.1 is $O(t^{1/3})$ as $t\downarrow 0$. By corollary 8.7.2, proposition 14.31.5, one has $\mathcal D_\omega ^0=L^3$ on this example. Hence, for every nonzero nonnegative $L^1$ density, a threshold subsolution has zero generic Lelong coefficient along $H$. Otherwise divisorial removal, as in proposition 26.21.1, would give $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq a[H]$ for some $a>0$, contradicting corollary 28.8.3 applied to $aH$. This exclusion uses no bounded mass property. It is also stronger here than nonbigness of the class of $H$: the residual formulas of proposition 26.22.1 allow a positive coefficient along $H$ when the equation constant is smaller than the threshold.

These conditions concern the maximal global coefficient. Whether threshold subsolutions with divisorial components can be constructed from the resulting annihilating currents is open; the threshold within a prescribed singularity type is a different problem.

28.9 Currents on the elliptic threefold

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

On the elliptic threefold of section 26.18, closed classes can be described completely. The product of the two horizontal forms is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact, so every positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(1,1)$-current has zero vertical component, and the fiberwise descent of lemma 4.7.4 identifies it with a current on the base. Closedness descends as well. The closed positive cones, the singularity envelopes and the spaces of sections selected by multiplier ideals are therefore all computed on the base surface.

Let $X$ be the compact threefold of section 26.18, and let

\[ \pi :X\longrightarrow B=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1 \]

be its elliptic fibration. Write $\sigma _1,\sigma _2$ for the Fubini–Study forms of the two factors, each of integral one, and put $\alpha =\pi ^*\sigma _1$ and $\beta =\pi ^*\sigma _2$. The product coordinates of that construction show that $\pi $ is locally a holomorphic product with fiber the elliptic curve $E$. The logarithmic ratio $t$ of that construction is defined only on the covering space, but its differentials descend to $X$. We take for $\omega $ the balanced metric $\omega =\alpha +\beta +D$, where $D=\mathrm{d}t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t$. By proposition 26.18.7, it satisfies

\[ \alpha \wedge \beta =\tfrac 12\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega . \]

The arguments below use this identity, not the vanishing of the lower Monge–Ampère mass.

Lemma 28.9.1. Every positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed real $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $X$ has the form

\[ T=\pi ^*S \]

for a unique positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(1,1)$-current $S$ on $B$. Moreover, $T$ is closed if and only if $S$ is closed. Here $\pi ^*S$ is the pullback of currents under the holomorphic submersion $\pi $.

Proof. The measure $T\wedge \alpha \wedge \beta $ is positive. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$, Stokes' theorem for currents gives

\[ \int _ XT\wedge \alpha \wedge \beta =\tfrac 12\int _ X\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0, \]

so this measure vanishes. All products in this identity pair a current with smooth forms. Now apply lemma 4.7.4 to $\pi $, with the base Hermitian form $\eta =\sigma _1+\sigma _2$. Since $\eta ^2=2\sigma _1\wedge \sigma _2$, we have $T\wedge \pi ^*\eta ^2=0$, and since the fiber is a compact connected elliptic curve, the lemma gives the unique positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed current $S$. The same lemma shows that $S$ is closed when $T$ is; conversely, pullback commutes with $\mathrm{d}$. $\square$

This description concerns $(1,1)$-currents. It does not conflict with the Gauduchon forms of lemma 26.18.4: those have bidegree $(2,2)$ and satisfy $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _ j=0$, with bounded degree and divergent volume of their positive roots, and they belong to a different cone of currents.

Proposition 28.9.2. Pullback

\[ \pi ^*:H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(B,\mathbb R) \longrightarrow H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R) \]

is injective. The pseudoeffective and nef cones on $X$ coincide and are

\[ \operatorname {Psef}(X)=\operatorname {Nef}(X) =\{ a\{ \alpha \} _{\mathrm{BC}}+b\{ \beta \} _{\mathrm{BC}}: a,b\geq 0\} . \]

For the trace functional of the Gauduchon metric $\omega $, the slice of this cone at trace one is the segment $a+b=(8\pi )^{-1}$. Every class in the cone has a smooth semipositive representative, and none is big. The real form $\beta -\alpha $ is de Rham exact but represents a nonzero Bott–Chern class.

Proof. Suppose a smooth closed real form $\tau $ on $B$ satisfies $\pi ^*\tau =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $u$ smooth on $X$. The restriction of $u$ to each compact connected elliptic fiber is harmonic, hence constant, and local holomorphic sections show that $u=v\circ \pi $ for a smooth function $v$ on $B$. Thus $\pi ^*(\tau -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)=0$, which implies $\tau =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ because $\pi $ is a submersion. This proves injectivity.

The usual cohomology computation for $\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, together with the $\partial \bar\partial $-lemma on this Kähler surface, gives

\[ H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(B,\mathbb R) =\mathbb R\{ \sigma _1\} \oplus \mathbb R\{ \sigma _2\} , \qquad \int _ B\sigma _1\wedge \sigma _2=1, \qquad \sigma _1^2=\sigma _2^2=0. \]

If $S$ is a positive closed current on $B$, the two coefficients of its class are

\[ a=\int _ BS\wedge \sigma _2\geq 0, \qquad b=\int _ BS\wedge \sigma _1\geq 0. \]

By lemma 23.4.1 and lemma 28.9.1, every pseudoeffective class on $X$ is the pullback of the class of such a current, and it therefore lies in the cone of the statement. Conversely, every class in that cone has the smooth semipositive representative $a\alpha +b\beta $ and is therefore nef. A nef class is pseudoeffective by the compactness argument following proposition 28.1.2. This proves the equality of the cones; the whole Bott–Chern group of $X$ is not needed.

Since $\omega ^2=2\alpha \wedge \beta +2(\alpha +\beta )\wedge D$ and $\int _ E D=4\pi $ on each fiber, integration along the fibers gives

\[ \int _ X(a\alpha +b\beta )\wedge \omega ^2=8\pi (a+b). \]

This proves the assertion about the slice. By proposition 26.18.9, $X$ carries no closed Kähler current, so none of these classes is big. Finally, the one-form $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t$ is globally defined on $X$ and satisfies

\[ \mathrm{d}(\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}t)=\beta -\alpha . \]

The class of $\sigma _2-\sigma _1$ on $B$ is nonzero, and injectivity makes its pullback nonzero in Bott–Chern cohomology. Thus de Rham exactness does not imply $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exactness in this example. $\square$

Consequently, every pseudoeffective class on this threefold is nef and has empty non-nef locus, whereas its non-Kähler locus is all of $X$, since no class is big. All its closed positive representatives come from the two-dimensional base; in particular $(a\alpha +b\beta )^3=0$.

28.9.1 Potentials and envelopes from the base

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Descent also applies to potentials and preserves both ordinary singularity types and multiplier ideals, so the singularity envelopes in these backgrounds are computed on the base. The three-dimensional Monge–Ampère measures of these potentials nevertheless vanish, because their currents have only two horizontal directions.

We use the ordinary singularity order $\preceq $ and equivalence $\sim $ of definition 1.12.1, the ideal order $\preceq _{\mathcal I}$ and equivalence $\sim _{\mathcal I}$, which compare the multiplier ideals $\mathcal I(ku)$ at all weights $k>0$, and the ordinary and multiplier-ideal envelopes $P_\theta [u]$ and $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ of definition 1.19.4, definition 16.2.1.

Proposition 28.9.3. Let $a,b\geq 0$, put $\theta _ B=a\sigma _1+b\sigma _2$ and $\theta =\pi ^*\theta _ B$. Pullback is a bijection

\[ \mathrm{PSH}(B,\theta _ B)\longrightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\qquad v\longmapsto v\circ \pi . \]

It preserves the ordinary singularity order and equivalence, as well as the ideal order and equivalence. For every $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B,\theta _ B)$,

\[ P_\theta [v\circ \pi ]=P_{\theta _ B}[v]\circ \pi , \qquad P_\theta [v\circ \pi ]_{\mathcal I}=P_{\theta _ B}[v]_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi . \]

Nevertheless, every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=0. \]

Proof. The form $\theta _ B$ is closed, and $\pi $ is a proper holomorphic submersion with connected fibers, so proposition 1.22.3 gives the asserted bijection. Since $\pi $ is surjective, an ordinary singularity inequality holds upstairs exactly when it holds downstairs, with the same constant.

By lemma 1.16.6, multiplier ideals of potentials on $B$ pull back to those of the composed potentials on $X$, at every positive weight. Restriction to local holomorphic sections shows conversely that an inclusion of multiplier ideals on $X$ implies the corresponding inclusion on $B$. Hence the ideal orders and equivalences correspond as well.

The competitors defining both envelopes correspond under the bijection, including the bound by zero and the required ordinary or ideal condition, and their pointwise suprema commute with pullback. Upper semicontinuous regularization also commutes with pullback: in a product chart, the supremum of a pulled-back function over a sufficiently small product neighborhood equals its supremum over the corresponding base neighborhood. This proves the two envelope identities.

Finally, write $u=v\circ \pi $ and, for positive integers $j$, set $u_ j=\max \{ u,-j\} $, which descends to $v_ j=\max \{ v,-j\} $. Since $\theta _ B$ is semipositive, these are bounded admissible potentials. By lemma 1.7.3, $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^3=0$ because the base has dimension two. The definition of the extended non-pluripolar measure by increasing truncations now proves the last assertion. $\square$

Thus the ordinary and ideal envelopes coincide in these backgrounds exactly when they coincide on the base, while every three-dimensional Monge–Ampère measure vanishes. Criteria from the closed theory that require positive volume do not apply to these classes, which are not big. The Hermitian background $\omega $ behaves differently: its Monge–Ampère equations with smooth positive densities are solvable, even though the infimum of its masses is zero.

28.9.2 Sections selected by multiplier ideals

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Descent also identifies the spaces of holomorphic sections selected by multiplier ideals, together with their weighted $L^2$ norms. In particular, sections of pulled-back line bundles grow quadratically, as on the two-dimensional base. For integers $a,b$, write $\mathcal O_ B(a,b)$ for the tensor product of the pullbacks of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(a)$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(b)$ from the two factors of $B$.

Proposition 28.9.4. Let $a,b$ be positive integers, let $L_ B=\mathcal O_ B(a,b)$ and $L=\pi ^*L_ B$. For every quasi-plurisubharmonic $v$ on $B$ and positive integer $m$, pullback gives an isomorphism

\[ H^0\bigl (B,L_ B^ m\otimes \mathcal I(mv)\bigr ) \longrightarrow H^0\bigl (X,L^ m\otimes \mathcal I(mv\circ \pi )\bigr ). \]

For $v=0$ these spaces have dimension $(am+1)(bm+1)$.

Equip $L_ B$ with a smooth Hermitian metric and $L$ with its pullback, choose a continuous real function $\varphi $ on $B$, let $\mathrm{d}V_ X$ be a smooth positive volume form on $X$, and put $\nu =\pi _*\mathrm{d}V_ X$. Use the norms

\[ \| s\| _ B^2=\int _ B|s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi }\, \mathrm{d}\nu , \qquad \| \pi ^*s\| _ X^2=\int _ X|\pi ^*s|^2 \mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi \circ \pi }\, \mathrm{d}V_ X. \]

Then the isomorphism is isometric. Let $K_{m,B}$ and $K_{m,X}$ be the corresponding partial Bergman kernels, that is, the sums of the weighted pointwise squared norms $|s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi }$ and $|\pi ^*s|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\varphi \circ \pi }$ over orthonormal bases of the two spaces. Then

\[ K_{m,X}=K_{m,B}\circ \pi , \qquad \pi _*(K_{m,X}\, \mathrm{d}V_ X)=K_{m,B}\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

Proof. The identification of the section spaces is proposition 27.7.1, applied to $L_ B$ and $v$ on the base. For $v=0$, the usual basis of bihomogeneous monomials on $\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$ has $(am+1)(bm+1)$ elements.

Integration along the fibers of the proper submersion shows that $\nu $ is a smooth positive volume form, and the defining identity $\int _ Bg\, \mathrm d\nu =\int _ X(g\circ \pi )\, \mathrm dV_ X$ proves equality of the two norms. Pulling back an orthonormal basis and comparing pointwise norms gives the first kernel identity, and the same integration identity gives the second. The assertions include the case of a zero selected space, for which both kernels vanish. $\square$

Even without a singular selecting weight, the growth is therefore quadratic:

\[ \lim _{m\to \infty }m^{-2}h^0(X,L^ m)=ab, \qquad \lim _{m\to \infty }m^{-3}h^0(X,L^ m)=0. \]

The next calculation shows that the variational volume of a small Hermitian perturbation detects this missing dimension of growth.

28.9.3 The boundary volume and section growth

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Although no class $a\{ \alpha \} +b\{ \beta \} $ is big, its small Hermitian perturbations have positive Gauduchon variational volume $\mathcal V_\mu $ (definition 26.1.1). For the volume form of the balanced metric, the zero potential solves the normalized Monge–Ampère equation for every positive combination of $\alpha $, $\beta $ and $D$, so the volume is an explicit cubic polynomial in the perturbation parameter. Its order of vanishing at the nef boundary records how many of the two horizontal coefficients vanish.

Proposition 28.9.5. On the elliptic threefold, let $a,b\geq 0$, put $\theta =a\alpha +b\beta $, and let $\mu _0=\omega ^3/(24\pi )$. Then for every $\epsilon >0$,

28.15
\begin{equation} \mathcal V_{\mu _0}(\theta +\epsilon \omega ) =24\pi \epsilon (a+\epsilon )(b+\epsilon ). \end{equation}

In particular, $\mathcal V_\mu (\theta )=0$ for every smooth positive probability measure $\mu $. Let $q$ be the number of positive coefficients among $a,b$. Then the degeneration orders of proposition 26.19.1 are

\[ \underline a(\theta )=\overline a(\theta )=3-q, \]

for every smooth positive probability measure and every Hermitian perturbation direction.

Proof. The forms $\alpha ,\beta ,D$ are semipositive of rank one, so their squares vanish, and their sum $\omega $ is strictly positive. Thus

\[ \Omega _\epsilon =\theta +\epsilon \omega =(a+\epsilon )\alpha +(b+\epsilon )\beta +\epsilon D>0. \]

Since $\omega ^3=6\alpha \wedge \beta \wedge D$ and $\int _ X\omega ^3=24\pi $, the wedge expansion gives

\[ \Omega _\epsilon ^3 =6\epsilon (a+\epsilon )(b+\epsilon )\alpha \wedge \beta \wedge D =24\pi \epsilon (a+\epsilon )(b+\epsilon )\mu _0. \]

Hence the zero potential solves the smooth normalized equation for $\Omega _\epsilon $ and $\mu _0$. By theorem 26.1.3, the scalar of this equation is $\mathcal V_{\mu _0}(\Omega _\epsilon )$, which proves (28.15). The continuity of the volume at nef classes in theorem 26.10.1 and comparison of smooth densities then give $\mathcal V_\mu (\theta )=0$.

As $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, the leading term of (28.15) is $24\pi ab\epsilon $ if $a$ and $b$ are both positive, $24\pi (a+b)\epsilon ^2$ if exactly one of them is positive, and $24\pi \epsilon ^3$ if both vanish. The logarithmic quotients defining the orders therefore have limits $1,2,3$, respectively. Their independence of the density and of the perturbing metric is part of proposition 26.19.1. $\square$

The same number $q$ governs the growth of sections. For nonnegative integers $a,b$, the line bundle $L=\pi ^*\mathcal O_ B(a,b)$ satisfies

\[ h^0(X,L^ m)=(am+1)(bm+1), \]

since the descent along compact fibers and the monomial count in the proof of proposition 28.9.4 also apply when one or both integers vanish. The growth degree of these spaces is exactly $q$, whereas the boundary order of the smooth curvature form $a\alpha +b\beta $ of $L$ is $3-q$. In this explicit family, the boundary order thus records the loss of algebraic growth dimension, even though the three-dimensional Monge–Ampère measures in the unperturbed background vanish.

Formula (28.15) concerns one fixed smooth density; it does not assert that a total mass is conserved over all potentials. Indeed, by theorem 26.18.1 every Hermitian form on $X$, in particular each $\Omega _\epsilon $, has zero lower mass, while its upper mass is finite because $X$ has the bounded mass property (definition 6.5.1, proposition 26.18.7). Whether comparable identifications hold beyond such explicit families is part of 26.19.49.

28.9.4 Limits of the minimizing Gauduchon forms

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The Gauduchon metrics that minimize the quotient defining the volume in (28.15) can be identified explicitly: they are the equation metrics themselves. Their normalized squares converge smoothly at the nef boundary, although the normalized metrics may diverge. Recall from definition 26.1.1 that the root volume $\lambda _{\mu _0}$, with $\lambda _{\mu _0}^3=\mathcal V_{\mu _0}$, is the infimum over Gauduchon metrics $\gamma $ of $\int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^2$ divided by the denominator $D_{\mu _0}(\gamma )=\int _ X(\mu _0/\gamma ^3)^{1/3}\gamma ^3$. For positive functions of $\epsilon $, we write $f\asymp g$ if $f/g$ is bounded above and below by positive constants as $\epsilon \downarrow 0$.

Proposition 28.9.6. Let $a,b\geq 0$, $\theta =a\alpha +b\beta $ and $\Omega _\epsilon =\theta +\epsilon \omega $. For every $\epsilon >0$, the metric $\Omega _\epsilon $ is Gauduchon, and it minimizes the quotient defining $\lambda _{\mu _0}(\Omega _\epsilon )$. Put

\[ I_\epsilon =8\pi \bigl (ab+2(a+b)\epsilon +3\epsilon ^2\bigr ), \qquad T_\epsilon =\frac{\Omega _\epsilon ^2}{I_\epsilon }, \qquad \gamma _\epsilon =\frac{\Omega _\epsilon }{\sqrt{I_\epsilon }}. \]

Then $\int _ X\omega \wedge T_\epsilon =1$, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_\epsilon =0$ and $T_\epsilon =\gamma _\epsilon ^2$. As $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, the forms $T_\epsilon $ converge smoothly to

\[ T_0=\begin{cases} (4\pi )^{-1}\alpha \wedge \beta ,& a,b>0,\\ (8\pi )^{-1}\alpha \wedge (\beta +D),& a>0,\ b=0,\\ (8\pi )^{-1}\beta \wedge (\alpha +D),& a=0,\ b>0,\\ (24\pi )^{-1}\omega ^2,& a=b=0. \end{cases} \]

In every case $\theta \wedge T_0=0$. If $q$ is the number of positive coefficients among $a,b$, the denominator of the normalized minimizing metric satisfies

\[ D_{\mu _0}(\gamma _\epsilon ) =\frac{\bigl (24\pi \epsilon (a+\epsilon )(b+\epsilon )\bigr )^{2/3}}{I_\epsilon } \asymp \epsilon ^{q/3}, \]

where the two comparison constants may depend on the fixed pair $(a,b)$.

Proof. Write $x=a+\epsilon $, $y=b+\epsilon $ and $z=\epsilon $. Since $\alpha ^2=\beta ^2=D^2=0$,

\[ \Omega _\epsilon ^2 =2xy\alpha \wedge \beta +2xz\alpha \wedge D+2yz\beta \wedge D. \]

The forms $\alpha ,\beta $ are closed and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}D=2\alpha \wedge \beta $. Hence the first term is closed, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of each of the last two terms contains $\alpha ^2$ or $\beta ^2$ and therefore vanishes. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega _\epsilon ^2)=0$. Since the zero potential solves the normalized equation for $\Omega _\epsilon $ and $\mu _0$, the characterization of minimizing metrics in theorem 26.1.3 shows that $\Omega _\epsilon $ is a minimizer.

Integration along the fibers gives $\int _ X\alpha \wedge \beta \wedge D=4\pi $. Hence

\[ \int _ X\omega \wedge \Omega _\epsilon ^2 =8\pi (xy+xz+yz)=I_\epsilon , \]

which is the trace normalization. Dividing the three coefficients in the wedge expansion of $\Omega _\epsilon ^2$ by $I_\epsilon $ gives each of the stated limits; the convergence holds in every smooth norm, because the forms $\alpha \wedge \beta $, $\alpha \wedge D$ and $\beta \wedge D$ are fixed. The identities $\alpha ^2=\beta ^2=0$ then give $\theta \wedge T_0=0$ in all four cases.

Set $c_\epsilon =24\pi \epsilon (a+\epsilon )(b+\epsilon )$. The equation for $\Omega _\epsilon $ gives

\[ \gamma _\epsilon ^3 =\frac{c_\epsilon }{I_\epsilon ^{3/2}}\mu _0. \]

Substituting this in the definition of $D_{\mu _0}$ and using $\int _ X\mu _0=1$ yields $D_{\mu _0}(\gamma _\epsilon )=c_\epsilon ^{2/3}/I_\epsilon $. For fixed $(a,b)$ one has $c_\epsilon \asymp \epsilon ^{3-q}$ and $I_\epsilon \asymp \epsilon ^{2-q}$, so the quotient is of order $\epsilon ^{q/3}$. $\square$

In a coframe adapted to the three rank-one forms $\alpha $, $\beta $ and $D$, the three complementary products in the proof show that the limiting positive $(2,2)$-forms have coefficient matrices of rank $1$, $2$ and $3$ when $q=2$, $1$ and $0$, respectively. If $a,b>0$, the normalized metrics themselves converge smoothly to

\[ \frac{a\alpha +b\beta }{\sqrt{8\pi ab}}. \]

If $a>0$ and $b=0$, however, the coefficient of $\alpha $ in $\gamma _\epsilon $ grows like a positive constant times $\epsilon ^{-1/2}$, while its other two coefficients tend to zero. Thus the metrics $\gamma _\epsilon $ are unbounded relative to the fixed metric $\omega $, although their squares converge smoothly; the same holds with $\alpha $ and $\beta $ interchanged. This divergence is unavoidable. In dimension three, a positive $(2,2)$-form of rank two is not the square of a semipositive $(1,1)$-form: the coefficients of such a square are the pairwise products of the eigenvalues of the $(1,1)$-form, and the number of nonzero products is three, one or zero. At the vertex $a=b=0$, the normalized metric instead stays equal to $\omega /\sqrt{24\pi }$.

For a nonzero boundary class, the smooth limit $T_0$ annihilates $\theta $, and the denominators $D_{\mu _0}(\gamma _\epsilon )$ tend to zero; no concentration of the coefficient measures occurs in this family. This degeneration of the minimizing tests for a fixed density differs from the sequence of Gauduchon forms used in lemma 26.18.4 to prove that the lower mass vanishes. At the vertex, the denominator stays positive, and the order three comes entirely from the scaling $\Omega _\epsilon =\epsilon \omega $ of the background.

28.10 Smooth equations on the ample locus

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We now turn to the metric geometry of solutions of Monge–Ampère equations in big classes. In this section $X$ is a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and $\alpha \in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ is a big class with a smooth closed representative $\theta $. Its ample locus is $\operatorname {Amp}(\alpha )=X\setminus E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha )$. The solution of the Monge–Ampère equation with a smooth positive density has minimal singularities, and a Kähler current with analytic singularities provides a barrier that is smooth on the ample locus. The interior estimate of corollary 14.17.11 then gives smoothness there.

Let $\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )$ be the volume of $\alpha $, the non-pluripolar mass of a current with minimal singularities in $\alpha $, and let $\mathcal E(X,\theta )$ be the class of potentials $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ of full mass, that is, with $\int _ X\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n\rangle =\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )$.

Theorem 28.10.1. Let $\mu $ be a smooth positive volume form with $\mu (X)=\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )$. The normalized full-mass solution $\varphi \in \mathcal E(X,\theta )$ of

28.20
\begin{equation} \bigl \langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n\bigr \rangle =\mu , \qquad \sup _ X\varphi =0, \end{equation}

is smooth on $\operatorname {Amp}(\alpha )$, and the restriction of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ to this locus is a Kähler form.

The existence and uniqueness of $\varphi $ are part of the classical theory of Monge–Ampère equations in big classes, discussed in section 34.19; only the regularity assertion needs proof.

Proof. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ and put $f=\mu /\omega ^ n$. Since $X$ is compact and $\mu $ is smooth, $f\in L^ p(X,\omega ^ n)$ for every finite $p>1$. The classical estimate for this equation, discussed in section 34.19, shows that $\varphi $ has minimal singularities: there is a constant $C>0$ such that

28.21
\begin{equation} V_\theta -C\leq \varphi \leq V_\theta . \end{equation}

The upper bound follows from the normalization.

Let $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ be a Kähler current with analytic singularities as in theorem 28.3.2. Its potential $\rho $ has analytic singularities with smooth remainder, and for some constant $\varepsilon >0$,

\[ T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \varepsilon \omega , \qquad \{ \rho =-\infty \} =E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha ). \]

Thus $\rho $ is smooth on $\Omega =\operatorname {Amp}(\alpha )$. After subtracting a constant we may assume $\rho \leq 0$, hence $\rho \leq V_\theta $, and (28.21) gives

\[ \rho -C\leq \varphi \leq 0. \]

In particular $\varphi $ is locally bounded on $\Omega $, and by locality its non-pluripolar equation there is the equation for bounded potentials. The density $f$ belongs to $L^ p$ on $X$ and is smooth and strictly positive on $\Omega $. Hence all hypotheses of corollary 14.17.11 hold for the strict barrier $\rho $, so $\varphi $ is smooth and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi >0$ on $\Omega $. Since $\theta $ is closed, this form is Kähler. $\square$

The proof uses that the density is smooth and positive on the entire regular locus of the barrier. The theorem says nothing about the distance from the ample locus to its complement: the metric $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ may reach the omitted set along paths of finite length. To determine that length, one must compare the normal and tangential directions of the metric. The following sections first examine zeros of the density; singular radial densities then separate boundedness of the potential from completeness of the metric.

28.11 Density zeros and metric degeneration

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Where the density vanishes, the metric defined by a solution degenerates, and the rate of degeneration depends on how the zero arises. In complex dimension $n\geq 2$, a zero of the determinant can come from a ramified holomorphic map, from an exceptional fiber of a blowup, or from a singular Hessian. These mechanisms give different relations between the regularity of the potential and the distance to the zero set. Bounded Hölder solutions in a positive background exist by theorem 10.1.1, theorem 14.4.1, but their existence gives no pointwise estimate for the metric at such a zero.

Proposition 28.11.1. Let $F:X\to Y$ be a finite surjective holomorphic map between compact Kähler manifolds of dimension $n$, let $\omega _ Y$ be a Kähler form on $Y$, and suppose that the class $F^*[\omega _ Y]$ contains a Kähler form $\theta $. Then the full-mass solution in the background $\theta $ with measure $(F^*\omega _ Y)^ n$ has a smooth bounded potential with minimal singularities, and its current is the smooth semipositive form $F^*\omega _ Y$. Fix a smooth positive local volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. In a chart at a generic point of the ramification divisor, away from its other components, in which $F(z,w)=(z^ k,w)$ with $k\geq 2$,

28.22
\begin{equation} (F^*\omega _ Y)^ n=|z|^{2(k-1)}G\, \mathrm{d} V, \qquad F^*\omega _ Y\asymp |z|^{2(k-1)}|\mathrm{d} z|^2+|\mathrm{d} w|^2, \end{equation}

where $G$ is smooth and positive and $\asymp $ means that each side is bounded by a constant multiple of the other. The distance for $F^*\omega _ Y$ to the ramification divisor $\{ z=0\} $ is comparable to $|z|^ k$. Thus the metric on the complement of the ramification divisor is incomplete, although the current has no divisorial component. Finally, there is a strict barrier with logarithmic pole along the global ramification divisor.

Proof. By the $\partial \bar\partial $-lemma on the compact Kähler manifold $X$, the de Rham class $F^*[\omega _ Y]$ determines the Bott–Chern class, as in section 23.4. Thus $F^*\omega _ Y=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ for a smooth function $u$ on $X$. Its mass is the full mass of the class, and uniqueness of full-mass solutions in the closed theory, discussed in section 34.19, identifies $u$ with the solution up to a constant. The Jacobian determinant of $(z,w)\mapsto (z^ k,w)$ is $kz^{k-1}$, and comparison of $\omega _ Y$ with the Euclidean form gives (28.22). Hence every path to $\{ z=0\} $ has length bounded below by the variation of a fixed multiple of $|z|^ k$ along it, and a radial path with fixed $w$ attains this order.

The determinant of the differential $\mathrm{d}F$ defines the global ramification divisor $R$. Choose a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(R)$, with curvature form $\Theta _ R$ and canonical section $s_ R$. Since $\theta $ is Kähler, $\theta -\epsilon \Theta _ R>0$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon >0$, so by the Poincaré–Lelong formula $\epsilon \log |s_ R|^2-C$ is the potential of a strict positive $\theta $-current. The barrier is built from the global divisor $R$; no local branch coordinate is treated as a global line bundle. $\square$

Nonclosed backgrounds show the same behavior on manifolds that are not Kähler. On a Hopf manifold, the pullback of the standard invariant metric under a coordinate power map solves an equation in a nonclosed Hermitian background.

Example 28.11.2. Let $n\geq 2$, $q>1$ and let $k\geq 2$ be an integer. Let $H_ q=(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )/(Z\sim qZ)$ be the Hopf manifold, and on the covering space put $R=\sum _ j|Z_ j|^2$, $S=\sum _ j|Z_ j|^{2k}$,

\[ h_0=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\frac{\sum _ j\mathrm{d} Z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar Z_ j}{R}, \quad u_ k=\log S-k\log R, \]
\[ \theta _ k=k\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log R+ \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi }\partial \log S\wedge \bar\partial \log S. \]

These forms and functions are invariant under $Z\mapsto qZ$, so they descend to $H_ q$; the form $h_0$ descends to every Hopf manifold $H_{q'}$. The coordinate power map $F_ k([Z])=[(Z_1^ k,\ldots ,Z_ n^ k)]$ defines a finite surjective map $H_ q\to H_{q^ k}$: indeed $F_ k(qZ)=q^ kF_ k(Z)$, and the choices of roots are finite modulo scaling in the source. The form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log R$ is positive transverse to the complex Euler direction, while $\partial \log S$ is nonzero on that direction; hence $\theta _ k>0$. Direct differentiation gives

28.23
\begin{equation} \theta _ k+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ k=F_ k^*h_0 =\frac{\mathrm{i} k^2}{2\pi S}\sum _ j|Z_ j|^{2k-2}\mathrm{d} Z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar Z_ j. \end{equation}

The background $\theta _ k$ is not closed, since $\mathrm{d}\theta _ k=F_ k^*(\mathrm{d} h_0)$ is not identically zero. Along a coordinate divisor $D=\{ Z_ j=0\} $, at a generic point and in local coordinates $(z,w)$ with $D=\{ z=0\} $, the determinant vanishes to order $2(k-1)$, the metric $F_ k^*h_0$ is comparable to $|z|^{2(k-1)}|\mathrm{d} z|^2+|\mathrm{d} w|^2$, and the distance to $D$ is of order $|z|^ k$, as in proposition 28.11.1.

A small logarithmic potential along $D$ gives a strict barrier. Choose a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(D)$ with curvature form $\Theta _ D$ and canonical section $s_ D$. For small $c>0$, set $h_ c=\theta _ k+c\Theta _ D$ and $U_ c=c\log |s_ D|^2+u_ k$. Then $h_ c>0$ and

\[ h_ c+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ c=c[D]+F_ k^*h_0. \]

By theorem 6.1.2, the extended Monge–Ampère measure of $U_ c$ in the background $h_ c$ is the singular measure $(F_ k^*h_0)^ n$. The ordinary singularity type of $U_ c$ is exactly that of $c\log |s_ D|^2$, which is not minimal, while its metric off $D$ is the same smooth metric $F_ k^*h_0$. Finally, $H_ q\cong S^{2n-1}\times S^1$ has $H^2(H_ q;\mathbb R)=0$. A Kähler form would therefore be exact, and Stokes' theorem would make its positive top-degree integral zero; thus $H_ q$ is not Kähler.

In these constructions, the potential becomes bounded once its divisorial part is removed, and the zero set lies at finite distance. More generally, theorem 1.18.1 bounds the distance by the square root of a Hölder modulus of the potential wherever the current is a smooth positive metric. Continuity alone has no such consequence, as the radial examples of section 28.12 will show.

28.11.1 Determinant and normal distance

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The order of vanishing of the determinant alone does not determine the normal distance. An upper bound for the trace bounds all eigenvalues from above; combined with a lower bound for the determinant, it bounds the smallest eigenvalue from below, and hence the normal length. We use the local length distance $d_\eta ^ U$ of theorem 1.18.1, the infimum of $\eta $-lengths of piecewise smooth paths in $U\setminus D$; the distance $d^ U_\eta (x,D)$ to $D$ is the infimum of the lengths of such paths starting at $x$ and tending to $D$.

Proposition 28.11.3. Let $n\geq 2$, let $U\subset \mathbb C\times \mathbb C^{n-1}$ be a polydisk with coordinates $(z,w)$, and put $D=\{ z=0\} $. Let $\eta $ be a smooth semipositive $(1,1)$-form on $U$, positive off $D$, whose Euclidean trace is bounded and whose determinant is comparable to $|z|^{2\ell }$ for an integer $\ell \geq 1$. Then on a smaller concentric polydisk,

28.24
\begin{equation} c|z|^{\ell +1}\leq d_\eta ^ U((z,w),D)\leq C|z| \end{equation}

for constants $c,C>0$. Both bounds are attained, up to constants, by smooth closed forms with the same determinant order. If the block of $\eta $ in the $w$-directions is uniformly positive, the distance is comparable to $|z|^{\ell +1}$.

Proof. The least eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda _{\min }\geq \det \eta /(\operatorname {tr}\eta )^{n-1}\geq c|z|^{2\ell }$. Every path to $D$ therefore has length at least a constant times the integral of $|z|^\ell |\mathrm{d} z|$ along it, which gives the lower bound; a radial path and the trace bound give the upper bound. To see that both bounds occur, let $n=2$ first. For an integer $0\leq a\leq \ell $, take $\eta _ a=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|G_ a|^2$ with $G_ a(z,w)=(z^{a+1}/(a+1),z^{\ell -a}w)$; for $n>2$, append Euclidean coordinates. The Jacobian determinant of $G_ a$ is $z^\ell $, but at $(z,0)$ the first component of $G_ a$ forces a distance of order $|z|^{a+1}$, attained along a radial path. The cases $a=\ell $ and $a=0$ give the two endpoints.

Finally, write the Hermitian coefficient matrix of $\eta $ in the normal and tangential directions as $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}h& b^*\\ b& B\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. When $B$ is uniformly positive, its Schur complement $h-b^*B^{-1}b=\det \eta /\det B$ is comparable to $|z|^{2\ell }$. Completing the square gives the lower bound of order $|z|^{\ell +1}$, and solving $\mathrm{d} w/\mathrm{d} s=-B^{-1}b\, \mathrm{d} z/\mathrm{d} s$ along a radial normal curve, on nested charts, gives a path of matching length. $\square$

28.11.2 Smoothness of exceptional residuals

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We return to the blowup $\pi :X=\operatorname {Bl}_ Z Y\to Y$ of section 14.22. Here $(Y,h)$ is a compact Hermitian $n$-fold, the smooth center $Z$ has codimension $r\geq 2$, $E$ is the exceptional divisor with canonical section $s_ E$, $\Theta _ E$ is the curvature form of a smooth metric on $\mathcal O(E)$, $L=\log |s_ E|^2$, and the background is $\theta =\pi ^*h+c\Theta _ E$ with $c>0$. By proposition 14.22.1, every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has the form $u=cL+q\circ \pi +C$ with $q$ an $h$-plurisubharmonic function, and $u$ has minimal singularities exactly when $q$ is bounded; we then call $u$ a potential of minimal type. Fix a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ on $X$, and use adapted coordinates in which $\pi (y,z,w)=(y,z,zw)$ and $E=\{ z=0\} $.

Proposition 28.11.4. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be of minimal type, and suppose that its residual current $S=(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)-c[E]$ extends smoothly and semipositively across $E$. Then, on smaller adapted charts, $0\leq S^ n/\mathrm{d} V\leq C|z|^{2(r-1)}$. Consequently, if the Monge–Ampère measure of $u$ has density $|z|^{2\ell }g$ with $g$ smooth and strictly positive, then $\ell \geq r-1$. This order condition is necessary but not sufficient for a smooth residual. In particular, in this background a smooth strictly positive density is incompatible with a smooth residual for a potential of minimal type.

Proof. Write $u=cL+v$. Since $S=\pi ^*h+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ is smooth, the distributional Laplacian of $v$ is smooth, and local elliptic regularity makes $v$ smooth. On each fiber of $E\to Z$, a compact projective space, $\pi ^*h$ restricts to zero and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ restricts to the semipositive form $S$; the maximum principle therefore makes $v$ constant on the fiber. Hence every vertical diagonal entry of $S$ is a smooth nonnegative function vanishing at $z=0$, and is therefore $O(|z|^2)$. The other diagonal entries are bounded, so Hadamard's determinant inequality gives the stated bound. The pullback $\pi ^*h$ attains this order, while the examples below show that the order condition is not sufficient. The argument does not apply to prescribed nonminimal singularities, whose residuals can be positive on the exceptional fibers. $\square$

A singular Hessian, rather than a zero of the density, can also make a solution nonsmooth. The following local example has constant positive density and a potential that is not $C^1$, and it can be placed in a compact manifold.

Example 28.11.5. On a polydisk with coordinates $(z,w)\in \mathbb C\times \mathbb C^{n-1}$, $n\geq 2$, set $P(z,w)=|z|^{2/n}(1+|w|^2)$. Off $z=0$, its Hessian block in the $w$-directions is $|z|^{2/n}I$ and its normal Schur complement is $n^{-2}|z|^{2/n-2}$, so its Hessian determinant is the positive constant $n^{-2}$. Since $P$ is bounded, it extends across $z=0$ as a plurisubharmonic function whose Monge–Ampère measure has no additional mass on the divisor. Yet $P$ is not $C^1$ along that divisor.

This local example extends to a compact Kähler model on $\mathbb P^ n$. On an affine chart with coordinates $y=(z,w)$, the Fubini–Study form is $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+|y|^2)$; for constants $\lambda >0$ and $B$, put $H=\lambda \log (1+|y|^2)$ and $Q=H-B$. Choose nested radii and the parameters so that $Q>P+2\epsilon $ on an outer collar of a ball and $Q<P-2\epsilon $ on an inner ball. A smooth convex regularized maximum $U=M_\epsilon (P,Q)$ then equals $P$ on the inner ball and $Q$ on the collar. Put $v=U-H$ on the ball and $v=-B$ outside it, so that $\lambda \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ equals $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U$ on the ball and $\lambda \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ outside. This current is positive and smooth off $\{ z=0\} $; on the gluing annulus its density is at most $C|z|^{2/n-2}$, so it belongs globally to $L^ p$ for every $1<p<n/(n-1)$. On the inner ball the density is smooth and strictly positive, but the potential is not $C^1$. There the metric is comparable to $|z|^{2/n-2}|\mathrm{d} z|^2+|z|^{2/n}|\mathrm{d} w|^2$, and the distance to the divisor is of order $|z|^{1/n}$.

The construction also works on a Hopf manifold. Choose a global Hermitian form equal to $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\lambda |y|^2)$ on a coordinate ball and equal to a fixed nonclosed Hermitian form outside a slightly larger ball, using a convex interpolation of positive forms. Carry out the same regularized maximum with $H=\lambda |y|^2$ on the inner ball, and set $v=-B$ outside it. The background is still nonclosed on the outer region, and the local potential and density are unchanged. As in the projective case, the density is controlled globally only in $L^ p$.

This example does not contradict corollary 14.17.11. The density loses smoothness along the divisor $\{ z=0\} $ in the gluing region, so the polar set of an admissible barrier must contain that part of the divisor. Being analytic, it then also contains the continuation of the divisor into the inner ball. The corollary requires a smooth positive density on the entire regular locus of the barrier, and local smoothness of the density on the inner ball cannot replace this hypothesis.

On a blowup, a radial potential of power type produces densities with exceptional zeros or poles of every order. These examples show that the order condition in proposition 28.11.4 is not sufficient.

Example 28.11.6. Let $\pi :X=\operatorname {Bl}_0\mathbb P^ n\to \mathbb P^ n$ be the blowup at the origin of an affine chart with coordinates $y$, let $E$ be its exceptional divisor, and write $s=|y|^2$. For every $\gamma ,c>0$, one can choose $\lambda >0$ so that the background $\theta =\pi ^*(\lambda \omega _{\mathrm{FS}})+c\Theta _ E$ admits a potential $u=cL+q\circ \pi $ with bounded Hölder remainder $q$, hence of minimal type, whose total local potential $\lambda \log (1+s)+q$ equals $As^\gamma +B$ near the origin, for constants $A>0$ and $B\in \mathbb R$. To construct $q$, choose a smooth function $m$ with $m(s)>0$ and $m'(s)>0$, equal to $A\gamma s^\gamma $ near $s=0$ and to $\lambda s/(1+s)$ outside a slightly larger interval; taking $A$ small puts the values at the two ends in the right order. Match the positive derivative germs on narrow collars, fill the remaining positive deficit of the integral with a positive interior derivative and a bump, and integrate $U'=m/s$, choosing the constant so that $U(s)=\lambda \log (1+s)$ for large $s$. Set $q(y)=U(|y|^2)-\lambda \log (1+|y|^2)$ on the affine chart and extend it by zero outside. Since $m$ and $m'$ are positive, positivity holds throughout the compact interpolation region.

On the blowup, in coordinates with $y=(z,zw)$, the total local potential of $u$ is $A|z|^{2\gamma }(1+|w|^2)^\gamma +B$. The density of $u$ is $|z|^{2\ell }G$ with $G$ smooth and positive and $\ell =n\gamma -1>-1$, since the Hessian determinant of $A|y|^{2\gamma }$ is $A^ n\gamma ^{n+1}s^{n(\gamma -1)}$ and the squared Jacobian of the blowup is $|z|^{2(n-1)}$. The metric off $E$ is comparable to

28.25
\begin{equation} |z|^{2\gamma -2}|\mathrm{d} z|^2+|z|^{2\gamma }|\mathrm{d} w|^2, \end{equation}

the distance to $E$ is of order $|z|^\gamma $, and the metric completion collapses $E$ to a point. If $\ell $ is a nonnegative integer, the density is smooth. At $\ell =0$ the density is smooth and strictly positive, but the remainder has exact normal Hölder exponent $2/n$ for $n>2$, is Lipschitz but not $C^1$ for $n=2$, and the normal coefficient of the metric is unbounded. For $-1<\ell <0$, the density belongs to $L^ p$ exactly when $p\ell >-1$. Taking $\ell =n$ gives a smooth density with a zero of order $2n\geq 2(n-1)$, while the residual Hessian remains nonsmooth; this proves the insufficiency asserted in proposition 28.11.4. A small nonclosed perturbation of the background supported away from the origin leaves these local calculations unchanged, and gives the same examples in a nonclosed background, with the Monge–Ampère measure of the perturbed potential as prescribed measure.

28.12 Radial densities and metric completeness

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a radially symmetric equation, the mass of the density inside a ball determines the radial derivative of the potential and hence both eigenvalues of its Hessian. Boundedness of the potential and finiteness of the distance to the center are then expressed by two different integrals of this derivative, and suitable singular densities separate the two properties. Throughout this section $n\geq 2$, the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ n$ is normalized by $\int _{\mathbb P^ n}\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^ n=1$, and on the standard affine chart with coordinates $y\in \mathbb C^ n$ we put $\omega _{\mathrm E}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|y|^2$, so that $\int _{\{ |y|^2<S\} }\omega _{\mathrm E}^ n=S^ n$ for $S>0$. We write $s=|y|^2$.

Theorem 28.12.1. Fix $\lambda >0$ and a smooth positive function $D:(0,\infty )\to (0,\infty )$ such that $D(s)=\lambda ^ n/(1+s)^{n+1}$ for all sufficiently large $s$, and

28.26
\begin{equation} n\int _0^\infty s^{n-1}D(s)\, \mathrm{d} s=\lambda ^ n. \end{equation}

Let $\mu _ D$ be the measure on $\mathbb P^ n$ equal to $D(|y|^2)\omega _{\mathrm E}^ n$ on the affine chart and to zero on the hyperplane at infinity; near that hyperplane it agrees with $(\lambda \omega _{\mathrm{FS}})^ n$. Then the equation $\langle (\lambda \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n\rangle =\mu _ D$ has a unique normalized full-mass solution $q$. It is smooth off $y=0$, and on the affine chart its total local potential $\lambda \log (1+|y|^2)+q(y)$ is a radial function $U(s)$ satisfying

28.27
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} m(s)^ n& =n\int _0^ s t^{n-1}D(t)\, \mathrm{d} t,\qquad m(s)=sU'(s),\\ m'(s)& =\frac{s^{n-1}D(s)}{m(s)^{n-1}}>0. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

The tangential and radial eigenvalues of its Hessian are $m/s$ and $m'$. For any sufficiently small $s_0>0$, the solution is bounded, equivalently has minimal singularities, precisely when

28.30
\begin{equation} \int _0^{s_0}\frac{m(s)}s\, \mathrm{d} s<\infty . \end{equation}

The distance from the origin for the metric $\lambda \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$ on $\mathbb P^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $ is finite precisely when

28.31
\begin{equation} \int _0^{s_0}\frac{\sqrt{m'(s)}}{2\sqrt{s}}\, \mathrm{d} s<\infty . \end{equation}

If this integral diverges, the metric on $\mathbb P^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $ is complete.

Let $\pi :X=\operatorname {Bl}_0\mathbb P^ n\to \mathbb P^ n$ be the blowup at the origin, with exceptional divisor $E$, and let $s_ E$ and $\Theta _ E$ be as in section 14.22. For $\theta =\pi ^*(\lambda \omega _{\mathrm{FS}})+c\Theta _ E$ with $c>0$, the full-mass solution on $X$ whose measure is the pullback of $\mu _ D$ has potential $c\log |s_ E|^2+q\circ \pi +C$, generic Lelong number exactly $c$ along $E$, and mass $\lambda ^ n$. In a blowup chart $\pi (z,w)=(z,zw)$, its density relative to the Euclidean volume form of the coordinates $(z,w)$, normalized as above, is

28.32
\begin{equation} |z|^{2(n-1)}D\bigl (|z|^2(1+|w|^2)\bigr ). \end{equation}

Its ordinary singularity type is minimal precisely under (28.30). In the unbounded cases, the residual $q\circ \pi $ grows sublogarithmically and has zero generic Lelong number, but the type is not minimal. The metric off $E$ satisfies the same completeness criterion (28.31).

Proof. Define $m$ by the first equation in (28.27). It is positive for $s>0$ and tends to zero at the origin. By (28.26) and the prescribed outer density, $m(s)=\lambda s/(1+s)$ for large $s$: the two expressions for $m^ n$ have the same limit at infinity and the same derivative for large $s$. Integrate $U'=m/s$, choosing the additive constant so that $U(s)=\lambda \log (1+s)$ for large $s$. The Hessian of $U(|y|^2)$ has eigenvalues $m/s$ and $m'$, and its determinant is $(m/s)^{n-1}m'=D$. Thus $q=U(|y|^2)-\lambda \log (1+|y|^2)$ extends by zero outside the affine chart and defines a positive current that is smooth off the origin. Subtracting its supremum gives the normalization.

Since $U$ is increasing, it is bounded above near zero, so lemma 1.3.7 gives a plurisubharmonic extension across the origin, possibly with the value $-\infty $ there. If $U$ is unbounded, then $m(s)\to 0$ implies $U(s)=o(|\log s|)$: for each $\epsilon >0$, bound $m$ by $\epsilon $ on a small interval and integrate $m/s$. The closed non-pluripolar product equals $\mu _ D$ off the origin and puts no mass at the origin; the measure $\mu _ D$ has no atom there and has mass $\lambda ^ n$ by (28.26). Hence $q$ is a full-mass solution, and it is unique by the uniqueness of full-mass solutions in the closed theory, discussed in section 34.19. Integrating $U'=m/s$ shows that $U$ is bounded near the origin precisely under (28.30).

Write $R=|y|$. For a path approaching the origin, the metric norm of its velocity is at least a fixed multiple of $\sqrt{m'(R^2)}|\dot R|$, and radial paths attain this bound up to a fixed constant. Since $\mathrm{d} R=\mathrm{d} s/(2\sqrt{s})$, this gives (28.31). The metric is smooth and positive on compact subsets away from the origin, so divergence of the integral is equivalent to completeness of the metric on $\mathbb P^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $.

On the blowup, apply proposition 14.22.1 and use the squared Jacobian $|z|^{2(n-1)}$. The closed non-pluripolar product is the extension by zero of the pullback density, has mass $\lambda ^ n=\operatorname {vol}([\theta ])$, and is unique in its full-mass class. By theorem 6.1.2, the extended operator agrees with it on potentials that are continuous off $E$, even when the residual tends to $-\infty $ along $E$. Sublogarithmic growth gives the residual zero Lelong number, so the coefficient along $E$ is exactly $c$, but it does not give a bounded difference from $c\log |s_ E|^2$. The metric off $E$ is isometric to the metric on $\mathbb P^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $, which proves the same distance criterion.

The inner asymptotics considered below can be glued to the prescribed outer density while keeping (28.26): take a small inner amplitude or radius, join through a smooth positive annulus, and add a nonnegative smooth annular bump to supply the remaining mass. Thus each of these asymptotic behaviors occurs for a density satisfying the hypotheses of the theorem. The assertions below concern the total local potential $U$; subtracting a smooth background potential can change a leading smooth term. $\square$

In the next theorem, $f\sim g$ means that $f/g\to 1$ as $s\downarrow 0$.

Theorem 28.12.2. For the normalized family in theorem 28.12.1, put $L=\log (1/s)$ for $0<s<1$.

  1. If $D(s)\sim Cs^{\ell -n+1}L^{-b}$ with $C>0$, $\ell >-1$ and $b\in \mathbb R$, put $\gamma =(\ell +1)/n$ and $K=(C/\gamma )^{1/n}$. Then

    28.33
    \begin{equation} m\sim Ks^\gamma L^{-b/n},\qquad U(s)-U(0)\sim \frac K\gamma s^\gamma L^{-b/n},\qquad m'\sim \gamma K s^{\gamma -1}L^{-b/n}. \end{equation}

    The residual is bounded and Hölder continuous. In bounded blowup charts, its metric is comparable to $L^{-b/n}(|z|^{2\gamma -2}|\mathrm{d} z|^2+|z|^{2\gamma }|\mathrm{d} w|^2)$, and its distance to $E$ is of order $|z|^\gamma (\log (1/|z|))^{-b/(2n)}$. In particular, the metric is incomplete near $E$.

  2. If $D(s)\sim Cs^{-n}L^{-b}$, the local mass is finite precisely for $b>1$. In that range put $a=(b-1)/n$ and $K=(nC/(b-1))^{1/n}$. Then

    28.34
    \begin{equation} m\sim KL^{-a},\qquad m'\sim Ka s^{-1}L^{-a-1}. \end{equation}

    The potential has a finite limit at the origin, the solution has minimal singularities, and the distance is finite, each precisely when $b>n+1$; in that case $U(s)-U(0)\sim K L^{1-a}/(a-1)$. At $b=n+1$, $U$ diverges as $-K\log L$, and for $1<b<n+1$ as $-K L^{1-a}/(1-a)$. On the product of a curve with a Hermitian manifold, a density depending only on the curve variable reduces the Monge–Ampère equation to a Poisson equation on the curve, even when the second factor is not Kähler. For a single logarithmic factor, boundedness of the potential and finiteness of the distance have the same threshold, whereas the iterated logarithmic family of theorem 28.12.2 separates them.

Theorem 28.13.1. Proof. On the curve, the real current $F\omega _ C-\omega _ C$ has integral zero and is therefore $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ for a global distribution $v$, unique up to a constant. 28.14 An incomplete metric in a big class

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We now consider exponential Monge–Ampère equations whose reference measure has a nonintegrable pole along a divisor, and ask whether the solution defines a complete metric on the locus where its current is smooth and positive. A divisorial part of a solution with minimal singularities can cancel the pole and leave the deleted divisor at finite distance. On the blowup of the projective plane at a point, the resulting metric away from the exceptional divisor is the pullback of the Fubini–Study metric. The class is big but not nef, and the equation has a unique solution of finite energy.

Finite energy refers to the classical closed theory of [SGPT]. For a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ with big class on a compact Kähler surface $Y$, and for $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$ with minimal singularities, the Monge–Ampère energy relative to the envelope $V_\theta $ is

\[ E_\theta (u)=\frac13\sum _{j=0}^2 \int _ Y(u-V_\theta ) \left\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ j\wedge (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\theta )^{2-j}\right\rangle . \]

For general $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$, $E_\theta (u)$ is the decreasing limit of $E_\theta (\max (u,V_\theta -k))$ as $k\to \infty $, and $\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta )$ is the set of potentials with $E_\theta (u)>-\infty $; they have full mass.

Theorem 28.14.1. There exist a smooth projective surface $Y$, a smooth irreducible divisor $E$, a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ with big class $\alpha $, a Hermitian metric on $\mathcal O_ Y(E)$, a defining section $\sigma $ of $\mathcal O_ Y(E)$, and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V_ Y$ with the following properties. The equation

28.40
\begin{equation} \left\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^2\right\rangle =\mathrm{e}^{\rho }|\sigma |^{-2}\, \mathrm{d}V_ Y \end{equation}

has a unique solution $\rho \in \mathcal E^1(Y,\theta )$, and this solution has minimal singularities. Moreover,

\[ \operatorname {Amp}(\alpha )=Y\setminus E \]

is precisely the open set on which $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ is smooth and strictly positive. The restriction of this metric to every nonempty connected open subset of $Y\setminus E$ is incomplete. In particular, no dense Zariski open subset carries a complete induced metric.

Let $p=[1:0:0]\in \mathbb P^2$, and let $\omega _2$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^2$, normalized by $\int _{\mathbb P^2}\omega _2^2=1$.

\[ \beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-\rho )=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0 \quad \text{on }Y\setminus E, \]

\[ \rho \leq V_\theta \leq \rho +C. \]

\[ E_\theta (\rho )=\frac13\sum _{j=0}^2 \int _ Y(\rho -V_\theta ) \left\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^ j\wedge (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\theta )^{2-j}\right\rangle . \]

Uniqueness. Suppose $u\in \mathcal E^1(Y,\theta )$ is another solution, and let $\mathrm{d}\mu =s^{-1}\mathrm{d}V_ Y$ on $Y\setminus E$, extended by zero on $E$. This measure is smooth and positive off $E$, but has infinite total mass. The classical comparison principle for full-mass potentials in the closed theory gives

\[ \int _{\{ u<\rho \} }\mathrm{e}^{\rho }\, \mathrm{d}\mu \leq \int _{\{ u<\rho \} }\mathrm{e}^{u}\, \mathrm{d}\mu . \]

Both integrals are finite, since the two Monge–Ampère measures have mass $1$. The integrand on the left is strictly larger, which forces $\mu (\{ u<\rho \} )=0$. Interchanging $u$ and $\rho $ gives $\mu (\{ \rho <u\} )=0$ as well. The ample locus and incompleteness. The Kähler current in (28.43) is smooth off $E$. On the other hand, every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\theta )$ satisfies $u\leq \rho +C_ u$, so its Lelong numbers at points of $E$ are at least those of $\rho =2\log s$, which are positive. \[ \operatorname {Amp}(\alpha )=Y\setminus E_{\mathrm{nK}}(\alpha ) =Y\setminus E. \]

\[ t_0=\min \{ t\in [0,1]:c(t)\notin W\} \]

It has no limit in $W$: intrinsic convergence implies ambient convergence, and the ambient limit is $c(t_0)\notin W$. Thus $U$ is incomplete. Applying this argument to each connected component proves the assertion for a dense Zariski open set, which may be disconnected. $\square$

28.14.2 An incomplete metric with compatible flow data

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Exponential equations of this kind arise as auxiliary elliptic equations in the smoothing of singular initial data by geometric flows; the completeness question for them is discussed in section 34.17. In that setting the class, the divisor and the volume form are not arbitrary: they are determined by the initial potential through a log resolution. The following example shows that incompleteness persists under these compatibility conditions.

Let $(X,\omega )$ be a compact Kähler manifold and $\phi _0\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ an initial potential with analytic singularities. Compatible data on a log resolution $\pi :Y\to X$ satisfy

28.48
\begin{gather} \pi ^*\phi _0=\sum _ jm_ j\log |\sigma _ j|^2+\ell _0,\qquad \pi ^*(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi _0)=\sum _ jm_ j[D_ j]+R_0, \\ K_ Y=\pi ^*K_ X+\sum _ jb_ jD_ j,\qquad \pi ^*\mathrm{d}V_ X=\prod _ j|\sigma _ j|^{2b_ j}\mathrm{d}V_ Y. \end{gather}

Here $D=\sum _ jD_ j$ is a divisor with simple normal crossings, $\sigma _ j$ is a defining section of $\mathcal O_ Y(D_ j)$ with a smooth Hermitian metric, the numbers $m_ j$ and $b_ j$ are the coefficients of the pulled-back singularity and of the relative canonical divisor, $\ell _0$ is smooth, $R_0$ is a smooth closed semipositive form, and $\mathrm{d}V_ X$, $\mathrm{d}V_ Y$ are smooth positive volume forms. If $\Theta _ j$ denotes the curvature form of the metric on $\mathcal O_ Y(D_ j)$, put

\[ \lambda =\max _ j\frac{m_ j}{1+b_ j},\qquad \Theta =\sum _ j(1+b_ j)\Theta _ j,\qquad \theta =\lambda \Theta +R_0,\qquad |\sigma |^2=\prod _ j|\sigma _ j|^2. \]

The number $\lambda $ is the inverse of the exponential integrability index of $\phi _0$: by (28.48) and (28.49), $\mathrm{e}^{-q\phi _0}$ is integrable exactly when $q\lambda <1$. The metric in the next theorem is the solution of the auxiliary elliptic equation (28.50) in this background.

Theorem 28.14.4. There are compatible data (28.48)–(28.49) on smooth projective surfaces, with $\mathrm{d}V_ X=\omega ^2$, $R_0$ Kähler and $D$ a disjoint union of two smooth curves, such that the equation

28.50
\begin{equation} \langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^2\rangle =\mathrm{e}^{\rho }|\sigma |^{-2}\, \mathrm{d}V_ Y \end{equation}

has a unique solution in $\mathcal E^1(Y,\theta )$. The solution has the Lelong numbers of a potential with minimal singularities, but every dense Zariski open set on which its current is smooth and strictly positive is incomplete. The measure $|\sigma |^{-2}\mathrm{d}V_ Y$ has a nonintegrable pole with smooth strictly positive coefficient along both components of $D$.

The class $\{ \theta \} $ in this example is not nef. Thus this theorem and theorem 28.14.2 show separately that neither the compatibility identities nor nefness of the class implies completeness. We do not know whether completeness holds when the class is nef and the compatibility identities hold. In neither example is the auxiliary metric identified with the metric of the maximal flow.

The cusp in the example comes from a rotation-invariant solution of an exponential equation on $\mathbb P^1$ whose measure has a simple pole.

Lemma 28.14.5. On $\mathbb P^1$, let $z=Z_1/Z_0$, $r=|z|^2$, $\alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+r)$ and $h=r/(1+r)$, so that $\int _{\mathbb P^1}\alpha =1$. For every $c>2$ there is a $c\alpha $-plurisubharmonic function $u$, depending only on $|z|$ and smooth off $0$, such that

28.51
\begin{equation} g_1:=c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm{e}^{u}h^{-1}c\alpha . \end{equation}

\[ \int _{\mathbb P^1}|u|\alpha +\int _{\mathbb P^1}|u|g_1<\infty . \]

Moreover, with $t=\log r$ and $s=-\log r$, there is $A\in \mathbb R$ such that, as $s\to \infty $,

\[ \begin{aligned} u& =\log (2/c)-2\log (s+A)+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}),\\ u_ t& =\frac{2}{s+A}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}),\qquad u_{tt}=\frac{2}{(s+A)^2}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-s}). \end{aligned} \]

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w=\left(-\frac2L+\frac2{rL^2}\right)\alpha ,\qquad Q(r):=\frac{c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w}{\mathrm{e}^{w}h^{-1}c\alpha } =\frac{crL^2-2rL+2}{c(1+r)}. \]

The function $Q$ is positive, since $L\geq 1$ and $c>2$. Its limits are $2/c$ at $0$ and $1-2/c$ at infinity, so $0<q_-\leq Q\leq q_+<\infty $ for some constants $q_\pm $. Choose $a_0\leq \min (0,\log q_-)$ and $b_0\geq \max (0,\log q_+)$. Then $l=w+a_0$ and $H=w+b_0$ satisfy

\[ c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}l\geq \mathrm{e}^{l}h^{-1}c\alpha ,\qquad c\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H\leq \mathrm{e}^{H}h^{-1}c\alpha ,\qquad l\leq w\leq H. \]

\[ \Delta u=f(q)\mathrm{e}^{u}-g(q),\qquad f(q)=\frac{4c}{1+q},\qquad g(q)=\frac{4c}{(1+q)^2}, \]

For $k>0$, the regular radial solution $v=V(q)$ of $(-\Delta +k)v=F(q)$ satisfies

28.54
\begin{equation} V(q)=A_0+\frac14\int _0^ q\log (q/t)\bigl (kV(t)-F(t)\bigr )\, \mathrm{d}t. \end{equation}

The $j$-fold iterate of the integral kernel applied to the constant $1$ is $q^ j/(4^ j(j!)^2)$, so successive approximation converges uniformly on every finite interval. Choose $k\geq \sup f\mathrm{e}^{H}$ on the fixed disk, and let $T(v)$ be the radial solution of $(-\Delta +k)T(v)=kv-f\mathrm{e}^{v}+g$ with boundary value $w$; the right side is nondecreasing in $v$ on $[l,H]$. \[ U'(q)=\frac14\int _0^1\bigl (f(qt)\mathrm{e}^{U(qt)}-g(qt)\bigr )\, \mathrm{d}t, \]

and induction gives bounds for all derivatives on compact intervals. A diagonal limit is therefore smooth on the $\zeta $-plane, including the point $z=\infty $, and satisfies $u=-2\log (-\log r)+O(1)$ at $0$.

28.15 Normal length near a divisor

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

To prove completeness near a divisor, one needs a lower bound for the metric in the normal direction. A lower bound for the determinant alone does not give it, because the tangential eigenvalues may absorb the volume. Dividing the determinant by the determinant of the tangential block gives the normal Schur complement. A lower bound for it by the inverse square of a defining function of the divisor, weakened by at most the square of the logarithm of that function, forces completeness. For solutions of exponential equations, this bound follows from bounds on the density, the potential and the tangential determinant, and an example on a torus shows that the logarithmic exponent $2$ is sharp.

In this section $X$ is again a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$ with a Hermitian form $\omega $. Let $D\subset X$ be a nonempty smooth effective divisor, and put $M=X\setminus D$. Let $s_ D$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal O_ X(D)$, equipped with a smooth Hermitian norm, and set

\[ R=|s_ D|,\qquad \tau =-\log R^2. \]

Rescale the norm so that $\tau \geq 1$ on $M$. Write $J$ for the complex structure. For a smooth positive real $(1,1)$-form $T$ on $M$, we use the Riemannian metric $g_ T(v,v)=\pi T(v,Jv)$ on real tangent vectors $v$. Thus, if $T=(\mathrm{i}/2\pi )\sum H_{j\bar k}\, \mathrm{d}z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_ k$, then $g_ T(v,v)=\sum H_{j\bar k}\, \mathrm{d}z_ j(v)\overline{\mathrm{d}z_ k(v)}$, and Hermitian norms below use this normalization of coefficients.

Near $D$, the form $\partial \tau $ does not vanish (see (28.59) below), and we define the tangential hyperplane field, the tangential block and the determinant

\[ E_\tau =\ker \partial \tau ,\qquad B_\tau =T|_{E_\tau },\qquad \det _\omega T=\frac{T^ n}{\omega ^ n}. \]

Similarly, $\det _\omega B_\tau $ denotes the determinant of $B_\tau $ relative to the restriction of $\omega $ to the complex hyperplane $E_\tau $; when $n=1$, it is $1$.

Proposition 28.15.1. Suppose that there are constants $c>0$, $\tau _0\geq 1$ and $p\leq 2$ such that, on $\{ \tau >\tau _0\} $,

\begin{equation} \frac{\det _\omega T}{\det _\omega B_\tau } \geq cR^{-2}\tau ^{-p}. \end{equation}

Proof. In a coordinate neighborhood meeting $D$, write $s_ D=z_1 e$, where $e$ is a local holomorphic frame with $|e|^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\psi }$ and $\psi $ is smooth. Then

\[ \tau =-\log |z_1|^2+\psi ,\qquad \partial \tau =-\frac{\mathrm{d}z_1}{z_1}+\partial \psi . \]

At a point of this set, choose an $\omega $-unitary frame whose last $n-1$ vectors span $E_\tau $. Let $H$ be the Hermitian matrix that represents $g_ T$ as the quadratic form $w^*Hw$ in the complex components $w$, and write

\[ H=\begin{pmatrix} h & a^* \\ a & B \end{pmatrix},\qquad S=h-a^*B^{-1}a=\frac{\det H}{\det B}, \]

\[ \begin{pmatrix} \bar\xi & \eta ^* \end{pmatrix} H\begin{pmatrix} \xi \\ \eta \end{pmatrix} =S|\xi |^2+(\eta +B^{-1}a\xi )^*B(\eta +B^{-1}a\xi ) \geq S|\xi |^2. \]

where $c_2>0$ is uniform. The last inequality uses $\mathrm{d}\tau (v)=2\operatorname {Re}\partial \tau (v)$.

\[ \Phi _ p(t)= \begin{cases} 0,& t\leq \tau _0,\\ \displaystyle \int _{\tau _0}^ t x^{-p/2}\, \mathrm{d}x,& t>\tau _0. \end{cases} \]

Lemma 28.15.3. For $C>0$ sufficiently large, put $\theta =C\omega _0$. For either profile $v$ in (28.64), the function $u=\pi _1^*v$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the form $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is smooth and positive on $X\setminus D$, and

\[ \frac{\partial ^2G(L)}{\partial z\partial \bar z} =\frac{G''(L)}{|z|^2}. \]

28.16 Rigid boundary classes on a torus

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We now turn to the boundary of the nef cone. A nef class with zero top intersection number may have a smooth semipositive representative and yet leave little freedom to vary it. On a complex torus, the kernel of a constant semipositive form controls this freedom: when the leaves of the kernel are dense, the form is the only closed positive current in its class. The same condition decides whether adding a small fixed Kähler form produces metrics whose diameters tend to zero. For Kähler forms converging to the boundary in other ways, the diameters can tend to zero even at a class with many semipositive representatives.

Let $X=V/\Lambda $ be a complex torus, where $V\simeq \mathbb C^ n$ and $\Lambda $ is a full lattice. Let $\theta $ be a translation-invariant semipositive real $(1,1)$-form of rank $r$ with $1\leq r<n$, and fix a translation-invariant Kähler form $\kappa $. The class $\alpha =\{ \theta \} _{\mathrm{BC}}$ is nef and has $\int _ X\theta ^ n=0$. It lies on the boundary of the nef cone: a Kähler form in $\alpha $ would have positive top intersection number, whereas Stokes' theorem gives every representative the top intersection number $\int _ X\theta ^ n=0$. We call $\alpha $ rigid if it contains exactly one closed positive current. Let $K=\ker \theta \subset V$, a complex subspace of dimension $n-r$, and let $H\subset X$ be the closure of its image in $X$. This is a connected real subtorus, which need not be complex. Diameters are computed for the Riemannian metric $g_\eta (v,w)=\eta (v,Jw)$ associated with a positive form $\eta $, where $J$ is the complex structure.

Theorem 28.16.1. The following are equivalent:

  1. $\alpha $ is rigid;

  2. $H=X$;

  3. $\operatorname {diam}(X,\theta +t\kappa )\to 0$ as $t\downarrow 0$.

In the first case the unique current is $\theta $. The third condition is independent of the chosen translation-invariant Kähler form $\kappa $.

Proof. Rigidity when $H=X$. Suppose first that $H=X$. By lemma 23.4.1, a positive current in $\alpha $ can be written $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $u$ quasi-plurisubharmonic, in particular $u\in L^1(X)$. For a constant real vector $v\in K$, the coefficient $T(v,Jv)$ is a positive Radon measure. Its total integral against Haar measure is zero, because $\theta (v,Jv)=0$ and the remaining part of the coefficient is a combination of constant-coefficient derivatives of $u$. Hence this coefficient vanishes. Writing $D_ v$ for differentiation along $v$ and using that $K$ is complex, we obtain

\[ (D_ v^2+D_{Jv}^2)u=0 \quad \text{as a distribution for every }v\in K. \]

For a real linear functional $\ell $ with $\ell (\Lambda )\subset \mathbb Z$, let $\widehat u(\ell )$ be the Fourier coefficient of $u$ at the character $\exp (2\pi \mathrm i\ell )$. The identity above gives

\[ \bigl (\ell (v)^2+\ell (Jv)^2\bigr )\widehat u(\ell )=0. \]

Since the image of $K$ is dense, a nonzero character cannot vanish identically on $K$. Thus every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of $u$ is zero, and convolution with a smooth approximate identity shows that $u$ is constant. Hence $T=\theta $.

Nonrigidity when $H\ne X$. If $H\ne X$, a nontrivial character of the real torus $X/H$ gives a nonzero $\ell $ that vanishes on $K$. Put $\phi =\cos (2\pi \ell )$. Since $K$ is complex, the form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi $ vanishes whenever one of its arguments lies in $K$. On a fixed complement of $K$, $\theta $ is positive definite and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi $ is uniformly bounded. Consequently, the forms $\theta \pm \varepsilon \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi $ are semipositive for sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$. They represent the same class, and they are distinct because a nonconstant smooth function on a compact torus cannot have zero $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$. Hence $\alpha $ is not rigid.

Collapse when $H=X$. Suppose again that $H=X$, and fix $\delta >0$. By density and compactness, there are finitely many vectors $k_1,\ldots ,k_ N\in K$ whose images form a $\delta $-net in the flat metric of $\kappa $. Put $R=\max _ j|k_ j|_\kappa $. Since $\theta (k_ j,Jk_ j)=0$, the straight path from $0$ to the image of $k_ j$ has $(\theta +t\kappa )$-length $\sqrt t\, |k_ j|_\kappa $. For $0<t\leq 1$ these metrics are bounded above by a fixed multiple of $\kappa $. Joining this path to a point within $\delta $ therefore gives

\[ \operatorname {diam}(X,\theta +t\kappa ) \leq \sqrt t\, R+C\delta , \]

since by translation invariance the diameter equals the supremum of the distances from $0$. Letting first $t\downarrow 0$ for this fixed net and then $\delta \downarrow 0$ proves the third condition.

No collapse when $H\ne X$. If $H\ne X$, choose $\ell $ as above. Its differential vanishes on $K$, so $|\mathrm d\ell (v)|\leq L|v|_\theta $ for some $L>0$, where $|v|_\theta $ is the seminorm defined by $\theta $. Since $\theta +t\kappa \geq \theta $, the surjective map $\ell \bmod \mathbb Z$ onto the circle of length one is $L$-Lipschitz for every $t>0$. Two points mapping to opposite points of the circle therefore have distance at least $1/(2L)$, and the diameters cannot tend to zero. This completes the proof of the equivalences. Since the second condition does not involve $\kappa $, neither does the third. $\square$

The straight path along this displacement proves the bound for the distance from zero, and translation invariance gives the diameter bound. For $0<|s|<1/\pi $, the form $\theta _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(s\cos (2\pi x_2))=(1-\pi s\cos (2\pi x_2))\theta _0$ is another semipositive representative, so the limiting class is not rigid. Finally, the coefficient matrix of $\beta _ t-\theta _0$ has eigenvalues $t^2\pm t$, so this difference is indefinite for $0<t<1$: the forms $\beta _ t$ do not approach $\theta _0$ from above. $\square$

28.17 Nonrigid classes on a quotient resolution

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In contrast with example 28.16.2, an irrational nef boundary ray can admit many smooth semipositive representatives. On a suitable two-dimensional torus, such representatives come from semipositive forms depending on a single real coordinate, with an irrational lattice direction making the ray irrational. If these forms vanish near the fixed points of the involution $z\mapsto -z$, they pass smoothly to the resolution of the quotient, a Kummer surface.

Let $A=\mathbb C^2/\Lambda $ be a complex two-torus, let $Y=A/\{ \pm 1\} $, and let $r:X\to Y$ be the minimal resolution. The fixed points of the involution are the sixteen points of $A[2]=(\frac12\Lambda )/\Lambda $, and their images are the sixteen singular points of $Y$.

Lemma 28.17.1. The surface $X$ is compact Kähler. Its exceptional set consists of sixteen disjoint projective lines with self-intersection $-2$.

Proof. Blow up the sixteen fixed points on $A$ and take the quotient by the lifted involution. Near one exceptional line, a blowup chart has coordinates $(u,v)$ with $z_1=u$ and $z_2=uv$, and the involution is $(u,v)\mapsto (-u,v)$. The quotient chart has smooth coordinates $(s,v)=(u^2,v)$. On the other chart, $v'=1/v$ and $s'=v^2s$. These charts describe the total space of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)$ near its zero section. The quotient is therefore smooth and resolves the double point, with the stated exceptional curve. Doing this at all fixed points gives the compact surface $X$.

Choose a flat Kähler form on $A$ that equals $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $, where $\rho =|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2$, on disjoint balls centered at the fixed points. Away from those points it descends to $Y$. On the resolution chart,

\[ \rho =|s|(1+|v|^2),\qquad \rho ^2=|s|^2(1+|v|^2)^2. \]

Choose a smooth function $F$ on $[0,+\infty )$ with $F(\rho )=c\rho ^2$ near zero, where $c>0$, with $F'>0$ on $(0,+\infty )$ and $F''\geq 0$, and with $F(\rho )=\rho -C$ for $\rho \geq \delta $, for a sufficiently small $\delta >0$. Such a function is obtained by choosing $F'$ nondecreasing, linear near zero and equal to one beyond $\delta $. Replace the flat form in each ball by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F(\rho )$. This agrees with the flat form near the boundary of the ball and extends smoothly across the exceptional line. The resulting closed form $\eta $ is positive off the exceptional set, while along it $\eta $ is positive in the normal direction and zero in the tangent direction.

To restore positivity in the tangent direction, choose a smooth cutoff $\chi $ equal to one for $\rho \leq \delta $ and zero for $\rho \geq 2\delta $. For $s\ne 0$, set

\[ q_ v=\chi (\rho )\log \rho -\log |s|. \]

Near $s=0$ this equals $\log (1+|v|^2)$, so it extends smoothly. On the overlap of the two charts, $q_{v'}-q_ v=-2\log |v|$ is pluriharmonic, so their $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ forms agree. They define a smooth closed form $R$ supported in the resolved ball, positive in the tangent direction along the exceptional line and semipositive where $\chi =1$. Hence $\eta +\varepsilon R$ is positive where $\chi =1$, including along the line. On the compact transition annulus $\eta $ is already positive, so $\eta +\varepsilon R$ remains positive there for sufficiently small $\varepsilon >0$, and outside the ball $R=0$. Adding these corrections in all the disjoint balls produces a Kähler form on $X$. $\square$

Fix now $a\in \mathbb R\setminus \mathbb Q$ and take for $\Lambda $ the lattice generated by

\[ \lambda _1=(1,0),\quad \lambda _2=(a,1),\quad \lambda _3=(\mathrm i,0),\quad \lambda _4=(0,\mathrm i). \]

Write $z_ j=x_ j+\mathrm i y_ j$. Since $y_1$ takes integer values on $\Lambda $, the coordinate $y_1\bmod \mathbb Z$ defines a map from $A$ to the circle $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. Call a smooth even function $h:\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to [0,+\infty )$ admissible if it has integral one and vanishes near $\frac12\mathbb Z$; such functions are obtained from even pairs of nonnegative bumps supported away from that set. For an admissible $h$, put

\[ \theta _ h=h(y_1)\, \mathrm dx_1\wedge \mathrm dy_1. \]

Proposition 28.17.2. The forms $\theta _ h$ descend and extend by zero to smooth semipositive closed forms $T_ h$ on $X$. They all represent one nonzero nef Bott–Chern class $\alpha $ with $\alpha ^2=0$. This class is not rigid, that is, it contains more than one closed positive current, and its image in real de Rham cohomology spans an irrational ray. Each $T_ h$ vanishes on a neighborhood of the exceptional set.

Proof. The form $\theta _ h$ is closed, because $\mathrm dh(y_1)$ is proportional to $\mathrm dy_1$, and it is semipositive. Since $h$ is even, $\theta _ h$ is invariant under the involution. At every fixed point, $y_1$ belongs to $\frac12\mathbb Z$ modulo $\mathbb Z$, so the form vanishes on a neighborhood of that point. Its descent to the complement of the singular points of $Y$ therefore extends by zero across their resolved neighborhoods. This proves smoothness and the asserted vanishing.

Let $h_1,h_2$ be admissible and put $k=h_1-h_2$. This function is even, has mean zero and vanishes near $\frac12\mathbb Z$. Choose $f$ with

\[ f'(y)=4\pi \int _0^ y k(s)\, \mathrm ds, \qquad f''=4\pi k. \]

The derivative $f'$ is periodic and odd, so its mean is zero, and $f$ can be chosen periodic and even. Evenness and the zero mean of $k$ give $\int _0^{1/2}k=0$, hence $f'=0$ near $\frac12\mathbb Z$. Therefore $f(y_1)$ descends, and it extends by constants near each exceptional curve to a smooth function $G$ on $X$. Since

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f(y_1)=\frac{f''(y_1)}{4\pi } \, \mathrm dx_1\wedge \mathrm dy_1 \quad \text{on }A, \qquad \text{we have}\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=T_{h_1}-T_{h_2}. \]

Thus all the forms $T_ h$ have the same Bott–Chern class $\alpha $. Distinct admissible functions give distinct forms, so $\alpha $ is not rigid. The forms are semipositive and have square zero, so $\alpha ^2=0$. By lemma 28.17.1, adding any positive multiple of a Kähler form gives a Kähler representative of a nearby class; therefore $\alpha $ is nef.

By the mean-one condition, the periods of $\theta _ h$ on the real two-tori generated by $(\lambda _1,\lambda _3)$ and by $(\lambda _2,\lambda _3)$ are $1$ and $a$, respectively. Translate both cycles by $(0,\mathrm i/4)$. The translated cycles lie in $\{ y_2=1/4\} $ modulo $\mathbb Z$ and avoid the sixteen fixed points, and their periods are unchanged because the form is closed. Their images in $X$ lie in the complement of the exceptional curves, which is doubly covered without ramification by $A\setminus A[2]$. The first period shows that $\alpha \ne 0$. If a nonzero multiple $b\alpha $ had a rational de Rham class, then evaluating it on these cycles would make both $b$ and $ba$ rational, contradicting $a\notin \mathbb Q$. Finally, since $\alpha ^2=0$, Stokes' theorem shows that $\alpha $ itself has no Kähler representative, so it lies on the boundary of the nef cone. $\square$

28.18 Semipositive metrics in a four-point blowup family

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The preceding constructions keep the manifold fixed. For a family of compact manifolds and a line bundle on the total space, one may ask whether a semipositive singular metric on the restriction of the bundle to a special fiber is the restriction of a semipositive metric over a neighborhood of that fiber. In a family of blowups of the projective plane at four points, the answer is negative. The obstruction comes from compact curves on the nearby fibers on which the bundle has degree zero: the curvature of a semipositive metric vanishes on such a curve, and this persists on a component of the limiting curve in the central fiber. It forces the restricted metric to have a logarithmic singularity of coefficient one at a point of that component, whereas the prescribed metric has coefficient one half there. Varying these curves also identifies exactly which central metrics extend.

Let $\Delta $ be a disk with coordinate $t$, and let $[x:y:z]$ be homogeneous coordinates on $\mathbb P^2$. Blow up $\mathbb P^2\times \Delta $ along the four disjoint sections

\[ \begin{aligned} p_1(t)& =[1:0:0],& \qquad p_2(t)& =[0:1:0],\\ p_3(t)& =[1:1:t],& \qquad p_4(t)& =[0:0:1], \end{aligned} \]

write $\pi :\mathcal X\to \Delta $ for the resulting family and $X_ t=\pi ^{-1}(t)$ for its fibers. Let $H$ be the pullback of the hyperplane line bundle, let $E_ i$ be the exceptional divisor over $p_ i$, and put

\[ L=2H-E_1-E_2-E_3-E_4,\qquad L_ t=L|_{X_ t}. \]

We use additive notation for tensor products of line bundles and also for their divisor classes, and we write $K_{X_ t}$ for the canonical line bundle. A singular Hermitian metric $h$ has squared norm $|e|_ h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi }$ in a local holomorphic frame $e$; the function $\varphi $ is its local weight, and its curvature is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $. The metric is semipositive if its local weights are plurisubharmonic. For an effective divisor with local equation $w$, the divisor metric on the associated line bundle is the singular metric for which the canonical section has norm one; its weight is $\log |w|^2$ and its curvature is the integration current along the divisor.

Lemma 28.18.1. The family is smooth and projective with rational surface fibers, and $L$ is effective on $\mathcal X$. For every $t\ne 0$, $L_ t$ is generated by global sections, has square zero and is nef. For every integer $m\geq 1$,

\[ H^0(X_ t,K_{X_ t}+mL_ t)=0\qquad (t\ne 0). \]

Proof. Near one of the sections, coordinates centered on it identify the blowup with $\operatorname {Bl}_0(\mathbb C^2)\times \Delta $. Since the four centers are disjoint, these charts prove smoothness and the submersion property. The blowup of the projective morphism $\mathbb P^2\times \Delta \to \Delta $ is projective and proper, and each fiber $X_ t$ is the blowup of $\mathbb P^2$ at the four points $p_ i(t)$.

The quadratic forms

\[ F_1=x(z-ty),\qquad F_2=y(z-tx) \]

vanish along all four sections and therefore lift to sections of $L$. They are nonzero, which proves that $L$ is effective. For $t\ne 0$, their common zeros in $\mathbb P^2$ are exactly the four points. In suitable affine coordinates $(u,v)$ centered at $p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4$, their linear initial terms are

\[ (v-tu,-tu),\quad (-tu,v-tu),\quad (v-tu,v),\quad (u,v). \]

Each pair is linearly independent. Hence, after the exceptional divisors are subtracted, the transformed sections have no common zero on an exceptional curve. The two transformed sections therefore generate $L_ t$, and $L_ t$ has nonnegative degree on every irreducible curve.

On a fiber, $H^2=1$, $H\cdot E_ i=0$, and $E_ i\cdot E_ j=-\delta _{ij}$. Hence $L_ t^2=0$, and $K_{X_ t}=-3H+\sum _ iE_ i$ gives $K_{X_ t}\cdot L_ t=-2$. A nonzero section of $K_{X_ t}+mL_ t$ would have an effective zero divisor whose intersection with the nef line bundle $L_ t$ is $-2<0$, a contradiction. This argument also excludes a nowhere-vanishing section, whose zero divisor would have intersection zero. $\square$

On $X_0$, let $\ell $ be the strict transform of the line $\{ z=0\} $, which passes through $p_1(0)$, $p_2(0)$ and $p_3(0)$. In additive notation,

\[ \ell =H-E_1-E_2-E_3,\qquad A=2L_0-\ell =3H-E_1-E_2-E_3-2E_4. \]

Lemma 28.18.2. The line bundle $A$ is generated by global sections. There is a semipositive metric $h_0$ on $L_0$ whose weight along $\ell $ is

\[ \varphi _0=\tfrac 12\log |w|^2+\text{a smooth function}, \]

where $w$ is a local equation of $\ell $. It is smooth off $\ell $ and has analytic singularities.

Proof. Consider the cubic forms

\[ x^2z,\qquad xyz,\qquad y^2z,\qquad xy(x-y). \]

Their common zero set in $\mathbb P^2$ consists of the four central points. In affine coordinates at $p_1$, $p_2$ and $p_3$, their linear initial terms include $(z,y)$, $(z,x)$ and $(z,x-y)$, respectively, while at $p_4$ their quadratic initial terms include $x^2,xy,y^2$. These have no common zero on the corresponding exceptional projective lines. After the simple exceptional factors at $p_1,p_2,p_3$ and the double factor at $p_4$ are removed, the transformed cubics therefore generate $A$.

Their Fubini–Study metric $h_ A$ is smooth and semipositive. Let $h_\ell $ be the divisor metric on $\mathcal O(\ell )$. Since $2L_0=A+\ell $, the square root of $h_ A\otimes h_\ell $ defines a metric $h_0$ on $L_0$, with

\[ \Theta _{h_0}=\tfrac 12\Theta _{h_ A}+\tfrac 12[\ell ]\geq 0. \]

The stated form of the weight and of its singularities follows directly. $\square$

Lemma 28.18.3. The strict transforms of the conics

\[ F_1-2F_2=z(x-2y)+txy=0 \]

form a surface $\Gamma \subset \mathcal X$. For $t\ne 0$, its fiber $C_ t$ is a smooth compact rational curve with $L_ t\cdot C_ t=0$. The central fiber of $\Gamma $ is $\ell +q$, where $q$ is the strict transform of the line $\{ x=2y\} $. The curves $\ell $ and $q$ meet transversely at one point $p$, and $L_0\cdot q=1$. Near every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, $\Gamma $ is smooth and $\pi |_\Gamma $ is a submersion.

Proof. The symmetric matrix of the quadratic form has determinant $-t/2$, so the conic is smooth for $t\ne 0$. It passes simply through all four centers, so its strict transform is a smooth rational curve of class $L_ t$, and its degree against $L_ t$ is $L_ t^2=0$.

At $t=0$ the conic is the union of the lines $\{ z=0\} $ and $\{ x=2y\} $. The second line avoids $p_1,p_2,p_3$ and passes through $p_4$. Each component has multiplicity one at every center it contains, so the central fiber of $\Gamma $ contains no additional exceptional component. The two lines meet at $[2:1:0]$, transversely and away from every center. Since $q=H-E_4$, we have $L_0\cdot q=1$.

Away from the centers, the defining equation at $t=0$ has nonzero derivative in the directions of $\mathbb P^2$ at every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, which proves the assertion there. Near $p_4$, use the blowup chart $z=1$, $x=r$, $y=rs$. After the exceptional factor is removed, the equation becomes

\[ 1-2s+trs=0. \]

At the central point of $q\cap E_4$ we have $r=0$ and $s=1/2$, and the derivative in $s$ is $-2$. Thus $(r,t)$ are local coordinates on $\Gamma $ there. These two cases cover $q\setminus \{ p\} $. $\square$

Theorem 28.18.4. There is no semipositive metric on $L$ over an open neighborhood of $X_0$ whose local weights, restricted to $X_0$, satisfy

\[ \psi \geq \varphi _0-C \]

in compatible local frames, for a constant $C$. In particular, $h_0$ is neither the restriction of a semipositive metric on such a neighborhood nor of the same singularity type as such a restriction.

Proof. Suppose that such a metric exists. Since $\pi $ is proper, we may shrink $\Delta $ so that the whole family lies in its domain: otherwise points outside the neighborhood over $t_ j\to 0$ would have a limit point on $X_0$ outside it. The lower bound makes the restricted weight finite at every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, because $\varphi _0$ is smooth there. Hence the restrictions of the weight to $\Gamma $ and to $q$ are not identically $-\infty $.

Near a point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $, choose a product chart $(\zeta ,t)$ on $\Gamma $, as given by lemma 28.18.3, and a holomorphic frame of $L$. The restricted weight $u(\zeta ,t)$ is plurisubharmonic and locally integrable. By Fubini's theorem, for almost every small $t\ne 0$ its restriction to $C_ t$ is not identically $-\infty $, so the metric restricts to $C_ t$. The curvature of this restriction is a positive measure on the compact curve $C_ t$ of total mass $L_ t\cdot C_ t=0$, so it vanishes. Thus $u(\cdot ,t)$ is harmonic for almost every $t$. By lemma 28.18.5 below, whose proof does not use this theorem, the central slice $u(\cdot ,0)$ is harmonic or identically $-\infty $, and it is finite. Hence the restricted weight is harmonic near every point of $q\setminus \{ p\} $.

The restricted metric on $L_0|_ q$ therefore has curvature supported at $p$. This curvature is a positive measure of total mass $L_0\cdot q=1$, so it is the Dirac measure $[p]$. In a coordinate $w$ on $q$ centered at $p$, the restricted weight is therefore $\log |w|^2$ plus a harmonic function, because their difference has zero distributional Laplacian. Since $\ell $ and $q$ meet transversely, $\varphi _0|_ q=\tfrac 12\log |w|^2+O(1)$. The difference $\psi |_ q-\varphi _0|_ q$ thus tends to $-\infty $ at $p$, contradicting the assumed lower bound. $\square$

The obstruction concerns the prescribed singularity type. Since $L$ is effective on the total space, the divisor metric of the zero divisor of a nonzero section of $L$ is a semipositive metric on $\mathcal X$, with a different restriction to $X_0$.

28.18.1 The image of metric restriction

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The degree-zero conics also determine exactly which central metrics extend. We say that a semipositive metric $h$ on $L_0$ extends if there is a semipositive metric on $L$ over a neighborhood of $X_0$ whose local weights restrict to local weights of $h$; in particular, these restrictions are not identically $-\infty $. The following lemma is the slice argument used in the proof of theorem 28.18.4.

Lemma 28.18.5. Let $u$ be a plurisubharmonic function on a product of disks $D_\zeta \times D_ t$ with $0\in D_ t$, and suppose that $u$ is not identically $-\infty $. Suppose that $u(\cdot ,t)$ is harmonic for almost every $t$. Then $u(\cdot ,0)$ is harmonic or identically $-\infty $.

Proof. Since $u$ is locally integrable, Fubini's theorem gives $\partial _\zeta \partial _{\bar\zeta }u=0$ in the sense of distributions. On smaller product disks, form the averages

\[ u_\varepsilon (\zeta )=\frac1{\pi \varepsilon ^2} \int _{|t|<\varepsilon }u(\zeta ,t)\, \mathrm{d}A(t), \]

where $\mathrm{d}A$ is planar area. Local upper bounds for $u$ and the reverse Fatou lemma give upper semicontinuity, and Fubini's theorem gives the submean inequality in $\zeta $. The averages are locally integrable, since $u$ is integrable on compact product disks. Thus each $u_\varepsilon $ is subharmonic with zero distributional Laplacian, hence harmonic.

Since $u$ is subharmonic in $t$, the disk averages decrease to $u(\zeta ,0)$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. A decreasing limit of harmonic functions is harmonic or identically $-\infty $: if the values are bounded below at one point, Harnack's inequality, applied to the differences from the first function, bounds the differences on compact subdisks, and compactness of harmonic functions gives a harmonic limit. This proves the alternative on every smaller disk, and hence on $D_\zeta $. $\square$

Let $f:X_0\to \mathbb P^1$ be the morphism defined by the pencil of lines through $p_4=p_4(0)$, that is, by $[x:y:z]\mapsto [x:y]$, let $a=x/y$ be the affine coordinate on $\mathbb P^1$, and put $P=f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$.

Lemma 28.18.6. The pencil $f$ is a holomorphic map with connected fibers, $P=H-E_4$, and $L_0=\mathcal O(\ell )+P$. Let $h_\ell $ be the divisor metric on $\mathcal O(\ell )$, for which the canonical section $s_\ell $ satisfies $|s_\ell |_{h_\ell }=1$ away from $\ell $, and let $h_{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study metric on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$, with curvature $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$. Then the metric

\[ h_*=h_\ell \otimes f^*h_{\mathrm{FS}} \]

on $L_0$ extends to a semipositive metric on $L$ over the whole family. Its curvature is $[\ell ]+f^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$.

Proof. First blow up $p_4$ in $\mathbb P^2$. The map $[x:y]$ then becomes holomorphic: in the chart $x=r$, $y=rs$ with $z=1$, it is $[1:s]$, and the other chart covers the remaining direction. Its fibers are the strict transforms of the lines through $p_4$, and the pullback of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$ is $H-E_4$. Next blow up the other three central points, which lie in the directions $a=\infty ,0,1$, respectively. Each affected fiber becomes the union of its strict transform and one exceptional line meeting it, while every other fiber remains a projective line. This proves connectedness of the fibers and the formula for $P$. The equality $L_0=\mathcal O(\ell )+P$ follows from the divisor classes.

In this identification, the restrictions of $F_1,F_2$ to $X_0$ are $s_\ell \otimes f^*x$ and $s_\ell \otimes f^*y$, where $x$ and $y$ are regarded as sections of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$. Indeed, at $t=0$ the forms are $xz$ and $yz$; the pullback of $\{ z=0\} $ is $\ell +E_1+E_2+E_3$, and removing the four exceptional factors from the transforms of $xz$ and $yz$ leaves exactly these sections. In a local frame of $L$, the weight $\log (|F_1|^2+|F_2|^2)$ defines a semipositive metric, since a change of frame multiplies both coefficients by the same nonvanishing holomorphic function. On every fiber $X_ t$ with $t\ne 0$ the two sections have no common zero, so the weight is not identically $-\infty $ there. On the central fiber the weight is the sum of the divisor weight of $\ell $ and the pullback of the Fubini–Study weight, which proves the claim and the curvature formula. $\square$

Lemma 28.18.7. For $a\in \mathbb C\setminus \{ 0,1\} $, the strict transforms of the conics

\[ F_1-aF_2=z(x-ay)+t(a-1)xy=0 \]

have smooth rational fibers of $L_ t$-degree zero for $t\ne 0$. Their central fiber is $\ell +q_ a$, where $q_ a=f^{-1}(a)$ is a smooth projective line of $L_0$-degree one. The two components meet transversely at $p_ a=[a:1:0]$, away from the blowup centers. Along $q_ a\setminus \{ p_ a\} $, the surface formed by these conics is smooth and its projection to the disk is a submersion.

Proof. The symmetric matrix of the quadratic form has determinant $-a(a-1)t/4$, so the conic is smooth for $t\ne 0$; it passes simply through the four centers. Its strict transform has class $L_ t$, so its degree is $L_ t^2=0$. At $t=0$ the conic splits into the two lines $\{ z=0\} $ and $\{ x=ay\} $. Since $a$ is finite and $a\notin \{ 0,1\} $, the line $\{ x=ay\} $ does not pass through the other three central points, so no additional exceptional component occurs, and $q_ a=H-E_4$ has degree one against $L_0$. The two lines meet transversely at $p_ a$, away from all centers.

Away from the centers, the central equation has nonzero derivative in the directions of $\mathbb P^2$ along $q_ a\setminus \{ p_ a\} $. At the point of $q_ a$ on $E_4$, the chart $z=1$, $x=r$, $y=rs$ gives, after the exceptional factor is removed,

\[ 1-as+t(a-1)rs=0. \]

At $r=t=0$ and $s=1/a$, the derivative in $s$ is $-a\ne 0$. These charts prove the final assertion. $\square$

Theorem 28.18.8. The semipositive metrics on $L_0$ that extend to semipositive metrics on $L$ over a neighborhood of $X_0$ are exactly the metrics

\[ h_\ell \otimes f^*h_ B, \]

where $h_ B$ ranges over all semipositive singular Hermitian metrics on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$. Every such central metric extends over the whole family.

Proof. Necessity. Let $h$ be a semipositive metric on $L_0$ that extends. By properness we may shrink the disk so that the extending metric is defined on the whole family. On the locus where $f$ is smooth, local integrability and Fubini's theorem show that for almost every $a\in \mathbb C\setminus \{ 0,1\} $ the restriction of the weights of $h$ to $q_ a$ is not identically $-\infty $. Fix such an $a$. On each local product chart of the conic surface of lemma 28.18.7 along $q_ a\setminus \{ p_ a\} $, the weight of the extending metric is not identically $-\infty $, since its central slice is not. By Fubini's theorem, the metric restricts to almost every nearby compact conic, and on such a conic its curvature is a positive measure of degree zero, hence zero. Lemma 28.18.5 therefore makes the central slice of the weight harmonic on each chart; the alternative $-\infty $ is excluded by the choice of $a$.

Consequently, the curvature of $h|_{q_ a}$ is supported at $p_ a$. It is a positive measure of degree one, so it equals $[p_ a]$, which is also the curvature of $h_*|_{q_ a}$. The difference of the two weights is a globally defined harmonic function on the projective line $q_ a$: near $p_ a$, subtracting $\log |w|^2$ from either weight leaves a harmonic function, so the difference extends across $p_ a$. It is therefore constant. Thus the weight difference $u$ between $h$ and $h_*$ is constant along almost every fiber of $f$, away from $\ell $.

Over every point of the base there are local holomorphic sections of $f$ avoiding $\ell $ and the exceptional curves: over the finite chart use $[a:1:1]$, and near infinity use $[1:b:1]$ with $b=1/a$. If $e_ B$ is a local frame of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$, then $s_\ell \otimes f^*e_ B$ is a nonvanishing holomorphic frame of $L_0$ along these sections. Composing the weight of $h$ in this frame with a section defines a subharmonic weight on the base. It is not identically $-\infty $, because on almost every fiber the weight is harmonic away from $p_ a$. Two choices of section give the same function at almost every base point, because $u$ is constant on those fibers, and hence everywhere, since subharmonic functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere. Changes of the base frame give the transition law of a metric. These weights therefore define a semipositive metric $h_ B$ on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$. Fubini's theorem on local product charts, together with the fact that $\ell $ and the exceptional fibers have zero volume, shows that $h$ and $h_\ell \otimes f^*h_ B$ agree almost everywhere on $X_0$. By lemma 1.3.3, their local plurisubharmonic weights agree everywhere, including on the three exceptional fibers. This proves necessity.

Sufficiency. Let $h_ B$ be a semipositive metric on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)$, and represent it by a homogeneous weight $V$ on $\mathbb C^2\setminus \{ 0\} $, with coordinates $(\xi _1,\xi _2)$ and $a=\xi _1/\xi _2$. In the chart $\xi _2\ne 0$, if $\phi _ B$ is the weight of $h_ B$ in the frame given by the section $\xi _2$, set

\[ V(\xi _1,\xi _2)=\phi _ B(\xi _1/\xi _2)+\log |\xi _2|^2. \]

The other chart gives the same function, by the transition law of the metric. The function $V$ is plurisubharmonic and satisfies $V(\lambda \xi _1,\lambda \xi _2)=V(\xi _1,\xi _2)+\log |\lambda |^2$ for $\lambda \ne 0$. The weight difference between $h_ B$ and $h_{\mathrm{FS}}$ is bounded above on the compact base, so

\[ V(\xi _1,\xi _2)\leq \log (|\xi _1|^2+|\xi _2|^2)+C. \]

Hence $V$ extends plurisubharmonically across the origin, with value $-\infty $ there, by lemma 1.3.7. In each local frame of $L$, the function $V(F_1,F_2)$ is plurisubharmonic, and by homogeneity it transforms as the weight of a metric. Its restriction to each fiber $X_ t$ with $t\ne 0$ is not identically $-\infty $: there the two generating sections define a nonconstant map onto $\mathbb P^1$, and the weight of $h_ B$ is not identically $-\infty $. On the central fiber, the factorization in lemma 28.18.6 and homogeneity give exactly the divisor weight of $\ell $ plus the pullback of the weight of $h_ B$. This is the required extension over $\mathcal X$. $\square$

Corollary 28.18.9. Among all semipositive metrics on $L_0$ that extend, $h_*$ has the least singular weights: every such metric has weights $\psi $ satisfying $\psi \leq \psi _*+C$ in compatible local frames, where $\psi _*$ is the weight of $h_*$; and $h_*$ itself extends. Proof. By theorem 28.18.8, such a metric has the form $h_\ell \otimes f^*h_ B$. 28.19 Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The compact manifolds studied so far in this chapter carry many closed positive currents, coming from divisors, fibrations or blowups. Inoue surfaces and their higher-dimensional analogues, the Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds, behave very differently. They are built from number fields, contain few or no complex subvarieties, and carry a natural closed semipositive $(1,1)$-form whose kernel is a holomorphic foliation by complex affine spaces. These leaves are Liouville and fill out real tori. As a result, every potential in this class is, up to a fixed logarithmic term, a continuous convex function of the transverse coordinates, so the class has no singular potentials at all, even though it contains positive currents supported on real hypersurfaces. In dimension three, a natural Gauduchon metric has an exactly quadratic mass functional of indefinite sign. Its masses lie on both sides of the background mass. These threefolds carry no Hermitian metric whose $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ has a sign, so the known curvature criteria for bounded masses do not apply, and whether the masses remain bounded is an open question (33.3.16).

28.19.1 The construction

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $K$ be a number field of degree $s+2t$, with $s\geq 1$ real embeddings $\sigma _1,\ldots ,\sigma _ s$ and $t\geq 1$ pairs of complex conjugate embeddings $\sigma _{s+k},\overline{\sigma }_{s+k}$, $1\leq k\leq t$. Write $\mathcal O_ K$ for its ring of integers and $\mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ for the group of totally positive units, that is, of units $\varepsilon $ with $\sigma _ j(\varepsilon )>0$ for $1\leq j\leq s$. Let $\mathbb H\subset \mathbb C$ be the upper half-plane. Write points of $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$ as $(w,z)$, with $w_ j=x_ j+\mathrm i y_ j$, and put

\[ \sigma _{\mathbb R}(a)=(\sigma _1(a),\ldots ,\sigma _ s(a)),\qquad \sigma _{\mathbb C}(a)=(\sigma _{s+1}(a),\ldots ,\sigma _{s+t}(a)). \]

An element $a\in \mathcal O_ K$ acts by the translation $(w,z)\mapsto (w+\sigma _{\mathbb R}(a),z+\sigma _{\mathbb C}(a))$, and $\varepsilon \in \mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ acts by

\[ (w,z)\longmapsto \bigl (\sigma _1(\varepsilon )w_1,\ldots ,\sigma _ s(\varepsilon )w_ s,\, \sigma _{s+1}(\varepsilon )z_1,\ldots ,\sigma _{s+t}(\varepsilon )z_ t\bigr ). \]

A subgroup $U\subset \mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ is admissible if its image

\[ \Lambda _ U=\{ (\log \sigma _1(\varepsilon ),\ldots ,\log \sigma _ s(\varepsilon )):\varepsilon \in U\} \]

is a lattice in $\mathbb R^ s$. By Dirichlet's unit theorem, when $t=1$ every subgroup of finite index in $\mathcal O_ K^{*,+}$ is admissible. For admissible $U$, Oeljeklaus and Toma showed that the semidirect product $\Gamma =\mathcal O_ K\rtimes U$ acts freely and properly discontinuously on $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$ with compact quotient. The compact complex manifold

\[ X=X(K,U)=(\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t)/\Gamma , \qquad n=\dim X=s+t, \]

is the Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold of type $(s,t)$. The same construction works if $\mathcal O_ K$ is replaced by any lattice $L\subset K$ of rank $s+2t$ that is stable under multiplication by $U$; for $s=t=1$ this produces the Inoue surfaces $S_ M$. The action is still free and cocompact: a point fixed by $(a,\varepsilon )$ with $\varepsilon \neq 1$ would have real coordinates $w_ j$, since $\sigma _ j(\varepsilon )\neq 1$ by injectivity of $\sigma _ j$, and $(\sigma _{\mathbb R},\sigma _{\mathbb C})(L)$ is again a lattice in $\mathbb R^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$. All results of this section other than corollary 28.19.4 hold, with the same proofs, in this generality, with $L$ in place of $\mathcal O_ K$; the only property of $\mathcal O_ K$ they use is that it spans $K$ over $\mathbb Q$. Corollary 28.19.4 also holds for every Inoue surface $S_ M$, since these contain no curves. The sources are discussed in section 34.16.

The functions $y_ j=\operatorname {Im}w_ j$ are invariant under the translations and are multiplied by $\sigma _ j(\varepsilon )$ under the unit $\varepsilon $. Hence the closed real $(1,1)$-form

\[ \theta =-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\sum _{j=1}^ s\log y_ j =\sum _{j=1}^ s\frac{\mathrm i\, \mathrm dw_ j\wedge \mathrm d\overline w_ j}{8\pi y_ j^2} \]

on $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$ is $\Gamma $-invariant and descends to a closed semipositive form on $X$, still denoted $\theta $. It has rank $s$ everywhere, and its kernel is spanned by the vectors $\partial /\partial z_ k$. The potential $-\sum _ j\log y_ j$ itself is not invariant, since a unit adds $-\sum _ j\log \sigma _ j(\varepsilon )$ to it.

28.19.2 Potentials of the transverse form

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Two properties of the action force rigidity: the leaves $\{ w\} \times \mathbb C^ t$ are Liouville, and the real parts of the translations are dense.

Lemma 28.19.1. Since $t\geq 1$, the subgroup $\sigma _{\mathbb R}(\mathcal O_ K)$ is dense in $\mathbb R^ s$.

Proof. A subgroup $G$ of $\mathbb R^ s$ is dense unless there is a nonzero linear form $\xi $ on $\mathbb R^ s$ with $\xi (G)\subseteq \mathbb Z$: the closure of $G$ is the sum of a linear subspace $V$ and a discrete subgroup of a complement of $V$, and if $V\neq \mathbb R^ s$ one can take $\xi $ vanishing on $V$ and integral on that discrete subgroup. Suppose $\xi (\sigma _{\mathbb R}(a))=\sum _ j\xi _ j\sigma _ j(a)\in \mathbb Z$ for every $a\in \mathcal O_ K$. Since $\mathcal O_ K$ spans $K$ over $\mathbb Q$, the map $a\mapsto \sum _ j\xi _ j\sigma _ j(a)$ is a $\mathbb Q$-linear map $K\to \mathbb Q$. The trace form of $K$ is nondegenerate, so there is $b\in K$ with

\[ \sum _{j=1}^ s\xi _ j\sigma _ j(a)=\operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(ba) =\sum _{\sigma }\sigma (b)\sigma (a) \qquad (a\in K), \]

where $\sigma $ runs over all $s+2t$ embeddings of $K$ into $\mathbb C$. Distinct field embeddings are linearly independent over $\mathbb C$, by Dedekind's lemma on characters. Comparing the coefficients of a complex embedding $\sigma _{s+1}$, which occurs only on the right, gives $\sigma _{s+1}(b)=0$. Hence $b=0$, and then $\xi _ j=\sigma _ j(b)=0$ for every $j$. $\square$

For $\varepsilon \in U$ and $y\in (0,\infty )^ s$, write $\sigma (\varepsilon )y=(\sigma _1(\varepsilon )y_1,\ldots ,\sigma _ s(\varepsilon )y_ s)$.

Theorem 28.19.2. Let $\mathcal F_ U$ be the set of convex functions $f:(0,\infty )^ s\to \mathbb R$ such that

28.68
\begin{equation} f(\sigma (\varepsilon )y)=f(y)-\sum _{j=1}^ s\log \sigma _ j(\varepsilon ) \qquad (\varepsilon \in U). \end{equation}

For $f\in \mathcal F_ U$, the function $u_ f(w,z)=f(y)+\sum _ j\log y_ j$ is $\Gamma $-invariant, and $f\mapsto u_ f$ is a bijection from $\mathcal F_ U$ onto $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. In particular, every $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ is continuous and depends only on $y=\operatorname {Im}w$.

Proof. We first note that a function $g$ on $\mathbb H^ s$ that depends only on $y$ is plurisubharmonic if and only if, as a function on $(0,\infty )^ s$, it is convex (with values in $[-\infty ,+\infty )$ and not identically $-\infty $). For smooth $g$ this follows from $\partial ^2g/\partial w_ j\partial \overline w_ k=\frac14\partial ^2g/\partial y_ j\partial y_ k$. In general, convolution with a smooth radial kernel in $w$ produces smooth functions of $y$ alone; they decrease to $g$ if $g$ is plurisubharmonic, and they converge locally uniformly to $g$ if $g$ is convex, because a convex function that is not identically $-\infty $ on the open convex set $(0,\infty )^ s$ is finite and continuous there. Since decreasing limits of convex functions are convex and locally uniform limits of plurisubharmonic functions are plurisubharmonic, the claim follows.

The identity (28.68) says exactly that $u_ f$ is invariant under $U$, and $u_ f$ is clearly invariant under the translations. On the universal cover, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ f=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f(y)\geq 0$. Hence $u_ f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $f$ is recovered from $u_ f$, so the map is injective.

Conversely, let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and let $\tilde u$ be its lift to $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$. Since $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tilde u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\tilde u-\sum _ j\log y_ j)$ there, the function $q=\tilde u-\sum _ j\log y_ j$ is plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb H^ s\times \mathbb C^ t$.

Step 1: constancy on the leaves. Fix $w^0\in \mathbb H^ s$. The restriction of $q$ to the slice $\{ w^0\} \times \mathbb C^ t$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $, and it is bounded above because $u$ is upper semicontinuous on the compact manifold $X$. A plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb C^ t$ that is bounded above is constant, since its restriction to every complex line through two given points is a subharmonic function on $\mathbb C$ bounded above. Hence $\tilde u(w,z)=\tilde u(w,0)$ for all $(w,z)$. In particular, the function $F(w)=q(w,0)$ is not identically $-\infty $, because $\tilde u$ is not, and it is plurisubharmonic on $\mathbb H^ s$.

Step 2: invariance under real translations. The translations by $\mathcal O_ K$ and Step 1 give $F(w+\sigma _{\mathbb R}(a))=F(w)$ for every $a\in \mathcal O_ K$, since $\log y_ j$ does not change under real translations. For $r>0$, the averages $F_ r$ of $F$ over the Euclidean balls of radius $r$ are defined on $\{ w:\min _ jy_ j>r\} $ and continuous there, because $F$ is locally integrable, and they are invariant under the same translations. By lemma 28.19.1 and continuity, each $F_ r$ is invariant under all translations by $\mathbb R^ s$. Since $F_ r$ decreases to $F$ as $r\downarrow 0$, the function $F$ is invariant under all real translations as well, so $F(w)=f(y)$ for a function $f$ on $(0,\infty )^ s$.

Step 3: convexity and automorphy. Since $F=f(y)$ is plurisubharmonic, the first paragraph of the proof shows that $f$ is convex, finite and continuous. Finally, $u=u_ f$, and invariance of $u$ under $U$ is (28.68). $\square$

Corollary 28.19.3. On every Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold, all functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ are continuous. Consequently, they all have the ordinary singularity type of $0$, all their Lelong numbers vanish, all their multiplier ideals are trivial, and $P_\theta [u]=V_\theta =0$ for every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Moreover $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=0$ for every $u$, so $\theta $ is nef but not big.

Proof. Continuous functions on a compact manifold are bounded, so any two of them are equivalent, and their Lelong numbers and multiplier ideals are those of a bounded function. Since $\theta \geq 0$, the constant $0$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, so $V_\theta =0$, and $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [0]=V_\theta $. By theorem 28.19.2, locally $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f(y)$ with $f$ continuous and depending on the $s$ real variables $y_ j$; the Bedford–Taylor products of such a current vanish in degree $s+1\leq n$, because they are limits of the corresponding products for smooth convex approximants of $f$, whose complex Hessians have rank at most $s$. Thus $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=0$. The form $\theta $ is smooth and semipositive, hence nef. Suppose that $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfied $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \omega $ with $\delta >0$. The function $\rho $ is continuous, and on a small coordinate ball with coordinates $\zeta $ we may write $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ with $a$ smooth and $\delta \omega \geq \delta '\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|\zeta |^2$ with $\delta '>0$. Then $a+\rho -\delta '|\zeta |^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and expanding $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a+\rho ))^ n$ as a sum of positive mixed Bedford–Taylor products gives $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\rho )\geq \delta '^ n(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|\zeta |^2)^ n\neq 0$ on the ball, a contradiction. Hence $\theta $ is not big. $\square$

Singular currents nevertheless occur in this class. Let $s=t=1$, so that $X$ is an Inoue surface and $U$ is generated by a unit $\varepsilon _0$ with $\lambda =\sigma _1(\varepsilon _0)>1$. Fix $y_0>0$, put $y_ k=\lambda ^ ky_0$ for $k\in \mathbb Z$, and let $f$ be the continuous function that is affine on each interval $[y_ k,y_{k+1}]$ with slope

\[ a_ k=-\frac{\log \lambda }{(\lambda -1)y_ k}, \qquad f(y_0)=0. \]

Then $a_ k(y_{k+1}-y_ k)=-\log \lambda $, so $f(\lambda y)=f(y)-\log \lambda $, and the slopes increase because $a_{k+1}=a_ k/\lambda $ and $a_ k<0$. Thus $f\in \mathcal F_ U$. On the universal cover,

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ f=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f(y)=\frac1{4\pi }\sum _{k\in \mathbb Z}(a_ k-a_{k-1})\, \delta (y-y_ k)\, \mathrm dx\wedge \mathrm dy, \]

with $a_ k-a_{k-1}>0$. The closed positive current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ f$ therefore has a continuous potential but is supported on the compact real hypersurface of $X$ formed by the images of the hyperplanes $\{ y=y_ k\} $, a three-dimensional torus that is a union of leaves of the foliation $\{ w=\text{const}\} $.

On an Inoue surface, $\theta $ represents the canonical Bott–Chern class. Indeed, the Tricerri metric $\omega _ T=8\pi \theta +y\, \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$ has $\omega _ T^2=(2/y)\, \mathrm i\, \mathrm dw\wedge \mathrm d\overline w\wedge \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$, so its normalized Chern–Ricci form $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (\omega _ T^2/\mathrm i\, \mathrm dw\wedge \mathrm d\overline w\wedge \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z)$, which is $-\mathrm i\partial \overline\partial \log \det \omega _ T$ divided by $2\pi $ and represents $c_1^{BC}(X)$, equals $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log y=-\theta $. Hence theorem 28.19.2 describes all positive currents in $c_1^{BC}(K_ X)$, and none of them has a pole.

Arbitrary backgrounds behave differently, since a Hermitian form has potentials with logarithmic poles. Nevertheless, when $t=1$ their singularities are confined to finite sets. By a theorem of Ornea and Verbitsky (section 34.16), an Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold with $t=1$ contains no complex subvarieties of positive dimension.

Corollary 28.19.4. Let $X$ be an Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold with $t=1$, let $\eta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\eta )$. For every $c>0$, the set $\{ x:\nu (u,x)\geq c\} $ is finite. Consequently, for every $k>0$ the multiplier ideal $\mathcal I(ku)$ is trivial outside a finite set, and $\mathcal I(ku)$ has finite colength.

Proof. By Siu's theorem (1.13.1), the set $E_ c=\{ \nu (u,\cdot )\geq c\} $ is a compact analytic subset of $X$. It is a proper subset, because $\nu (u,x)=0$ outside the pluripolar set $\{ u=-\infty \} $, which has Lebesgue measure zero. Hence $E_ c$ is finite by the theorem of Ornea and Verbitsky. By Skoda's integrability criterion, recalled in [SGPT], $\mathcal I(ku)_ x=\mathcal O_{X,x}$ whenever $k\nu (u,x)<1$, so $\mathcal I(ku)$ is trivial outside the finite set $E_{1/k}$. A coherent ideal sheaf that is trivial outside a finite set has finite colength. $\square$

We shall use an elementary property of lattices in the plane: every lattice $\Lambda \subset \mathbb R^2$ contains vectors in each of the four open quadrants. Proposition 28.19.5. Let $X$ be an Oeljeklaus–Toma threefold of type $(2,1)$ with the Hermitian form $\omega $ above.

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(y_1y_2)\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2, \]

\[ H=I+D^2g-\operatorname {diag}(\partial _1g,\partial _2g), \]

as one checks from $y_ iy_ j\, \partial ^2f/\partial y_ i\partial y_ j=\partial ^2\Phi /\partial \varsigma _ i\partial \varsigma _ j-\delta _{ij}\, \partial \Phi /\partial \varsigma _ i$ for $\Phi (\varsigma )=f(\mathrm e^{\varsigma })=g(\varsigma )-\varsigma _1-\varsigma _2$. By the continuity of bounded Monge–Ampère products under uniform convergence and dominated convergence, it suffices to prove all assertions for smooth $g$ with $H\geq 0$.

For such $g$, the function $u$ depends only on $y$, so $\partial u/\partial w_ j=-\frac{\mathrm i}2\partial u/\partial y_ j$ and the integrand in (2) is $\frac14\partial _{y_1}u\, \partial _{y_2}u=\partial _1g\, \partial _2g/(4y_1y_2)$. \[ \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\frac{\int _ X\omega ^3}{|T_ U|}\int _{T_ U} \bigl (1+\partial _1g\, \partial _2g\bigr )\, \mathrm d\varsigma =\frac{\int _ X\omega ^3}{|T_ U|}\int _{T_ U}PQ\, \mathrm d\varsigma , \]

The diagonal entry $H_{11}=P-\partial _1P\geq 0$ says that $\mathrm e^{-\varsigma _1}P$ is nonincreasing in $\varsigma _1$, that is,

\begin{equation} P(\varsigma +\tau e_1)\leq \mathrm e^{\tau }P(\varsigma )\qquad (\tau \geq 0). \end{equation}

If $P(\varsigma )<0$, the right side tends to $-\infty $ as $\tau \to \infty $, which is impossible for the bounded function $P$; hence $P\geq 0$, and likewise $Q\geq 0$. Thus $\partial _ jg\leq 1$. Hence $\operatorname {osc}g\leq L$, with $L=|\lambda |_1+|\lambda '|_1$ depending only on $\Lambda _ U$.

Averaging (28.70) in the form $P(\varsigma -\tau e_1)\geq \mathrm e^{-\tau }P(\varsigma )$ over $\tau \in [0,1]$ gives

\[ (1-\mathrm e^{-1})P(\varsigma )\leq \int _0^1P(\varsigma -\tau e_1)\, \mathrm d\tau =1-g(\varsigma )+g(\varsigma -e_1)\leq 1+L. \]

On the other hand, averaging $P(\varsigma )\geq \mathrm e^{-\tau }P(\varsigma +\tau e_1)$ over $\tau \in [0,R]$ with $R=\max (2L,1)$ gives

\[ P(\varsigma )\geq \frac{\mathrm e^{-R}}R\int _0^ RP(\varsigma +\tau e_1)\, \mathrm d\tau =\frac{\mathrm e^{-R}}R\bigl (R-g(\varsigma +Re_1)+g(\varsigma )\bigr ) \geq \frac{\mathrm e^{-R}}2. \]

The same bounds hold for $Q$. Therefore the average of $PQ$ over $T_ U$ lies between $\mathrm e^{-2R}/4$ and $\bigl ((1+L)/(1-\mathrm e^{-1})\bigr )^2$, which proves (3).

(4) Let $k$ belong to the dual lattice $\Lambda _ U^*=\{ k\in \mathbb R^2:\langle k,\lambda \rangle \in \mathbb Z\text{ for all }\lambda \in \Lambda _ U\} $, and put $g_\epsilon (\varsigma )=\epsilon \cos (2\pi \langle k,\varsigma \rangle )$. For small $\epsilon >0$, the matrix $H$ of $g_\epsilon $ is positive definite, so $u_\epsilon =g_\epsilon (\varsigma )$ is a smooth function with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\epsilon >0$. By (3),

\[ \frac{\mathrm M_\omega (u_\epsilon )}{\int _ X\omega ^3} =1+\frac1{|T_ U|}\int _{T_ U}\partial _1g_\epsilon \, \partial _2g_\epsilon \, \mathrm d\varsigma =1+2\pi ^2\epsilon ^2k_1k_2. \]

Since the lattice $\Lambda _ U^*$ has vectors in every open quadrant, we may choose $k$ with $k_1k_2>0$, respectively $k_1k_2<0$, which gives the two inequalities. $\square$

For a general smooth potential, averaging over the fibers separates the invariant part from the fluctuations. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ let $Au$ be the average of $u$ over the fibers of $X\to T_ U$, that is, over the translations $(w,z)\mapsto (w+r,z+\zeta )$ with $(r,\zeta )\in (\mathbb R^2\times \mathbb C)/(\sigma _{\mathbb R},\sigma _{\mathbb C})(\mathcal O_ K)$. These translations preserve $\omega $ on the universal cover. They do not descend to $X$ individually, but conjugation by a unit $\varepsilon $ carries the translation by $(r,\zeta )$ to the translation by $(\sigma _1(\varepsilon )r_1,\sigma _2(\varepsilon )r_2,\sigma _3(\varepsilon )\zeta )$, and this automorphism of the translation torus preserves its Haar measure because $\varepsilon $ has norm one. Hence $Au$ is a well-defined $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function depending only on $y$, and $c_ U^{-1}\int _ X\omega ^3\leq \mathrm M_\omega (Au)\leq c_ U\int _ X\omega ^3$ by proposition 28.19.5. For smooth $u$, the integral of a $y$-derivative of $u-Au$ over a fiber vanishes, and so does the integral of an $x$-derivative. The cross terms between $Au$ and $u-Au$ in proposition 28.19.5(2) therefore integrate to zero, and

28.71
\begin{equation} \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\mathrm M_\omega (Au)+\frac{3}{16\pi ^3}\int _ X \operatorname {Re}\Bigl (\frac{\partial v}{\partial w_1}\, \overline{\frac{\partial v}{\partial w_2}}\Bigr )\mathrm dV_0, \qquad v=u-Au. \end{equation}

Since the extrema of the masses of bounded potentials equal the extrema over smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic potentials (proposition 6.5.2, theorem 26.14.3), $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $ if and only if the fluctuation term in (28.71) is bounded above on smooth $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions; likewise, $v_-(\omega )>0$ if and only if $\mathrm M_\omega (Au)$ plus the fluctuation term is bounded below by a positive constant on such functions.

28.19.4 Hermitian metrics whose ddc has a sign

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Finally, we show that no Hermitian metric on these manifolds has a $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of constant sign, so that curvature criteria based on such metrics cannot settle the boundedness of the masses.

Proposition 28.19.6. Let $X$ be an Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold of type $(s,1)$ with $s\geq 2$. Then $X$ admits no Hermitian form $\widetilde\omega $ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde\omega $ is semipositive, or seminegative, on every complex $2$-plane.

Proof. Suppose $\widetilde\omega $ is such a form, with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde\omega \geq 0$ on every complex $2$-plane; the other case is analogous. Average the lift of $\widetilde\omega $ over the translations $(w,z)\mapsto (w+r,z+\zeta )$, $(r,\zeta )\in (\mathbb R^ s\times \mathbb C)/(\sigma _{\mathbb R},\sigma _{\mathbb C})(\mathcal O_ K)$, as in the definition of $Au$ above. The averaged form $\overline\omega $ is again $\Gamma $-invariant and Hermitian, its coefficients in the frame $\mathrm dw_1,\ldots ,\mathrm dw_ s,\mathrm dz$ depend only on $y$, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\overline\omega \geq 0$ on complex $2$-planes, because translations are holomorphic and positivity on $2$-planes is preserved by averaging.

Let $h(y)>0$ be the coefficient of $\mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$ in $\overline\omega $. Invariance under a unit $\varepsilon $ gives $h(\sigma (\varepsilon )y)|\sigma _{s+1}(\varepsilon )|^2=h(y)$, that is, $h(\sigma (\varepsilon )y)=\sigma _1(\varepsilon )\cdots \sigma _ s(\varepsilon )\, h(y)$, since $\varepsilon $ has norm one. Hence $h/(y_1\cdots y_ s)$ is invariant under $U$, and since $\Lambda _ U$ is a lattice it is bounded between two positive constants:

28.72
\begin{equation} c\, y_1\cdots y_ s\leq h(y)\leq C\, y_1\cdots y_ s. \end{equation}

Let $v$ be a nonzero vector in the span of $\partial /\partial w_1,\ldots ,\partial /\partial w_ s$, and let $P$ be the complex $2$-plane spanned by $v$ and $\partial /\partial z$. Write $\overline\omega =\sum _{a,b}g_{ab}(y)\, \mathrm i\, \xi _ a\wedge \overline\xi _ b$ with $\xi =(\mathrm dw_1,\ldots ,\mathrm dw_ s,\mathrm dz)$. Since the forms $\xi _ a$ are closed, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\overline\omega =\sum _{a,b}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_{ab}\wedge \mathrm i\, \xi _ a\wedge \overline\xi _ b$, and each $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_{ab}$ has only $w$-components. A term restricted to $P$ is nonzero only if it contains both $\mathrm dz$ and $\mathrm d\overline z$, which happens only for $\xi _ a=\xi _ b=\mathrm dz$. Hence the restriction of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\overline\omega $ to $P$ equals the restriction of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\overline z$, and its sign is that of the value of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ on $v$. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\geq 0$ in the $w$-variables, and $h$ is convex in $y$.

Let $R>1$ and put $A=(R,R^{-1},1,\ldots ,1)$, $B=(R^{-1},R,1,\ldots ,1)$ and $M=(A+B)/2$. By (28.72), $h(A),h(B)\leq C$, whereas $h(M)\geq c(R+R^{-1})^2/4$, which contradicts convexity for large $R$. In the seminegative case, $h$ is concave, so $t\mapsto h(t,\ldots ,t)$ grows at most linearly as $t\to \infty $; this contradicts the lower bound $h(t,\ldots ,t)\geq ct^ s$ with $s\geq 2$. $\square$

For $s=t=1$ the same averaging produces a coefficient $h$ comparable to $y$, which may be affine; indeed the Tricerri metric is pluriclosed. In dimension three, the curvature criteria for finite upper and positive lower mass recalled in section 34.16 require a metric with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \geq 0$, respectively $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \leq 0$, and proposition 28.19.6 excludes both on threefolds of type $(2,1)$.
