5.28 Products with smooth backgrounds5E2B332B89B4

5E2B332B89B4

The Bedford–Taylor construction also defines products of currents of the form $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $u$ locally bounded: one expands them into Hessians of plurisubharmonic functions and smooth forms. Since the result does not depend on the local correction, these products are defined globally. Their positivity is not visible term by term; it follows from the lower curvature bound by freezing the coefficients of the background. For a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ and a function $v$, we write $\theta _ v=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$, and similarly $\omega _ v=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ for other backgrounds. Fix $1\leq k\leq n$.

Let $u$ be locally bounded and $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. On a relatively compact coordinate ball with coordinates $z$, choose $A>0$ such that $p=u+q$ is plurisubharmonic, where $q=A|z|^2$. Set $\beta =\theta -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$ and define

C87F0ACE9C76 5.28.1
\begin{equation} (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ k :=\sum _{j=0}^ k\binom {k}{j}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^ j\wedge \beta ^{k-j}. \end{equation}

Each summand is a product from theorem 5.27.1 multiplied by a smooth form. The individual terms of this expansion need not be positive.

Proposition 5.28.2.3EDAB69A7289 The construction (5.28.1) and its mixed version are independent of the local correction $q$ and of the decomposition of each current into a smooth form and the Hessian of a potential. They define global products of currents with locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic potentials. These products are symmetric, multilinear and plurifinely local, and they do not charge pluripolar sets. If every factor is positive, their mixed product is positive.

Proof. On the intersection of two coordinate balls, the two corrected potentials differ by a smooth function. The rule $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p+f)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$, for bounded $p$ and smooth $f$ in bounded local products, follows by smooth approximation; together with the binomial formula, it shows that the two expansions agree. The same argument handles a smooth change in the decomposition of a current. Moreover, any two decompositions of the same $(1,1)$-current differ by a smooth function: the difference of the potentials has a smooth Hessian, and taking its local Euclidean trace and applying elliptic regularity shows that it is smooth. Symmetry, multilinearity and locality then follow term by term. Bounded Bedford–Taylor products wedged with smooth forms have locally finite variation and do not charge pluripolar sets, so these properties pass to the finite expansion as well.

It remains to prove positivity, which does not follow from the signs of the summands. Write the factors as $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$, $1\leq i\leq k$, where each $\theta _ i$ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form and each $u_ i$ is locally bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic, and assume $T_ i\geq 0$. Fix $\varepsilon >0$, a point of the coordinate ball and a positive constant form $\kappa $. On a sufficiently small neighborhood of the point, each $\theta _ i$ is close to a constant, hence closed, form $\theta _ i'$:

\[ -\varepsilon \kappa \leq \theta _ i-\theta _ i' \leq \varepsilon \kappa . \]

Then $S_ i=\theta _ i'+\varepsilon \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i\geq T_ i$ is a positive closed current there, with a bounded local plurisubharmonic potential, and

\[ T_ i+2\varepsilon \kappa =S_ i+(\theta _ i-\theta _ i'+\varepsilon \kappa ), \]

where the last term is a smooth semipositive form. Expanding the product of the currents $T_ i+2\varepsilon \kappa $ therefore gives a sum of positive currents, so this product is positive near the chosen point. Such neighborhoods, although they depend on $\varepsilon $, cover the coordinate ball, so the product of the $T_ i+2\varepsilon \kappa $ is positive on the whole ball. By multilinearity, it converges weakly to the product of the $T_ i$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$, which proves positivity. $\square$

The local expansion also gives continuity along decreasing sequences of uniformly locally bounded potentials: apply theorem 5.27.1 to the corrected potentials and sum the finitely many terms. Since $X$ is compact, every bounded top-degree product has finite total mass. For bounded $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ we write $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$.

Decreasing maxima compare the products of two ordered potentials on the set where they are equal. The comparison also holds in the presence of fixed positive factors, which will be useful in integration by parts.

Lemma 5.28.3.5087175B2C39 Let $a\leq b$ be locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on an $n$-dimensional complex manifold, and let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form such that $\theta _ a,\theta _ b\geq 0$. For $1\leq k\leq n$, let $W$ be a product of $n-k$ positive currents with smooth backgrounds and locally bounded potentials, with $W=1$ when $k=n$. Then

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ a=b\} }\theta _ a^ k\wedge W \leq \mathbf1_{\{ a=b\} }\theta _ b^ k\wedge W. \]

Proof. The functions $m_\delta =\max (a+\delta ,b)$ satisfy $b\leq m_\delta \leq b+\delta $ and decrease to $b$ as $\delta \downarrow 0$. By lemma 5.24.1 and proposition 5.28.2, the products $\theta _{m_\delta }^ k\wedge W$ are positive, and by bounded decreasing continuity they converge weakly to $\theta _ b^ k\wedge W$. On the plurifine open set $\{ a+\delta >b\} $, which contains $\{ a=b\} $, we have $m_\delta =a+\delta $, so plurifine locality identifies the restriction of $\theta _{m_\delta }^ k\wedge W$ to this set with that of $\theta _ a^ k\wedge W$. Hence, for every compact set $K\subset \{ a=b\} $ in a coordinate chart, the upper semicontinuity of the mass of compact sets under weak convergence gives

\[ \int _ K\theta _ a^ k\wedge W =\lim _{\delta \downarrow 0}\int _ K\theta _{m_\delta }^ k\wedge W \leq \int _ K\theta _ b^ k\wedge W. \]

Since the measures are locally finite, inner regularity extends this inequality to all Borel subsets of the contact set, and a countable cover by charts proves the assertion. $\square$

The products commute with pullback by holomorphic submersions. Here the pullback of a current is defined in local product coordinates by pulling back its distributional coefficients.

Lemma 5.28.4.636D22C6199B Let $\pi :Z\to Y$ be a holomorphic submersion of complex manifolds. For $1\leq r\leq \dim Z$, let $u_1,\ldots ,u_ r$ be locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $Y$, and let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ r$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $Y$. Then

\[ \bigwedge _{i=1}^ r\bigl (\pi ^*\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ i\circ \pi )\bigr ) =\pi ^*\! \left(\bigwedge _{i=1}^ r(\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)\right). \]

For $r>\dim Y$, the product on the left vanishes, and the right-hand side is interpreted as zero.

Proof. Work in product coordinates in which $\pi $ is a coordinate projection. On a smaller chart of $Y$, choose smooth quadratic functions $q_ i$ such that $p_ i=u_ i+q_ i$ is plurisubharmonic, and approximate each $p_ i$ by a decreasing sequence of smooth plurisubharmonic functions. Their pullbacks have the same properties on the product chart, and for smooth forms pullback commutes with wedge products. Applying bounded decreasing continuity to the local expansions (5.28.1) on both charts gives the asserted identity, because pullback of currents by the projection is weakly continuous: pairing the pullback with a compactly supported test form amounts to pairing the original current with the integral of the test form over the fiber variables. If $r>\dim Y$, every smooth approximating product vanishes for degree reasons, and so does its limit. $\square$

For $C^{1,1}$ potentials, these products of currents agree with the products of their measurable Hessian coefficients.

Proposition 5.28.5.6CBD4E4A94B6 Let $u_1,\ldots ,u_ k$ be locally $C^{1,1}$ quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, and let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ k$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms. Then the bounded product

\[ (\theta _1+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\theta _ k+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ k) \]

is the form with locally bounded measurable coefficients obtained by taking the wedge product of the factors pointwise almost everywhere.

Proof. On a smaller coordinate ball, add a common smooth quadratic function $q$ such that each $u_ i+q$ is plurisubharmonic, and convolve with a smooth radial probability kernel. The Hessians of the convolutions are uniformly locally bounded and converge to the weak Hessians in every $L^ s$ space with $s<\infty $, so their finite products converge in $L^1$ on smaller balls. Since the potentials converge uniformly, the continuity of bounded Bedford–Taylor products identifies this limit with the pluripotential product. Expanding with respect to the smooth correction, as in (5.28.1), proves the assertion for the original factors. $\square$

When the Hessian coefficients are bounded, a positive lower bound for the top-degree product gives a lower bound for the current itself.

Proposition 5.28.6.2A843EB4B4AE Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, let $h$ be a Hermitian form on $X$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cap C^{1,1}(X)$. If

\[ (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n\geq a h^ n \]

for a constant $a>0$, then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \delta h$ as a current for some $\delta >0$.

Proof. Put $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. Its measurable coefficients are bounded on the compact manifold $X$, so there is $K>0$ such that, almost everywhere, the eigenvalues of $T$ relative to $h$ lie in $[0,K]$. By proposition 5.28.5, their product is the density of $T^ n$ with respect to $h^ n$, and hence it is at least $a$. Each eigenvalue is therefore at least $a/K^{n-1}$ almost everywhere. This inequality of matrices almost everywhere gives the asserted inequality of currents with $\delta =a/K^{n-1}$. $\square$

The mixed Monge–Ampère inequality of theorem 5.27.5 also holds after a smooth background is added. In the following three lemmas, $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$ is an open set and $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$.

Lemma 5.28.7.6C81352D9EE7 Let $T_ i=\vartheta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i\geq 0$, $i=1,2$, where the $\vartheta _ i$ are smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $U$ and the $u_ i$ are locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $U$. If

\[ T_ i^ n\geq F_ i\beta ^ n, \qquad F_ i\geq 0\text{ measurable}, \]

then, for every integer $0\leq k\leq n$,

C06268DF902D 5.28.8
\begin{equation} T_1^ k\wedge T_2^{n-k} \geq F_1^{k/n}F_2^{(n-k)/n}\beta ^ n. \end{equation}

Consequently, for $a,b\geq 0$,

4547B4E0976D 5.28.9
\begin{equation} (aT_1+bT_2)^ n \geq \bigl (aF_1^{1/n}+bF_2^{1/n}\bigr )^ n\beta ^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. Fix $W\Subset U$. Since the forms $\vartheta _ i$ are smooth, there is a constant $C$ such that, for every $x\in W$ and every small ball $B_ r(x)$, the constant forms

\[ A_ i=\vartheta _ i(x)+Cr\beta \]

satisfy $0\leq S_ i\leq 2Cr\beta $ on $B_ r(x)$, where $S_ i=A_ i-\vartheta _ i$. If $q_ i$ is a quadratic potential for $A_ i$, then $u_ i+q_ i$ is plurisubharmonic on $B_ r(x)$ and

\[ \bigl (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ i+q_ i)\bigr )^ n=(T_ i+S_ i)^ n\geq F_ i\beta ^ n. \]

Apply theorem 5.27.5 to these two closed Hessians. After expansion, every term other than $T_1^ k\wedge T_2^{n-k}$ contains some $S_ i$ and is therefore bounded by $C'r\mu $, where

\[ \mu =\sum _{\ell +m\leq n-1}T_1^\ell \wedge T_2^ m\wedge \beta ^{n-\ell -m} \]

is a locally finite measure.

A finite cover and a partition of unity give, on $W$,

\[ T_1^ k\wedge T_2^{n-k} \geq F_1^{k/n}F_2^{(n-k)/n}\beta ^ n-C'r\mu . \]

Letting $r\downarrow 0$ proves (5.28.8), and the binomial expansion then gives (5.28.9). $\square$

Lemma 5.28.10.C5B4B944142C Let $\vartheta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $U$. Suppose that $u$ is continuous, $T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$, and $T^ n\geq g^ n\beta ^ n$ with $g\geq 0$ continuous. For a nonnegative convolution kernel $\chi _\delta $ of mass one,

D12EFD5440D2 5.28.11
\begin{equation} (T*\chi _\delta )^ n\geq (g*\chi _\delta )^ n\beta ^ n \end{equation}

on every smaller domain on which the convolution is defined.

Proof. Approximate the convolution by finite convex combinations of translates, and apply (5.28.9), extended by induction to finitely many terms. Along these approximations, the background forms converge smoothly and the potentials converge uniformly on compact sets. To pass to the limit, add a common quadratic function and write the difference of two $n$-th powers as a telescoping sum. After pairing with a test form, integration by parts bounds each term by the uniform distance between the potentials times the mass of a mixed current, and successive integration against nested cutoff functions bounds these masses uniformly. Thus the products of the bounded potentials converge, and (5.28.11) follows. $\square$

For a positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $U$, write $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ for the measurable $(1,1)$-form whose coefficients are the absolutely continuous parts of the coefficients of $T$, and define $\det _\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ by $(T_{\mathrm{ac}})^ n=(\det _\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}})\beta ^ n$. When the top-degree product has a continuous density, the convolution estimate identifies this determinant.

Lemma 5.28.12.00AB47766404 Let $u$ be continuous and $T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$, with $\vartheta $ a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $U$. If $T^ n=F\beta ^ n$ for a continuous function $F\geq 0$, then

\[ \det _\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}}=F\qquad \text{almost everywhere}. \]

Proof. Take a nonnegative smooth radial mollifier $\chi _\delta $ supported in the ball of radius $\delta $. Then $T*\chi _\delta \to T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ almost everywhere. Indeed, the convolutions of the absolutely continuous parts converge at Lebesgue points of their densities, while the convolution of the singular part $\lambda _ s$ of a coefficient measure is bounded by $C\delta ^{-2n}|\lambda _ s|(B_\delta )$, which tends to zero almost everywhere by the differentiation theorem for measures. Applying lemma 5.28.10 with $g=F^{1/n}$ and letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ gives $\det _\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}}\geq F$.

For the reverse inequality, write the convolved current as the Hessian of a decreasing regularization of a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function plus a convergent smooth correction. The continuity of bounded products gives $(T*\chi _\delta )^ n\rightharpoonup T^ n$. Fatou's lemma, applied against a nonnegative compactly supported test function, then gives $\det _\beta T_{\mathrm{ac}}\, \beta ^ n\leq T^ n=F\beta ^ n$. $\square$

Lemma 5.28.13.D263A93C1D69 Let $h\leq a$ be locally bounded $\theta $-plurisubharmonic functions, and let $\chi $ be a smooth function on a neighborhood of $(-\infty ,0]$ with $0\leq \chi '\leq 1$ and $\chi ''\geq 0$. Then $w=a+\chi (h-a)$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and

\[ \theta _ w=(1-\chi '(h-a))\theta _ a+\chi '(h-a)\theta _ h +\chi ''(h-a)\, \mathrm{d}(h-a)\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(h-a). \]

In particular, $\theta _ w^ n\geq \chi '(h-a)^ n\theta _ h^ n$. The positivity of $\theta _ w$ and this inequality also hold for convex functions $\chi $ of class $C^{1,1}$ on compact intervals with $0\leq \chi '\leq 1$.

Proof. The function $G(x,y)=x+\chi (y-x)$, for $y\leq x$, is convex and nondecreasing in each variable: its first derivatives are $1-\chi '$ and $\chi '$, and its Hessian is

\[ \chi ''\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\geq 0. \]

For smooth potentials, the chain rule gives the identity. For bounded potentials, add a common quadratic correction and convolve. Convolution preserves the order of the potentials, and since $G(x+r,y+r)=r+G(x,y)$, adding the same correction to both arguments adds it to $G$. The bounded chain rule (5.27.9) and its products with the gradient term give the identity in the limit. Freezing the smooth background as in the proof of proposition 5.28.2 preserves the exact lower bound. Every term on the right is positive, and the positivity of bounded mixed products gives the last assertion.

For a convex weight of class $C^{1,1}$, extend it convexly across zero with derivative in $[0,1]$, and smooth it in one variable. The smooth convex weights and their first derivatives converge uniformly on compact intervals, and the derivatives stay in $[0,1]$. On each smaller coordinate ball, the corresponding potentials converge uniformly to $w$. The continuity of bounded products gives convergence of their top powers, while the weights on the right-hand side converge uniformly against the fixed measure $\theta _ h^ n$. Passing to the limit proves the positivity and the product inequality with the original smooth background. $\square$
