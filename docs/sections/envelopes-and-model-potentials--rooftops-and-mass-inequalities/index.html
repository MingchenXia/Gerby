16.23 Rooftops and mass inequalities4CC29395A55C

4CC29395A55C

Two potentials need not have a common minorant, as example 16.11.7 shows. We prove that a strict mass inequality guarantees one, and that the masses of $u$, $v$, $\max \{ u,v\} $ and $P_\theta (u,v)$ satisfy the inequality of theorem 16.23.5. Contact concentration for model potentials gives these results for model potentials, and extrapolation transfers them to arbitrary potentials of full mass relative to model potentials. As in the preceding section, $\{ \theta \} $ is big.

Proposition 16.23.1.3499B9FD713C Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle >\int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ u,v\} }^ n\rangle . \]

Then $P_\theta (u,v)\ne -\infty $.

Proof. Subtracting the same constant from $u$ and $v$ changes neither the hypothesis nor the conclusion, so we may assume that $u,v\leq 0$. Put $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ u,v\} }^ n\rangle $ and $\phi =P_\theta [\max \{ u,v\} ]$. By monotonicity $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle \leq m$ and $\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle \leq m$, so the strict inequality forces both masses to be positive. Hence $m>0$, and by theorem 16.22.11, $\phi $ is a model potential of mass $m$. Moreover $u,v\leq \max \{ u,v\} \leq \phi $, because the nonpositive potential $\max \{ u,v\} $ is a competitor for $\phi $.

Since $\mu _ u$ and $\mu _ v$ do not charge pole sets, $\mu _ u(\{ u>\phi -k\} )\to \int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle $ and $\mu _ v(\{ v>\phi -k\} )\to \int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle $ as $k\to \infty $. Choose $k>0$ so large that

\[ \mu _ u(\{ u>\phi -k\} )+\mu _ v(\{ v>\phi -k\} )>m. \]

For $C>k$ put

\[ u_ C=\max \{ u,\phi -C\} ,\quad v_ C=\max \{ v,\phi -C\} ,\quad w_ C=P_\theta (u_ C,v_ C). \]

Since $\phi -C$ is a common minorant, all three potentials have the singularity type of $\phi $ and mass $m$. As $C\to \infty $, $w_ C$ decreases to $P_\theta (u,v)$ by proposition 16.11.6. Suppose that $P_\theta (u,v)=-\infty $, so that $w_ C$ decreases to $-\infty $ identically. Then $\sup _ Xw_ C\to -\infty $ by corollary 10.3.6, and lemma 16.22.2 gives $w_ C\leq \phi -k$ for all large $C$. On $\{ w_ C=u_ C\} $ we then have $u_ C\leq \phi -k$, and similarly for $v_ C$, so corollary 16.13.5 gives

\[ \begin{split} m=\int _ X\langle \theta _{w_ C}^ n\rangle & \leq \mu _{u_ C}(\{ u_ C\leq \phi -k\} ) +\mu _{v_ C}(\{ v_ C\leq \phi -k\} )\\ & =2m-\mu _ u(\{ u>\phi -k\} )-\mu _ v(\{ v>\phi -k\} )<m. \end{split} \]

For the equality we used that, since $C>k$, $u_ C=u$ on the plurifine open set $\{ u>\phi -k\} $, so $\mu _{u_ C}=\mu _ u$ there, and similarly for $v$. This contradiction shows that $P_\theta (u,v)\ne -\infty $. $\square$

Proposition 16.23.2.E33C504A684F Let $\phi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $P_\theta [\phi ]=\phi $ and $P_\theta [\psi ]=\psi $, and suppose that $w=P_\theta (\phi ,\psi )\ne -\infty $. Then $P_\theta [w]=w$. Thus the rooftop envelope of two model potentials is a model potential whenever its mass is positive.

Proof. Since $w\leq \phi $, monotonicity gives $P_\theta [w]\leq P_\theta [\phi ]=\phi $, and likewise $P_\theta [w]\leq \psi $. Thus $P_\theta [w]$ is a common minorant of $\phi $ and $\psi $, and $P_\theta [w]\leq w$. Conversely, $w\leq \phi \leq 0$, so $w$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [w]$. $\square$

Proposition 16.23.3.37241FAAC9EF Let $\phi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be such that their rooftop envelope $w=P_\theta (\phi ,\psi )$ is a potential of positive mass. If $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\psi )$, then $P_\theta (u,v)$ is a potential with the same mass as $w$.

Proof. Shifting $u$ and $v$ by constants affects neither the existence of a common minorant nor the mass of $P_\theta (u,v)$. Indeed, a common minorant can be shifted down by the largest absolute change, and by proposition 16.11.4(4) the rooftop envelopes of the original and of the shifted pair differ by a bounded amount, because their obstacles do. We may therefore assume that $u\leq \phi $ and $v\leq \psi $.

We first replace $\phi $ by $u$, and then $\psi $ by $v$; both steps combine extrapolation with proposition 16.23.1. Since $w\leq \phi $ and $w\leq \psi $, the masses of $\phi $ and $\psi $ are at least that of $w$, hence positive. For $a>1$ let $u_ a=E_ a(u,\phi )$. Since $u$ and $\phi $ have the same positive mass, theorem 16.22.4 shows that $u_ a$ also has this mass and that $a^{-1}u_ a+(1-a^{-1})\phi \leq u$; moreover $u_ a\leq u\leq \phi $, because the obstacle $u+(a-1)(u-\phi )$ of $u_ a$ is at most $u$. Since $\max \{ u_ a,w\} \leq \phi $ and $\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle >0$,

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _{u_ a}^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle >\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle \geq \int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ u_ a,w\} }^ n\rangle . \]

By proposition 16.23.1, the rooftop envelope $P_\theta (u_ a,w)$ is a potential. By associativity and $u_ a\leq \phi $, it equals $P_\theta (u_ a,\psi )$. The potential

\[ a^{-1}P_\theta (u_ a,\psi )+(1-a^{-1})w \]

lies below $a^{-1}u_ a+(1-a^{-1})\phi \leq u$ and below $\psi $, so it is a common minorant of $u$ and $\psi $. Hence $z=P_\theta (u,\psi )$ is a potential, and positivity and monotonicity of mass give $\int _ X\langle \theta _ z^ n\rangle \geq (1-a^{-1})^ n\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle $. On the other hand $z\leq P_\theta (\phi ,\psi )=w$, so the mass of $z$ is at most that of $w$. Letting $a\to \infty $, we see that $z$ and $w$ have the same positive mass.

Now let $v_ a=E_ a(v,\psi )$. In the same way, $v_ a\leq v\leq \psi $, the mass of $v_ a$ is that of $\psi $, and $a^{-1}v_ a+(1-a^{-1})\psi \leq v$. Since $\max \{ v_ a,z\} \leq \psi $ and $z$ has positive mass, we have

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _{v_ a}^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _ z^ n\rangle >\int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle \geq \int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ v_ a,z\} }^ n\rangle , \]

so proposition 16.23.1 shows that $P_\theta (z,v_ a)$ is a potential, and by associativity and $v_ a\leq \psi $ it equals $P_\theta (u,v_ a)$. The potential

\[ a^{-1}P_\theta (u,v_ a)+(1-a^{-1})z \]

lies below $u$ and below $a^{-1}v_ a+(1-a^{-1})\psi \leq v$, and its mass is at least $(1-a^{-1})^ n\int _ X\langle \theta _ z^ n\rangle $. Hence $P_\theta (u,v)$ is a potential whose mass is at least $(1-a^{-1})^ n\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle $, for every $a>1$. Since $P_\theta (u,v)\leq w$, its mass is at most that of $w$, and letting $a\to \infty $ completes the proof. $\square$

The proof of the mass inequality for rooftop envelopes passes to the limit along a decreasing sequence of potentials. The following lemma shows that the mass that nonpositive potentials put on their zero sets can only increase in such a limit.

Lemma 16.23.4.F4294ED919CC Let $q_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be nonpositive potentials decreasing to a potential $q$. Then

\[ \varlimsup _{j\to \infty }\mu _{q_ j}(\{ q_ j=0\} )\leq \mu _ q(\{ q=0\} ). \]

Proof. Put $\lambda _ j=\mathbf1_{\{ q_ j=0\} }\mu _{q_ j}$. Since $q_ j\leq V_\theta \leq 0$, the set $\{ q_ j=0\} $ is contained in $\{ q_ j=V_\theta \} $, and lemma 16.13.1 gives $\lambda _ j\leq \mu _{V_\theta }$. Let $A$ be the proper analytic subset of lemma 16.13.2: potentials with minimal singularities are locally bounded on $X\setminus A$, and $\mu _{V_\theta }(A)=0$.

For $t>0$ put $b_ j=\max \{ 1+tq_ j,0\} $ and $b=\max \{ 1+tq,0\} $, with value zero at the poles. Fix a coordinate ball $D\Subset X\setminus A$ with a smooth potential $\rho $ of $\theta $ near $\overline D$ (lemma 5.4.2) and a number $L>1/t$, and put $\widetilde q_ j=\max \{ q_ j,V_\theta -L\} $ and $\widetilde q=\max \{ q,V_\theta -L\} $. On $D$ the functions $\widetilde q_ j$ are uniformly bounded and decrease to $\widetilde q$, so they converge in capacity (proposition 7.5.6). Since $V_\theta -L<-1/t$, the weight $b_ j$ vanishes on $\{ q_ j\leq V_\theta -L\} $, and on the complementary plurifine open set $\widetilde q_ j=q_ j$; hence $b_ j\mu _{q_ j}=b_ j\mu _{\widetilde q_ j}$ by plurifine locality, and likewise $b\mu _ q=b\mu _{\widetilde q}$. Moreover $b_ j=\max \{ 1+t\widetilde q_ j,0\} $ and $b=\max \{ 1+t\widetilde q,0\} $ on $D$, so the weights are bounded and quasi-continuous and converge in capacity to $b$. The weighted form of theorem 7.5.8, applied to the bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\rho +\widetilde q_ j$, therefore gives

\[ b_ j\mu _{q_ j}=b_ j\mu _{\widetilde q_ j} \longrightarrow b\mu _{\widetilde q}=b\mu _ q \quad \text{weakly on }D. \]

Let $\delta >0$. Since $\mu _{V_\theta }(A)=0$, lemma 16.12.1(1) gives a compact set $K\subset X\setminus A$ with $\mu _{V_\theta }(X\setminus K)<\delta $; choose a continuous function $0\leq \zeta \leq 1$ with compact support in $X\setminus A$ and $\zeta =1$ on $K$ (proposition 5.2.6). Since $b_ j=1$ on $\{ q_ j=0\} $ and $\lambda _ j\leq \mu _{V_\theta }$,

\[ \lambda _ j(X)\leq \int _ X\zeta b_ j\, \mu _{q_ j}+\delta . \]

Cover the support of $\zeta $ by finitely many balls $D_ l$ as above, and write $\zeta =\sum _ l\zeta _ l$ with continuous functions $\zeta _ l\geq 0$ with compact support in $D_ l$ (for instance $\zeta _ l=\zeta \psi _ l/\sum _ k\psi _ k$, where $\psi _ l\geq 0$ is continuous with compact support in $D_ l$ and $\sum _ k\psi _ k>0$ on the support of $\zeta $). The weak convergence on each $D_ l$ gives $\int _ X\zeta b_ j\, \mu _{q_ j}\to \int _ X\zeta b\, \mu _ q\leq \int _ Xb\, \mu _ q$, and letting then $\delta \downarrow 0$ gives $\varlimsup _ j\lambda _ j(X)\leq \int _ Xb\, \mu _ q$. Finally, as $t\to \infty $ the weights $b$, which depend on $t$, decrease to $\mathbf1_{\{ q=0\} }$, because $q\leq 0$; dominated convergence for the finite measure $\mu _ q$ proves the lemma. $\square$

Theorem 16.23.5.641CB44C78E9 Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w=P_\theta (u,v)\ne -\infty $. Then

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle \leq \int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ u,v\} }^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle . \]

Proof. We first reduce to model potentials, and then apply contact concentration to the rooftop envelopes of their truncations.

If $u$ has mass zero, the inequality follows from the monotonicity of mass, $\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle \leq \int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ u,v\} }^ n\rangle $, and similarly if $v$ has mass zero. Otherwise, subtracting the same constant from $u$ and $v$, which subtracts it from $\max \{ u,v\} $ and $w$ and changes no mass, we may assume that $u,v\leq 0$. Put $\phi =P_\theta [u]$ and $\psi =P_\theta [v]$; by theorem 16.22.11, these are model potentials with the same masses as $u$ and $v$, respectively, and $u\leq \phi $, $v\leq \psi $, since $u$ and $v$ are competitors for their own singularity envelopes. Put also $\gamma =\max \{ \phi ,\psi \} $ and $r=P_\theta (\phi ,\psi )$.

The four masses in the inequality do not change when $u,v$ are replaced by $\phi ,\psi $. Indeed, for every integer $C$ the potential $\max \{ P_\theta (u+C,V_\theta ),P_\theta (v+C,V_\theta )\} $ has the singularity type of $\max \{ u,v\} $, by proposition 16.11.6, and by proposition 16.21.2(2) these potentials increase almost everywhere to $\gamma $ as $C\to \infty $; so $\gamma $ has the same mass as $\max \{ u,v\} $, by corollary 13.8.9. Moreover $w$ is a common minorant of $\phi $ and $\psi $, so $r\geq w$ is a potential. If $r$ has mass zero, then so has $w$, by monotonicity. If the mass of $r$ is positive, then $w$ has the same mass as $r$ by proposition 16.23.3, applied to $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\psi )$. It therefore suffices to prove the theorem for the model potentials $\phi $ and $\psi $, whose maximum is $\gamma $ and whose rooftop envelope is $r$.

Put $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\gamma ^ n\rangle $, and for $C>0$ let

\[ \phi _ C=\max \{ \phi ,\gamma -C\} ,\quad \psi _ C=\max \{ \psi ,\gamma -C\} ,\quad r_ C=P_\theta (\phi _ C,\psi _ C). \]

These potentials have the singularity type of $\gamma $, hence mass $m$, and $r_ C$ decreases to $r$ as $C\to \infty $. By proposition 16.21.3, the measure $\mu _\phi $ is carried by $\{ \phi =0\} $, which is contained in the plurifine open set $\{ \phi >\gamma -C\} $, where $\phi _ C=\phi $. Plurifine locality therefore gives

\[ \mu _{\phi _ C}=\mu _\phi + \mathbf1_{\{ \phi \leq \gamma -C\} }\mu _{\phi _ C}, \]

and the last summand has mass $m-\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle $; the analogous decomposition holds for $\psi _ C$. By corollary 16.13.5, $\mu _{r_ C}\leq \mathbf1_{\{ r_ C=\phi _ C\} }\mu _{\phi _ C}+\mathbf1_{\{ r_ C=\psi _ C\} }\mu _{\psi _ C}$. On the set $\{ r_ C<0\} $, the summand $\mu _\phi $ of $\mu _{\phi _ C}$ contributes nothing to this bound, because at a point of $\{ \phi =0\} \cap \{ r_ C=\phi _ C\} $ we have $r_ C=\phi _ C=0$; the same holds for $\mu _\psi $. Consequently

\[ \mu _{r_ C}(\{ r_ C<0\} )\leq 2m-\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle -\int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle . \]

Since $r_ C\leq 0$ and $\int _ X\langle \theta _{r_ C}^ n\rangle =m$, it follows that

\[ \mu _{r_ C}(\{ r_ C=0\} )\geq \int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle -m. \]

Applying lemma 16.23.4 along the integers $C\to \infty $ to the potentials $r_ C$, which decrease to $r$, we find that

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _ r^ n\rangle \geq \mu _ r(\{ r=0\} ) \geq \int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _\psi ^ n\rangle -m, \]

which is the assertion for $\phi $ and $\psi $. $\square$
