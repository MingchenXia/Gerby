12.5 Relative equations under positive lower mass

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

31 Relative equations under positive lower mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, with a smooth semipositive big real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ and a model reference $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Given a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu $, we seek solutions of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $, $\lambda >0$, and of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $, $c>0$, relative to a prescribed model reference. The main difficulty is to keep the singularity condition when approximating $\mu $: a bounded difference from the reference may be lost in the limit.

We assume that the reference $\varphi $ satisfies (13.26): $\varphi $ is an envelope fixed point, $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $ in the sense of definition 1.19.4, and $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty $ for some $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$.

Throughout this chapter, unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume that $\theta $ has positive lower mass:

31.1
\begin{equation} v_-(\theta )>0. \end{equation}

Here $v_-(\theta )=\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (0)$ is the infimum of $\int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)$ over all bounded $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$; see definition 6.5.1 and the discussion following definition 13.3.1. The bounded mass property is not assumed.

Approximating $\mu $ by measures with $L^ p$ densities gives solutions of the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi $ by theorem 14.11.1, but the constants in their bounds $|u-\varphi |\leq C$ need not remain uniform, so a limit can leave this type. What survives the limit is membership in the relative envelope class of definition 13.4.1: stretching the potential away from $\varphi $ by any finite factor still leaves an envelope that is not identically $-\infty $.

Positive lower mass of $\theta $ prevents these stretched envelopes from collapsing. On disjoint contact sets, one finite measure controls the rooftop envelopes of arbitrarily many potentials. Consequently, a sequence of potentials whose measures are dominated by a fixed finite measure has, after extraction, a common lower envelope in the relative class. This envelope keeps limits in the class and controls the measures of deep sublevel sets, so convergence in capacity passes to the Monge–Ampère measures themselves. When the dominating measures are bounded Monge–Ampère measures that vary with the potentials, exponential comparison provides the common envelope instead.

For a positive exponent the solution is unique. At exponent zero the scalar is unique, but the normalized potential need not be: on projective manifolds with nonclosed Hermitian backgrounds, the same smooth measure can have several smooth normalized solutions (corollary 31.7.2).

Since bounded mass is not assumed, members of the relative class may have infinite extended mass; the envelope constructions of section 13.5 remain available for them. The solutions constructed below have finite mass because the right-hand sides of their equations are finite.

Fix $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ with $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty $, and write

\[ \mathcal E_\varphi =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ), \qquad \mathcal E_0=\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,0), \qquad \mu _ u=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \]

Here $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\psi )$ denotes the relative envelope class of a reference $\psi $ (definition 13.4.1), and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta $ is the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 6.1.2, a positive measure whose total mass may be infinite. As in definition 1.12.1, $u\preceq v$ means $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$, and $u\sim v$ means $u\preceq v\preceq u$. For $u,w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $b\geq 1$, we use the stretching envelopes $Q_ b(u,w)=P_\theta (bu-(b-1)w)$ introduced before definition 13.4.1, and the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta (u_1,\ldots ,u_ N)=P_\theta (\min _ iu_ i)$. Fix also a Hermitian form $\omega \geq \theta $ for capacity and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$ for $L^1$ convergence.

For $a\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, proposition 13.4.5 gives $P_\theta [a]=P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $. Since $a-\sup _ Xa$ is one of the competitors defining $P_\theta [a]$, it follows that

31.2
\begin{equation} a-\sup _ Xa\leq P_\theta [a]=\varphi . \end{equation}


31.1 Equations for finite measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a positive exponent, the right-hand side $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $ changes when $u$ is translated, and this fixes the additive constant. At exponent zero, translation leaves the equation unchanged; instead, a positive scalar in front of the prescribed measure is needed to match its mass.

Theorem 31.1.1. Let $\mu $ be a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$. Then:

  1. For every $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ with $\mu _ u=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $.

  2. There are $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and a unique $c>0$ with $\mu _ u=c\mu $.

The theorem is proved in section 31.6. The case of the reference zero, which has minimal singularities because $\theta \geq 0$, is treated first in theorem 31.5.1; its solutions then serve as comparison potentials for the reference $\varphi $. For general measures the theorem asserts membership in $\mathcal E_\varphi $, not a bounded difference from $\varphi $.

31.2 Domination

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The domination principle compares a potential $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ with a potential $v$ whose measure is larger on $\{ u<v\} $. We first treat the case in which $\mu _ u$ is finite. Stretching $u$ away from $\max (u,v)$ produces envelopes whose measures are bounded, by contact support, by the fixed measure $\mu _ u$; the small tails of this finite measure then exclude collapse. By theorem 13.5.3 and (31.1), the uniform lower mass bound is

\[ \mu _ a(X)\geq \kappa :=(1-\varepsilon )^ n v_-(\theta )>0 \qquad (a\in \mathcal E_\varphi ). \]

Theorem 31.2.1. Let $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ have finite $\mu _ u$, and let $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $v\preceq \varphi $. If, for some $0\leq c<1$,

\[ \mu _ u\leq c\mu _ v\quad \hbox{on }\{ u<v\} , \]

then $u\geq v$.

Proof. It suffices to prove $u\geq w$ for $w=\max (u,v)$. Upward stability (proposition 13.4.2) gives $w\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, and locality gives $\mu _ u\leq c\mu _ w$ on $\{ u<w\} $. For $b>1$ put $q_ b=Q_ b(u,w)$, which belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by lemma 13.4.3. It satisfies $q_ b\leq u$, and by contact support its measure is carried by

\[ D_ b=\{ b^{-1}q_ b+(1-b^{-1})w=u\} . \]

On $D_ b$ the potential $b^{-1}q_ b+(1-b^{-1})w$ touches $u$ from below, so positivity of mixed products and ordered contact give

\[ b^{-n}\mu _{q_ b}+(1-b^{-1})^ n\mu _ w\leq \mu _ u. \]

Choose $b$ so large that $(1-b^{-1})^ n>c$. Comparing this inequality with $\mu _ u\leq c\mu _ w$ on $D_ b\cap \{ u<w\} $ forces both $\mu _ w$ and $\mu _{q_ b}$ to vanish there. Since $\mu _ w(X)$ may be infinite, this subtraction is first carried out on common finite truncation levels of the potentials, where all measures are finite; exhausting the complements of the pole sets then gives the assertion for the extended measures.

It follows that $\mu _{q_ b}$ is carried by $D_ b\cap \{ u=w\} \subseteq \{ q_ b=u\} $. Since $q_ b\leq u$, ordered contact applied once more gives

31.3
\begin{equation} \mu _{q_ b}\leq \mathbf1_{\{ q_ b=u\} }\mu _ u, \end{equation}

with coefficient one, independent of $b$.

As $b\to \infty $ through the integers, the potentials $q_ b$ decrease, because the obstacles $bu-(b-1)w=u-(b-1)(w-u)$ do. Suppose that they decreased to $-\infty $ identically. Then theorem 1.4.2 would give $M_ b:=\sup _ Xq_ b\to -\infty $, and (31.3) would imply

\[ \kappa \leq \int _ X\mu _{q_ b} \leq \mu _ u(\{ u\leq M_ b\} )\longrightarrow 0, \]

because $\mu _ u$ is finite and does not charge the pole set of $u$. This is impossible, so the decreasing limit $q$ is not identically $-\infty $. Now fix $a>0$. On $\{ u<w-a\} $, the defining inequality of $q_ b$ gives $q_ b\leq u-(b-1)a$, so $q=-\infty $ at every point of this set where $u$ is finite. Since neither $q$ nor $u$ is identically $-\infty $, their pole sets have zero smooth volume, and hence so does $\{ u<w-a\} $. Thus $u\geq w$ almost everywhere, and then everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. In particular $u\geq v$. $\square$

31.2.1 Domination for extended measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

We now remove the finiteness assumption on $\mu _ u$. The key case is an ordered pair $u\leq v$ in $\mathcal E_\varphi $ such that $\mu _ u$ puts no mass on $\{ u<v\} $. A difference envelope of $u$ and a smaller-background envelope of $v$ then has no mass where it is negative. Its measure is therefore finite, and theorem 31.2.1, applied in a smaller background, proves that $u=v$. The general domination principle follows by the stretching argument used above.

Lemma 31.2.2. Let $u,v\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ satisfy $u\leq v$. If

\[ \mu _ u(\{ u<v\} )=0, \]

then $u=v$.

Proof. Translate both potentials by a common constant so that $u\leq v\leq \varphi \leq 0$. Fix $\delta $ with $\varepsilon <\delta <1$, put $\beta =1-\delta $, and set

\[ q=P_{\delta \theta }(v). \]

This envelope is not identically $-\infty $ by theorem 13.5.3, applied to $v$. By lemma 13.5.1,

\[ w=P_\theta (u-\varphi )\in \mathcal E_0, \qquad w+\varphi \leq u,\qquad w\leq 0. \]

The stretching lemma lemma 13.4.3, applied with reference zero, shows that $z=P_\theta (w/\beta )$ belongs to $\mathcal E_0$. Scaling the background and each defining competitor gives

\[ \beta z\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\beta \theta ,0). \]

Indeed, if $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ lies below $t z$, then $\beta a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta \theta )$ lies below $t\beta z$, for every $t\geq 1$.

Since $q\leq v\leq \varphi $, we have

\[ \beta z\leq w\leq u-\varphi \leq u-q \]

off the pole sets. Thus the envelope

\[ k=P_{\beta \theta }(u-q) \]

is not identically $-\infty $. Here the difference obstacle is taken off the pluripolar pole sets; it is quasi-continuous and finite almost everywhere. The general envelope theorem gives a finite upper bound for $k$ and the inequality $k+q\leq u$. Since $\beta z\leq k$, upward stability gives $k\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\beta \theta ,0)$.

Write $\nu _ k=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\beta \theta }(k)$. By contact support, $\nu _ k$ is carried by

\[ D=\{ k+q=u\} \]

outside the pole sets. Positivity of mixed products and ordered contact give

\[ \nu _ k\leq \mathbf1_ D\mu _ u. \]

These inequalities are first proved on common finite truncation levels and then pass to the limit by exhaustion. On $D\cap \{ k<0\} $ we have $u=k+q<q\leq v$, so the hypothesis gives $\nu _ k(\{ k<0\} )=0$.

This vanishing implies that $\nu _ k$ is finite. Indeed, $\widehat k=\max (k,-1)$ is a bounded $\beta \theta $-plurisubharmonic function, and locality gives

\[ \nu _ k(X)=\nu _ k(\{ k\geq 0\} ) =\int _{\{ k\geq 0\} }\mathrm{MA}_{\beta \theta }(\widehat k) \leq \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_{\beta \theta }(\widehat k)<\infty . \]

We now apply theorem 31.2.1 in the background $\beta \theta $, with reference zero, to the potentials $k$ and $0$ and the coefficient zero. Its hypotheses hold: $\beta \theta $ is semipositive and big, the reference zero satisfies the model condition, $\nu _ k$ is finite and vanishes on $\{ k<0\} $, and

\[ v_-(\beta \theta )=\beta ^ n v_-(\theta )>0. \]

The scaling identity follows from the bijection $a\mapsto \beta a$ between bounded potentials in the two backgrounds. The theorem yields $k\geq 0$, and hence $u\geq q$.

It remains to let $\delta \uparrow 1$. Fix $\delta _0\in (\varepsilon ,1)$ and put $r=P_{\delta _0\theta }(v)$. For $\delta _0<\delta <1$, set $t=(\delta -\delta _0)/(1-\delta _0)$. The potential $t v+(1-t)r$ is $\delta \theta $-plurisubharmonic and lies below $v$. Therefore

\[ u\geq P_{\delta \theta }(v)\geq t v+(1-t)r. \]

Letting $t\uparrow 1$ at points where $v$ and $r$ are finite gives $u\geq v$ almost everywhere, and then everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Together with $u\leq v$, this proves equality. $\square$

Theorem 31.2.3. Let $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $v\preceq \varphi $. If $0\leq c<1$ and

\[ \mu _ u\leq c\mu _ v\quad \hbox{on }\{ u<v\} , \]

then $u\geq v$.

Proof. Put $w=\max (u,v)\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and choose $b>1$ with $(1-b^{-1})^ n>c$. The stretching lemma gives $p=Q_ b(u,w)\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $p\leq u$. By contact support, its measure is carried by

\[ D=\{ b^{-1}p+(1-b^{-1})w=u\} . \]

On $D$, positivity and ordered contact imply

\[ b^{-n}\mu _ p+(1-b^{-1})^ n\mu _ w\leq \mu _ u. \]

On $\{ u<w\} $, locality gives $\mu _ u\leq c\mu _ w$. We restrict both inequalities to $D\cap \{ u<w\} $ and to common finite truncation levels, where all measures are finite and may be subtracted. Since $(1-b^{-1})^ n>c$, it follows that $\mu _ p$ vanishes there. Exhausting the finite levels, we conclude that $\mu _ p$ is carried by $D\cap \{ u=w\} \subseteq \{ p=u\} $; that is, $\mu _ p(\{ p<u\} )=0$.

Now lemma 31.2.2, applied to $p\leq u$, both in $\mathcal E_\varphi $, gives $p=u$. Substituting this into the defining inequality $b^{-1}p+(1-b^{-1})w\leq u$ yields $w\leq u$ off the poles, hence everywhere by lemma 1.3.3. Thus $v\leq w\leq u$. $\square$

Corollary 31.2.4. Let $u,v$ be as in theorem 31.2.3. Then, for every $\lambda >0$,

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\mu _ u\leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda v}\mu _ v \quad \Longrightarrow \quad u\geq v. \]

Moreover, if $\mu _ u\leq c\mu _ v$ on $X$ for a finite constant $c\geq 0$, then $c\geq 1$.

Proof. Fix $a>0$. On $\{ u<v-a\} $ the exponential inequality gives $\mu _ u\leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a}\mu _ v$; here all weights are evaluated off the pluripolar pole sets. The theorem, applied to $v-a$, gives $u\geq v-a$, and we let $a\downarrow 0$. For the second assertion, suppose $c<1$. Then the theorem applies to $v+A$ for every real $A$, since $\mu _{v+A}=\mu _ v$, and gives $u\geq v+A$. This is impossible at a point where $u$ and $v$ are both finite. $\square$

In particular, applying the exponential implication in both directions shows that a solution of $\mu _ u=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\nu $ in $\mathcal E_\varphi $, for a fixed $\lambda >0$, is unique. If $\mu _ u=c_1\nu $ and $\mu _ v=c_2\nu $ with $c_1,c_2>0$ and $\nu \ne 0$, the second assertion, applied in both directions, gives $c_1=c_2$. The last section of the chapter shows that this uniqueness of the scalar does not extend to the normalized potentials.

31.3 Common envelopes and dominated continuity

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $u_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ converge in $L^1$, with measures dominated by one finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu $. To keep the limit in $\mathcal E_\varphi $, we construct, after passing to a subsequence, a common lower envelope in this class. Partitioning the contact set of a finite rooftop according to the obstacle it touches shows that the rooftop measure is again dominated by $\nu $, with no factor depending on the number of obstacles. A summable integration error, combined with the lower mass bound $\kappa $, then prevents the stretched rooftops from collapsing, uniformly in the number of obstacles.

Lemma 31.3.1. Suppose $u_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $u_ j\to u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu $ for a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu $. There is a subsequence, denoted again by $u_ j$, such that

\[ h=P_\theta \left(\inf _ j u_ j\right)\in \mathcal E_\varphi . \]

In particular $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

Proof. We first bound stretched rooftops of the tail of the sequence from below and then add the finitely many remaining terms.

The suprema of an $L^1$-convergent sequence are bounded, so (31.2) gives a constant $C$ with $u_ j\leq \varphi +C$ for all $j$. After a common translation we may assume $u_ j\leq \varphi \leq 0$, and then $u\leq \varphi $. Since $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu $, lemma 1.23.1 gives

\[ I_ j=\int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{u_ j}\longrightarrow 0. \]

We extract, once and for all, a subsequence with $I_ j\leq 2^{-j}$ along which $u_ j\to u$ almost everywhere for smooth volume.

For $N\geq j$ put $p_{j,N}=P_\theta (u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N)$. These rooftops belong to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by proposition 13.4.4. Partition their contact sets according to the first index at which the minimum is attained. Ordered contact on these disjoint pieces gives both

31.4
\begin{equation} \mu _{p_{j,N}}\leq \nu , \qquad \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{p_{j,N}} \leq \sum _{\ell =j}^ N I_\ell \leq 2^{1-j}. \end{equation}

The first bound has no factor $N$, because each piece is controlled by the restriction of the same measure $\nu $ to that piece.

Fix a real number $B\geq 1$ and set $r_{j,N}=Q_ B(p_{j,N},\varphi )$. It belongs to $\mathcal E_\varphi $ by the stretching lemma, so $\mu _{r_{j,N}}(X)\geq \kappa $. With $D_{j,N}=\{ B^{-1}r_{j,N}+(1-B^{-1})\varphi =p_{j,N}\} $, contact support and positivity give

\[ \mu _{r_{j,N}}\leq B^ n\mathbf1_{D_{j,N}}\mu _{p_{j,N}} \leq B^ n\nu . \]

On this contact set, $\varphi \leq 0$ gives $\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}\leq \mathrm{e}^{r_{j,N}/B}$. Consequently

31.5
\begin{equation} \int _ X\mathrm{e}^ u\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{r_{j,N}} \leq B^ n2^{1-j} +B^ n\nu (X)\exp \! \left(\frac{\sup _ X r_{j,N}}B\right). \end{equation}

On the other hand, choose $K>0$ with $B^ n\nu (\{ u\leq -K\} )<\kappa /2$; this is possible because $u\not\equiv -\infty $ and $\nu $ does not charge its pole set. The measure domination then gives $\mu _{r_{j,N}}(\{ u\leq -K\} )<\kappa /2$, and the lower mass bound yields

\[ \int _ X\mathrm{e}^ u\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{r_{j,N}}\geq \mathrm{e}^{-K}\kappa /2. \]

Choose $j$ so large that $B^ n2^{1-j}<\mathrm{e}^{-K}\kappa /4$. Then (31.5) bounds $\sup _ Xr_{j,N}$ from below, independently of $N$. Since the sequence decreases in $N$ and is bounded above by $\varphi $, its limit $R$ is not identically $-\infty $. It satisfies, for every $\ell \geq j$,

31.6
\begin{equation} B^{-1}R+(1-B^{-1})\varphi \leq u_\ell , \qquad R\leq \varphi . \end{equation}

The second inequality follows already from $r_{j,N}\leq p_{j,N}\leq \varphi $.

It remains to include the finitely many earlier terms and to verify every defining condition of the relative class. Fix $b\geq 1$, apply the preceding construction with $B=2b$, and let $j$ and $R$ be as above. If $j>1$, let $p_0=P_\theta (u_1,\ldots ,u_{j-1})$ and $S=Q_{2b}(p_0,\varphi )$. By the finite-rooftop and stretching lemmas, neither is identically $-\infty $, and $S\leq \varphi $. If $j=1$, set $S=\varphi $. The potential $H=(R+S)/2$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. For a tail index $\ell \geq j$ we use (31.6) and $S\leq \varphi $; for an earlier index we use the analogous inequality for $S$ and $R\leq \varphi $. In either case,

\[ b^{-1}H+(1-b^{-1})\varphi \leq u_\ell \quad \hbox{for every }\ell \geq 1. \]

For $b=1$ this shows that $h=P_\theta (\inf _\ell u_\ell )$ is not identically $-\infty $. For general $b$ the left side is a competitor for $h$, so the same inequality holds with $h$ in place of every $u_\ell $; this is the defining condition of the relative class for $b$. Also $h\leq \varphi $. Thus $h\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

Finally $h\leq u_ j$ for all $j$, so the almost-everywhere convergence and lemma 1.3.3 give $h\leq u\leq \varphi $. Upward stability proves $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Undoing the common translation proves the lemma as stated. $\square$

Once a common lower envelope is available, convergence in capacity follows from a single integral condition. The proof uses the zero-domination lemma, so infinite masses are allowed.

Proposition 31.3.2. Suppose $u_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and, for a fixed $h\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and a constant $C$,

\[ h\leq u_ j\leq \varphi +C,\qquad u_ j\longrightarrow u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\quad \hbox{in }L^1(X). \]

If

\[ I_ j:=\int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _{u_ j}\longrightarrow 0, \]

then $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ and $u_ j\to u$ in capacity.

Proof. The bounds pass to $u$ almost everywhere along a subsequence, and then everywhere by lemma 1.3.3; upward stability gives $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. After translating the entire family and its bounds by a common constant, we may assume that all potentials are nonpositive.

It suffices to prove convergence in capacity along a further subsequence of every subsequence. Extract so that $I_ j\leq 2^{-j}$ and $u_ j\to u$ almost everywhere for smooth volume, and form

\[ p_{j,N}=P_\theta (u_ j,\ldots ,u_ N),\qquad p_ j=P_\theta \left(\inf _{\ell \geq j}u_\ell \right). \]

All these envelopes lie above $h$ and below $\varphi +C$; hence none of them is identically $-\infty $, and all belong to $\mathcal E_\varphi $. Partition the contact set of $p_{j,N}$ according to the first index $\ell $ with $p_{j,N}=u_\ell $. Ordered contact on each part gives

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{p_{j,N}}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _{p_{j,N}} \leq \sum _{\ell =j}^ N I_\ell \leq 2^{1-j}. \]

Only integrals of nonnegative functions occur here, so the estimate holds even if a rooftop has infinite mass.

As $N\to \infty $, $p_{j,N}\downarrow p_ j$; as $j\to \infty $, the $p_ j$ increase, and their regularized limit $p$ is not identically $-\infty $. Both monotone limits hold in capacity by lemma 1.8.12. Moreover $h\leq p\leq u\leq \varphi +C$, where $p\leq u$ follows from the almost-everywhere convergence of the extracted $u_ j$. Thus $p\in \mathcal E_\varphi $.

Apply lemma 6.3.1, first as $N\to \infty $ and then as $j\to \infty $, with the bounded quasi-continuous weights in the preceding estimate. These weights converge in capacity because the exponential is Lipschitz on a common upper half-line. We obtain

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^ p-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mu _ p=0, \qquad \mu _ p(\{ p<u\} )=0. \]

The second equality follows off the poles, which the extended measures ignore. The zero-domination lemma lemma 31.2.2 now gives $p=u$; no finiteness of $\mu _ p$ is needed.

Finally, the upper envelopes $U_ j=(\sup _{\ell \geq j}u_\ell )^*$ decrease to $u$, by theorem 1.4.2 and the almost-everywhere convergence. Applying lemma 1.8.12 to $p_ j\uparrow u$ and $U_ j\downarrow u$, the inequalities $p_ j\leq u_ j\leq U_ j$ give convergence in capacity along this subsequence, and hence for the original sequence. $\square$

31.3.1 Convergence under a fixed measure

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The extraction lemma provides the lower envelope required by the integral criterion. The same fixed measure then controls the measures of deep sublevel sets, so convergence in capacity passes to the Monge–Ampère measures themselves.

Theorem 31.3.3. Suppose $u_ j\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $u_ j\to u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1(X)$, and $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu $ for a fixed finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu $. Then

\[ u\in \mathcal E_\varphi , \qquad u_ j\longrightarrow u\text{ in capacity}, \qquad \mu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mu _ u\leq \nu . \]

Proof. Start with an arbitrary subsequence. By lemma 31.3.1, a further subsequence has a common lower envelope $h\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, and $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $. Inequality (31.2) and Hartogs' lemma give a common upper bound $u_ j\leq \varphi +C$.

After a common translation, assume $u_ j,u\leq 0$. Since $\mu _{u_ j}\leq \nu $, lemma 1.23.1 gives

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{d}\mu _{u_ j} \leq \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{u_ j}-\mathrm{e}^ u|\, \mathrm{d}\nu \longrightarrow 0. \]

Thus proposition 31.3.2 gives convergence in capacity along this subsequence. For the measures themselves, the common lower bound gives

\[ \mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j\leq -T\} ) \leq \nu (\{ h\leq -T\} )\longrightarrow 0 \quad \hbox{uniformly in }j, \]

since $\nu $ is finite and does not charge the pole set of $h\not\equiv -\infty $. \[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ h(v_\lambda ) =c_\lambda \mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }f\, \mathrm{d}V \longrightarrow b_ h(f)f\, \mathrm{d}V \quad \text{in total variation}. \]

Since $v_\lambda \leq 0$ and $c_\lambda $ stays bounded for small $\lambda $, these measures are dominated by one finite non-pluripolar measure $C f\, \mathrm{d}V$. Applying theorem 31.3.3 to the given $L^1$-convergent sequence $v_{\lambda _ j}$, we obtain class membership, convergence in capacity and the equation for its limit. $\square$

By theorem 1.4.2, every sequence $\lambda _ j\downarrow 0$ has a subsequence along which $v_{\lambda _ j}$ converges in $L^1$. The corollary identifies the equation of every such limit; the examples below show that this equation alone need not determine the normalized potential.

31.7.1 Nonuniqueness in the minimal envelope class

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In this subsection the manifold and the background are no longer fixed. The examples of 11.9.1 are smooth solutions on projective manifolds with nonclosed Hermitian backgrounds. Comparison with a Kähler form shows that these backgrounds have both positive lower mass and bounded mass, and smooth potentials belong to the minimal envelope class. Thus even both mass bounds together allow several normalized solutions with the same scalar.

Corollary 31.7.2. In every complex dimension $n\geq 3$, there are a projective manifold $X$ of dimension $n$, a smooth nonclosed Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$, and a nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\nu $ on $X$ such that

\[ 0<v_-(\omega )\leq v_+(\omega )<+\infty , \]

but the equation

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)=c\nu , \qquad u\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega ,0), \qquad \sup _ Xu=0, \]

has at least two smooth solutions. The only positive constant for which it has a solution is $c=1$. Relative to a Kähler volume form on $X$, the measure $\nu $ has a real analytic density, positive off two connected smooth real hypersurfaces and vanishing quadratically along them.

Proof. Use the form and the two potentials of 11.9.1. Since the manifold is projective, lemma 21.4.1 gives bounded mass. Fix a Kähler form $\Omega $ on $X$ and choose $\varepsilon >0$ with $\varepsilon \Omega \leq \omega $. Lower-volume monotonicity, theorem 30.1.1, applied to the zero potential in both backgrounds, gives

\[ v_-(\omega )\geq v_-(\varepsilon \Omega ) =\varepsilon ^ n\int _ X\Omega ^ n>0. \]

Here the potentials of the same ordinary type as zero are exactly the bounded ones, so these lower volumes are the infima defining $v_-$.

The two smooth potentials belong to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega ,0)$ by proposition 13.4.2, and their extended measures are the ordinary top powers. Their common measure $\nu $ is finite and does not charge pluripolar sets. The construction gives $c=1$, and corollary 31.2.4 shows that no other positive scalar is possible. The density and its zero set were computed in 11.9.1. $\square$
