13.1 The envelope operators

3 The envelope operators

This chapter develops the envelope formalism that will be used throughout the rest of the book. There are two parallel constructions. The first is the $P$-envelope, which records the singularity type relevant to non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère mass. The second is the $\mathcal I$-envelope, which records the singularity type detected by multiplier ideal sheaves. The distinction between these two envelopes is one of the main organizing principles of the book.

The starting point is the rooftop operator. Given two singular potentials, the rooftop construction produces the largest potential lying below both, and it provides a flexible way to compare singularities. From this we define the $P$-envelope $P_\theta [\varphi ]$. Its fixed points are the model potentials. Model potentials are the natural representatives of $P$-singularity types: they preserve non-pluripolar mass and satisfy strong contact-set properties. The first part of the chapter establishes the basic algebra of these envelopes, their behavior under monotone limits, and the domination and contact principles needed later.

We then introduce the Monge–Ampère energy relative to a model potential. This leads to the finite-energy classes $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and to stability results for rooftop envelopes. These results will be used in the metric theory of singularities and in the study of geodesic rays.

The second part of the chapter develops the $\mathcal I$-envelope $P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$. This envelope is defined by imposing multiplier ideal conditions rather than ordinary singularity-type conditions. It is therefore sensitive to valuative data in a way that the ordinary $P$-envelope is not. We prove its basic functorial properties, its independence of the chosen representative of the cohomology class, its bimeromorphic behavior, and its compatibility with analytic singularities.

The comparison between $P_\theta [\varphi ]$ and $P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$ is delicate. They agree in important cases, for instance for analytic singularities, but they differ in general. Later chapters use this comparison to define $\mathcal I$-good singularities, trace operators, partial Okounkov bodies and non-Archimedean potentials. Thus the technical results of this chapter should be regarded as the basic calculus of singularity types used in the sequel.

Several results about $P$-envelopes are developed by Darvas–Di Nezza–Lu in various papers and summarized in their survey [DDNLsurv]. The reader is invited to read their survey for more details and further results.

Lemma 3.1.3. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\varphi \land \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

3.2
\begin{equation} \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\varphi \} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\psi \} } \, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. We first observe that as a consequence of 2.4.10, we have

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\varphi \} } \, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\varphi \} } \, \theta _{\varphi }^ n,\quad \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\psi \} } \, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\psi \} } \, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

Applying theorem 2.4.14 to $\min \{ \varphi ,\psi \} $, we conclude that

\[ \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\varphi \} } \, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\psi \} }\, \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\varphi \} } \, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \land \psi =\psi \} } \, \theta _{\psi }^ n, \]

We recall that the relations $\preceq $ and $\sim $ are introduced in definition 1.5.2.

Definition 3.1.4. Given $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we define its $P$-envelope as follows:

3.3
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\left\{ \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \psi \leq 0,\psi \preceq \varphi \right\} . \end{equation}
Note that $\varphi -\sup _ X \varphi $ is always a candidate for the supremum. By proposition 1.2.2, we have $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\leq 0$. Moreover, the definition can be equivalently described as
3.4
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=\operatorname *{sup}_{C\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}{}\! {}^{*}(\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta }. \end{equation}

Recall that $V_{\theta }$ is introduced in (2.20). For any $C\in \mathbb {R}$, we have $(\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and

\[ (\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta }\sim \varphi . \]

In other words, in (3.3), we may replace the condition $\psi \preceq \varphi $ by $\psi \sim \varphi $. Also note that as a consequence,

3.5
\begin{equation} \varphi \preceq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]. \end{equation}

The idea lying behind the definition of $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ is that we choose the least singular element out of all potentials with the same singularity type as $\varphi $. As we shall see in example 3.1.14 below, $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ does not necessarily have the same singularity type as $\varphi $. This forces us to define a rougher equivalence relation in definition 6.1.1.

The envelope depends on the choice of $\theta $, but the dependence is easy to understand:

Proposition 3.1.5. Let $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ for some $g\in C^{\infty }(X)$. Then for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we have $\varphi '\coloneqq \varphi -g\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$ and

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\sim P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']. \]

Proof. By symmetry, it suffices to show that

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\preceq P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']. \]

We may assume that $g\geq 0$ (after replacing $g$ by $g+C$ with $C\geq -\inf _ X g$, which leaves $\theta '$ unchanged and shifts $\varphi '$ by an additive constant). Then for any $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\psi \preceq \varphi $ and $\psi \leq 0$, we set $\psi '\coloneqq \psi -g\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$. Then $\psi '\preceq \varphi '$ and $\psi '\leq 0$, so $\psi '\leq P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']$. Since $\psi $ is arbitrary, it follows that

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]-\sup _ X g\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]-g\leq P_{\theta '}[\varphi '], \]

which gives $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\preceq P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']$. $\square$

The $P$-envelope preserves the non-pluripolar masses:

Proposition 3.1.6. Suppose that $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ are smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$. Let $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ for each $i=1,\ldots ,n$. Then

3.6
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{1,P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,P_{\theta _ n}[\varphi _ n]}=\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}. \end{equation}
This proposition together with theorem 2.4.8 will be combined to a common generalization in proposition 6.1.16 after introducing the $P$-preorder.

Proof. For each $C\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$ and each $i=1,\ldots ,n$, we have

\[ (\varphi _ i+C)\land V_{\theta _ i}\sim \varphi _ i. \]

It follows from the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{1,(\varphi _1+C)\land V_{\theta _1}}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,(\varphi _ n+C)\land V_{\theta _ n}}=\int _ X \theta _{1,\varphi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{n,\varphi _ n}. \]

So (3.6) follows from (3.4) and corollary 2.4.9. $\square$

Conversely, proposition 3.1.6 characterizes the $P$-envelope, as we will see in a moment in theorem 3.1.9. We need some preparation for the proof. We first show that the $P$-envelope can be regarded as a concentration of the non-pluripolar mass:

Theorem 3.1.7. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

3.7
\begin{equation} \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=0\} }\, \theta ^ n. \end{equation}
In fact, equality holds as we will see in proposition 3.1.21 below.

Proof. Thanks to 3.1.3, for each $C>0$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \theta _{(\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta }}^ n\leq & \mathds {1}_{\{ (\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta }=\varphi +C\} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ (\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta }=V_{\theta }\} }\, \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n\\ \leq & \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi +C\leq V_{\theta }\} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=V_{\theta }\} } \, \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n. \end{aligned} \]

We wish to let $C\to \infty $. The dominated convergence theorem and the semicontinuity theorem corollary 2.4.9 give, as $C\to \infty $,

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi +C\leq V_{\theta }\} } \theta _{\varphi }^ n\rightharpoonup 0,\quad \theta _{(\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta }}^ n\rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n. \]

So we conclude that

\[ \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=V_{\theta }\} } \, \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n. \]

Taking corollary 2.4.25 into consideration, we conclude (3.7). $\square$

Using essentially the same proof, we arrive at the following conclusion:

Corollary 3.1.8. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let

\[ \eta \coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{C>0}{}\! {}^{*} (\varphi +C)\land \psi .\footnotemark \]
Note. Using the terminology to be introduced in definition 3.1.19, we can also write $\eta =P_{\theta }[\varphi ](\psi )$.
Assume that $\eta \not\equiv -\infty $ (for example when $\varphi \preceq \psi $). Then
\[ \theta _{\eta }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \eta =\psi \} }\, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

Proof. Thanks to 3.1.3, for each $C>0$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \theta _{(\varphi +C)\land \psi }^ n\leq & \mathds {1}_{\{ (\varphi +C)\land \psi =\varphi +C\} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ (\varphi +C)\land \psi =\psi \} }\, \theta _{\psi }^ n\\ \leq & \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi +C\leq \psi \} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \eta =\psi \} } \, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \end{aligned} \]

We wish to let $C\to \infty $. The dominated convergence theorem and the semicontinuity theorem corollary 2.4.9 give, as $C\to \infty $,

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi +C\leq \psi \} } \theta _{\varphi }^ n\rightharpoonup 0,\quad \theta _{(\varphi +C)\land \psi }^ n\rightharpoonup \theta _{\eta }^ n. \]

So we conclude that

\[ \theta _{\eta }^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \eta =\psi \} } \, \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

3.12
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=\sup \left\{ \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \psi \leq 0,\varphi \preceq \psi , \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n\right\} . \end{equation}
In particular, in this case,
Note. Pang–Sun–Wang–Zhou [PSWZ26] recently proved that, (3.13) continues to hold even when $\varphi $ has vanishing mass.
3.13
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }\Bigl [P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\Bigr ]=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]. \end{equation}
Note that in (3.12) and (3.3), the auxiliary function $\psi $ lies on different sides of $\varphi $.

Proof. Let $\psi $ be a candidate of the right-hand side of (3.12). It follows from theorem 3.1.7 that

\[ \int _{\{ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]<\psi \} } \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n\leq \int _{\{ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]<\psi \} \cap \{ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=0\} } \theta ^ n=0. \]

On the other hand, we have

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\preceq P_{\theta }[\psi ],\quad \psi \preceq P_{\theta }[\psi ] \]

and all these potentials have the same mass due to proposition 3.1.6. So the domination principle theorem 2.4.17 is applicable and gives

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\geq \psi . \]

Hence we get the $\geq $ direction in (3.12).

On the other hand, $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ itself is a candidate of the right-hand side of (3.12), as a consequence of proposition 3.1.6. Therefore, (3.12) follows.

As for (3.13), the $\geq $ direction is trivial. For the other direction, let $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a potential satisfying

\[ \psi \leq 0,\quad P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\preceq \psi ,\quad \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

Then it follows from proposition 3.1.6 that

\[ \psi \leq 0,\quad \varphi \preceq \psi ,\quad \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

In view of (3.12), we conclude the $\leq $ direction of (3.13). $\square$

Definition 3.1.10. If $\varphi =P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ and $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$, we say $\varphi $ is a model potential.

Recall that the notion of model potentials depends heavily on the choice of $\theta $. When there is a risk of confusion, we also say $\varphi $ is a model potential in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Remark 3.1.11. definition 3.1.10 is different from the common definition in the literature: We impose the extra condition $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$. The author believes that this is the only case where this notion is natural. We sometimes emphasize this point by saying $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ is a model potential.

There are plenty of model potentials:

Corollary 3.1.12. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ is a model potential in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Moreover,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

Proof. This follows immediately from theorem 3.1.9 and proposition 3.1.6. $\square$

As we have already seen in the proof of 2.4.15, we have the following interesting property:

Proposition 3.1.13. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Consider a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Assume that $\varphi \preceq \phi $. Then

\[ \sup _{\{ \phi \neq -\infty \} } (\varphi -\phi )=\sup _ X \varphi . \]
In particular, for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $\varphi \preceq \phi $ and $\varphi \leq 0$ implies $\varphi \leq \phi $.

Proof. Observe that $\phi \leq 0$, so on the set $\{ \phi \neq -\infty \} $, we have

\[ \varphi -\phi \geq \varphi . \]

So the $\geq $ direction follows from corollary 1.3.18. Conversely, by assumption, we can find a constant $C>0$ so that

\[ \varphi -\sup _ X \varphi \leq \min \{ \phi +C,0 \} . \]

It follows that $\varphi -\sup _ X \varphi \leq P_{\theta }[\phi ]=\phi $. Therefore, the reverse inequality follows. $\square$

Example 3.1.14. We continue our favorite example example 1.8.5. Let $X=\mathbb {P}^1$ and $\omega $ be the Fubini–Study metric. We define $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ as follows: For $z\in \mathbb {C}$, we let

\[ \varphi (z)= \begin{cases} -\log \left(|z|^2+1\right)+\Bigl (-\log \left(-\log |z|^2\right)\Bigr ) \lor \Bigl (2+\log |z|^2\Bigr ),& \textup{if }|z|<1/\sqrt{2},\\ 2+\log \frac{|z|^2}{|z|^2+1},& \textup{otherwise}, \end{cases} \]

while $\varphi (\infty )=2$. Note that $\varphi $ is $\omega $-psh on $X=\mathbb {P}^1$ by the gluing lemma 1.2.12. The singularity of $\varphi $ only occurs at $z=0$, close to which, $\varphi $ is approximately $-\log \left(-\log |z|^2\right)$. This type of singularity is therefore called the log-log type singularity.

We claim that

3.16
\begin{equation} P_{\omega }[\varphi ]=0. \end{equation}

In particular, we find that $\varphi $ and $P_{\omega }[\varphi ]$ have different singularity types.

By theorem 3.1.9, in order to verify (3.16), it suffices to verify that

3.17
\begin{equation} \int _ X \omega _{\varphi }^1=1. \end{equation}

Here $\omega _{\varphi }^1$ is taken in the non-pluripolar sense. Since $\{ 0,\infty \} \subseteq \mathbb {P}^1$ is pluripolar, this reduces to showing that

\[ \int _{\mathbb {C}^*} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\frac{1}{4\pi }\int _{\mathbb {C}^*} (\Delta \psi )\, \mathrm{d}\mu =1, \]

where $\psi (z)=\varphi (z)+\log (|z|^2+1)$ and $\mu $ is the standard Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb {C}$.

Note that the Laplacian vanishes outside $\overline{B(0,0.7)}$ since $\psi (z)=2+\log |z|^2$ there, which is harmonic. Therefore,

\[ \int _{\mathbb {C}^*} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\frac{1}{4\pi }\int _{|z|<1/\sqrt{2}} (\Delta \psi )(z)\, \mathrm{d}\mu . \]

It is an elementary exercise to see that the right-hand side is exactly equal to $1$. If you are familiar with toric geometry, this is more or less trivial since

\[ \nabla _ r \Bigl ( \left(-\log (-r)\right)\lor (2+r) \Bigr )\bigl ((-\infty ,-\log 2)\bigr )= (0,1]. \]

Otherwise, just try to evaluate the integral using Green's identities. Therefore, (3.17) is proved and our assertion (3.16) follows.

Next we give a criterion on when the rooftop operator is not identically $-\infty $.

Proposition 3.1.15. Assume that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and

3.18
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n>\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n. \end{equation}

Then $\varphi \land \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Thanks to the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8, we may also replace $\varphi \lor \psi $ on the right-hand side of (3.18) by any $\gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\varphi \lor \psi \preceq \gamma $.

Proof. Without loss of generality, we may assume that $\varphi ,\psi \leq 0$. For simplicity, we write

\[ \eta =P_{\theta }[\varphi \lor \psi ]. \]

Note that due to our assumption (3.18), $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, and hence so is $\varphi \lor \psi $ thanks to the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. By corollary 3.1.12, $\eta $ is a model potential, and $\int _ X\theta _\eta ^ n=\int _ X\theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n>0$.

Take $C>0$ large enough, so that

3.19
\begin{equation} \int _{\{ \varphi >\eta -C\} } \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\int _{\{ \psi >\eta -C\} } \theta _{\psi }^ n>\int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n. \end{equation}

This is possible thanks to proposition 2.2.2 4. Fix $C'>C$. Write

\[ \gamma _{C'}\coloneqq \Bigl (\varphi \lor (\eta -C')\Bigr )\land \Bigl (\psi \lor (\eta -C')\Bigr ). \]

Then observe that $\gamma _{C'}\sim \eta $, and

\[ \inf _{C'>C}\gamma _{C'}=\varphi \land \psi . \]

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\varphi \land \psi \equiv -\infty $. Then

\[ \adjustlimits \lim _{C'\to \infty } \sup _ X\gamma _{C'}=-\infty . \]

Thanks to proposition 3.1.13, for each $C'>C$,

\[ \sup _{X} \gamma _{C'}=\sup _{\{ \eta \neq -\infty \} } (\gamma _{C'}-\eta ), \]

since $\eta $ is a model potential. It follows that

3.20
\begin{equation} \adjustlimits \lim _{C'\to \infty } \sup _{\{ \eta \neq -\infty \} } (\gamma _{C'}-\eta )=-\infty . \end{equation}

In particular, we can take $C'$ large enough so that

\[ \gamma _{C'}\leq \eta -C. \]

For such $C'$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ X \theta _{\gamma _{C'}}^ n=& \int _{\{ \gamma _{C'}\leq \eta -C\} } \theta _{\gamma _{C'}}^ n \\ \leq & \int _{\left\{ \varphi \lor (\eta -C')\leq \eta -C\right\} } \theta _{\varphi \lor (\eta -C')}^ n +\int _{\left\{ \psi \lor (\eta -C')\leq \eta -C \right\} } \theta _{\psi \lor (\eta -C')}^ n\\ =& 2\int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n-\int _{\{ \varphi >\eta -C\} }\theta _{\varphi }^ n-\int _{\{ \psi >\eta -C\} }\theta _{\psi }^ n\\ <& \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where the second line follows from 3.1.3, the fourth line follows from (3.19). This contradicts the fact that $\gamma _{C'}\sim \eta $ in view of theorem 2.4.8. $\square$

Proposition 3.1.16. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that

3.25
\begin{equation} \varphi =P_{\theta }[\varphi ],\quad \psi =P_{\theta }[\psi ],\quad \varphi \land \psi \not\equiv -\infty . \end{equation}

Then

3.26
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }[\varphi \land \psi ]=\varphi \land \psi . \end{equation}

Proof. Observe that by proposition 3.1.24 3,

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi \land \psi ]\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=\varphi ,\quad P_{\theta }[\varphi \land \psi ]\leq P_{\theta }[\psi ]=\psi . \]

So the $\leq $ direction in (3.26) holds. The reverse direction is trivial. $\square$

Lemma 3.1.17. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that (3.25) holds and

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n>0. \]

Suppose that $\gamma ,\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and satisfy

\[ \gamma \preceq \varphi ,\quad \eta \preceq \psi ,\quad \int _ X \theta _{\gamma }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n,\quad \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

Then $\gamma \land \eta \not\equiv -\infty $ and

3.27
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\gamma \land \eta }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n. \end{equation}
Later on in chapter 6, we shall reformulate and generalize this lemma in terms of the $P$-preorder, which is a more natural language to express the result. See proposition 6.1.21.

Proof. Choose constants $A,B>0$ such that

\[ \gamma -A\leq \varphi ,\quad \eta -B\leq \psi . \]

Since

\[ (\gamma -A)\land (\eta -B)\sim \gamma \land \eta , \]

the non-pluripolar masses of these two rooftop potentials are equal by the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. Hence it suffices to prove the lemma after replacing $\gamma ,\eta $ by $\gamma -A,\eta -B$. We can therefore assume that

\[ \gamma \leq \varphi ,\quad \eta \leq \psi . \]

Step 1. We first show that $\gamma \land \psi \not\equiv -\infty $ and

3.28
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\gamma \land \psi }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n. \end{equation}

For any $a>1$, we define

\[ \gamma _ a\coloneqq P_{\theta }\Bigl ( a\gamma +(1-a)\varphi \Bigr ). \]

When $a=1$, we simply write $\gamma _1=\gamma $.

Thanks to corollary 2.4.16, for any $a\geq 1$, we have $\gamma _ a\leq \varphi $ and

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\gamma _ a}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

By our assumption, for any $a\geq 1$, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\gamma _ a}^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n> \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n,\quad \gamma _ a\preceq \varphi ,\quad \varphi \land \psi \preceq \varphi . \]

So proposition 3.1.15 gives

\[ \gamma _ a\land \varphi \land \psi =\gamma _ a\land \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

In particular, for $a=1$, we find $\gamma \land \psi \not\equiv -\infty $. It remains to verify (3.28).

For $a>1$, we have

\[ \gamma \geq a^{-1}\gamma _ a+\left(1-a^{-1}\right)\varphi , \]

thanks to the definition of $\gamma _ a$ (cf. (2.52)). Hence

\[ \gamma \land \psi \geq a^{-1}\left( \gamma _ a\land \psi \right)+\left(1-a^{-1}\right)\left(\varphi \land \psi \right). \]

Therefore, by the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\gamma \land \psi }^ n\geq \left(1-a^{-1}\right)^ n \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n. \]

Letting $a\to \infty $ and taking theorem 2.4.8 into account, (3.28) follows.

Step 2. We complete the proof.

For any $a>1$, we let

\[ \eta _ a\coloneqq P_{\theta }\Bigl ( a\eta +(1-a)\psi \Bigr ). \]

When $a=1$, we set $\eta _1=\eta $. Thanks to corollary 2.4.16, for each $a\geq 1$, we have $\eta _ a\leq \psi $ and

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\eta _ a}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

From Step 1, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\gamma \land \psi }^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\eta _ a}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n>\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n. \]

Applying proposition 3.1.15 again, we find

\[ \gamma \land \psi \land \eta _ a =\gamma \land \eta _ a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

When $a=1$, we find $\gamma \land \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. It remains to prove (3.27).

By definition of $\eta _ a$, we have

\[ \eta \geq a^{-1}\eta _ a+\left(1-a^{-1}\right)\psi . \]

Therefore,

\[ \gamma \land \eta \geq a^{-1} (\gamma \land \eta _ a)+\left(1-a^{-1}\right) \left( \gamma \land \psi \right). \]

By the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\gamma \land \eta }^ n\geq \left(1-a^{-1}\right)^ n\int _ X \theta _{\gamma \land \psi }^ n. \]

Letting $a\to \infty $ and applying theorem 2.4.8 again, we arrive at (3.27). $\square$

There is an interesting diamond-like inequality regarding the non-pluripolar masses:

Theorem 3.1.18. Assume that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \land \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

3.29
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. We may assume that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, as otherwise (3.29) follows immediately from the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8.

Step 1. We claim that it suffices to prove (3.29) with $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ and $P_{\theta }[\psi ]$ in place of $\varphi $ and $\psi $.

In fact, as $\varphi \land \psi \not\equiv -\infty $, we also have $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\land P_{\theta }[\psi ]\not\equiv -\infty $. Moreover,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n,\quad \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\psi ]}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n \]

by proposition 3.1.6. On the other hand, as $C\to \infty $, the potentials

\[ \Bigl ( (\varphi +C)\land V_{\theta } \Bigr ) \lor \Bigl ( (\psi +C)\land V_{\theta } \Bigr ) \]

converge to $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\lor P_{\theta }[\psi ]$ almost everywhere. Therefore, thanks to corollary 2.4.9 and theorem 2.4.8,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\lor P_{\theta }[\psi ]}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n. \]

Finally, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\land P_{\theta }[\psi ]}^ n= \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n \]

as well. When the left-hand side vanishes, this follows from theorem 2.4.8. Otherwise, it follows from 3.1.17.

In particular, (3.29) is equivalent to the corresponding result with $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ and $P_{\theta }[\psi ]$ in place of $\varphi $ and $\psi $.

Step 2. We shall assume that $\varphi $ and $\psi $ are model potentials in the sequel.

We write $\gamma =\varphi \lor \psi $ for simplicity. Note that $\gamma \leq 0$.

For each $C>0$, we introduce

\[ \varphi _ C=\varphi \lor (\gamma -C),\quad \psi _ C=\psi \lor (\gamma -C). \]

Note that $\varphi _ C\sim \psi _ C\sim \gamma $. Observe that

\[ \inf _{C>0} \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C=\varphi \land \psi . \]

The $\geq $ direction is trivial, and the $\leq $ direction follows from the observation that the left-hand side is dominated by both $\varphi _ C$ and $\psi _ C$ for any $C>0$, and therefore by both $\varphi $ and $\psi $.

For $C>0$, we compute

3.30
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n=& \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi >\gamma -C\} } \, \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n+ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \leq \gamma -C\} } \, \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n & \\ =& \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi >\gamma -C\} } \, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \leq \gamma -C\} } \, \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n & \textup{by proposition \href{../../tag/9E81D36B1290/}{2.2.2} \href{../../tag/9E81D36B1290/#sgpt-1C036AD6}{1}}\\ =& \theta _{\varphi }^ n+ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \leq \gamma -C\} } \, \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n & \textup{by theorem \href{../../tag/553335F185CE/}{3.1.7}}. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

In the last line we use that $\varphi $ is model, so $\theta _\varphi ^ n$ is carried by $\{ \varphi =0\} \subseteq \{ \varphi >\gamma -C\} $. We also get a similar formula for $\theta _{\psi _ C}^ n$.

Therefore, taking the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 into consideration, we find

3.34
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\gamma }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\int _{\{ \varphi \leq \gamma -C\} } \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n,\quad \int _ X \theta _{\gamma }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n=\int _{\{ \psi \leq \gamma -C\} } \theta _{\psi _ C}^ n. \end{equation}

On the other hand, using 3.1.3 and (3.30), we find

\[ \begin{aligned} \theta _{\varphi _ C\land \psi _ C}^ n \leq & \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C=\varphi _ C\} } \, \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C=\psi _ C\} } \, \theta _{\psi _ C}^ n \\ \leq & \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C=\varphi _ C=0\} }\, \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C=\psi _ C=0\} }\, \theta _{\psi }^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \leq \gamma -C\} }\, \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n\\ & +\mathds {1}_{\{ \psi \leq \gamma -C\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ C}^ n. \end{aligned} \]

In particular,

\[ \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C<0 \} }\, \theta _{\varphi _ C\land \psi _ C}^ n \leq \mathds {1}_{\{ \varphi \leq \gamma -C\} }\, \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n+\mathds {1}_{\{ \psi \leq \gamma -C\} }\, \theta _{\psi _ C}^ n. \]

Integrating, we find

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _{\{ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C=0 \} } \theta _{\varphi _ C\land \psi _ C}^ n \geq & \int _ X \theta _{\gamma }^ n-\int _{\{ \varphi \leq \gamma -C\} } \theta _{\varphi _ C}^ n-\int _{\{ \psi \leq \gamma -C\} } \theta _{\psi _ C}^ n\\ =& \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n+\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\gamma }^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where on the first line we used the fact that $\varphi _ C\land \psi _ C\sim \gamma $, while on the second line, we used (3.34).

Letting $C\to \infty $, and using corollary 2.4.11, we conclude (3.29). $\square$

3.1.2 Properties of the -envelope

Let $\theta ,\theta _1,\theta _2$ be smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$.

Proposition 3.1.23. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold $Y$ to $X$. Then for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we have

\[ P_{\pi ^*\theta }[\pi ^*\varphi ]=\pi ^*P_{\theta }[\varphi ]. \]

In particular, a potential $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ is model if and only if $\pi ^*\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )_{>0}$ is model.

Proof. By proposition 1.5.8, every $\chi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$ is of the form $\chi =\pi ^*\psi $ for a unique $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Since $\pi $ is surjective, $\chi \leq 0$ if and only if $\psi \leq 0$. Moreover,

\[ \chi \preceq \pi ^*\varphi \Longleftrightarrow \psi \preceq \varphi . \]

Thus the candidates defining $P_{\pi ^*\theta }[\pi ^*\varphi ]$ are exactly the pull-backs of the candidates defining $P_\theta [\varphi ]$, and hence

\[ P_{\pi ^*\theta }[\pi ^*\varphi ]=\pi ^*P_\theta [\varphi ]. \]

Finally, the non-pluripolar mass is preserved under bimeromorphic pull-back by proposition 2.4.3. Therefore $\varphi $ is model of positive mass if and only if $\pi ^*\varphi $ is. $\square$

We have the following concavity property of the $P$-envelope.

Proposition 3.1.24.

  1. Suppose that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb {R}_{>0}$. Then

    \[ P_{\lambda \theta }[\lambda \varphi ]=\lambda P_{\theta }[\varphi ]. \]

  2. Suppose that $\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _1)$ and $\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _2)$. Then

    \[ P_{\theta _1+\theta _2}[\varphi _1+\varphi _2]\geq P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]+P_{\theta _2}[\varphi _2]. \]

  3. Suppose that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \preceq \psi $. Then

    \[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\leq P_{\theta }[\psi ]. \]

Proof. 1 This follows by rescaling the candidates in the definition of the $P$-envelope.

2 Suppose that $\psi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _1)$ and $\psi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _2)$ satisfy

\[ \psi _ i\leq 0,\quad \psi _ i\preceq \varphi _ i \]

for $i=1,2$. Then

\[ \psi _1+\psi _2\leq 0,\quad \psi _1+\psi _2\preceq \varphi _1+\varphi _2. \]

It follows from (3.3) that

\[ \psi _1+\psi _2\leq P_{\theta _1+\theta _2}[\varphi _1+\varphi _2]. \]

Since $\psi _1$ and $\psi _2$ are arbitrary, we conclude.

3 This follows by inclusion of the defining candidate sets. $\square$

Proposition 3.1.25. Let $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in I}$ be a decreasing net of potentials in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfying $P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]=\varphi _ j$ for each $j\in I$. Set $\varphi \coloneqq \inf _{j\in I} \varphi _ j$. Then $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=\varphi $.

Proof. Since $P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]=\varphi _ j$, we have $\sup _ X\varphi _ j=0$. By corollary 1.5.6, we have $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $\varphi \leq 0$. Therefore, for each $j\in I$,

\[ \varphi \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j] =\varphi _ j. \]

Therefore, $\varphi =P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$. $\square$

Proposition 3.1.26. Let $(\epsilon _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}$ with limit $0$. Take a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. Consider a decreasing net $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ with $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _ i \omega )$ satisfying

3.44
\begin{equation} P_{\theta +\epsilon _ i\omega }\left[\varphi _ i\right]=\varphi _ i \end{equation}

for every $i\in I$. Set $\varphi \coloneqq \inf _{i\in I}\varphi _ i$. Then $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=\varphi $, and

3.45
\begin{equation} \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \left(\theta + \epsilon _ i \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi _ i}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \end{equation}

Moreover, if $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$, then for any prime divisor $E$ over $X$, we have

3.46
\begin{equation} \lim _{i\in I} \nu \left(\varphi _ i,E\right)=\nu (\varphi ,E). \end{equation}
Note that both (3.45) and (3.46) fail without the assumption (3.44).

Proof. We first observe that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. In fact, for each $i_0\in I$, the tail $(\varphi _ i)_{i\geq i_0}$ lies in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _{i_0}\omega )$ and decreases to $\varphi $. Since $\sup _ X\varphi _ i=0$ by (3.44), the limit is not identically $-\infty $. Thus $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _{i_0}\omega )$ for every $i_0\in I$. Since $\epsilon _{i_0}\to 0$, it follows that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For every $i\in I$ we have

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\leq P_{\theta +\epsilon _ i\omega }[\varphi ]\leq P_{\theta +\epsilon _ i\omega }[\varphi _ i]=\varphi _ i. \]

Therefore, $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\leq \varphi $. The reverse inequality is trivial. Hence $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=\varphi $.

By the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 and the multi-linearity of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 6, we have

\[ \varliminf _{i\in I} \int _ X \left(\theta + \epsilon _ i \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi _ i} \geq \varliminf _{i\in I} \int _ X \left(\theta + \epsilon _ i \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi } = \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

We now argue the reverse inequality.

Fix $i_0\in I$. Since passing to the tail $i\geq i_0$ does not change the limsup, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \varlimsup _{i\in I} \int _ X \left(\theta + \epsilon _ i \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi _ i}=& \varlimsup _{i\geq i_0} \int _{\left\{ \varphi _ i =0\right\} } \left(\theta + \epsilon _ i \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi _ i} \\ \leq & \varlimsup _{i\geq i_0} \int _{\left\{ \varphi _ i =0\right\} } \left(\theta + \epsilon _{i_0} \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi _ i}\\ \leq & \int _{\left\{ \varphi =0\right\} } \left(\theta + \epsilon _{i_0} \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi }, \end{aligned} \]

where in the first line we used (3.44) and theorem 3.1.7, and in the last line we used the fact that $\varphi _ i \searrow \varphi $ on the tail $i\geq i_0$, and on this tail, $\varphi _ i \to \varphi $ in $L^1$ as well by corollary 1.5.6, and therefore, corollary 2.4.11 is applicable. Taking the limit with respect to $i_0$, we arrive at the desired conclusion:

\[ \varlimsup _{i\in I} \int _{X} \left(\theta + \epsilon _ i \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi _ i} \leq \varliminf _{i_0\in I}\int _{\{ \varphi =0\} } \left(\theta + \epsilon _{i_0} \omega \right)^ n_{\varphi } = \int _{\{ \varphi =0\} } \theta ^ n_{\varphi } \leq \int _{X} \theta ^ n_{\varphi }, \]

where the equality follows from the multi-linearity of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 6 and the last inequality follows from theorem 3.1.7. This finishes the proof of (3.45).

It remains to argue (3.46). Fix $i_0\in I$. By proposition 1.5.7, there is a constant $B>0$ such that

\[ \nu (\psi ,E)\leq B,\quad \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _{i_0}\omega ). \]

By 2.4.15 and (3.45), for any $\delta \in (0,1)$ there exists $i_{\delta }\geq i_0$ such that for all $i\geq i_{\delta }$ there exists $\psi _ i \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _ i\omega )$ satisfying $(1-\delta ) \varphi _ i + \delta \psi _ i \leq \varphi $. This implies that for $i\geq i_{\delta }$ we have

\[ (1-\delta )\nu (\varphi _ i,E) + \delta \nu (\psi _ i,E) \geq \nu (\varphi ,E) \geq \nu (\varphi _ i,E). \]

Since $\psi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon _{i_0}\omega )$ for $i\geq i_{\delta }$, we get

\[ \varliminf _{i\in I}\nu (\varphi _ i,E)\geq \frac{\nu (\varphi ,E)-\delta B}{1-\delta }. \]

Letting $\delta \searrow 0$ gives $\varliminf _{i\in I}\nu (\varphi _ i,E)\geq \nu (\varphi ,E)$. The reverse inequality follows from $\varphi \leq \varphi _ i$, and hence we conclude (3.46). $\square$

Corollary 3.1.27. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be a model potential. Let $\omega $ be a Kähler form on $X$. Then

\[ \varphi =\inf _{\epsilon >0} P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]. \]

Proof. Set

\[ \eta \coloneqq \inf _{\epsilon >0}P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]. \]

Since $\varphi $ is model, $\varphi =P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\leq P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ]$ for every $\epsilon >0$, hence $\varphi \leq \eta \leq 0$.

Next we prove the reverse direction. Applying proposition 3.1.26 to the decreasing net $(P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega }[\varphi ])_{\epsilon >0}$ and using proposition 3.1.6, we get

\[ P_\theta [\eta ]=\eta ,\quad \int _ X\theta _\eta ^ n=\int _ X\theta _\varphi ^ n. \]

Thus $\eta $ is a candidate in the characterization theorem 3.1.9 of $P_\theta [\varphi ]$. Hence $\eta \leq P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $. $\square$

Proposition 3.1.28. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be an increasing net of potentials in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ uniformly bounded from above. Let $\varphi \coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]. \]

In particular, if $\varphi _ i$ is model for all $i\in I$, then so is $\varphi $.

Proof. We may assume that $I$ is infinite since otherwise, there is nothing to prove.

We write

\[ \eta \coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]. \]

Then it is clear that $\eta \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$.

By corollary 2.4.9, we have

\[ \lim _{i\in I} \int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n=\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n>0. \]

We may assume that for each $i\in I$, $\int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n<\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n$, since otherwise $P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ by theorem 3.1.9, and the same holds for all $j\geq i$; in that case there is nothing to prove.

So by 2.4.15, we can find a decreasing net $\epsilon _ i\searrow 0$ ($i\in I$) with $\epsilon _ i\in (0,1)$ and $\psi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ ($i\in I$) such that for all $i\in I$,

\[ (1-\epsilon _ i) \varphi +\epsilon _ i\psi _ i\leq \varphi _ i. \]

We may, for example, take

3.50
\begin{equation} \epsilon _ i^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\left( 1+\left(\frac{\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n}{\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n}\right)^{1/n} \right). \end{equation}

By the concavity property of the envelope proposition 3.1.24, we have

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]+\epsilon _ i P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]\leq (1-\epsilon _ i) P_{\theta }[\varphi ]+\epsilon _ i P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]\leq \eta . \]

Each $P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]$ has supremum $0$, hence the family is bounded in $L^1$ by proposition 1.5.4. Thus $\epsilon _ iP_{\theta }[\psi _ i]\to 0$ in $L^1$. 3.1.3 Relative full mass classes

Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Fix a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Definition 3.1.29. We define

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ;\phi )\coloneqq & \left\{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta \preceq \phi \right\} ,\\ \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )\coloneqq & \left\{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta \sim \phi \right\} ,\\ \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )\coloneqq & \left\{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ;\phi ): \int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n\right\} ,\\ \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )\coloneqq & \left\{ \eta \in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi ): \int _ X |\phi -\eta |\, \theta _{\eta }^ n<\infty \right\} . \end{aligned} \]

Potentials in the last three classes are said to have relatively minimal singularities, full mass and finite energy relative to $\phi $ respectively.

We have the following inclusions:
3.55
\begin{equation} \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )\subseteq \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )\subseteq \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )\subseteq \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ;\phi ). \end{equation}

The only non-trivial part is the first inclusion, which follows from the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8.

Remark 3.1.30. Note that this integral

\[ \int _ X |\phi -\eta |\, \theta _{\eta }^ n \]

is defined: The locus where $\phi -\eta $ is undefined is a pluripolar set, while the product $\theta _{\eta }^ n$ puts no mass on pluripolar sets (proposition 2.2.2).

When $\phi =V_{\theta }$, we usually write

Proposition 3.1.32 (Comparison principle). Fix $j\in \{ 0,\ldots ,n\} $. Let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ j$ be closed smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ and $\psi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ for $i=1,\ldots ,j$. Suppose that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} & \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}\\ \geq & \int _{\{ \varphi >\psi \} } \theta _{\varphi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}+\int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } \theta _{\psi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}\\ =& \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}-\int _{\{ \varphi \leq \psi \} } \theta _{\varphi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}\\ & +\int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } \theta _{\psi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}\\ =& \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}-\int _{\{ \varphi \leq \psi \} } \theta _{\varphi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}\\ & +\int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } \theta _{\psi }^{n-j}\wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}, \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _{\{ \varphi \leq \psi \} }& \theta _{\varphi }^{n-j} \wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}\\ & \geq \int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } \theta _{\psi }^{n-j} \wedge \theta _{1,\psi _1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{j,\psi _ j}. \end{aligned} \]

Proposition 3.1.47. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net in $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Assume that $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and either one of the following conditions holds:

Step 1. We first prove the result when $\varphi _ j,\varphi \in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$ for each $j\geq 1$.

Due to corollary 2.4.9, we have

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ k\wedge \theta _{\varphi _ j}^{n-k}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n \]

for each $k=0,\ldots ,n$. By (3.81), we have

\[ \begin{aligned} 0\leq & E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ j)\\ =& \frac{1}{n+1}\sum _{k=0}^ n \int _ X(\varphi -\varphi _ j)\, \theta _{\varphi }^ k\wedge \theta _{\varphi _ j}^{n-k}. \end{aligned} \]

We then apply theorem 2.4.7 to conclude that the right-hand side converges to $0$.

Step 2. We prove the general case. The $\leq $ direction in (3.133) follows from the monotonicity of $E_{\theta }^{\phi }$, as proved in 3.1.36. It remains to prove the $\geq $ direction.

After subtracting a constant from all $\varphi _ j$ and from $\varphi $, which shifts all the energies by the same constant in view of (3.71), we may assume that $\varphi \leq \phi $. For each $C>0$ and $j\geq 1$, we let

\[ \varphi _ C\coloneqq \varphi \lor (\phi -C),\quad \varphi _{j,C}\coloneqq \varphi _ j\lor (\phi -C). \]

By Step 1, for each $C>0$, we have

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty } E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{j,C})= E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{C})\geq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi ). \]

It suffices therefore to show that

3.136
\begin{equation} \lim _{C\to \infty } E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{j,C})-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{j})=0\quad \textup{uniformly for $j$ in a tail}. \end{equation}

\[ \begin{aligned} & E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{j,C})-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{j})\\ \leq & \int _ X \left( \varphi _{j,C}-\varphi _{j}\right)\, \theta _{\varphi _{j}}^ n & \textup{by proposition \href{../../tag/51CFFEE97C34/}{3.1.46}}\\ \leq & \int _{\{ \varphi _ j\leq \phi -C\} } (\phi -C-\varphi _ j)\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\\ =& \int _ C^{\infty }\int _{\{ \varphi _ j\leq \phi -t\} } \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ \leq & \int _ C^{\infty }\int _{\{ \varphi _ i\leq (\varphi _ j+\phi -t)/2\} } \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ \leq & 2^ n\int _ C^{\infty }\int _{\{ \varphi _ i\leq (\varphi _ j+\phi -t)/2\} } \theta _{(\varphi _ j+\phi -t)/2}^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ \leq & 2^ n\int _ C^{\infty }\int _{\{ \varphi _ i\leq (\varphi _ j+\phi -t)/2\} } \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n\, \mathrm{d}t & \textup{by proposition \href{../../tag/FDFD0A6F6287/}{3.1.32}}\\ \leq & 2^ n\int _ C^{\infty }\int _{\{ \varphi _ i\leq \phi -t/2\} } \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ = & 2^{n+1}\int _{\{ \varphi _ i< \phi -C/2\} } (\phi -\varphi _ i-C/2)\, \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n\\ = & 2^{n+1}\int _{\{ \varphi _ i< \phi -C/2\} } (\phi -\varphi _ i)\, \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n-C 2^ n\int _{\{ \varphi _ i< \phi -C/2\} }\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n. \end{aligned} \]

\[ \lim _{C\to \infty }C\int _{\{ \varphi _ i< \phi -C/2 \} } \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n\leq 2 \lim _{C\to \infty }\int _{\{ \varphi _ i< \phi -C/2 \} }(\phi -\varphi _ i)\, \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n = 0. \]

\[ L\coloneqq \lim _{i\in I}E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ i). \]

Then $L\leq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )$.

By proposition 1.2.6, we have

\[ \varphi =\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i. \]

Moreover, $\varphi _ i\leq \varphi $ for all $i\in I$, so the family is locally uniformly bounded above. Applying Choquet's lemma proposition 1.2.3, we can find a countable subset $J\subseteq I$ such that

\[ \varphi =\operatorname *{sup}_{j\in J}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ j. \]

Enumerate $J=\{ j_1,j_2,\ldots \} $, repeating elements if necessary. It follows that $\int _{\{ \varphi _ j<\gamma _ j\} }\theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=0$, and the domination principle theorem 2.4.17 gives $\gamma _ j\leq \varphi _ j$. Similarly, $\gamma _ j\leq \psi _ j$. In particular, $\gamma _ j\leq \varphi _ j\land \psi _ j$. We claim that

\begin{equation} \int _ X (\gamma _ j-\phi )\, \theta _{\gamma _ j}^ n >-C \end{equation}

for some $C$ independent of $j$.

Assume the claim is true for now. We get immediately that

\[ \sup _{X}\gamma _ j=\sup _{X\setminus \{ \phi =-\infty \} }(\gamma _ j-\phi )\geq -\frac{C}{\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n}, \]

where the first equality follows from proposition 3.1.13. By proposition 1.5.4, after possibly extracting a subsequence, we may assume that $\gamma _ j\to \gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1$-topology. Then $\gamma \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ by 3.1.50 and proposition 3.1.40. Since $\gamma \leq \varphi \land \psi \leq \phi $, the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 gives

\[ \int _ X\theta _{\varphi \land \psi }^ n=\int _ X\theta _\phi ^ n. \]

Moreover, by the monotonicity of $E^\phi _\theta $,

\[ E^\phi _\theta (\varphi \land \psi )\geq E^\phi _\theta (\gamma )>-\infty . \]

Thus $\varphi \land \psi \in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ by proposition 3.1.40.

Now we prove the claim (3.166). By symmetry, it suffices to prove

\[ \int _ X (\phi -\gamma _ j) \mathrm{e}^{\gamma _ j-\varphi _ j}\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\leq C. \]

But note that

\[ \int _ X (\phi -\gamma _ j) \mathrm{e}^{\gamma _ j-\varphi _ j}\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\int _ X (\phi -\varphi _ j) \mathrm{e}^{\gamma _ j-\varphi _ j}\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n+\int _ X (\varphi _ j-\gamma _ j) \mathrm{e}^{\gamma _ j-\varphi _ j}\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n. \]

But $x \mathrm{e}^{-x}\leq \mathrm{e}^{-1}$ for $x\geq 0$ (with maximum at $x=1$), so the second term is bounded; it remains to prove

\[ \int _ X (\phi -\varphi _ j) \mathrm{e}^{\gamma _ j-\varphi _ j}\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\leq C. \]

As $\gamma _ j\leq \varphi _ j$, it suffices to prove

3.167
\begin{equation} \int _ X (\phi -\varphi _ j)\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\leq C. \end{equation}

We compute, using (3.69) and the fact that the $k<n$ terms $\int _ X(\varphi _ j-\phi )\theta _{\varphi _ j}^ k\wedge \theta _\phi ^{n-k}$ are all non-positive (since $\varphi _ j\leq \phi $),

\[ \int _ X (\varphi _ j-\phi )\, \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\geq (n+1)E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ j). \]

Since $\varphi _ j\searrow \varphi $ and $E_\theta ^{\phi }$ is continuous along decreasing sequences (proposition 3.1.39), $E_\theta ^{\phi }(\varphi _ j)$ is bounded below uniformly in $j$. Thus, (3.167) follows. $\square$

Proposition 3.1.52. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be potentials such that $\varphi \preceq \psi \preceq \phi $. Assume that $\varphi \in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ (resp. $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$, $\mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$), then so is $\psi $.

Proof. We may assume that $\varphi \leq \psi $.

The case $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ follows from the characterization of $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ in proposition 3.1.40 and the monotonicity of $E_{\theta }^{\phi }$ proved in 3.1.36. $\square$

Proposition 3.1.53. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be a uniformly bounded from above non-empty family in $\mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ (resp. $\mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$, $\mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$), then so is $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i$.

Since $\phi $ is model and $\varphi _ i\preceq \phi $, we know that

for any $i\in I$. Since $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is uniformly bounded from above by assumption, our assertion follows. $\square$

Proposition 3.1.54. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

Lemma 3.1.55. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Define

Lemma 3.1.56. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^{1}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, and set

\[ \varphi _ t\coloneqq (1-t)\varphi +t\psi ,\qquad t\in [0,1]. \]

\[ t\mapsto E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ t) \]

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ t) = \int _ X(\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. By corollary 3.1.42, $\varphi _ t\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ for all $t\in [0,1]$. For $a=0,\ldots ,n$, put

\[ M_ a=\int _ X(\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\psi }^ a\wedge \theta _{\varphi }^{n-a}. \]

Note that each $M_ a$ is finite by corollary 3.1.44. By the multi-linearity of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 6, the quantity

\[ \int _ X(\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ n \]

is therefore a polynomial in $t$ with coefficients given by the $M_ a$'s. In particular, the right-hand side of (3.173) is finite and continuous in $t$.

We prove the formula for the right derivative at a point $t\in [0,1)$. Let $s>0$ be small enough so that $t+s\leq 1$. By the cocycle formula proposition 3.1.45, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{t+s})-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ t)}{s} =& \frac{1}{(n+1)s}\sum _{j=0}^ n \int _ X(\varphi _{t+s}-\varphi _ t)\, \theta _{\varphi _{t+s}}^ j\wedge \theta _{\varphi _ t}^{n-j} \\ =& \frac{1}{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n \int _ X(\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\varphi _{t+s}}^ j\wedge \theta _{\varphi _ t}^{n-j}. \end{aligned} \]

Again by proposition 2.2.2 6, for each fixed $j$ the last integral is a polynomial in $s$, expressed as a linear combination of the $M_ a$'s with coefficients depending on $t$. Letting $s\to 0+$, it converges to

\[ \int _ X(\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ n. \]

Therefore,

\[ \lim _{s\to 0+} \frac{E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _{t+s})-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ t)}{s} = \int _ X(\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{\varphi _ t}^ n. \]

The proof for the left derivative is identical, using $s<0$ with $t+s\in [0,1]$. Thus the left and right derivatives agree for $t\in (0,1)$, and the endpoint derivatives are given by the same formula. $\square$

In particular, we have a diamond-like inequality.

Corollary 3.1.57. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

\[ \varphi _ C=\varphi \lor (\phi -C),\quad \psi _ C=\psi \lor (\phi -C). \]

Then

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ C\lor \psi _ C)+E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ C\land \psi _ C)\geq E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi _ C)+E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi _ C). \]

\[ \varphi _ C\searrow \varphi ,\quad \psi _ C\searrow \psi ,\quad \varphi _ C\lor \psi _ C\searrow \varphi \lor \psi , \]

and, by the monotonicity and maximality of the rooftop,

\[ \varphi _ C\land \psi _ C\searrow \varphi \land \psi . \]

Applying proposition 3.1.39 to these four decreasing sequences gives (3.176).

Step 2. We assume that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Thanks to 3.1.56 and the plurifine locality of the non-pluripolar product proposition 2.2.2 1, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \lor \psi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi )=& \int _0^1\int _ X (\varphi \lor \psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{(1-t)\varphi +t(\varphi \lor \psi )}^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ =& \int _0^1 \int _{\{ \psi >\varphi \} } (\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{(1-t)\varphi +t\psi }^ n\, \mathrm{d}t. \end{aligned} \]

On the other hand,

\[ \begin{aligned} & E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\psi )-E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\varphi \land \psi )\\ =& \int _0^1 \int _ X (\psi -\min \{ \varphi ,\psi \} )\, \theta _{((1-t)\varphi +t\psi )\land \psi }^ n\, \mathrm{d}t\\ \leq & \int _0^1 \int _{\{ \varphi <\psi \} } (\psi -\varphi )\, \theta _{(1-t)\varphi +t\psi }^ n\, \mathrm{d}t& \textup{by \href{../../tag/4CF7246E6E5A/}{3.1.3}}. \end{aligned} \]

Adding these equations together, we conclude (3.176). $\square$

Fix a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$.

Definition 3.2.5. Given $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we define its $\mathcal{I}$-envelope as follows:

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\preceq P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

After replacing $g$ by $g+C$ for a suitable constant $C$, which only changes $\varphi '$ by an additive constant, we may assume that $g\geq 0$. Then $\psi '\coloneqq \psi -g$ lies in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$, satisfies $\psi '\leq 0$, and is $\mathcal{I}$-equivalent to $\varphi '$ since adding a smooth function does not change multiplier ideals. \[ \psi -g=\psi '\leq P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

It follows that

\[ \psi -\sup _ X g\leq P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}-\sup _ X g\leq P_{\theta '}[\varphi ']_{\mathcal{I}}, \]

which proves the desired comparison. $\square$

\[ \eta =P_{\pi ^*\theta }[\pi ^*\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

3.187
\begin{equation} \varphi \sim P_{\theta }[\varphi ] \sim P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \end{equation}

Hence, $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ and $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ both have analytic singularities.

In particular,

3.188
\begin{equation} \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n= \operatorname{vol}\theta _{\varphi }. \end{equation}

Proof. First observe that (3.188) follows from (3.187) and theorem 2.4.8. It remains to establish (3.187).

In view of proposition 3.2.13, it suffices to show that

3.189
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\preceq \varphi . \end{equation}

By proposition 3.2.7, proposition 3.1.23 and theorem 1.6.7, after passing to a log resolution we may assume that $\varphi $ has log singularities along an effective $\mathbb Q$-divisor $D$. This reduction does not change the assertion, since both the $P$- and the $\mathcal I$-envelope are compatible with bimeromorphic pull-back.

After multiplying $\theta $ and $\varphi $ by a sufficiently divisible positive integer, and then dividing the final comparison by the same integer, we may assume that $D$ is integral. In this case the Bergman-type quasi-equisingular approximants of $\varphi $ associated to the ideals $\mathcal I(2^ j\varphi )$ have the same singularity type as $\varphi $.

Since

\[ P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}\sim _{\mathcal I}\varphi \]

by proposition 3.2.8, we have

\[ \mathcal I(2^ jP_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I})=\mathcal I(2^ j\varphi ). \]

Thus the quasi-equisingular approximants $\eta _ j$ of $P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$ and $\varphi _ j$ of $\varphi $ may be chosen with the same singularity type $(2^{-j},\mathcal I(2^ j\varphi ))$. Consequently $\eta _ j\sim \varphi _ j\sim \varphi $ for $j\gg 1$. Since $\eta _ j\searrow P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$, choosing one such large $j$ gives

\[ P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}\leq \eta _ j\leq \varphi +C_ j, \]

and hence (3.189) holds. $\square$

3.2.3 Properties of the -envelope

Let $\theta ,\theta _1,\theta _2$ be smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$.

We have the following properties of the $\mathcal{I}$-envelope.

Proposition 3.2.16.

  1. Suppose that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb {R}_{>0}$. Then

    \[ P_{\lambda \theta }[\lambda \varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}=\lambda P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

  2. Suppose that $\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _1)$ and $\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _2)$. Then

    \[ P_{\theta _1+\theta _2}[\varphi _1+\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}\geq P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}+P_{\theta _2}[\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

  3. Suppose that $\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _1)$ and $\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _2)$. Then

    \[ P_{\theta _1+\theta _2}[\varphi _1+\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}\sim _{\mathcal{I}} P_{\theta _1}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}+P_{\theta _2}[\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

  4. Suppose that $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

    \[ P_{\theta }[\varphi _1\lor \varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}\sim _{\mathcal{I}} P_{\theta }[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal{I}}\lor P_{\theta }[\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. 1 This follows by rescaling the candidates in the definition of the $\mathcal{I}$-envelope.

2 Suppose that $\psi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _1)$ and $\psi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _2)$ satisfy

\[ \psi _ i\leq 0,\quad \psi _ i \sim _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi _ i \]

for $i=1,2$. Then thanks to proposition 3.2.4,

\[ \psi _1+\psi _2\leq 0,\quad \psi _1+\psi _2 \sim _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi _1+\varphi _2. \]

It follows that

\[ \psi _1+\psi _2\leq P_{\theta _1+\theta _2}[\varphi _1+\varphi _2]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Since $\psi _1$ and $\psi _2$ are arbitrary, we conclude.

3 and 4: These follow easily from proposition 3.2.8 and proposition 3.2.4. $\square$

Lemma 3.2.17. Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\varphi \preceq \psi $. Then

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq P_{\theta }[\psi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proof. We may assume that $\psi \leq 0$. Observe that $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\lor \psi \sim _{\mathcal{I}} \psi $ as a consequence of proposition 1.4.5 and proposition 3.2.8. So

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\lor \psi \leq P_{\theta }[\psi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

The desired inequality follows. $\square$

Proposition 3.2.18. Consider a decreasing net $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ of model potentials, with $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ for all $i\in I$. Set

\[ \varphi \coloneqq \inf _{i\in I}\varphi _ i. \]

Suppose that $\varphi \not\equiv -\infty $ and $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$. Then

\[ \inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

In particular, if the $\varphi _ i$'s are all $\mathcal{I}$-model, then so is $\varphi $.

Proof. Let $\eta =\inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}$. We have $\eta \geq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ as a consequence of 3.2.17. In particular, $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

By proposition 3.1.26, we have

\[ \lim _{i\in I} \int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n=\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n>0. \]

We may assume that for each $i\in I$, $\int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n>\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n$, since otherwise $\varphi _ i=\varphi $ by theorem 3.1.9, and hence $P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ due to (3.185); the same holds for all $j\geq i$, and there is nothing to prove.

So by 2.4.15, we can find a decreasing net $\epsilon _ i\searrow 0$ ($i\in I$) with $\epsilon _ i\in (0,1)$ and $\psi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that for all $i\in I$,

\[ (1-\epsilon _ i) \varphi _ i+\epsilon _ i\psi _ i\leq \varphi . \]

We can, for example, take

\[ \epsilon _ i^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\left( 1+\left(\frac{\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n}{\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n}\right)^{1/n} \right). \]

By proposition 3.2.16 and 3.2.17, we have

\[ \eta +\epsilon _ i P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq (1-\epsilon _ i) \eta +\epsilon _ i P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq (1-\epsilon _ i) P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}+\epsilon _ i P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Each $P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}$ has supremum $0$, so proposition 1.5.4 shows that the family is bounded in $L^1$. Hence $\epsilon _ iP_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\to 0$ in $L^1$, and the preceding inequality gives $\eta \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ almost everywhere. By proposition 1.2.6, the inequality holds everywhere. $\square$

Proposition 3.2.19. Let $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ be an increasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ uniformly bounded from above. Let $\varphi \coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

In particular, if the $\varphi _ i$'s are all $\mathcal{I}$-model, then so is $\varphi $.

Proof. Let $\eta =\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}$. Then $\eta \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ as a consequence of 3.2.17.

By corollary 2.4.9, we have

\[ \lim _{i\in I} \int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n=\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n>0. \]

We may assume that for each $i\in I$, $\int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n<\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n$, since otherwise $P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$ by theorem 3.1.9, and hence $P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ due to (3.185); the same holds for all $j\geq i$, and there is nothing to prove.

So by 2.4.15, we can find a decreasing net $\epsilon _ i\searrow 0$ ($i\in I$) as in (3.50) with $\epsilon _ i\in (0,1)$ and $\psi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that for all $i\in I$,

\[ (1-\epsilon _ i) \varphi +\epsilon _ i\psi _ i\leq \varphi _ i. \]

By proposition 3.2.16 and 3.2.17, we have

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}+\epsilon _ i P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq (1-\epsilon _ i) P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}+\epsilon _ i P_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq \eta . \]

As above, $\epsilon _ iP_{\theta }[\psi _ i]_{\mathcal{I}}\to 0$ in $L^1$. It follows that $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq \eta $ almost everywhere, hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. $\square$

Remark 3.2.20. One could also define the following interpolation between the $\mathcal{I}$-envelope and the $P$-envelope: Suppose $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, $j\in \{ 0,\ldots ,n\} $. Then we let

\[ \begin{split} P_{\theta ,j}[\varphi ]\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\left\{ \psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \psi \leq 0,\varphi \preceq \psi , \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}}^{n-j}\right.\\ \left.=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ j\wedge \theta _{P_{\theta }[\psi ]_{\mathcal{I}}}^{n-j} \right\} . \end{split} \]

Based on the techniques developed in chapter 6, one could show that $P_{\theta ,j}[\bullet ]$ is a projection operator. The cases $j=0$ and $j=n$ recover the $\mathcal{I}$- and $P$-envelopes respectively; the intermediate operators $P_{\theta ,j}$ have not, to our knowledge, been systematically studied in the literature.
