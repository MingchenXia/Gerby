14.21 Extrema and density sublevels6267D9D97C43

6267D9D97C43

To apply the strong maximum principle to $w=u-v$, we first locate a maximum point and a minimum point of $w$ in the region where the linear equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$ is uniformly elliptic. Maximizing a small perturbation of $w$ by a function $\chi $ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi >0$ does this, even when the Hessians are only measurable. The strong maximum principle then makes $w$ constant on the components of this region that contain these points, and $w$ is constant if the closures of the two components meet.

In this section, $u,v\in C^{1,1}(X)$ satisfy $A,B\geq 0$ almost everywhere and

93A49A38E101 14.21.1
\begin{equation} A^ n=B^ n=f\, \mathrm{d}V,\qquad f\in C^0(X),\qquad f\geq 0. \end{equation}

Fix a smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\beta $, put $H=A+B$, and let $E_{\rm unif}$ be the set of points near which $H$ is uniformly positive:

E391B921798A 14.21.2
\begin{equation} E_{\rm unif} =\left\{ x:\begin{array}{l} \text{there are a neighborhood }V\ni x\text{ and }c>0\\ \text{with }H\geq c\beta \text{ almost everywhere on }V \end{array}\right\} . \end{equation}

This open set does not depend on $\beta $ or on the choice of representatives of the measurable coefficients.

Theorem 14.21.3.6EB399397505 Assume (14.21.1). If $\chi \in C^2(X,\mathbb R)$ satisfies $C:=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi >0$, put

\[ P_\chi =\{ x\in X:f(x)\mathrm{d}V_ x\geq C_ x^ n\} ,\qquad U=\{ f>0\} . \]

Then $P_\chi $ is a nonempty compact subset of $U$, and $U\subset E_{\rm unif}$. There are components $G_+,G_-$ of $E_{\rm unif}$, each meeting $P_\chi $, such that $w=u-v$ satisfies

7732F2B90E2A 14.21.4
\begin{equation} w\equiv \max _ Xw\quad \hbox{on }\overline{G_+},\qquad w\equiv \min _ Xw\quad \hbox{on }\overline{G_-}. \end{equation}

If $w$ is nonconstant, these closures are disjoint. Consequently $w$ is constant if any one of the following holds:

  1. every two components of $E_{\rm unif}$ meeting $P_\chi $ have intersecting closures;

  2. $E_{\rm unif}$ is connected;

  3. $E_{\rm unif}$ is dense and has at most two components.

The assertions about components, including the three criteria, also hold with $U$ in place of $E_{\rm unif}$. In particular, $w$ is constant when $\{ f=0\} $ is a nonempty connected embedded $C^1$ real hypersurface; this hypersurface need not be orientable or coorientable.

Since the Hessians of $C^{1,1}$ functions exist only almost everywhere, the contact argument must use points where they exist. The following lemma finds such points near a maximum; it also provides the strict comparison step in the measurable strong maximum principle.

Lemma 14.21.5.59B8A4C8DA05 In Euclidean space $\mathbb R^ m$, let $B(p,r)$ be the ball of center $p$ and radius $r>0$, and let $I$ be the identity matrix. Let $g\in C^{1,1}$ near $\overline{B(p,r)}\subset \mathbb R^ m$, suppose $g\leq g(p)$ on the ball, and let $E$ be any subset of full measure in the ball at whose points $g$ has a second-order Taylor expansion. For every $\delta >0$ and $0<\rho <r$, there is $x\in E\cap B(p,\rho )$ such that

\[ D^2g(x)\leq 2\delta I. \]

Proof. Put $g_\delta (x)=g(x)-\delta |x-p|^2$. The gradient map $F=\nabla g_\delta $ is Lipschitz on the ball, so it maps the null set $N=\overline{B(p,r)}\setminus E$ to a null set. Choose $s\in B(0,\delta \rho )\setminus F(N)$ and maximize $g_\delta (x)-s\cdot (x-p)$ on the closed ball. At any point with $|x-p|\geq \rho $, its value is strictly smaller than its value at $p$, because

\[ g(x)-g(p)-\delta |x-p|^2-s\cdot (x-p) \leq -\delta |x-p|^2+|s|\, |x-p|<0. \]

Thus a maximizer $x$ lies in $B(p,\rho )$. The first-order condition at $x$ gives $F(x)=s$, so $x\notin N$. The second-order Taylor expansion at this upper contact point gives $D^2g(x)\leq 2\delta I$. Finally, sets $E$ as in the statement exist: by Rademacher's theorem, applied to the Lipschitz gradient of $g$, the function $g$ has a second-order Taylor expansion almost everywhere. $\square$

Lemma 14.21.6.96D40C4808C2 Let $a=(a^{ij})$ be a measurable symmetric matrix field on a connected domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^ m$. For each compact $K\Subset \Omega $, suppose there are constants $0<\lambda _ K\leq \Lambda _ K<\infty $ such that

\[ \lambda _ K I\leq a(x)\leq \Lambda _ KI \]

almost everywhere on $K$, where $I$ is the identity matrix. Repeated indices $i,j=1,\ldots ,m$ are summed, and $z_{ij}=\partial _ i\partial _ jz$. If $z\in W^{2,\infty }_{\rm loc}(\Omega )$, $a^{ij}z_{ij}\geq 0$ almost everywhere, and $z$ attains its supremum at a point of $\Omega $, then $z$ is constant.

Proof. We first prove strict weak comparison on a relatively compact domain: if $b\in C^{1,1}$, $Lb:=a^{ij}b_{ij}\geq \kappa >0$ almost everywhere, and $b\leq M$ on the boundary, then $b\leq M$ inside. Otherwise an interior maximum point lies in a closed ball on which $a\leq \Lambda I$. Apply 14.21.5 to the set of full measure where both the differential inequality and the Taylor expansion hold. Choosing $2\delta m\Lambda <\kappa $ gives the contradiction $\kappa \leq Lb\leq 2\delta \operatorname {tr}a\leq 2\delta m\Lambda $.

Take the $C^{1,1}$ representative of $z$, write $M=\sup _\Omega z$, and suppose that $K=\{ z=M\} $ is a nonempty proper subset of $\Omega $. Near a relative boundary point of $K$, choose $y\notin K$ so that the ball of radius $r=\operatorname {dist}(y,K)>0$ is compactly contained in $\Omega $ and touches $K$ at $x_0$. On the closed annulus $r/2\leq |x-y|\leq r$, use fixed bounds $\lambda I\leq a\leq \Lambda I$ and set

\[ b(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha |x-y|^2}-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha r^2}. \]

For sufficiently large $\alpha $,

\[ Lb=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha |x-y|^2} \bigl (4\alpha ^2 a^{ij}(x_ i-y_ i)(x_ j-y_ j) -2\alpha \operatorname {tr}a\bigr )\geq \kappa >0 \]

almost everywhere on the annulus. On its inner sphere $z<M$ uniformly; on its outer sphere $b=0$. Choose $\eta >0$ small enough that $z+\eta b\leq M$ on both spheres. Strict weak comparison, applied to $z+\eta b$, gives this inequality throughout the annulus. At $x_0$, however, $z=M$, $b=0$, $\nabla z=0$, and $b$ increases to first order in the inward radial direction, which contradicts that inequality. Thus $K$ has no relative boundary point in $\Omega $, and connectedness proves the assertion. The argument uses no derivatives of the measurable coefficients. $\square$

At a point where $A,B$ are semipositive, define the cofactor quadratic form on real cotangent vectors

57588F1C3107 14.21.7
\begin{equation} Q_ x(\xi )\mathrm{d}V_ x =\mathrm{i}\, \xi ^{1,0}\wedge \overline{\xi ^{1,0}}\wedge T_ x . \end{equation}

Here $\xi ^{1,0}$ is the $(1,0)$-part of $\xi $, and forms of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$ are ordered by testing against $\mathrm i\xi ^{1,0}\wedge \overline{\xi ^{1,0}}$ in this way. Mixed products of semipositive $(1,1)$-forms are semipositive in this order. Expanding $H^{n-1}$, where $H=A+B$, gives

144125DEDD1C 14.21.8
\begin{equation} 2^{-(n-1)}H^{n-1}\leq T\leq H^{n-1}. \end{equation}

At a point, choose a $\beta $-unitary coframe $\mathrm{d}z_1,\ldots ,\mathrm{d}z_ n$ that diagonalizes $H$, and write $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ n$ for the eigenvalues of $H$. For a $(1,0)$-covector $\alpha =\sum _{k=1}^ n b_ k\, \mathrm{d}z_ k$ with complex coefficients $b_ k$, the coefficient of $\mathrm{i}\alpha \wedge \bar\alpha \wedge H^{n-1}$ in this coframe is a positive constant depending only on $n$, times

CEEE110C45FE 14.21.9
\begin{equation} \sum _{k=1}^ n |b_ k|^2\prod _{\ell \ne k}\lambda _\ell . \end{equation}

For $n\geq 2$, (14.21.8) and (14.21.9) show that $Q$ is locally uniformly elliptic exactly on $E_{\rm unif}$, that is, where $H$ has a local essential positive lower bound. The implication from ellipticity of $Q$ to a lower bound for $H$ uses the local upper bound for $H$: testing each eigendirection bounds every product $\prod _{\ell \ne k}\lambda _\ell $ below, and choosing $k$ different from an index of a smallest eigenvalue then bounds that eigenvalue below. For $n=1$ one has $T=1$, so the linearized equation is elliptic everywhere.

D7E23A564A00 14.21.10
\begin{equation} w-s(v-\chi ). \end{equation}

For smooth data, the complex Hessian of this function is nonpositive at a maximum point, that is,

C5CDE7D4BA13 14.21.11
\begin{equation} (1+s)B\geq A+sC. \end{equation}

Since $sC>0$, this gives $B>0$, and since $A^ n=B^ n$, also $A>0$. In a $B$-unitary frame put $J=B^{-1/2}AB^{-1/2}$, $K=B^{-1/2}CB^{-1/2}$. Since $\det J=1$, arithmetic–geometric mean and (14.21.11) give

\[ n+s\operatorname {tr}K\leq \operatorname {tr}J+s\operatorname {tr}K \leq n(1+s). \]

Consequently $\operatorname {tr}K\leq n$, $B\geq C/n$, and $\det K\leq (\operatorname {tr}K/n)^ n\leq 1$. In particular $B^ n\geq C^ n$.

For $C^{1,1}$ potentials, fix a maximum point $p$ of (14.21.10) and apply 14.21.5 on a coordinate ball, with radii tending to zero and errors $\delta _ j\downarrow 0$. For the set of full measure in that lemma, take the points where both Hessians are defined and semipositive and have the common determinant given by (14.21.1). With $\beta _0=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$, the resulting points $x_ j\to p$ satisfy

\[ A(x_ j)+s C_ j(x_ j)\leq (1+s)B(x_ j),\qquad C_ j=C-(\delta _ j/s)\beta _0>0 \]

for large $j$. The matrix argument just given yields $f(x_ j)\mathrm{d}V_{x_ j}\geq C_ j(x_ j)^ n$. Since $f$, $\mathrm{d}V$ and $C$ are continuous, letting $j\to \infty $ gives $p\in P_\chi $; no continuity of the Hessians is needed. Thus every maximum point of the perturbed function lies in $P_\chi $. The set $P_\chi $ is closed, by continuity of $f$, $\mathrm{d}V$ and $C$, hence compact, and it is contained in $U$ because $C^ n>0$. Letting $s\downarrow 0$, a limit point of these maximum points is a maximum point of $w$ lying in $P_\chi $. Applying the same argument to $-w$ gives a minimum point of $w$ in $P_\chi $ as well. In particular, $P_\chi $ is nonempty.

On a relatively compact ball in $U$, the determinants of $A$ and $B$ have a positive lower bound, while their coefficients have an essential upper bound. Every eigenvalue is therefore bounded below by the determinant lower bound divided by a fixed power of the upper bound. Thus $U\subset E_{\rm unif}$.

By (14.20.1), $w$ solves a linear equation in nondivergence form with measurable coefficients, whose principal quadratic form is a fixed positive multiple of $Q$. On each relatively compact coordinate ball in $E_{\rm unif}$, these coefficients are uniformly elliptic. 14.21.6, applied to $w$ and to $-w$, makes $w$ constant on the component of $E_{\rm unif}$ containing the maximum point found above, and constant on the component containing the minimum point found above. Continuity extends the two constant values to the closures, proving (14.21.4). Distinct extrema force disjoint closures.

The same argument applies on $U$ itself, since $U$ contains the extremal points found in $P_\chi $ and the cofactor is locally uniformly elliptic on $U\subset E_{\rm unif}$. This proves the assertions with $U$ in place of $E_{\rm unif}$.

The component criteria are immediate except for the dense two-component case. If an open dense subset of connected $X$ has at most two components with disjoint closures, those closures are disjoint nonempty closed sets whose union is $X$, contradicting connectedness. Finally, when $Z=\{ f=0\} $ is a connected embedded real hypersurface, flattening charts assign to each point of $Z$ the unordered pair of components containing its two local sides. This pair is locally constant along $Z$, so connectedness makes it independent of the point; the two labels may coincide or be exchanged along a loop. Every component of $X\setminus Z$ has a nonempty boundary in $Z$, since otherwise it would be both open and closed in connected $X$. Near such a boundary point it contains a local side. Thus the pair accounts for every component, and $U$ has at most two components. It is dense because a hypersurface has empty interior. This proves the last assertion without choosing a global side. $\square$

In this argument, the $C^{1,1}$ bound controls the measurable coefficients, and continuity of the density places the limiting contact points in $\{ f>0\} $. The next criterion gives a quantitative condition for $\{ f>0\} $ to be connected.

14.21.12 A square-root threshold95F071B4092F

A nonnegative $C^{1,1}$ density grows at most quadratically away from each of its zeros. If its zero set separates a small ball, a family of parallel segments crossing the zero set therefore contributes at least a constant times $\sqrt t$ to the volume of $\{ f\leq t\} $ for small $t>0$. Excluding this lower bound makes $\{ f>0\} $ connected, and 14.21.3 then gives uniqueness.

Write $\operatorname {Vol}(E)=\int _ E\mathrm{d}V$ for Borel sets $E$.

Theorem 14.21.13.61851D05B072 Let $u,v\in C^2(X)$ satisfy $A,B\geq 0$ and $A^ n=B^ n=f\, \mathrm{d}V$, where $f\geq 0$ is $C^{1,1}$. If

B72083672909 14.21.14
\begin{equation} \liminf _{t\downarrow 0} \frac{\operatorname {Vol}\{ f\leq t\} }{\sqrt t}=0, \end{equation}

then $\{ f>0\} $ is connected and $u-v$ is constant. In particular this holds when $f^{-1/2}\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, with $f^{-1/2}=+\infty $ on $\{ f=0\} $. The exponent $1/2$ is sharp in every dimension $n\geq 3$.

Proof. Write $Z=\{ f=0\} $. Condition (14.21.14) first implies $\operatorname {Vol}(Z)=0$. We show that the complement of $Z$ in every sufficiently small coordinate ball is connected. Fix such a ball with convex Euclidean image. If the complement of $Z$ in it were disconnected, choose points $a,b$ in different components and small closed disks centered at $a$ and $b$, perpendicular to the segment $[a,b]$ and contained in those respective components. By making the disks small, all parallel segments between corresponding points stay in the ball. Each segment meets $Z$, since otherwise it would connect the two components.

On a fixed coordinate region containing the closed ball, the gradient of $f$ is Lipschitz with some constant $K>0$. Since $f\geq 0$, we have $\nabla f(z)=0$ at every zero $z$, hence

BB22D6F8FAF9 14.21.15
\begin{equation} f(x)\leq \frac K2|x-z|^2 \end{equation}

whenever the segment from $z$ to $x$ stays in that region. The endpoint disks are compact and disjoint from $Z$, so the parts of the segments near them contain no zeros. Thus every parallel segment contains a zero at a fixed positive distance from its endpoints. For small $t>0$, (14.21.15) gives on that segment an interval of length $2\sqrt{2t/K}$ contained in $\{ f\leq t\} $. Integrating over the transverse disk, which has positive measure, and using that $\mathrm{d}V$ is comparable to coordinate volume, we obtain

\[ \operatorname {Vol}\{ f\leq t\} \geq c\sqrt t \]

for all sufficiently small $t$, contradicting (14.21.14). This proves the local connectedness assertion.

For each such ball, the complement of $Z$ in it is nonempty, since $Z$ has zero volume, and it lies in one component of $U=X\setminus Z$. Two overlapping balls give the same component, because their open overlap contains a point outside $Z$. Since $X$ is connected, all these balls give the same component. Hence $U$ is connected, and 14.21.3 shows that $u-v$ is constant.

If $f^{-1/2}\in L^1$, then, outside the null zero set,

\[ \frac{\operatorname {Vol}\{ f\leq t\} }{\sqrt t} \leq \int _{\{ f\leq t\} }f^{-1/2}\, \mathrm{d}V\longrightarrow 0 \]

by dominated convergence. This proves the sufficient integrability condition. The sharpness assertion follows from the examples in 14.29, as quantified at the end of that section. $\square$
