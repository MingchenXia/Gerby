18.39 Singularities along a section over a divisorDFED5BDE8678

DFED5BDE8678

We now replace the isolated poles by a pole along the image of a holomorphic section of the fibration over a divisor of the base. The selected sections must then vanish to a certain order along that divisor. As for point poles, the normal variables in the fiber shift the integrability threshold only by a bounded amount, so this order differs from the order imposed by a divisorial pole on the base by a bounded number. Restriction sequences along the divisor show that each unit of vanishing order costs $O(m^{q-1})$ sections, and kernel convergence again reduces to the equilibrium theorem on the base.

For the restriction sequences we need a bound for the dimensions of section spaces that is uniform over a range of twists growing linearly with $m$.

Lemma 18.39.1.EF14C0C90C6C Let $M$ be a compact complex manifold of dimension $d\geq 0$, and let $E,F$ be holomorphic line bundles on $M$. For each $C_0>0$ there is a constant $C_1$ such that

\[ h^0(M,E^ m\otimes F^ j)\leq C_1m^ d \qquad (m\geq 1,\ j\in \mathbb Z,\ |j|\leq C_0m). \]

Proof. For $d=0$, the manifold $M$ is a finite set and the dimension is at most its number of points. Let $d\geq 1$. Fix smooth Hermitian metrics on $E$ and $F$, a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$, and finitely many coordinate charts with holomorphic frames, together with smaller subcharts that still cover $M$. For $|j|\leq C_0m$, the local weight of the metric on $E^ m\otimes F^ j$ has second derivatives bounded by $Cm$. At a point $p$ of a smaller subchart, multiply the frame by the exponential of a holomorphic affine function to remove the constant and real-linear terms of this weight at $p$. The new weight vanishes at $p$ and is bounded by $Cm|z-p|^2$ on a fixed neighborhood. A ball of radius $\varepsilon m^{-1/2}$ about $p$ lies in the chart, with $\varepsilon >0$ independent of $p$, $m$ and $j$, and on this ball the weight is bounded. The submean inequality for the holomorphic coefficient of a section $s$ on this ball gives

\[ |s(p)|^2\leq Cm^ d\int _ M|s|^2\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where all constants depend only on the fixed charts, metrics, volume and $C_0$. Evaluation at $p$ has a one-dimensional target, so for an $L^2$-orthonormal basis $s_1,\ldots ,s_ N$ of $H^0(M,E^ m\otimes F^ j)$ its squared norm is $\sum _{\ell =1}^ N|s_\ell (p)|^2\leq Cm^ d$. Integrating over $M$ gives $N\leq C\operatorname {Vol}(M)m^ d$. $\square$

Let $\pi :X\to B$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion of connected compact complex manifolds, with connected fibers of dimension $r\geq 1$, and suppose that $B$ is Kähler of dimension $q\geq 1$. Fix a holomorphic line bundle $A$ on $B$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h_ A$ whose curvature $\theta =c_1(A,h_ A)$ is positive. Let $Z\subset B$ be a nonempty smooth divisor, and suppose that $\pi $ has a holomorphic section $\sigma :Z\to X$ over $Z$; put $Y=\sigma (Z)$. Fix $c>0$ and a smooth metric $h_ Z$ on $\mathcal O_ B(Z)$, with curvature $\Theta _ Z$, such that $\alpha :=\theta -c\Theta _ Z>0$. Let $s_ Z$ be the canonical section of $\mathcal O_ B(Z)$, and set $\psi =c\log |s_ Z|_{h_ Z}^2$.

Fix a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$, chosen independently of $h_ A$. Suppose that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus Y$ and that, in submersion coordinates $(x,z',w_1,\ldots ,w_ r)$ near each point of $Y$,

145C74FFDD88 18.39.2
\begin{equation} \begin{gathered} \varphi =c\log \left(|x|^2+\sum _{j=1}^ r|w_ j|^2\right)+O(1),\\ Z=\{ x=0\} ,\qquad Y=\{ x=w_1=\cdots =w_ r=0\} , \end{gathered} \end{equation}

with a locally bounded remainder. Here $x$ and $z'$ are coordinates on the base, and the vector $z'$ of the $q-1$ tangential coordinates is absent when $q=1$.

Theorem 18.39.3.AE701AEF7FDE The function $\psi $ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, with positive non-pluripolar mass

\[ \int _ B\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=\int _ B\alpha ^ q>0. \]

For every integer $m\geq 1$ put $\ell _ m=\lfloor mc\rfloor $ and $k_ m=\max \{ \ell _ m-r,0\} $. Under holomorphic descent, the spaces selected by $\varphi $ on $X$ and by $\psi $ on $B$ are

\[ \begin{split} V_ m& :=H^0\bigl (X,\pi ^*A^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\varphi )\bigr ) =H^0\bigl (B,A^ m(-k_ m Z)\bigr ),\\ W_ m& :=H^0\bigl (B,A^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\psi )\bigr ) =H^0\bigl (B,A^ m(-\ell _ m Z)\bigr )\subseteq V_ m. \end{split} \]

There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed data, such that

\[ 0\leq \dim V_ m-\dim W_ m\leq Cm^{q-1}. \]

For $q=1$ the difference is at most $r\, \# Z$ for every $m$.

Proof. By the Poincaré–Lelong formula, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\alpha +c[Z]\geq 0$. The divisorial measure identity (11.41.2), applied with the residual form $\alpha >0$ and the bounded part zero, gives $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=\alpha ^ q$, which proves the mass formula.

Every section of $\pi ^*A^ m$ descends by proposition 18.36.1. In the $r+1$ normal variables $(x,w)$, lemma 5.37.1 gives

\[ \mathcal I(m\varphi )=(x,w_1,\ldots ,w_ r)^{k_ m}. \]

The local coefficient $g(x,z')$ of a pulled-back section does not depend on $w$. Expanding $g$ in powers of $x$, we see that $g$ lies in this ideal exactly when the coefficients of $x^ j$ vanish for $j<k_ m$, that is, when $x^{k_ m}$ divides $g$. This is an identity of germs in all tangential variables, so it holds at every point of $Z$. Outside $Y$ the function $\varphi $ is locally bounded. On the base, the same lemma in the single normal variable $x$ gives $\mathcal I(m\psi )=\mathcal O_ B(-\ell _ mZ)$. These facts prove the two identities and the inclusion.

For $k_ m\leq j<\ell _ m$, the restriction sequence along $Z$,

\[ \begin{split} 0& \longrightarrow A^ m(-(j+1)Z)\longrightarrow A^ m(-jZ)\\ & \longrightarrow \bigl (A^ m(-jZ)\bigr )|_ Z\longrightarrow 0, \end{split} \]

shows that passing from vanishing order $j$ to order $j+1$ along $Z$ loses at most $h^0(Z,(A^ m(-jZ))|_ Z)$ dimensions. Apply lemma 18.39.1 on $Z$ with $E=A|_ Z$ and $F=\mathcal O_ B(-Z)|_ Z$; since $0\leq j\leq cm$, each loss is at most $Cm^{q-1}$, uniformly in $j$. There are at most $r$ steps, which proves the estimate. When $q=1$, the divisor $Z$ is a finite set and each restriction space has dimension $\# Z$, which gives the last assertion directly. $\square$

We call $\psi $ the reference potential, $W_ m$ the reference spaces, and $\dim V_ m-\dim W_ m$ the defect; the same terms are used for the analogous comparisons below. The following example shows that the order $m^{q-1}$ in theorem 18.39.3 is sharp.

Example 18.39.4.FE69C8A3CBBF For all integers $q,r,k\geq 1$ and every real $0<c<k$, the hypotheses above are satisfied on $X=\mathbb P^ q\times \mathbb P^ r$ with $B=\mathbb P^ q$ and $A=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^ q}(k)$, for suitable choices made in the proof. For $q\geq 2$ the defect is

\[ \dim V_ m-\dim W_ m =\frac{r(k-c)^{q-1}}{(q-1)!}m^{q-1}+O(m^{q-2}), \]

and for $q=1$ it equals $\min \{ r,\lfloor mc\rfloor \} $ for every $m$.

Proof. Use homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots :Z_ q]$ on the base and $[W_0:\cdots :W_ r]$ on the fiber. Let $Z=\{ Z_0=0\} $ and $Y=Z\times \{ [1:0:\cdots :0]\} $, and let $\omega _ B,\omega _ F$ be the Fubini–Study forms normalized to have top integrals one. Define

\[ R_ B=\frac{|Z_0|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ q|Z_ i|^2},\qquad R_ F=\frac{\sum _{j=1}^ r|W_ j|^2}{\sum _{j=0}^ r|W_ j|^2},\qquad \varphi =c\log (R_ B+R_ F),\quad \psi =c\log R_ B. \]

The function $\varphi $ is smooth off $Y$ and satisfies (18.39.2). Over a common denominator, the numerator of $R_ B+R_ F$ is

\[ \sum _{j=0}^ r|Z_0W_ j|^2+\sum _{i=0}^ q\sum _{j=1}^ r|Z_ iW_ j|^2, \]

whose logarithm is plurisubharmonic in each affine chart. Hence $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -c(\omega _ B+\omega _ F)$. Choose an integer $b\geq c$ and set $\omega =k\omega _ B+b\omega _ F$. This is a Hermitian form, and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq (k-c)\omega _ B+(b-c)\omega _ F\geq 0$. On the base, $\theta =k\omega _ B$, $\Theta _ Z=\omega _ B$ and $\alpha =(k-c)\omega _ B>0$.

Put $\ell _ m=\lfloor mc\rfloor $. Divisibility of homogeneous polynomials by powers of $Z_0$ gives the exact dimensions

\[ \dim W_ m=\binom {km-\ell _ m+q}{q},\qquad \dim V_ m=\binom {km-\max \{ \ell _ m-r,0\} +q}{q}. \]

For large $m$, the two residual degrees differ by $r$ and equal $(k-c)m+O(1)$. Subtracting the two binomial coefficients, which are polynomials of degree $q$ in the residual degrees, gives the asserted leading coefficient and remainder. For $q=1$ the difference is $\ell _ m-\max \{ \ell _ m-r,0\} =\min \{ r,\ell _ m\} $ for every $m$. $\square$

The bounded shift of divisorial order thus has negligible normalized codimension. To compare the kernels under the hypotheses of theorem 18.39.3, fix a smooth positive probability volume $\nu _ B$ on $B$, smooth fiber probabilities, and the lifting map $\mathcal L$ of section 18.36, and put $\nu _ X=\mathcal L\nu _ B$. Use the pullback metric on $\pi ^*A$ and, for a real function $v\in C(B)$, the weight $v\circ \pi $ in the norm on $X$. Write $\beta _{X,m,v\circ \pi ,\varphi }^{(q)}$ and $\mathcal F_{X,m}^{(q)}$ for the kernel measure and the determinant functional on $X$, as in section 18.37, and

\[ E(v)=E_{\theta ,B}[\psi ]_{\mathcal I}(v). \]

Corollary 18.39.5.115614FB1F9D The total variation distance between the kernel measure $\beta _{X,m,v\circ \pi ,\varphi }^{(q)}$ selected by $\varphi $ on $X$ and the lift of the kernel measure selected by $\psi $ on $B$ is $O(m^{-1})$. In particular,

\[ \beta _{X,m,v\circ \pi ,\varphi }^{(q)} \rightharpoonup \mathcal L\bigl (\mathrm{MA}_\theta (E(v))\bigr ). \]

The determinant functionals, normalized to vanish at the zero weight, satisfy

\[ \mathcal F_{X,m}^{(q)}(v\circ \pi )\longrightarrow \int _0^1\left(\int _ Bv\, \mathrm{d}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (E(tv))\right)\mathrm{d}t. \]

With the normalization $(q+r)!/m^{q+r}$ given by the dimension of $X$, the kernel measures tend to zero in total variation.

Proof. As in proposition 18.36.1, fiber integration identifies the norm on $X$ with the norm on $V_ m\subseteq H^0(B,A^ m)$, so the kernel measure on $X$ is the lift of the kernel measure of $V_ m$ on $B$. By lemma 18.37.1, theorem 18.39.3, the latter differs from the kernel measure of $W_ m$ by a positive measure of total mass $q!(\dim V_ m-\dim W_ m)/m^ q=O(m^{-1})$, and so do the lifts. Since $B$ is Kähler and $\psi $ has positive non-pluripolar mass, the equilibrium theorem used in the proof of theorem 18.36.3 applies to $W_ m$ and gives the weak limit. By lemma 18.37.1, the determinant functionals of $V_ m$ and $W_ m$ differ by at most $O(m^{-1})\| v\| _\infty $, and corollary 18.35.6, applied on $B$ with the identity modification, gives the limit of the functionals; its dominated convergence bound is $\| v\| _\infty $ times the bounded sequence of $q$-normalized dimensions. Finally, the $q$-normalized masses are bounded, so multiplying them by $(q+r)!m^{-r}/q!$ proves the last assertion. $\square$

Thus the selecting potentials of (18.39.2) admit the approximation asked for in 24.10.2, whenever the curvature margin $\theta -c\Theta _ Z$ is positive.

18.39.6 A bounded divisorial band from fiber integrability14F47AA5A61B

The comparison of theorem 18.39.3 does not need the exact local form (18.39.2). It suffices to bound the selecting potential from below by a divisorial pole on all of $X$, and from above by an anisotropic model in a single chart over the divisor. The lower bound gives the inclusion of the reference spaces into the selected spaces. The upper bound forces vanishing along the divisor to an order that falls short of the reference order by a bounded amount: separate powers of the normal variables replace the shift by the fiber dimension with a weighted shift. The selecting potential may then be singular along a positive-dimensional subset of a fiber, and it need not descend to the base.

Let $\pi :X\to B$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion of connected compact complex manifolds with connected fibers of dimension $f\geq 1$, and let $B$ be Kähler of dimension $q\geq 1$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$, a holomorphic line bundle $A$ on $B$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. Write $\theta =c_1(A,h)$, and use holomorphic descent to regard the selected spaces as subspaces of $H^0(B,A^ m)$:

\[ V_ m=H^0\bigl (X,\pi ^*A^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\varphi )\bigr ) \subseteq H^0(B,A^ m),\qquad m\geq 1. \]

Let $D\subset B$ be a nonempty smooth connected reduced divisor. Fix a smooth metric $H_ D$ on $\mathcal O_ B(D)$, and write $s_ D$ for its canonical section and $\eta =c_1(\mathcal O_ B(D),H_ D)$ for its curvature.

Lemma 18.39.7.BBA6C67F74CF Let $r\geq 1$ and $\ell \geq 0$ be integers, let $p,b_1,\ldots ,b_ r$ be positive integers, and let $P>0$. The function

\[ |z|^{2\ell }\left(|z|^{2p}+\sum _{j=1}^ r|w_ j|^{2b_ j}\right)^{-P} \]

of $(z,w)\in \mathbb C\times \mathbb C^ r$ is locally integrable near the origin if and only if

\[ \frac{\ell +1}{p}+\sum _{j=1}^ r\frac1{b_ j}>P. \]

In the case of equality, the integral diverges logarithmically.

Proof. After integrating over the angles, substitute $x_0=|z|^{2p}$ and $x_ j=|w_ j|^{2b_ j}$. Up to a positive constant, the integral becomes

\[ \int (x_0+\cdots +x_ r)^{-P}x_0^{(\ell +1)/p-1} \prod _{j=1}^ r x_ j^{1/b_ j-1}\, \mathrm dx_0\cdots \mathrm dx_ r \]

over a box near the origin. This box contains a small simplex $\{ \sum _ jx_ j<\delta \} $ and is contained in a larger one. On either simplex use the coordinates $\rho =\sum _ jx_ j$ and $x_ j=\rho y_ j$. The integral over $y$ is finite and positive, because all its exponents exceed $-1$, and the integral over $\rho $ is

\[ \int _0^\delta \rho ^{(\ell +1)/p+\sum b_ j^{-1}-P-1}\, \mathrm d\rho . \]

This gives the threshold and the logarithmic divergence at equality. Bounded factors in the weight, holomorphic units and additional tangential variables do not affect this local test. $\square$

Theorem 18.39.8.A79139E2A3C6 Suppose that $c>0$ and $u\in C^\infty (B,\mathbb R)$ satisfy

\[ \beta =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-c\eta >0, \qquad \varphi \geq c\log \| \pi ^*s_ D\| _{H_ D}^2-C \quad \hbox{on }X \]

for a constant $C$. Suppose further that there is one submersion polydisk over an open subset of $D$, with coordinates

\[ \pi (z,t,w,v)=(z,t),\quad D=\{ z=0\} ,\quad t\in \mathbb C^{q-1},\quad w\in \mathbb C^ r,\quad v\in \mathbb C^{f-r}, \]

where $1\leq r\leq f$, on which

\[ \varphi \leq \frac cp\log \left(|z|^{2p}+\sum _{j=1}^ r|w_ j|^{2b_ j}\right)+C' \]

for positive integers $p,b_1,\ldots ,b_ r$ and a constant $C'$. Put

\[ a=p\sum _{j=1}^ r b_ j^{-1},\quad L_ m=\lfloor mc\rfloor ,\quad K_ m=\max \{ \lfloor mc-a\rfloor ,0\} ,\quad \psi =u+c\log \| s_ D\| _{H_ D}^2. \]

Then $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(B,\theta )$, its non-pluripolar mass is $\int _ B\beta ^ q\in (0,\infty )$, and for every $m\geq 1$,

\[ W_ m:=H^0\bigl (B,A^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\psi )\bigr ) =H^0(B,A^ m(-L_ mD))\subseteq V_ m \subseteq H^0(B,A^ m(-K_ mD)). \]

Moreover $\dim V_ m-\dim W_ m=O(m^{q-1})=o(m^ q)$. For $q=1$ this difference is at most $\lceil a\rceil $ for every $m$.

Proof. By the Poincaré–Lelong formula, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\beta +c[D]$. Off $D$, locality identifies the non-pluripolar top power with $\beta ^ q$; the non-pluripolar product puts no mass on $D$, and neither does the smooth measure $\beta ^ q$. Hence $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=\beta ^ q$, whose mass is finite and positive because $\beta >0$ and $B$ is compact. The one-variable integrability condition $\ell +1>mc$ gives $\mathcal I(m\psi )=\mathcal O_ B(-L_ mD)$, also when $mc$ is an integer. Since $u$ is bounded, the global lower bound implies $\varphi \geq \psi \circ \pi -C''$. Hence integrability on the base gives integrability of the pullback in every bounded submersion chart, which proves $W_ m\subseteq V_ m$.

For the upper inclusion, take a nonzero section in $V_ m$ and let $\ell $ be its vanishing order along $D$; note that $D$ is irreducible, being smooth and connected. In the polydisk of the upper bound, the coefficient of the section is $z^\ell g(z,t)$, where $g(0,t)$ does not vanish identically on the open subset of $D$, since otherwise the identity theorem would increase the order along $D$. Shrink the polydisk about a point with $w=0$ at which $g(0,t)\ne 0$, so that $g$ becomes a holomorphic unit. The upper bound on $\varphi $ and lemma 18.39.7 then force $\ell +1>mc-a$. For an integer $\ell \geq 0$ this is equivalent to $\ell \geq K_ m$, which proves the upper inclusion.

Since $\lfloor mc\rfloor -\lfloor mc-a\rfloor \leq \lceil a\rceil $, we have $0\leq L_ m-K_ m\leq \lceil a\rceil $. Use the restriction sequences along $D$ from the proof of theorem 18.39.3 for $K_ m\leq j<L_ m$. Each step loses at most $h^0(D,(A^ m(-jD))|_ D)$ dimensions, and by lemma 18.39.1 this is at most $Cm^{q-1}$, uniformly for $0\leq j\leq cm$. Since the number of steps is bounded, the estimate follows. For $q=1$ the connected divisor $D$ is a single point, and each restriction space has dimension one. $\square$

Corollary 18.39.9.094794C14195 Keep the positivity condition $\beta >0$ of theorem 18.39.8. Suppose that a nonempty compact smooth submanifold $Z\subset \pi ^{-1}(D)$ projects submersively onto $D$, and that near every point of $Z$ there are coordinates as in that theorem in which

\[ Z=\{ z=w_1=\cdots =w_ r=0\} ,\qquad \varphi =\frac cp\log \left(|z|^{2p}+\sum _{j=1}^ r|w_ j|^{2b_ j}\right)+O(1), \]

with fixed $c,p,b_ j$. Suppose also that $\varphi $ is locally bounded outside $Z$. Then $\varphi $ satisfies the hypotheses of theorem 18.39.8, so its conclusions hold, and moreover

\[ V_ m=H^0(B,A^ m(-K_ mD))\qquad (m\geq 1). \]

Proof. The local model is bounded below by $c\log |z|^2$ up to a constant. Near points of $\pi ^{-1}(D)$ outside $Z$, the function $\varphi $ is locally bounded, so the same lower bound holds there; away from $\pi ^{-1}(D)$ both functions are locally bounded. By compactness, finitely many charts give the global lower bound. The upper bound holds in any chart of the model. Conversely, if a section is divisible by $z^{K_ m}$, its residual coefficient is bounded and holomorphic in each chart of the model, and the strict inequality $K_ m+1>mc-a$ together with lemma 18.39.7 gives integrability there. Outside $Z$ the function $\varphi $ is locally bounded, which proves the reverse inclusion. $\square$

Such exact models exist with pole sets of positive dimension inside the fibers. Let $B$ be projective, let $A$ be ample, and let $X=B\times (\mathbb P^1)^ f$ with $\pi $ the first projection. Mark a point $o_ j$ in each of the first $r$ factors $\mathbb P^1$ of the fiber, and let $E_ j\subset X$ be the inverse image of $o_ j$. With smooth metrics on the corresponding line bundles, put

\[ Q=\| \pi ^*s_ D\| ^{2p}+\sum _{j=1}^ r\| s_{E_ j}\| ^{2b_ j}, \qquad \varphi =\lambda \log Q,\qquad c=p\lambda . \]

The common zero set of the terms of $Q$ is $D\times \{ o_1\} \times \cdots \times \{ o_ r\} \times (\mathbb P^1)^{f-r}$. Near it, the positive factors of the metrics give exactly the local model of corollary 18.39.9, and $\varphi $ is smooth elsewhere. The inequality $Q\geq \| \pi ^*s_ D\| ^{2p}$ gives the global lower bound. Locally, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log Q$ is bounded below by a convex combination of the negatives of the smooth curvature forms of the terms; hence $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log Q\geq -C_0\omega $ for a constant $C_0$, first off the common zero set and then across it, after a quadratic correction, by the extension theorem for plurisubharmonic functions. Since $A$ is ample, there is $u$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$. For sufficiently small $\lambda >0$ we then have $\lambda C_0\leq 1$, so that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-p\lambda \eta >0$. Thus the exact series of the corollary also occurs when $r<f$.

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=(k\alpha )^ q,\qquad \int _ B\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=k^ q. \]

\[ V_ m=W_ m=H^0(B,A^ m) =s_ E^ m\rho ^*H^0(\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(km)), \qquad \dim V_ m=\binom {km+q}{q}. \]

Proof. Since $(\rho ,\tau )$ is an embedding, $\kappa >0$. The quotient metric satisfies

\[ \| s_ E\| ^2=\rho ^*\left(\frac{\sum _{j=1}^ q|Z_ j|^2}{\sum _{j=0}^ q|Z_ j|^2}\right), \]

as in example 18.39.17. The same calculation over a common denominator, now with coefficient $1/2$, gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -\pi ^*\alpha -\tfrac 32\omega _{\mathbb P^1} \]

off the pole set $E\times \{ o\} $, and across it by the extension theorem. Hence $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq \pi ^*\gamma +\tfrac 12\omega _{\mathbb P^1}\geq 0$. Along the smooth pole set $E\times \{ o\} $, the function $\varphi $ has the exact local model $\tfrac 12\log (|z|^4+|w|^6)+O(1)$, and it is locally bounded elsewhere. It also satisfies the global lower bound $\varphi \geq \log R$.

We may therefore take $c=1$, $u=0$, $p=2$, $b_1=3$ and $a=2/3$, with residual $\beta =\theta -\eta =k\alpha \geq 0$. Its top integral is $k^ q$, since the normalized Fubini–Study form has top integral one and $\rho $ is a modification. Corollary 18.39.19, Corollary 18.39.9 therefore give

\[ W_ m=H^0(B,A^ m(-mE)),\qquad V_ m=H^0(B,A^ m(-(m-1)E)). \]

Let $t\geq 0$ be an integer. A section of $\rho ^*\mathcal O(km)\otimes \mathcal O_ B(tE)$, divided by $s_ E^ t$, gives a holomorphic section of $\mathcal O(km)$ on $\mathbb P^ q\setminus \{ p\} $, which extends across $p$ by Hartogs' theorem since $q\geq 2$. Pulling the extension back and multiplying by $s_ E^ t$ recovers the original section off $E$, hence everywhere by the identity theorem. Thus every section of $A^ m$ has the form $s_ E^ m\rho ^*s$, and the natural embeddings of $W_ m$ and $V_ m$ into $H^0(B,A^ m)$ both have this image. Its dimension is the number $\binom {km+q}{q}$ of monomials of degree $km$.

Finally, let $C\subset E\simeq \mathbb P^{q-1}$ be a projective line; it is contracted by $\rho $. For every smooth real function $u$, Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ C(k\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)=0$, whereas a strictly positive form has positive integral over $C$. Changing the smooth metric on the divisor bundle changes the residual only by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a smooth function, so this proves the obstruction. Moreover, $\varphi $ is finite on most of each fiber over $E$ and equals $-\infty $ at the point $o$ of that fiber, so it does not descend, even up to a bounded function. $\square$

Thus the selected and reference spaces agree, although the selecting potential does not descend and the reference has positive mass. The reason is that $E$ is a fixed component of every section of $A^ m$: all global sections vanish along $E$ to order $m$, more than the local integrability threshold requires. As before, the criterion presupposes the global bounds for the selecting potential and a residual with positive top integral; it does not produce them for arbitrary fiber singularities.

In example 18.39.20 the fixed divisor $E$ absorbs the local integrability conditions. If the coefficient of the selecting potential increases, the selected sections must eventually also vanish at the point that was blown up. The following family shows when this happens, and when the mass of the reference potential vanishes.

Keep the blowup $\rho :B\to \mathbb P^ q$ with $q\geq 2$, its exceptional divisor $E$, the extended projection $\tau $, the forms $\alpha ,\gamma ,\kappa $, and the quotient metric on $\mathcal O_ B(E)$ with curvature $\eta $ from that example, and write $x=\rho (E)$ for the point blown up. Let $k,e\geq 1$ be integers and equip

\[ A_ e=\rho ^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^ q}(k)\otimes \mathcal O_ B(eE), \qquad \theta =k\alpha +e\eta \]

with the product metric. Let $X=B\times \mathbb P^1$ with its projection $\pi $, let $o\in \mathbb P^1$, and let $R=\| \pi ^*s_ E\| ^2$ and $T=\| s_ o\| ^2$ as before. For positive integers $p,b$ and a real number $c>0$, put

\[ \varphi _ c=\frac cp\log (R^ p+T^ b),\quad \omega _ c=c\pi ^*\kappa +(cb/p+1)\omega _{\mathbb P^1},\quad a=\frac pb, \]

and let $V_ m(c)\subseteq H^0(B,A_ e^ m)$ be the space selected by $\mathcal I(m\varphi _ c)$, regarded on $B$ by descent.

Proposition 18.39.22.F3005ECDDA69 For every $c>0$ we have $\varphi _ c\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega _ c)$, and the selected space is

\[ \begin{gathered} V_ m(c)=s_ E^{em}\rho ^*H^0\bigl (\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(km)\otimes \mathfrak m_ x^{v_ m}\bigr ),\\ v_ m=\max \{ \lfloor m(c-e)-a\rfloor ,0\} ,\qquad m\geq 1, \end{gathered} \]

where $\mathfrak m_ x$ is the ideal sheaf of $x$. For $0<c\leq e+k$ put

\[ d=\max \{ c,e\} ,\quad \delta =d-e,\quad \psi _ d=d\log \| s_ E\| ^2,\quad \ell _ m=\lfloor m\delta \rfloor . \]

Then $\psi _ d\in \mathrm{PSH}(B,\theta )$ and

\[ \begin{gathered} \mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi _ d)=\bigl ((k-\delta )\alpha +\delta \gamma \bigr )^ q, \qquad \int _ B\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi _ d)=k^ q-\delta ^ q. \end{gathered} \]

The reference spaces satisfy

\[ \begin{split} W_ m(c)& :=H^0\bigl (B,A_ e^ m\otimes \mathcal I(m\psi _ d)\bigr )\\ & =s_ E^{em}\rho ^*H^0\bigl (\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(km)\otimes \mathfrak m_ x^{\ell _ m}\bigr ) \subseteq V_ m(c). \end{split} \]

If $0<c\leq e$, both spaces equal $H^0(B,A_ e^ m)$ for every $m$. If $e<c<e+k$, the defect and the leading term of the dimension are

\[ \begin{gathered} \dim V_ m(c)-\dim W_ m(c) =\binom {\ell _ m+q-1}{q}-\binom {v_ m+q-1}{q}=O(m^{q-1}),\\ \dim V_ m(c)=\frac{k^ q-(c-e)^ q}{q!}m^ q+O(m^{q-1}), \end{gathered} \]

where $\binom {q-1}{q}=0$. At $c=e+k$ the reference $\psi _ d$ has zero mass and

\[ \dim V_ m(e+k) =\frac{(\lceil a\rceil +1)k^{q-1}}{(q-1)!}m^{q-1}+O(m^{q-2}). \]

If $c>e+k$, the selected space is zero exactly when

\[ m(c-e-k)\geq a+1; \]

in particular, it is zero for every $m\geq \lceil (a+1)/(c-e-k)\rceil $.

Proof. As in example 18.39.20, the norm identity writes $R^ p+T^ b$ over the common denominator for $\mathcal O(p,b)$ on $\mathbb P^ q\times \mathbb P^1$, pulled back to $X$, and its numerator is a sum of squared moduli of holomorphic coefficients. The lower bound for the complex Hessian of the logarithm of such a sum, together with the extension theorem across the pole set, gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ c\geq -c\pi ^*\alpha -(cb/p)\omega _{\mathbb P^1}. \]

Thus $\omega _ c+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ c\geq c\pi ^*\gamma +\omega _{\mathbb P^1}\geq 0$.

Hartogs' theorem, as in that example, shows that every global section of $A_ e^ m$ can be written uniquely as $s_ E^{em}\rho ^*s$ with $s\in H^0(\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(km))$. If $s$ vanishes to order $j$ at $x$, this section vanishes to order $em+j$ generically along $E$. In a chart of the pole set, $\varphi _ c=(c/p)\log (|z|^{2p}+|w|^{2b})+O(1)$. The argument with a unit coefficient in the proof of theorem 18.39.8 and lemma 18.39.7 require

\[ em+j+1>mc-a, \quad \hbox{equivalently}\quad j\geq \max \{ \lfloor m(c-e)-a\rfloor ,0\} . \]

Conversely, this divisorial order gives integrability in every chart of the pole set, and $\varphi _ c$ is locally bounded elsewhere. The vanishing order of $s$ at $x$ equals the generic vanishing order of $\rho ^*s$ along $E$, as the blowup coordinates of example 18.39.17 show. This proves the formula for $V_ m(c)$.

For $0\leq \delta \leq k$, the Poincaré–Lelong formula gives

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ d=(k-\delta )\alpha +\delta \gamma +d[E]\geq 0. \]

The non-pluripolar measure does not charge $E$, and it equals the top power of the smooth residual $(k-\delta )\alpha +\delta \gamma $. The class of this residual is $k\rho ^*c_1(\mathcal O(1))-\delta [E]$. The mixed intersection numbers vanish and $\int _ B[E]^ q=(-1)^{q-1}$, as computed in example 18.39.17, so its mass is $k^ q-\delta ^ q$. The divisorial ideal is $\mathcal I(m\psi _ d)=\mathcal O_ B(-\lfloor md\rfloor E)$. Since $em$ is an integer, $\lfloor md\rfloor =em+\ell _ m$, and the condition on $s$ is exactly $j\geq \ell _ m$. This gives the formula for $W_ m(c)$, and the inclusion follows from $\ell _ m\geq v_ m$. If $c\leq e$, both point orders are zero.

For an integer $0\leq j\leq km+1$, homogeneous polynomials of degree $km$ realize every jet of total degree less than $j$ at $x$, and there are $\binom {j+q-1}{q}$ such jets. Hence

\[ h^0\bigl (\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(km)\otimes \mathfrak m_ x^ j\bigr ) =\binom {km+q}{q}-\binom {j+q-1}{q}. \]

For $e<c<e+k$ we have $0\leq v_ m\leq \ell _ m\leq km$ and $\ell _ m-v_ m\leq \lceil a\rceil $, and subtraction gives the exact defect. Each additional jet degree contributes $O(m^{q-1})$ monomials, which gives the bound, and $v_ m=m(c-e)+O(1)$ gives the leading term of $\dim V_ m(c)$.

At $c=e+k$ we have $v_ m=\max \{ km-\lceil a\rceil ,0\} $. For large $m$, the allowed monomials have vanishing orders $km-\lceil a\rceil ,\ldots ,km$ at $x$, and each of these $\lceil a\rceil +1$ degrees has $k^{q-1}m^{q-1}/(q-1)!+O(m^{q-2})$ monomials, which proves the asymptotic formula. The residual is then $k\gamma $, whose top power vanishes. For $c>e+k$, a nonzero polynomial of degree $km$ vanishes at $x$ to order at most $km$. Hence $V_ m(c)=0$ exactly when $v_ m>km$, which is equivalent to $m(c-e-k)-a\geq 1$. $\square$

For $0<c<e+k$ the reference $\psi _ d$ has positive mass and the defect is $O(m^{q-1})$, so this family admits the approximation asked for in 24.10.2. At $c=e+k$ the reference mass vanishes and the growth degree of the selected spaces drops to $q-1$; beyond this threshold the selected spaces eventually vanish.

18.39.23 A semipositive residual with nonzero defect5AF8FED16874

Blowing up two points instead of one, we obtain a residual that is semipositive, cannot be made strictly positive, and still has positive top integral. Unlike the example with one blown-up point, the gap between the two divisorial thresholds now produces a nonzero defect of the largest possible order $m^{q-1}$. Counting the spaces requires the following elementary interpolation of jets at several points.

Lemma 18.39.24.822490631827 Let $x_1,\ldots ,x_ N$ be distinct points of $\mathbb P^ q$, $q\geq 1$, and let $d,j_1,\ldots ,j_ N$ be nonnegative integers with $\sum _ i j_ i\leq d+1$. Then the map from $H^0(\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(d))$ to the jets of order less than $j_ i$ at the points $x_ i$ is surjective. In particular,

\[ h^0\left(\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(d)\otimes \bigcap _ i\mathfrak m_{x_ i}^{j_ i}\right) =\binom {d+q}{q}-\sum _ i\binom {j_ i+q-1}{q}, \]

where $\binom {q-1}{q}=0$.

Proof. We may discard the points with $j_ i=0$; if no point remains, the target is zero. Choose an affine chart containing all the remaining points, and use the corresponding trivialization of $\mathcal O(d)$. For each pair $i\ne k$ choose an affine linear function $L_{ik}$ vanishing at $x_ k$ and nonzero at $x_ i$, and set

\[ Q_ i=\prod _{k\ne i}L_{ik}^{j_ k}. \]

Given a jet $b_ i$ at $x_ i$, divide it by the invertible jet of $Q_ i$ at $x_ i$, and let $T_ i$ be the Taylor polynomial of degree at most $j_ i-1$ of the quotient. Then $Q_ iT_ i$ has the jet $b_ i$ at $x_ i$ and vanishes to order at least $j_ k$ at every other point $x_ k$, and its degree is at most $\sum _ kj_ k-1\leq d$. Hence $\sum _ iQ_ iT_ i$, homogenized to degree $d$, realizes all the prescribed jets. In $q$ variables there are $\binom {j_ i+q-1}{q}$ monomials of total degree less than $j_ i$, which gives the dimension formula. $\square$

Example 18.39.25.47ABA9EAB173 Let $q\geq 2$ and let $\rho :B\to \mathbb P^ q$ be the blowup of the two points $x_1=[1:0:\cdots :0]$ and $x_2=[0:1:0:\cdots :0]$, with exceptional divisors $E_1,E_2$. Let $\tau _ i:B\to \mathbb P^{q-1}$ be the extension to $B$ of the projection from $x_ i$. With the normalized Fubini–Study forms, put

\[ \alpha =\rho ^*\omega _{\mathbb P^ q},\qquad \gamma _ i=\tau _ i^*\omega _{\mathbb P^{q-1}},\qquad \kappa =\alpha +\gamma _1+\gamma _2>0. \]

Equip $\mathcal O_ B(E_ i)=\rho ^*\mathcal O(1)\otimes \tau _ i^*\mathcal O(-1)$ with the quotient metric, whose curvature is $\eta _ i=\alpha -\gamma _ i$, and let $s_ i$ be its canonical section. Equip

\[ A=\rho ^*\mathcal O(2)\otimes \mathcal O_ B(E_1+E_2), \qquad \theta =2\alpha +\eta _1+\eta _2 \]

with the product metric. On $X=B\times \mathbb P^1$, with projection $\pi $ and a point $o\in \mathbb P^1$, set

\[ \begin{gathered} R_ i=\| \pi ^*s_ i\| ^2,\qquad T=\| s_ o\| ^2,\\ \varphi =\log (R_1^2+T^3)+\log (R_2^2+T^3),\\ \omega =4\pi ^*\kappa +7\omega _{\mathbb P^1},\\ \psi =2\log \| s_1\| ^2+2\log \| s_2\| ^2, \qquad \beta =\gamma _1+\gamma _2. \end{gathered} \]

Then $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(B,\theta )$, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (\psi )=\beta ^ q,\qquad \int _ B\beta ^ q=2^ q-2>0. \]

No smooth change of gauge makes $\beta $ strictly positive. Nevertheless, writing $V_ m$ and $W_ m$ for the selected and reference spaces as before, their images in $H^0(B,A^ m)$ are, for every $m\geq 1$,

\[ \begin{aligned} V_ m& =s_1^ ms_2^ m\rho ^*H^0\left(\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(2m) \otimes \mathfrak m_{x_1}^{m-1}\mathfrak m_{x_2}^{m-1}\right),\\ W_ m& =s_1^ ms_2^ m\rho ^*H^0\left(\mathbb P^ q,\mathcal O(2m) \otimes \mathfrak m_{x_1}^{m}\mathfrak m_{x_2}^{m}\right). \end{aligned} \]

The defect and the common leading term of the dimensions are

\[ \begin{gathered} \dim V_ m-\dim W_ m=2\binom {m+q-2}{q-1} \sim \frac{2}{(q-1)!}m^{q-1},\\ \dim V_ m=\frac{2^ q-2}{q!}m^ q+O(m^{q-1}),\qquad \dim W_ m=\frac{2^ q-2}{q!}m^ q+O(m^{q-1}). \end{gathered} \]

Proof. The map $(\rho ,\tau _1,\tau _2)$ is an embedding: away from the exceptional divisors $\rho $ is already an embedding, and along $E_ i$ the map $\tau _ i$ separates the tangent directions of $E_ i$ while $\rho $ detects the normal direction. Hence $\kappa >0$. The identity for the quotient metric is the one-point identity of example 18.39.20, applied at each center. For each $i$, the calculation over a common denominator writes $R_ i^2+T^3$ as a sum of squared moduli of holomorphic coefficients over the metric denominator for $\mathcal O(2,3)$. Together with the extension theorem across the pole set, this gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -4\pi ^*\alpha -6\omega _{\mathbb P^1}. \]

It follows that $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq 4\pi ^*\beta +\omega _{\mathbb P^1}\geq 0$.

By the Poincaré–Lelong formula,

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\beta +2[E_1]+2[E_2]\geq 0. \]

The non-pluripolar product does not charge the divisors and equals $\beta ^ q$. The class of $\beta $ is $2H-[E_1]-[E_2]$, where $H=\rho ^*c_1(\mathcal O(1))$. As in example 18.39.17, $H^ q=1$, the mixed intersection numbers involving $H$ and some $[E_ i]$ vanish, and $[E_ i]^ q=(-1)^{q-1}$; moreover, the two exceptional divisors are disjoint. Expanding gives $\int _ B\beta ^ q=2^ q-2$.

Let $C$ be the strict transform of the line through $x_1$ and $x_2$. Both maps $\tau _ i$ are constant on $C$, so $\int _ C\beta =0$. By Stokes' theorem, $\int _ C(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)=0$ for every smooth real function $u$, whereas a strictly positive form has positive integral over $C$. This proves the obstruction, although the top integral is positive.

The selecting potential satisfies $\varphi \geq \pi ^*\psi $. Its pole set is the disjoint union of the sets $E_ i\times \{ o\} $. Near either of them the other summand is smooth, and

\[ \varphi =\log (|z|^4+|w|^6)+O(1),\qquad E_ i=\{ z=0\} ,\quad o=\{ w=0\} . \]

Thus $c_ i=p_ i=2$, $b_{i1}=3$ and $a_ i=2/3$. By theorem 18.39.13, corollary 18.39.19, $W_ m=H^0(B,A^ m(-2m(E_1+E_2)))$, and $V_ m$ is contained in $H^0(B,A^ m(-(2m-1)(E_1+E_2)))$; for an integer $\ell $, the strict local integrability condition $\ell +1>2m-2/3$ means $\ell \geq 2m-1$. Conversely, by lemma 18.39.7, divisibility by these orders gives integrability in every chart of the pole set, and off the pole set the selecting potential is locally bounded. This proves equality for $V_ m$.

Dividing a section of $A^ m$ by $s_1^ ms_2^ m$ gives a section of $\mathcal O(2m)$ away from the two centers, which extends across them by Hartogs' theorem since $q\geq 2$. The pullback of the extension, multiplied by $s_1^ ms_2^ m$, agrees with the original section everywhere, by the identity theorem. If the extended section vanishes to order $j_ i$ at $x_ i$, the corresponding section of $A^ m$ vanishes to order $m+j_ i$ generically along $E_ i$. The two divisorial conditions therefore give exactly the vanishing orders at $x_1$ and $x_2$ displayed in the statement. Since the two point ideals have disjoint supports, their product equals their intersection.

Apply lemma 18.39.24 with $d=2m$ and orders $(m,m)$ or $(m-1,m-1)$; in both cases their sum is at most $d+1$. This gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \dim W_ m& =\binom {2m+q}{q}-2\binom {m+q-1}{q},\\ \dim V_ m& =\binom {2m+q}{q}-2\binom {m+q-2}{q}. \end{aligned} \]

Pascal's identity gives the exact defect, and the leading terms give both growth laws. For $m=1$ the vanishing orders for $V_1$ are zero, and the defect is already two. For $q=2$ the dimensions are $\dim W_ m=(m+1)^2$ and $\dim V_ m=m^2+4m+1$. $\square$

In this example the residual $\beta $ has zero integral over the curve $C$, so no smooth change of gauge makes it positive, and yet its top integral is positive. Thus the bound $O(m^{q-1})$ of corollary 18.39.19 cannot be improved to $o(m^{q-1})$ for semipositive residuals either. As in the previous examples, the selecting potential is finite on most of each fiber over $E_ i$ and equals $-\infty $ at the point $o$ of that fiber, so it does not descend, even up to a bounded function.
