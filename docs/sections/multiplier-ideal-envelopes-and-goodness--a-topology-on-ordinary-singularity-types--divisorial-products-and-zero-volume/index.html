18.15 Divisorial products and zero volumeFCC95AC9CD7B

FCC95AC9CD7B

The simplest case of the fibration reduction is a product of a curve $C$ with a compact Kähler manifold $Z$, with the poles of $\Psi $ along $C\times D$ for a smooth divisor $D\subset Z$. If the divisor potential on $Z$ has supremum zero, the induced zero obstacle on $C$ is zero, so the envelopes of $\Psi +w\circ p$ are determined by those of $w$ on the curve. Projective products then give potentials without analytic ordinary type whose two envelopes agree, as well as distinct models with the same zero volumes. Since top-degree mass vanishes on all these models, volumes cannot distinguish them. Polar curvature on the curve can, and it gives a complete metric on the resulting class of product models.

Let $C$ be a compact connected curve with a smooth positive form $\eta $, and let $Z$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $k\geq 1$. Suppose that $D\subset Z$ is a nonempty smooth reduced divisor, $\kappa $ is a Kähler form on $Z$, and $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Z,\kappa )$ is smooth off $D$ and satisfies

\[ \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D],\qquad \sup _ Z\psi =0. \]

Thus, near $D$, $\psi $ is $\log |z_1|^2$ plus a smooth function, where $z_1$ is a local equation of $D$. On $M=C\times Z$, write $p:M\to C$ and $r:M\to Z$ for the projections, and put

\[ \Theta =p^*\eta +r^*\kappa ,\qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r,\qquad H=C\times D. \]

For $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(C,\eta )$ set $u=\Psi +w\circ p$.

On the curve $(C,\eta )$, we use the description of models given in section 13.28. Recall from subsection 5.27.13 that the polar curvature of $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(C,\eta )$ is the restriction of the curvature measure $\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q$ to the pole set $\{ q=-\infty \} $; it is a positive measure carried by a polar set, of mass at most $\int _ C\eta $. By lemma 13.28.1, every such measure is the polar curvature of exactly one ordinary model, and the ordinary envelope of any potential is the model with the same polar curvature. Ideal projection replaces the polar curvature by its atomic part, by lemma 20.3.20, proposition 13.28.11. Finally, the total variation of the difference of polar curvatures is a complete geodesic metric on ordinary models, which coincides with the upper-volume comparison $d_\eta $ of definition 13.22.1, by theorem 13.28.4, corollary 13.28.7.

Proposition 18.15.1.C083614DAC5B For $F\in C^\infty (M,\mathbb R)$, the function

\[ \widehat F(y)=\min _{z\in Z\setminus D}\{ F(y,z)-\psi (z)\} \qquad (y\in C) \]

is finite and continuous. The two envelopes satisfy

95879292B693 18.15.2
\begin{equation} \begin{split} P_{\Theta ,F}[u]& =\Psi +P_{\eta ,\widehat F}[w]\circ p,\\ P_{\Theta ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} & =\Psi +P_{\eta ,\widehat F}[w]_{\mathcal I}\circ p. \end{split} \end{equation}

Proof. Apply proposition 18.14.1 to $p:M\to C$, with $H=C\times D$, $\beta =\eta $ and $\Psi =\psi \circ r$. The local logarithmic form of $\psi $ defines a smooth metric on $\mathcal O_ M(H)$ with curvature $r^*\kappa $. Thus $\Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi =p^*\eta +[H]$, and $H$ is smooth and transverse to the fibers. The induced fiber minimum is the function $\widehat F$ above. For $F=0$, the normalization $\sup _ Z\psi =0$ gives $\widehat0=0$. $\square$

Example 18.15.3.87F63F4EB505 Take $C=\mathbb P^1$ and $Z=\mathbb P^ k$, $k\geq 1$, with their standard Fubini–Study forms, and let $D$ be a hyperplane. In this product setting there is a potential $u$ without an analytic ordinary type such that

\[ P_\Theta [u]=P_\Theta [u]_{\mathcal I}=\Psi , \qquad u\not\sim \Psi , \]

and the two envelopes of $u$ agree below every smooth obstacle. Their common model for the zero obstacle has zero singularity volume.

Proof. Use the unbounded curve potential $w$ constructed in the proof of proposition 18.10.1, whose two envelopes are zero. A logarithmic potential of a hyperplane on $Z$ gives $\psi $ with $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]$ and supremum zero. Set $u=\Psi +w\circ p$ and apply proposition 18.15.1, corollary 18.14.5. Fixing $z\notin D$ shows that $u-\Psi =w\circ p$ is unbounded, so $u\not\sim \Psi $. If a potential $a$ with analytic singularities were ordinarily equivalent to $u$, proposition 18.4.8 would give $u\sim a\sim P_\Theta [a]=P_\Theta [u]=\Psi $, a contradiction. $\square$

Vanishing of the volumes does not force the two envelopes to agree.

Example 18.15.4.6D425566CDE7 In the product setting, take $C=\mathbb P^1$ and $Z=\mathbb P^ k$, $k\geq 1$, with probability Fubini–Study forms and a hyperplane $D$. The positive closed background $\Theta $ admits normalized ordinary models $a\leq b$ with

\[ \begin{gathered} P_\Theta [a]=a,\qquad P_\Theta [a]_{\mathcal I}=P_\Theta [b]=P_\Theta [b]_{\mathcal I}=b,\\ a\sim _{\mathcal I}b,\qquad a\not\sim b,\\ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (a) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (a) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (b) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (b)=0. \end{gathered} \]

Thus equal singularity volumes of a type and of its ideal model do not imply equality of the envelopes at zero volume.

Proof. Use the normalized diffuse polar curve model $w$ of example 13.28.14 on $\mathbb P^1$ with its probability Fubini–Study form. It satisfies $P_\eta [w]=w$ and $P_\eta [w]_{\mathcal I}=0$, and it is unbounded. Take the normalized hyperplane potential on $\mathbb P^ k$ and put

\[ a=\Psi +w\circ p,\qquad b=\Psi . \]

The identities for the zero obstacle in proposition 18.15.1 give all four envelope identities. Both functions have supremum zero, and $w\leq 0$ gives $a\leq b$. Ideal projection preserves the ideal type by theorem 18.4.2, so $a\sim _{\mathcal I}b$. Restriction to $\mathbb P^1\times \{ z\} $, for any $z\notin D$, gives the unbounded difference $w$, so $a\not\sim b$. Finally, proposition 18.14.1 gives zero extended mass for every ordinary representative of either model, which proves all four volume identities. $\square$

The same failure occurs in a nonclosed background once a second curve factor is added.

Example 18.15.5.CB61780C4135 In every complex dimension $n\geq 3$, there is a projective manifold with the bounded mass property that admits a smooth positive nonclosed background $\Theta $ and normalized ordinary models $a\leq b$ such that

\[ \begin{gathered} P_\Theta [a]=a,\qquad P_\Theta [a]_{\mathcal I}=P_\Theta [b]=P_\Theta [b]_{\mathcal I}=b,\\ a\sim _{\mathcal I}b,\qquad a\not\sim b,\\ \underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (a) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (a) =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (b) =\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\Theta (b)=0. \end{gathered} \]

In particular, equality of the upper volumes of a type and of its ideal model does not force equality of the envelopes when the ideal model has zero lower volume.

Proof. Let $Y=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, with projections $\pi _1,\pi _2$, and let $\sigma $ be the probability Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^1$. Choose a positive nonconstant smooth function $f$ on the first factor and put

\[ \beta =\pi _1^*\sigma +(f\circ \pi _1)\pi _2^*\sigma . \]

This form is positive, and $\mathrm d\beta =\pi _1^*(\mathrm df)\wedge \pi _2^*\sigma \ne 0$. Let $w_0$ be the normalized diffuse polar model of example 13.28.14, and put $w=w_0\circ \pi _1$. Then $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\beta )$ and

\[ P_\beta [w]=w,\qquad P_\beta [w]_{\mathcal I}=0. \]

Indeed, replace an ordinary competitor $v\leq 0$ by $\max (v,w)$. On every horizontal section $y\mapsto (y,z)$, where $\beta $ restricts to $\sigma $, this maximum is a $\sigma $-plurisubharmonic function, not identically $-\infty $ since it dominates $w_0$, and it is at least as singular as $w_0$. Its restriction is therefore at most $P_\sigma [w_0]=w_0$. This proves $v\leq w$ on $Y$; the reverse inequality for the envelope holds because $w$ is itself a competitor. All multiplier ideals of $w_0$ are trivial, so lemma 5.37.7 shows that the same holds for $w$. Since $\beta $ is positive, zero is a competitor for the ideal envelope of $w$, which proves the second identity.

Set $k=n-2$ and $M=Y\times \mathbb P^ k$, with projections $p,r$. On the second factor take the probability Fubini–Study form $\kappa $, a hyperplane $D$, and its normalized potential $\psi $ with $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]$. Put

\[ \Theta =p^*\beta +r^*\kappa ,\qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r,\qquad a=\Psi +w\circ p,\qquad b=\Psi . \]

The form $\Theta $ is positive and not closed. A sufficiently large multiple of the closed positive product form dominates $\Theta $, so the bounded mass property follows from Stokes' theorem for that closed form and the metric independence in proposition 13.14.15.

Apply proposition 18.14.1 to $p:M\to Y$ and the divisor $Y\times D$. The induced zero obstacle is zero because $\sup \psi =0$. The two identities on the base give all four identities upstairs, and both functions have supremum zero. Ideal projection preserves the ideal type, so $a\sim _{\mathcal I}b$. Moreover $a\leq b$, while their difference restricts to the unbounded function $w$ on any section outside the divisor, so $a\not\sim b$. Since $\dim Y=2<n$, the same proposition gives zero extended mass for every ordinary representative of $a$ or $b$, which proves the volume assertions. $\square$

We return to the product $M=C\times Z$ and its fixed divisor potential $\Psi $. All the models $\Psi +q\circ p$ have zero top-degree volumes, but the polar curvature of $q$ on $C$ still distinguishes them.

Definition 18.15.6.F217B40D9074 For a normalized ordinary model $q$ in background $\eta $ on $C$, put

\[ \Phi (q)=\Psi +q\circ p,\qquad \mathcal S_\Psi =\{ \Phi (q):P_\eta [q]=q\} . \]

Write $\mu _ q=\mathbf1_{\{ q=-\infty \} }(\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)$ for the polar curvature of $q$, and define

7DFF38FB6056 18.15.7
\begin{equation} \rho _\Psi (\Phi (q_1),\Phi (q_2)) =\| \mu _{q_1}-\mu _{q_2}\| _{\mathrm{TV}}. \end{equation}

Proposition 18.15.8.6DEB26AFE67E The set $\mathcal S_\Psi $ consists of normalized ordinary models in background $\Theta $, and $(\mathcal S_\Psi ,\rho _\Psi )$ is a complete geodesic metric space. The upper-volume comparison $d_\Theta $ of definition 13.22.1, which is formed from the upper singularity volumes of $a$, $b$ and $\max (a,b)$, vanishes identically on this set:

\[ d_\Theta (a,b)=0\qquad (a,b\in \mathcal S_\Psi ). \]

The ideal models in $\mathcal S_\Psi $ form a closed geodesically convex subspace, equal to the $\rho _\Psi $-closure of the models with analytic singularities. Ideal projection is a $1$-Lipschitz retraction onto this subspace and selects the unique nearest ideal model. For $a=\Phi (q)$, the distance from $a$ to this subspace is the mass of the nonatomic part of $\mu _ q$. If $\rho _\Psi (a_ j,a)\to 0$, then $a_ j\to a$ in $L^1(M)$ for every smooth positive volume form.

Proof. The identities for the zero obstacle in proposition 18.15.1 give

\[ P_\Theta [\Phi (q)]=\Phi (q),\qquad P_\Theta [\Phi (q)]_{\mathcal I} =\Phi (P_\eta [q]_{\mathcal I}). \]

Both summands of $\Phi (q)$ have supremum zero on their respective factors, so $\Phi (q)$ is normalized. Restriction to any section outside $D$ shows that $\Phi $ is injective. The classification in lemma 13.28.1 and the distance formula in theorem 13.28.4 therefore make $\Phi $ an isometry from the space of curve models onto $\mathcal S_\Psi $. Completeness and the existence of geodesics follow from corollary 13.28.7.

By proposition 18.14.1, every ordinary representative of $\Phi (q)$ has zero extended mass. Moreover,

\[ \max (\Phi (q_1),\Phi (q_2)) =\Psi +\max (q_1,q_2)\circ p, \]

and the same proposition, applied to the curve potential $\max (q_1,q_2)$, gives zero upper volume for the maximum. Thus all three upper volumes in the definition of $d_\Theta $ vanish.

Under $\Phi $, ideal projection corresponds to taking the atomic part of $\mu _ q$. The assertions on closedness, convexity and nearest points therefore follow from corollary 13.28.7, proposition 13.28.11. To prove analytic closure upstairs, approximate an atomic measure by finite atomic measures with rational coefficients and mass less than $\int _ C\eta $. By lemma 20.3.20, their curve models have the corresponding logarithmic poles up to bounded remainders. Adding $\Psi $ preserves an analytic presentation: in the local product monomial, clear the denominators of the rational pole coefficients together with the unit coefficient of $D$. The resulting models in $\mathcal S_\Psi $ converge for $\rho _\Psi $. Conversely, by proposition 18.4.8, every ordinary model with analytic singularities in this locus is equal to its ideal projection, so the closure of these models lies in the closed ideal subspace.

Finally, if $\rho _\Psi (\Phi (q_ j),\Phi (q))\to 0$, then theorem 13.28.8 gives $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(C)$. Off the pole sets, which have smooth volume zero, the difference of the two product potentials is $(q_ j-q)\circ p$. For a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm dV$ on $M$, integration along the compact fibers makes $p_*\mathrm dV$ a smooth positive density on $C$, and Fubini's theorem gives

\[ \int _ M|\Phi (q_ j)-\Phi (q)|\, \mathrm dV \leq C_{\mathrm dV}\| q_ j-q\| _{L^1(C,\eta )}\longrightarrow 0, \]

where $C_{\mathrm dV}$ bounds the density of $p_*\mathrm dV$ with respect to $\eta $. $\square$

The same complete metric exists on the corresponding product locus in the nonclosed background of example 18.15.5.

Corollary 18.15.9.DFCE5415EAB5 Let $M=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^ k$, $k\geq 1$, with projections $h,s,r$. Let $\sigma $ and $\kappa $ be the probability Fubini–Study forms on $\mathbb P^1$ and $\mathbb P^ k$, respectively, and let $f$ be a positive nonconstant smooth real function on the first factor. Set

\[ \Theta =h^*\sigma +(f\circ h)s^*\sigma +r^*\kappa , \qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r, \]

where $\psi $ is the normalized hyperplane potential with $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]$. For every normalized ordinary model $q$ on $(\mathbb P^1,\sigma )$, put $\Phi _ f(q)=\Psi +q\circ h$. Then

\[ P_\Theta [\Phi _ f(q)]=\Phi _ f(q),\qquad P_\Theta [\Phi _ f(q)]_{\mathcal I} =\Phi _ f(P_\sigma [q]_{\mathcal I}). \]

On $\mathcal T_\Psi =\{ \Phi _ f(q):P_\sigma [q]=q\} $, define

\[ \begin{gathered} \rho _{\Psi ,f}(\Phi _ f(q_1),\Phi _ f(q_2)) =\| \mu _{q_1}-\mu _{q_2}\| _{\mathrm{TV}},\\ \mu _ q=\mathbf1_{\{ q=-\infty \} }(\sigma +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q). \end{gathered} \]

All the conclusions of proposition 18.15.8 hold on this locus: completeness and geodesics, vanishing of the upper-volume comparison, a closed ideal subspace in which analytic models are dense, ideal projection as the nearest-point retraction, and $L^1$ convergence upstairs.

Proof. On $Y=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, write $\pi _1,\pi _2$ for the projections and put $\beta =\pi _1^*\sigma +(f\circ \pi _1)\pi _2^*\sigma $. The restriction argument of example 18.15.5 applies to every normalized ordinary curve model and gives

\[ P_\beta [q\circ \pi _1]=q\circ \pi _1. \]

Indeed, the maximum of an ordinary competitor with $q\circ \pi _1$ restricts on every horizontal section to a competitor for $P_\sigma [q]=q$ that is $\sigma $-plurisubharmonic and not identically $-\infty $.

Put $q_{\mathcal I}=P_\sigma [q]_{\mathcal I}$ and let $E$ be the atomic part of $\mu _ q$. The Green potential $w_ E$ of lemma 20.3.20 satisfies $q_{\mathcal I}\sim w_ E$ and $q\sim _{\mathcal I}w_ E$. Its pullback satisfies

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w_ E\circ \pi _1) =\sum _ xE(\{ x\} )[\{ x\} \times \mathbb P^1] -E(\mathbb P^1)\pi _1^*\sigma . \]

The divisor current is closed and has finite mass, and $w_ E\circ \pi _1$ is $\beta $-plurisubharmonic because $E(\mathbb P^1)\leq 1$. By corollary 18.4.9, its ordinary and ideal envelopes are equal. Multiplier ideals pull back by lemma 5.37.7, so ordinary equivalence and the ordinary identity just proved give

\[ P_\beta [q\circ \pi _1]_{\mathcal I} =P_\beta [w_ E\circ \pi _1]_{\mathcal I} =P_\beta [w_ E\circ \pi _1] =q_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi _1. \]

This includes the cases $E=0$ and $E$ with countably many atoms.

Apply proposition 18.14.1 to $M\to Y$ and the divisor $Y\times D$, whose induced zero obstacle is zero. The two identities on the base give the asserted identities upstairs. Thus $\Phi _ f$ identifies the locus isometrically with the whole space of curve models, and ideal projection again takes the atomic part. The metric, nearest-point and analytic-closure arguments of proposition 18.15.8 apply unchanged; rational atomic coefficients and the unit coefficient of the hyperplane give local analytic monomials. Each pairwise maximum still has the form $\Psi +\max (q_1,q_2)\circ h$, so the fibration proposition makes all three upper volumes zero. Finally, integration of a smooth volume form along the compact fibers of $h$ transfers $L^1$ convergence on the curve to $M$, as in that proposition. The background is positive and not closed, and, as in example 18.15.5, a dominating closed product form gives the bounded mass property. $\square$

The locus and its metric persist when the coefficient $f$ of the second curve factor is allowed to vanish.

Corollary 18.15.10.13B4DE048053 Keep $M,h,s,r,\sigma ,\kappa ,D,\Psi $ from corollary 18.15.9. For any smooth real function $f\geq 0$ on the first curve, put

\[ \Theta _ f=h^*\sigma +(f\circ h)s^*\sigma +r^*\kappa , \qquad \Phi (q)=\Psi +q\circ h. \]

For every normalized ordinary curve model $q$, one has

\[ P_{\Theta _ f}[\Phi (q)]=\Phi (q),\qquad P_{\Theta _ f}[\Phi (q)]_{\mathcal I} =\Phi (P_\sigma [q]_{\mathcal I}). \]

All the metric conclusions of corollary 18.15.9 hold on the same set $\mathcal T_\Psi $, with the same distance given by total variation of polar curvature. The identity map of this set is an isometry between any two of the backgrounds $\Theta _ f$ and commutes with ideal projection. This includes uniformly convergent families $f_ j\to 0$, whose limiting background $\Theta _0$ is not big.

Proof. On $Y=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$, put $\beta _ f=\pi _1^*\sigma +(f\circ \pi _1)\pi _2^*\sigma $. This form is semipositive, and the argument with horizontal sections in corollary 18.15.9 still gives

\[ P_{\beta _ f}[q\circ \pi _1]=q\circ \pi _1. \]

Indeed, the pullback of $q$ is $\beta _ f$-plurisubharmonic, and every horizontal section has background $\sigma $, also where $f$ vanishes.

Write $q_{\mathcal I}=P_\sigma [q]_{\mathcal I}$, let $E$ be the atomic part of $\mu _ q$, and set $g=w_ E\circ \pi _1$, with the Green potential of the preceding proof. Then $g$ is $\beta _ f$-plurisubharmonic, $q\circ \pi _1\sim _{\mathcal I}g$, and $g\sim q_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi _1$. Its complex Hessian is the same countable divisor current of finite mass minus $E(\mathbb P^1)\pi _1^*\sigma $. For every ideal competitor $v\leq 0$ for $q\circ \pi _1$, lemma 5.38.5 gives $v\preceq g$. Therefore

\[ v\leq P_{\beta _ f}[g] =P_{\beta _ f}[q_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi _1] =q_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi _1. \]

The last equality uses the ordinary identity just proved, since $q_{\mathcal I}$ is an ordinary curve model. Conversely, $q_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi _1$ is a normalized $\beta _ f$-plurisubharmonic ideal competitor, because ideal projection preserves the ideal type on the curve and multiplier ideals pull back. This proves the ideal identity on the base, including the cases $E=0$ and $E$ with countably many atoms.

Proposition 18.14.1 allows this semipositive background on the base and gives both identities upstairs. Normalization follows because the factors are independent. The metric space is the same isometric copy of the whole space of curve models, and ideal projection, analytic closure, geodesics and $L^1$ convergence upstairs follow as in the preceding proof. Since the relevant currents are still pulled back from the surface $Y$, the same proposition gives zero upper volumes, so the upper-volume comparison vanishes. Both $\Phi (q)$ and its polar measure are independent of $f$, so the stated identifications and the compatibility with ideal projection hold exactly, including at the limiting background. Analytic singularities depend only on the functions, so their closure is also unchanged.

To see that $\Theta _0$ is not big, let $n=k+2$, and take the smooth closed positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form and the Hermitian form

\[ T=h^*\sigma \wedge r^*(\kappa ^ k),\qquad \Omega =h^*\sigma +s^*\sigma +r^*\kappa . \]

One has $\int _ M\Theta _0\wedge T=0$ and $\int _ M\Omega \wedge T=1$. If $\Theta _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \varepsilon \Omega $ for some $u\in \mathrm{QPSH}(M)$ and $\varepsilon >0$, pairing with $T$ and using Stokes' theorem would give $0\geq \varepsilon $. Thus $\Theta _0$ is not big, although the model locus keeps its complete metric. $\square$
