15.8 Contact support in a big backgroundACC753805E21

ACC753805E21

Throughout this section, $\theta $ is an arbitrary smooth big background, not necessarily semipositive. For a continuous obstacle, regularization shows that the envelope is continuous off the poles of a strict analytic barrier. On a small ball where the envelope stays below the obstacle, a homogeneous replacement then shows that the envelope already solves the homogeneous equation, so its Monge–Ampère measure vanishes away from the contact set.

By theorem 5.36.5, applied to a strict barrier with a sufficiently small curvature error, we may fix $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with analytic singularities and a smooth remainder after resolution such that

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \kappa \omega ,\qquad \kappa >0. \]

Its pole set $Z=\{ \rho =-\infty \} $ is an analytic, hence pluripolar, subset of $X$.

Theorem 15.8.1.F6FD255985FF Let $h\in C(X)$. Then $p=P_\theta (h)$ is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function, continuous on $X\setminus Z$, whose Monge–Ampère measure satisfies

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ p<h\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)=0. \]

Here $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$ is allowed to have infinite total mass.

Proof. Subtract a constant from $\rho $ so that $\rho +2\leq h$. Then $\rho +2$ is a competitor, and since the obstacle is continuous, the envelope lies below it everywhere; thus

\[ \rho +2\leq p\leq h. \]

In particular, $p$ is locally bounded off $Z$. We first prove that $p$ is continuous there; this part does not use the contact assertion.

Apply lemma 5.42.10 to $p-\sup _ Xp$ and add back the constant. The transforms $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ of that lemma are defined on $X$ and, with fixed constants $A,K,C>0$ and $\epsilon (\delta )=C\delta $, satisfy

EE69BB255211 15.8.2
\begin{equation} \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _{c,\delta }\geq -(Ac+\epsilon (\delta ))\omega . \end{equation}

Off $Z$ the infimum defining $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ is attained at a positive radius, and

\[ p-K(\delta ^2+\delta )\leq \Phi _{c,\delta } \leq R_\delta p\leq R_\delta h. \]

For small $\delta $ the first inequality gives $\Phi _{c,\delta }\geq \rho +1$, hence $\rho \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }$ on $X$.

Set

\[ m(\delta )=\sup _{0<t\leq \delta }\| R_ th-h\| _\infty , \qquad c=m(\delta )+\epsilon (\delta )+\delta , \qquad s=\frac{Ac+\epsilon (\delta )}{\kappa }. \]

The averaging kernels are positive, have integral one and are supported in balls of radius comparable to $t$, so uniform continuity of $h$ gives $m(\delta )\to 0$. Define

\[ v_\delta =\frac{\Phi _{c,\delta }+s\rho }{1+s}. \]

By the choice of $s$, the strict barrier and (15.8.2) show that $v_\delta $ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. Since $\rho \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }$,

\[ v_\delta \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }\leq R_\delta h\leq h+m(\delta ). \]

Thus $v_\delta -m(\delta )$ is a competitor for the envelope, and $v_\delta \leq p+m(\delta )$.

Let $L\Subset X\setminus Z$ be compact. On $L$ the difference $p-\rho $ is bounded above. Since $s\leq Cc$, the identity

\[ \Phi _{c,\delta }-p=(1+s)(v_\delta -p)+s(p-\rho ) \]

therefore gives $\Phi _{c,\delta }-p\leq C_ Lc$ on $L$. At a radius $t$ where the infimum is attained,

\[ -c\log (t/\delta ) \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }-p+K(\delta ^2+\delta )\leq C_ Lc. \]

Since $c\geq \delta $, this yields $t\geq a_ L\delta $ with a fixed $a_ L>0$. Because $R_ tp+Kt^2$ is increasing in $t$, it follows that

\[ p-K(a_ L\delta )^2\leq R_{a_ L\delta }p \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }+K\delta ^2+K\delta . \]

Consequently $R_{a_ L\delta }p\to p$ uniformly on $L$. These averages are smooth for positive radius, even though $p$ is unbounded on $X$. Applying this to compact neighborhoods of the points of $X\setminus Z$ proves that $p$ is continuous on $X\setminus Z$.

We turn to the contact assertion. By the continuity just proved, the set $\{ p<h\} \setminus Z$ is open. Let $x$ be one of its points, and choose a small coordinate ball $B_ r$ centered at $x$ whose closure lies in this set. Let $H$ be the homogeneous replacement of theorem 15.7.2 with boundary values $p|_{\partial B_ r}$. Its domination property gives $H\geq p$ on the ball.

We claim that $H\leq h$ on $B_ r$ if $r$ is small enough. Indeed, in the chosen Euclidean coordinates $z$, with Laplacian $\Delta $, the inequality $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H\geq 0$ implies a fixed bound $\Delta H\geq -C$. Comparison with the function $\sup _{\partial B_ r}p+C'(r^2-|z-x|^2)$, with $C'$ fixed large enough, gives

\[ H\leq \sup _{\partial B_ r}p+C'r^2. \]

As $r\to 0$, the right-hand side tends to $p(x)$, whereas $\inf _{B_ r}h\to h(x)>p(x)$. This proves the claim for sufficiently small $r$.

Now replace $p$ by $H$ on the ball. The resulting function is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. To see this, fix $a>0$ and take $\max \{ p,H-a\} $ inside the ball and $p$ outside. Since $H$ and $p$ are continuous with equal boundary values, $H-a<p$ on a collar of $\partial B_ r$, so this is an ordinary local maximum construction. Letting $a\downarrow 0$ gives the function equal to $H$ on $B_ r$ and to $p$ outside, with locally uniform convergence near the boundary of the ball. This function is bounded above by $h$ on all of $X$, so the definition of the envelope forces it to be at most $p$. Thus $p=H$ on $B_ r$, and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$ vanishes there.

These balls cover $\{ p<h\} \setminus Z$. There the extended measure agrees with the local bounded product, and it does not charge the pluripolar set $Z$. This proves the contact assertion on $X$; no bound on the total mass of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$ was needed. $\square$

15.8.3 Quasi-continuous obstacles5E57580F8E39

We now extend the contact property to discontinuous obstacles. Continuous approximations of an obstacle need not preserve every competitor of its envelope pointwise. For lower semicontinuous obstacles, the approximation of lemma 5.40.5 repairs this, and quasi-continuity then allows us to pass to limits of weighted measures. An unbounded obstacle is truncated at finite levels; in an arbitrary smooth background this produces signed products, whose limits are controlled by lemma 5.29.17 before we restrict to the region where the product is positive. The strict analytic barrier $\rho $ and its pole set $Z$ are those fixed above.

Proposition 15.8.4.29A74E6E8A18 If $f$ is bounded and lower semicontinuous, then $p=P_\theta (f)$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus Z$, and

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ p<f\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)=0. \]

Proof. Using the infimum construction in the proof of proposition 16.28.3, take continuous $f_ j\uparrow f$ with common bounds $m\leq f_ j\leq M$, and shift $\rho $ below $m$. The envelopes $p_ j=P_\theta (f_ j)$ increase and satisfy

\[ \rho \leq p_ j\leq M. \]

Their regularized limit $q=(\lim _ jp_ j)^*$ is therefore $\theta $-plurisubharmonic. Outside a pluripolar set it equals the pointwise limit, which is at most $f$; hence $q\leq p=P_\theta (f)$.

We prove the reverse inequality without using a domination theorem. Let $u$ be any competitor for $P_\theta (f)$, and let $u_ t\leq f$ be the potentials given by lemma 5.40.5, which satisfy this inequality everywhere. For fixed $t,\eta >0$, the sets

\[ \{ u_ t<f_ j+\eta \} ,\qquad j\geq 1, \]

are open, increase with $j$, and cover $X$. By compactness, $u_ t\leq f_ j+\eta $ everywhere for all large $j$. Thus $u_ t-\eta $ is a competitor for $p_ j$, and $q\geq u_ t-\eta $. Letting first $\eta \downarrow 0$ and then $t\downarrow 0$, we obtain $q\geq u$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere by lemma 5.24.3. Taking the supremum over all competitors $u$ gives $q\geq p$, so $q=p$.

For fixed $k$, the set $U_ k=\{ p<f_ k\} $ is open. If $j\geq k$, then $p_ j\leq p<f_ k\leq f_ j$ on $U_ k$, so theorem 15.8.1 gives $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p_ j)=0$ there. On every relatively compact open subset of $X\setminus Z$ the functions $p_ j$ have common bounds. After a common smooth correction and a finite expansion, the local Bedford–Taylor continuity along increasing sequences shows that their measures converge to $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$ there. Since the measure of an open set is at most the lower limit of the measures of the approximating sequence, $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$ vanishes on $U_ k\setminus Z$. The union of these sets is $\{ p<f\} \setminus Z$, and the extended measure does not charge the pluripolar set $Z$. $\square$

Proposition 15.8.5.BDE06B833BC8 The conclusion of proposition 15.8.4 holds for every bounded quasi-continuous obstacle $f$.

Proof. By lemma 5.29.15, there are bounded lower semicontinuous $f_ j$ with common bounds and $f_ j\downarrow f$ quasi-everywhere. The envelopes $p_ j=P_\theta (f_ j)$ decrease to $p=P_\theta (f)$. Indeed, the common lower barrier shows that the limit is not identically $-\infty $; the limit lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere and is thus a competitor for $P_\theta (f)$, while monotonicity gives the other inequality. All these functions have common local bounds off $Z$.

For fixed $k$, put $H_ k=\min \{ 1,(f-p_ k)_+\} $ on $X\setminus Z$, a bounded quasi-continuous function there. If $j\geq k$, then on $\{ H_ k>0\} $ we have $p_ j\leq p_ k<f\leq f_ j$ outside a pluripolar set, so proposition 15.8.4 gives $H_ k\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p_ j)=0$ off $Z$.

Let $g$ be continuous with compact support in $X\setminus Z$. The continuity of bounded products against quasi-continuous weights from section 5.27 applies to the fixed quasi-continuous coefficient $gH_ k$. Consequently $H_ k\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)=0$ off $Z$. As $k\to \infty $, the sets $\{ H_ k>0\} $ exhaust $\{ p<f\} \setminus Z$, and $Z$ is not charged because it is pluripolar. $\square$

For an unbounded obstacle, we truncate and use the convergence of signed products in lemma 5.29.17.

Theorem 15.8.6.6F087EC35E2E Let $\theta $ be smooth and big. Let $f$ be a quasi-continuous obstacle, finite almost everywhere, such that $p=P_\theta (f)\not\equiv -\infty $. Then $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ p<f\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)=0. \]

As before, the measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$ may have infinite total mass.

Proof. As in the proof of theorem 16.28.5, the local envelope theorem and a countable Choquet family give $p\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. Set $f_ j=\max (f,-j)$ and $p_ j=P_\theta (f_ j)$. Then $p$ is a competitor for every $f_ j$. Since $f_ j\leq f_1$ and $f_1$ is finite almost everywhere, the finite-sublevel argument in section 5.40 gives a common upper bound for all competitors. Thus the envelopes $p_ j$ are $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and uniformly bounded above. Choose $M$ strictly larger than this bound.

The obstacle $g_ j=\min (f_ j,M)$ is bounded and quasi-continuous. Since $p_ j<M$ and $p_ j\leq f_ j$ quasi-everywhere, $p_ j$ is a competitor for $P_\theta (g_ j)$; the reverse inequality follows from $g_ j\leq f_ j$. Thus $p_ j=P_\theta (g_ j)$, and proposition 15.8.5 gives contact support for $p_ j$ with respect to $g_ j$. Because $p_ j<M$, no contact occurs at the artificial upper cutoff, so

5ACCF67CCE26 15.8.7
\begin{equation} \mathbf1_{\{ p_ j<f_ j\} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p_ j)=0. \end{equation}

Each $p_ j$ is locally bounded off $Z$, with a lower barrier of the form $\rho -C_ j$. The sequence decreases to $p$: its limit is not identically $-\infty $ because it dominates $p$, and it lies below $f$ quasi-everywhere, so it is itself a competitor for $P_\theta (f)$.

Fix a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta $. For integers $C,k\geq 1$, consider the bounded $\Omega $-plurisubharmonic functions

\[ v_{j,C}=\max (p_ j,-C),\qquad v_ C=\max (p,-C), \]

and set

\[ H_{k,C} =\min \{ 1,(f-p_ k)_+\} \min \{ 1,(p+C)_+\} \quad \hbox{on }X\setminus Z, \]

extended by zero on $Z$. This function is bounded and quasi-continuous. Indeed, off $Z$ the function $p_ k$ is a finite quasi-plurisubharmonic function, truncation by bounded continuous functions preserves quasi-continuity, and the pluripolar set $Z$ can be removed by an open set of arbitrarily small capacity. At a pole of $p$ the second factor vanishes.

For $j\geq k$, on $\{ H_{k,C}>0\} $ we have, outside pluripolar sets,

\[ p_ j\geq p>-C,\qquad p_ j\leq p_ k<f\leq f_ j. \]

By plurifine locality, the signed measure $\nu _{j,C}=(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{j,C})^ n$ therefore agrees there with $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p_ j)$, and (15.8.7) gives

\[ H_{k,C}\nu _{j,C}=0. \]

For fixed $C$, the potentials $v_{j,C}$ have common global bounds and decrease to $v_ C$, so lemma 5.29.17 yields

\[ \int _ X H_{k,C}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ C)^ n=0. \]

On the set where $H_{k,C}$ is positive we have $p>-C$, so the restricted product in theorem 13.14.3 identifies $H_{k,C}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ C)^ n$ with the positive measure $H_{k,C}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$; on the complement the coefficient is zero. Hence $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (p)$ vanishes on $\{ H_{k,C}>0\} $.

Finally, if $p(x)$ is finite and $p(x)<f(x)$, then $p_ k(x)<f(x)$ for large $k$ and $p(x)>-C$ for large $C$. The countable union of the sets $\{ H_{k,C}>0\} $ therefore covers $\{ p<f\} $ outside $Z\cup \{ p=-\infty \} $. Both excluded sets are pluripolar, and the theorem follows. $\square$
