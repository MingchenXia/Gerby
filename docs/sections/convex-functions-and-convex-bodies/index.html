18.2 Convex functions and convex bodies

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

A Convex functions and convex bodies

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

This appendix collects the basic facts about convex functions and convex bodies that are used throughout the book. Our standard reference is [Roc70]. All results stated for convex functions apply equally to concave functions, after replacing the function by its negative.

A.1 The notion of convex functions

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $N$ be a real vector space of finite dimension.

Definition A.1.1.Let $F\colon N\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ be a function. The epigraph of $F$ is defined as the following set

\[ \operatorname{epi}F\coloneqq \left\{ (n,r)\in N\times \mathbb {R}: r\geq F(n) \right\} . \]

Definition A.1.2. A convex function on $N$ is a function $F\colon N\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ such that the epigraph $\operatorname{epi}F$ is a convex subset of $N\times \mathbb {R}$.

The effective domain of $F$ is the set

\[ \operatorname{Dom}F\coloneqq \left\{ n\in N : F(n)<\infty \right\} . \]

A convex function $F$ on $N$ such that $\operatorname{Dom}F\neq \varnothing $ and $F(n)\neq -\infty $ for all $n\in N$ is said to be proper.

The set of convex functions on $N$ is denoted by $\mathrm{Conv}(N)$. The subset of proper convex functions is denoted by $\mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$.

The following characterization of convex functions is well-known.

Lemma A.1.3. Let $F\colon N\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$. Then $F$ is convex if and only if the following condition holds: suppose that $n,r\in N$ and $a,b\in \mathbb {R}$ such that $a>F(n)$, $b>F(r)$, then for any $t\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ F(tn+(1-t)r)<ta+(1-t)b. \]
See [Theorem 4.2, Roc70] for the proof.

Example A.1.4. Let $A\subseteq N$ be a convex subset. Then the characteristic function $\chi _ A\colon N\rightarrow \{ 0,\infty \} $ of $A$ is defined by

\[ \chi _ A(n)\coloneqq \left\{ \begin{aligned} 0,& \quad n\in A;\\ \infty ,& \quad n\not\in A. \end{aligned} \right. \]

The function $\chi _ A$ lies in $\mathrm{Conv}(N)$.

Example A.1.5. Let $M$ be the dual vector space of $N$ and $P\subseteq M$ be a convex subset. The support function $\operatorname{Supp}_ P\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ of $P$ is defined as follows:

\[ \operatorname{Supp}_ P(n)\coloneqq \sup \{ \langle m,n \rangle : m\in P \} . \]

It is well-known that convexity is preserved by a number of natural operations. We recall a few to fix the notation.

Definition A.1.6. Let $F_1,\ldots ,F_ m\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$ ($m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$). We define their infimal convolution $F_1 \square \cdots \square F_ m\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ as follows:

\[ F_1 \square \cdots \square F_ m(n)\coloneqq \inf \left\{ \sum _{i=1}^ m F_ i(n_ i):n_ i\in N, \sum _{i=1}^ m n_ i=n \right\} . \]
The fact $F_1 \square \cdots \square F_ m\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ is proved in [Theorem 5.4, Roc70]. One should note that $F_1 \square \cdots \square F_ m$ is not always proper.

Proposition A.1.7. Let $\{ F_ i\} _{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{Conv}(N)$. Then $\sup _{i\in I} F_ i\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$.

This follows from [Theorem 5.5, Roc70]. In particular, this allows us to introduce

Definition A.1.8. Let $f\colon N\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$. The lower convex envelope of $f$ is defined as

\[ \operatorname{CE}f\coloneqq \sup \{ F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N): F\leq f \} . \]

It follows from proposition A.1.7 that $\operatorname{CE}f\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$.

Definition A.1.9. Given a non-empty family $\{ F_ i\} _{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{Conv}(N)$, we define

\[ \bigwedge _{i\in I}F_ i\coloneqq \operatorname{CE}\left(\inf _{i\in I} F_ i\right). \]

When the family $I$ is finite, say $I=\{ 1,\ldots ,m\} $, we also write

\[ F_1\land \cdots \land F_ m=\bigwedge _{i\in I}F_ i. \]

Definition A.1.10. Given a non-empty family $\{ F_ i\} _{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{Conv}(N)$, we define

\[ \bigvee _{i\in I}F_ i\coloneqq \sup _{i\in I} F_ i. \]

When the family $I$ is finite, say $I=\{ 1,\ldots ,m\} $, we also write

\[ F_1\lor \cdots \lor F_ m=\bigvee _{i\in I}F_ i. \]
Recall that $\bigvee _{i\in I}F_ i\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ by proposition A.1.7.

Proposition A.1.11. Let $F_1,\ldots ,F_ m\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$. Then

\[ \begin{split} F_1\land \cdots \land F_ m(x)=\inf \left\{ \sum _{i=1}^ m \lambda _ i F_ i(x_ i):x_ i\in \operatorname{Dom}(F_ i),\right.\\ \left.\lambda _ i\in [0,1],\sum _{i=1}^ m\lambda _ i=1, \sum _{i=1}^ m\lambda _ i x_ i=x \right\} . \end{split} \]
See [Theorem 5.6, Roc70] for the more general result.

Lemma A.1.12. Let $\{ F_ i\} _{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{Conv}(N)$. Then $\inf _{i\in I} F_ i\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$.

Proof. Write $F=\inf _{i\in I} F_ i$. We shall apply the characterization in A.1.3. Take $n,r\in N$, $a,b\in \mathbb {R}$ such that $a>F(n)$, $b>F(r)$ and $t\in (0,1)$. We need to show that

A.5
\begin{equation} F(tn+(1-t)r)<ta+(1-t)b. \end{equation}

By definition, there are $j_ n,j_ r\in I$ such that $a>F_ i(n)$ for $i\geq j_ n$ and $b>F_ i(r)$ for $i\geq j_ r$. Since $I$ is directed, we can find $j\in I$ dominating both $j_ n$ and $j_ r$, and then for any $i\geq j$, we have

\[ a>F_ i(n),\quad b>F_ i(r). \]

It follows from A.1.3 that

\[ F_ i(tn+(1-t)r)<ta+(1-t)b \]

for any $i\geq j$. Since $F_ i$ is decreasing in $i$, we conclude (A.5). $\square$

Definition A.1.13. Let $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$. The closure $\operatorname{cl}F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ of $F$ is defined as follows: If $F(n)=-\infty $ for some $n\in N$, then $\operatorname{cl}F\coloneqq -\infty $. Otherwise, we define $\operatorname{cl}F$ as the lower semicontinuity regularization of $F$.

A convex function $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ is closed if $F=\operatorname{cl}F$. In other words, $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ is closed if one of the following conditions holds:

  1. $F\equiv -\infty $;

  2. $F\equiv \infty $;

  3. $F$ is proper and lower semicontinuous.

Proposition A.1.14.Let $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ be a closed convex function. Then $F$ is the supremum of all affine functions lying below $F$.

See [Theorem 12.1, Roc70].

Theorem A.1.15. Let $F\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$. Then $\operatorname{cl}F$ is a closed proper convex function. Moreover, $\operatorname{cl}F$ agrees with $F$ except possibly on the relative boundary of $\operatorname{Dom}F$.

See [Theorem 7.4, Roc70].

Proposition A.1.16. Let $F,F'\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ be closed convex functions. Assume that

  1. $\operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F=\operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F'$, and

  2. $F=F'$ on $\operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F$.

Then $F=F'$.

This is a special case of [Corollary 7.3.4, Roc70].

Definition A.1.17. Given $F,F'\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$, we write $F\preceq F'$ if there is $C\in \mathbb {R}$ such that

\[ F\leq F'+C. \]

We say $F\sim F'$ if $F\preceq F'$ and $F'\preceq F$ both hold.

Theorem A.1.18. Let $C\subseteq N$ be an open subset. Let $(f_ i)_{i>0}$ be a sequence of real-valued convex functions on $C$. Suppose that the sequence converges on a dense subset of $C$ and the limit is finite. Then the limit

\[ f(x)\coloneqq \lim _{i\to \infty } f_ i(x) \]

exists for all $x\in C$ and is convex on $C$. Moreover, the sequence $(f_ i)_ i$ converges uniformly to $f$ on each compact subset of $C$.

This is a special case of [Theorem 10.8, Roc70].

A.2 Legendre transform

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $N$ be a real vector space of finite dimension and $M$ be the dual vector space. The pairing $M\times N\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ will be denoted by $\langle \bullet , \bullet \rangle $.

Definition A.2.1. Let $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ be a convex function. We define the Legendre transform of $F$ as the function $F^*\in \mathrm{Conv}(M)$:

A.6
\begin{equation} F^*(m)\coloneqq \sup _{n\in N} \left( \langle m,n \rangle -F(n) \right)=\sup _{n\in \operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F} \left( \langle m,n \rangle -F(n) \right). \end{equation}
The latter equality follows from [Corollary 12.2.2, Roc70]. Recall the well-known Legendre–Fenchel duality [Theorem 12.2, Roc70].

Theorem A.2.2. Let $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$. Then $F^*$ is a closed convex function. The function $F^*$ is proper if and only if $F$ is.

Moreover, we have $(\operatorname{cl}F)^*=F^*$ and

\[ F^{**}=\operatorname{cl}F. \]

Example A.2.3. Let $P\subseteq M$ be a closed convex subset. Then

\[ \operatorname{Supp}_ P^*=\chi _ P,\quad \chi _ P^*=\operatorname{Supp}_ P. \]

See [Theorem 13.2, Roc70].

The following special case will be useful to us in the sequel.

Corollary A.2.4. Let $F\colon (0,\infty )\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ be a convex function. If we define $G\colon \mathbb {R}\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ by

\[ G(\tau )=\sup _{t>0} \Bigl ( t\tau -F(t) \Bigr ), \]

then $G$ is a convex function and

A.7
\begin{equation} F(t)=G^*(t),\quad \forall t>0. \end{equation}

Moreover,

A.8
\begin{equation} G(\tau )=\sup _{t\in \mathbb {R}_{>0}} \Bigl ( t\tau -F(t) \Bigr )=\sup _{t\in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}} \Bigl ( t\tau -F(t) \Bigr ). \end{equation}

Proof. We distinguish two cases.

First suppose that $F(t)=-\infty $ for some $t>0$. Then $F(t)=-\infty $ for all $t>0$ by the convexity of $F$. Our assertions are clear in this case.

Next assume that $F(t)\neq -\infty $ for all $t>0$. In this case, theorem A.1.15 guarantees that $F$ admits a closed proper extension $\tilde{F}\in \mathrm{Conv}(\mathbb {R})$ with

\[ \tilde{F}(t)=\infty ,\quad \forall t<0. \]

It follows from (A.6) that

\[ G(\tau )=\tilde{F}^*(\tau ),\quad \forall \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \]

Now theorem A.2.2 implies (A.7). Finally (A.8) follows from the continuity of $F$. $\square$

Proposition A.2.5. Let $F\colon N\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$. Define $F^*\colon M\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ by

\[ F^*(m)\coloneqq \sup _{n\in N} \left( \langle m,n \rangle -F(n) \right). \]

Then

\[ F^*=(\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{CE}F)^*. \]
See [Corollary 12.1.1, Roc70].

Definition A.2.6. Let $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ and $n\in N$. An element $m\in M$ is a subgradient (in the sense of Alexandrov) of $F$ at $n$ if

A.9
\begin{equation} F(n')\geq F(n)+\langle n'-n,m \rangle ,\quad \forall n'\in N. \end{equation}

The set of subgradients of $F$ at $n$ is denoted by $\nabla F(n)$.

More generally, for any subset $E\subseteq N$, we write

\[ \nabla F(E)=\bigcup _{n\in E}\nabla F(n). \]

Definition A.2.7. Given $F,F'\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$, we write $F\preceq _ P F'$ if

\[ \overline{\nabla F(N)}\subseteq \overline{\nabla F'(N)}. \]

We write $F\sim _ P F'$ if $F\preceq _ P F'$ and $F'\preceq _ P F$.

Theorem A.2.8. Suppose that $F\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$. Then the following hold:

  1. For any $n\not\in \operatorname{Dom}F$, $\nabla F(n)=\varnothing $;

  2. for any $n\in \operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F$, $\nabla F(n)\neq \varnothing $; moreover, for any $n'\in N$, we have

    \[ \partial _{n'} F(n)=\sup \left\{ \langle n', m \rangle : m\in \nabla F(n)\right\} ; \]

  3. for $n\in N$, the set $\nabla F(n)$ is non-empty and bounded if and only if $n\in \operatorname{Int}\operatorname{Dom}F$.

For the proof, we refer to [Theorem 23.4, Roc70].

Proposition A.2.9. Let $F\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$. Then

\[ \nabla F(N)\subseteq \operatorname{Dom}F^*. \]

If moreover $F$ is closed, we have

A.10
\begin{equation} \operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F^*\subseteq \nabla F(N). \end{equation}

In particular, if $F$ is a proper closed convex function on $N$, then

\[ \overline{\nabla F(N)}=\overline{\operatorname{Dom}F^*}. \]

Proof. Suppose that $m\in \nabla F(n)$ for some $n\in N$. Then (A.9) holds. In particular,

\[ \langle m,n'\rangle -F(n')\leq \langle m,n\rangle -F(n). \]

It follows that

\[ F^*(m)\leq \langle m,n\rangle -F(n)<\infty . \]

(A.10) is proved in [Corollary 23.5.1, Roc70]. For the last assertion, it suffices to observe that $\overline{\operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}F^*}=\overline{\operatorname{Dom}F^*}$. $\square$

Proposition A.2.10. Let $\{ F_ i\} _{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$. Then

\[ \left(\bigwedge _{i\in I} F_ i\right)^*=\bigvee _{i\in I} F_ i^*,\quad \left(\bigvee _{i\in I} \operatorname{cl}F_ i\right)^*=\operatorname{cl}\bigwedge _{i\in I} F_ i^*. \]

If $I$ is finite and $\overline{\operatorname{Dom}F_ i}$ is independent of the choice of $i\in I$, then

\[ \left(\bigvee _{i\in I} F_ i\right)^*=\bigwedge _{i\in I} F_ i^*. \]
Recall that $\wedge $ is defined in definition A.1.9 and $\lor $ in definition A.1.10. See [Theorem 16.5, Roc70] for the proof.

Proposition A.2.11. Let $F_1,\ldots ,F_ r\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$ ($r\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$). Assume that

\[ \bigcap _{i=1}^ r\operatorname{RelInt}\operatorname{Dom}(F_ i)\neq \varnothing , \]

then for any $m\in M$,

\[ \left( \sum _{i=1}^ r F_ i \right)^*(m)=\inf \left\{ \sum _{i=1}^ r F_ i^*(m_ i):m_1,\ldots ,m_ r\in M, \sum _{i=1}^ r m_ i=m \right\} . \]
See [Theorem 16.4, Roc70] for the proof.
Proposition A.2.12. Let $P\subseteq M$ be a convex body
Note. Here a convex body refers to a non-empty compact convex subset, not necessarily having non-empty interior.
and $F\in \mathrm{Conv}^{\mathrm{prop}}(N)$. The following are equivalent:

  1. $F\preceq \operatorname{Supp}_ P$;

  2. $\operatorname{Dom}F=N$ and $F^*|_{M\setminus P}\equiv \infty $;

  3. $\operatorname{Dom}F=N$ and $\nabla F(N)\subseteq P$.

Moreover, under these conditions,

A.11
\begin{equation} F(n)-\operatorname{Supp}_ P(n)\leq F(0),\quad \forall n\in N. \end{equation}

Proof. 1 $\implies $ 2. It is clear that $\operatorname{Dom}F=N$ since $\operatorname{Dom}\operatorname{Supp}_ P=N$. From $F\preceq \operatorname{Supp}_ P$ and example A.2.3, we know that

\[ \chi _ P=\operatorname{Supp}_ P^*\preceq F^*. \]

So 2 follows.

2 $\implies $ 3. This follows from proposition A.2.9.

3 $\implies $ 1. The function $t\mapsto F(tn)$ is convex on $[0,1]$, hence absolutely continuous and differentiable almost everywhere. For almost every $t\in (0,1)$,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}F(tn)=\partial _ n F(tn) \leq \operatorname{Supp}_ P(n) \]

by theorem A.2.8 and the assumption $\nabla F(N)\subseteq P$. Integrating in $t$ gives

\[ F(n)-F(0)\leq \operatorname{Supp}_ P(n). \]

In particular, (A.11) also follows. $\square$

A.3 Classes of convex functions

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $N$ be a real vector space of finite dimension and $M$ be the dual vector space.

We shall fix a convex body $P\subseteq M$.

The following classes are introduced in [BB13].

Definition A.3.1. We define the set $\mathcal{P}(N,P)$ as the set of proper convex functions $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ such that $F\preceq \operatorname{Supp}_ P$.

We define the set $\mathcal{E}^{\infty }(N,P)$ as the set of closed convex functions $F\in \mathrm{Conv}(N)$ such that $F\sim \operatorname{Supp}_ P$.

We define the set $\mathcal{E}(N,P)$ as follows: Suppose that $\operatorname{Int}P=\varnothing $. Then $\mathcal{E}(N,P)\coloneqq \mathcal{P}(N,P)$; otherwise, let

\[ \mathcal{E}(N,P)=\left\{ F\in \mathcal{P}(N,P) : P=\overline{\nabla F(N)} \right\} . \]

We define the set $\mathcal{E}^1(N,P)$ as the subset of $\mathcal{E}(N,P)$ consisting of $F\in \mathcal{E}(N,P)$ with

\[ \int _ P F^*\, \mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}<\infty , \]

where $\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}$ is any Lebesgue measure on $M$.

Observe that for any $F\in \mathcal{P}(N,P)$, we have $\operatorname{Dom}F=N$ and $F$ is necessarily closed.

Proposition A.3.2. We have

\[ \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(N,P)\subseteq \mathcal{E}^{1}(N,P)\subseteq \mathcal{E}(N,P) \subseteq \mathcal{P}(N,P). \]

Proof. When $\operatorname{Int}P=\varnothing $, the assertion is clear. We assume that $\operatorname{Int}P\neq \varnothing $. The second inclusion follows from the definition. We only handle the first inequality. Take $F\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(N,P)$. By definition, $F\sim \operatorname{Supp}_ P$ and hence $F^*\sim \chi _ P$. It follows that $P=\operatorname{Dom}F^*$.

By proposition A.2.12, we already know that

\[ \nabla F(N)\subseteq P=\operatorname{Dom}F^*. \]

On the other hand, by proposition A.2.9, we have

\[ \operatorname{Int}P\subseteq \nabla F(N). \]

So it follows that

\[ P=\overline{\nabla F(N)}. \]

It is clear that $F^*\sim \chi _ P$ is integrable. $\square$

Proposition A.3.3. For any $F\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(N,P)$, we have $F^*|_{M\setminus P}\equiv \infty $ and $F^*$ is bounded on $P$.

Proof. From $F\sim \operatorname{Supp}_ P$, we take the Legendre transform to get $F^*\sim \operatorname{Supp}_ P^*=\chi _ P$, where we applied example A.2.3. $\square$

Definition A.3.4. We endow the topology of pointwise convergence on $\mathcal{P}(N,P)$.

Note that this topology coincides with the compact-open topology. See [Theorem 10.8, Roc70].

Proposition A.3.5. Let $F\in \mathcal{P}(N,P)$. Then there is a decreasing sequence $F_ j\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }(N,P)\cap C^{\infty }(N)$ converging to $F$.

See [Lemma 2.2, BB13]. We observe that the point $0\in N$ plays a special role since it does in the definition of the support function.

Proposition A.3.6. For any $F\in \mathcal{P}(N,P)$, we have

\[ \max _{N}(F-\operatorname{Supp}_ P)=F(0). \]

Proof. It follows from (A.11) that

\[ \sup _{N}(F-\operatorname{Supp}_ P)\leq F(0). \]

The equality is clearly obtained at $0\in N$. $\square$

Lemma A.3.7. Let $P'\subseteq M$ be another convex body. Then for any $F\in \mathcal{P}(N,P)$ and $F'\in \mathcal{P}(N,P')$, we have

\[ F+F'\in \mathcal{P}(N,P+P'). \]

Moreover, let $F$ and $F'$ be real-valued convex functions on $N$. Assume that $\operatorname{Dom}F^*$ is bounded. Then

\[ \overline{\nabla F(N)}+\overline{\nabla F'(N)}=\overline{\nabla (F+F')(N)}. \]

In particular, if $F\in \mathcal{P}(N,P)$, $F'\in \mathcal{P}(N,P')$ and

\[ \overline{\nabla F(N)}=P,\quad \overline{\nabla F'(N)}=P', \]

then $F+F'\in \mathcal{E}(N,P+P')$.

Proof. The former assertion follows immediately from the observation

\[ \operatorname{Supp}_{P+P'}=\operatorname{Supp}_ P+\operatorname{Supp}_{P'}. \]

We prove the gradient-image equality. In view of proposition A.2.9, this means

A.12
\begin{equation} \overline{\operatorname{Dom}(F+F')^*}=\overline{\operatorname{Dom}F^*}+\overline{\operatorname{Dom}F'{}^*}. \end{equation}

It follows from proposition A.2.11 that

\[ \operatorname{Dom}(F+F')^*=\operatorname{Dom}F^*+\operatorname{Dom}F'{}^*. \]
Since $\overline{\operatorname{Dom}F^*}$ is compact, (A.12) follows
Note. In general, the Minkowski sum does not commute with the closure.
.

Finally, under the assumptions in the last assertion, the former assertion gives $F+F'\in \mathcal{P}(N,P+P')$. If $\operatorname{Int}(P+P')=\varnothing $, this is exactly the definition of $\mathcal{E}(N,P+P')$. Otherwise, the gradient-image equality gives

\[ \overline{\nabla (F+F')(N)}=P+P', \]

so again $F+F'\in \mathcal{E}(N,P+P')$. $\square$

A.4 Monge–Ampère measures

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $N$ be a free Abelian group of finite rank $n$ (i.e., a lattice) and $M$ be its dual lattice. There is a canonical Lebesgue-type measure on $M_{\mathbb {R}}$, denoted by $\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}$, normalized so that the smallest cubes in $M$ have volume $1$. Similarly, the canonical measure on $N_{\mathbb {R}}$ is normalized in the same way and is denoted by $\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}$ as well.

We will write

\[ N_{\mathbb {R}}=N\otimes _{\mathbb {Z}}\mathbb {R},\quad M_{\mathbb {R}}=M\otimes _{\mathbb {Z}}\mathbb {R}. \]

Definition A.4.1. Let $F$ be a finite convex function on $N_{\mathbb {R}}$. We define the real Monge–Ampère measure $\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}F$ as the Alexandrov measure on $N_{\mathbb {R}}$ given as follows: for each Borel measurable set $E\subseteq N_{\mathbb {R}}$, define

A.13
\begin{equation} \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}F(E)\coloneqq n!\int _{\nabla F(E)} \mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}. \end{equation}

The measurability and countable additivity in this definition are standard properties of the subgradient map; see for example [Section 2.1, Fig17].

Proposition A.4.2. Suppose that $F\in C^{1,1}(N_{\mathbb {R}})\cap \mathrm{Conv}(N_{\mathbb {R}})$. Fix an identification $N=\mathbb {Z}^ n$. Then

\[ \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}F=n!\cdot \det \nabla ^2 F \, \mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}. \]
See [Example 2.2, Fig17].

Proposition A.4.3. Let $P\subseteq M_{\mathbb {R}}$ be a convex body and $F\in \mathcal{P}(N_{\mathbb {R}},P)$. Then $F\in \mathcal{E}(N_{\mathbb {R}},P)$ if and only if

A.14
\begin{equation} \int _{N_{\mathbb {R}}}\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}F=n! \operatorname{vol}P. \end{equation}

Proof. Since $F\in \mathcal{P}(N_{\mathbb {R}},P)$, the function $F$ is closed and has domain $N_{\mathbb {R}}$. By proposition A.2.9, $\overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb {R}})}=\overline{\operatorname{Dom}F^*}$, so this closure is convex. As $\nabla F(N_{\mathbb {R}})$ contains the relative interior of $\operatorname{Dom}F^*$, the definition of $\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}$ shows that (A.14) is equivalent to

\[ \operatorname{vol}\overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb {R}})}=\operatorname{vol}P. \]

We first handle the case where $\operatorname{Int}P\neq \varnothing $. By proposition A.2.12, $\overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb {R}})}\subseteq P$. Hence the latter is equivalent to

\[ \overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb {R}})}=P. \]

Indeed, a proper closed subset of the full-dimensional convex body $P$ has complement containing a non-empty relatively open subset of $P$, hence has strictly smaller volume.

Now assume that $\operatorname{Int}P=\varnothing $, then $\operatorname{vol}\overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb {R}})}=\operatorname{vol}P=0$ by proposition A.2.12. The assertion is clear. $\square$

Theorem A.4.4. Let $F,F_ j\in \mathcal{P}(N_{\mathbb {R}},P)$ ($j\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$). Assume that $F_ j\to F$. Then

\[ \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}(F_ j)\rightharpoonup \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}(F) \]

weakly.

See [Proposition 2.6, Fig17]. There is a well-known comparison principle.

Theorem A.4.5. Let $F,F'\in \mathcal{P}(N_{\mathbb {R}},P)$. Assume that $F\preceq F'$. Then

\[ \overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb {R}})}\subseteq \overline{\nabla F'(N_{\mathbb {R}})},\quad \int _{N_{\mathbb {R}}} \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}(F)\leq \int _{N_{\mathbb {R}}} \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb {R}}(F'). \]

Proof. It suffices to observe that $F'{}^*\preceq F^*$, and hence the first assertion follows from proposition A.2.9. By the definition of the real Monge–Ampère measure,

\[ \int _{N_{\mathbb R}}\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}F=n!\operatorname{vol}\overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb R})}. \]

So the second assertion follows from the first. $\square$

Lemma A.4.6. Let $F\in \mathcal{E}(N_{\mathbb {R}},P)$ and set $G=F^*$. Then the map $\nabla G$ pushes forward $n!\, \mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}|_{P}$ to $\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F)$. Equivalently, for every bounded Borel function $h$ on $N_{\mathbb R}$,

\[ \int _{N_{\mathbb R}} h\, \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F)= n!\int _ P h(\nabla G(m))\, \mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}(m). \]

Proof. If $\operatorname{vol}P=0$, the assertion is clear from proposition A.2.12 and the definition of $\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}$. We may therefore assume that $P$ has non-empty interior.

Since $F\in \mathcal P(N_{\mathbb R},P)$, we have $\operatorname{Dom}G\subseteq P$ by proposition A.2.12. On the other hand, by the definition of $\mathcal E(N_{\mathbb R},P)$ and proposition A.2.9,

\[ P=\overline{\nabla F(N_{\mathbb R})}\subseteq \overline{\operatorname{Dom}G}. \]

Hence $\overline{\operatorname{Dom}G}=P$. Since $\operatorname{Dom}G$ is convex, $G$ is finite on $\operatorname{Int}P$ and is equal to $\infty $ outside $P$. Let $D$ be the set of points in $\operatorname{Int}P$ at which $G$ is differentiable. By [Theorem 25.5, Roc70], $P\setminus D$ has measure zero. By [Theorem 23.5, Roc70], $m\in \nabla F(n)$ if and only if $n\in \nabla G(m)$. Hence for any Borel set $E\subseteq N_{\mathbb R}$,

\[ \{ m\in D:\nabla G(m)\in E\} =D\cap \nabla F(E). \]

It follows from definition A.4.1 that

\[ n!\operatorname{vol}\{ m\in P:\nabla G(m)\in E\} =n!\operatorname{vol}\nabla F(E)=\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F)(E). \]

Our assertions follow. $\square$

Finally, let us recall a mixed version of the real Monge–Ampère measure:

Theorem A.4.7. There is a symmetric map assigning to every $n$-tuple of finite convex functions $F_1,\ldots ,F_ n$ on $N_{\mathbb R}$ a positive Radon measure

\[ \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F_1,\ldots ,F_ n) \]

on $N_{\mathbb R}$ with the following properties:

  1. For every $q\in \mathbb Z_{>0}$, finite convex functions $F_1,\ldots ,F_ q$ on $N_{\mathbb R}$, and $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ q\geq 0$,

    \[ \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(\lambda _1F_1+\cdots +\lambda _ qF_ q) =\sum _{i_1,\ldots ,i_ n=1}^ q \lambda _{i_1}\cdots \lambda _{i_ n} \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F_{i_1},\ldots ,F_{i_ n}). \]

  2. For every finite convex function $F$ on $N_{\mathbb R}$,

    \[ \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F,\ldots ,F)=\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F). \]

  3. The map $\operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}$ is additive and positively homogeneous of degree $1$ in each entry. In other words, for finite convex functions $F,G,F_2,\ldots ,F_ n$ on $N_{\mathbb R}$ and $\lambda ,\mu \geq 0$,

    \[ \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(\lambda F+\mu G,F_2,\ldots ,F_ n) =\lambda \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(F,F_2,\ldots ,F_ n) +\mu \operatorname{MA}_{\mathbb R}(G,F_2,\ldots ,F_ n). \]
This is [Theorem 4.3(a),(b),(f), CLM22], multiplied by $n!$ to match the normalization in definition A.4.1. The positivity is part of the construction: it is the non-negativity of the mixed discriminant in the smooth case and follows in general by weak continuity. The mixed real Monge–Ampère measure was also studied in [TW02, PR04].

A.5 Several lemmata

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Lemma A.5.1. Let $\alpha ,\beta _1,\ldots ,\beta _ m\in \mathbb {Z}^ n$. Let $\Delta $ be the polytope generated by $\beta _1,\ldots ,\beta _ m$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. A.15
    \begin{equation} |z^{\alpha }|^2\left(\sum _{i=1}^ m |z^{\beta _ i}|^2 \right)^{-1} \end{equation}

    is a bounded function on $\mathbb {C}^{*n}$.

  2. $\alpha \in \Delta $.

Proof. 2 $\implies $ 1. Write $\alpha =\sum _ i t_ i\beta _ i$, where $t_ i\in [0,1]$, $\sum _ i t_ i=1$. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} |z^{\alpha }|^2\left(\sum _{i=1}^ m |z^{\beta _ i}|^2 \right)^{-1} & =\prod _ i |z^{\beta _ i}|^{2t_ i}\left(\sum _{i=1}^ m |z^{\beta _ i}|^2 \right)^{-1}\\ & \leq \prod _ i\left(\sum _{j=1}^ m |z^{\beta _ j}|^2\right)^{t_ i} \left(\sum _{i=1}^ m |z^{\beta _ i}|^2 \right)^{-1}=1. \end{aligned} \]

1 $\implies $ 2. Assume that $\alpha \not\in \Delta $. By the strict separation theorem, there is $a=(a_1,\ldots ,a_ n)\in \mathbb {R}^ n$ such that

\[ a\cdot \alpha >\max _ i a\cdot \beta _ i. \]

Set

\[ z(t)\coloneqq (t^{a_1},\ldots ,t^{a_ n}). \]

Then (A.15) evaluated at $z(t)$ grows like

\[ \frac{t^{2a\cdot \alpha }}{\sum _ i t^{2a\cdot \beta _ i}} \]

as $t\to \infty $, and hence is not bounded. $\square$

Lemma A.5.2. Let $\beta _1,\ldots ,\beta _ m\in \mathbb {N}^ n$ and $\beta \in \mathbb {R}^ n$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. $\log \sum _{i=1}^ m \mathrm{e}^{x\cdot \beta _ i}-x\cdot \beta $ is bounded from below.

  2. $\beta $ is in the convex hull of the $\beta _ i$'s.

Proof. If $\beta =\sum _ i t_ i\beta _ i$ with $t_ i\in [0,1]$ and $\sum _ i t_ i=1$, then by the weighted arithmetic–geometric mean inequality,

\[ x\cdot \beta =\sum _ i t_ i\, x\cdot \beta _ i \leq \log \sum _ i t_ i\mathrm{e}^{x\cdot \beta _ i} \leq \log \sum _ i \mathrm{e}^{x\cdot \beta _ i}. \]

This proves that the function in 1 is bounded from below.

Conversely, if $\beta $ does not lie in the convex hull of the $\beta _ i$'s, choose $a\in \mathbb {R}^ n$ such that

\[ a\cdot \beta >\max _ i a\cdot \beta _ i. \]

Evaluating at $x=ta$ and letting $t\to \infty $, we find

\[ \log \sum _ i \mathrm{e}^{t a\cdot \beta _ i}-t\, a\cdot \beta \to -\infty , \]

contradicting 1. $\square$

Lemma A.5.3. Let $I\subseteq \mathbb {R}$ be an open interval containing $0$. Let $(f_ k\colon I\rightarrow \mathbb {R})_{k\geq 1}$ be a sequence of convex functions, and let $g\colon I\to \mathbb {R}$ be a function. Assume that

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } f_ k(t)\leq g(t)\quad \text{for every } t\in I, \]

and that

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } f_ k(0)=g(0). \]

If each $f_ k$ and $g$ is differentiable at $0$, then

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } f_ k'(0)=g'(0). \]

Proof. Since $f_ k$ is convex and differentiable at $0$, for every $t\in I$,

\[ f_ k(t)\geq f_ k(0)+t f_ k'(0). \]

Taking the upper limit as $k\to \infty $ and using the assumptions gives

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } t f_ k'(0) \leq g(t)-g(0). \]

For $t>0$, this yields

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } f_ k'(0) \leq \frac{g(t)-g(0)}{t}. \]

Letting $t\to 0+$ and using the differentiability of $g$ at $0$, we get

\[ \varlimsup _{k\to \infty } f_ k'(0)\leq g'(0). \]

For $t<0$, the same inequality gives

\[ \varliminf _{k\to \infty } f_ k'(0) \geq \frac{g(t)-g(0)}{t}. \]

Letting $t\to 0-$ and again using the differentiability of $g$ at $0$, we obtain

\[ \varliminf _{k\to \infty } f_ k'(0)\geq g'(0). \]

Therefore

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } f_ k'(0)=g'(0), \]

as claimed. $\square$
