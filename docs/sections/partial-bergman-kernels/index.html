15.3 Partial Bergman kernels

14 Partial Bergman kernels

The classical Bergman kernel of a Hermitian line bundle $L$ on a compact Kähler manifold $X$ encodes, through its asymptotic expansion as the tensor power $L^ k$ goes to infinity, deep geometric information about the underlying metric. This chapter develops the corresponding partial Bergman kernel. The word partial refers to the fact that we only use sections cut out by an ideal, typically the multiplier ideal of an auxiliary psh weight on a closed subset $K\subseteq X$.

The main result is the convergence theorem theorem 14.3.7. Given a $\theta $-psh potential $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, a closed non-pluripolar weighted subset $(K,v)$, and a Bernstein–Markov measure $\mu $, the normalized partial Bergman measures on $X$ converge weakly to the partial equilibrium measure of $(K,v,\varphi )$ as $k\to \infty $. This is the relative version of the theorem of Berman–Boucksom–Witt Nyström [BBWN11], corresponding to the case $\varphi =V_\theta $. The assumptions are essentially the mildest ones under which the statement makes sense.

The first part of the chapter introduces the relative envelope operators. In section 14.1 we define the relative $P$-envelope $P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)$ and the relative $\mathcal{I}$-envelope $P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)$ by replacing the constraint $\eta \leq 0$ in the usual $P$- and $\mathcal{I}$-envelopes with the constraint $\eta |_ K\leq v$. We establish their basic properties, including monotonicity, mass-preservation, and the relation with the envelope operators of chapter 3.

The second part develops the equilibrium theory associated with a weighted subset $(K,v)$. In section 14.2 we show that the Monge–Ampère measure $\theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}^ n$ is carried by the contact locus $\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)|_ K=v\} \subseteq K$, which justifies the name partial equilibrium measure. The chapter concludes in section 14.3 with the proof of the weak convergence of partial Bergman measures towards this partial equilibrium.

14.1 Partial envelopes

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $K \subseteq X$ be a closed non-pluripolar set. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a pseudo-effective cohomology class.

Definition 14.1.1. Given a function $f\colon K\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we introduce the relative $P$-envelope of $\varphi $ (with respect to $K,f,\theta $) as

14.1
\begin{equation} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere and } \eta \preceq \varphi \Bigr \} . \end{equation}

Similarly, we define the relative $\mathcal{I}$-envelope of $\varphi $ (with respect to $K,f,\theta $) as

14.2
\begin{equation} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere and } \eta \preceq _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi \Bigr \} . \end{equation}
By $\eta |_ K\leq f$ quasi-everywhere, we mean that there is a pluripolar set $S\subseteq X$ so that $\eta |_{K\setminus S}\leq f|_{K\setminus S}$. In general the upper envelope in (14.1) or (14.2) may be identically $\infty $.

Observe that when $f$ is bounded from below, neither envelope is identically $-\infty $.

The envelopes contain essentially all envelope operators that we have studied in this book:

Example 14.1.2. When $K=X$ and $f=0$, we have

\[ P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](0)=P_{\theta }[\varphi ],\quad P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(0)=P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

When $K=X$, we have

\[ P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](f)=P_{\theta }[\varphi ](f). \]

These definitions reduce to the envelopes studied in chapter 3. On the other hand, when $\varphi =V_{\theta }$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](f) & =P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\\ & =\operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\left\{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \begin{array}{l} \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere} \end{array} \right\} . \end{aligned} \]

In an even more special case where $\varphi =V_{\theta }$ and $K=X$, we find that

\[ P_{\theta ,X}[V_{\theta }](f)=\operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere}\Bigr \} . \]

This is nothing but $P_{\theta }(f)$ as we studied in definition 2.4.12.

Proposition 14.1.3. Let $f,g\colon K\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ]$ be functions and $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then we have the following:

  1. We have

    \[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f); \]

  2. assume that $f$ is bounded from above, then

    \[ \begin{aligned} \left.P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\right|_ K\leq & f\textup{ quasi-everywhere},\\ \left. P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\right|_ K\leq & f\textup{ quasi-everywhere}; \end{aligned} \]

  3. suppose that $\varphi \preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\psi $, then

    \[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\psi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f); \]

  4. if $f\leq g$, then

    \[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](g),\quad P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(g); \]

  5. for any $t \in (0,1)$, we have

    \[ \begin{aligned} P_{\theta ,K}\left[t\varphi +(1-t)\psi \right](f)\geq & tP_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)+(1-t)P_{\theta ,K}[\psi ](f),\\ P_{\theta ,K}\left[t\varphi +(1-t)\psi \right]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\geq & tP_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)+(1-t)P_{\theta ,K}[\psi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f). \end{aligned} \]

Proof. 1 This follows from $\eta \preceq \varphi \Rightarrow \eta \preceq _{\mathcal I}\varphi $.

2 This follows from proposition 1.2.5 and 1.5.5.

3 This follows by inclusion of the defining candidate sets.

4 This follows by inclusion of the defining candidate sets.

5 If $\eta ,\eta '\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ are such that

\[ \begin{split} & \eta |_ K\leq f\textup{ quasi-everywhere},\quad \eta '|_ K\leq f\textup{ quasi-everywhere},\\ & \eta \preceq \varphi ,\quad \eta '\preceq \psi , \end{split} \]

then for $t\in (0,1)$,

\[ \left.\left(t\eta +(1-t)\eta '\right)\right|_ K\leq f\textup{ quasi-everywhere},\quad t\eta +(1-t)\eta ' \preceq t\varphi +(1-t)\psi . \]

Therefore,

\[ t\eta +(1-t)\eta '\leq P_{\theta ,K}\left[t\varphi +(1-t)\psi \right](f). \]

Since $\eta $ and $\eta '$ are arbitrary, the first inequality follows. The second follows from the same argument. $\square$

Similar to the usual envelopes studied in chapter 3, the $P$-partial envelopes are well-behaved only when $\varphi $ has positive mass.

Lemma 14.1.4. Suppose that $f\colon K\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ is bounded from below, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

14.11
\begin{equation} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)= \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}: \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere and } \eta \preceq \varphi \Bigr \} . \end{equation}
In other words, in this situation, in the definition (14.1), we may restrict to only those $\eta $ with positive mass.

Proof. By our assumption, we can find $C>0$ so that $\varphi |_ K-C\leq f$. Then if $\eta $ is a candidate in (14.1), $\eta \lor (\varphi -C)$ is also a candidate, and $\eta \lor (\varphi -C)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Therefore, $\eta \lor (\varphi -C)$ is also a candidate for the right-hand side of (14.11). The equality (14.11) follows. $\square$

Note that in the most important cases, we can actually replace the condition $\eta \leq f$ quasi-everywhere in (14.1) by everywhere.

Lemma 14.1.5. Let $f\colon K\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ be bounded from below, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

14.12
\begin{equation} \begin{split} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)=& \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ and } \eta \preceq \varphi \Bigr \} \\ =& \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}: \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ and } \eta \preceq \varphi \Bigr \} . \end{split} \end{equation}

Proof. Both $\geq $ directions in (14.12) are trivial.

Conversely, let $\eta $ be a candidate in (14.11). Let $S\subseteq X$ be a pluripolar set so that $\eta |_{K\setminus S}\leq f|_{K\setminus S}$.

We now claim that we can choose $\chi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ with $\chi |_ S\equiv -\infty $ and $\chi \leq \eta $.

Assume this for the moment. For any $\delta \in (0,1)$, we have

\[ (1-\delta )\eta |_ K+\delta \chi |_ K\leq f,\quad (1-\delta )\eta +\delta \chi \preceq \varphi . \]

Hence, $(1-\delta )\eta +\delta \chi $ is dominated by the right-hand side of (14.12). Letting $\delta \to 0+$, we conclude that $\eta $ is dominated by the right-hand side of (14.12) and hence, (14.12) follows.

It remains to construct $\chi $. Since $\varphi $ has positive mass, the class $\{ \theta \} $ is big. Fix a Kähler form $\omega _0$ on $X$ and choose $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\theta _{\rho }\geq 2\epsilon _0\omega _0$ for some $\epsilon _0>0$. By theorem 1.1.17, there is a quasi-psh function $q$ such that $q|_ S\equiv -\infty $. Choose $A>0$ so that $A\omega _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq 0$. For $a>0$ small enough, $\rho +aq\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and still has pole $-\infty $ on $S$. Replacing $\rho $ by $\rho +aq$, we may therefore assume that $\rho |_ S\equiv -\infty $. Since $\int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n>0$, fixing such a $\rho $ and taking $\epsilon \in (0,1)$ small enough, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta ^ n_{\epsilon \rho +(1-\epsilon )V_{\theta }}+\int _ X \theta _{\eta }^ n>\int _ X \theta _{V_{\theta }}^ n. \]

Thus, by proposition 3.1.15 and theorem 3.1.18, we have

\[ \Bigl (\epsilon \rho +(1-\epsilon )V_{\theta }\Bigr ) \land \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}. \]

It suffices to define this function as $\chi $. $\square$

Corollary 14.1.6. Suppose that $f\colon K\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ is bounded, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then there is a constant $C=C(\sup _ K f,K,X)>0$ such that

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\leq C,\quad P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\leq C. \]

Proof. Since $K$ is non-pluripolar, 1.5.5 then gives a constant $C>0$, depending only on $\sup _ K f$, $K$ and $X$, such that every $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfying $\eta |_ K\leq f$ quasi-everywhere satisfies $\eta \leq C$ on $X$.

This applies to the candidates defining both envelopes. Hence both candidate families are locally uniformly bounded above, so their upper semicontinuous suprema are genuine $\theta $-psh functions. By proposition 14.1.3 2, both envelopes are dominated by $f$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Applying 1.5.5 once more gives

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\leq C,\quad P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(f)\leq C. \]
$\square$

Proposition 14.1.7. Let $f\colon K\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ be a function bounded from below and $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Suppose that $\varphi \preceq _{P} \psi $. Then

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\psi ](f). \]

Proof. Let $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be such that $\eta |_ K\leq f$ quasi-everywhere and $\eta \preceq \varphi $. Then $\eta \preceq _ P \psi $, and thanks to corollary 6.1.22 for any $C>0$, we have $\eta \land (\psi +C)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. But then $\eta \land (\psi +C)$ is a candidate in the definition of $P_{\theta ,K}[\psi ](f)$, and we find

\[ \eta \land (\psi +C)\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\psi ](f). \]

By corollary 6.1.22 again, we get

\[ \eta =P_{\theta }[\psi ](\eta )\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\psi ](f). \]

Our assertion follows now from 14.1.4. $\square$

Corollary 14.1.8. Let $f\colon K\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ be a function bounded from below and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

\[ \begin{aligned} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)= & \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere and } \eta \preceq _ P \varphi \Bigr \} \\ =& \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}: \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere and } \eta \preceq _ P \varphi \Bigr \} \\ =& \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ and } \eta \preceq _ P \varphi \Bigr \} \\ =& \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}: \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ and } \eta \preceq _ P \varphi \Bigr \} . \end{aligned} \]

Proof. We first apply proposition 14.1.7,

\[ \begin{split} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)= & P_{\theta ,K}\left[P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\right](f)\\ =& \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere and } \eta \preceq _ P \varphi \Bigr \} . \end{split} \]

Next observe that by 14.1.4, the latter can also be written as

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}: \eta |_ K \leq f \textup{ quasi-everywhere and } \eta \preceq _ P \varphi \Bigr \} . \]

Next we apply 14.1.5; the remaining statements follow. $\square$

Corollary 14.1.9. Let $f\colon K\rightarrow (-\infty ,\infty ]$ be a function bounded from below and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be $\mathcal{I}$-good. Then

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)=P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f). \]

Proof. In fact, since $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-good, a potential $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies $\eta \preceq _ P \varphi $ if and only if $\eta \preceq _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi $. Therefore, our assertion follows from corollary 14.1.8 and (14.2). $\square$

Next, we make the following observations about the singularity types of our envelopes:

Lemma 14.1.10. Let $f \colon K\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ be a bounded function, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. We have

14.21
\begin{equation} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\sim P_{\theta }[\varphi ],\quad P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\sim P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \end{equation}

Proof. We first prove the $P$-part of (14.21).

If $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\eta \preceq \varphi $ and $\eta \leq 0$, then $\eta +\inf _ K f$ is a candidate in the definition (14.1), and hence,

\[ \eta +\inf _ K f \leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f). \]

It follows that

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]+\inf _ K f \leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f). \]

Conversely, let $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be such that

\[ \eta |_ K\leq f,\quad \eta \preceq \varphi . \]

Since $K$ is non-pluripolar, by 1.5.5 we can find $C=C(\sup _ K f,K, X)>0$ so that $\eta \leq C$. Hence $\eta -C$ is a candidate in the definition of $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$, and hence

\[ \eta -C\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]. \]

By 14.1.5, this implies that

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f) \leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ] + C. \]

The proof of the $\mathcal{I}$-part is similar, but we spell it out since this comparison will be used below. If $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies $\eta \leq 0$ and $\eta \preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\varphi $, then $\eta +\inf _ K f$ is a candidate in (14.2). Hence

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}+\inf _ K f\leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f). \]

Conversely, let $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a candidate in (14.2). Since $K$ is non-pluripolar and $f$ is bounded, the same compactness estimate gives a constant $C>0$, independent of $\eta $, such that $\eta \leq C$. Then $\eta -C\leq 0$ and $\eta -C\preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\varphi $. By corollary 6.1.11,

\[ \eta -C\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Taking the upper envelope over all such $\eta $ gives

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\leq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}+C. \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Corollary 14.1.11. Let $f\colon K\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ be a bounded function, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

14.22
\begin{equation} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)=P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ]\left( P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](f) \right). \end{equation}

Proof. Take any $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\eta \preceq \varphi $ and $\eta |_ K\leq f$ quasi-everywhere, then

\[ \eta \leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)\leq P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](f). \]

Therefore,

\[ \eta \leq P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ]\left( P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](f) \right). \]

So we conclude the $\leq $ direction in (14.22).

For the reverse direction, take $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ such that

\[ \eta \preceq \varphi ,\quad \eta \leq P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](f) \quad \textup{quasi-everywhere}. \]

Then $\eta |_ K\leq f$ quasi-everywhere by proposition 14.1.3. Hence

\[ \eta \leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f). \]

By corollary 14.1.8, the $\geq $ direction in (14.22) follows. $\square$

14.2 Partial equilibrium measures

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$, and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a pseudo-effective cohomology class.

Definition 14.2.1. A weighted subset of $X$ is a pair $(K,v)$ consisting of a closed non-pluripolar subset $K\subseteq X$ and a function $v\in C^0(K)$.

The next result motivates our terminology to call the measures $\theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](f)}^ n$ the partial equilibrium measures of our context:

Lemma 14.2.2. Let $(K,v)$ be a weighted subset of $X$, and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

14.23
\begin{equation} \int _{X\setminus K}\theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}^ n=0,\quad \int _ K \mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)|_ K\neq v\right\} }\theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}^ n=0. \end{equation}

More precisely, we have

14.24
\begin{equation} \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}^ n\leq \mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)|_ K=P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)|_ K=v\right\} }\, \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)}^ n. \end{equation}
Here $\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)|_ K\neq v\} $ and $\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)|_ K=P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)|_ K=v\} $ are understood as consisting of points of $K$ satisfying these constraints.

Proof. Step 1. We first prove (14.23) when $\varphi = V_{\theta }$.

Let $S\subseteq X$ be a proper analytic subset, such that $V_{\theta }$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus S$. Such $S$ exists by 2.4.6. Fix holomorphic balls $B\Subset B'\Subset X\setminus S$.

By proposition 1.2.3 and 14.1.5, we can take an increasing sequence $(\eta _ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

\[ \eta _ j\nearrow P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)\textup{ a.e.},\quad \eta _ j|_ K\leq v\textup{ for all }j\geq 1. \]

Note that $P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)\sim V_{\theta }$ by 14.1.10. Take $C>0$ so that

\[ \left.\left( P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v) \right)\right|_ K-C\leq v. \]

Then we may replace $\eta _ j$ by $\eta _ j\lor (P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)-C)$ and assume that $\eta _ j\sim V_{\theta }$ for each $j$. In particular, $\eta _ j$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus S$.

For each $j\geq 1$, let $\gamma _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be the balayage of $\eta _ j$ on $B$, defined in proposition 1.5.9. Note that $(\gamma _ j)_ j$ is increasing due to proposition 1.2.15.

For the first equality in (14.23), we may assume that $B\Subset X\setminus (S \cup K)$. It suffices to show that $\theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)}^ n$ does not charge $B$.

Since $B$ lies outside $K$ by assumption, we also have $\gamma _ j|_ K=\eta _ j|_ K \leq v$ for all $j\geq 1$. In particular, $\gamma _ j\leq P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)$.

It follows that $\gamma _ j \nearrow P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)$ almost everywhere as well. Testing against non-negative continuous functions supported in $B$ and using theorem 2.4.7, we find that $\theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)}$ does not charge $B$, as desired.

We next prove the second equality in (14.23). The proof is essentially the same as that of 2.4.13.

It suffices to show that $\theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)}$ does not charge the set $K\cap \{ P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)|_ K<v\} $.

Let $x\in (X\setminus S) \cap K$ be a point such that $P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)(x)<v(x)-\epsilon $ for some $\epsilon >0$. We may assume that $x\in B$ and $B'$ is small enough, so that

  1. $P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)|_{\overline{B'}}<v(x)-\epsilon $.

  2. $v|_{\overline{B'}\cap K}>v(x)-\epsilon $.

By 1, we have $\gamma _ j|_{\partial B}\leq v(x)-\epsilon $, hence by proposition 1.2.15, $\gamma _ j|_ B\leq v(x)-\epsilon $. By 2, we have $\gamma _ j|_{B\cap K}\leq v|_{B\cap K}$. On the other hand, $\gamma _ j|_{K\setminus B}=\eta _{j}|_{K\setminus B}\leq v|_{K\setminus B}$. Therefore, $\gamma _ j|_ K\leq v$. It follows again that $\gamma _ j\leq P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)$. Therefore, we conclude as in the previous paragraph that $\gamma _ j\nearrow P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)$. Thus, testing against non-negative continuous functions supported in $B$, we conclude that $\theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)}$ does not charge $B$ again by theorem 2.4.7.

Step 2. We handle the general case.

By corollary 14.1.11,

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v) = P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ]\left(P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)\right). \]

Now corollary 3.1.8 gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}^ n \leq & \mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v) = P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)\right\} } \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)}^ n \\ \leq & \mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)|_ K=P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)|_ K=v\right\} }\, \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[V_{\theta }](v)}^ n, \end{aligned} \]

where in the second inequality we have used Step 1. Hence (14.24) follows, and a fortiori (14.23) follows as well. $\square$

Corollary 14.2.3. Let $v\in C^0(K)$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

14.27
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \int _{(X\setminus K)\cup \left\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)<v\right\} }\theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}=& 0,\\ \int _{(X\setminus K)\cup \left\{ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)<v\right\} }\theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}=& 0. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

Proof. The first equation in (14.27) follows from 14.2.2. For the second, it suffices to observe that

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)=P_{\theta ,K}\left[P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)=P_{\theta ,K}\left[P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\right](v) \]

by corollary 14.1.9. $\square$

Definition 14.2.4. Given $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, we define the partial equilibrium energy functional $\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }\colon C^0(K)\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ as follows: given $v \in C^0(K)$, we set

14.30
\begin{equation} \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v)\coloneqq E_{\theta }^{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}}\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right). \end{equation}
Recall that the energy $E_{\theta }^{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}}$ functional is defined in definition 3.1.35.

Note that by 14.1.10, we have

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\in \mathcal{E}^{\infty }\left(X,\theta ;P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\right), \]

so $\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v)\in \mathbb {R}$.

Proposition 14.2.5. Let $v,f \in C^0(K)$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then $\mathbb {R}\ni t \mapsto \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v+tf)$ is differentiable and

14.31
\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v+tf) = \int _ K f\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)} \end{equation}

for all $t\in \mathbb {R}$.

Proof. We may assume that $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-model by replacing $\varphi $ by $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$.

Note that it suffices to prove (14.31) at $t=0$, which is equivalent to

14.32
\begin{equation} \lim _{t\to 0}\frac{E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)\right)-E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right)}{t}=\int _ K f\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}. \end{equation}

It suffices to prove the right derivative at $0$ for an arbitrary $f$; applying the same argument to $-f$ then gives the left derivative. In other words, we need to prove that

14.33
\begin{equation} \lim _{t\to 0+}\frac{E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)\right)-E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right)}{t}=\int _ K f\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}. \end{equation}

We first reduce the proof of (14.33) to the case $f\leq 0$. Assume that this case is known. Choose $A\in \mathbb R$ such that $f-A\leq 0$ on $K$. Since for any $t>0$,

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}\Bigl ( v+t(f-A) \Bigr )= P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(v+tf)-tA, \]

then we have

\[ \int _ K f\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}=\int _ K (f-A)\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}+A\int _ X \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}. \]

By (3.71), for any $t>0$,

\[ E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\Bigl (P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}\left(v+t(f-A)\right)\Bigr )=E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)\right)-tA\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

So the limit in (14.33) exists and is equal to the left-hand side of (14.33) with $f-A$ in place of $f$ plus $A\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n$. So (14.33) for $f-A$ implies the desired formula for $f$ in view of 14.1.10 and the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. Hence we may assume that $f\leq 0$.

Fix $t>0$. Set

\[ \eta _ t\coloneqq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(v+tf),\quad \eta _0\coloneqq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(v). \]

By the comparison principle (3.59) and proposition 3.1.45, we find

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta }^{\varphi }(\eta _ t)-E_{\theta }^{\varphi }(\eta _0) ={}& \frac{1}{n+1}\sum _{i=0}^ n\int _ X(\eta _ t-\eta _0)\, \theta _{\eta _ t}^ i\wedge \theta _{\eta _0}^{n-i}\\ \leq {}& \int _ X(\eta _ t-\eta _0)\, \theta _{\eta _0}^ n. \end{aligned} \]

By 14.2.2 and corollary 14.1.9, the measure $\theta _{\eta _0}^ n$ is carried by $K\cap \{ \eta _0=v\} $, and the measure $\theta _{\eta _ t}^ n$ is carried by $K\cap \{ \eta _ t=v+tf\} $. On $K\cap \{ \eta _0=v\} $, we have $\eta _ t\leq v+tf=\eta _0+tf$, hence

\[ \eta _ t-\eta _0\leq tf. \]

On $K\cap \{ \eta _ t=v+tf\} $, we have $\eta _0\leq v$, hence

\[ \eta _ t-\eta _0\geq tf. \]

It follows that

\[ \int _ X(\eta _ t-\eta _0)\, \theta _{\eta _0}^ n \leq t\int _ K f\, \theta _{\eta _0}^ n \]

and

\[ \int _ X(\eta _ t-\eta _0)\, \theta _{\eta _ t}^ n \geq t\int _ K f\, \theta _{\eta _ t}^ n. \]

Thus, letting $t\to 0+$, we get the inequality

\[ \varlimsup _{t\to 0+}\frac{E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)\right)-E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right)}{t}\leq \int _ K f\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}. \]

Similarly, for $t>0$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} & E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)\right)-E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right)\\ \geq & \int _ X \left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)-P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right)\, \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)}^ n\\ \geq & t\int _ K f\, \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)}^ n. \end{aligned} \]

Letting $t\to 0+$, we find

\[ \varliminf _{t\to 0+}\frac{E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)\right)-E_{\theta }^{\varphi }\left(P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right)}{t}\geq \int _ K f\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}. \]

Here we used the following elementary continuity consequence:

\[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)-t\| f\| _{C^0(K)} \leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf) \leq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v) \]

for $t>0$, and hence $P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf) \nearrow P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)$ almost everywhere when $t\to 0+$. Next we claim that

\[ \int _ K f\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}\leq \lim _{t\to 0+} \int _ K f\, \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v+tf)}^ n. \]

Choose a continuous extension $\tilde f\in C^0(X)$ of $f$. Since 14.2.2, corollary 14.1.9 show that all the measures appearing here are carried by $K$, the integrals over $K$ against $f$ are the same as the integrals over $X$ against $\tilde f$. Thus corollary 2.4.9 applies.

We conclude (14.32). $\square$

Lemma 14.2.6. Assume that $\{ \theta \} $ represents a big cohomology class. Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$ and a weighted subset $(K,v)$ of $X$. There exists a uniformly bounded increasing sequence $(v^-_ j)_ j$ in $C^\infty (X)$ and a uniformly bounded decreasing sequence $(v^+_ j)_ j$ in $C^\infty (X)$, such that

14.39
\begin{equation} \sup _ X |v_ j^-|\leq C,\quad \sup _ X |v_ j^+|\leq C \end{equation}

for some constant $C$ depending only on $\| v\| _{C^0(K)}$, $X$, $K$ and $\theta +\omega $. Furthermore, for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and any $\delta \in [0,1]$ we have the following:

  1. We have

    \[ P_{\theta +\delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v_ j^+) \searrow P_{\theta +\delta \omega ,K}[\varphi ](v), \]

    and

    \[ P_{\theta +\delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v^-_ j) \nearrow P_{\theta +\delta \omega ,K}[\varphi ](v) \]

    almost everywhere.

  2. Suppose in addition that $\varphi $ is $\mathcal{I}$-good, then

    \[ \lim _{j\to \infty }\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(v_ j^-)=\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v),\quad \lim _{j\to \infty }\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(v_ j^+)=\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v). \]

Proof. Step 1. First we construct $(v^-_ j)_ j$.

Let

\[ C_{K,v} \coloneqq \sup \left\{ \sup _ X \eta : \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\omega ), \eta |_ K \leq v\right\} . \]

Since $K$ is non-pluripolar, we have $C_{K,v} \in \mathbb {R}$ by 1.5.5. Set

\[ M_{K,v}\coloneqq \max \{ C_{K,v}+1,\sup _ K v\} . \]

Now define $\widetilde v \colon X \to \mathbb {R}$ as

\[ \widetilde{v}(x)=\left\{ \begin{aligned} v(x),& \quad x\in K;\\ M_{K,v},& \quad x\in X\setminus K. \end{aligned} \right. \]

Since $M_{K,v}\geq \sup _ K v$, the function $\widetilde v$ is lower semicontinuous. Therefore there exists an increasing and uniformly bounded sequence $(v^-_ j)_ j$ in $C^\infty (X)$ such that $v^-_ j \nearrow \widetilde v$ and

\[ \| v_ j^-\| _{C^0(X)}\leq \| \tilde{v}\| _{L^{\infty }(X)}+1. \]

The right-hand side can clearly be bounded by a constant as in (14.39).

Step 2. We construct $(v_ j^+)_ j$.

Since

\[ h \coloneqq P_{\theta + \omega ,K}[V_{\theta +\omega }](v) \lor \left(\inf _ K v -1\right) \]

is usc, there exists a decreasing and uniformly bounded sequence $(v^+_ j)_ j$ in $C^\infty (X)$ such that $v^+_ j \searrow h$. We may guarantee that

\[ \| v^+_ j\| _{C^0(X)}\leq \| h\| _{L^{\infty }(X)}+1. \]

Note that $\inf _ K v-1\leq h\leq C_{K,v} \lor (\inf _ K v-1)$. So (14.39) is satisfied.

Step 3. We finish the proof. Fix $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\delta \in [0,1]$.

Note that 2 follows from corollary 14.1.9, 14.1.10, proposition 3.1.47 and 1. Let us prove 1.

Step 3.1. We prove 1 for $(v^-_ j)_ j$.

Observe that $P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v^-_ j)$ is increasing in $j\geq 1$, and

\[ P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ]\left(v^-_ j\right)\leq P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,K}[\varphi ](v). \]

We claim that

14.42
\begin{equation} P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ]\left(v^-_ j\right) \nearrow P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,K}[\varphi ](v)\quad \textup{a.e.}. \end{equation}

Let $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\delta \omega )$ be a potential such that $\eta \preceq \varphi $ and $\eta |_ K\leq v$. Take some $\epsilon >0$. Then, since $\eta $ is upper semicontinuous, $\widetilde v$ is lower semicontinuous and $\eta < \epsilon +\widetilde v$, compactness gives $j_0>0$ such that $\eta < \epsilon + v^-_ j$ for $j \geq j_0$. Hence,

\[ \eta \leq \epsilon +P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v^-_ j) \]

for $j\geq j_0$. Therefore,

\[ \eta \leq \operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq 1}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ]\left(v^-_ j\right)+\epsilon . \]

Letting $\epsilon \to 0+$, we find

\[ \eta \leq \operatorname *{sup}_{j\geq 1}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ]\left(v^-_ j\right). \]

Since $\eta $ is arbitrary, (14.42) follows.

Step 3.2. We prove 1 for $(v^+_ j)_ j$.

Note that $P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v^+_ j)$ is decreasing in $j$, and

14.43
\begin{equation} \chi \coloneqq \lim _{j\to \infty } P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ]\left(v^+_ j\right) \geq P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,K}[\varphi ](v). \end{equation}

In particular, $\chi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\delta \omega )_{>0}$, since $P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,K}[\varphi ](v)$ has the same singularity types as $P_{\theta + \delta \omega }[\varphi ]$ by 14.1.10. Observe that

\[ P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ]\left(v_ j^+\right)\leq v_ j^+ \]

for all $j\geq 1$, since $v_ j^+$ is continuous. Thus, $\chi \leq h$. On the other hand, $h|_ K\leq v$ quasi-everywhere. So in particular, $\chi |_{K}\leq v$ quasi-everywhere. On the other hand,

\[ \chi \leq P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,X}[\varphi ]\left(v^+_1\right)\sim _ P \varphi \]

by 14.1.10. Hence, by corollary 14.1.8,

\[ \chi \leq P_{\theta + \delta \omega ,K}[\varphi ](v). \]

Therefore, (14.43) is an equality. $\square$

Proposition 14.2.7. Let $(K,v)$ be a weighted subset of $X$. Let $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ be a sequence in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then the following hold:

  1. If $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is decreasing, and $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $, then

    14.44
    \begin{equation} P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}(v) \searrow P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v),\quad P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi _ j](v) \searrow P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v). \end{equation}

  2. If $\varphi _ j \nearrow \varphi $ almost everywhere then

    \[ P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}(v) \nearrow P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\textup{ a.e.},\quad P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi _ j](v) \nearrow P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v) \textup{ a.e.}. \]

Proof. We only give the proof for the relative $\mathcal{I}$-envelopes; the proof for the relative $P$-envelopes is identical, replacing $P_{\theta }[\bullet ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ by $P_{\theta }[\bullet ]$ and using corollary 14.1.8 for the final candidate condition.

Set

\[ \eta _ j\coloneqq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}(v),\quad \eta \coloneqq P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v). \]

By 14.1.10, $d_ S(\eta _ j,P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}})=0$ and $d_ S(\eta ,P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}})=0$ for all $j$. Moreover, theorem 6.2.27 gives

\[ P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}} \]

in case 1, and the same convergence holds in case 2 by corollary 6.2.9 and theorem 6.2.27. Consequently, $\eta _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\eta $ in both cases.

Assume first that $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ is decreasing. Then $(\eta _ j)_ j$ is decreasing and $\eta _ j\geq \eta $. Let $\eta _\infty \coloneqq \inf _ j \eta _ j$. The Monge–Ampère masses of $\eta _ j$ converge to the mass of $\eta $, by the preceding $d_ S$-convergence and theorem 6.2.17. Since $\eta _ j\geq \eta _\infty \geq \eta $, the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 gives

\[ \int _ X\theta _{\eta _\infty }^ n=\int _ X\theta _\eta ^ n. \]

Hence $\eta _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\eta _\infty $ by corollary 6.2.11. The uniqueness of the $d_ S$-limit gives $d_ S(\eta _\infty ,\eta )=0$. Since $\eta _\infty \geq \eta $, proposition 6.2.4 implies $\eta _\infty \sim _ P \eta $. In particular, $\eta _\infty \preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\varphi $ by corollary 6.1.9. The condition $\eta _\infty \leq v$ on $K$ quasi-everywhere is inherited from the $\eta _ j$'s, so $\eta _\infty $ is a candidate for $P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)$. Thus $\eta _\infty \leq \eta $, and the reverse inequality was already observed. This proves the $\mathcal{I}$-part of 1.

Assume next that $\varphi _ j\nearrow \varphi $ almost everywhere. Then $(\eta _ j)_ j$ is increasing and $\eta _ j\leq \eta $. Put $\tilde\eta \coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}_ j \eta _ j$. By corollary 14.1.6 and corollary 6.2.9, we have $\eta _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\tilde\eta $. The $d_ S$-convergence above therefore gives $d_ S(\tilde\eta ,\eta )=0$, hence $\tilde\eta \sim _ P \eta $ by proposition 6.2.4. In particular, $\tilde\eta \preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\varphi $ by corollary 6.1.9. Also $\tilde\eta \leq v$ on $K$ quasi-everywhere by proposition 1.2.5. Hence $\tilde\eta $ is a candidate for $P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)$, so $\tilde\eta \leq \eta $. Since $\eta _ j\leq \eta $ for all $j$, this proves $\eta _ j\nearrow \eta $ almost everywhere. $\square$

14.3 Quantization of partial equilibrium measures

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$, let $L$ be a pseudo-effective line bundle on $X$, and let $h$ be a Hermitian metric on $L$. Set $\theta =c_1(L,h)$. Let $(T,h_ T)$ be a Hermitian line bundle on $X$.

14.3.1 Bernstein–Markov measures

Let $K\subseteq X$ be a closed non-pluripolar subset, and $v\colon K\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ be a measurable function.

Definition 14.3.1. Let $\mu $ be a Borel probability measure on $K$. We introduce the following functions on $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T)$ ($k\geq 1$), with values possibly equal to $\infty $:

14.45
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} N^ k_{v,\mu }(s) \coloneqq & \left(\int _ K h^ k \otimes h_ T(s,s) \mathrm{e}^{-kv} \, \mathrm{d}\mu \right)^{1/2},\\ N^ k_{v,K}(s) \coloneqq & \sup _{K\setminus \{ v=-\infty \} } \left( h^ k \otimes h_ T(s,s)\mathrm{e}^{-kv}\right)^{1/2}. \end{aligned} \end{equation}
Observe that if $\mu $ puts no mass on $\{ v=-\infty \} $ then we always have
14.48
\begin{equation} N^ k_{v,\mu }(s)\leq N^ k_{v,K}(s). \end{equation}
We start with the following elementary observation:

Lemma 14.3.2. Let $v_1,v_2\colon K\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty )$ be two measurable functions such that

  1. $v_1\leq v_2$;

  2. $\{ v_1=-\infty \} =\{ v_2=-\infty \} $.

Then for any $s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes T)$ ($k\geq 1$), we have

\[ N_{v_1,K}^ k(s)\geq N_{v_2,K}^ k(s). \]

Proof. This follows immediately from the definition (14.45). $\square$

Definition 14.3.3. Let $(K,v)$ be a weighted subset of $X$. A Borel probability measure $\mu $ on $K$ is Bernstein–Markov with respect to $(K,v)$ if for each $\epsilon >0$, there is a constant $C_\epsilon >0$ such that

14.49
\begin{equation} N^ k_{v,K}(s) \leq C_\epsilon \mathrm{e}^{\epsilon k} N^ k_{v,\mu }(s) \end{equation}

for any $s \in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T)$ and any $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. We write $\mathrm{BM}(K,v)$ for the set of Bernstein–Markov measures with respect to $(K,v)$.

As pointed out in [BBWN11], any volume form on $X$ is Bernstein–Markov with respect to $(X,v)$, with $v \in C^\infty (X)$.

Proposition 14.3.4. Assume that $(K,v)$ is a weighted subset of $X$. Then

  1. $N^ k_{v,K}$ is a norm on $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes T)$.

  2. For any $\mu \in \mathrm{BM}(K,v)$, $N^ k_{v,\mu }$ is a norm on $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes T)$.

Proof. 1 As $v$ is bounded, $N^ k_{v,K}$ is clearly finite on $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes T)$. In order to show that it is a norm, it suffices to show that for any $s\in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k\otimes T)$, $N^ k_{v,K}(s)=0$ implies that $s=0$. In fact, we have $s|_ K=0$, hence $s=0$ by the connectedness of $X$ and the assumption that $K$ is non-pluripolar.

2 As $v$ is bounded, clearly $N^ k_{v,\mu }$ is finite and satisfies the triangle inequality. Non-degeneracy follows from the fact that $N^ k_{v,K}$ is a norm and (14.49). $\square$

14.3.2 Partial Bergman kernels

In this section, we fix a weighted subset $(K,v)$ of $X$ and $\mu \in \mathrm{BM}(K,v)$.

Definition 14.3.5. For any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, we introduce the partial Bergman kernels of $\varphi $ (with respect to $(K,v)$) as follows: For any $k\geq 1$, we introduce

14.50
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} B^ k_{v,\varphi , \mu }(x) \coloneqq \sup \left\{ h^ k \otimes h_ T(s,s)\mathrm{e}^{-kv}(x):s \in \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right),\right.\\ \left. N^ k_{v,\mu }(s) \leq 1 \right\} ,\quad x\in K. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

We extend $B^ k_{v,\varphi , \mu }$ to the whole $X$ by setting it to be $0$ outside $K$.

The partial Bergman measures of $\varphi $ (with respect to $(K,v)$) are defined as

14.53
\begin{equation} \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu } \coloneqq \frac{n!}{k^ n} B^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu } \, \mathrm{d}\mu \end{equation}

for each $k\geq 1$.

Remark 14.3.6. Recall that in the definition of $\beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu }$, the singular metric $\varphi $ only appears through the conditions $s \in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))$. The pointwise norm used to define $\beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu }$ and $N^ k_{v,\mu }$ is defined via $v$ not $\varphi $.

This leads to the following seemingly paradoxical consequence, if $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \preceq _{\mathcal{I}}\psi $, then

\[ \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu }\leq \beta ^ k_{v,\psi ,\mu }\quad \forall \, k\geq 1. \]

We shall apply this consequence without further mention in the sequel.

Observe that

\[ \int _ X \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu }=\frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,T\otimes L^ k \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right). \]

In particular,

14.54
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty }\int _ X \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu }=\operatorname{vol}\left(\theta _{\varphi }\right)=\int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n \end{equation}
by theorem 7.4.2 and 14.1.10. The goal of this section is to improve this result to the convergence of the Bergman kernels themselves:

Theorem 14.3.7. Suppose that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then

14.55
\begin{equation} \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu } \rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n,\quad \textup{as }k\to \infty . \end{equation}

The proof will be given at the end of this subsection. We shall prove a few preliminaries at first.

Take a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$ so that

\[ \int _ X \omega ^ n = 1. \]

Proposition 14.3.8. Assume here that $K=X$. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a potential with analytic singularities such that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. If $v \in C^\infty (X)$, then

14.56
\begin{equation} \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\omega ^ n} \rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n=\theta _{P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v)}^ n\quad \textup{as }k\to \infty . \end{equation}

Proof. The equality part in (14.56) follows from corollary 14.1.9. It remains to prove the weak convergence.

Let $\tau $ be the weak limit of a subsequence of $(\beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\omega ^ n})_ k$. In view of (14.54), it suffices to show that

14.57
\begin{equation} \tau \leq \theta _{P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v)}^ n. \end{equation}

We can obtain an upper bound of $\tau $ via the observation:

\[ \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\omega ^ n} \leq \beta ^ k_{v,V_{\theta },\omega ^ n}. \]

After replacing $\varphi $ by $V_{\theta }$, the corresponding problem is handled by [Theorem 1.2, Ber11], giving us

\[ \beta ^ k_{v,V_{\theta },\omega ^ n} \rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta ,X}[V_{\theta }](v)}^ n=\mathds {1}_{\left\{ v = P_{\theta ,X}[V_{\theta }](v)\right\} }\, \theta _ v^ n \quad \textup{as }k\to \infty , \]

where the last identity is due to proposition 3.1.21.

We obtain that

14.58
\begin{equation} \tau \leq \mathds {1}_{\left\{ v = P_{\theta ,X}[V_{\theta }](v)\right\} }\, \theta _ v^ n. \end{equation}

Now observe that it suffices to show that

14.59
\begin{equation} \tau \left\{ P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v) < v\right\} =0. \end{equation}

Assuming this, then (14.58) further gives

\[ \tau \leq \mathds {1}_{\left\{ P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v)= v\right\} }\, \theta _ v^ n= \theta _{P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v)}^ n, \]

where the equality follows from proposition 3.1.21, and (14.57) follows.

It now remains to argue (14.59). Fix a Kähler form $\omega $ on $X$. Observe that

\[ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v) \sim \varphi \]

for all $\epsilon \geq 0$ by 14.1.10 and proposition 3.2.15. The family $P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v)$ is decreasing as $\epsilon \to 0+$. Let

\[ \chi \coloneqq \inf _{\epsilon >0}P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v). \]

Then $\chi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ since $\chi \geq P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v)$. Moreover $\chi \leq v$ and $\chi \preceq \varphi $. Hence

\[ \chi \leq P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v). \]

Thus, equality holds, and

\[ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v)\searrow P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v). \]

In other words,

\[ \left\{ P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](v) < v\right\} =\bigcup _{\epsilon \in \mathbb {Q}_{>0}}\left\{ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v) < v\right\} . \]

So in order to show (14.59), we may fix $\epsilon >0$ and argue that

14.60
\begin{equation} \tau \left\{ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v) < v\right\} =0. \end{equation}

Let $k\geq 1$, and $s \in \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal I(k\varphi ))$ be a section such that $N^ k_{v,\omega ^ n}(s) \leq 1$. Then by [Lemma 4.1, Ber11], there exists $C>0$ such that

\[ h^ k\otimes h_ T(s,s)\mathrm{e}^{-kv} \leq B^ k_{v,\varphi ,\omega ^ n} \leq B^ k_{v,V_{\theta },\omega ^ n} \leq k^ n C. \]

This implies that

\[ \frac{1}{k}\log h^ k \otimes h_ T(s,s) \leq v + \frac{\log C }{k} + n \frac{\log k}{ k }. \]

We define $\varphi _ k$ as in proposition 1.8.13. Take $\alpha _ k\nearrow 1$ as in proposition 1.8.13. After adding a constant to $\varphi $, we may assume that $\varphi \leq 0$ on $X$. After replacing $\alpha _ k$ by a subsequence, we also have $\alpha _ k\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\epsilon \omega )$ for all $k>0$, and $\alpha _ k\varphi \searrow \varphi $. Then

\[ \frac{1}{k}\log h^ k \otimes h_ T(s,s)\preceq \varphi _ k\preceq \alpha _ k\varphi . \]

We notice that $\frac{1}{k}\log h^ k \otimes h_ T(s,s) \in \mathrm{PSH}(X, \theta + \epsilon \omega )$ for all $k \geq k_0(\epsilon )$. Indeed,

\[ \frac{1}{k}\log h^ k\otimes h_ T(s,s) \in \mathrm{PSH}\left(X,\theta + k^{-1} c_1(T,h_ T)\right) \]

by proposition 1.8.4.

In particular,

\[ \frac{1}{k}\log h^ k \otimes h_ T(s,s)-\frac{\log C}{k}-n\frac{\log k}{k}\leq P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\alpha _ k \varphi ](v). \]

Now taking supremum over all candidates $s$, we obtain that

14.61
\begin{equation} B^ k_{v,\varphi ,\omega ^ n} \leq C k^ n \mathrm{e}^{k \left(P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\alpha _ k \varphi ](v)-v\right)},\quad k \geq k_0. \end{equation}

Now we prove (14.60). First observe that $\alpha _ k\varphi \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $ by 6.2.14. Thus, by proposition 14.2.7,

\[ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\alpha _ k \varphi ](v) \searrow P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v) \]

and we get that

\[ \bigcup _{k=1}^{\infty }\left\{ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\alpha _ k \varphi ](v) < v\right\} =\left\{ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\varphi ](v) < v\right\} . \]

It suffices to show that $\tau $ does not put mass on the set $\{ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\alpha _ m \varphi ](v) < v\} $ for each fixed $m$. Fix a positive rational $\delta >0$, it suffices to show that $\tau $ puts no mass on $F\coloneqq \{ P_{\theta +\epsilon \omega ,X}[\alpha _ m \varphi ](v) < v-\delta \} $. Note that $F$ is open, and (14.61) gives $\beta ^\ell _{v,\varphi ,\omega ^ n}(F)\to 0$. Therefore, $\tau $ puts no mass on $F$ as well, proving (14.60). $\square$

Definition 14.3.9. Take $k\geq 1$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, let $\operatorname{Norm}\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))$ be the space of norms on the vector space $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))$.

Let $\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi } \colon \operatorname{Norm}\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )) \to \mathbb R$ be the partial Donaldson functional:

14.62
\begin{equation} \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(H) = \frac{n!}{k^{n+1}} \log \frac{\operatorname{vol}\{ s \in \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right) : H(s) \leq 1\} }{\operatorname{vol}\left\{ s\in \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right) : N^ k_{0,\omega ^ n}(s) \leq 1\right\} }, \end{equation}

where $\operatorname{vol}$ is a Lebesgue measure on $\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))$. The normalization does not matter.

Proposition 14.3.10. Let $w,w' \in C^0(X)$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a potential with analytic singularities such that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. Then

14.63
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty } \left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n}) - \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w',\omega ^ n}) \right)= \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w)-\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w'). \end{equation}

In particular,

14.64
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty }\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n})=\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w)\, . \end{equation}

Proof. First observe that by proposition 14.3.4, for any $k\geq 1$, $N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n}$ and $N^ k_{w',\omega ^ n}$ are both norms, hence the expressions inside the limit in (14.63) make sense.

We first assume that $w,w'\in C^\infty (X)$. To start, we make the following observation:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n}) - \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w',\omega ^ n})& = \int _0^1 \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w' + t(w-w'),\omega ^ n})\, \mathrm{d}t\\ & = \int _0^1 \int _ X (w-w') \, \beta ^ k_{w' + t(w-w'),\varphi ,\omega ^ n}\, \mathrm{d}t. \end{aligned} \]

The second equality follows from differentiating the Gram matrix of the Hilbert norm $N^ k_{w'+t(w-w'),\omega ^ n}$:

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\mathcal L_{k,\varphi } \left(N^ k_{w'+t(w-w'),\omega ^ n}\right)=\int _ X (w-w')\, \beta ^ k_{w'+t(w-w'),\varphi ,\omega ^ n}. \]

By proposition 14.3.8, we have

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty }\int _ X (w-w') \, \beta ^ k_{w' + t(w-w'),\varphi ,\omega ^ n}=\int _ X (w-w')\, \theta _{P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](w' + t(w-w'))}^ n. \]

By theorem 7.4.2, we have

\[ \bigl |\int _ X (w-w')\beta ^ k_{w' + t(w-w'),\varphi ,\omega ^ n}\bigr | \leq C \sup _ X |w - w'|. \]

Hence, by the dominated convergence theorem we obtain that

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim _{k\to \infty } \left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n}) - \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w',\omega ^ n})\right) & = \int _0^1 \int _ X (w-w') \, \theta _{P_{\theta ,X}[\varphi ](w' + t(w-w'))}^ n \, \mathrm{d}t\\ & = \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w)-\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w'), \end{aligned} \]

where in the last line we have used proposition 14.2.5.

This proves (14.63) for smooth weights.

For general $w,w'\in C^0(X)$, choose smooth functions $w_ j,w'_ j$ converging uniformly to $w,w'$, respectively. If $\| w_ j-w\| _{C^0}\leq \delta _ j$, then

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-k\delta _ j/2}N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n}\leq N^ k_{w_ j,\omega ^ n}\leq \mathrm{e}^{k\delta _ j/2}N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n}, \]

and hence

\[ \left|\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w_ j,\omega ^ n})-\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,\omega ^ n})\right| \leq \frac{n!}{k^ n}h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes T\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)\delta _ j. \]

The same estimate holds for $w'_ j$ and $w'$. Moreover, proposition 14.2.5 shows that the energy side is Lipschitz with respect to the uniform norm of the weight. Letting first $k\to \infty $ in the smooth case, and then $j\to \infty $, proves (14.63) for continuous weights. Equation (14.64) again follows by taking $w'=0$. $\square$

Lemma 14.3.11. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Let $(K,v)$ be a weighted subset of $X$. Let $\mu \in \mathrm{BM}(K,v)$. Then

14.69
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty } \big ( \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K}) - \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,\mu })\big ) = 0\, . \end{equation}

Proof. By (14.48) and (14.49), for every $\epsilon >0$ and all $k\geq 1$,

\[ N^ k_{v,\mu }\leq N^ k_{v,K}\leq C_\epsilon \mathrm{e}^{\epsilon k}N^ k_{v,\mu }. \]

The corresponding unit balls therefore satisfy inclusions with radii differing by at most the factor $C_\epsilon \mathrm{e}^{\epsilon k}$. Taking logarithmic volumes and multiplying by $n!k^{-n-1}$ gives

\[ \left|\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K})-\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,\mu })\right| \leq \frac{2n!}{k^{n+1}} h^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes T\otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right)(\log C_\epsilon +\epsilon k). \]

The right-hand side has limsup at most $2\epsilon \operatorname{vol}(\theta _\varphi )$ by theorem 7.4.2. Letting $\epsilon \to 0+$ proves (14.69). $\square$

Corollary 14.3.12. Let $w \in C^0(X)$, $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a potential with analytic singularities such that $\theta _\varphi $ is a Kähler current. Then

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty }\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X})=\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w). \]

Proof. This follows from 14.3.11 and proposition 14.3.10: indeed $\omega ^ n \in \mathrm{BM}(X,0)$, hence $\omega ^ n\in \mathrm{BM}(X,w)$ as $w$ is bounded. $\square$

It would be helpful to consider the following auxiliary functions:

\[ \begin{aligned} P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\coloneqq & \sup \Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq v \textup{ and } \eta \preceq \varphi \Bigr \} ,\\ P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\coloneqq & \sup \Bigl \{ \eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): \eta |_ K \leq v \textup{ and } \eta \preceq _{\mathcal{I}} \varphi \Bigr \} . \end{aligned} \]
We note the following maximum principles that follow from the above definitions:

Lemma 14.3.13. Let $v\in C^0(K)$, $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Assume that $\eta \preceq \varphi $. Then

14.72
\begin{equation} \sup _ K (\eta -v) = \sup _{\{ \eta \neq -\infty \} } \left(\eta - P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\right)=\sup _{\left\{ P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\neq -\infty \right\} } \left(\eta - P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\right). \end{equation}

Proof. We prove the first equality first. We write $S=\{ \eta =-\infty \} $.

By definition, $P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)|_ K\leq v$, so

\[ \left.\left(\eta -P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\right)\right|_{K\setminus S}\geq \eta |_{K\setminus S}-v|_{K\setminus S}\, . \]

This implies that

\[ \sup _ K (\eta -v) \leq \sup _{X\setminus S} \left(\eta - P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\right). \]

Conversely, observe that $\sup _ K(\eta -v)>-\infty $ as $K$ is non-pluripolar. It follows that $\eta -\sup _ K (\eta -v)$ is a candidate in the definition of $P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)$. Hence,

14.73
\begin{equation} \eta -\sup _ K (\eta -v)\leq P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v). \end{equation}

This implies that

\[ \sup _ K (\eta -v) \geq \sup _{X\setminus S} \left(\eta - P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\right), \]

finishing the proof of the first identity in (14.72).

In order to prove the latter identity, observe that

\[ \left\{ P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v) =-\infty \right\} \subseteq S \]

as a consequence of (14.73). On $\{ P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)\neq -\infty \} \cap S$, we have $\eta -P'_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)=-\infty $. Hence the last equality of (14.72) also follows. $\square$

Proposition 14.3.14. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a potential with analytic singularities such that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. Let $(K,v)$, $(K',v')$ be two weighted subsets of $X$. Then

14.74
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty } \left( \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K}) - \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v',K'}) \right) = \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v)-\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K'}^{\varphi }(v'). \end{equation}

In particular,

14.75
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty }\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K})=\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v). \end{equation}

Proof. First observe that by proposition 14.3.4, for any $k>0$, $N^ k_{v,K}$ and $N^ k_{v',K'}$ are both norms, hence the expressions inside the limit in (14.74) make sense. Moreover, (14.75) is just a special case of (14.74) for $K'=X$ and $v'= 0$.

To prove (14.74) it is enough to show that for any fixed $w \in C^\infty (X)$ we have

14.76
\begin{equation} \lim _{k\to \infty } \big ( \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K}) - \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X}) \big ) = \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v) - \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w)\, . \end{equation}

For $\epsilon \in (0,1)$ small enough, $\theta _{(1-\epsilon )\varphi }$ is still a Kähler current. Let us fix such $\epsilon $, along with an arbitrary $\epsilon ' \in (0,1)$.

Let $(v^-_{j})_ j$ and $(v^+_{j})_ j$ be the sequences of smooth functions constructed in 14.2.6 for the data $(K,v)$.

By proposition 1.8.13 there exists $k_0(\epsilon ,\epsilon ') \in \mathbb N$ such that

\[ \frac{1}{k} \log h^ k \otimes h_ T (s,s) \preceq (1-\epsilon )\varphi , \]

and $\frac{1}{k} \log h^ k \otimes h_ T (s,s) \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta + \epsilon '\omega )$ for any $s \in \mathrm{H}^0(X,T\otimes L^ k \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi ))$, as long as $k \geq k_0(\epsilon ,\epsilon ')$.

In particular, 14.3.13 gives that

\[ \begin{aligned} N^ k_{P'_{\theta + \epsilon '\omega ,K}[(1-\epsilon )\varphi ](v),X}(s)=& N^ k_{v,K}(s),\\ N^ k_{P'_{\theta + \epsilon '\omega ,X}[(1-\epsilon )\varphi ](v_{j}^-),X}(s)=& N^ k_{v_{j}^-,X}(s),\\ N^ k_{P'_{\theta + \epsilon '\omega ,X}[(1-\epsilon )\varphi ](v_{j}^+),X}(s)=& N^ k_{v^+_{j},X}(s). \end{aligned} \]

As

\[ P'_{\theta + \epsilon '\omega ,X}[(1-\epsilon )\varphi ](v_{j}^-) \leq P'_{\theta + \epsilon '\omega ,K}[(1-\epsilon )\varphi ](v) \leq P'_{\theta + \epsilon '\omega ,X}[(1-\epsilon )\varphi ](v_{j}^+), \]

where the second inequality follows from 14.2.6 and the fact that $P'=P$ for the global continuous obstacles $v_ j^\pm $. By 14.3.2 we have

\[ N^ k_{v^+_{j},X}(s) \leq N^ k_{v,K}(s) \leq N^ k_{v_{j}^-,X}(s), \quad s \in \mathrm{H}^0(X,T \otimes L^ k \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )), k \geq k_0(\epsilon ,\epsilon '). \]

Composing with $\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }$ we arrive at

\[ \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v^-_{j},X})\leq \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K})\leq \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v^+_{j},X})\, , \quad k \geq k_0(\epsilon ,\epsilon '). \]

For any $j>0$, by corollary 14.3.12 we get

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(v^-_{j}) - \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w)=& \lim _{k\to \infty } \left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v^-_{j},X})-\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X})\right)\\ \leq & \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K})- \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X})\right)\\ \leq & \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K})- \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X})\right)\\ \leq & \lim _{k\to \infty } \left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v^+_{j},X})-\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X})\right)\\ =& \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(v^+_{j}) - \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w). \end{aligned} \]

Using 14.2.6, we can let $j \to \infty $ to arrive at

\[ \begin{split} \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v) -\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w) & \leq \varliminf _{k\to \infty }\left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K})- \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X})\right)\\ & \leq \varlimsup _{k\to \infty }\left(\mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,K})- \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{w,X})\right)\\ & \leq \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v) -\mathcal{E}_{\theta ,X}^{\varphi }(w). \end{split} \]

Hence, (14.76) follows. $\square$

Corollary 14.3.15. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a potential with analytic singularities such that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. Then

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v,\mu })= \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v). \]

Proof. Our claim follows from proposition 14.3.14 and 14.3.11. $\square$

Proposition 14.3.16. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be a potential with analytic singularities such that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. Then

\[ \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi , \mu } \rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n=\theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}^ n\quad \textup{as }k\to \infty . \]

Proof. For $w\in C^0(X)$, let $f_ k,g\colon \mathbb {R}\rightarrow \mathbb {R}$ ($k\geq 1$) be defined as

\[ f_ k(t)\coloneqq \mathcal{L}_{k,\varphi }(N^ k_{v+tw,\mu }),\quad g(t)\coloneqq \mathcal{E}_{\theta ,K}^{\varphi }(v+tw). \]

By corollary 14.3.15, for all $t\in \mathbb {R}$, we have

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } f_ k(t)= g(t). \]

Here we use that $\mu \in \mathrm{BM}(K,v+tw)$ for all fixed $t$, which follows from $\mu \in \mathrm{BM}(K,v)$ and the boundedness of $tw$ on $K$. Note that $f_ k$ is concave by Hölder's inequality. For each $k$, the function $f_ k$ is differentiable and

\[ f_ k'(0)=\int _ X w\, \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu }. \]

This follows by differentiating the Gram matrix of the Hilbert norm $N^ k_{v+tw,\mu }$. The function $g$ is differentiable at $0$ by proposition 14.2.5, with

\[ g'(0)=\int _ K w\, \theta ^ n_{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(v)}. \]

Applying A.5.3 to the convex functions $-f_ k$ and $-g$ gives

\[ \lim _{k\to \infty } f_ k'(0)=g'(0), \]

which is equivalent to $\beta ^ k_{v, \varphi ,\mu } \rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ](v)}^ n$, by proposition 14.2.5. $\square$

Proposition 14.3.17. Suppose that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is such that $\theta _{\varphi }$ is a Kähler current. Let $(K,v)$ be a weighted subset of $X$ and $\mu \in \mathrm{BM}(K,v)$. Then

14.88
\begin{equation} \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu } \rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n\quad \textup{as } k\to \infty . \end{equation}

Proof. Let $\tau $ be the weak limit of a subsequence of $\beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu }$. We claim that

14.89
\begin{equation} \tau \leq \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n. \end{equation}

Observe that this claim implies the conclusion. In fact, by theorem 7.4.2, we have equality of the total masses, so equality holds in (14.89). As $\tau $ is an arbitrary cluster point of the sequence $(\beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu })_ k$, we get (14.88).

It remains to prove (14.89). Let $(\varphi _ j)$ be a quasi-equisingular approximation of $\varphi $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. We may assume that $\theta _{\varphi _ j}$ is a Kähler current for all $j\geq 1$. By corollary 7.1.10, we know that

\[ \varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

The quasi-equisingular approximation is decreasing, so applying the decreasing case of proposition 14.2.7 to the limit $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}$, and using the definition of the relative $\mathcal{I}$-envelope, we get

\[ P_{\theta ,K}\left[\varphi _ j\right]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\searrow P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v). \]

In particular,

14.90
\begin{equation} \lim _{j\to \infty } \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}\left[\varphi _ j\right]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n= \int _ X \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n. \end{equation}

Observe that

\[ \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu } \leq \beta ^ k_{v,\varphi _ j,\mu } \]

for any $k \geq 1$. As $\mu \in \mathrm{BM}(K,v)$, by proposition 14.3.16,

\[ \tau \leq \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi _ j]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n, \]

for any $j \geq 1$ fixed. By (14.90) and the semicontinuity theorem theorem 2.4.7, we have

\[ \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}\left[\varphi _ j\right]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n\rightharpoonup \theta _{P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)}^ n \]

as $j\to \infty $. Therefore, (14.89) follows. $\square$

Proof of theorem 14.3.7. By 14.1.10 and proposition 3.2.8, the three potentials below have the same multiplier ideals. Hence

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal{I}(k\varphi )\right) & = \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal I(kP_{\theta }[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}})\right)\\ & = \mathrm{H}^0\left(X,L^ k \otimes T \otimes \mathcal I\left(kP_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)\right)\right). \end{aligned} \]

This allows us to replace $\varphi $ with $P_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal{I}}(v)$.

By 2.4.19, there exists a sequence $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $\varphi _ j\nearrow \varphi $ a.e. and $\theta _{\varphi _ j}$ is a Kähler current for each $j\geq 1$. This gives

Let $\tau $ be the weak limit of a subsequence of $(\beta ^ k_{v,\varphi ,\mu })_ k$. Then by proposition 14.3.17,
