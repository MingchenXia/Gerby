11.34 Supporting probability measuresEE2EC5B3CDE0

EE2EC5B3CDE0

A supporting functional of the concave function $B_\varphi $ describes the first-order response of the scalar to a change of logarithmic density. Monotonicity and the translation rule force such a functional to be minus the integral against a probability measure. We work first on the space $C(X)$ of continuous real functions, with the uniform norm. For $F\in C(X)$, let $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ be the set of Borel probability measures $\nu $ on $X$ satisfying

4DA0C1CF8861 11.34.1
\begin{equation} B_\varphi (G)\leq B_\varphi (F)-\int _ X(G-F)\, \mathrm{d}\nu \qquad \text{for every }G\in C(X). \end{equation}

Thus $G\mapsto -\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $ is a supergradient of $B_\varphi $ at $F$. We call the members of $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ the supporting probabilities at $F$.

Theorem 11.34.2.86A7D85266AA For every $F\in C(X)$, the set $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is nonempty, convex and weakly compact. Every one of its measures is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$.

Proof. The function $C_\varphi =-B_\varphi $ is convex and Lipschitz. Fix $F\in C(X)$. For a direction $h\in C(X)$, the limit

\[ p(h)=\lim _{t\downarrow 0} \frac{C_\varphi (F+th)-C_\varphi (F)}{t} \]

exists and is finite: convexity makes the difference quotients monotone in $t>0$, and their absolute values are at most $\| h\| _\infty $. Clearly $p$ is positively homogeneous. Given another direction $k\in C(X)$, apply convexity at the midpoint of $F+2th$ and $F+2tk$ and let $t\downarrow 0$; this gives $p(h+k)\leq p(h)+p(k)$. Thus $p$ is sublinear.

By the Hahn–Banach theorem, the zero functional on $\{ 0\} $ extends to a linear functional $\ell \leq p$. Since $p(h)\leq \| h\| _\infty $, applying this bound to $h$ and $-h$ shows that $\ell $ is continuous. Monotonicity of the convex difference quotients gives

\[ C_\varphi (F+h)\geq C_\varphi (F)+\ell (h), \]

which is (11.34.1) once $\ell $ is represented by a probability. Testing the translation rule with positive and negative constants gives $\ell (1)=1$. If $h\geq 0$, monotonicity of $B_\varphi $ gives $C_\varphi (F-th)\leq C_\varphi (F)$ for $t>0$, whereas the inequality just proved gives the lower bound $C_\varphi (F)-t\ell (h)$. Hence $\ell (h)\geq 0$. By the Riesz representation theorem, $\ell (h)=\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\nu $ for a probability measure $\nu $, and $\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)$. Each inequality in (11.34.1) defines a weakly closed half-space in the compact space of probability measures, so their intersection is closed and convex, hence weakly compact.

It remains to prove absolute continuity. Let $K$ be compact with $\mathrm{d}V(K)=0$, and choose continuous functions $0\leq \chi _ j\leq 1$, equal to one on $K$, whose supports have volumes tending to zero. For any $\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)$, the supporting inequality with $G=F+\chi _ j$ gives

\[ 0\leq \nu (K)\leq \int _ X\chi _ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq B_\varphi (F)-B_\varphi (F+\chi _ j). \]

The right-hand side tends to zero by theorem 11.33.5, so $\nu (K)=0$. By inner regularity of finite Borel measures on $X$, the same holds for every Borel set of zero volume. The Radon–Nikodym theorem now gives $\mathrm{d}\nu =h_\nu \mathrm{d}V$ with $h_\nu \geq 0$ and $\int _ Xh_\nu \mathrm{d}V=1$. $\square$

Proposition 11.34.3.CB2DC1F2B712 Let $F_ j,F\in C(X)$ with $F_ j\to F$ uniformly, and let $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F_ j)$. Then every weak cluster point of $(\nu _ j)$ belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$. In particular, if the latter set consists of a single measure, $\nu _ j$ converges weakly to it.

Proof. Take a weakly convergent subsequence and fix $G\in C(X)$. In the inequality

\[ B_\varphi (G)\leq B_\varphi (F_ j)-\int _ X(G-F_ j)\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j, \]

the Lipschitz continuity of $B_\varphi $, the uniform convergence of $F_ j$ and the weak convergence of the probabilities allow passage to the limit. The limiting inequality holds for every $G$, so the limit measure belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$. If that set is a singleton, every convergent subsequence has the same limit, and compactness of the space of probability measures proves the final assertion. $\square$

11.34.4 Directional derivativesBD1A1A3DE2D3

As $\nu $ runs through the supporting probabilities at $F$, the integral $-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $ ranges exactly between the two one-sided derivatives of $B_\varphi $ at $F$ in the direction $G$. Consequently, differentiability is equivalent to uniqueness of the supporting probability. For $F,G\in C(X)$, write $D^+B_\varphi (F)[G]$ and $D^-B_\varphi (F)[G]$ for the limits of the difference quotient $(B_\varphi (F+tG)-B_\varphi (F))/t$ as $t\downarrow 0$ and $t\uparrow 0$, respectively.

Proposition 11.34.5.792D6FEF2F3C For $F,G\in C(X)$, the one-sided derivatives exist, and

\begin{align*} D^+B_\varphi (F)[G]& =-\max _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu ,\\ D^-B_\varphi (F)[G]& =-\min _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \end{align*}

Consequently $B_\varphi $ is Gâteaux differentiable at $F$ if and only if $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is a singleton. Its derivative is then $G\mapsto -\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $.

Proof. The one-sided limits exist and are finite by concavity and the Lipschitz bound. For $t>0$, every supporting probability $\nu $ at $F$ gives

\[ \frac{B_\varphi (F+tG)-B_\varphi (F)}{t} \leq -\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

Conversely, choose $\nu _ t\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F+tG)$ and apply its supporting inequality with the test function $F$; this bounds the same quotient below by $-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ t$. Along a sequence $t\downarrow 0$, the measures $\nu _ t$ have a weak limit point, which belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ by proposition 11.34.3. The two bounds identify the right derivative with minus the maximum. Applying this identity to $-G$ gives the formula for the left derivative.

If $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is a singleton $\{ \nu \} $, the two derivatives coincide for every $G$ and equal $-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $, a continuous linear functional of $G$. Conversely, if the two one-sided derivatives agree for every $G$, all supporting probabilities have the same integral against every continuous function, and the Riesz representation theorem shows that they coincide. $\square$

11.34.6 Generic uniqueness39ED4CCC5AE2

On every affine line in $C(X)$, the concave function $B_\varphi $ is differentiable outside a countable set, so the gap between its two directional derivatives along the line vanishes on a dense subset. Upper semicontinuity of this gap and separability of $C(X)$ turn the one-dimensional fact into uniqueness of the supporting probability on a dense $G_\delta $ subset of $C(X)$.

Theorem 11.34.7.A4850266BE73 The set

\[ \mathscr U_\varphi =\{ F\in C(X,\mathbb R):\mathcal A_\varphi (F) \text{ is a singleton}\} \]

is a dense $G_\delta $ subset of $C(X)$ with the uniform norm. At every $F\in \mathscr U_\varphi $, the functional $B_\varphi $ is Gâteaux differentiable, and its derivative is minus the integral against the unique supporting probability. If $F_ j\to F$ uniformly and $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F_ j)$, then $\nu _ j$ converges weakly to that probability.

Proof. For $h\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ define the width

\[ w_ h(F)=\max _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\nu -\min _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\nu \geq 0. \]

The extrema exist by weak compactness. We claim that the width is upper semicontinuous in $F$. Indeed, let $F_ j\to F$ and choose maximizing and minimizing probabilities at each $F_ j$. Along a subsequence realizing the upper limit of the widths, compactness of the space of probability measures gives weak limits of both choices. By proposition 11.34.3, both limits belong to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$, so the difference of their integrals against $h$ is at most $w_ h(F)$.

Consequently, for every $\epsilon >0$ the set $\{ F:w_ h(F)<\epsilon \} $ is open. It is also dense. If $h=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise fix $F$ and an open ball about it. The function

\[ b(t)=B_\varphi (F+th) \]

is finite and concave on the real line, so it is differentiable outside an at most countable set: its one-sided derivatives are finite and monotone, and the nonempty open intervals between unequal one-sided derivatives at distinct points are disjoint, so each contains a different rational number. Choose a point of differentiability $t$ so close to zero that $F+th$ lies in the given ball. The derivative formulas of proposition 11.34.5 give $w_ h(F+th)=0$, which proves density.

The compact manifold $X$ is metrizable, so $C(X)$ is separable. Choose a countable uniformly dense family $(h_ j)$ in $C(X)$. By the Baire category theorem in the complete metric space $C(X)$, the set

\[ \bigcap _{j,k\geq 1}\{ F:w_{h_ j}(F)<1/k\} \]

is a dense $G_\delta $. At every point of this intersection, all supporting probabilities have the same integral against every $h_ j$. Since they have unit mass, uniform approximation gives equal integrals against every continuous function, and the Riesz representation theorem shows that the probabilities coincide. Conversely, a singleton has zero width in every direction, so the intersection is exactly $\mathscr U_\varphi $. The formula for the derivative follows from proposition 11.34.5, and the approximation assertion from proposition 11.34.3. $\square$

11.34.8 Gauduchon adjoints9E3723511E88

For a smooth Hermitian equation, differentiating the scalar identifies the supporting probability with the adjoint probability of the linearized operator.

Theorem 11.34.9.7E60599DA339 Let $\theta =\omega $ be a Hermitian form, let $\varphi =0$, and let $F$ be a smooth real function. Let $u$ be the smooth solution of (11.33.1), unique up to an additive constant by corollary 14.24.17, and let $\mu _ u$ be its Gauduchon adjoint probability from proposition 11.23.2; for $n=1$, this is $\mu _ u=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)/\int _ X\omega $. Then

\[ \mathcal A_0(F)=\{ \mu _ u\} . \]

Proof. Suppose first that $n\geq 2$. By uniqueness of the constant, the normalization scalar of the smooth equation in theorem 14.8.1 is $B_0$, and that theorem gives, for every smooth real $G$,

\[ \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}B_0(F+tG) =-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ u. \]

Let $\nu \in \mathcal A_0(F)$. Testing the supporting inequality at $F+tG$ for both signs of $t$ and letting $t\to 0$ shows that $G$ has the same integral against $\nu $ and $\mu _ u$. Since smooth functions are uniformly dense in $C(X)$, it follows that $\nu =\mu _ u$, and the assertion follows from nonemptiness of $\mathcal A_0(F)$. In dimension one, Stokes' theorem gives $c_0(F)=\int _ X\omega /\int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F \mathrm{d}V$; differentiating its logarithm in the same way yields the stated measure, since $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=c_0(F)\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V$. $\square$

Consequently, in the Hermitian case the supporting probabilities at a continuous $F$ can be approached through smooth data. If smooth $F_ j$ converge uniformly to $F$, then by proposition 11.34.3 every weak cluster point of the Gauduchon adjoint probabilities of the solutions for $F_ j$ belongs to $\mathcal A_0(F)$, and is therefore absolutely continuous. If $\mathcal A_0(F)$ is a singleton, which holds in particular for every $F\in \mathscr U_0$ and under the continuous positive Hessian hypothesis of theorem 11.34.11, these probabilities converge to its unique member, independently of the approximation. We do not know whether $\mathcal A_0(F)$ is a singleton for every continuous $F$, even in this Hermitian setting; the same question is open for the singular references considered above.

For $n\geq 2$, the Hermitian zero-reference case admits a quantitative strengthening: corollary 12.9.3 bounds the densities of all supporting probabilities uniformly in $L^ q$, for $1<q<n/(n-1)$, when $\| F\| _\infty $ is bounded, and also for $q=n/(n-1)$ when $\omega $ has positive lower mass $v_-(\omega )>0$ in the sense of definition 13.14.14. The proof uses the Gauduchon-current formulation of section 12.1, and we do not claim such a bound for an arbitrary singular reference.

A weak linearized operator requires more than a supporting probability. In the same Hermitian zero-reference setting, theorem 12.8.2 represents each supporting probability by a determinant-weighted positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed current. Identifying that current with a scalar multiple of a weak power $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{n-1}$ would require a well-defined product and its compatibility with approximation.

A similar representation holds for a functional defined by subsolutions rather than by an equation in a prescribed singularity type. Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero with $f^{1/n}\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$, the space of densities with uniformly vanishing Gauduchon tails from 15.32.7. Then proposition 12.10.9 describes the supporting probabilities of $F\mapsto \log b_\omega (\mathrm{e}^ Ff)$, where $b_\omega (\cdot )$ is the density threshold of theorem 15.28.4.

11.34.10 Continuous positive complex Hessians4C927FD81A61

The smooth recovery theorem extends to a bounded solution whose complex Hessian is continuous and everywhere positive. In this case one can vary the potential in both directions, and the resulting densities force every supporting probability to annihilate the same elliptic operator. The Sobolev range theorem then gives uniqueness.

Theorem 11.34.11.F4FDB7BC8A03 Let $n\geq 2$, let $\theta =\omega $ be a Hermitian form, and let $\varphi =0$. Suppose $F\in C(X)$ and a bounded solution $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ of (11.33.1) has distributional complex Hessian represented by a continuous form

\[ \sigma =\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0 \]

everywhere on $X$. Then $\mathcal A_0(F)$ consists of one probability $\nu _ F$. It satisfies

\begin{align*} \int _ X L_\sigma h\, \mathrm d\nu _ F& =0\quad (h\in C^\infty (X)),\\ DB_0(F)[H]& =-\int _ XH\, \mathrm d\nu _ F\quad (H\in C(X)). \end{align*}

The derivative is taken in the Gâteaux sense.

Proof. The continuous trace of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is the Euclidean Laplacian of $u$, up to the fixed normalization. Local constant-coefficient elliptic regularity gives $u\in W^{2,p}$ for every finite $p$. Indeed, subtract the Newtonian potential of a cutoff of this trace. Its second high-frequency derivatives are order-zero multipliers, and the low-frequency kernels are locally bounded; the remaining distribution is harmonic. Sobolev embedding gives a $C^{1,\alpha }$ representative for every $\alpha <1$, which agrees with the quasi-psh representative.

On a coordinate ball choose smooth $\rho $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \omega $. The radial regularizations of the bounded psh function $u+\rho $ decrease to it, and their complex Hessians converge uniformly on smaller balls. Continuity of bounded products and the finite smooth-background expansion identify $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ with $\sigma ^ n$. For smooth real $h$, the same argument applies to $u+th$, which is admissible for all sufficiently small signed $t$ by uniform positivity. Define

\[ F_ t=F+\log \frac{(\sigma +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^ n}{\sigma ^ n}. \]

Its equation has the same normalization scalar as the equation for $u$, so $B_0(F_ t)=B_0(F)$. The supporting inequality gives $\int _ X(F_ t-F)\, \mathrm d\nu \leq 0$ for each $\nu \in \mathcal A_0(F)$. Since $F_ t-F=tL_\sigma h+O(t^2)$ uniformly, using both signs yields stationarity.

Choose $p=4n+1$ and $q=p/(p-1)=1+1/(4n)$. By corollary 12.9.3, every supporting probability has an $L^ q$ density, since $q<n/(n-1)$. Smooth density in $W^{2,p}$ extends its stationarity to every $W^{2,p}$ test. By lemma 11.24.1, all these densities lie in a one-dimensional space. Their integrals equal one, so they coincide. Nonemptiness is theorem 11.34.2, and the derivative formula follows from proposition 11.34.5. $\square$

11.34.12 Products with a Hermitian factorC277B7427384

An explicit product construction exhibits the criterion for densities with an arbitrary continuous modulus. Let $(Y,\kappa )$ be a connected compact Hermitian $k$-fold, $k\geq 1$, with $\int _ Y\kappa ^ k=1$. Denote by $\nu _\kappa $ the smooth stationary probability of $L_\kappa =\operatorname {tr}_\kappa \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$, given by the Gauduchon multiplier when $k\geq 2$; for $k=1$ it is $\kappa $. Write $w$ for the Fubini–Study area of mass one on $\mathbb P^1$. All factor forms and functions below are pulled back to $X=\mathbb P^1\times Y$.

Proposition 11.34.13.D2CCD76890C8 Let $a>0$, let $b$ be smooth and positive on $\mathbb P^1$, and let $A$ be continuous and positive there, with $\int _{\mathbb P^1}Aw=a$. For

\[ \omega =aw+b\kappa ,\qquad \mathrm dV=w\wedge \kappa ^ k, \qquad F=\log ((k+1)Ab^ k), \]

one has

\[ c_0(F)=1,\qquad \mathcal A_0(F)=\left\{ \frac Aa\, w\otimes \nu _\kappa \right\} . \]

Proof. The Green solution of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=(A-a)w$ on $\mathbb P^1$ has continuous complex Hessian and is bounded. Its pullback yields $\sigma =Aw+b\kappa >0$, and $\sigma ^{k+1}=(k+1)Ab^ kw\wedge \kappa ^ k$. Thus scalar uniqueness gives $c_0(F)=1$, and theorem 11.34.11 gives a unique support. For every smooth $h$ depending on all variables, the block decomposition

\[ L_\sigma h=A^{-1}L_ wh+b^{-1}L_\kappa h \]

shows that $(A/a)w\otimes \nu _\kappa $ is stationary: the first term integrates to zero by Stokes on $\mathbb P^1$, and the second by stationarity on $Y$ and Fubini. This candidate has a bounded density with respect to $\mathrm dV$. The one-dimensional annihilator of lemma 11.24.1 and probability normalization therefore identify it with the supporting probability. $\square$

Example 11.34.14.1B8C3827F8D7 There is a non-Kähler threefold with a nonclosed Hermitian background and a positive continuous density whose logarithm is neither Dini continuous nor Hölder continuous of any positive order, but whose supporting probability is explicit and unique.

Proof. Take the Hopf surface $Y=(\mathbb C^2\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto 2z\rangle $ and its unit-volume metric $\kappa =c|z|^{-2}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$, where $c>0$ is chosen so that $\int _ Y\kappa ^2=1$. Put $s=\log |z|^2$, $\alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s$ and $\beta =\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s$, so that $\kappa =c(\alpha +\beta )$. The differentiation in (23.1.3) applies in this dimension. Here $\alpha $ has rank one, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\kappa =-c\alpha ^2=0$ and $\nu _\kappa =\kappa ^2$. Polar coordinates identify $Y$ with $S^3\times S^1$, hence $H^2(Y,\mathbb R)=0$. A closed positive form would be exact and have positive square integral, contradicting Stokes. Thus $Y$ is non-Kähler, and so is $\mathbb P^1\times Y$, by restriction.

Let $\tau \in [-1,1]$ be the height on $\mathbb P^1$, set $b=2+\tau $, and put

\[ f(t)=\begin{cases} 1/\log (e/|t|),& 0<|t|\leq 1,\\ 0,& t=0, \end{cases} \]
\[ A=a+\varepsilon \left(f(\tau )-\int _{\mathbb P^1}f(\tau )w\right), \quad 0<\varepsilon <a. \]

Since $0\leq f\leq 1$, this is a positive continuous function of mean $a$. Along a normal segment to the equator, $|\tau |$ is comparable to the distance $r$. The oscillation of $A$ is therefore bounded below by a positive multiple of $1/\log (e/r)$. Its Dini integral $\int _0^{r_0}\mathrm dr/(r\log (e/r))$ diverges, and this modulus is larger than every $r^\gamma $, $\gamma >0$, as $r\downarrow 0$. The logarithm of $A$ has the same lower bound, because $A$ is uniformly positive and bounded. The smooth term $2\log b$ contributes only $O(r)$, so $F=\log (3Ab^2)$ has neither regularity. The mixed component $\mathrm db\wedge \kappa $ shows that $\omega =aw+b\kappa $ is nonclosed. By proposition 11.34.13, its supporting probability is $(A/a)w\wedge \kappa ^2$. $\square$

The theorem assumes a continuous positive complex Hessian for a bounded solution. It does not establish that hypothesis for every continuous $F$; the uniqueness question for arbitrary continuous data remains open.

11.34.15 Divisorial smoothingA54217F2E13D

For a singular reference, the supporting probability can be unique while smoothings of the solution have different limiting adjoint probabilities. The reason is that the non-pluripolar product discards the mass on a divisor that the smoothings keep. On a product of two projective lines, explicit formulas exhibit both the lost mass and its dependence on the smoothing.

In this subsection, $X=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$. Let $\omega _1$ and $\omega _2$ be the pullbacks of the Fubini–Study forms of the two factors, normalized to have integral one on their factors, and put

\[ \theta =2\omega _1+2\omega _2,\qquad \beta =\omega _1+2\omega _2,\qquad \omega =\omega _1+\omega _2,\qquad \mathrm dV=\omega _1\wedge \omega _2. \]

Thus $\mathrm dV$ is a probability volume form, $\theta ^2=8\mathrm dV$ and $\beta ^2=4\mathrm dV$. Let $z$ be the affine coordinate on the first factor, and put

\[ r=\frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2},\qquad \psi =\log r,\qquad D=\{ 0\} \times \mathbb P^1. \]

The function $r$ extends smoothly to the first factor, with value one at infinity, and $\psi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic with value $-\infty $ exactly on $D$. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]-\omega _1$, so $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\beta +[D]$.

For a smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\Omega $ on the surface $X$, the linearization $v\mapsto 2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \Omega /\Omega ^2$ of the logarithmic Monge–Ampère operator has the adjoint probability $\rho \Omega ^2$, where the Gauduchon multiplier $\rho >0$ satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \Omega )=0$ and $\int _ X\rho \Omega ^2=1$ (proposition 11.23.2). We call $Q=\rho \Omega $ the adjoint cofactor of $\Omega $: it is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed, and $\Omega \wedge Q$ is the adjoint probability.

Lemma 11.34.16.FADB0C708F54 Write $x=\log |z|^2$ and $\chi (x)=\log (1+\mathrm e^ x)$. The envelope $\varphi =P_\theta [\psi ]$ is

Proposition 11.34.18.3A41CC697C2A Every $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $v\sim \varphi $ is uniquely of the form $v=\psi +w$, where $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$ is bounded. Its non-pluripolar measure is

and Stokes' theorem against the constant function yields $\int _ XT^2=4$. Theorem 11.34.21.D68D3638A657 Let $g$ be a smooth real function on the second factor with $\gamma =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\geq -\omega _2$, regarded as a function on $X$. For $0<\delta \leq 1$ put

\[ u_{\delta ,g}=\log (r+\delta \mathrm e^ g),\qquad \Omega _{\delta ,g}=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{\delta ,g},\qquad F_{\delta ,g}=\log \frac{\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2}{4\mathrm dV}. \]

The forms satisfy $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\geq \omega $ and $\int _ X\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2=8$. For each fixed $g$, as $\delta \downarrow 0$ the smooth potentials $u_{\delta ,g}$ decrease pointwise to $\psi $, converge in $L^1$ and in the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $, and converge smoothly off $D$. Moreover $F_{\delta ,g}\to 0$ in $L^ p(\mathrm dV)$ for every $1\leq p<\infty $. The adjoint cofactors and adjoint probabilities of the forms $\Omega _{\delta ,g}$ are

\[ Q_{\delta ,g}=\Omega _{\delta ,g}/8,\qquad \nu _{\delta ,g}=\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2/8, \]

and they have the weak limits

\begin{equation} \begin{split} Q_{\delta ,g}& \longrightarrow (\beta +[D])/8,\\ \nu _{\delta ,g}& \longrightarrow \tfrac 12\mathrm dV+\tfrac 12[D]\wedge \omega _2 +\tfrac 18[D]\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g. \end{split} \end{equation}

Proof. Smoothness and monotone convergence follow directly from the defining formula, also on $D$, where $\psi =-\infty $. Off $D$, set

\[ \lambda =\frac{r}{r+\delta \mathrm e^ g},\qquad h=\psi -g-\log \delta . \]

The chain rule gives $\Omega _{\delta ,g}=A_\delta +R_\delta $, where

\[ A_\delta =(2-\lambda )\omega _1+2\omega _2+(1-\lambda )\gamma , \qquad R_\delta =\lambda (1-\lambda )\mathrm dh\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}h. \]

Thus $A_\delta \geq \omega _1+\omega _2$ and $R_\delta \geq 0$, and the inequality $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\geq \omega $ extends across $D$ by continuity. Closedness of $\theta $ and Stokes' theorem give the mass eight. Since $\Omega _{\delta ,g}$ is closed, the Gauduchon condition $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \Omega _{\delta ,g})=0$ reduces to the elliptic equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \wedge \Omega _{\delta ,g}=0$ on the connected surface, whose smooth solutions are constant by the maximum principle. Normalization against $\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2$ gives $\rho =1/8$ and the stated adjoint cofactors and probabilities.

The rank-one form $R_\delta $ has square zero, hence $\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2=A_\delta ^2+2A_\delta \wedge R_\delta $. The bounded coefficients of $A_\delta $ converge off $D$ to those of $\beta $, which gives $A_\delta ^2\to 4\mathrm dV$. Write $N=\mathrm d\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\psi $. In $A_\delta \wedge R_\delta $, the part in which $R_\delta $ contributes only derivatives along the first factor (the normal part) is

\[ \lambda (1-\lambda )N\wedge \bigl (2\omega _2+(1-\lambda )\gamma \bigr ). \]

Terms mixing a derivative along the first factor with one along the second vanish for degree reasons. The remaining tangential term is $\lambda (1-\lambda )(2-\lambda )\omega _1\wedge \mathrm dg\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g$, which tends to zero by dominated convergence.

The normal coefficients can be computed in polar coordinates. Put $s=\delta \mathrm e^{g}$ and $t=|z|^2$. Then

\[ \lambda =\frac{t}{s+(1+s)t},\qquad N=\frac{\mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\bar z}{2\pi t(1+t)^2}. \]

After angular integration the radial measure of $\lambda (1-\lambda )N$ is

\[ \frac{s\, \mathrm dt}{(1+t)(s+(1+s)t)^2} =\frac{\mathrm dy}{(1+sy)(1+(1+s)y)^2},\qquad t=sy. \]

It is bounded by $\mathrm dy/(1+y)^2$ and tends to that measure, whose integral is one. With an additional factor $1-\lambda $, the rescaled measure becomes $\mathrm dy/(1+(1+s)y)^3$, which is bounded by $\mathrm dy/(1+y)^3$ and has limiting integral $1/2$. The mass of both measures outside every fixed tube around $D$ tends to zero, uniformly in the point of the second factor because $g$ is bounded. Testing against continuous functions therefore gives

\[ \lambda (1-\lambda )N\longrightarrow [D],\qquad \lambda (1-\lambda )^2N\longrightarrow \tfrac 12[D]. \]

Taking $g=0$ and a small nonconstant smooth $g$ gives different limits, since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ vanishes identically only for constant $g$.

The difference of the potentials satisfies

\[ 0\leq u_{\delta ,g}-\psi \leq \log \bigl (1+\delta \mathrm e^{\max g}/r\bigr ) \]

off $D$. The right-hand side tends to zero and is dominated by an integrable logarithmic pole, which proves $L^1$ convergence. Continuity of distributional differentiation then gives the limit of the cofactors. A locally integrable function times the smooth form $\beta $ has absolutely continuous coefficients, so it cannot produce the singular coefficients of $[D]$.

For convergence in capacity, use the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ of section 5.29; after subtracting one, its test potentials range over $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $-1\leq v\leq 0$. For such $v$, apply the cutoff argument of (11.34.20) with $\beta $ replaced by $\omega $ and $w=v+1$. Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ X\omega _1\wedge (2\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)=2$, so

This is a sublevel set of $\psi $ whose level tends to $-\infty $ as $\delta \downarrow 0$. The capacity bound for such sublevel sets, together with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (D)=0$, proves convergence in capacity.

Finally, $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\geq \omega $ gives $F_{\delta ,g}\geq -\log 2$. In the affine chart $|\mathrm d\psi |_\omega ^2\leq C/r$, while $g$ and its first two derivatives are bounded. The decomposition above yields $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\leq C_ g(1+r^{-1})\omega $, hence

\[ |F_{\delta ,g}|\leq C_ g+2|\log r|. \]

The exponential densities satisfy $\int _ X\mathrm e^{F_{\delta ,g}}\mathrm dV=2$ for every $\delta $, whereas their pointwise limit has integral one; they are therefore not uniformly integrable. The smoothings leave the ordinary type of $\varphi $ and keep the mass eight of bounded potentials instead of the mass four of that type. The example thus shows which concentration a recovery theorem must exclude. The hypotheses of proposition 11.34.3 (uniform convergence) and of theorem 11.33.5 (uniformly bounded logarithmic densities converging in $L^1$) are stronger than the convergence in every $L^ p$ obtained here. In the fixed divisorial type, a common bound on the residual potentials gives a direct recovery estimate instead.

Corollary 11.34.24.1AB249BC820A Let $w_ j,w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$ satisfy $|w_ j|,|w|\leq M$ for a fixed $M\geq 1$, and suppose that $w_ j\to w$ in capacity. Put

\[ \begin{gathered} T_ j=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j,\qquad T=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w,\\ Q_ j=T_ j/4,\quad Q=T/4,\qquad \nu _ j=T_ j^2/4,\quad \nu =T^2/4. \end{gathered} \]

Then $Q_ j\rightharpoonup Q$ as currents and $\nu _ j\rightharpoonup \nu $ as probability measures. More precisely, for real $f\in C^\infty (X)$ choose $C_ f\geq 0$ with $-C_ f\beta \leq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\leq C_ f\beta $. For every $\eta >0$, writing $E_{j,\eta }=\{ |w_ j-w|>\eta \} $, one has

5D44ECE3CA31 11.34.25
\begin{equation} \left|\int _ X f\, (\mathrm d\nu _ j-\mathrm d\nu )\right| \leq C_ f\bigl (2\eta +2M^2\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E_{j,\eta })\bigr ). \end{equation}

If $T_ j$ is smooth and strictly positive, then $Q_ j$ and $\nu _ j$ are its adjoint cofactor and adjoint probability. If $\psi +w$ solves (11.33.1) for some $F\in C(X)$, the limit $\nu $ is the unique member of $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$.

Proof. The capacity is the one of section 5.29, and $\beta \leq 2\omega $ gives $\operatorname {Cap}_\beta \leq 4\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. Set $a_ j=(w_ j+M)/(2M)$ and $S_ j=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ j$. Then $0\leq a_ j\leq 1$ and

\[ \begin{gathered} S_ j=\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)\beta +\frac1{2M}T_ j,\\ S_ j^2\geq \left(\frac1M-\frac1{2M^2}\right)\beta \wedge T_ j \geq \frac1{2M}\beta \wedge T_ j. \end{gathered} \]

Positivity of bounded mixed products justifies these inequalities. Thus $\int _ E\beta \wedge T_ j\leq 2M\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E)$ for every Borel set $E$, and the same bound holds for $T$. By closedness and Stokes' theorem, $\int _ X\beta \wedge (T_ j+T)=8$. Now test the identity

\[ T_ j^2-T^2=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl ((w_ j-w)(T_ j+T)\bigr ) \]

against $f$. On $X\setminus E_{j,\eta }$ the potential difference has absolute value at most $\eta $, and on $E_{j,\eta }$ at most $2M$. Consequently

\[ \begin{aligned} \left|\int _ Xf(T_ j^2-T^2)\right| & \leq C_ f\left(8\eta +2M\int _{E_{j,\eta }}\beta \wedge (T_ j+T)\right)\\ & \leq C_ f\left(8\eta +8M^2\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E_{j,\eta })\right). \end{aligned} \]

Dividing by four proves (11.34.25). Letting first $j\to \infty $ with $\eta $ fixed and then $\eta \downarrow 0$ shows that $\int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu _ j\to \int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu $. All $\nu _ j$ and $\nu $ have mass one by proposition 11.34.18, and smooth functions are uniformly dense in $C(X)$, so $\nu _ j\rightharpoonup \nu $.

The constant test potential in the definition of capacity gives $4\mathrm dV(E)\leq \operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E)$. Splitting the potential difference over the same sets yields

\[ \| w_ j-w\| _{L^1(\mathrm dV)} \leq \eta +\tfrac M2\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E_{j,\eta }). \]

Taking the limits in the same order proves $L^1$ convergence, and hence convergence of the currents $Q_ j$ by continuity of distributional differentiation. The limiting cofactor satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}Q=0$, $\int _ X\beta \wedge Q=1$, and $T\wedge Q=\nu $ as a bounded product. For smooth real $h$, the signed measure $2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge T$ is the formal linearization of the residual operator at $w$ in the direction $h$, and its integral against the constant $1/4$ vanishes by Stokes' theorem. It is the derivative of the residual measure along $w+th$ whenever these functions remain $\beta $-plurisubharmonic.

If $T_ j$ is smooth and strictly positive, the maximum principle on the connected surface makes its Gauduchon multiplier constant, and the mass four normalizes it to $1/4$. Finally, if $\psi +w$ solves the equation at $F$, then $\nu =T^2/4=\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV/\int _ X\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV$, which is the unique supporting probability by proposition 11.34.18. $\square$

In the corollary, the potentials $\psi +w_ j$ keep their pole along $D$, and the adjoint objects refer to the smooth residual forms $T_ j$. We do not know whether a similar recovery holds for more general singular references, for unbounded residual sequences, or when the Gauduchon multipliers are not constant.
