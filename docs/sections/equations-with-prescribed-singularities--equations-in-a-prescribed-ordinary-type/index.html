15.12 Equations in a prescribed ordinary typeC6737BA51F0E

C6737BA51F0E

We return to a semipositive big background $\theta $ and now require the solution to have the ordinary singularity type of a fixed reference $\varphi $. Throughout this section, $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is an envelope fixed point admitting a smaller-background envelope; that is, (16.28.67) holds:

\[ P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi ,\qquad P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty \quad \text{for some }0<\varepsilon <1. \]

We fix such an $\varepsilon $. Note that $\varphi \leq 0$, since every competitor for $P_\theta [\varphi ]$ is nonpositive. For a normalized solution $u$, the fixed-point property gives the upper bound $u\leq \varphi $, while the smaller-background envelope $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ yields a uniform lower bound $u\geq \varphi -C$. A supersolution and the addition theorem then prove existence in this type.

Theorem 15.12.1.7AFA75E73767 Let $\theta $ and $\varphi $ be as above, and let $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$, where $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\mu (X)>0$. Then:

  1. For every $\lambda >0$ there is a unique $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ such that

    \[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu . \]

  2. There are $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ and a uniquely determined constant $c>0$ such that

    \[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu . \]

The potential in part (2) need not be unique up to an additive constant, even for a Hermitian background and the reference $\varphi =0$; see the counterexample in theorem 14.26.1.

The proof uses two estimates in the prescribed type. The first is a lower bound for the norm of a Monge–Ampère density.

Lemma 15.12.2.D0B5A6CE28E9 Fix $q>1$. There is $b_ q>0$ such that every $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=F\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad 0\leq F\in L^ q(\mathrm{d}V), \]

satisfies $\| F\| _ q\geq b_ q$. For fixed $\theta $ and $\varepsilon $, the constant $b_ q$ is independent of $\varphi $.

Proof. Since $u\sim \varphi $, the smaller-background envelope $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(u)$ is not identically $-\infty $. If $F=0$, then theorem 15.2.2, applied to $u$ and $u+1$ with coefficient zero, would give $u\geq u+1$; hence $N=\| F\| _ q>0$.

Apply lemma 15.3.1 in the semipositive big background $(1-\varepsilon )\theta $ to $F/N$, whose $L^ q$ norm is one. It gives a bounded $s$ with $-1\leq s\leq 0$ and a constant $b_ q>0$, depending only on this fixed background and on $q$, such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}_{(1-\varepsilon )\theta }(s)\geq (b_ q/N)F\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Put $h=P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )+s$. This is a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function with $h\preceq u$. Since $\theta _ h$ is the sum of the positive currents $\varepsilon \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ and $(1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s$, the same density bound holds for $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h)$. To justify this, we truncate $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ at finite levels, use positivity of bounded mixed products, and exhaust the set where $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ is finite; its pole set has zero volume.

If $N<b_ q$, we would have

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq (N/b_ q)\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h+t) \quad \text{for every }t\in \mathbb R. \]

Since $N/b_ q<1$, theorem 15.2.2 would then imply $u\geq h+t$ for every $t$, which is impossible at a point where both potentials are finite. Thus $N\geq b_ q$. The construction of $s$ used no property of $\varphi $, which proves the last assertion. $\square$

The lemma bounds the $L^ q$ norm of the density from below; it gives no positive lower bound for the total mass $\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}V$.

The second estimate bounds a normalized solution from below by $\varphi $ minus a uniform constant. It is what keeps limits of solutions in the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi $.

Theorem 15.12.3.413B92CD2B72 Let $\theta $ and $\varphi $ be as above. Suppose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $u\sim \varphi $, $\sup _ Xu=0$, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)\leq f\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad 0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V),\quad p>1. \]

There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed geometry, $\varphi $, $p$, and an upper bound for $\| f\| _{L^ p}$, such that

\[ \varphi -C\leq u\leq \varphi . \]

Proof. The upper bound follows directly from the fixed-point property: since $u\leq 0$ and $u\preceq \varphi $, the function $u$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $.

For the lower bound we construct a subsolution $\psi $ of the type of $\varphi $ whose measure dominates a multiple of $\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$, and then apply singular domination. Put $\varphi _\varepsilon =P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$, so that $\varphi _\varepsilon \leq \varphi \leq 0$. Uniform exponential integrability (lemma 5.26.2) for the normalized potentials $u$ and exponential integrability for the fixed function $\varphi _\varepsilon $ allow us to choose $\gamma >0$ and $1<q<p$ such that

\[ g=\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma (\varphi _\varepsilon +u)}f \]

has a uniform $L^ q$ bound. Explicitly, we first choose $q<p$ and apply Hölder's inequality with exponents $p/q$ and $p/(p-q)$; then we choose $\gamma $ so small that the exponential factor is controlled by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the two exponential integrability estimates.

Theorem 15.3.5, applied in the semipositive big background $(1-\varepsilon )\theta $ to the density $1+g$, gives a bounded $v$ with $\sup _ Xv=0$ and a constant $c>0$ such that

\[ ((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n=c(1+g)\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Both $\| v\| _\infty $ and $c^{-1}$ are uniformly bounded. Adding the constant one to $g$ makes the root integral of the density at least one, so no lower bound for the integral of $f$ is needed.

Put $h=\varphi _\varepsilon +v\leq \varphi $, which is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and define

\[ \chi (t)=\frac1{1-t}\quad (t\leq 0),\qquad \psi =\varphi +\chi (h-\varphi ), \]

where the formula for $\psi $ is evaluated off the pole sets. Then $0\leq \chi \leq 1$, $0<\chi '\leq 1$, and $\chi ''>0$. For bounded potentials with $h\leq \varphi $, lemma 5.28.13, applied with $G(x,y)=x+\chi (y-x)$, gives

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G(\varphi ,h) \geq (1-\chi '(h-\varphi ))\theta _\varphi +\chi '(h-\varphi )\theta _ h\geq 0. \]

For unbounded potentials we apply this bounded assertion to $\varphi _ k=\max (\varphi ,-k)$ and $h_ k=\max (h,-k)\leq \varphi _ k$. Since $G$ is nondecreasing in both variables, $G(\varphi _ k,h_ k)$ decreases as $k$ increases. Its limit is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and lies between $\varphi $ and $\varphi +1$; hence it is not identically $-\infty $, and it equals $\psi $. Locality on common finite levels and positivity of bounded products give

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi ) \geq (\varphi -h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h) \geq c(\varphi -h+1)^{-2n}\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma \varphi _\varepsilon } \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where the second inequality uses $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h)\geq ((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\geq cg\, \mathrm{d}V$. These inequalities are first obtained off the pole sets; since pole sets have zero volume, they extend by exhaustion to all Borel sets.

Write $T=-\varphi _\varepsilon \geq 0$. If $C_1\geq 1+\| v\| _\infty $, then $\varphi -h+1\leq T+C_1$. Since

\[ A:=\inf _{T\geq 0}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\gamma T}}{(T+C_1)^{2n}}>0, \]

we obtain, with a uniform $d>0$,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi ) \geq d \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma u}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \]

Choose $K=2+\gamma ^{-1}|\log d|$. On $\{ u<\psi -K\} $ we have $u<1-K$, because $\psi \leq \varphi +1\leq 1$; hence

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq d^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{\gamma (1-K)} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi -K) \leq \mathrm{e}^{-\gamma }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\psi -K). \]

The potentials $u$ and $\psi -K$ have the same ordinary singularity type. Moreover, $u\geq \varphi -C_ u$ for some constant $C_ u$, so $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(u)\geq \varphi _\varepsilon -C_ u$ is not identically $-\infty $. Theorem 15.2.2 therefore yields $u\geq \psi -K\geq \varphi -K$. All constants used to choose $K$ are uniform over the family in the theorem; the possibly nonuniform constant $C_ u$ serves only to show that the smaller-background envelope of $u$ is not identically $-\infty $, and it enters no estimate. $\square$

The existence proof starts from a supersolution in the prescribed type. It is obtained from a bounded solution by a rooftop envelope with $\varphi $.

Lemma 15.12.4.5A797D7301BB For nonzero $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and $\lambda >0$, there exists $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w\sim \varphi $ such that

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda w}f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Proof. Let $v$ be the bounded solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v}f\, \mathrm{d}V$ given by theorem 15.3.3, and write $M=\sup _ Xv$ and $a=v-M\leq 0$. The rooftop envelope $p=P_\theta (a,\varphi )$ satisfies $\varphi -\| a\| _\infty \leq p\leq \varphi $, so $p\sim \varphi $. By the rooftop inequality of proposition 16.28.6,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p) \leq \mathbf1_{\{ p=a\leq \varphi \} }\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a) +\mathbf1_{\{ p=\varphi <a\} }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\varphi ). \]

The second term vanishes: by proposition 16.28.7, the measure of $\varphi $ is carried by $\{ \varphi =0\} $, whereas $\varphi <a\leq 0$ on the set in question. On the first contact set, $a=p$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (a+M)}f\, \mathrm{d}V$. Thus $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (p)\leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda (p+M)}f\, \mathrm{d}V$, and $w=p+M$ has the required properties. $\square$

15.12.5 Existence for a positive exponent3EFAC1693CBD

We prove part (1) of theorem 15.12.1 by maximizing a weighted mass. Among the potentials $z\sim \varphi $ whose weighted measure $\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda z}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (z)$ lies below $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$, we find one of maximal weighted mass; theorem 15.12.3 provides the compactness needed for this. If its weighted measure were strictly smaller than $\mu $, the addition theorem would produce a potential of larger weighted mass.

of theorem 15.12.1, part (1). Step 1: a maximizing sequence. Fix $\lambda >0$. By lemma 15.12.4, there is $w\sim \varphi $ such that

\[ \nu _0:=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda w}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (w) \leq \mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Consider all potentials $z\sim \varphi $ for which

\[ \nu _0\leq \nu _ z:=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda z} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (z)\leq \mu , \]

and let $B$ be the supremum of their weighted masses $\nu _ z(X)$. This family is nonempty, since it contains $w$, and $B\leq \mu (X)<+\infty $. Choose $u_ j$ in the family with $\nu _{u_ j}(X)\to B$. Since $\nu _0\leq \nu _{u_ j}$, corollary 15.2.3 gives $u_ j\leq w$.

Step 2: uniform bounds. We need a lower bound for the suprema of the $u_ j$. Put $\varphi _\varepsilon =P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\leq 0$, and let $\psi $ be the bounded solution of

\[ ((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \psi }f\, \mathrm{d}V \]

given by theorem 15.3.3. For $h=\psi +\varphi _\varepsilon $, positivity of the splitting $\theta _ h=(\varepsilon \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _\varepsilon )+((1-\varepsilon )\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )$ gives

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda h}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h) \geq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda \varphi _\varepsilon }f\, \mathrm{d}V \geq \mu \geq \nu _{u_ j}. \]

Here $h\preceq u_ j$, because $h\preceq \varphi $ and $u_ j\sim \varphi $, and each $u_ j$ admits a smaller-background envelope because $u_ j\sim \varphi $. Corollary 15.2.3 therefore gives $u_ j\geq h$. Consequently

\[ \sup _ Xh\leq M_ j:=\sup _ Xu_ j\leq \sup _ Xw, \]

and the lower bound is finite.

We now apply theorem 15.12.3 to $v_ j=u_ j-M_ j$. Their measures satisfy

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j) \leq \mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xw}f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

so $\varphi -C\leq v_ j\leq \varphi $. Together with the bounds for $M_ j$, this shows that all $u_ j$ lie within a fixed bounded distance of $\varphi $.

Step 3: the limit. Extract an $L^1$ limit $u$. By corollary 15.9.4, applied with the dominating measure $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda \sup _ Xw}\mu $, the convergence holds in capacity, $u\sim \varphi $, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j) \rightharpoonup \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u). \]

After a further extraction we may also assume $\nu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \nu $. Since $\nu _0\leq \nu _{u_ j}\leq \mu $, the limit satisfies the same inequalities. We claim that

\[ \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\nu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\nu . \]

Indeed, $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\to \mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ in $L^1(\mu )$ by lemma 5.44.2, and all the measures $\nu _{u_ j}$ are at most $\mu $; this controls the error caused by changing the weight. The fixed bounded weight $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}$ can be approximated in $L^1(\mu )$ by continuous functions, and since both $\nu _{u_ j}$ and $\nu $ are dominated by $\mu $, this approximation is uniform in the integrals. The weak convergence $\nu _{u_ j}\rightharpoonup \nu $ then gives the claim, and the same argument applies after multiplication by any continuous test function.

Since $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u_ j}\nu _{u_ j}=\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)$, it follows that

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\nu , \qquad \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=\nu , \qquad \nu (X)=B. \]

The second identity holds off the pole set of $u$, which has zero $\mu $-measure. Hence $u$ belongs to the family and attains the supremum $B$. Write $\nu =g\, \mathrm{d}V$, with $0\leq g\leq f$.

Step 4: no deficit. Suppose that $g\neq f$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. Applying lemma 15.12.4 to $f-g$, we find $v\sim \varphi $ with

\[ \nu _ v:=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda v}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v) \leq (f-g)\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

This measure is nonzero. In fact, no potential $v$ admitting a smaller-background envelope has zero Monge–Ampère measure, since theorem 15.2.2, applied with coefficient zero to $v$ and $v+1$, would give $v\geq v+1$. Thus $\nu _ v(X)>0$.

The sum $\nu +\nu _ v$ is finite, so theorem 15.11.1 gives $a\sim \varphi $ with

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (a)=\nu +\nu _ v. \]

This measure still lies between $\nu _0$ and $\mu $, but its mass is $B+\nu _ v(X)>B$, a contradiction. Therefore $g=f$, and $u$ solves the required equation. Uniqueness in the prescribed ordinary type follows from corollary 15.2.3. $\square$

15.12.6 The normalized equation21A9095B1925

For the normalized equation we let the positive exponent tend to zero. Bounded comparison controls the normalizing constants from above, while the lower density estimate of lemma 15.12.2 keeps them away from zero. The common barriers then give convergence of the Monge–Ampère measures and keep the limit in the prescribed type.

of theorem 15.12.1, part (2). For each $j\geq 1$, part (1) gives $u_ j\sim \varphi $ with

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j)=\mathrm{e}^{u_ j/j}f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Write $M_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j$, $v_ j=u_ j-M_ j$ and $c_ j=\mathrm{e}^{M_ j/j}$. Let $h$ be a bounded normalized solution of $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (h)=\alpha f\, \mathrm{d}V$, with $\alpha >0$, given by theorem 15.3.5.

For $H_ j=h+\| h\| _\infty +j\log \alpha $ we have

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-H_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}_\theta (H_ j) =\mathrm{e}^{-(h+\| h\| _\infty )/j}f\, \mathrm{d}V \leq f\, \mathrm{d}V =\mathrm{e}^{-u_ j/j}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u_ j). \]

The bounded function $H_ j$ admits a smaller-background envelope, and $u_ j\preceq H_ j$, so corollary 15.2.3 gives $u_ j\leq H_ j$. In particular,

\[ 0<c_ j\leq \alpha \exp (2\| h\| _\infty /j). \]

The lower density bound of lemma 15.12.2, applied to $v_ j$ with $q=p$, gives

\[ b_ p\leq \| c_ j\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}f\| _ p\leq c_ j\| f\| _ p, \]

so the $c_ j$ are also bounded away from zero.

Since $\sup _ Xv_ j=0$ and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v_ j)=c_ j\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}f\, \mathrm{d}V\leq C f\, \mathrm{d}V$, theorem 15.12.3 gives $\varphi -C'\leq v_ j\leq \varphi $. Extract a subsequence with $v_ j\to v$ in $L^1(X)$ and $c_ j\to c>0$. By corollary 15.9.4, the convergence holds in capacity, $v\sim \varphi $, and the Monge–Ampère measures converge weakly. Meanwhile, lemma 5.44.5 gives

\[ \int _ X|\mathrm{e}^{v_ j/j}-1|f\, \mathrm{d}V\longrightarrow 0. \]

Thus the right-hand sides converge in total variation to $cf\, \mathrm{d}V$, and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$.

For the uniqueness of the constant, suppose that $a,b\sim \varphi $ solve the normalized equation with constants $c_ a<c_ b$. For every real $t$,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (a) =\frac{c_ a}{c_ b}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (b+t), \]

so theorem 15.2.2 gives $a\geq b+t$. This is impossible as $t\to +\infty $, at any point where both potentials are finite. Interchanging the roles of the two constants proves that $c$ is unique. $\square$
