14.1 Relative energy and Perron segments

17 Relative energy and Perron segments

Let $(X,\omega )$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. In this chapter we study the Monge–Ampère energy of $\theta $-plurisubharmonic potentials relative to a model potential $\phi $ (definition 16.3.1); recall that model potentials have positive mass. The energy is a concave functional whose derivative is the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure. We use it for two purposes: to solve Monge–Ampère equations with prescribed non-pluripolar measures by maximizing suitable functionals, and to show that the energy is affine along weak geodesics, which we construct as Perron envelopes of subgeodesics.

Everything rests on integration by parts for non-pluripolar products of potentials with bounded difference. Such potentials may be unbounded, so Stokes' formula for bounded potentials (lemma 15.4.2) does not apply to them directly. In section 17.1 we truncate them and control the truncation errors with the relative capacities of chapter 16. This control requires a strictly positive background, a restriction that a polynomial expansion in an auxiliary Kähler form removes at the end. The resulting formula (theorem 17.1.3) makes no assumption on the masses of the potentials.

The energy $E_\theta ^\phi $ is first defined for potentials with bounded difference from $\phi $, where integration by parts computes its first and second variations, and is then extended by decreasing truncation to all potentials $u$ with $u\leq \phi +O(1)$. It is finite exactly on the relative finite-energy class $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ (theorem 17.2.8), continuous along monotone sequences, and upper semicontinuous for $L^1$ convergence, so that sets of normalized potentials with energy bounded below are compact.

In section 17.4 these properties turn maximizers of suitable functionals into solutions of Monge–Ampère equations, first for bounded densities and for the exponential equations $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $, and then, letting $\lambda \to 0$, for $\mu _ u=\nu $ with an arbitrary non-pluripolar measure $\nu $ of the same mass as $\phi $ (theorem 17.4.13). The solution is unique up to an additive constant (theorem 17.4.14), and it has bounded difference from $\phi $ when $\nu $ has an $L^ p$ density with $p>1$ (corollary 17.4.17). Two further consequences are that a pseudo-effective class of positive volume is big (theorem 17.4.20) and that mixed non-pluripolar masses dominate the geometric mean of the individual masses (theorem 17.4.23).

With these equations we show in section 17.5 that, in a big class, the singularity ceiling of a potential $u$ (definition 17.5.2) coincides with its singularity envelope $P_\theta [u]$, and that $P_\theta [P_\theta [u]]=P_\theta [u]$ (theorem 17.5.3). This is [Theorem 1.2, PSWZ26]; it includes the case of zero mass, $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =0$, to which the characterization of $P_\theta [u]$ in theorem 16.4.7 does not apply.

In the last three sections we return to a fixed model potential $\phi $ and join two potentials of full mass relative to $\phi $ by a Perron segment, the upper envelope of all subgeodesics lying below them at the endpoints. Rooftop envelopes show that the segment attains the prescribed endpoints. Its complexification is locally maximal on the product of $X$ with a strip, so its top-degree non-pluripolar measure vanishes, and integration by parts on $X\times \mathbb P^1$ then shows that the energy is affine along the segment (theorem 17.7.3). We conclude with estimates for the extremal slopes of a segment and for long segments; the latter produce profiles that depend only on the singularity type of the endpoint (proposition 17.8.2).

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in section B.17, section B.18, section B.19.

17.1 Integration by parts for bounded differences

As in chapter 16, for a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$ and $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ we write

\[ \theta _ q=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q,\qquad \mu _ q=\langle \theta _ q^ n\rangle , \]

so that $\mu _ q(X)=\int _ X\langle \theta _ q^ n\rangle $ is the total mass of $q$, and we indicate the background when several occur. Recall from definition 14.2.6 that $v$ is more singular than $u$, written $v\preceq u$, if $v\leq u+C$ on $X$ for some constant $C$, and that $u$ and $v$ have the same singularity type, written $u\sim v$, if $u\preceq v$ and $v\preceq u$. A difference $u-v$ of two quasi-psh functions is evaluated on their common finite locus $\{ u>-\infty \} \cap \{ v>-\infty \} $, whose complement is pluripolar. Non-pluripolar products do not charge pluripolar sets (lemma 15.1.2), so the values assigned to such a difference on the pole sets never affect the integrals below.

A bounded quasi-psh difference is a bounded Borel function $a$ on $X$ such that $a=\varphi _1-\varphi _2$ quasi-everywhere, that is, outside a pluripolar set, for two quasi-psh functions $\varphi _1,\varphi _2$ on $X$; we call $(\varphi _1,\varphi _2)$ a presentation of $a$. The two functions then have the same singularity type. Indeed, if $|a|\leq C$, then $\varphi _1\leq \varphi _2+C$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, because an inequality between quasi-psh functions that holds quasi-everywhere holds everywhere (section 16.1); by symmetry, $\varphi _1\sim \varphi _2$.

Let $T_1,\ldots ,T_{n-1}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$ and put $R=\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle $, with $R=1$ if $n=1$. For a bounded quasi-psh difference $a$ with presentation $(\varphi _1,\varphi _2)$ we define the signed measure

17.1
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R & =\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \\ & \quad -\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _2)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle , \end{aligned} \end{equation}

where $\kappa $ is a multiple of $\omega $ so large that $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$. Both terms are non-pluripolar products of $n$ closed positive $(1,1)$-currents, hence finite measures by theorem 15.4.3; for $n=1$ they are first-degree non-pluripolar products.

The measure $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge R$ does not depend on the choices made. First, let $\theta $ be any smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form with $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and let $\kappa \geq \theta $ be a multiple of $\omega $, so that $\varphi _1,\varphi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\kappa )$. Multilinearity of non-pluripolar products (proposition 15.1.5) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle & =\left\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \\ & \quad +\left\langle (\kappa -\theta )\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \end{aligned} \]

for $j=1,2$. The last term cancels in the difference, so the right side of (17.1) does not change when $\kappa $ is replaced by $\theta $. Taking for $\theta $ a smaller multiple of $\omega $ with respect to which $\varphi _1,\varphi _2$ are psh, we see that the measure does not depend on $\kappa $; hence it may be computed with any form $\theta $ as above in place of $\kappa $. Finally, the measure does not depend on the presentation of $a$. Let $(\psi _1,\psi _2)$ be another presentation and choose $\kappa $ so that all four functions are $\kappa $-psh. Then $\varphi _1+\psi _2=\varphi _2+\psi _1$ quasi-everywhere, hence everywhere, since both sides are $2\kappa $-psh. Therefore the currents $2\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi _1+\psi _2)$ and $2\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi _2+\psi _1)$ coincide. By multilinearity,

\[ \begin{aligned} & \left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _1)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle +\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _2)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle \\ & \qquad =\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _2)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle +\left\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _1)\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\right\rangle . \end{aligned} \]

All four measures are finite, so the two presentations give the same measure (17.1). We extend the notation linearly to finite sums of such products.

The truncation argument below passes to the limit in integrals whose weights converge only in capacity. We use the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega =\operatorname {Cap}_{\omega ,0}$ of definition 16.7.5, relative to $V_\omega =0$. Quasi-continuity of functions on $X$ is understood as in section 16.1; by proposition 16.9.9, the exceptional open sets may be taken of arbitrarily small $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. Recall from theorem 16.9.8 that Borel functions $f_ j$ converge in capacity to $f$ if $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ |f_ j-f|>\varepsilon \} )\to 0$ for every $\varepsilon >0$, and that a family of positive Borel measures on $X$ is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ if the supremum of $\lambda (A)$, over all members $\lambda $ of the family and all Borel sets $A$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A)\leq \delta $, tends to zero as $\delta \downarrow 0$.

Lemma 17.1.1. Let $\nu _ j$ and $\nu $ be positive Borel measures on $X$ such that $\nu _ j\to \nu $ weakly, $\sup _ j\nu _ j(X)<\infty $, and the family $\{ \nu ,\nu _1,\nu _2,\ldots \} $ is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. Let $f_ j$ and $f$ be Borel functions on $X$ with $|f_ j|,|f|\leq B$ for a constant $B$, and suppose that $f_ j\to f$ in capacity and that $f$ is continuous outside open sets of arbitrarily small capacity: for every $\eta >0$ there is an open set $G\subset X$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (G)<\eta $ such that the restriction of $f$ to $X\setminus G$ is continuous. (By proposition 16.9.9(3), the last condition holds if $f$ is quasi-continuous.) Then

\[ \int _ Xf_ j\, \nu _ j\longrightarrow \int _ Xf\, \nu . \]

Proof. We write the difference of the two integrals as

\[ \int _ X(f_ j-f)\, \nu _ j+\left(\int _ Xf\, \nu _ j-\int _ Xf\, \nu \right) \]

and show that both terms tend to zero. For the first term fix $\varepsilon >0$ and put $A_ j=\{ |f_ j-f|>\varepsilon \} $. Splitting the integral over $A_ j$ and its complement gives

\[ \left|\int _ X(f_ j-f)\, \nu _ j\right|\leq 2B\nu _ j(A_ j)+\varepsilon \sup _ k\nu _ k(X). \]

Since $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A_ j)\to 0$, uniform absolute continuity gives $\nu _ j(A_ j)\to 0$. Thus the upper limit of the first term is at most $\varepsilon \sup _ k\nu _ k(X)$, and $\varepsilon $ is arbitrary.

For the second term let $\eta >0$, and choose an open set $G$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (G)<\eta $ such that the restriction of $f$ to the compact set $X\setminus G$ is continuous. By lemma 7.1.4, there is a metric $d$ on $X$ which induces its topology, and by lemma 9.4.3, applied in the metric space $(X,d)$, the restriction of $f$ to $X\setminus G$ extends to a continuous function $g$ on $X$ with $|g|\leq B$. Since $X$ is compact, $g$ has compact support, so weak convergence of measures (see the discussion following definition 7.2.4) gives $\int _ Xg\, \nu _ j\to \int _ Xg\, \nu $. Since $f=g$ outside $G$, we have $|\int _ Xf\, \lambda -\int _ Xg\, \lambda |\leq 2B\lambda (G)$ for each $\lambda \in \{ \nu ,\nu _1,\nu _2,\ldots \} $. The upper limit of the second term is therefore at most $4B$ times the supremum of $\lambda (G)$ over the family, which tends to zero as $\eta \downarrow 0$ by uniform absolute continuity. $\square$

Lemma 17.1.2. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $\varphi _1,\varphi _2,\psi _1,\psi _2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $\varphi _1\sim \varphi _2$ and $\psi _1\sim \psi _2$. Put $a=\varphi _1-\varphi _2$, $b=\psi _1-\psi _2$, and

\[ S_ j=\sum _{k=0}^{n-1} \left\langle \theta _{\varphi _ j}\wedge \theta _{\psi _1}^{k} \wedge \theta _{\psi _2}^{n-1-k}\right\rangle , \qquad j=1,2. \]

Then

17.8
\begin{equation} \int _ Xa(\mu _{\psi _1}-\mu _{\psi _2}) =\int _ Xb(S_1-S_2). \end{equation}

Proof. If the potentials were bounded, (17.8) would follow from Stokes' formula. We reduce to that case by truncation in three steps, assuming first that $\theta $ is a Kähler form, and remove this assumption in a fourth step.

Step 1: agreement on deep sublevel sets. Let $\theta =\kappa $ be a Kähler form, and assume that $\varphi _1\leq \varphi _2$, $\psi _1\leq \psi _2$, and that there is $L>0$ such that $\varphi _1=\varphi _2$ on $U=\{ \varphi _1<-L\} $ and $\psi _1=\psi _2$ on $V=\{ \psi _1<-L\} $. These sets are open, because $\varphi _1$ and $\psi _1$ are upper semicontinuous. For an integer $l>L$ put $\varphi _ j^ l=\max \{ \varphi _ j,-l\} $ and $\psi _ j^ l=\max \{ \psi _ j,-l\} $. These are bounded $\kappa $-psh functions; let $a^ l=\varphi _1^ l-\varphi _2^ l$, $b^ l=\psi _1^ l-\psi _2^ l$, and let $S_ j^ l$ be defined as $S_ j$ with the truncated functions. The algebraic identity $x^ n-y^ n=(x-y)\sum _ kx^ ky^{n-1-k}$ for the commuting Bedford–Taylor products gives

\[ \mu _{\psi _1^ l}-\mu _{\psi _2^ l}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l,\qquad S_1^ l-S_2^ l=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l,\qquad \Sigma ^ l=\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}\kappa _{\psi _1^ l}^ k\wedge \kappa _{\psi _2^ l}^{n-1-k}. \]

Since $\Sigma ^ l$ is a closed positive current, Stokes' formula for bounded potentials (lemma 15.4.2) yields $\int _ Xa^ l\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l=\int _ Xb^ l\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a^ l\wedge \Sigma ^ l$, which is (17.8) for the truncated functions.

We show that both sides of this identity coincide with the corresponding sides of (17.8). On $U$ we have $a^ l=a=0$. On the open set $V$ we have $\psi _1^ l=\psi _2^ l$ and $\psi _1=\psi _2$, so $\mu _{\psi _1^ l}=\mu _{\psi _2^ l}$ and $\mu _{\psi _1}=\mu _{\psi _2}$ there, by locality of Bedford–Taylor and non-pluripolar products. Hence both left sides are integrals over the closed set $F=X\setminus (U\cup V)$. On $F$ we have $\varphi _2\geq \varphi _1\geq -L$ and $\psi _2\geq \psi _1\geq -L$. Thus $F$ is contained in the plurifine open set where all four potentials exceed $-l$; there every truncation coincides with its potential, so the bounded products of the truncations coincide with the corresponding non-pluripolar products, by plurifine locality (proposition 15.1.5). Consequently the two left sides are equal. The right sides are treated in the same way with the roles of $U$ and $V$ exchanged: $b^ l=b=0$ on $V$, while $S_1^ l=S_2^ l$ and $S_1=S_2$ on $U$. This proves (17.8) under the assumptions of this step.

Step 2: a Kähler background with a margin. Let $\theta =\kappa $ be Kähler, and suppose that all four potentials are $\lambda \kappa $-psh for some $0<\lambda <1$. Adding a constant to one of the four potentials changes neither side of (17.8). Indeed, the measures are unchanged, and a constant added to $a$ or $b$ contributes nothing, since $\int _ X(\mu _{\psi _1}-\mu _{\psi _2})=0$ and $\int _ X(S_1-S_2)=0$ by corollary 15.7.5. Subtracting suitable constants from $\varphi _1$ and $\psi _1$, and then a common constant from each pair, we may therefore assume that all four potentials are nonpositive and that

\[ \varphi _1\leq \varphi _2\leq \varphi _1+B, \qquad \psi _1\leq \psi _2\leq \psi _1+B \]

for a constant $B\geq 1$. Take a sequence $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ with $\lambda (1+\varepsilon )<1$ and put

\[ \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }=\max \{ \varphi _1,(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2\} , \qquad \psi _{2,\varepsilon }=\max \{ \psi _1,(1+\varepsilon )\psi _2\} . \]

Since $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2$ is $(1+\varepsilon )\lambda \kappa $-psh, the function $\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }$ is $\kappa $-psh, and $\varphi _1\leq \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }\leq \varphi _2\leq \varphi _1+B$ because $\varphi _2\leq 0$. Where $\varphi _1<-B-B/\varepsilon $,

\[ (1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2\leq (1+\varepsilon )(\varphi _1+B)=\varphi _1+B+\varepsilon (\varphi _1+B)<\varphi _1, \]

so $\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }=\varphi _1$ there. The same holds for the $\psi $'s. Step 1 therefore applies to the pairs $(\varphi _1,\varphi _{2,\varepsilon })$ and $(\psi _1,\psi _{2,\varepsilon })$ and gives (17.8) with $a_\varepsilon =\varphi _1-\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }$ and $b_\varepsilon =\psi _1-\psi _{2,\varepsilon }$ in place of $a,b$, and with the corresponding measures.

Step 3: the limit $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, the functions $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2$ increase to $\varphi _2$, so $\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }$ increases to $\varphi _{2,0}=\max \{ \varphi _1,\varphi _2\} =\varphi _2$; likewise $\psi _{2,\varepsilon }\uparrow \psi _{2,0}=\psi _2$. By corollary 15.7.6, every measure in the identity of Step 2 converges weakly to the corresponding measure in (17.8). To pass to the limit in the integrals we apply lemma 17.1.1 to each of the finitely many terms, each of which is the integral of a bounded weight against a positive measure.

We first check uniform absolute continuity. For $\varepsilon \geq 0$ put

\[ \zeta _\varepsilon =\tfrac 15 (\varphi _1+\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }+\psi _1+\psi _{2,\varepsilon }), \qquad \chi =\zeta _0-B. \]

Since $-B\leq \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }-\varphi _2\leq 0$ and $-B\leq \psi _{2,\varepsilon }-\psi _2\leq 0$, we have $\chi \leq \zeta _\varepsilon \leq \chi +B$ for all $\varepsilon \geq 0$. Each of the four potentials is $\lambda \kappa $-psh, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \geq -\frac45\lambda \kappa $, and $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $ is the sum of $(1-4\lambda /5)\kappa $ and the closed positive current $\frac45\lambda \kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $. In particular $\chi $ is $\kappa $-psh, and expanding $\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi )^ n\rangle $ by multilinearity and compatibility with smooth factors (proposition 15.1.5, proposition 15.3.3) gives $\int _ X\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi )^ n\rangle \geq (1-4\lambda /5)^ n\int _ X\kappa ^ n>0$.

Now let $\mu $ refer to the background $\kappa $. By the identity

\[ 5\kappa _{\zeta _\varepsilon } =\kappa _{\varphi _1}+\kappa _{\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }} +\kappa _{\psi _1}+\kappa _{\psi _{2,\varepsilon }}+\kappa \]

and multilinearity, each measure occurring in the identity of Step 2 (for $\varepsilon >0$) or in (17.8) (for $\varepsilon =0$) is a non-pluripolar product of $n$ of the currents on the right, and hence is at most $5^ n\mu _{\zeta _\varepsilon }$. The potential $q=B^{-1}\zeta _\varepsilon +(1-B^{-1})\chi $ satisfies $\chi \leq q\leq \chi +1$, so $q-1$ is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }$, and $\mu _ q\geq B^{-n}\mu _{\zeta _\varepsilon }$ by multilinearity, since $B\geq 1$. Hence $\mu _{\zeta _\varepsilon }(A)\leq B^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }(A)$ for every Borel set $A$, and every measure in question, for every $\varepsilon \geq 0$, is bounded by $5^ nB^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }$; in particular its total mass is at most $5^ nB^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }(X)=5^ nB^ n\int _ X\langle (\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi )^ n\rangle \leq 5^ nB^ n\int _ X\kappa ^ n$, by proposition 16.7.6, theorem 15.4.3. Since $\chi $ has positive mass, theorem 16.9.8 bounds $\operatorname {Cap}_{\kappa ,\chi }(A)$ by a continuous nondecreasing function of $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (A)$ vanishing at zero. Uniform absolute continuity follows.

Next we check the weights. Assign the value zero to $a_\varepsilon ,b_\varepsilon ,a,b$ on the pole sets; these are Borel functions with values in $[-B,0]$. Given $\eta >0$, proposition 16.9.9(3), applied to each of the four potentials with the bound $\eta /4$, and the countable subadditivity of $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ (proposition 16.7.6) give an open set $G$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (G)<\eta $ which contains the pole sets of the four potentials and such that the four potentials are finite and continuous on the compact set $X\setminus G$. Then $a$ and $b$ are continuous on $X\setminus G$, so they satisfy the continuity hypothesis of lemma 17.1.1. Moreover the four potentials are bounded in absolute value on $X\setminus G$ by a constant $C_\eta >0$. Since $(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2\leq \varphi _{2,\varepsilon }\leq \varphi _2$, we have on $X\setminus G$

\[ |a_\varepsilon -a|=|\varphi _{2,\varepsilon }-\varphi _2|\leq |(1+\varepsilon )\varphi _2-\varphi _2|\leq \varepsilon C_\eta , \]

and likewise $|b_\varepsilon -b|\leq \varepsilon C_\eta $. Hence, for every $\delta >0$ and $\varepsilon <\delta /C_\eta $, the sets $\{ |a_\varepsilon -a|>\delta \} $ and $\{ |b_\varepsilon -b|>\delta \} $ lie in $G$ and have capacity less than $\eta $. Since $\eta $ is arbitrary, $a_\varepsilon \to a$ and $b_\varepsilon \to b$ in capacity. Lemma 17.1.1 now passes the identity of Step 2 to the limit, which is (17.8).

Step 4: an arbitrary background. For all sufficiently large $s>0$ we have $s\omega \geq \theta $ and the form $\kappa _ s=\theta +s\omega $ is Kähler. Each $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is then $\tfrac 12\kappa _ s$-psh, because $\tfrac 12\kappa _ s+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q=\theta _ q+\tfrac 12(s\omega -\theta )\geq 0$. By Steps 2 and 3 with $\lambda =\frac12$, (17.8) holds in the background $\kappa _ s$ for all large $s$. Since $\kappa _ s+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q=\theta _ q+s\omega $, multilinearity and compatibility with smooth factors (proposition 15.1.5, proposition 15.3.3) show that every measure in the identity is a polynomial in $s$ whose coefficients are finite measures of the form $\langle \theta _{q_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{q_ k}\rangle \wedge \omega ^{n-k}$; the weights $a,b$ do not depend on $s$. Both sides of the identity are therefore polynomials in $s$, and since they agree for all large $s$, their coefficients agree. The constant coefficients are the two sides of (17.8) in the background $\theta $. $\square$

Theorem 17.1.3. Let $a,b$ be bounded quasi-psh differences on $X$, and let $T_1,\ldots ,T_{n-1}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then

17.9
\begin{equation} \int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle =\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle , \end{equation}

where the Hessian factors are the signed measures defined in (17.1).

Proof. Step 1: a repeated factor and a common background. Let $(\varphi _1,\varphi _2)$ and $(\psi _1,\psi _2)$ be presentations of $a$ and $b$, and suppose that these four functions and a further potential $\eta $ all belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for one smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $. For $0\leq t\leq 1$ the functions $\psi _ j^ t=(1-t)\eta +t\psi _ j$ are $\theta $-psh, and $\psi _1^ t-\psi _2^ t=tb$ is bounded. Lemma 17.1.2, applied to $\varphi _1,\varphi _2,\psi _1^ t,\psi _2^ t$, gives

\[ \int _ Xa(\mu _{\psi _1^ t}-\mu _{\psi _2^ t})=t\int _ Xb(S_1^ t-S_2^ t), \qquad S_ j^ t=\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}\left\langle \theta _{\varphi _ j}\wedge \theta _{\psi _1^ t}^ k\wedge \theta _{\psi _2^ t}^{n-1-k}\right\rangle . \]

Since $\theta _{\psi _ j^ t}=(1-t)\theta _\eta +t\theta _{\psi _ j}$, multilinearity expands every measure here as a polynomial in $t$ with finite measures as coefficients, so both sides are polynomials in $t$. We compare their coefficients of $t$. On the left this coefficient is

\[ n\int _ Xa\left(\langle \theta _{\psi _1}\wedge \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle -\langle \theta _{\psi _2}\wedge \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle \right) =n\int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \langle \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle , \]

where we used that (17.1) may be computed in any smooth background with respect to which the presentation is psh. On the right it is $\int _ Xb(S_1^0-S_2^0)$, and $S_ j^0=n\langle \theta _{\varphi _ j}\wedge \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle $, so it equals $n\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \langle \theta _\eta ^{n-1}\rangle $. This proves (17.9) when $T_1=\cdots =T_{n-1}=\theta _\eta $ and all potentials share the background $\theta $. For $n=1$ there are no currents $T_ j$, and (17.9) is (17.8) for a Kähler background with respect to which the four functions are psh.

Step 2: arbitrary currents. Let $n>1$. By theorem 14.1.7, write $T_ j=\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ j$ with $\theta _ j$ smooth closed and $\eta _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$. For positive numbers $r_1,\ldots ,r_{n-1}$ with $\sum _ jr_ j=1$ put $\theta _ r=\sum _ jr_ j\theta _ j$ and $\eta _ r=\sum _ jr_ j\eta _ j$, so that $\sum _ jr_ jT_ j=\theta _ r+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ r$. The potential $\eta _ r$ is $(\theta _ r+s\omega )$-psh for every $s\geq 0$, since $\theta _ r+s\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ r=\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega \geq 0$. Let $\kappa $ be a multiple of $\omega $ such that the presentations of $a$ and $b$ are $\kappa $-psh. Since the forms $\theta _ r$ are convex combinations of the finitely many smooth forms $\theta _ j$, there is $s_0$ with $\theta _ r+s\omega \geq \kappa $ for all $r$ and all $s\geq s_0$, and then the presentations are $(\theta _ r+s\omega )$-psh as well. Step 1 applied to the current $\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega =(\theta _ r+s\omega )+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ r$ gives

\[ \int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega \Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle =\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j+s\omega \Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle , \]

where, by the independence of (17.1) from the background, the Hessian factors may be computed in one fixed Kähler background. Expanding the repeated current by multilinearity, both sides become polynomials in $s$, and comparing constant coefficients gives

\[ \int _ Xa\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j\Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle =\int _ Xb\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge \Bigl \langle \Bigl (\sum _ jr_ jT_ j\Bigr )^{n-1}\Bigr \rangle . \]

Both sides are homogeneous polynomials of degree $n-1$ in $(r_1,\ldots ,r_{n-1})$. Since they agree on the open simplex, homogeneity makes them agree on the open positive cone of $\mathbb R^{n-1}$. Hence their coefficients agree: a polynomial that vanishes on a nonempty open set is zero, because all its partial derivatives vanish at a point $r_0$ of that set and it equals its finite Taylor expansion at $r_0$. The coefficient of $r_1\cdots r_{n-1}$ is $(n-1)!$ times the corresponding side of (17.9). $\square$

Lemma 17.1.4. Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $u\sim v$, let $T_1,\ldots ,T_{n-1}$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, and put $R=\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle $. Then

\[ \int _ X(u-v)\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-v)\wedge R\leq 0. \]

Proof. Put $\lambda _ u=\langle \theta _ u\wedge T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_{n-1}\rangle $ and define $\lambda _ v$ in the same way, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-v)\wedge R=\lambda _ u-\lambda _ v$ by (17.1). Since $u-v$ is bounded, corollary 15.7.5, applied after writing $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta _ i$ with smooth $\theta _ i$ and $\eta _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ (theorem 14.1.7), gives $\lambda _ u(X)=\lambda _ v(X)$. Hence the integral does not change if a constant is added to $u$, and we may assume $u\leq v$. We must show that $\int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ v\leq \int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ u$.

Let $t\geq 0$ and $\varepsilon >0$, and put $q=\max \{ u,v-t-\varepsilon \} $. Then $v-t-\varepsilon \leq q\leq v$, so $\lambda _ q(X)=\lambda _ v(X)=\lambda _ u(X)$ by the same corollary. The sets $O_1=\{ u<v-t-\varepsilon \} $ and $O_2=\{ u>v-t-\varepsilon \} $ are disjoint and plurifine open, by proposition 10.5.1 applied in charts after adding the same smooth local potential of $\theta $ to $u$ and $v$; neither of them meets the common pole set of $u$ and $v$. Moreover $q=v-t-\varepsilon $ on $O_1$ and $q=u$ on $O_2$, so plurifine locality (proposition 15.1.5) gives $\lambda _ q=\lambda _ v$ on $O_1$ and $\lambda _ q=\lambda _ u$ on $O_2$, and hence

\[ \lambda _ v(O_1)+\lambda _ u(O_2)\leq \lambda _ q(X)=\lambda _ u(X). \]

Since pole sets carry no mass, it follows that

\[ \lambda _ v(\{ u<v-t-\varepsilon \} ) \leq \lambda _ u(\{ u\leq v-t-\varepsilon \} ) \leq \lambda _ u(\{ u<v-t\} ). \]

As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ the sets $\{ u<v-t-\varepsilon \} $ increase to $\{ u<v-t\} $, and therefore

\[ \lambda _ v(\{ u<v-t\} )\leq \lambda _ u(\{ u<v-t\} )\qquad \text{for every }t\geq 0. \]

On the common finite locus the bounded function $v-u\geq 0$ equals $\int _0^\infty \mathbf1_{\{ u<v-t\} }\, \mathrm dt$. Integrating the last inequality in $t$ and using Tonelli's theorem, we obtain $\int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ v\leq \int _ X(v-u)\lambda _ u$, as required. $\square$

17.2 Relative Monge–Ampère energy

Fix a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ whose class is big, and a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, that is, $P_\theta [\phi ]=\phi $ and $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle >0$ (definition 16.3.1). In particular $\phi \leq 0$ and $\sup _ X\phi =0$. Recall from definition 15.8.3 that $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ consists of the potentials $u\preceq \phi $ with $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =m$. If $u_1,\ldots ,u_ n\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _{u_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{u_ n}\rangle =m. \]

Indeed $P_\theta [u_ j]=P_\theta [\phi ]=\phi $ by proposition 16.4.10, and the singularity envelope preserves mixed masses by proposition 16.3.2.

Definition 17.2.1. Put

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi ) & =\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\sim \phi \} ,\\ \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi ) & =\left\{ u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi ): \int _ X|u-\phi |\, \mu _ u<\infty \right\} . \end{aligned} \]

The potentials in $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ have bounded difference from $\phi $, but they need not be bounded functions. The class $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ is the relative finite-energy class. For the potential $\phi =V_\theta $ with minimal singularities (definition 14.2.6) we omit the reference from these notations.

If $u\sim \phi $, then $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =m$ by corollary 15.7.5, and $|u-\phi |$ is bounded. Hence
\[ \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )\subset \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )\subset \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi ). \]

The potential $V_\theta $ is indeed a model potential: $P_\theta [V_\theta ]=V_\theta $ by proposition 16.3.2(4), and $\int _ X\langle \theta _{V_\theta }^ n\rangle =\operatorname {vol}(\{ \theta \} )>0$ by definition 15.8.1, proposition 15.8.2. For $\phi =V_\theta $, the class $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta )$ consists of the potentials with minimal singularities, and $\mathcal E(X,\theta )=\mathcal E(X,\theta ,V_\theta )$ is the class of potentials of full Monge–Ampère mass of definition 15.8.3. These classes of global potentials are not related to the local class $\mathcal E_0(B)$ of bounded functions on a ball in definition 9.10.1.

Definition 17.2.2. For $u\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ define

17.12
\begin{equation} E_\theta ^\phi (u)=\frac1{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n \int _ X(u-\phi )\, \langle \theta _ u^ j\wedge \theta _\phi ^{n-j}\rangle . \end{equation}

All these integrals are finite, since the weight is bounded and every mixed measure has total mass $m$. For $\phi =V_\theta $ we write $E_\theta $.

Proposition 17.2.3. Let $u,v\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ and put $u_ t=(1-t)u+tv$ for $0\leq t\leq 1$. Then

17.13
\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t) =\int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _{u_ t}, \end{equation}

with one-sided derivatives at the endpoints, and

17.14
\begin{equation} E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u) =\frac1{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n\int _ X(v-u) \langle \theta _ v^ j\wedge \theta _ u^{n-j}\rangle . \end{equation}

In particular, the energy is increasing, and $E_\theta ^\phi (u+C)=E_\theta ^\phi (u)+Cm$ for every constant $C$.

Proof. Let $w\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$. For $0\leq r\leq 1$ the potential $\phi +r(w-\phi )=(1-r)\phi +rw$ again belongs to $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, and multilinearity gives

\[ \mu _{\phi +r(w-\phi )}=\sum _{j=0}^ n\binom nj r^ j(1-r)^{n-j}\langle \theta _ w^ j\wedge \theta _\phi ^{n-j}\rangle . \]

Since $\binom nj\int _0^1r^ j(1-r)^{n-j}\, \mathrm dr=1/(n+1)$, as one sees by integrating by parts $j$ times, we obtain

17.15
\begin{equation} E_\theta ^\phi (w)=\int _0^1\int _ X(w-\phi )\, \mu _{\phi +r(w-\phi )}\, \mathrm dr. \end{equation}

Now put $h=v-u$ and $q_{r,t}=\phi +r(u_ t-\phi )$. Since $\theta _{q_{r,t}}=(1-r)\theta _\phi +r(1-t)\theta _ u+rt\theta _ v$, the measures $\mu _{q_{r,t}}$ are polynomials in $(r,t)$ whose coefficients are mixed measures of total mass $m$, and the weights $u_ t-\phi =(u-\phi )+th$ are bounded. All expressions below are therefore polynomials in $(r,t)$ with finite coefficients, and the derivatives are derivatives of polynomials. Identities between such polynomials of measures follow from the corresponding identities between polynomials in commuting variables $x,y,z$, by substituting the mixed measure $\langle \theta _\phi ^ a\wedge \theta _ u^ b\wedge \theta _ v^ c\rangle $ for the monomial $x^ ay^ bz^ c$; multilinearity shows that this substitution is compatible with the products occurring here. For instance, the identities $\partial _ t\ell ^ n=nr(z-y)\ell ^{n-1}$ and $\partial _ r\ell ^ n=n\bigl ((1-t)y+tz-x\bigr )\ell ^{n-1}$ for $\ell =(1-r)x+r(1-t)y+rtz$ give

17.16
\begin{equation} \partial _ t\mu _{q_{r,t}}=nr\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle ,\qquad \partial _ r\mu _{q_{r,t}}=n\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ t-\phi )\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle , \end{equation}

in the sense of (17.1). Differentiating (17.15) with $w=u_ t$ and using the first of these identities, we obtain

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t) =\int _0^1\left[\int _ Xh\, \mu _{q_{r,t}} +nr\int _ X(u_ t-\phi )\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle \right]\mathrm dr. \]

By theorem 17.1.3, applied to the bounded quasi-psh differences $u_ t-\phi $ and $h$, the second integral equals $\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ t-\phi )\wedge \langle \theta _{q_{r,t}}^{n-1}\rangle $, which is $\frac1n\partial _ r\int _ Xh\, \mu _{q_{r,t}}$ by the second identity. The integrand in $r$ is therefore $\partial _ r\bigl [r\int _ Xh\, \mu _{q_{r,t}}\bigr ]$, and since $q_{1,t}=u_ t$,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t)=\int _ Xh\, \mu _{u_ t}. \]

This is (17.13). Integrating it from $t=0$ to $t=1$, and expanding $\mu _{u_ t}=\sum _ j\binom nj t^ j(1-t)^{n-j}\langle \theta _ v^ j\wedge \theta _ u^{n-j}\rangle $ with the same beta integrals, gives (17.14). If $u\leq v$, every term of the cocycle formula is nonnegative, so the energy is increasing. Every mixed measure has total mass $m$, so taking $v=u+C$ in the cocycle formula gives the last assertion. $\square$

Proposition 17.2.4. The energy is concave on $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, and for $u,v\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$,

17.17
\begin{equation} \int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _ v \leq E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u) \leq \int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _ u. \end{equation}

Moreover $|E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u)|\leq m\sup |v-u|$, the supremum being taken over the common finite locus. If $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\psi \sim \phi $, then the functional $E_\theta ^\psi $ defined by (17.12) with $\psi $ in place of $\phi $ satisfies

\[ E_\theta ^\psi (u)=E_\theta ^\phi (u)-E_\theta ^\phi (\psi ). \]

Proof. Let $u_ t=(1-t)u+tv$ and $h=v-u$. Then $\partial _ t\mu _{u_ t}=n\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \langle \theta _{u_ t}^{n-1}\rangle $. Indeed, by multilinearity and (17.1), both sides expand into combinations of the mixed measures $\langle \theta _ u^ a\wedge \theta _ v^{n-a}\rangle $, obtained by substituting $\langle \theta _ u^ a\wedge \theta _ v^{n-a}\rangle $ for $y^ az^{n-a}$ in the two sides of the identity $\partial _ t((1-t)y+tz)^ n=n(z-y)((1-t)y+tz)^{n-1}$ in commuting variables $y,z$. Differentiating (17.13) once more, again as a polynomial in $t$, and using this identity, we obtain

\[ \frac{\mathrm d^2}{\mathrm dt^2}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t) =n\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \langle \theta _{u_ t}^{n-1}\rangle \leq 0 \]

by lemma 17.1.4. Thus $t\mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t)$ is concave, and its derivative decreases from $\int _ Xh\, \mu _ u$ at $t=0$ to $\int _ Xh\, \mu _ v$ at $t=1$. Integrating the derivative over $[0,1]$ gives the tangent bounds (17.17). Since $\int _ X\langle \theta _{u_ t}^ n\rangle =m$, the first variation formula also gives $|E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u)|\leq m\sup |v-u|$. Finally, the cocycle formula (17.14) applied to the pair $\psi ,u$ reads $E_\theta ^\phi (u)-E_\theta ^\phi (\psi )=E_\theta ^\psi (u)$. $\square$

We now extend the energy to all potentials that are at least as singular as $\phi $.

Definition 17.2.5. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi $ put

\[ E_\theta ^\phi (u)=\inf \{ E_\theta ^\phi (w):w\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi ),\ u\leq w\} . \]
The family on the right is nonempty, since $\phi +C$ belongs to it for large $C$; hence $E_\theta ^\phi (u)\in [-\infty ,\infty )$. Because the energy is increasing on $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, the new definition agrees with (17.12) there.

Proposition 17.2.6. The extended energy is increasing, satisfies $E_\theta ^\phi (u+C)=E_\theta ^\phi (u)+Cm$, and

\[ E_\theta ^\phi (u)=\lim _{k\to \infty } E_\theta ^\phi (\max \{ u,\phi -k\} ), \]

where the limit exists in $[-\infty ,\infty )$.

Proof. If $u\leq u'$, every majorant of $u'$ in the definition is a majorant of $u$, so $E_\theta ^\phi (u)\leq E_\theta ^\phi (u')$. Translating majorants by a constant and using the shift formula on $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ gives the shift formula. The potentials $u_ k=\max \{ u,\phi -k\} $ belong to $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, decrease with $k$ and dominate $u$; hence $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)$ decreases and is bounded below by $E_\theta ^\phi (u)$. Conversely, let $w$ be any majorant in the definition, and choose $C$ with $w\geq \phi -C$. For $k\geq C$ both $u$ and $\phi -k$ lie below $w$, so $u_ k\leq w$ and $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)\leq E_\theta ^\phi (w)$. Letting $k\to \infty $ and taking the infimum over $w$ gives $\lim _ kE_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)\leq E_\theta ^\phi (u)$. $\square$

Proposition 17.2.7. Let $(u_ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u_ i\preceq \phi $, and suppose that $u=\inf _ iu_ i$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

\[ E_\theta ^\phi (u_ i)\downarrow E_\theta ^\phi (u). \]

Consequently the extended energy is concave on $\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\preceq \phi \} $, and the set where it is finite is convex.

Proof. First let the net be a sequence $(u_ j)$. By monotonicity $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j)$ decreases to a limit $L\geq E_\theta ^\phi (u)$. Let $w\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ with $w\geq u$, and put $v_ j=\max \{ u_ j,w\} $. Then $v_ j$ decreases pointwise to $\max \{ u,w\} =w$. Since $u_ j\leq u_1\leq \phi +C$ and $w\geq \phi -C$ for some constant $C$, the differences $v_ j-w$ are uniformly bounded. The tangent bound (17.17) gives

\[ 0\leq E_\theta ^\phi (v_ j)-E_\theta ^\phi (w) \leq \int _ X(v_ j-w)\, \mu _ w\longrightarrow 0 \]

by bounded convergence. Since $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j)\leq E_\theta ^\phi (v_ j)$, we get $L\leq E_\theta ^\phi (w)$, and taking the infimum over $w$ gives $L\leq E_\theta ^\phi (u)$.

For a general net, corollary 14.2.5 shows that $u_ i\to u$ in $L^1(X)$; its hypothesis holds because $\sup _ Xu_ i\geq \sup _ Xu>-\infty $. Choose indices $i_1\leq i_2\leq \cdots $ with $\| u_{i_ k}-u\| _{L^1}<1/k$. The decreasing pointwise limit $\tilde u$ of $(u_{i_ k})$ is a $\theta $-psh function by theorem 8.3.1, applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $, since it dominates $u$ and so is not identically $-\infty $. By monotone convergence, $\int _ X(\tilde u-u)\, \omega ^ n=\lim _ k\int _ X(u_{i_ k}-u)\, \omega ^ n=0$, so $\tilde u=u$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere. The sequence case gives $E_\theta ^\phi (u_{i_ k})\to E_\theta ^\phi (u)$. The net $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ i)$ is decreasing, so its limit is its infimum, which is at most $\lim _ kE_\theta ^\phi (u_{i_ k})=E_\theta ^\phi (u)$; the reverse inequality is monotonicity. (The selected indices need not be cofinal in $I$; this is why we compare infima.)

For concavity let $u,v\preceq \phi $ and $0<t<1$; we may assume that $E_\theta ^\phi (u)$ and $E_\theta ^\phi (v)$ are finite, since otherwise there is nothing to prove. The potentials $(1-t)\max \{ u,\phi -k\} +t\max \{ v,\phi -k\} $ belong to $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ and decrease to $(1-t)u+tv$ as $k\to \infty $. Concavity on $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ and decreasing continuity give

\[ E_\theta ^\phi ((1-t)u+tv) \geq (1-t)E_\theta ^\phi (u)+tE_\theta ^\phi (v). \]

In particular the finite locus of the energy is convex. $\square$

Theorem 17.2.8. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi $,

\[ E_\theta ^\phi (u)>-\infty \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi ). \]

In particular, finite extended energy forces full mass relative to $\phi $. If $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $u\leq \phi $, then

17.18
\begin{equation} \frac1{n+1}\int _ X(\phi -u)\, \mu _ u \leq -E_\theta ^\phi (u) \leq \int _ X(\phi -u)\, \mu _ u. \end{equation}

Proof. Both conditions are invariant under adding a constant to $u$, so we may assume $u\leq \phi $. Put $u_ k=\max \{ u,\phi -k\} $ and $A_ k=\{ u>\phi -k\} $. The potentials $u_ k$ belong to $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$ and have mass $m$. Since $u_ k\leq \phi $, all terms in (17.12) are nonpositive, and keeping only the term $j=n$ gives

\[ \int _ X(\phi -u_ k)\mu _{u_ k}\leq (n+1)\bigl (-E_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)\bigr ). \]

Suppose that $E_\theta ^\phi (u)>-\infty $. Since $E_\theta ^\phi (u)\leq E_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)\leq E_\theta ^\phi (\phi )=0$, the right side is bounded by $C=-(n+1)E_\theta ^\phi (u)$. On $X\setminus A_ k$ the weight $\phi -u_ k$ equals $k$ outside a pluripolar set; on the plurifine open set $A_ k$ we have $u_ k=u$, so $\mu _{u_ k}=\mu _ u$ there by locality. Hence

\[ m-\mu _ u(A_ k)=\mu _{u_ k}(X\setminus A_ k)\leq C/k. \]

The sets $A_ k$ increase to the set where $u$ is finite, which carries $\mu _ u$, so $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle \geq m$; since $u\leq \phi $, mass monotonicity (theorem 15.7.4) gives the reverse inequality, and therefore $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. Locality also gives $\int _{A_ k}(\phi -u)\mu _ u\leq C$, and monotone convergence yields $\int _ X(\phi -u)\mu _ u\leq C$. This proves that $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and the first inequality in (17.18).

Conversely, suppose $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and let $I=\int _ X(\phi -u)\mu _ u<\infty $. The measures $\mu _{u_ k}$ and $\mu _ u$ have the same total mass $m$ and agree on $A_ k$, so $\mu _{u_ k}(X\setminus A_ k)=\mu _ u(X\setminus A_ k)$. Thus

\[ \int _ X(\phi -u_ k)\mu _{u_ k} =\int _ X\min \{ \phi -u,k\} \, \mu _ u\leq I. \]

The lower tangent bound in (17.17), applied to $u_ k$ and $\phi $, gives $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)\geq -I$. Letting $k\to \infty $ and using proposition 17.2.6, we obtain $E_\theta ^\phi (u)\geq -I>-\infty $, which is the second inequality in (17.18). The first inequality was proved above. $\square$

Example 17.2.9. Let $X$ be a compact Kähler curve, so $n=1$, and take $\theta =\omega $ and the reference $V_\omega =0$. For a smooth $\omega $-psh function $u$,

\[ E_\omega (u)=\int _ Xu\, \omega -\tfrac 12 \int _ X\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u, \]

where $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ is the real operator of (7.6), so that $\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$. Indeed, by definition $E_\omega (u)=\frac12\bigl (\int _ Xu\, \omega +\int _ Xu(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\bigr )$, and Stokes' formula (lemma 15.4.2) gives $\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=-\int _ X\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u$. Since $\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial u\wedge \bar\partial u$, the second term is a multiple of the classical Dirichlet energy of $u$.

The unbounded potential $u$ on $\mathbb P^1$ of example 16.4.8, which has full mass, also lies in $\mathcal E^1(\mathbb P^1,\omega )$. Away from its only pole $z=0$ the function $u$ is bounded, and $\mu _ u$ is a finite measure. Near the pole write $r=\log |z|^2$. If $G$ is a smooth function of one real variable, then $\bar\partial G(\log |z|^2)=G'(r)\, \mathrm d\bar z/\bar z$ and $\partial \bar\partial G(\log |z|^2)=G''(r)\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\bar z/|z|^2$ on $\mathbb C\setminus \{ 0\} $; since $\mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\bar z=2\, \mathrm dA$, where $\mathrm dA$ is Lebesgue measure, we get $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G(\log |z|^2)=\pi ^{-1}G''(r)|z|^{-2}\, \mathrm dA$, which in polar coordinates $z=\rho \mathrm e^{\mathrm i\vartheta }$, with $r=2\log \rho $, is $(2\pi )^{-1}G''(r)\, \mathrm dr\, \mathrm d\vartheta $.

The function $F$ of example 16.4.8 satisfies $F(r)=-\log (-r)$ for $r\leq r_0$, with $r_0$ sufficiently negative, and is therefore smooth there, with $F''(r)=r^{-2}$. On the punctured disc $\{ 0<|z|^2<\mathrm e^{r_0}\} $ the measure $\mu _ u$ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F(\log |z|^2)$, and it does not charge the pole; hence the image of its restriction to $\{ |z|^2<\mathrm e^{r_0}\} $ under $z\mapsto \log |z|^2$ is $F''(r)\, \mathrm dr=r^{-2}\, \mathrm dr$ on $(-\infty ,r_0)$. Since $u-F(\log |z|^2)=-\log (1+|z|^2)$ is bounded near the pole, the finiteness of $\int |u|\, \mu _ u$ near the pole reduces, after the substitution $t=-r$, to

\[ \int _ a^\infty \frac{\log t}{t^2}\, \mathrm dt =\frac{\log a+1}{a}<\infty \qquad (a>1). \]

Hence $u$ is a potential of finite energy whose difference from the reference $V_\omega =0$ is unbounded.

Corollary 17.2.10. The classes $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ are convex. If $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $u\leq v\preceq \phi $, then $v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Proof. Convex combinations of potentials with the same singularity type as $\phi $ have that singularity type. For $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, convexity follows from the concavity of the extended energy (proposition 17.2.7) and theorem 17.2.8. If $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $0<t<1$, then $(1-t)u+tv\preceq \phi $, and by multilinearity $\mu _{(1-t)u+tv}$ is a combination of the mixed measures $\langle \theta _ u^ j\wedge \theta _ v^{n-j}\rangle $ with coefficients $\binom nj(1-t)^ jt^{n-j}$. Each of these measures has total mass $m$, as noted at the beginning of this section, so $\int _ X\langle \theta _{(1-t)u+tv}^ n\rangle =m$. The last assertion follows from the monotonicity of the extended energy and theorem 17.2.8. $\square$

Lemma 17.2.11. Let $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, let $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $v\preceq \phi $, and let $\gamma \colon X\to [-\infty ,\infty ]$ be a Borel function, for instance a $\theta $-psh function, with $\gamma \geq \max \{ u,v\} $. Then, allowing infinite values,

\[ \int _ X(\gamma -u)\mu _ v \leq 2\int _ X(\gamma -u)\mu _ u +2\int _ X(\gamma -v)\mu _ v. \]

Consequently, if $u,v,w_1,\ldots ,w_ n\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then

\[ \int _ X|u-v|\, \langle \theta _{w_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{w_ n}\rangle <\infty . \]

Proof. For $t\geq 0$ we have

\[ \{ \gamma >u+2t\} \subset \{ \gamma >v+t\} \cup \{ v>u+t\} , \]

because $\gamma \leq v+t$ and $v\leq u+t$ imply $\gamma \leq u+2t$. The weights $\gamma -u$ and $\gamma -v$ are Borel functions with values in $[0,\infty ]$ on the complements of the pole sets of $u$ and $v$, respectively, and these pole sets carry no mass; the level sets above are taken on these complements. Since $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $v-t\preceq \phi $, the comparison principle of proposition 16.7.4, applied with $p=n$ to $u$ and $v-t$, gives

\[ \mu _ v(\{ v>u+t\} )\leq \mu _ u(\{ v>u+t\} )+\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle -\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =\mu _ u(\{ v>u+t\} ). \]

Integrating over level sets, we obtain

\[ \begin{split} \int _ X(\gamma -u)\mu _ v & \leq 2\int _0^\infty \mu _ v(\{ \gamma >v+t\} )\, \mathrm dt +2\int _0^\infty \mu _ u(\{ v>u+t\} )\, \mathrm dt\\ & \leq 2\int _ X(\gamma -v)\mu _ v+2\int _ X(\gamma -u)\mu _ u, \end{split} \]

where in the last step we used $(v-u)_+\leq \gamma -u$, which holds because $\gamma \geq \max \{ u,v\} $. All functions involved are nonnegative, so Tonelli's theorem applies without any integrability assumption.

For the second assertion, add constants to $u$ and $v$ so that $u,v\leq \phi $; this changes $|u-v|$ by at most a constant, and the mixed measure has finite mass $m$. Put $w=\frac1n\sum _ jw_ j$, which lies in $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by corollary 17.2.10. By multilinearity, $\langle \theta _{w_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _{w_ n}\rangle \leq n^ n\mu _ w$. Choose $C$ with $w\leq \phi +C$ and apply the first assertion to $u$, $w$ and $\gamma =\phi +C$: its right side is finite because $u,w\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$. Hence $\phi -u$ is integrable for $\mu _ w$, and so for the mixed measure. The same holds for $\phi -v$, and $|u-v|\leq (\phi -u)+(\phi -v)$. $\square$

Proposition 17.2.12. For $u,v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, the cocycle formula (17.14) and the tangent bounds (17.17) remain valid. In particular, (17.12) computes the extended energy on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Proof. All mixed measures involved have total mass $m$, so both assertions are unchanged if constants are added to $u$ and $v$; we may therefore assume $u,v\leq \phi $. Put $u_ k=\max \{ u,\phi -k\} $ and $v_ k=\max \{ v,\phi -k\} $. Fix $0\leq j\leq n$ and write $\lambda _ k=\langle \theta _{u_ k}^ j\wedge \theta _{v_ k}^{n-j}\rangle $ and $\lambda =\langle \theta _ u^ j\wedge \theta _ v^{n-j}\rangle $. We show that

17.21
\begin{equation} \int _ X(u_ k-v_ k)\lambda _ k\longrightarrow \int _ X(u-v)\lambda . \end{equation}

On the plurifine open set $A_ k=\{ u>\phi -k\} \cap \{ v>\phi -k\} $ the measures $\lambda _ k$ and $\lambda $ agree by locality, and $u_ k-v_ k=u-v$. By lemma 17.2.11, $|u-v|$ is $\lambda $-integrable. The sets $X\setminus A_ k$ decrease to the union of the pole sets of $u$, $v$ and $\phi $, which is pluripolar and therefore $\lambda $-null, so dominated convergence gives $\int _{X\setminus A_ k}|u-v|\lambda \to 0$. It remains to show that the integral of $|u_ k-v_ k|$ against $\lambda _ k$ over $X\setminus A_ k$ tends to zero.

For $z\in \{ u,v\} $ put $D_ k(z)=\{ z\leq \phi -k\} $, so that $X\setminus A_ k=D_ k(u)\cup D_ k(v)$. We claim that

17.22
\begin{equation} k\lambda _ k(D_ k(z))\leq 2^ n k\mu _ z(\{ z\leq \phi -k/2\} )\longrightarrow 0. \end{equation}

Consider $z=u$ and $j<n$, and put $q=(v_ k+\phi -k)/2$. Since $\phi -k\leq v_ k\leq \phi $, we have $q\leq \phi -k/2$ on $X$, and $u_ k=\phi -k\leq q$ on $D_ k(u)$. Moreover $\theta _{v_ k}\leq 2\theta _ q$, since $2\theta _ q-\theta _{v_ k}=\theta _\phi \geq 0$, so multilinearity gives $\lambda _ k\leq 2^{n-j}\langle \theta _{u_ k}^ j\wedge \theta _ q^{n-j}\rangle $. The potentials $q$ and $u_ k$ belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and the non-strict comparison principle of proposition 16.7.4, with $n-j$ factors $\theta _ q$ and the $j$ fixed factors $\theta _{u_ k}$, gives

\[ \lambda _ k(D_ k(u)) \leq 2^{n-j}\mu _{u_ k}(\{ u_ k\leq q\} ) \leq 2^{n-j}\mu _{u_ k}(\{ u\leq \phi -k/2\} ). \]

For $j=n$ the same bound holds directly, since then $\lambda _ k=\mu _{u_ k}$ and $D_ k(u)\subset \{ u\leq \phi -k/2\} $. The measures $\mu _{u_ k}$ and $\mu _ u$ have the same total mass and agree on $\{ u>\phi -k/2\} $ by locality, so the last mass equals $\mu _ u(\{ u\leq \phi -k/2\} )$. Since exchanging $u$ and $v$ and replacing $j$ by $n-j$ does not change $\lambda _ k$, the case $z=v$ follows from the case $z=u$. Finally,

\[ k\mu _ z(\{ z\leq \phi -k/2\} )\leq 2\int _{\{ z\leq \phi -k/2\} }(\phi -z)\mu _ z\longrightarrow 0 \]

because $\phi -z$ is $\mu _ z$-integrable. This proves (17.22).

Since $u_ k-\phi $ and $v_ k-\phi $ take values in $[-k,0]$, we have $|u_ k-v_ k|\leq k$, and (17.22) shows that the integral of $|u_ k-v_ k|$ against $\lambda _ k$ over $X\setminus A_ k$ tends to zero. This proves (17.21). Now pass to the limit in the cocycle formula for $u_ k,v_ k$: its left side converges to $E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u)$ by proposition 17.2.6, and each term on the right converges by (17.21). The tangent bounds pass to the limit in the same way, using the terms $j=n$ and $j=0$. Taking $v=\phi $ in the cocycle formula gives the last assertion. $\square$

17.3 Continuity and compactness

We keep $\theta $ and the model potential $\phi $ of mass $m$. Convergence in $L^1(X)$ refers to the volume form $\omega ^ n$, and a $\theta $-psh potential is determined by its class in $L^1(X)$. The energy is continuous along increasing nets and upper semicontinuous for $L^1$ convergence, so that normalized potentials with energy bounded below form compact sets (corollary 17.3.4), as the variational method of section 17.4 requires. The finite-energy class is also stable under rooftop envelopes, which gives finite energy along the Perron segments of section 17.6.

Proposition 17.3.1. Let $(u_ i)_{i\in I}$ be an increasing net in $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ which is uniformly bounded above on $X$. Then its regularized upper envelope $u=(\sup _ i u_ i)^*$ belongs to $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and

\[ E_\theta ^\phi (u_ i)\uparrow E_\theta ^\phi (u). \]

Proof. By lemma 16.4.2, $u_ i\leq \phi +\sup _ Xu_ i$, and the suprema $\sup _ Xu_ i$ are bounded above by hypothesis; hence $u\preceq \phi $. Since $u$ dominates every $u_ i$, monotonicity of the energy and theorem 17.2.8 show that $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Bounded differences. Consider first an increasing sequence $(u_ j)$ and a constant $C\geq 1$ such that $\phi -C\leq u_ j\leq u\leq \phi $ for all $j$. The pole sets of $u_ j$ and $u$ coincide with that of $\phi $. Let $f_ j$ be the function equal to $u-u_ j$ outside this pole set and to $0$ on it; it is a Borel function with values in $[0,C]$. By the tangent bound (17.17),

\[ 0\leq E_\theta ^\phi (u)-E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j) \leq \int _ X(u-u_ j)\, \mu _{u_ j}=\int _ Xf_ j\, \mu _{u_ j}, \]

and we show that the right side tends to zero by applying lemma 17.1.1 with $\nu _ j=\mu _{u_ j}$, $\nu =\mu _ u$, the weights $f_ j$ and the limit weight $0$. For each $j$ the potential $C^{-1}u_ j+(1-C^{-1})\phi $ lies between $\phi -1$ and $\phi $ and its measure is at least $C^{-n}\mu _{u_ j}$; hence $\mu _{u_ j}\leq C^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ on Borel sets, and likewise $\mu _ u\leq C^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$. By theorem 16.9.8, the measures $\mu _{u_ j}$ and $\mu _ u$ are therefore uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. They converge weakly by corollary 15.7.6, since $u_ j$ increases to $u$ quasi-everywhere (corollary 10.2.2).

It remains to show that $f_ j\to 0$ in capacity. Fix finitely many pairs of concentric coordinate balls $B_\alpha \Subset B'_\alpha $ as in proposition 16.9.9, chosen so that $\theta $ has a smooth potential $h_\alpha $ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B'_\alpha }$ (this is possible by lemma 13.1.2, applied on slightly larger concentric balls), and let $c$ be the constant of that proposition. Let $\delta ,\eta >0$. By proposition 16.9.9(3) there is an open set $G$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (G)<\eta $ which contains the pole set of $\phi $ and such that $\phi $ is finite and continuous on the compact set $X\setminus G$. Let $L$ be a common bound for $|\phi |$ on $X\setminus G$ and for the functions $|h_\alpha |$ on $B'_\alpha $. The functions

\[ w_{\alpha ,j}=\max \{ u_ j+h_\alpha ,-2L-C\} ,\qquad w_\alpha =\max \{ u+h_\alpha ,-2L-C\} \]

are bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $B'_\alpha $, and $w_{\alpha ,j}$ increases to $w_\alpha $ almost everywhere, because $u_ j$ increases to $u$ quasi-everywhere and pluripolar sets have Lebesgue measure zero. By theorem 9.5.1, $w_{\alpha ,j}\to w_\alpha $ locally in capacity on $B'_\alpha $. On $B_\alpha \setminus G$ we have $u_ j+h_\alpha \geq \phi -C-L\geq -2L-C$, so the truncations are inactive there and $w_\alpha -w_{\alpha ,j}=u-u_ j$. Since the balls $B_\alpha $ cover $X$,

\[ \{ f_ j>\delta \} \subset G\cup \bigcup _\alpha \bigl (B_\alpha \cap \{ w_{\alpha ,j}<w_\alpha -\delta \} \bigr ). \]

By the countable subadditivity of $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ (proposition 16.7.6) and the bound $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (E)\leq c\operatorname {Cap}_{B'_\alpha }(E)$ for Borel sets $E\subset B'_\alpha $ in proposition 16.9.9,

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ f_ j>\delta \} )\leq \eta +c\sum _\alpha \operatorname {Cap}^*_{B'_\alpha }\bigl (\overline{B_\alpha }\cap \{ w_{\alpha ,j}<w_\alpha -\delta \} \bigr ), \]

where we also used that the capacity of a Borel set is at most its outer capacity. By definition 9.4.5, the sum tends to zero as $j\to \infty $. Hence $\varlimsup _ j\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (\{ f_ j>\delta \} )\leq \eta $, and since $\eta $ is arbitrary, $f_ j\to 0$ in capacity. Lemma 17.1.1 now gives $\int _ Xf_ j\, \mu _{u_ j}\to 0$, and hence $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j)\to E_\theta ^\phi (u)$.

Sequences. Now let $(u_ j)$ be an arbitrary increasing sequence; subtracting a common constant, we may assume $u_ j\leq u\leq \phi $. Put $u_{j,k}=\max \{ u_ j,\phi -k\} $ and $u_ k=\max \{ u,\phi -k\} $. For fixed $k$, the sequence $u_{j,k}$ increases to $\max \{ \sup _ ju_ j,\phi -k\} $, whose upper semicontinuous regularization is $\max \{ (\sup _ ju_ j)^*,\phi -k\} =u_ k$, because $\phi -k$ is upper semicontinuous; moreover $\phi -k\leq u_{j,k}\leq u_ k\leq \phi $. The preceding case, with $\max \{ k,1\} $ in place of $C$, gives $E_\theta ^\phi (u_{j,k})\to E_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)$ as $j\to \infty $. We bound the truncation error uniformly in $j$. The tangent bound of proposition 17.2.12 gives

\[ 0\leq E_\theta ^\phi (u_{j,k})-E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j) \leq \int _ X(u_{j,k}-u_ j)\, \mu _{u_ j} =\int _ k^\infty \mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j<\phi -t\} )\, \mathrm dt. \]

For $t\geq k$ put $q_ t=(u_ j+\phi -t)/2$. On $\{ u_ j<\phi -t\} $ we have $u_1\leq u_ j<q_ t$, and $q_ t\leq \phi -t/2$ on $X$. Since $\mu _{q_ t}\geq 2^{-n}\mu _{u_ j}$ by multilinearity, and $q_ t,u_1\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, the comparison principle of proposition 16.7.4 gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \mu _{u_ j}(\{ u_ j<\phi -t\} ) & \leq 2^ n\mu _{q_ t}(\{ u_1<q_ t\} )\\ & \leq 2^ n\mu _{u_1}(\{ u_1<q_ t\} ) \leq 2^ n\mu _{u_1}(\{ u_1<\phi -t/2\} ). \end{aligned} \]

Consequently

17.25
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} 0& \leq E_\theta ^\phi (u_{j,k})-E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j)\\ & \leq 2^{n+1}\int _ X(\phi -u_1-k/2)_+\, \mu _{u_1}\longrightarrow 0 \end{aligned} \end{equation}

as $k\to \infty $, uniformly in $j$; the limit is zero by dominated convergence, since $\phi -u_1$ is $\mu _{u_1}$-integrable. Since $E_\theta ^\phi (u_ k)\geq E_\theta ^\phi (u)$, letting first $j\to \infty $ and then $k\to \infty $ gives $\lim _ jE_\theta ^\phi (u_ j)\geq E_\theta ^\phi (u)$, and monotonicity gives the reverse inequality.

Nets. Let $(u_ i)_{i\in I}$ be a net as in the statement. Cover $X$ by finitely many coordinate charts $D_\beta $ on which $\theta $ has smooth potentials $h_\beta $. By theorem 8.3.2, applied to the functions $u_ i+h_\beta $ on $D_\beta $, there are countable sets $J_\beta \subset I$ such that $(\sup _{i\in J_\beta }u_ i)^*=u$ on $D_\beta $. The countable set $J=\bigcup _\beta J_\beta $ then satisfies $(\sup _{i\in J}u_ i)^*=u$ on $X$, since on each $D_\beta $ this envelope lies between the envelopes over $J_\beta $ and over $I$. Since the index set is directed, we can choose indices $i_1\leq i_2\leq \cdots $ such that $i_ k$ dominates the first $k$ elements of an enumeration of $J$. Since the net is increasing, $\sup _ ku_{i_ k}\geq \sup _{i\in J}u_ i$, so the increasing sequence $(u_{i_ k})$ has regularized upper envelope $u$, and the sequence case gives $E_\theta ^\phi (u_{i_ k})\to E_\theta ^\phi (u)$. Each of these energies is at most $\sup _ iE_\theta ^\phi (u_ i)$, which in turn is at most $E_\theta ^\phi (u)$ by monotonicity. Hence $\sup _ iE_\theta ^\phi (u_ i)=E_\theta ^\phi (u)$, and since the net of energies is increasing, its limit is this supremum. (The selected indices need not be cofinal in $I$, which is why we compare suprema.) $\square$

Upper semicontinuity of the energy for $L^1$ convergence rests on the following description of $L^1$ limits of potentials by decreasing sequences.

Lemma 17.3.2. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $q_ k,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $q_ k\to q$ in $L^1(X)$. Then the functions $q_ k$ are uniformly bounded above on $X$, the tail envelopes $a_ j=(\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k)^*$ belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and $a_ j$ decreases pointwise to $q$ as $j\to \infty $. In particular, if $\psi \colon X\to [-\infty ,\infty )$ is upper semicontinuous and $q_ k\leq \psi $ for every $k$, then $q\leq \psi $.

Proof. By proposition 14.2.1 the suprema $\sup _ Xq_ k$ converge to $\sup _ Xq$, so they are bounded. Hence the tail envelopes $a_ j$ are $\theta $-psh, by theorem 8.3.2 applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $, and they decrease as $j$ increases. A subsequence of $(q_ k)$ converges to $q$ almost everywhere, so $a_ j\geq q$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, since both functions are $\theta $-psh (section 16.1).

Conversely, let $x\in X$ and $\varepsilon >0$, let $h$ be a smooth local potential of $\theta $ on a coordinate ball $\Omega $ around $x$, and let $M>q(x)+h(x)$ be a real number. By upper semicontinuity there is a closed coordinate ball $K\subset \Omega $ around $x$ on which $q+h<M$ and $|h-h(x)|<\varepsilon $. The plurisubharmonic functions $q_ k+h$ converge to $q+h$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega )$, so Hartogs' lemma in the form of theorem 8.3.4, applied with the constant function $M$ on $K$, gives $q_ k+h<M$ on $K$ for all large $k$. For such $k$ we have $q_ k<M-h(x)+\varepsilon $ on $K$. Since $K$ is a neighborhood of $x$, it follows that $a_ j(x)\leq M-h(x)+\varepsilon $ for all large $j$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and $M\downarrow q(x)+h(x)$, or $M\to -\infty $ if $q(x)=-\infty $, we obtain $\lim _ ja_ j(x)\leq q(x)$. Thus $a_ j\downarrow q$.

Finally, if $q_ k\leq \psi $ for all $k$ with $\psi $ upper semicontinuous, then $\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k\leq \psi $, so $a_ j\leq \psi $ for every $j$, and therefore $q=\lim _ ja_ j\leq \psi $. $\square$

Proposition 17.3.3. The set $\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\preceq \phi \} $ is closed in $L^1(X)$, and $E_\theta ^\phi $ is upper semicontinuous on it, with values in $[-\infty ,\infty )$: if $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$, then

\[ E_\theta ^\phi (u)\geq \varlimsup _ j E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j). \]

Proof. Let $u_ j\preceq \phi $ converge to $u$ in $L^1(X)$. By lemma 17.3.2, the $u_ j$ are uniformly bounded above, so lemma 16.4.2 gives a constant $C$ with $u_ j\leq \phi +C$ for all $j$. Since $\phi +C$ is upper semicontinuous, the same lemma gives $u\leq \phi +C$, which proves closedness. The lemma also shows that the tail envelopes $v_ j=(\sup _{k\geq j}u_ k)^*$ are $\theta $-psh potentials which decrease pointwise to $u$; moreover $v_ j\leq \phi +C$, because $\phi +C$ is upper semicontinuous and dominates every $u_ k$. Since $u_ j\leq v_ j$, monotonicity and proposition 17.2.7 give

\[ \varlimsup _ j E_\theta ^\phi (u_ j) \leq \lim _ jE_\theta ^\phi (v_ j)=E_\theta ^\phi (u). \]

Since $L^1(X)$ is a metric space, sequential upper semicontinuity is upper semicontinuity. $\square$

Corollary 17.3.4. For $B\geq 0$, the set

\[ \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\leq \phi ,\quad E_\theta ^\phi (u)\geq -B\} \]

is compact in $L^1(X)$ and is contained in $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$. The set

\[ K_\phi =\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\leq \phi ,\quad \sup _ Xu=0\} \]

is also compact in $L^1(X)$.

Proof. For $u$ in the first set, lemma 16.4.2 gives $u\leq \phi +\sup _ Xu$, so monotonicity and the shift formula give $-B\leq E_\theta ^\phi (u)\leq m\sup _ Xu$. Also $\sup _ Xu\leq \sup _ X\phi =0$. Hence the set lies in $\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):-B/m\leq \sup _ Xu\leq 0\} $, which is compact in $L^1(X)$ by proposition 14.2.1. The set is closed in $L^1(X)$: the inequality $u\leq \phi $ passes to $L^1$ limits by lemma 17.3.2, since $\phi $ is upper semicontinuous, and the energy bound passes to limits by upper semicontinuity (proposition 17.3.3). It is therefore compact. Its members have finite energy, so they lie in $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by theorem 17.2.8.

The set $K_\phi $ is contained in $\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):\sup _ Xu=0\} $, which is compact in $L^1(X)$ by proposition 14.2.1, and it is closed in $L^1(X)$, because the supremum is continuous for $L^1$ convergence of potentials (by the same proposition) and the inequality $u\leq \phi $ passes to $L^1$ limits by lemma 17.3.2. Hence $K_\phi $ is compact. $\square$

Proposition 17.3.5. Let $\mathcal F$ be one of the three classes $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$. The regularized upper envelope of a nonempty family in $\mathcal F$ which is uniformly bounded above on $X$ belongs to $\mathcal F$.

Proof. By lemma 16.4.2, every member of the family is bounded above by $\phi +C$ for a common constant $C$, and so is the regularized upper envelope $U$. Fix a member $u_0$ of the family; then $u_0\leq U\preceq \phi $. For $\mathcal F=\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, mass monotonicity gives $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _{u_0}^ n\rangle \leq \int _ X\langle \theta _ U^ n\rangle \leq m$. For $\mathcal F=\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, the last assertion of corollary 17.2.10, applied to $u_0\leq U$, gives $U\in \mathcal F$. For $\mathcal F=\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, the member $u_0$ supplies a lower bound $U\geq u_0\geq \phi -C'$. $\square$

Proposition 17.3.6. Let $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. Then the rooftop envelope $w=P_\theta (u,v)$ of definition 16.1.5 is not identically $-\infty $, and it belongs to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. If moreover $u,v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $w\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and if in addition $u,v\leq \phi $, then

\[ \int _ X(\phi -w)\, \mu _ w \leq \int _ X(\phi -u)\, \mu _ u+\int _ X(\phi -v)\, \mu _ v. \]

Proof. Since $u\leq \max \{ u,v\} \preceq \phi $, mass monotonicity gives $\int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ u,v\} }^ n\rangle =m$. Thus $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle +\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle =2m>\int _ X\langle \theta _{\max \{ u,v\} }^ n\rangle $, and proposition 16.5.1 shows that $w\neq -\infty $. Theorem 16.5.5 gives $2m\leq m+\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle $, while $\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle \leq m$ by mass monotonicity, since $w\leq u$. Hence $w\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Now let $u,v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$. The rooftop commutes with adding a common constant to $u$ and $v$ (proposition 16.1.4(3)), so we may assume $u,v\leq \phi $; then $w\leq \phi $. The contact inequality of corollary 16.2.6 gives

\[ \mu _ w\leq \mathbf1_{\{ w=u\} }\mu _ u +\mathbf1_{\{ w=v\} }\mu _ v. \]

Integrate the nonnegative weight $\min \{ \phi -w,N\} $ against both sides. On the first contact set the weight equals $\min \{ \phi -u,N\} $, and on the second it equals $\min \{ \phi -v,N\} $; pole sets carry no mass. Letting $N\to \infty $ gives the displayed bound. Both terms on the right are finite, so $w\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$. $\square$

Corollary 17.3.7. For $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, the singularity-constrained envelope of definition 16.7.1 satisfies $P_\theta [u](v)=v$.

Proof. By proposition 17.3.6, the rooftop $w=P_\theta (u,v)$ belongs to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. Since $w\preceq v$ and $\int _ X\langle \theta _ w^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle >0$, proposition 16.4.10 gives $P_\theta [w](v)=v$. Every competitor for $P_\theta [w](v)$ is more singular than $w$, hence than $u$, so it is a competitor for $P_\theta [u](v)$; and $P_\theta [u](v)\leq v$ quasi-everywhere by proposition 16.7.2(1), hence everywhere. Therefore $v=P_\theta [w](v)\leq P_\theta [u](v)\leq v$. $\square$

Proposition 17.3.8. For $u,v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $u_ t=(1-t)u+tv$, the function $t\mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t)$ is continuously differentiable on $[0,1]$, with one-sided derivatives at the endpoints, and

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t) =\int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _{u_ t}. \]

The derivative is a polynomial in $t$.

Proof. By lemma 17.2.11 every integral $\int _ X(v-u)\langle \theta _ v^ a\wedge \theta _ u^{n-a}\rangle $ is finite. Expanding $\mu _{u_ t}$ by multilinearity shows that the right side is a polynomial in $t$ with these integrals as coefficients. The potentials $u_ t$ lie in $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by corollary 17.2.10. Apply the cocycle formula of proposition 17.2.12 to $u_{t+s}$ and $u_ t$, with $s\neq 0$ and $t,t+s\in [0,1]$, and use $u_{t+s}-u_ t=s(v-u)$:

\[ \frac{E_\theta ^\phi (u_{t+s})-E_\theta ^\phi (u_ t)}s =\frac1{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n\int _ X(v-u)\langle \theta _{u_{t+s}}^ j\wedge \theta _{u_ t}^{n-j}\rangle . \]

Each mixed integral on the right is a polynomial in $s$ with the finite coefficients above, and at $s=0$ each of the $n+1$ terms equals $\int _ X(v-u)\mu _{u_ t}$. Letting $s\to 0$ proves the formula for the derivative, including the one-sided derivatives at the endpoints, and its continuity. $\square$

Lemma 17.3.9. For $u,v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$,

\[ \int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _ v\leq E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u) \leq \int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _ u. \]

If moreover $u\leq v$, then

\[ \frac1{n+1}\int _ X(v-u)\, \mu _ u\leq E_\theta ^\phi (v)-E_\theta ^\phi (u). \]

Proof. The first display is the tangent bound (17.17), which holds on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by proposition 17.2.12. By the same proposition, the cocycle formula (17.14) holds for $u$ and $v$. If $u\leq v$, all its terms are nonnegative, and retaining the term $j=0$, whose measure is $\mu _ u$, gives the second display. $\square$

Example 17.3.10. The energy is not continuous for $L^1$ convergence, even for smooth potentials on $\mathbb P^1$. Let $\omega =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+|z|^2)$, which has mass one. For $0<a<1$ put $\varepsilon _ a=\exp (-a^{-2})$ and

\[ u_ a(z)=a\log \frac{|z|^2+\varepsilon _ a}{1+|z|^2}. \]

These functions extend smoothly across infinity, and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ a=(1-a)\omega +a\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (|z|^2+\varepsilon _ a)>0$. They tend to zero in $L^1$ as $a\downarrow 0$, since $a\log (|z|^2/(1+|z|^2))\leq u_ a\leq 0$ and the left side is $a$ times a fixed integrable function.

We compute their Dirichlet energies. Write $r=\log |z|^2$ and $F(r)=\log (\mathrm e^ r+\varepsilon )-\log (1+\mathrm e^ r)$ with $\varepsilon =\varepsilon _ a$, so that $u_ a=aF(\log |z|^2)$. For a smooth function $g=G(\log |z|^2)$ we have $\partial g=G'(r)\, \mathrm dz/z$, hence $\mathrm dg\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial g\wedge \bar\partial g=\pi ^{-1}G'(r)^2|z|^{-2}\, \mathrm dA$, where $\mathrm dA$ is Lebesgue measure; integration in polar coordinates gives $\int \mathrm dg\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g=\int _{-\infty }^\infty G'(r)^2\, \mathrm dr$. Therefore

\[ \int _ X\mathrm du_ a\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u_ a =a^2\int _{-\infty }^\infty F'(r)^2\, \mathrm dr =a^2(-\log \varepsilon _ a+O(1))\longrightarrow 1. \]

The bound $O(1)$ is uniform in $a$. Indeed, we have

\[ F'(r)=\frac{\mathrm e^ r}{\mathrm e^ r+\varepsilon }-\frac{\mathrm e^ r}{1+\mathrm e^ r}\in [0,1],\qquad 1-F'(r)=\frac{\varepsilon }{\mathrm e^ r+\varepsilon }+\frac{\mathrm e^ r}{1+\mathrm e^ r}\leq \varepsilon \mathrm e^{-r}+\mathrm e^ r. \]

On $[\log \varepsilon ,0]$ the integral of $\varepsilon \mathrm e^{-r}+\mathrm e^ r$ is at most $2$, and $1\geq F'^2\geq 1-2(1-F')$, so $\int _{\log \varepsilon }^0F'^2\, \mathrm dr=-\log \varepsilon +O(1)$. On the left tail $F'(r)\leq \mathrm e^ r/\varepsilon $, and on the right tail $F'(r)\leq \mathrm e^{-r}$; the corresponding integrals of $F'^2$ are at most $\frac12$. Since $\int _ Xu_ a\, \omega \to 0$, the formula of example 17.2.9 gives $E_\omega (u_ a)\to -\frac12$, whereas $E_\omega (0)=0$.

17.4 Variational Monge–Ampère equations

We keep the big class of $\theta $, the model potential $\phi $ and its mass $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle >0$, and write $E=E_\theta ^\phi $. We solve the equation $\mu _ u=\nu $ for non-pluripolar measures $\nu $ of mass $m$ (definition 17.4.1) by a variational method. By the tangent bounds, a potential $u$ with $\mu _ u=\nu $ maximizes $v\mapsto E(v)-\int _ X(v-\phi )\, \mathrm d\nu $. For the converse we perturb a maximizer $u$ in the direction of a continuous function $f$. Since $u+tf$ need not be $\theta $-psh, we replace it by its envelope $P_\theta (u+tf)$ and compute the derivative of the energy along these envelopes (proposition 17.4.3).

Maximizers exist by the compactness of corollary 17.3.4 when the integral term of the functional is continuous for $L^1$ convergence, as it is for measures with bounded densities. For an arbitrary non-pluripolar measure $\nu $ we replace the linear term by the exponential term $\lambda ^{-1}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu $, $\lambda >0$, which is continuous for $L^1$ convergence of potentials, and then let $\lambda \to 0$. The remaining subsections prove uniqueness (section 17.4.2) and the boundedness of $u-\phi $ for $L^ p$ densities (section 17.4.3), and derive two consequences of the existence theorem (section 17.4.4, section 17.4.5).

17.4.1 Existence by maximization

Definition 17.4.1. A finite positive Radon measure $\nu $ on $X$ is non-pluripolar if $\nu (A)=0$ for every pluripolar Borel set $A\subset X$. Integrals against $\nu $ use its completion, so subsets of pluripolar sets are also $\nu $-null.

For example, every measure of the form $g\, \omega ^ n$ with $0\leq g\in L^1(X,\omega ^ n)$ is non-pluripolar, since pluripolar sets have volume zero (see the remarks after definition 10.1.1). Every $\theta $-psh function is finite $\nu $-almost everywhere, because its pole set is a pluripolar Borel set.

Finite positive Borel measures on $X$ are compared through their integrals of continuous functions.

Lemma 17.4.2. Let $\lambda $ and $\sigma $ be finite positive Borel measures on $X$.

  1. If $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\lambda \geq \int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\sigma $ for every continuous function $g\geq 0$ on $X$, then $\lambda (E)\geq \sigma (E)$ for every Borel set $E\subset X$. In particular, two finite positive Borel measures on $X$ with the same integrals of all continuous functions coincide.

  2. If finite positive Borel measures $\lambda _ j$ converge weakly to $\lambda $ and satisfy $\lambda _ j\geq \sigma $ for every $j$, then $\lambda \geq \sigma $.

Proof. (1) This is lemma 16.2.1(2) with $W=X$; the second assertion follows by applying the first in both directions.

(2) Since $X$ is compact, weak convergence of measures on $X$ is convergence of the integrals of all continuous functions (see the discussion following definition 7.2.4). Hence $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\lambda =\lim _ j\int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\lambda _ j\geq \int _ Xg\, \mathrm d\sigma $ for every continuous function $g\geq 0$ on $X$, and (1) gives $\lambda \geq \sigma $. $\square$

Proposition 17.4.3. Let $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, and put $u_ t=P_\theta (u+tf)$ for $t\in \mathbb R$, the envelope of definition 16.1.3. Then $u_0=u$, and for every $t$ we have $u_ t\sim u$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$ on $X$. Each $u_ t$ has finite energy, and

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E(u_ t)=\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}. \]

The derivative is continuous on $\mathbb R$.

Proof. Let $B=\sup _ X|f|$. The potential $u-|t|B$ is a competitor for the obstacle $u+tf$, and every competitor lies below $u+|t|B$, so $u-|t|B\leq u_ t\leq u+|t|B$. Hence $u_ t\sim u$, and $E(u_ t)\geq E(u)-|t|Bm>-\infty $, so $u_ t\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by theorem 17.2.8. The obstacle $u+tf$ is upper semicontinuous, so $u_ t\leq u+tf$ everywhere, by proposition 16.1.4(1),(2). In particular $u_0\leq u$; since $u$ is itself a competitor for the obstacle $u$, also $u\leq u_0$, so $u_0=u$.

Next we show that $\mu _{u_{t+s}}\to \mu _{u_ t}$ weakly as $s\to 0$. The obstacles $u+tf$ and $u+(t+s)f$ differ by at most $|s|B$, so proposition 16.1.4(4) gives $u_ t-|s|B\leq u_{t+s}\leq u_ t+|s|B$. In particular all $u_ t$ have the pole set of $u$, and $|u_{t+s}-u_ t|\leq |s|B$ outside it. Since $|\arctan a-\arctan b|\leq |a-b|$ for real $a,b$, and adding the same smooth local potential of $\theta $ to $u_{t+s}$ and $u_ t$ does not change their difference, the distance $d_*$ of definition 15.6.2 between the local potentials of $u_{t+s}$ and $u_ t$ is at most $|s|B$ everywhere. Hence, for every $\delta >0$, the sets where it exceeds $\delta $ are empty once $|s|B<\delta $, and $u_{t+s}\to u_ t$ in extended capacity as $s\to 0$. These potentials have full mass relative to $\phi $, so corollary 15.8.4, applied along every sequence $s_ k\to 0$, gives the asserted weak convergence; in particular $\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_{t+s}}\to \int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}$.

The obstacle $u+tf$ is bounded above, and it is quasi-continuous, because $u$ is (section 16.1) and $f$ is continuous. Hence theorem 16.2.5 shows that $\mu _{u_ t}$ is carried by the contact set $\{ u_ t=u+tf\} $. On the contact set of $u_{t+s}$ we have $u_{t+s}=u+(t+s)f$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$, so $u_{t+s}-u_ t\geq sf$ there. On the contact set of $u_ t$ we have $u_ t=u+tf$ and $u_{t+s}\leq u+(t+s)f$, so $u_{t+s}-u_ t\leq sf$ there. The differences are bounded and the pole sets carry no mass, so the tangent bounds of lemma 17.3.9 give, for every real $s$,

\[ s\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_{t+s}} \leq E(u_{t+s})-E(u_ t) \leq s\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}. \]

Dividing by $s>0$, respectively $s<0$, and letting $s\to 0$, we obtain the right and left derivatives from the weak convergence just proved. Both equal $\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}$, which depends continuously on $t$ by the same weak convergence. $\square$

Theorem 17.4.4. Let $\nu $ be a non-pluripolar measure on $X$, and suppose that $\mathcal D_\nu =\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )\cap L^1(\nu )$ is nonempty. Then $\phi \in L^1(\nu )$, and for the functional

\[ F_\nu (v)=E(v)-\int _ X(v-\phi )\, \mathrm d\nu , \qquad v\in \mathcal D_\nu , \]

a potential $u\in \mathcal D_\nu $ maximizes $F_\nu $ if and only if $\mu _ u=\nu $. In particular, $F_\nu $ has no maximizer unless $\nu (X)=m$.

Proof. Let $v\in \mathcal D_\nu $. Then $v\leq \phi +C$ for a constant $C$, and $\phi \leq 0$. Thus the negative part of $\phi $ is at most that of $v$ plus $C$, and the positive part of $\phi $ vanishes; hence $\phi \in L^1(\nu )$. If $\mu _ u=\nu $, the tangent bound of lemma 17.3.9 gives

\[ E(v)-E(u)\leq \int _ X(v-u)\, \mathrm d\nu , \]

that is, $F_\nu (v)\leq F_\nu (u)$.

Conversely, let $u$ be a maximizer and $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$. By proposition 17.4.3, the potentials $u_ t=P_\theta (u+tf)$ have bounded difference from $u$ and finite energy, so they belong to $\mathcal D_\nu $; moreover $u_0=u$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$ everywhere, hence $\nu $-almost everywhere. Therefore

\[ E(u_ t)-E(u)-t\int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu \leq F_\nu (u_ t)-F_\nu (u)\leq 0. \]

The left side vanishes at $t=0$ and is differentiable there by proposition 17.4.3; since it attains its maximum at $t=0$, its derivative vanishes there. Thus $\int _ Xf\, \mu _ u=\int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu $ for every $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, and lemma 17.4.2(1) gives $\mu _ u=\nu $. Since $\mu _ u(X)=m$, a maximizer can exist only if $\nu (X)=m$. $\square$

Theorem 17.4.5. Let $\mathrm dV$ be a smooth positive volume form on $X$, and let $g\in L^\infty (\mathrm dV)$ satisfy $g\geq 0$ and $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm dV=m$. Then there is $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with

\[ \mu _ u=g\, \mathrm dV,\qquad \sup _ Xu=0. \]

It maximizes $F_{g\, \mathrm dV}$.

Proof. Put $\nu =g\, \mathrm dV$, a non-pluripolar measure of mass $m$. Every $\theta $-psh potential is integrable for $\mathrm dV$, hence for $\nu $, so $\mathcal D_\nu =\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$; we write $F=F_\nu $. By the shift formula for the energy and $\nu (X)=m$, the functional $F$ is invariant under adding constants, so we may restrict it to potentials $v$ with $\sup _ Xv=0$. For such $v$, lemma 16.4.2 gives $v\leq \phi $, hence $E(v)\leq 0$. Since $\mathrm dV\leq c_ V\, \omega ^ n$ for a constant $c_ V$, proposition 14.2.1 shows that $\int _ X|v|\, \mathrm dV$ is bounded independently of $v$, and $\phi \in L^1(\mathrm dV)$, so

\[ 0\leq -\int _ X(v-\phi )g\, \mathrm dV\leq C \]

with $C$ independent of $v$. Consequently $\sup F\in [0,C]$, since $F(\phi )=0$ and $F(v)\leq 0+C$.

Let $(v_ j)$ be a normalized maximizing sequence with $F(v_ j)\geq -1$. Then $E(v_ j)=F(v_ j)+\int _ X(v_ j-\phi )g\, \mathrm dV\geq -C-1$. By corollary 17.3.4 a subsequence converges in $L^1(X)$ to some $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and $\sup _ Xu=0$, because the supremum is continuous for $L^1$ convergence of potentials (proposition 14.2.1). The linear term $v\mapsto \int _ X(v-\phi )g\, \mathrm dV$ is continuous for $L^1$ convergence because $g$ is bounded, and the energy is upper semicontinuous by proposition 17.3.3. Hence $F(u)\geq \varlimsup _ jF(v_ j)=\sup F$, so $u$ is a maximizer, and theorem 17.4.4 gives $\mu _ u=\nu $. $\square$

Proposition 17.4.6. Let $\lambda >0$ and let $\nu $ be a finite positive non-pluripolar measure on $X$. If $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ satisfy

\[ \mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu , \qquad \mu _ v\geq \mathrm e^{\lambda v}\nu , \]

then $u\geq v$. In particular, the equation $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $ has at most one solution in $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Proof. Let $A=\{ u<v\} $. The comparison principle of proposition 16.7.4 gives

\[ \int _ A\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu \leq \mu _ v(A)\leq \mu _ u(A) =\int _ A\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\, \mathrm d\nu \leq m. \]

Both potentials are finite $\nu $-almost everywhere, and on $A$ we have $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}>\mathrm e^{\lambda u}$. The difference $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}-\mathrm e^{\lambda u}$ is therefore positive on $A$ and has nonpositive integral over $A$, so $\nu (A)=0$. Hence $\mu _ u(A)=0$, and the domination principle (theorem 16.4.6) gives $u\geq v$ everywhere. If $u$ and $v$ are two solutions, applying this in both directions gives $u=v$. $\square$

Solutions of the exponential equations are obtained as maximizers of the functionals

17.28
\begin{equation} F_\lambda (v)=E(v)-\lambda ^{-1}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu ,\qquad v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi ), \end{equation}

where $\lambda >0$ and $\nu $ is a finite positive Borel measure on $X$. These functionals are finite: the energy is finite on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and the Borel function $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}$ satisfies $0\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda v}\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda \sup _ Xv}$, where $\mathrm e^{\lambda v}=0$ on the pole set of $v$.

Lemma 17.4.7. Let $\nu $ be a finite positive Borel measure on $X$ and $\lambda >0$. If $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ maximizes the functional $F_\lambda $ of (17.28) on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $.

Proof. Let $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ and $u_ t=P_\theta (u+tf)$ for $t\in \mathbb R$. By proposition 17.4.3, $u_ t\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, $u_0=u$ and $u_ t\leq u+tf$ everywhere. Hence $\mathrm e^{\lambda u_ t}\leq \mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}$, and $F_\lambda (u_ t)\leq F_\lambda (u)$ gives

\[ E(u_ t)-E(u)-\lambda ^{-1} \int _ X\left(\mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}-\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\right) \, \mathrm d\nu \leq 0. \]

The left side vanishes at $t=0$. Its energy term is differentiable, with derivative $\int _ Xf\, \mu _{u_ t}$, by proposition 17.4.3. For $|t|\leq 1$ the $t$-derivative $\lambda f\mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}$ of the integrand is bounded by $\lambda \sup _ X|f|\, \mathrm e^{\lambda \sup _ Xu+\lambda \sup _ X|f|}$, which is integrable for the finite measure $\nu $; by the mean value theorem and dominated convergence, the integral is differentiable as well, with derivative $\lambda \int _ Xf\mathrm e^{\lambda (u+tf)}\, \mathrm d\nu $. Since the left side is maximal at $t=0$, its derivative vanishes there: $\int _ Xf\, \mu _ u=\int _ Xf\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\, \mathrm d\nu $. Since $f$ is arbitrary and $\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $ is a finite measure, lemma 17.4.2(1) gives $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $. $\square$

To find maximizers for an arbitrary non-pluripolar measure $\nu $, we need the continuity of $v\mapsto \int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu $ for $L^1$ convergence of potentials. This rests on the following two lemmas, in which $\theta $ is an arbitrary smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form.

Lemma 17.4.8. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. If $q_ j,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(X)$, then $\varlimsup _ jq_ j=q$ quasi-everywhere.

Proof. By lemma 17.3.2, the tail envelopes $a_ j=(\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k)^*$ are $\theta $-psh and decrease pointwise to $q$. For each $j$, corollary 10.2.2, applied after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $, gives $a_ j=\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k$ outside a pluripolar set. The countable union of these sets is pluripolar (theorem 10.1.4), and outside it $q=\lim _ ja_ j=\varlimsup _ jq_ j$. $\square$

Lemma 17.4.9. Let $\nu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$, let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $B\geq 0$. Suppose that $q_ j,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $|q_ j|,|q|\leq B$ and $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(X)$. Then $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(\nu )$.

Proof. By lemma 17.4.8, $\varlimsup _ jq_ j=q$ outside a pluripolar set, hence $\nu $-almost everywhere. Thus $(q_ j-q)_+\to 0$ $\nu $-almost everywhere, and $\int _ X(q_ j-q)_+\, \mathrm d\nu \to 0$ by bounded convergence, since $0\leq (q_ j-q)_+\leq 2B$ and $\nu $ is finite. Since $|q_ j-q|=2(q_ j-q)_+-(q_ j-q)$, it remains to show that $\int _ Xq_ j\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int _ Xq\, \mathrm d\nu $. We have just seen that $\varlimsup _ j\int _ X(q_ j-q)\, \mathrm d\nu \leq 0$, so it suffices to prove that $L=\varliminf _ j\int _ Xq_ j\, \mathrm d\nu $ is at least $\int _ Xq\, \mathrm d\nu $.

Choose a subsequence $(q_{j_ k})$ with $\int _ Xq_{j_ k}\, \mathrm d\nu \to L$. For bounded Borel functions $y$ on $X$ put $\| y\| =(\int _ Xy^2\, \mathrm d\nu )^{1/2}$. For $l\geq 1$ let $C_ l$ be the set of finite convex combinations of the functions $q_{j_ k}$ with $k\geq l$, and let $d_ l=\inf \{ \| y\| :y\in C_ l\} $. Since $C_{l+1}\subset C_ l$ and $\| y\| \leq B\nu (X)^{1/2}$ for $y\in C_ l$, the numbers $d_ l$ increase to a finite limit. Choose $y_ l\in C_ l$ with $\| y_ l\| ^2\leq d_ l^2+1/l$. For $l\leq l'$ the function $\frac12(y_ l+y_{l'})$ belongs to the convex set $C_ l$, so its norm is at least $d_ l$. The parallelogram identity, obtained by integrating the pointwise identity $(a-b)^2+(a+b)^2=2a^2+2b^2$, therefore gives

\[ \| y_ l-y_{l'}\| ^2=2\| y_ l\| ^2+2\| y_{l'}\| ^2-4\bigl \| \tfrac 12(y_ l+y_{l'})\bigr \| ^2 \leq 2(d_{l'}^2-d_ l^2)+\frac2l+\frac2{l'}, \]

and the right side tends to zero as $l\to \infty $, uniformly in $l'\geq l$.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, $\int _ X|y_ l-y_{l'}|\, \mathrm d\nu \leq \nu (X)^{1/2}\| y_ l-y_{l'}\| $, so we can choose $l_1<l_2<\cdots $ with $\int _ X|y_{l_{i+1}}-y_{l_ i}|\, \mathrm d\nu \leq 2^{-i}$. By monotone convergence the series $\sum _ i|y_{l_{i+1}}-y_{l_ i}|$ has finite integral, hence converges $\nu $-almost everywhere, and therefore $y_{l_ i}$ converges $\nu $-almost everywhere; let $h=\varlimsup _ iy_{l_ i}$, a Borel function with $|h|\leq B$. By bounded convergence, $\int _ Xy_{l_ i}\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int _ Xh\, \mathrm d\nu $. On the other hand, $\int _ Xy_ l\, \mathrm d\nu $ is a convex combination of the numbers $\int _ Xq_{j_ k}\, \mathrm d\nu $ with $k\geq l$, which tend to $L$, so $\int _ Xy_ l\, \mathrm d\nu \to L$. Hence $L=\int _ Xh\, \mathrm d\nu $.

It remains to show that $h=q$ $\nu $-almost everywhere. Each $y_{l_ i}$ is a convex combination of $\theta $-psh functions, hence $\theta $-psh, and $\| y_{l_ i}-q\| _{L^1(X)}\leq \sup _{k\geq l_ i}\| q_{j_ k}-q\| _{L^1(X)}$, which tends to zero. By lemma 17.4.8, applied to the sequence $(y_{l_ i})_ i$, the function $h=\varlimsup _ iy_{l_ i}$ equals $q$ outside a pluripolar set, hence $\nu $-almost everywhere. Thus $L=\int _ Xq\, \mathrm d\nu $, as required. $\square$

Corollary 17.4.10. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $q_ j,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(X)$, and let $\nu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$. Then for every $\lambda >0$,

\[ \mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}\longrightarrow \mathrm e^{\lambda q} \quad \text{in }L^1(\nu ). \]

Proof. By proposition 14.2.1 there is a constant $C$ with $q_ j,q\leq C$. We show that the functions $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}$ and $\mathrm e^{\lambda q}$, with value zero on the pole sets, are $C_1\omega $-psh for a constant $C_1$ independent of $j$; the preceding lemma then applies to them.

Choose $C_0\geq 0$ with $\theta \leq C_0\omega $, and a finite cover of $X$ by coordinate balls $W'\Subset W$ such that the smaller balls $W'$ still cover $X$, together with smooth functions $h_ W$ near $\overline W$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ W=\theta $. The function $q_ j+h_ W$ is plurisubharmonic on $W$, so for every $\delta >0$ smaller than the distance from $W'$ to the boundary of $W$, its regularization $g_\delta =(q_ j+h_ W)*\rho _\delta $ by the radial mollifier is a smooth plurisubharmonic function on the ball $W'$, and $g_\delta $ decreases to $q_ j+h_ W$ as $\delta \downarrow 0$, by proposition 8.2.2(2). Put $q_{j,\delta }=g_\delta -h_ W$. Then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_{j,\delta }\geq -\theta \geq -C_0\omega $. Moreover $g_\delta (x)$ is an average of values of $q_ j+h_ W\leq C+h_ W$ at points within distance $\delta $ of $x$, so by uniform continuity of $h_ W$ on a compact neighborhood of $\overline{W'}$ in $W$ we have $q_{j,\delta }\leq C+1$ on $W'$ for all small $\delta $, independently of $j$.

For a smooth real function $s$, the chain rule $\partial \bar\partial \, \mathrm e^{\lambda s}=\lambda \mathrm e^{\lambda s}\partial \bar\partial s+\lambda ^2\mathrm e^{\lambda s}\partial s\wedge \bar\partial s$ and the identity $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial s\wedge \bar\partial s=\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s$, which follows from (7.6), give

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }} =\lambda \mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_{j,\delta } +\lambda ^2\mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }} \mathrm dq_{j,\delta }\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}q_{j,\delta } \geq -\lambda C_0\mathrm e^{\lambda (C+1)}\omega , \]

because $\mathrm dq_{j,\delta }\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}q_{j,\delta }$ is a semipositive $(1,1)$-form. Thus $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_{j,\delta }}$ is $C_1\omega $-psh on $W'$ with $C_1=\lambda C_0\mathrm e^{\lambda (C+1)}$, and so is its decreasing limit $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}$ as $\delta \downarrow 0$, by theorem 8.3.1 applied after adding a smooth local potential of $C_1\omega $; the limit is not identically $-\infty $, since it is nonnegative. Since the balls $W'$ cover $X$, the function $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,C_1\omega )$, and the same argument applies to $\mathrm e^{\lambda q}$.

The exponential is Lipschitz with constant $\lambda \mathrm e^{\lambda C}$ on $(-\infty ,C]$, so $\mathrm e^{\lambda q_ j}\to \mathrm e^{\lambda q}$ in $L^1(X)$. These functions take values in $[0,\mathrm e^{\lambda C}]$, and lemma 17.4.9, with the background $C_1\omega $, gives the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 17.4.11. Let $\nu $ be a finite positive non-pluripolar measure on $X$ with $\nu (X)>0$, and let $\lambda >0$. There is a unique $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ satisfying

\[ \mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu . \]

This solution belongs to $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and maximizes $F_\lambda (v)=E(v)-\lambda ^{-1}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu $ on $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Proof. By corollary 17.3.4, the set $K_\phi $ of potentials $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $w\leq \phi $ and $\sup _ Xw=0$ is compact in $L^1(X)$; it contains $\phi $. By corollary 17.4.10, the function $w\mapsto \int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda w}\, \mathrm d\nu $ is continuous on $K_\phi $. Its values are positive, since the pole set of $w$ is $\nu $-null and $\nu (X)>0$. Hence it has a positive minimum $a$ on $K_\phi $.

Any $v\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ can be written $v=w+c$ with $c=\sup _ Xv$ and $w\in K_\phi $, by lemma 16.4.2. Since $E(w)\leq 0$, the shift formula for the energy gives

\[ F_\lambda (v)=E(w)+mc-\lambda ^{-1}\mathrm e^{\lambda c}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda w}\, \mathrm d\nu \leq mc-\lambda ^{-1}a\mathrm e^{\lambda c}. \]

The right side tends to $-\infty $ as $c\to \pm \infty $. Since $F_\lambda (\phi )$ is finite, a maximizing sequence $v_ j=w_ j+c_ j$ with $F_\lambda (v_ j)\geq F_\lambda (\phi )-1$ therefore has bounded constants $c_ j$. Moreover $E(v_ j)\geq F_\lambda (v_ j)$, because the exponential term is nonnegative, so $E(w_ j)=E(v_ j)-mc_ j$ is bounded below. By corollary 17.3.4, after passing to a subsequence, $c_ j$ converges and $w_ j$ converges in $L^1(X)$; hence $v_ j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$ for some $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$. The energy is upper semicontinuous (proposition 17.3.3), and $\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v_ j}\, \mathrm d\nu \to \int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\, \mathrm d\nu $ by corollary 17.4.10, so $F_\lambda (u)\geq \varlimsup _ jF_\lambda (v_ j)=\sup F_\lambda $, and $u$ is a maximizer. By lemma 17.4.7, $\mu _ u=\mathrm e^{\lambda u}\nu $. Uniqueness in $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ is proposition 17.4.6. $\square$

For instance, the theorem applies to $\nu =g\, \mathrm dV$, where $\mathrm dV$ is a smooth positive volume form and $g\in L^\infty (\mathrm dV)$ satisfies $g\geq 0$ and $\int _ Xg\, \mathrm dV>0$: this measure is finite, and it is non-pluripolar because pluripolar sets have volume zero.

To let $\lambda \to 0$, we need a lower bound for the measures of $L^1$ limits.

Lemma 17.4.12. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and suppose that $q_ j,q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $q_ j\to q$ in $L^1(X)$. Let $\nu $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure on $X$, and suppose that $\mu _{q_ j}\geq f_ j\nu $, where $f_ j\geq 0$ are measurable and $f_ j\to f$ $\nu $-almost everywhere. Then $\mu _ q\geq f\nu $.

Proof. Put $g_ j=\inf _{k\geq j}f_ k$, so that $g_ j\uparrow f$ $\nu $-almost everywhere. We first prove that $\mu _{a_ j}\geq g_ j\nu $ for the tail envelopes $a_ j=(\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k)^*$, and then let $j\to \infty $. For $l\geq j$, the finite maximum $\max \{ q_ j,\ldots ,q_ l\} $ has measure at least $g_ j\nu $. Indeed, let $r_1,r_2\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $\mu _{r_1},\mu _{r_2}\geq g_ j\nu $, and put $r=\max \{ r_1,r_2\} $. The set $\{ r_1\geq r_2\} $ is the contact set $\{ r_1=r\} $, so lemma 16.2.2, applied to $r_1\leq r$, gives $\mu _ r\geq \mu _{r_1}\geq g_ j\nu $ on $\{ r_1\geq r_2\} $; in the same way $\mu _ r\geq g_ j\nu $ on $\{ r_2>r_1\} $. Hence $\mu _ r\geq g_ j\nu $. Since $\mu _{q_ k}\geq f_ k\nu \geq g_ j\nu $ for $k\geq j$, induction on $l$ proves the claim.

The suprema $\sup _ Xq_ k$ are bounded, by proposition 14.2.1, so the regularized upper envelope $a_ j$ is $\theta $-psh, and it equals $\sup _{k\geq j}q_ k$ almost everywhere (theorem 8.3.2, applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $). The finite maxima $\max \{ q_ j,\ldots ,q_ l\} $ therefore increase almost everywhere to $a_ j$ as $l\to \infty $, and their measures converge weakly to $\mu _{a_ j}$ by corollary 15.7.6. The fixed measure $g_ j\nu \leq \mu _{q_ j}$ is finite, so the inequality passes to the weak limit, by lemma 17.4.2(2):

\[ \mu _{a_ j}\geq g_ j\nu . \]

By lemma 17.3.2, $a_ j$ decreases pointwise to $q$.

To let $j\to \infty $ we truncate. Fix $C>0$ and an index $j_0$. The truncations $a_ j^ C=\max \{ a_ j,V_\theta -C\} $ have uniformly bounded difference from $V_\theta $: they are at least $V_\theta -C$, and since the suprema of the $a_ j$ are bounded, minimality of $V_\theta $ bounds them above by $V_\theta +C'$. They decrease to $q^ C=\max \{ q,V_\theta -C\} $, so they converge to $q^ C$ in extended capacity, by lemma 15.6.3 applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $. Since $q^ C$ has bounded difference from $V_\theta $, it has the mass of $V_\theta $ (corollary 15.7.5), that is, $q^ C\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,V_\theta )$. Corollary 15.8.4, with $V_\theta $ in place of $\phi $, therefore gives $\mu _{a_ j^ C}\to \mu _{q^ C}$ weakly.

On the set $A_ C=\{ q>V_\theta -C\} $ we have $a_ j\geq q>V_\theta -C$, so $a_ j^ C=a_ j$ on the plurifine open set $\{ a_ j>V_\theta -C\} \supset A_ C$, and locality gives $\mu _{a_ j^ C}\geq \mathbf1_{A_ C}g_{j_0}\nu $ for $j\geq j_0$. Since $\mathbf1_{A_ C}g_{j_0}\nu $ is a fixed finite measure, the inequality passes to the weak limit, by lemma 17.4.2(2), and gives $\mu _{q^ C}\geq \mathbf1_{A_ C}g_{j_0}\nu $. On the plurifine open set $A_ C$ we have $q^ C=q$, so locality gives $\mu _ q\geq \mathbf1_{A_ C}g_{j_0}\nu $. Thus $\mu _ q(E)\geq \int _{E\cap A_ C}g_{j_0}\, \mathrm d\nu $ for every Borel set $E$. Let $j_0\to \infty $ and then $C\to \infty $. The sets $A_ C$ increase to the set where $q$ and $V_\theta $ are finite, whose complement is pluripolar and hence $\nu $-null, so monotone convergence gives $\mu _ q(E)\geq \int _ Ef\, \mathrm d\nu $, that is, $\mu _ q\geq f\nu $. $\square$

Theorem 17.4.13. Let $\nu $ be a finite positive non-pluripolar measure on $X$ with $\nu (X)=m$. Then there is

\[ u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )\quad \text{with}\quad \mu _ u=\nu \quad \text{and}\quad \sup _ Xu=0. \]

Proof. Let $\lambda _ j\downarrow 0$, let $u_ j$ be the solution of $\mu _{u_ j}=\mathrm e^{\lambda _ ju_ j}\nu $ given by theorem 17.4.11, and write $u_ j=w_ j+c_ j$ with $c_ j=\sup _ Xu_ j$. Since $u_ j\preceq \phi $ and $\sup _ Xw_ j=0$, lemma 16.4.2 gives $w_ j\leq \phi $, so $w_ j$ belongs to the set $K_\phi $ of corollary 17.3.4, which is compact in $L^1(X)$. On $K_\phi $ the functions

\[ H_ j(w)=\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda _ jw}\, \mathrm d\nu \]

are continuous, by corollary 17.4.10, and they increase pointwise to the constant $m$, by monotone convergence, since $w\leq 0$ and the pole set of $w$ is $\nu $-null. The convergence is uniform on $K_\phi $ (Dini's theorem): for $\varepsilon >0$ the compact sets $\{ w\in K_\phi :m-H_ j(w)\geq \varepsilon \} $ decrease and have empty intersection, so they are empty for large $j$.

Comparing total masses in the equation gives $m=\mathrm e^{\lambda _ jc_ j}H_ j(w_ j)$, so

\[ \mathrm e^{\lambda _ jc_ j}=\frac m{H_ j(w_ j)}\longrightarrow 1. \]

Also $\int _ X|1-\mathrm e^{\lambda _ jw_ j}|\, \mathrm d\nu =m-H_ j(w_ j)\to 0$. Hence $r_ j=\mathrm e^{\lambda _ ju_ j}=\mathrm e^{\lambda _ jc_ j}\mathrm e^{\lambda _ jw_ j}\to 1$ in $L^1(\nu )$, and $\mu _{w_ j}=\mu _{u_ j}=r_ j\nu $. Pass to a subsequence along which $r_ j\to 1$ $\nu $-almost everywhere and, by compactness of $K_\phi $, $w_ j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$ for some $u\in K_\phi $. Lemma 17.4.12 gives $\mu _ u\geq \nu $. Since $u\leq \phi $, mass monotonicity gives $\mu _ u(X)\leq m=\nu (X)$. Hence $\mu _ u=\nu $, $u$ has full mass relative to $\phi $, and $\sup _ Xu=0$ because $u\in K_\phi $. $\square$

17.4.2 Uniqueness by concentration of mass

We now show that the solution given by theorem 17.4.13 is unique up to an additive constant. The proof uses existence once more. If two solutions $u,v$ with $\mu _ u=\mu _ v=\mu $ do not differ by a constant $\mu $-almost everywhere, then, after a shift of $v$, the set $U=\{ u<v\} $ carries a proper fraction of the mass of $\mu $. We solve the equation whose right-hand side is $\mu $ restricted to $U$ and rescaled to total mass $m$. Mixing this auxiliary solution into $v$ raises the density of the Monge–Ampère measure on $U$ (corollary 15.4.7), in contradiction with the comparison principle. The domination principle then turns equality $\mu $-almost everywhere into equality everywhere.

Theorem 17.4.14. Let $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ satisfy $\mu _ u=\mu _ v$. Then there is a constant $C\in \mathbb R$ such that $u=v+C$ everywhere on $X$. Consequently, every non-pluripolar measure of mass $m$ is the Monge–Ampère measure $\mu _ u$ of exactly one $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with $\sup _ Xu=0$.

Proof. Put $\mu =\mu _ u=\mu _ v$. Since $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, this is a finite measure of mass $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle =m$, and it is non-pluripolar, because non-pluripolar products give no mass to pluripolar sets (lemma 15.1.2). Hence the pole sets of $u$ and $v$ are $\mu $-null, and $u-v$ is defined and finite $\mu $-almost everywhere. We first show that $u-v$ is constant $\mu $-almost everywhere.

Suppose not. Let $\lambda $ be the image of $\mu $ under $u-v$, a finite Borel measure on $\mathbb R$ of total mass $m>0$, and put

\[ c_-=\sup \{ c\in \mathbb R:\lambda ((-\infty ,c))=0\} ,\qquad c_+=\inf \{ c\in \mathbb R:\lambda ((c,\infty ))=0\} , \]

with $\sup \varnothing =-\infty $ and $\inf \varnothing =+\infty $. The sets $(-\infty ,c)$ increase to $\mathbb R$ as $c\to +\infty $, so $\lambda ((-\infty ,c))\to m>0$; hence $c_-<+\infty $, and similarly $c_+>-\infty $. If $c_->-\infty $, the $\lambda $-null sets $(-\infty ,c)$ with $c<c_-$ increase to $(-\infty ,c_-)$, so $\lambda ((-\infty ,c_-))=0$; similarly $\lambda ((c_+,\infty ))=0$ if $c_+<+\infty $. Hence $c_+<c_-$ is impossible, since $\mathbb R=(-\infty ,c_-)\cup (c_+,\infty )$ would then be $\lambda $-null. If $c_-=c_+$, then $\lambda $ is carried by the point $c_-$, that is, $u-v$ is constant $\mu $-almost everywhere, contrary to our assumption. Thus $c_-<c_+$, and every $c\in (c_-,c_+)$ satisfies $\lambda ((-\infty ,c))>0$ and $\lambda ((c,\infty ))>0$, that is,

\[ \mu (\{ u-v<c\} )>0,\qquad \mu (\{ u-v>c\} )>0. \]

The singletons $\{ c\} $ with $\lambda (\{ c\} )>0$ are pairwise disjoint sets of positive measure, so there are only countably many of them by lemma 1.1.5. Since the interval $(c_-,c_+)$ is uncountable, we may choose $c$ in it with $\mu (\{ u-v=c\} )=0$. Replacing $v$ by $v+c$ changes neither the class $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ nor the measure $\mu _ v$, so we may assume $c=0$. Then $\mu (\{ u<v\} )$ and $\mu (\{ u>v\} )$ are positive, and the set $U=\{ u<v\} $ satisfies

\[ 0<\mu (U)<m,\qquad \mu (\{ u=v\} )=0. \]

Set $q=(m/\mu (U))^{1/n}>1$. The measure $q^ n\mathbf1_ U\mu $ is non-pluripolar and has mass $m$, so theorem 17.4.13 gives $h\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with

\[ \mu _ h=q^ n\mathbf1_ U\mu . \]

For $0<t<1$ put $w_ t=(1-t)v+th$. By corollary 17.2.10, $w_ t\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. Since $\theta _{w_ t}=(1-t)\theta _ v+t\theta _ h$, corollary 15.4.7, applied to the currents $\theta _ v,\theta _ h$, the common measure $\mu $ and the densities $1$ and $q^ n\mathbf1_ U$, gives

\[ \mu _{w_ t}\geq \big ((1-t)+tq\mathbf1_ U\big )^ n\mu . \]

Let $U_ t=\{ u<w_ t\} $, and put

\[ d_ t=(1-t)^ n,\qquad b_ t=(1-t+tq)^ n. \]

The comparison principle for relative full mass, proposition 16.7.4, applied to $u$ and $w_ t$, and the preceding lower bound give

\[ \mu (U_ t)=\mu _ u(U_ t)\geq \mu _{w_ t}(U_ t) \geq d_ t\mu (U_ t)+(b_ t-d_ t)\mu (U_ t\cap U). \]

Since $1-d_ t>0$, it follows that

17.29
\begin{equation} \frac{b_ t-d_ t}{1-d_ t}\, \mu (U_ t\cap U)\leq \mu (U_ t). \end{equation}

We let $t\downarrow 0$. At every point where $u,v,h$ are finite and $u\neq v$, we have $w_ t=v+t(h-v)$, so $\mathbf1_{U_ t}\to \mathbf1_ U$ there. These points have full $\mu $-measure, because the three pole sets and the level set $\{ u=v\} $ are $\mu $-null. By dominated convergence,

\[ \mu (U_ t\cap U)\longrightarrow \mu (U),\qquad \mu (U_ t)\longrightarrow \mu (U). \]

Moreover $b_ t-d_ t=ntq+O(t^2)$ and $1-d_ t=nt+O(t^2)$, so $(b_ t-d_ t)/(1-d_ t)\to q$. Passing to the limit in (17.29) gives $q\mu (U)\leq \mu (U)$, which contradicts $q>1$ and $\mu (U)>0$.

Hence, for the original pair, there is a finite constant $C$ such that $u=v+C$ $\mu $-almost everywhere. Both $u$ and $v+C$ belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and their common measure $\mu $ gives zero mass to $\{ u<v+C\} $ and to $\{ v+C<u\} $. The domination principle (theorem 16.4.6), applied in both directions, gives $u=v+C$ everywhere, including at the common poles. This proves the first assertion.

For the second, theorem 17.4.13 provides a solution with supremum zero. If $u$ and $v$ are two such solutions, then $u=v+C$, and comparing suprema gives $C=0$. $\square$

17.4.3 Bounded differences from the reference

The solutions constructed so far belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, but in general their difference from $\phi $ is unbounded. We now show that the difference is bounded when the measure is dominated by a power $1+\delta >1$ of the Monge–Ampère capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ relative to $\phi $ (definition 16.7.5). If $\mu _ u\leq A\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }^{1+\delta }$, the sublevel estimate of proposition 16.7.7 bounds the capacity of the deeper sublevel set $\{ u<\phi -s-t\} $ by $At^{-n}$ times the $(1+\delta )$th power of the capacity of $\{ u<\phi -s\} $. Iterating with geometrically decreasing steps $t$, we reach a finite level beyond which the capacity vanishes. Measures with $L^ p$ densities, $p>1$, satisfy such a bound by corollary 16.9.5.

Theorem 17.4.15. Let $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with $u\leq \phi $. Let $0\leq a<1$, $A>0$ and $\delta >0$, and let $\nu $ be a positive Borel measure on $X$ such that

17.30
\begin{equation} \mu _ u\leq \nu +a\mu _\phi ,\qquad \nu (E)\leq A\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)^{1+\delta } \quad \text{for every Borel set }E\subset X. \end{equation}

Then there is a finite constant $C\geq 0$ such that $\phi -C\leq u\leq \phi $ on $X$. If, in addition, $I=\int _ X(\phi -u)\, \mathrm d\nu <\infty $, then one can take

17.31
\begin{equation} C=2+\frac I{1-a}\left(2\left(\frac A{1-a}\right)^{1/n}\right)^{n/\delta } +\frac1{1-2^{-\delta }}. \end{equation}

Here $\phi -u$ and the sublevel sets $\{ u<\phi -s\} $ are taken on the set where $\phi $ is finite; its complement is pluripolar, hence of zero capacity (proposition 16.7.6) and therefore $\nu $-null.

Proof. We derive a recursive inequality for the capacities of the sublevel sets of $u-\phi $ and iterate it along levels that converge to a finite depth. For $s>0$ put

\[ E_ s=\{ u<\phi -s\} ,\qquad g(s)=\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E_ s)^{1/n},\qquad B=\left(\frac A{1-a}\right)^{1/n}. \]

The sets $E_ s$ decrease as $s$ increases, so $g$ is nonincreasing.

We first compare the measures on $E_ s$. Both $u$ and $\phi -s$ belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, so the comparison principle of proposition 16.7.4, applied to $u$ and $\phi -s$, gives $\mu _\phi (E_ s)\leq \mu _ u(E_ s)$. By (17.30),

\[ \mu _ u(E_ s)\leq \nu (E_ s)+a\mu _\phi (E_ s)\leq \nu (E_ s)+a\mu _ u(E_ s), \]

and hence $(1-a)\mu _ u(E_ s)\leq \nu (E_ s)$. Since $u$ has full mass relative to $\phi $, proposition 16.7.7 gives $t^ n\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E_{s+t})\leq \mu _ u(E_ s)$ for $0<t\leq 1$. Combining this with the last inequality and the capacity bound in (17.30), we obtain $t^ ng(s+t)^ n\leq B^ ng(s)^{n(1+\delta )}$, that is,

17.32
\begin{equation} t g(s+t)\leq B g(s)^{1+\delta },\qquad s>0,\quad 0<t\leq 1. \end{equation}

By the second assertion of proposition 16.7.7, $g(s)\to 0$ as $s\to \infty $. Choose $s_0>0$ with $2Bg(s_0)^\delta \leq 1$, and put

17.33
\begin{equation} C=s_0+\frac{2B}{1-2^{-\delta }}g(s_0)^\delta . \end{equation}

We claim that $g(C)=0$. If $g(s_0)=0$, then $C=s_0$ and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, as long as $g(s_ j)>0$, define inductively

\[ t_ j=2B g(s_ j)^\delta ,\qquad s_{j+1}=s_ j+t_ j. \]

Since $g$ is nonincreasing, $t_ j\leq 2Bg(s_0)^\delta \leq 1$, and (17.32) with $s=s_ j$ and $t=t_ j$ gives

\[ g(s_{j+1})\leq \frac{Bg(s_ j)^{1+\delta }}{t_ j}=\frac{g(s_ j)}2. \]

Hence $g(s_ j)\leq 2^{-j}g(s_0)$ and $t_ j\leq 2^{-j\delta }\cdot 2Bg(s_0)^\delta $, so that

\[ s_ j\leq s_0+2B g(s_0)^\delta \sum _{i=0}^\infty 2^{-i\delta }=C \qquad \text{for every }j. \]

If the construction stops because $g(s_ j)=0$ for some $j$, then $g(C)\leq g(s_ j)=0$. Otherwise $g(C)\leq g(s_ j)\leq 2^{-j}g(s_0)$ for every $j$, and again $g(C)=0$. This proves the claim.

Thus $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E_ C)=0$. The capacity bound in (17.30) gives $\nu (E_ C)=0$. Since $\phi $ itself is a competitor for $\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }$ (definition 16.7.5), we also have $\mu _\phi (E_ C)\leq \operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E_ C)=0$. The measure inequality in (17.30) therefore gives $\mu _ u(\{ u<\phi -C\} )=0$. As $\phi -C\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, the domination principle, theorem 16.4.6, yields $u\geq \phi -C$ everywhere.

Now suppose that $I<\infty $. On $E_ s$ the weight $\phi -u$ exceeds $s$, so Markov's inequality gives $\nu (E_ s)\leq I/s$. Taking $t=1$ in the sublevel estimate and using the measure inequality on $E_ s$, we find

\[ g(s+1)^ n\leq \mu _ u(E_ s)\leq \frac{\nu (E_ s)}{1-a} \leq \frac I{(1-a)s}. \]

Choose $s=1+I(2B)^{n/\delta }/(1-a)$ and $s_0=s+1$. Since $(1-a)s>I(2B)^{n/\delta }$, we get $g(s_0)^ n\leq (2B)^{-n/\delta }$, that is, $2Bg(s_0)^\delta \leq 1$. Thus $s_0$ satisfies the condition imposed on it above, and the constant in (17.33) is at most $s_0+(1-2^{-\delta })^{-1}$. Since $(2B)^{n/\delta }=\bigl (2(A/(1-a))^{1/n}\bigr )^{n/\delta }$, this is the right side of (17.31). The lower bound $\phi -C\leq u$ remains true when $C$ is increased, which proves (17.31). $\square$

Corollary 17.4.16. Let $u\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with $u\leq \phi $, and let $A>0$ and $\delta >0$ be such that

\[ \mu _ u(E)\leq A\operatorname {Cap}_{\theta ,\phi }(E)^{1+\delta } \quad \text{for every Borel set }E\subset X. \]

If $L\geq 0$ and $E_\theta ^\phi (u)\geq -L$, then

\[ \phi -\left(2+(n+1)L(2A^{1/n})^{n/\delta } +\frac1{1-2^{-\delta }}\right)\leq u\leq \phi . \]

Proof. Apply theorem 17.4.15 with $a=0$ and $\nu =\mu _ u$. By the first inequality in (17.18), $I=\int _ X(\phi -u)\, \mu _ u\leq (n+1)\bigl (-E_\theta ^\phi (u)\bigr )\leq (n+1)L$, and (17.31) with $a=0$ gives the stated bound. $\square$

Corollary 17.4.17. Let $p>1$, let $0\leq f\in L^ p(X,\omega ^ n)$ and $0\leq a<1$, and let $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ satisfy $\sup _ Xu=0$ and

\[ \mu _ u\leq f\omega ^ n+a\mu _\phi . \]

Then $\phi -C\leq u\leq \phi $, where $C$ depends only on $X,\theta ,\omega ,p,m,\| f\| _{L^ p}$ and $(1-a)^{-1}$. In particular, if $\int _ X f\omega ^ n=m$, then the equation $\mu _ u=f\omega ^ n$ has a solution $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with $\sup _ Xu=0$, and this solution has bounded difference from $\phi $.

Proof. Since $\sup _ Xu=0$, lemma 16.4.2 gives $u\leq \phi $; recall also that $\sup _ X\phi =0$, by definition 16.3.1. Let $q=p/(p-1)$ be the conjugate exponent. By corollary 14.2.2, the $L^ q(X,\omega ^ n)$ norms of $u$ and $\phi $ are at most a constant $M_ q$ depending only on $X,\theta ,\omega ,q$. Hölder's inequality gives

\[ I=\int _ X(\phi -u)f\omega ^ n \leq \| f\| _{L^ p}\| \phi -u\| _{L^ q} \leq 2M_ q\| f\| _{L^ p}. \]

By corollary 16.9.5 with $r=2$, the measure $\nu =f\omega ^ n$ satisfies the capacity bound in (17.30) with $\delta =1$ and $A=B_2\| f\| _{L^ p}/m^2$, where $B_2$ depends only on $X,\theta ,\omega ,p$. (If $f=0$ almost everywhere, take $A=1$ instead.) With $\delta =1$, the constant (17.31) equals $4+2^ nAI/(1-a)^2$. Inserting the bounds for $A$ and $I$, and recalling that the lower bound $\phi -C\leq u$ survives an increase of $C$, we see that theorem 17.4.15 gives the assertion with

\[ C=4+\frac{2^{n+1}B_2M_ q\| f\| _{L^ p}^2}{m^2(1-a)^2}, \]

which has the stated dependence.

Finally, the measure $f\omega ^ n$ is non-pluripolar, as noted after definition 17.4.1. If its mass is $m$, theorem 17.4.13 gives a solution $u\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ of $\mu _ u=f\omega ^ n$ with $\sup _ Xu=0$, and the bound just proved, with $a=0$, shows that $u$ has bounded difference from $\phi $. $\square$

17.4.4 Positive mass and bigness

So far the background class has been big, and big classes have positive volume by proposition 15.8.2. The equations solved above give the converse for pseudo-effective classes. In this subsection $\theta $ denotes an arbitrary smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$; its class need not be big, and no model potential is fixed.

Let $\alpha =\{ \theta \} $ be a pseudo-effective class whose volume, in the sense of definition 15.8.1, is positive. The classes $\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $, $\varepsilon >0$, are big, and in each of them we solve the Monge–Ampère equation whose right-hand side is a fixed smooth measure rescaled to the total mass $\operatorname {vol}(\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )$, which is at least $\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )$. A pointwise inequality between traces, integrated against these solutions, yields lower bounds for the pairings $\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau $ with strictly positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-forms $\tau $, uniformly in $\varepsilon $. Lamari's duality criterion, which we prove first, turns such bounds into a Kähler current in $\alpha $.

Recall from section 7.2 that weak and strong positivity coincide for real $(n-1,n-1)$-forms, so that we speak simply of positive forms, and that such a form is strictly positive if at every point it lies in the interior of the cone of positive forms. For instance, $\omega ^{n-1}$ is strictly positive: at a point $x$, choose linear coordinates on the tangent space in which $\omega (x)$ is the standard form $\beta $; then $\omega (x)^{n-1}=\beta ^{n-1}$ is an interior point of the cone by proposition 7.2.1, and complex-linear isomorphisms preserve positivity. For real $(n-1,n-1)$-forms we write $\eta \leq \tau $ if $\tau -\eta $ is positive.

Lemma 17.4.18 (Lamari's criterion). Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $ if and only if

17.34
\begin{equation} \int _ X\theta \wedge \tau \geq 0 \end{equation}

for every smooth strictly positive real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\tau $ on $X$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0$. Consequently, the class $\{ \theta \} $ is big if and only if there is $\delta >0$ such that every such form $\tau $ satisfies

17.35
\begin{equation} \int _ X\theta \wedge \tau \geq \delta \int _ X\omega \wedge \tau . \end{equation}

Proof. Necessity. If $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, then $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$. For $\tau $ as in the statement, the definition of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ as a current gives $\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \tau =\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0$, hence $\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau =\int _ XT\wedge \tau $, which is nonnegative by the definition of a positive current, since $\tau $ is positive, hence strongly positive.

Sufficiency. Conversely, assume (17.34) for all forms $\tau $ as in the statement. We extend the functional $\tau \mapsto \int _ X\theta \wedge \tau $ from $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed forms to a positive linear functional on all smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-forms, regard the extension as a positive $(1,1)$-current $T$, and show that $T-\theta $ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a distribution.

A positive extension. Let $F$ be the real vector space of smooth real $(n-1,n-1)$-forms on $X$, let $S=\{ \tau \in F:\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0\} $, and put $e=\omega ^{n-1}$, with $e=1$ if $n=1$. Since $\omega $ is closed, $e\in S$, and $e$ is strictly positive. Define $\ell _0(\tau )=\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau $ for $\tau \in S$. By hypothesis $\ell _0(\tau )\geq 0$ when $\tau \in S$ is strictly positive. If $\tau \in S$ is merely positive, then $\tau +te\in S$ is strictly positive for every $t>0$. Indeed, at each point $x$ the cone of positive forms contains a ball about $e(x)$, for some norm; since the cone is stable under sums and positive multiples, it also contains the ball of $t$ times that radius about $\tau (x)+te(x)$. Hence $\ell _0(\tau )+t\ell _0(e)\geq 0$, and letting $t\downarrow 0$ gives $\ell _0(\tau )\geq 0$.

We next compare the forms in $F$ with $e$. Fix finitely many holomorphic charts $U_1,\ldots ,U_ N$ and compact sets $K_ a\subset U_ a$ with $\bigcup _ aK_ a=X$. In $U_ a$ write $\eta \in F$ as $\eta =\sum _{I,J}\eta ^ a_{IJ}\, \mathrm dz_ I\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ J$, the sum running over increasing multi-indices of length $n-1$, and put

\[ \| \eta \| =\max _{1\leq a\leq N}\ \max _{I,J}\ \sup _{K_ a}|\eta ^ a_{IJ}|. \]

We claim that there is a constant $M>0$ such that

17.36
\begin{equation} -M\| \eta \| e\leq \eta \leq M\| \eta \| e\qquad \text{for every }\eta \in F. \end{equation}

Let $Z$ be the finite-dimensional real vector space of real $(n-1,n-1)$-forms with constant coefficients on $\mathbb C^ n$, normed by the maximum modulus of the coefficients, and let $P\subset Z$ be the closed convex cone of positive forms (proposition 7.2.1). For $x\in U_ a$, the differential of the chart is a complex-linear isomorphism from the tangent space at $x$ onto $\mathbb C^ n$, under which $\mathrm dz_ j$ corresponds to the $j$th coordinate form. Since complex-linear isomorphisms preserve positivity, a real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\eta $ is positive at $x$ if and only if its coefficient form $\eta ^ a(x)=\sum _{I,J}\eta ^ a_{IJ}(x)\, \mathrm dz_ I\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ J\in Z$ lies in $P$.

Because $e$ is strictly positive, the continuous map $x\mapsto e^ a(x)$ sends the compact set $K_ a$ into the interior of $P$; the complement of this interior is closed and disjoint from the compact image, so it has positive distance $r_ a$ from it. Put $r=\frac12\min _ ar_ a$. If $\eta \in F$ satisfies $\| \eta \| \leq r$ and $x\in K_ a$, then $e^ a(x)\pm \eta ^ a(x)$ lies within distance $r<r_ a$ of $e^ a(x)$, hence in $P$; thus $e\pm \eta $ is positive at $x$. Since the sets $K_ a$ cover $X$, the forms $e\pm \eta $ are positive. Applying this to $r\eta /\| \eta \| $ for $\eta \neq 0$ proves (17.36) with $M=1/r$.

For $\eta \in F$, a form $\tau \in S$ with $\tau \geq \eta $ is called an $S$-majorant of $\eta $, and we put

\[ p(\eta )=\inf \{ \ell _0(\tau ):\tau \text{ is an $S$-majorant of }\eta \} . \]

Let $\eta \in F$ and $C=M\| \eta \| $, so that $-Ce\leq \eta \leq Ce$ by (17.36). The form $Ce$ is an $S$-majorant of $\eta $, so $p(\eta )\leq C\ell _0(e)$. If $\tau $ is an $S$-majorant of $\eta $, then $\tau +Ce\geq \eta +Ce\geq 0$, so $\tau +Ce\in S$ is positive and $\ell _0(\tau )\geq -C\ell _0(e)$. Thus $p$ is finite. It is sublinear: the sum of $S$-majorants of $\eta _1$ and $\eta _2$ is an $S$-majorant of $\eta _1+\eta _2$, and for $\lambda >0$ the $S$-majorants of $\lambda \eta $ are $\lambda $ times those of $\eta $. If $\eta \in S$, then $\eta $ is its own $S$-majorant, and every $S$-majorant $\tau $ of $\eta $ satisfies $\ell _0(\tau )-\ell _0(\eta )=\ell _0(\tau -\eta )\geq 0$ because $\tau -\eta \in S$ is positive. Hence $p=\ell _0$ on $S$.

Next we extend $\ell _0$ to a linear functional on $F$ dominated by $p$; this is the Hahn–Banach theorem, whose proof we recall. Let $\ell $ be a linear functional on a subspace $W\supset S$ which extends $\ell _0$ and satisfies $\ell \leq p$ on $W$, and let $\eta _0\in F\setminus W$. For $\eta _1,\eta _2\in W$,

\[ \ell (\eta _1)+\ell (\eta _2)=\ell (\eta _1+\eta _2)\leq p(\eta _1+\eta _2)\leq p(\eta _1-\eta _0)+p(\eta _2+\eta _0), \]

so

\[ \sup _{\eta _1\in W}\bigl (\ell (\eta _1)-p(\eta _1-\eta _0)\bigr ) \leq \inf _{\eta _2\in W}\bigl (p(\eta _2+\eta _0)-\ell (\eta _2)\bigr ). \]

Taking $\eta _1=\eta _2=0$ shows that the supremum is greater than $-\infty $ and the infimum is less than $+\infty $; hence both are finite, and we may choose a real number $c$ between them. Define $\ell (\eta +t\eta _0)=\ell (\eta )+tc$ for $\eta \in W$ and $t\in \mathbb R$. For $t>0$, the upper bound for $c$ with $\eta _2=\eta /t$, multiplied by $t$, gives $\ell (\eta )+tc\leq p(\eta +t\eta _0)$; for $t<0$, the lower bound for $c$ with $\eta _1=\eta /|t|$, multiplied by $|t|$, gives the same inequality. Thus the extension to $W+\mathbb R\eta _0$ is still dominated by $p$. The union of a chain of such extensions is again such an extension, so Zorn's lemma gives a maximal one, and by the step just proved its domain is $F$. We denote it again by $\ell $.

The functional $\ell $ is positive: if $\eta \in F$ is positive, then $0$ is an $S$-majorant of $-\eta $, so $\ell (-\eta )\leq p(-\eta )\leq 0$. It is also bounded: by (17.36), the form $M\| \eta \| e$ is an $S$-majorant of both $\eta $ and $-\eta $, so

17.37
\begin{equation} |\ell (\eta )|\leq M\ell _0(e)\| \eta \| \qquad (\eta \in F). \end{equation}

We now turn $\ell $ into a current. Every smooth form on the compact manifold $X$ is a test form. For a complex form $\varphi $ of degree $2n-2$, let $\varphi '$ be its component of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$, and put $T(\varphi )=\ell (\operatorname{Re}\varphi ')+\mathrm i\, \ell (\operatorname{Im}\varphi ')$, where $\operatorname{Re}\varphi '=(\varphi '+\overline{\varphi '})/2$ and $\operatorname{Im}\varphi '=(\varphi '-\overline{\varphi '})/(2\mathrm i)$ are real forms in $F$. This is a complex-linear functional: replacing $\varphi $ by $\mathrm i\varphi $ replaces $(\operatorname{Re}\varphi ',\operatorname{Im}\varphi ')$ by $(-\operatorname{Im}\varphi ',\operatorname{Re}\varphi ')$. Moreover, $T$ has the continuity property of a current of degree $2$. Indeed, let $Q$ be a compact subset of a chart $U$, and let $\varphi $ be supported in $Q$, with coefficients $\varphi _ J$ in real coordinates on $U$. On the compact set $K_ a\cap Q$, the coefficients of $\operatorname{Re}\varphi '$ and $\operatorname{Im}\varphi '$ in the chart $U_ a$ are linear combinations of the functions $\varphi _ J$ and $\overline{\varphi _ J}$ with coefficients that are smooth on $U\cap U_ a$, and they vanish on $K_ a\setminus Q$. Hence $\| \operatorname{Re}\varphi '\| +\| \operatorname{Im}\varphi '\| \leq C_ Q\sum _ J\sup _ Q|\varphi _ J|$ with a constant $C_ Q$ independent of $\varphi $, and (17.37) gives $|T(\varphi )|\leq MC_ Q\ell _0(e)\sum _ J\sup _ Q|\varphi _ J|$.

By construction $T$ vanishes on forms of bidegree other than $(n-1,n-1)$, so it is a current of bidegree $(1,1)$. It is real, because the $(n-1,n-1)$-component of $\overline\varphi $ is $\overline{\varphi '}=\operatorname{Re}\varphi '-\mathrm i\operatorname{Im}\varphi '$, so that $T(\overline\varphi )=\overline{T(\varphi )}$. It is positive, because a strongly positive test form $\varphi $ of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$ is real and positive, so that $T(\varphi )=\ell (\varphi )\geq 0$. Since $\ell $ extends $\ell _0$, the real $(1,1)$-current $R=T-\theta $ annihilates $S$.

The current $R$ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact. We first show that $R$ is closed. If $\xi $ is a smooth form of bidegree $(n-2,n-1)$, then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\partial \xi =\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial \partial \xi =-\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \partial \bar\partial \xi =0$, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bar\partial \xi '=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \bar\partial \bar\partial \xi '=0$ for $\xi '$ of bidegree $(n-1,n-2)$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ is a real operator, the conjugate of a $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed complex $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\sigma $ is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed, so the real forms $\operatorname{Re}\sigma $ and $\operatorname{Im}\sigma $ lie in $S$; as $R$ is complex-linear and annihilates $S$, it annihilates $\sigma $. In particular $R(\partial \xi )=R(\bar\partial \xi ')=0$. For a smooth $(2n-3)$-form $\gamma $, the $(n-1,n-1)$-component of $\mathrm d\gamma $ is $\partial \gamma ^{n-2,n-1}+\bar\partial \gamma ^{n-1,n-2}$; since $R$ has bidegree $(1,1)$, it follows that $R(\mathrm d\gamma )=0$. Therefore $\mathrm dR=0$.

Since $e\in S$, we also have $\int _ XR\wedge \omega ^{n-1}=0$. Let $\Lambda $ denote contraction with $\omega $, as in lemma 14.1.6; the identity $R\wedge \omega ^{n-1}=(\Lambda R)\omega ^ n/n$ shows that the trace $\Lambda R$ has mean zero. By lemma 14.1.3(2), applied to the Laplacian $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$ of section 14.1, there is a real distribution $f$ with $Lf=-4\pi \Lambda R$. By lemma 14.1.6, $\Lambda \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\Lambda R$, so $R'=R-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ is a closed real $(1,1)$-current with zero trace. By the last assertion of lemma 14.1.6, $R'$ is harmonic, and by lemma 14.1.3(1) it is smooth. If $n=1$, a $(1,1)$-form with zero trace vanishes, so $R'=0$. If $n\geq 2$, (14.13) gives $*R'=-R'\wedge \omega ^{n-2}/(n-2)!$, a smooth closed $(n-1,n-1)$-form; hence $*R'\in S$. Since $R$ annihilates $S$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}{*R'}=0$,

\[ \| R'\| _{L^2}^2 =\int _ XR'\wedge *R' =R(*R')-\int _ Xf\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}{*R'}=0. \]

Thus $R'=0$ and $R=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ in every dimension.

Conclusion. The current $T=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ is closed and positive, and it lies in the class $\{ \theta \} $, since it differs from $\theta $ by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a distribution. By corollary 14.1.9, there is $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=T$. This proves sufficiency.

For the last assertion, $\{ \theta \} $ is big exactly when $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta -\delta \omega )\neq \varnothing $ for some $\delta >0$. Indeed, if $T\geq \delta \omega $ is a Kähler current in $\{ \theta \} $ (definition 14.3.1), then $T-\delta \omega $ is a closed positive current in the class $\{ \theta -\delta \omega \} $, so $T-\delta \omega =\theta -\delta \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta -\delta \omega )$, by corollary 14.1.9. Conversely, if $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta -\delta \omega )$, then $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \delta \omega $ is a Kähler current in $\{ \theta \} $. Applying the first part to $\theta -\delta \omega $ shows that this holds if and only if (17.35) holds for that $\delta $. $\square$

The proof of the bigness criterion rests on the following lower bound for the product of two trace masses of a current with prescribed Monge–Ampère measure.

Lemma 17.4.19. Let $A$ be a closed subset of $X$ of Lebesgue measure zero in every chart, and let $S$ be a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$ whose local potentials are locally bounded on $X\setminus A$. Let $\tau $ be a smooth strictly positive real $(n-1,n-1)$-form, and put $\nu =\omega \wedge \tau $. If

\[ \langle S^ n\rangle =m\nu \qquad \text{for some }m\geq 0, \]

then

17.38
\begin{equation} \left(\int _ X S\wedge \tau \right) \left(\int _ X\langle S^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega \right) \geq \frac mn\left(\int _ X\nu \right)^2. \end{equation}

For $n=1$ the second factor is $\int _ X\omega $.

Proof. We prove a pointwise inequality for smooth forms, apply it to regularizations of the potentials of $S$ on small balls in $X\setminus A$, and globalize with a partition of unity.

Step 1: a pointwise inequality. Let $\sigma $ be a smooth positive definite real $(1,1)$-form. At a given point, choose linear coordinates on the tangent space that diagonalize the Hermitian matrices of $\omega $ and $\sigma $ simultaneously, so that $\omega =\sum _ j\gamma _ j$ and $\sigma =\sum _ j\lambda _ j\gamma _ j$ with $\lambda _ j>0$, where $\gamma _ j=\mathrm i\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$, and put $\mathrm dV=\gamma _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \gamma _ n$. Since $\tau $ is strictly positive (section 7.2), the form $\tau -c\, \omega ^{n-1}$ is positive at this point for some $c>0$. The product of the strongly positive form $\gamma _ j$ with this positive form is a nonnegative multiple of $\mathrm dV$ (proposition 7.2.1), and $\gamma _ j\wedge \omega ^{n-1}=(n-1)!\, \mathrm dV$, so $\gamma _ j\wedge \tau \geq c(n-1)!\, \mathrm dV$; thus $\gamma _ j\wedge \tau =t_ j\, \mathrm dV$ with $t_ j>0$. Expanding the products, we obtain

\[ \frac{\sigma \wedge \tau }{\omega \wedge \tau } =\frac{\sum _ j\lambda _ jt_ j}{\sum _ jt_ j},\qquad \frac{\sigma ^{n-1}\wedge \omega }{\sigma ^ n} =\frac1n\sum _ j\lambda _ j^{-1}. \]

Since $\lambda _ j\sum _ k\lambda _ k^{-1}\geq 1$ for every $j$, the product of these two ratios is at least $1/n$. If $\sigma $ is only semipositive, we apply this to $\sigma +s\omega $ and let $s\downarrow 0$. Thus, for every smooth semipositive real $(1,1)$-form $\sigma $, as densities with respect to any fixed smooth volume form,

17.39
\begin{equation} (\sigma \wedge \tau )(\sigma ^{n-1}\wedge \omega ) \geq \frac1n\sigma ^ n\nu . \end{equation}

Step 2: regularization on a ball. Since the local potentials of $S$ are locally bounded on the open set $X\setminus A$, every point of $X\setminus A$ is the center of a coordinate ball $B\Subset X\setminus A$ on which $S=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ with $v$ plurisubharmonic and bounded. Fix such a ball, put $\eta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ as in section 9.7, and write $\nu =g\eta ^ n$ with $g$ smooth and positive on $B$. Since $v$ is bounded on $B$, the non-pluripolar powers of $S$ on $B$ are the Bedford–Taylor powers of $v$ (see the remark following definition 15.1.3); in particular $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n=mg\eta ^ n$.

Let $v_\varepsilon =v*\rho _\varepsilon $ be the regularizations of $v$ by the radial mollifiers of section 1.4. On each ball $B'$ concentric with $B$ and of smaller radius, the functions $v_\varepsilon $ are defined for $\varepsilon $ smaller than the difference of the radii, and by proposition 8.2.2(2) they are smooth and plurisubharmonic on $B'$ and decrease to $v$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. As recalled at the beginning of section 9.7, $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\varepsilon )^ n=\det \mathcal H(v_\varepsilon )\, \eta ^ n$. The last assertion of proposition 9.7.3, applied on $B$ to $v$ and the continuous density $mg$, therefore gives

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\varepsilon )^ n=F_\varepsilon \eta ^ n, \qquad F_\varepsilon =\det \mathcal H(v_\varepsilon )\geq \bigl ((mg)^{1/n}*\rho _\varepsilon \bigr )^ n. \]

The right side converges to $mg$ uniformly on compact subsets of $B$, by lemma 1.4.1(1) applied to the continuous function $(mg)^{1/n}$. Hence $\sqrt{F_\varepsilon g}$ is bounded below by functions converging uniformly to $\sqrt mg$ on compact sets, and for every nonnegative $\chi \in C^\infty _ c(B)$,

17.40
\begin{equation} \varliminf _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \int \chi \sqrt{F_\varepsilon g}\, \eta ^ n \geq \sqrt m\int \chi \nu . \end{equation}

Moreover, for every $\chi \in C^\infty _ c(B)$, as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ along any sequence,

\[ \int \chi \, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\varepsilon \wedge \tau \longrightarrow \int \chi \, S\wedge \tau , \qquad \int \chi \, (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\varepsilon )^{n-1}\wedge \omega \longrightarrow \int \chi \, \langle S^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega . \]

For the first convergence, note that $\int \chi \, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\varepsilon \wedge \tau =\int v_\varepsilon \, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi \tau )$, by the definition of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ on currents (section 7.2); since $v_\varepsilon \to v$ in $L^1$ on $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $ (lemma 1.4.1(2)), this tends to $\int v\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi \tau )=\int \chi \, S\wedge \tau $.

For the second convergence, choose a ball $B'$ as above containing $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $, and a sequence $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$. By theorem 9.2.5, applied on $B'$ to the closed positive current $\omega $ of bidimension $(n-1,n-1)$ and to $n-1$ copies of the decreasing sequence $(v_{\varepsilon _ j})$, the currents $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{\varepsilon _ j})^{n-1}\wedge \omega $ converge weakly to the Bedford–Taylor product $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^{n-1}\wedge \omega $. By proposition 9.1.2(1) and (4), these products are the exterior products with $\omega $ of the smooth forms $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{\varepsilon _ j})^{n-1}$ and of the current $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^{n-1}$, which equals $\langle S^{n-1}\rangle $ on $B$. (For $n=1$ both sides of the second convergence equal $\int \chi \, \omega $.)

Step 3: globalization. Let $\chi $ be a smooth function with compact support in $X\setminus A$ and $0\leq \chi \leq 1$. Cover $\operatorname{Supp}\chi $ by finitely many balls $B_ l$ as in Step 2, and write $\chi =\sum _ l\chi _ l$ with nonnegative $\chi _ l\in C^\infty _ c(B_ l)$; for instance, multiply $\chi $ by a partition of unity with compact supports subordinate to the cover of $X$ by the balls $B_ l$ and $X\setminus \operatorname{Supp}\chi $ (proposition 7.1.3). In each $B_ l$ we use the notation of Step 2 with an index $l$ and put

\[ a_{l,\varepsilon }=\int \chi _ l\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{l,\varepsilon }\wedge \tau ,\qquad b_{l,\varepsilon }=\int \chi _ l\, (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{l,\varepsilon })^{n-1}\wedge \omega . \]

Let $\alpha _{l,\varepsilon }$ and $\beta _{l,\varepsilon }$ be the densities of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{l,\varepsilon }\wedge \tau $ and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{l,\varepsilon })^{n-1}\wedge \omega $ with respect to $\eta _ l^ n$. By (17.39) with $\sigma =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{l,\varepsilon }$, $\alpha _{l,\varepsilon }\beta _{l,\varepsilon }\geq F_{l,\varepsilon }g_ l/n$, so the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\[ \int \chi _ l\sqrt{F_{l,\varepsilon }g_ l}\, \eta _ l^ n \leq \sqrt n\int \chi _ l\sqrt{\alpha _{l,\varepsilon }\beta _{l,\varepsilon }}\, \eta _ l^ n \leq \sqrt{n\, a_{l,\varepsilon }b_{l,\varepsilon }}. \]

Summing over $l$ and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the finite sum $\sum _ l\sqrt{a_{l,\varepsilon }b_{l,\varepsilon }}$, we obtain

\[ \left(\sum _ l a_{l,\varepsilon }\right) \left(\sum _ l b_{l,\varepsilon }\right) \geq \frac1n\left(\sum _ l \int \chi _ l\sqrt{F_{l,\varepsilon }g_ l}\, \eta _ l^ n\right)^2. \]

Now let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ along a sequence. By Step 2, the two sums on the left converge to $\int _ X\chi \, S\wedge \tau $ and $\int _ X\chi \langle S^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega $. These are at most the corresponding integrals over $X$ without $\chi $, because $S\wedge \tau $ and $\langle S^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega $ are positive measures and $0\leq \chi \leq 1$. By (17.40) and $g_ l\eta _ l^ n=\nu $, the lower limit of the right side is at least $(m/n)(\int _ X\chi \nu )^2$. Therefore

\[ \left(\int _ X S\wedge \tau \right) \left(\int _ X\langle S^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega \right) \geq \frac mn\left(\int _ X\chi \nu \right)^2. \]

Finally, we remove the cutoff. Fix a metric $d$ inducing the topology of $X$ (lemma 7.1.4), and for $k\geq 1$ let $K_ k=\{ x\in X:d(x,A)\geq 1/k\} $, with $K_ k=X$ if $A=\varnothing $. These compact subsets of $X\setminus A$ increase to $X\setminus A$, because $A$ is closed. By proposition 7.1.3 there are smooth functions $\chi _ k\colon X\to [0,1]$ with compact support in $X\setminus A$ and $\chi _ k=1$ on $K_ k$. Since $\nu $ has a smooth density and $A$ has Lebesgue measure zero, $\nu (K_ k)\to \nu (X\setminus A)=\int _ X\nu $, so $\int _ X\chi _ k\nu \to \int _ X\nu $. Applying the last inequality to $\chi =\chi _ k$ and letting $k\to \infty $ proves (17.38). Both factors on its left are finite: the first is the pairing of a current with a smooth form on the compact manifold $X$, and the second is finite by theorem 15.4.3. $\square$

Theorem 17.4.20. A pseudo-effective class $\alpha \in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb R)$ is big if and only if $\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )>0$. In particular, if a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ satisfies $\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle >0$, then its class is big.

Proof. By proposition 15.8.2, big classes have positive volume, and every closed positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ satisfies $\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle \leq \operatorname {vol}(\{ T\} )$. It therefore suffices to show that a pseudo-effective class $\alpha $ with $m=\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )>0$ is big, and we do so by verifying Lamari's criterion (lemma 17.4.18). Write $\alpha =\{ \theta \} $ with $\theta $ smooth and closed, choose $C>0$ such that $\theta +C\omega $ is positive definite, and put

\[ C_0=\int _ X(\theta +(C+1)\omega )^{n-1}\wedge \omega >0; \]

the integrand is a positive multiple of the volume form at every point, by the positivity of mixed discriminants of positive definite matrices (lemma 7.2.2(2),(3)).

For $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$ let $\theta _\varepsilon =\theta +\varepsilon \omega $. Since $\alpha $ is pseudo-effective, there is $q\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ (corollary 14.1.9), and $\theta _\varepsilon +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \varepsilon \omega $; thus $\{ \theta _\varepsilon \} =\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ is big. By proposition 16.3.2(4), $P_{\theta _\varepsilon }[V_{\theta _\varepsilon }]=V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$, and the mass of $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$ is

\[ m_\varepsilon =\operatorname {vol}(\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} ) \geq m>0; \]

the inequality holds because $\operatorname {vol}(\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )$ decreases to $\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, by definition 15.8.1, proposition 15.8.2. Hence $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$ is a model potential of mass $m_\varepsilon $.

Fix a smooth strictly positive real $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\tau $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0$, normalized by $\int _ X\omega \wedge \tau =1$. The measure $m_\varepsilon \omega \wedge \tau $ has a smooth density with respect to $\omega ^ n$ and total mass $m_\varepsilon $. By corollary 17.4.17, applied in the big class $\{ \theta _\varepsilon \} $ with the reference $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$, there are $u_\varepsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _\varepsilon )$ and finite constants $B_\varepsilon $ such that

\[ \begin{gathered} S_\varepsilon =\theta _\varepsilon +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon \geq 0, \qquad \langle S_\varepsilon ^ n\rangle =m_\varepsilon \omega \wedge \tau ,\\ V_{\theta _\varepsilon }-B_\varepsilon \leq u_\varepsilon \leq V_{\theta _\varepsilon }. \end{gathered} \]

The constants $B_\varepsilon $ need not be bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon $; we only use that $u_\varepsilon $ is locally bounded outside a closed set of measure zero. By lemma 16.2.3, applied to the big class $\{ \theta _\varepsilon \} $, there is a closed pluripolar set $A_\varepsilon \subset X$ such that $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus A_\varepsilon $; being pluripolar, $A_\varepsilon $ has Lebesgue measure zero in every chart (see the remarks after definition 10.1.1). Hence $u_\varepsilon $, which lies between $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }-B_\varepsilon $ and $V_{\theta _\varepsilon }$, is locally bounded on $X\setminus A_\varepsilon $, and so are the local potentials of $S_\varepsilon $, which differ from $u_\varepsilon $ by smooth local potentials of $\theta _\varepsilon $ (lemma 7.3.1).

We may thus apply lemma 17.4.19 to $S_\varepsilon $, with $A_\varepsilon $, $\nu =\omega \wedge \tau $ and $m_\varepsilon $ in place of $A$, $\nu $ and $m$:

\[ \left(\int _ X S_\varepsilon \wedge \tau \right) \left(\int _ X\langle S_\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega \right) \geq \frac{m_\varepsilon }{n}\geq \frac mn. \]

We bound the two factors on the left. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =0$, the first factor equals the cohomological pairing $\int _ X\theta _\varepsilon \wedge \tau =\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau +\varepsilon $. If $n\geq 2$, the form $\theta _\varepsilon +C\omega $ is positive definite, so theorem 15.4.3 gives

\[ \int _ X\langle S_\varepsilon ^{n-1}\rangle \wedge \omega \leq \int _ X(\theta +(C+\varepsilon )\omega )^{n-1}\wedge \omega \leq C_0; \]

the last inequality holds because $\theta +(C+1)\omega =(\theta +(C+\varepsilon )\omega )+(1-\varepsilon )\omega $ and products of positive $(1,1)$-forms are positive. If $n=1$, the second factor equals $\int _ X\omega =C_0$. As the first factor is nonnegative, in both cases

\[ \left(\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau +\varepsilon \right)C_0\geq \frac mn. \]

Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $\int _ X\theta \wedge \tau \geq m/(nC_0)$. Removing the normalization of $\tau $, we conclude that every smooth strictly positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed $(n-1,n-1)$-form $\tau $ satisfies (17.35) with $\delta =m/(nC_0)>0$. By lemma 17.4.18, the class $\alpha $ is big. $\square$

If $\{ \theta \} $ is pseudo-effective but not big, the theorem shows that every $\theta $-psh potential has zero non-pluripolar mass. This allows us to extend the contact theorems of chapter 16 from big to pseudo-effective classes.

Corollary 17.4.21. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form, and let $f\colon X\to [-\infty ,+\infty ]$ be a quasi-continuous Borel function. Suppose that the family $\mathcal A_\theta (f)$ of definition 16.1.3 is nonempty and uniformly bounded above. Then $u=P_\theta (f)$ satisfies

\[ \mu _ u\bigl (X\setminus \{ u=f\} \bigr )=0. \]

If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, then $\mu _ u=0$. Moreover, if $r\geq 1$ and $u_1,\ldots ,u_ r\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ have a rooftop $w=P_\theta (\min _{1\leq j\leq r}u_ j)$ that is not identically $-\infty $, then

\[ \mu _ w\leq \sum _{j=1}^ r\mathbf1_{\{ w=u_ j\} }\mu _{u_ j}. \]

Proof. By proposition 16.1.4(1), $u\in \mathcal A_\theta (f)$; that is, $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, then $\mu _ u(X)=0$ by theorem 17.4.20, since otherwise $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ would be a closed positive current of positive non-pluripolar mass in $\{ \theta \} $. Hence $\mu _ u=0$, and in particular the first assertion holds.

If $\{ \theta \} $ is big, the first assertion is theorem 16.2.5.

For the rooftop, the function $\min _ ju_ j$ is quasi-continuous, as a finite minimum of potentials (see the beginning of section 16.1), and it is bounded above; its envelope is $w$. Since $w$ is not identically $-\infty $, the family $\mathcal A_\theta (\min _ ju_ j)$ is nonempty, and it is uniformly bounded above because the obstacle is. By the first assertion, $\mu _ w$ is carried by $\{ w=\min _ ju_ j\} \subset \bigcup _ j\{ w=u_ j\} $. The obstacle is upper semicontinuous, so $w\leq u_ j$ everywhere by proposition 16.1.4(1),(2), and lemma 16.2.2 gives $\mathbf1_{\{ w=u_ j\} }\mu _ w\leq \mathbf1_{\{ w=u_ j\} }\mu _{u_ j}$ for every $j$. Summing over $j$ proves the rooftop inequality. $\square$

Corollary 17.4.22. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form with $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $, and let $f$ be a continuous real function on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian. Then

\[ \langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_\theta (f))^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ P_\theta (f)=f\} }(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ n, \]

where the right side is understood as in theorem 16.2.31. In particular $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\theta )^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ V_\theta =0\} }\theta ^ n$. If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, both sides of these two identities vanish.

Proof. The potential $V_\theta +\min _ Xf$ lies below $f$, so $\mathcal A_\theta (f)$ is nonempty, and it is bounded above by $\max _ Xf$. Put $u=P_\theta (f)$. Since $f$ is continuous, $u\leq f$ everywhere by proposition 16.1.4(1),(2). The scalar case of theorem 16.2.31, with all $\theta _ j=\theta $, $u_ j=u$ and $f_ j=f$, gives

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u=f\} }\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ u=f\} }(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ n, \]

and corollary 17.4.21 shows that $\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle $ is carried by $\{ u=f\} $. This proves the first identity. For $f=0$ the envelope $P_\theta (0)$ is $V_\theta $, by definition 14.2.6, which gives the second. If $\{ \theta \} $ is not big, the left sides vanish by corollary 17.4.21, and hence so do the right sides. $\square$

17.4.5 Mixed masses and concavity

We now compare the mixed non-pluripolar mass of $n$ closed positive currents, which may lie in different classes, with their individual masses. The idea is to replace each current by a solution of a Monge–Ampère equation with one and the same smooth right-hand side. Since singularity envelopes preserve mixed masses (proposition 16.3.2(3)), such a replacement does not change the mixed mass, and for the solutions the pointwise mixed lower bound of proposition 15.4.5 integrates to the desired inequality. The equations can be solved because, by theorem 17.4.20, every current of positive mass lies in a big class.

Theorem 17.4.23. Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, and let $T_1,\ldots ,T_ n$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$. Then

17.41
\begin{equation} \int _ X\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n\rangle \geq \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle \right)^{1/n}. \end{equation}

Consequently the functions

\[ T\longmapsto \left(\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle \right)^{1/n}, \qquad T\longmapsto \log \left(\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle \right) \]

are concave on the convex cone of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents on $X$, with the convention $\log 0=-\infty $ for the second function.

Proof. All the masses involved are finite by theorem 15.4.3. If one of the masses $\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle $ vanishes, the right side of (17.41) is zero and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise put $m_ i=\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle >0$. Both sides of (17.41) are positively homogeneous of degree one in each $T_ i$, by the multilinearity of non-pluripolar products (proposition 15.1.5), so after replacing $T_ i$ by $m_ i^{-1/n}T_ i$ we may assume $m_ i=1$ for all $i$.

By theorem 17.4.20, the class of each $T_ i$ is big. Let $\theta _ i$ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form in the class of $T_ i$ (definition 14.1.8). By corollary 14.1.9 we can write $T_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$ with $u_ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$. Set $\phi _ i=P_{\theta _ i}[u_ i]$. By the mass preservation in proposition 16.3.2(3) and by theorem 16.4.7, $\phi _ i$ is a model potential of mass one.

Let $\nu =\omega ^ n/\int _ X\omega ^ n$. This smooth probability measure is non-pluripolar, as noted after definition 17.4.1. In each big class $\{ \theta _ i\} $, theorem 17.4.13 gives

\[ w_ i\in \mathcal E(X,\theta _ i,\phi _ i),\qquad S_ i=\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ i\geq 0,\qquad \langle S_ i^ n\rangle =\nu . \]

By proposition 16.4.10, $P_{\theta _ i}[w_ i]=P_{\theta _ i}[\phi _ i]=\phi _ i$. Applying the mixed mass preservation of proposition 16.3.2(3) first to the tuple $(w_1,\ldots ,w_ n)$ and then to the tuple $(u_1,\ldots ,u_ n)$, we obtain

\[ \int _ X\langle S_1\wedge \cdots \wedge S_ n\rangle =\int _ X\left\langle \bigwedge _{i=1}^ n (\theta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi _ i)\right\rangle =\int _ X\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n\rangle . \]

On the other hand, proposition 15.4.5, with all densities equal to one, gives $\langle S_1\wedge \cdots \wedge S_ n\rangle \geq \nu $. Hence the common value of these mixed masses is at least $\nu (X)=1$, which is the normalized inequality. Undoing the scalings proves (17.41).

For the concavity, let $T,S$ be closed positive $(1,1)$-currents and $0<t<1$; for $t=0,1$ there is nothing to prove. Put $a=\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle $ and $b=\int _ X\langle S^ n\rangle $. By multilinearity,

\[ \int _ X\langle ((1-t)T+tS)^ n\rangle =\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(1-t)^ kt^{n-k} \int _ X\langle T^ k\wedge S^{n-k}\rangle . \]

For $1\leq k\leq n-1$, (17.41) with $k$ factors $T$ and $n-k$ factors $S$ gives $\int _ X\langle T^ k\wedge S^{n-k}\rangle \geq a^{k/n}b^{(n-k)/n}$; the terms with $k=n$ and $k=0$ are $a$ and $b$, also when one of them vanishes. The binomial formula therefore yields

\[ \int _ X\langle ((1-t)T+tS)^ n\rangle \geq \big ((1-t)a^{1/n}+tb^{1/n}\big )^ n. \]

Taking $n$th roots proves that the function $T\mapsto (\int _ X\langle T^ n\rangle )^{1/n}$ is concave. If $a$ and $b$ are both positive, then, since the logarithm is increasing and concave,

\[ \begin{aligned} \log \Big(\int _ X\langle ((1-t)T+tS)^ n\rangle \Big) & \geq n\log \big ((1-t)a^{1/n}+tb^{1/n}\big )\\ & \geq (1-t)\log a+t\log b. \end{aligned} \]

If one of them is zero, the right side is $-\infty $ for $0<t<1$, so there is nothing to prove. $\square$

The connectedness of $X$ cannot be omitted when $n\geq 2$. Let $X$ be the disjoint union of two compact Kähler manifolds $X',X''$ of dimension $n\geq 2$, with Kähler forms $\omega '$ and $\omega ''$. Let $T_1$ equal $\omega '$ on $X'$ and zero on $X''$, let $T_2$ equal zero on $X'$ and $\omega ''$ on $X''$, and let $T_3,\ldots ,T_ n$ equal $\omega '$ on $X'$ and $\omega ''$ on $X''$. Every mass $\int _ X\langle T_ i^ n\rangle $ is positive, but $\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ n\rangle =0$. The theorem applies separately on each connected component.

17.5 Singularity envelopes at arbitrary mass

Throughout this section $\theta $ is a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ with big class, and we put $V=V_\theta $ and $\Omega =\int _ X\omega ^ n$; volumes of Borel sets are taken with respect to $\omega ^ n$. No model potential is fixed in this section.

For a potential $u$ of positive mass, theorem 16.4.7 describes $P_\theta [u]$ as the largest potential $v\leq 0$ with $u\preceq v$ and $\int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle $, and it shows that $P_\theta [P_\theta [u]]=P_\theta [u]$. Its proof relies on the domination principle, which requires positive mass. For arbitrary $u$ we replace the mass by the collection of all mixed masses of $u$ against $V$; this leads to the singularity ceiling of definition 17.5.2. We prove that the ceiling equals $P_\theta [u]$ and that $P_\theta [\cdot ]$ is idempotent, without any assumption on the mass of $u$.

The main step, proposition 17.5.1, perturbs $u$ to $w_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )u+\varepsilon V$, which has positive mass $m_\varepsilon $, and shows that $P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]$ decreases to $P_\theta [u]$. Since $m_\varepsilon $ tends to zero when $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =0$, the estimates must not deteriorate as the mass degenerates. We therefore use a tail bound normalized by $m_\varepsilon $, which is uniform in $\varepsilon $, and convert it into a volume bound by means of constant-density solutions of Monge–Ampère equations.

Proposition 17.5.1. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\leq 0$. For $0<\varepsilon <1$ put

\[ w_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )u+\varepsilon V, \qquad \phi _\varepsilon =P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]. \]

Then $\phi _\varepsilon $ decreases to $P_\theta [u]$, pointwise and in $L^1(X)$, as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$.

Proof. The envelopes $\phi _\varepsilon $ decrease to a $\theta $-psh limit $q\geq P_\theta [u]$; the point is the reverse inequality, which we obtain by showing that $\{ q>P_\theta [u]+\delta \} $ has arbitrarily small volume for every $\delta >0$.

Step 1: the limit exists. Put $\phi =P_\theta [u]$. Since $u\leq 0$, we have $u\leq V$, so $w_\varepsilon =u+\varepsilon (V-u)$ decreases to $u$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ (at the poles of $u$ all these functions equal $-\infty $). In particular $u\leq w_\varepsilon \leq 0$. By the monotonicity of $P_\theta [\cdot ]$ in proposition 16.3.2(1) and the bound $P_\theta [\cdot ]\leq V$ of definition 16.3.1, we get $\phi \leq \phi _\varepsilon \leq V$, and $\phi _\varepsilon $ decreases as $\varepsilon $ decreases. Hence $q=\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}\phi _\varepsilon $ exists pointwise; as a decreasing limit of $\theta $-psh potentials bounded below by $\phi $, it is $\theta $-psh (corollary 14.2.5), and $\phi \leq q\leq V$. Since $\phi $ and $V$ are integrable, dominated convergence gives $\phi _\varepsilon \to q$ in $L^1(X)$. It remains to prove that $q\leq \phi $.

Step 2: masses and a normalized tail bound. For $0\leq k\leq n$ consider the finite non-pluripolar measures

\[ \nu _ k=\langle \theta _ u^ k\wedge \theta _ V^{n-k}\rangle , \qquad a_ k=\nu _ k(X). \]

Since $\theta _{w_\varepsilon }=(1-\varepsilon )\theta _ u+\varepsilon \theta _ V$, multilinearity (proposition 15.1.5) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \mu _{w_\varepsilon } & =\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(1-\varepsilon )^ k\varepsilon ^{n-k}\nu _ k, \\ m_\varepsilon :=\int _ X\langle \theta _{w_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle & =\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(1-\varepsilon )^ k\varepsilon ^{n-k}a_ k. \end{aligned} \]

Here $a_0=\int _ X\langle \theta _ V^ n\rangle >0$ by proposition 15.8.2, so $m_\varepsilon \geq \varepsilon ^ na_0>0$. By the mass preservation in proposition 16.3.2(3) and by theorem 16.4.7, $\phi _\varepsilon $ is a model potential of mass $m_\varepsilon $.

In the rest of the proof, sets defined by inequalities between potentials, such as $\{ u<V-A\} $, are understood pointwise, with values in $[-\infty ,+\infty )$. The pole sets of all potentials involved are pluripolar, hence null for all the non-pluripolar measures below and for $\omega ^ n$. For $A>0$ put

\[ r(A)=\max _{k:\, a_ k>0}\frac{\nu _ k(\{ u<V-A\} )}{a_ k}. \]

The maximum is over a finite set which contains $k=0$. As $A\to \infty $, the sets $\{ u<V-A\} $ decrease to a subset of the pole set of $u$, which is $\nu _ k$-null; since the measures $\nu _ k$ are finite, continuity from above gives $r(A)\downarrow 0$. Moreover $w_\varepsilon -V=(1-\varepsilon )(u-V)$, so $\{ w_\varepsilon <V-A\} \subset \{ u<V-A\} $. Using the expansion of $\mu _{w_\varepsilon }$ and the bound $\nu _ k(\{ u<V-A\} )\leq r(A)a_ k$, valid for every $k$ (when $a_ k=0$ both sides vanish), we obtain the estimate

17.46
\begin{equation} \frac{\mu _{w_\varepsilon }(\{ w_\varepsilon <V-A\} )}{m_\varepsilon } \leq r(A),\qquad 0<\varepsilon <1, \end{equation}

which is uniform in $\varepsilon $.

Step 3: rooftops. For fixed $A>0$ put

\[ p_{\varepsilon ,A}=P_\theta (w_\varepsilon +A,V), \qquad p_ A=P_\theta (u+A,V). \]

The potential $w_\varepsilon $ is a common minorant of $w_\varepsilon +A$ and $V$, so $w_\varepsilon \leq p_{\varepsilon ,A}$. On the other hand $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\leq V\leq 0$ and $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\leq w_\varepsilon +A$, so $p_{\varepsilon ,A}$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]$. Hence $w_\varepsilon \leq p_{\varepsilon ,A}\leq \phi _\varepsilon $, and since $w_\varepsilon $ and $\phi _\varepsilon $ both have mass $m_\varepsilon $, the monotonicity of mass (theorem 15.7.4) shows that $p_{\varepsilon ,A}$ has mass $m_\varepsilon $ as well; thus $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$. As $w_\varepsilon +A$ decreases to $u+A$, proposition 16.1.6 gives $p_{\varepsilon ,A}\downarrow p_ A$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Likewise $u\leq p_ A\leq V\leq 0$ and $p_ A\leq u+A$, so $p_ A$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [u]$, and $p_ A\leq \phi $.

Step 4: auxiliary solutions. Apply theorem 17.4.5 with the model potential $\phi _\varepsilon $ of mass $m_\varepsilon $ in place of $\phi $, the volume form $\omega ^ n$ and the constant density $m_\varepsilon /\Omega $. It gives $\chi _\varepsilon \in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$ with

\[ \mu _{\chi _\varepsilon }=\frac{m_\varepsilon }{\Omega }\, \omega ^ n, \qquad \sup _ X\chi _\varepsilon =0. \]

By lemma 16.4.2, $\chi _\varepsilon \leq \phi _\varepsilon $. By proposition 14.2.1, normalized $\theta $-psh potentials are uniformly bounded in $L^1$, so there is a constant $C_1$, independent of $\varepsilon $, with $\int _ X(-\chi _\varepsilon )\, \omega ^ n\leq C_1$.

Step 5: a volume bound uniform in $\varepsilon $. Fix $\delta >0$ and $B>1$, put $\tau =\min \{ 1/2,\delta /(4B)\} $, and define

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{\varepsilon ,B}& =\{ \chi _\varepsilon >\phi _\varepsilon -B\} ,\\ \psi _\varepsilon & =(1-\tau )\phi _\varepsilon +\tau \chi _\varepsilon -\delta /2,\\ E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }& =\{ \phi _\varepsilon >p_{\varepsilon ,A}+\delta \} . \end{aligned} \]

Since $\phi _\varepsilon \leq 0$, the complement of $G_{\varepsilon ,B}$ lies in $\{ \chi _\varepsilon \leq -B\} $, whose volume is at most $C_1/B$ by Markov's inequality. Moreover,

\[ \chi _\varepsilon -\delta /2\leq \psi _\varepsilon \leq \phi _\varepsilon -\delta /2, \]

and since $\chi _\varepsilon $ and $\phi _\varepsilon $ have the same mass, the monotonicity of mass places $\psi _\varepsilon $ in $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$. On $G_{\varepsilon ,B}$ we have $\psi _\varepsilon -\phi _\varepsilon =\tau (\chi _\varepsilon -\phi _\varepsilon )-\delta /2>-\tau B-\delta /2\geq -3\delta /4$, whereas $p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\phi _\varepsilon -\delta $ on $E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }$. Hence

\[ E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }\cap G_{\varepsilon ,B}\subset \{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} . \]

By multilinearity, $\mu _{\psi _\varepsilon }\geq \tau ^ n\mu _{\chi _\varepsilon }=\tau ^ n(m_\varepsilon /\Omega )\omega ^ n$. As $p_{\varepsilon ,A}$ and $\psi _\varepsilon $ both belong to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi _\varepsilon )$, the comparison principle of proposition 16.7.4 applies to them, and we obtain

\[ \tau ^ n\frac{m_\varepsilon }{\Omega } \int _{E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }\cap G_{\varepsilon ,B}}\omega ^ n \leq \int _{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} }\mu _{\psi _\varepsilon } \leq \int _{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} }\mu _{p_{\varepsilon ,A}}. \]

To estimate the right side, we use the contact inequality for rooftops, corollary 16.2.6:

\[ \mu _{p_{\varepsilon ,A}} \leq \mathbf1_{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}=w_\varepsilon +A\} }\mu _{w_\varepsilon } +\mathbf1_{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}=V\} }\mu _ V. \]

The second contact set does not meet $\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} $: at a common point we would have $V<\psi _\varepsilon \leq \phi _\varepsilon -\delta /2\leq V-\delta /2$. At a point of $\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} $ in the first contact set, $w_\varepsilon +A<\psi _\varepsilon \leq V-\delta /2$, hence $w_\varepsilon <V-A$. Therefore, by (17.46),

\[ \int _{\{ p_{\varepsilon ,A}<\psi _\varepsilon \} }\mu _{p_{\varepsilon ,A}} \leq \mu _{w_\varepsilon }(\{ w_\varepsilon <V-A\} )\leq m_\varepsilon r(A). \]

Dividing by the positive number $\tau ^ nm_\varepsilon /\Omega $ and adding the volume of the complement of $G_{\varepsilon ,B}$, we arrive at

17.50
\begin{equation} \int _{E_{\varepsilon ,A,\delta }}\omega ^ n \leq \frac{C_1}{B}+\Omega \tau ^{-n}r(A). \end{equation}

Step 6: passage to the limit. Take a sequence $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$. If $q(x)>p_ A(x)+\delta $, then $\phi _{\varepsilon _ j}(x)\geq q(x)$ and $p_{\varepsilon _ j,A}(x)\downarrow p_ A(x)$, so $x\in E_{\varepsilon _ j,A,\delta }$ for all large $j$. Thus

\[ \{ q>p_ A+\delta \} \subset \varliminf _ j E_{\varepsilon _ j,A,\delta }, \]

and Fatou's lemma together with (17.50) shows that the volume of $\{ q>p_ A+\delta \} $ is at most $C_1/B+\Omega \tau ^{-n}r(A)$. Since $p_ A\leq \phi $, the same bound holds for the set $\{ q>\phi +\delta \} $, which depends neither on $A$ nor on $B$, whereas $\tau $ depends on $B$ and $\delta $ but not on $A$. Letting first $A\to \infty $, so that $r(A)\to 0$, and then $B\to \infty $, we see that $\{ q>\phi +\delta \} $ has zero volume. As $\delta >0$ was arbitrary, $q\leq \phi $ almost everywhere, and hence everywhere, because both are $\theta $-psh (see the beginning of section 16.1). Together with $\phi \leq q$, this gives $q=\phi $. $\square$

Definition 17.5.2. For $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ let $\mathcal F_ u$ be the set of potentials $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that $v\leq 0$, $u\preceq v$ and

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _ v^ k\wedge \theta _ V^{n-k}\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ k\wedge \theta _ V^{n-k}\rangle \qquad \text{for }0\leq k\leq n. \]

The singularity ceiling of $u$ is the function $(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*$.

The family $\mathcal F_ u$ contains $u-\sup _ Xu$ and is bounded above by zero, so the singularity ceiling $(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*$ is a $\theta $-psh potential. Both the singularity ceiling of $u$ and $P_\theta [u]$ are unchanged when a constant $c$ is added to $u$. Indeed, $\theta _{u+c}=\theta _ u$, and a potential $v$ satisfies $u+c\preceq v$ if and only if $u\preceq v$; hence $\mathcal F_{u+c}=\mathcal F_ u$, and $P_\theta [u+c]$ and $P_\theta [u]$ have the same competitors.

Theorem 17.5.3. Let $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ with big class, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then the singularity ceiling of $u$ is its singularity envelope, that is,

\[ \Big(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v\Big)^*=P_\theta [u], \]

and $P_\theta [P_\theta [u]]=P_\theta [u]$. In particular, both identities hold when $\int _ X\langle \theta _ u^ n\rangle =0$.

Proof. By the remark following definition 17.5.2, both identities are unchanged when a constant is added to $u$, so we may assume $u\leq 0$. Put $\phi =P_\theta [u]$.

We first show that $\phi $ lies below the singularity ceiling of $u$. By definition 16.3.1, $\phi \leq 0$, and $u\leq \phi $ because $u$ is itself a competitor. Applying the mixed mass preservation of proposition 16.3.2(3) to the tuple consisting of $k$ copies of $u$ and $n-k$ copies of $V$, and using $P_\theta [V]=V$ (proposition 16.3.2(4)), we see that $\phi $ has the same mixed masses against $V$ as $u$. Hence $\phi \in \mathcal F_ u$, and therefore $\phi \leq (\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*$.

Conversely, let $v\in \mathcal F_ u$, and for $0<\varepsilon <1$ put

\[ w_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )u+\varepsilon V, \qquad z_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )v+\varepsilon V. \]

Expanding by multilinearity and using the mixed mass identities that define $\mathcal F_ u$, we get

\[ \int _ X\langle \theta _{w_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle =\int _ X\langle \theta _{z_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle \geq \varepsilon ^ n\int _ X\langle \theta _ V^ n\rangle >0. \]

Moreover $w_\varepsilon \preceq z_\varepsilon $, since $u\preceq v$. Let $\zeta _\varepsilon =P_\theta [z_\varepsilon ]$. By theorem 16.4.7, $\zeta _\varepsilon $ is a model potential with the same mass as $z_\varepsilon $, hence as $w_\varepsilon $. As $w_\varepsilon \preceq z_\varepsilon \leq \zeta _\varepsilon $, the potential $w_\varepsilon $ belongs to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\zeta _\varepsilon )$, and proposition 16.4.10 gives

\[ P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]=P_\theta [\zeta _\varepsilon ]=\zeta _\varepsilon =P_\theta [z_\varepsilon ]. \]

Now let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Proposition 17.5.1, applied to $u$ and to $v$, gives $P_\theta [u]=P_\theta [v]$. Since $v\leq 0$, the potential $v$ is itself a competitor for $P_\theta [v]$, so $v\leq P_\theta [v]=\phi $. Taking the supremum over $v\in \mathcal F_ u$ and its upper semicontinuous regularization, we obtain $(\sup _{v\in \mathcal F_ u}v)^*\leq \phi $.

For idempotence, let $\phi _\varepsilon =P_\theta [w_\varepsilon ]$ be as in proposition 17.5.1. Since $\phi \leq \phi _\varepsilon $, the monotonicity of $P_\theta [\cdot ]$ and theorem 16.4.7, which applies to $w_\varepsilon $ because $\int _ X\langle \theta _{w_\varepsilon }^ n\rangle >0$, give

\[ P_\theta [\phi ]\leq P_\theta [\phi _\varepsilon ] =\phi _\varepsilon . \]

Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and using the proposition, we obtain $P_\theta [\phi ]\leq \phi $. Conversely, $\phi \leq 0$ is a competitor for $P_\theta [\phi ]$, so $\phi \leq P_\theta [\phi ]$. This proves idempotence. $\square$

17.6 Subgeodesics and Perron segments

We return to a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ with big class and a model potential $\phi $ of mass $m$. Let $S=\{ \zeta \in \mathbb C:0<\operatorname{Re}\zeta <1\} $, and let $p\colon X\times S\to X$ be the projection. A family $(u_ t)_{0<t<1}$ of functions on $X$ defines the function $(x,\zeta )\mapsto u_{\operatorname{Re}\zeta }(x)$ on $X\times S$, which is invariant under imaginary translations in the sense of section 8.5, and we ask that this function be $p^*\theta $-psh. This is a joint condition in $x$ and $\zeta $. It forces $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ to be convex (lemma 17.6.3 below), but it is stronger than the convexity of these functions together with the $\theta $-plurisubharmonicity of the slices $u_ t$.

For instance, let $\theta $ be a Kähler form, and let $g$ be a nonconstant smooth function with $\theta +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\geq 0$ for $0\leq t\leq 1$ (any nonconstant smooth function multiplied by a small positive constant). The family $u_ t=tg$ has $\theta $-psh slices and affine functions $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$, but $(x,\zeta )\mapsto \operatorname{Re}(\zeta )g(x)$ is not $p^*\theta $-psh near a point where $\mathrm dg\neq 0$. Indeed, in local coordinates $(z_1,\ldots ,z_ n,\zeta )$, the complex Hessian of $\operatorname{Re}(\zeta )g(z)+h(z)$, where $h$ is a smooth local potential of $\theta $, has vanishing $\zeta \bar\zeta $-entry and mixed entries $\partial ^2/\partial z_ j\partial \bar\zeta =\frac12\partial g/\partial z_ j$, which do not all vanish because $g$ is real and $\mathrm dg\neq 0$. A positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix with a vanishing diagonal entry has vanishing entries in the corresponding row and column, as its $2\times 2$ principal minors show; so this Hessian is not positive semidefinite, and the smooth function $\operatorname{Re}(\zeta )g(z)+h(z)$ is not plurisubharmonic on any neighborhood of the point, by proposition 8.2.2(1).

Definition 17.6.1. Let $u_0,u_1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. A subgeodesic with endpoints $u_0,u_1$ is a family $(u_ t)_{0<t<1}$ of functions on $X$ whose complexification

\[ U(x,\zeta )=u_{\operatorname{Re}\zeta }(x) \]

is $p^*\theta $-psh on $X\times S$ and such that $u_ t\to u_0$ and $u_ t\to u_1$ in $L^1(X)$ as $t\to 0$ and $t\to 1$, respectively.

Subgeodesics on an interval $[a,b]$ with $a<b$ are defined in the same way, after the affine change of parameter $t\mapsto a+(b-a)t$; a subgeodesic on a one-point interval consists of its given potential. The endpoints of a subgeodesic are uniquely determined, because a class in $L^1(X)$ has at most one $\theta $-psh representative. The holomorphic covering $z=\mathrm e^{-\zeta }$ maps $S$ onto the annulus $\{ \mathrm e^{-1}<|z|<1\} $, and $U$ descends to the function $(x,z)\mapsto u_{-\log |z|}(x)$ on $X\times \{ \mathrm e^{-1}<|z|<1\} $, because $U$ is invariant under imaginary translations. The map $(x,\zeta )\mapsto (x,\mathrm e^{-\zeta })$ is a local biholomorphism, and plurisubharmonicity is a local property invariant under biholomorphisms (proposition 8.2.3). Hence $U$ is $p^*\theta $-psh if and only if this annulus complexification is psh relative to the pullback of $\theta $; we use both descriptions.

We need some elementary facts about convex functions of one real variable, allowing the value $-\infty $.

Lemma 17.6.2. Let $I\subset \mathbb R$ be an open interval, and let $g\colon I\to [-\infty ,\infty )$ be convex, that is, $g((1-\lambda )a+\lambda b)\leq (1-\lambda )g(a)+\lambda g(b)$ for $a,b\in I$ and $0<\lambda <1$.

  1. Either $g\equiv -\infty $, or $g$ is finite and continuous on $I$.

  2. Suppose that $g$ is finite, and for $a<b$ in $I$ put $\sigma (a,b)=(g(b)-g(a))/(b-a)$. Then $\sigma (a,b)$ is nondecreasing in $a$ and in $b$. For every $t\in I$ there is $c\in \mathbb R$ with $g(s)\geq g(t)+c(s-t)$ for all $s\in I$. Consequently, $g$ is the pointwise maximum of the affine functions that it dominates, and it is bounded below on every bounded subinterval of $I$.

  3. Let $I=(0,1)$, and suppose that $g$ is finite and bounded above on $(0,\frac12)$. Then $\lim _{t\downarrow 0}g(t)$ exists and is finite. Moreover, for fixed $s\in (0,1)$, the value $g(s)-s\sigma (t,s)$ at $0$ of the affine function that agrees with $g$ at $t$ and $s$ increases as $t\in (0,s)$ decreases, and it tends to $\lim _{t\downarrow 0}g(t)$ as $t\downarrow 0$.

  4. Let $I=(0,1)$, suppose that $|g|\leq C$ on $I$, and let $0<\delta <\frac12$. Then $|g(b)-g(a)|\leq (2C/\delta )|b-a|$ for all $a,b\in [\delta ,1-\delta ]$.

Proof. (1) Suppose that $g(t_0)=-\infty $ for some $t_0\in I$, and let $t\in I\setminus \{ t_0\} $. Choose $s\in I$ such that $t$ lies strictly between $t_0$ and $s$, and write $t=(1-\lambda )t_0+\lambda s$ with $0<\lambda <1$. Since $g(s)<\infty $, convexity gives $g(t)\leq (1-\lambda )g(t_0)+\lambda g(s)=-\infty $. Hence either $g\equiv -\infty $ or $g$ is finite, and a finite convex function on $I$ is continuous by example 3.2.4.

(2) For $a<b<c$ in $I$ we have $\sigma (a,b)\leq \sigma (a,c)\leq \sigma (b,c)$, as shown in example 3.2.4; this gives the monotonicity of $\sigma $. Now let $t\in I$. For $r<t<r'$ in $I$ we have $\sigma (r,t)\leq \sigma (t,r')$, so $c=\sup _{r<t}\sigma (r,t)$ is finite and $c\leq \sigma (t,r')$ for every $r'>t$. These two inequalities say that $g(s)\geq g(t)+c(s-t)$ for $s<t$ and for $s>t$. The affine function $s\mapsto g(t)+c(s-t)$ is therefore dominated by $g$ and agrees with $g$ at $t$, which proves the remaining assertions.

(3) Fix $s\in (0,1)$. For $0<t<s$ we have $g(t)=g(s)-(s-t)\sigma (t,s)$. By (2), $\sigma (t,s)$ decreases as $t$ decreases, so it has a limit $\sigma _0\in [-\infty ,\infty )$ as $t\downarrow 0$. If $\sigma _0=-\infty $, then $g(t)\to +\infty $ as $t\downarrow 0$, which contradicts the upper bound. Hence $\sigma _0$ is finite and $g(t)\to g(s)-s\sigma _0$. The value at $0$ of the affine function that agrees with $g$ at $t$ and $s$ is $g(s)-s\sigma (t,s)$; it increases as $t$ decreases and tends to $g(s)-s\sigma _0$.

(4) Let $\delta \leq a<b\leq 1-\delta $. For $b<r<1$, (2) gives $\sigma (a,b)\leq \sigma (b,r)\leq 2C/(r-b)$, and letting $r\uparrow 1$ we get $\sigma (a,b)\leq 2C/(1-b)\leq 2C/\delta $. Similarly, for $0<r<a$ we have $\sigma (a,b)\geq \sigma (r,a)\geq -2C/(a-r)$, and letting $r\downarrow 0$ we get $\sigma (a,b)\geq -2C/a\geq -2C/\delta $. $\square$

Lemma 17.6.3. Let $(v_ t)_{0<t<1}$ be a family of functions on $X$ whose complexification $V(x,\zeta )=v_{\operatorname{Re}\zeta }(x)$ is $p^*\theta $-psh on $X\times S$. Then:

  1. for every $x\in X$, the function $t\mapsto v_ t(x)$ is convex on $(0,1)$, and it is either identically $-\infty $ or finite and continuous;

  2. for almost every $x\in X$, this function is finite;

  3. every slice $v_ t$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. Let $D\subset X$ be a connected coordinate chart, identified with a domain in $\mathbb C^ n$, on which $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ for a smooth function $h$; by lemma 13.1.2, such charts, for instance small coordinate balls, cover $X$. Then $V+h\circ p$ is plurisubharmonic on the domain $D\times S\subset \mathbb C^{n+1}$.

(1) Fix $x\in D$. The complex line $\{ x\} \times \mathbb C$ meets $D\times S$ in $\{ x\} \times S$, so by definition 8.1.1 the function $\zeta \mapsto V(x,\zeta )+h(x)$ is subharmonic on $S$, possibly identically $-\infty $; hence so is $w(\zeta )=g(\operatorname{Re}\zeta )$, where $g(t)=v_ t(x)$. If $w\equiv -\infty $, then $g\equiv -\infty $ is convex. Otherwise $w$ is locally integrable on $S$ by proposition 3.1.5, and $\Delta w\geq 0$ by theorem 3.2.2. For $0<\delta <\frac12$, the mollification $w_\delta =w*\rho _\delta $ is smooth on $S_\delta =\{ \delta <\operatorname{Re}\zeta <1-\delta \} $, and $\Delta w_\delta =(\Delta w)_\delta \geq 0$ there, by proposition 1.4.3(1),(3). Since $w$ is invariant under imaginary translations, so is $w_\delta $; thus $w_\delta (\zeta )=g_\delta (\operatorname{Re}\zeta )$ for the smooth function $g_\delta (t)=w_\delta (t)$ on $(\delta ,1-\delta )$, and $g_\delta ''=\Delta w_\delta \geq 0$, so $g_\delta $ is convex by the mean value theorem. By (3.3), $w_\delta $ decreases to $w$ as $\delta \downarrow 0$, so $g_\delta $ decreases to $g$ on $(0,1)$. For $a,b\in (0,1)$ and $0<\lambda <1$, letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ in

\[ g((1-\lambda )a+\lambda b)\leq g_\delta ((1-\lambda )a+\lambda b)\leq (1-\lambda )g_\delta (a)+\lambda g_\delta (b) \]

shows that $g$ is convex. The rest of (1) is lemma 17.6.2(1).

(2) The plurisubharmonic function $V+h\circ p$ is locally integrable on $D\times S$ (theorem 8.2.1). Let $K\subset D$ be compact and $[a,b]\subset (0,1)$. Since $V$ does not depend on $\operatorname{Im}\zeta $, Tonelli's theorem on $K\times \{ \zeta :a\leq \operatorname{Re}\zeta \leq b,\ 0\leq \operatorname{Im}\zeta \leq 1\} $ gives

\[ \int _ K\int _ a^ b|v_ t(x)+h(x)|\, \mathrm dt\, \mathrm dV_{2n}(x)<\infty . \]

Hence, for almost every $x\in K$, the value $v_ t(x)$ is finite for almost every $t\in [a,b]$, and by (1) the function $t\mapsto v_ t(x)$ is then finite on $(0,1)$. Since $X$ is covered by countably many such compact sets $K$, this proves (2).

(3) Fix $t\in (0,1)$. The restriction of $V+h\circ p$ to $D\times \{ t\} $ is the function $v_ t+h$ on $D$. It is upper semicontinuous, and, by definition 8.1.1 applied to the complex lines contained in $\mathbb C^ n\times \{ t\} $, its restriction to every connected component of the intersection of $D$ with a complex line is subharmonic or identically $-\infty $. By (2), $v_ t$ is finite almost everywhere on $D$, so $v_ t+h$ is not identically $-\infty $, and $v_ t+h\in \mathrm{PSH}(D)$. Since such charts cover $X$, $v_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. $\square$

Proposition 17.6.4. Let $(u_ t)$ be a subgeodesic with endpoints $u_0,u_1$. Then for each $x\in X$ the function $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ is convex on $(0,1)$, possibly identically $-\infty $, and

\[ u_ t\leq (1-t)u_0+tu_1,\qquad 0<t<1. \]

The infimum $w=\inf _{0<t<1}u_ t$ is a $\theta $-psh potential and satisfies $w\leq P_\theta (u_0,u_1)$. The map $t\mapsto u_ t$ is continuous from $[0,1]$ to $L^1(X)$, and its image is compact. For $j=0,1$, the limits $\lim _{t\to j}u_ t(x)$ exist at every $x$, are at most $u_ j(x)$, and equal $u_ j(x)$ quasi-everywhere.

Proof. By lemma 17.6.3, for every $x\in X$ the function $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ is convex on $(0,1)$, and either identically $-\infty $ or finite and continuous; it is finite for almost every $x$; and every slice $u_ t$ is $\theta $-psh. Since a sequence converging in $L^1(X)$ has a subsequence converging almost everywhere, we can choose sequences $t_ j\downarrow 0$ and $s_ j\uparrow 1$ with $u_{t_ j}\to u_0$ and $u_{s_ j}\to u_1$ almost everywhere. For fixed $t$ and large $j$, convexity on $[t_ j,s_ j]$ gives

\[ u_ t\leq \frac{s_ j-t}{s_ j-t_ j}u_{t_ j}+\frac{t-t_ j}{s_ j-t_ j}u_{s_ j}, \]

and letting $j\to \infty $ gives $u_ t\leq (1-t)u_0+tu_1$ almost everywhere. Both sides are $\theta $-psh, so the inequality holds everywhere.

Let $D$ be a connected coordinate chart of $X$ on which $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ with $h$ smooth. The function $U+h\circ p$ is plurisubharmonic on $D\times S=D\times ((0,1)+\mathrm i\mathbb R)$ and invariant under imaginary translations, so corollary 8.5.2, with $V=(0,1)$, shows that $w+h=\inf _{0<t<1}(u_ t+h)$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $ on $D$. For almost every $x$, the function $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ is finite and convex, hence bounded below on $(0,1)$ by lemma 17.6.2(2). Thus $w$ is finite almost everywhere, the alternative $-\infty $ is excluded on every chart, and $w$ is $\theta $-psh. Since $w\leq u_ t$ for all $t$ and $u_ t\to u_0$, $u_ t\to u_1$ in $L^1(X)$, we get $w\leq u_0$ and $w\leq u_1$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere. Thus $w$ is a competitor for the obstacle $\min \{ u_0,u_1\} $, and $w\leq P_\theta (u_0,u_1)$.

The bounds $w\leq u_ t\leq \max \{ u_0,u_1\} $ hold for all $t$, with integrable outer functions. For almost every $x$ the function $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ is finite and continuous on $(0,1)$, so dominated convergence gives continuity of $t\mapsto u_ t$ from $(0,1)$ to $L^1(X)$; continuity at the endpoints is part of the definition. The image of $[0,1]$ under a continuous map is compact. Now fix $x\in X$. If $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ is identically $-\infty $, its limits at $0$ and $1$ are $-\infty $. Otherwise it is finite and convex and bounded above by $\max \{ u_0(x),u_1(x)\} <\infty $, so by lemma 17.6.2(3), applied to $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ and to $t\mapsto u_{1-t}(x)$, it has finite limits at $0$ and $1$. By the displayed bound these limits are at most $u_0(x)$ and $u_1(x)$. Since the pointwise limits exist everywhere and $u_{t_ j}\to u_0$, $u_{s_ j}\to u_1$ almost everywhere, they equal $u_0$ and $u_1$ almost everywhere.

It remains to prove equality quasi-everywhere; we treat the endpoint $0$, and the endpoint $1$ follows by reversing the parameter. Fix $s\in (0,1)$ and a sequence $t_ j\downarrow 0$ with $t_ j<s/2$. Consider the value at $t=0$ of the chord of $t\mapsto u_ t$ through the parameters $t_ j$ and $s$:

\[ a_ j=\frac{u_{t_ j}-(t_ j/s)u_ s}{1-t_ j/s}. \]

Where $u_ s$ is finite, the function $t\mapsto u_ t(x)$ is finite and convex, so by lemma 17.6.2(3) $a_ j$ increases with $j$ and tends to $\lim _{t\to 0}u_ t$; this limit equals $u_0$ almost everywhere and is at most $u_0$ everywhere. The functions

\[ a_ j+u_ s=\frac{u_{t_ j}+(1-2t_ j/s)u_ s}{1-t_ j/s} \]

are combinations of $u_{t_ j}$ and $u_ s$ with positive coefficients of sum two, hence $2\theta $-psh, since $2\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha u_{t_ j}+\beta u_ s)=\alpha \theta _{u_{t_ j}}+\beta \theta _{u_ s}$ for $\alpha ,\beta \geq 0$ with $\alpha +\beta =2$. They increase with $j$ and are bounded above by $u_0+u_ s$. Their pointwise limit is $\lim _{t\to 0}u_ t+u_ s$, which equals $u_0+u_ s$ almost everywhere; hence their regularized upper envelope, a $2\theta $-psh function by theorem 8.3.2 which agrees almost everywhere with the pointwise limit, is $u_0+u_ s$ itself. By corollary 10.2.2(2), applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $2\theta $, the pointwise limit $\lim _{t\to 0}u_ t+u_ s$ equals $u_0+u_ s$ quasi-everywhere. Removing the pole set of $u_ s$ proves the assertion at $0$. $\square$

Lemma 17.6.5. Subgeodesics are preserved by restriction to a subinterval, by addition of an affine function of $t$, and by a change of background $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$, $u'_ t=u_ t-g$, with $g$ smooth. If a subgeodesic on $[a,b]\subset [0,1]$ dominates a given subgeodesic on $[0,1]$ there and has the same values at $a$ and $b$, then replacing the given subgeodesic on $[a,b]$ by it gives a subgeodesic on $[0,1]$.

Proof. Let $0\leq a<b\leq 1$. The restriction of $(u_ t)$ to $[a,b]$, reparametrized as $t\mapsto u_{a+(b-a)t}$, has the complexification $U\circ F$, where $F(x,\zeta )=(x,a+(b-a)\zeta )$ is a holomorphic map of $X\times S$ into itself with $p\circ F=p$. In a chart $D$ of $X$ on which $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$, we have $(U+h\circ p)\circ F=U\circ F+h\circ p$, which is plurisubharmonic on $D\times S$ by proposition 8.2.3; it is not identically $-\infty $, because its slices are the functions $u_{a+(b-a)t}+h$, which are not identically $-\infty $ by lemma 17.6.3. Hence $U\circ F$ is $p^*\theta $-psh, and the restriction is a subgeodesic with endpoint potentials $u_ a$ and $u_ b$, by the $L^1$ continuity of proposition 17.6.4. An affine function $c_0+c_1\operatorname{Re}\zeta =\operatorname{Re}(c_0+c_1\zeta )$ is pluriharmonic, so adding it preserves $p^*\theta $-plurisubharmonicity, and the change of background replaces $U$ by $U-g\circ p$ and $p^*\theta $ by $p^*\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(g\circ p)$; this proves the first assertion.

For the replacement, let $U$ be the complexification of the given subgeodesic, and $V$ that of the new one on $W=X\times \{ a<\operatorname{Re}\zeta <b\} $. We verify the boundary condition of proposition 8.2.5 along each seam $\operatorname{Re}\zeta =a$ or $\operatorname{Re}\zeta =b$ lying in $S$; consider $a>0$. Let $(x_ k,\zeta _ k)\in W$ converge to $(x,\zeta )$ with $\operatorname{Re}\zeta =a$, and put $t_ k=\operatorname{Re}\zeta _ k$. Denote the slices of the new subgeodesic by $v_ t$, $a<t<b$; they converge to $u_ a$ in $L^1(X)$ as $t\downarrow a$.

We show that $\varlimsup _ kV(x_ k,\zeta _ k)\leq U(x,\zeta )=u_ a(x)$. Since $v_{t_ k}\to u_ a$ in $L^1(X)$, lemma 17.3.2 shows that the tail envelopes $w_ l=(\sup _{k\geq l}v_{t_ k})^*$ are $\theta $-psh and decrease pointwise to $u_ a$. For $k\geq l$ we have $V(x_ k,\zeta _ k)=v_{t_ k}(x_ k)\leq w_ l(x_ k)$, and $w_ l$ is upper semicontinuous, so $\varlimsup _ kV(x_ k,\zeta _ k)\leq w_ l(x)$ for every $l$. Letting $l\to \infty $ proves the claim. A seam $\operatorname{Re}\zeta =b$ with $b<1$ is treated in the same way, with $t_ k\uparrow b$. Hence, by proposition 8.2.5 applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $p^*\theta $, the function equal to $\max \{ U,V\} =V$ on $W$ and to $U$ elsewhere is $p^*\theta $-psh. Its endpoint limits at $t=0$ and $t=1$ are those of the given subgeodesic, or those of the new one if $a=0$ or $b=1$; in either case they are $u_0$ and $u_1$. $\square$

Lemma 17.6.6. Let $\{ (u_ t^\alpha )\} _{\alpha \in A}$ be a nonempty family of subgeodesics whose endpoint potentials $u_0^\alpha $, $u_1^\alpha $ are uniformly bounded above. Then the regularized upper envelope $U$ of their complexifications has slices

\[ U_ t=\Bigl (\sup _\alpha u_ t^\alpha \Bigr )^*,\qquad 0<t<1, \]

where the regularization on the right is taken on $X$. Moreover $(U_ t)$ is a subgeodesic whose endpoint potentials are the regularized upper envelopes $v_0=(\sup _\alpha u_0^\alpha )^*$ and $v_1=(\sup _\alpha u_1^\alpha )^*$ of the endpoints.

Proof. Denote by $U^\alpha $ the complexification of $(u_ t^\alpha )$. By proposition 17.6.4, $u_ t^\alpha \leq (1-t)u_0^\alpha +tu_1^\alpha $, so the functions $U^\alpha $ are uniformly bounded above. Their regularized upper envelope $U$ is therefore $p^*\theta $-psh (theorem 8.3.2, applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $p^*\theta $), and it is invariant under imaginary translations, because the family is and these translations are homeomorphisms. Thus $U$ is the complexification of the family of its slices $U_ t$, which by lemma 17.6.3 are $\theta $-psh and have convex fibers $t\mapsto U_ t(x)$.

We next reduce to a countable subfamily. Cover $X$ by finitely many coordinate balls $D_\beta $ on which $\theta $ has smooth potentials $h_\beta $ (lemma 13.1.2). By theorem 8.3.2, applied to the plurisubharmonic functions $U^\alpha +h_\beta \circ p$ on $D_\beta \times S$ and to the functions $u_0^\alpha +h_\beta $ and $u_1^\alpha +h_\beta $ on $D_\beta $, there are countable sets $J_\beta \subset A$ such that, on $D_\beta \times S$ and on $D_\beta $ respectively, the regularized suprema over $\alpha \in J_\beta $ of $U^\alpha $, of $u_0^\alpha $ and of $u_1^\alpha $ equal $U$, $v_0$ and $v_1$. The countable set $J=\bigcup _\beta J_\beta $ then satisfies $(\sup _{\alpha \in J}U^\alpha )^*=U$, $(\sup _{\alpha \in J}u_0^\alpha )^*=v_0$ and $(\sup _{\alpha \in J}u_1^\alpha )^*=v_1$, since on each $D_\beta \times S$, respectively $D_\beta $, these regularized suprema lie between the regularized suprema over $J_\beta $ and over $A$.

Let $v_ t=(\sup _\alpha u_ t^\alpha )^*$; we show that $U_ t=v_ t$. Since $U_ t$ is upper semicontinuous on $X$ and dominates every $u_ t^\alpha $, we have $v_ t\leq U_ t$. For $a<t<b$, the inequality $u_ t^\alpha \leq \frac{b-t}{b-a}u_ a^\alpha +\frac{t-a}{b-a}u_ b^\alpha \leq \frac{b-t}{b-a}v_ a+\frac{t-a}{b-a}v_ b$ and upper semicontinuity of the right side give $v_ t\leq \frac{b-t}{b-a}v_ a+\frac{t-a}{b-a}v_ b$. Thus $t\mapsto v_ t(x)$ is convex for every $x$. In the same way, the bound $u_ t^\alpha \leq (1-t)u_0^\alpha +tu_1^\alpha $ gives $v_ t\leq (1-t)v_0+tv_1$. Fix one member $\alpha _0$ of the family and let $w_0=\inf _ tu_ t^{\alpha _0}$, a $\theta $-psh potential by proposition 17.6.4. Since $w_0\leq v_ t\leq U_ t$ and $U$ is bounded above, all $v_ t(x)$ and $U_ t(x)$ are finite on the set where $w_0$ is finite, which has full measure.

The function $F_ J=\sup _{\alpha \in J}U^\alpha $ is Borel, being a countable supremum of upper semicontinuous functions, and by theorem 8.3.2 it agrees with its regularization $U$ outside a Lebesgue null set. Both $F_ J$ and $U$ are invariant under imaginary translations, so this exceptional set is $\{ (x,\zeta ):(x,\operatorname{Re}\zeta )\in N\} $ for a Borel set $N\subset X\times (0,1)$, which is null by Fubini's theorem. Applying Fubini's theorem to $N$, we find that for almost every $x$ we have $U_ t(x)=\sup _{\alpha \in J}u_ t^\alpha (x)\leq v_ t(x)$, hence $U_ t(x)=v_ t(x)$, for almost every $t\in (0,1)$. If moreover $w_0(x)$ is finite, both sides are finite convex functions of $t$, hence continuous (lemma 17.6.2(1)), so they agree for every $t\in (0,1)$. Thus, for each $t$, $U_ t=v_ t$ almost everywhere, and both are $\theta $-psh, so $U_ t=v_ t$ everywhere.

For the endpoints, note first that, since $U_ t=v_ t\leq (1-t)v_0+tv_1\leq \max \{ v_0,v_1\} $, lemma 17.6.2(1),(3) show that $\lim _{t\to 0}U_ t(x)$ exists at every $x$, as an element of $[-\infty ,\infty )$. For $\alpha \in J$ we have $U_ t\geq u_ t^\alpha $, so by proposition 17.6.4 $\lim _{t\to 0}U_ t\geq \lim _{t\to 0}u_ t^\alpha =u_0^\alpha $ outside a pluripolar set. Outside a countable union of pluripolar sets this holds for all $\alpha \in J$, so $\lim _{t\to 0}U_ t\geq \sup _{\alpha \in J}u_0^\alpha =v_0$ quasi-everywhere, by corollary 10.2.2(1); countable unions of pluripolar sets are pluripolar by theorem 10.1.4. The bound $U_ t=v_ t\leq (1-t)v_0+tv_1$ gives the reverse inequality. Hence $U_ t\to v_0$ almost everywhere as $t\to 0$. Since $w_0\leq U_ t\leq \max \{ v_0,v_1\} $, dominated convergence gives $U_ t\to v_0$ in $L^1(X)$. The endpoint $t=1$ is treated in the same way, after reversing the parameter. $\square$

Definition 17.6.7. Let $u_0,u_1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The subgeodesics whose endpoint potentials are at most $u_0$ and $u_1$, respectively, are called competitors. The Perron segment between $u_0$ and $u_1$ is the regularized upper envelope of the complexifications of all competitors; it is $-\infty $ if there are no competitors. When its endpoint potentials are $u_0$ and $u_1$, it is called the weak geodesic between $u_0$ and $u_1$. We write its slices as $G_ t(u_0,u_1)$, $0<t<1$, and, for a weak geodesic, put $G_0(u_0,u_1)=u_0$ and $G_1(u_0,u_1)=u_1$.

By lemma 17.6.6, a Perron segment which is not $-\infty $ is a subgeodesic, and its slices are the regularized upper envelopes of the slices of the competitors.

Theorem 17.6.8. If $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then the Perron segment between them is the weak geodesic between $u_0$ and $u_1$, and

\[ P_\theta (u_0,u_1)\leq G_ t(u_0,u_1) \leq (1-t)u_0+tu_1,\qquad 0<t<1. \]

Every slice belongs to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. If $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, every slice belongs to $\mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, and if $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, every slice belongs to $\mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$. The restriction of the weak geodesic to a subinterval $[a,b]\subset [0,1]$ is the weak geodesic between $G_ a(u_0,u_1)$ and $G_ b(u_0,u_1)$.

Proof. The rooftop $w=P_\theta (u_0,u_1)$ belongs to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, by proposition 17.3.6. The constant curve equal to $w$ is a competitor. By lemma 17.6.6, the Perron segment is therefore a subgeodesic, with endpoint potentials $a_0\leq u_0$ and $a_1\leq u_1$, and $w\leq G_ t\leq (1-t)a_0+ta_1\leq (1-t)u_0+tu_1$. Each slice is at most $\phi +C$ for some constant $C$ and at least $w$, so mass monotonicity puts it in $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$.

We show that $a_0=u_0$. For $k\geq 1$ the rooftop $q_ k=P_\theta (u_0,u_1+k)$ is not identically $-\infty $, since it dominates $w$, and the affine curve $q_ k-kt$ is a competitor: its value at $t=0$ is at most $u_0$, and its value at $t=1$ is $q_ k-k\leq u_1$. Hence $G_ t\geq q_ k-kt$ for $0<t<1$, and letting $t\to 0$ in $L^1(X)$ gives $a_0\geq q_ k$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, for every $k$. By the rooftop representation of proposition 16.7.2 and corollary 17.3.7,

\[ \Bigl (\sup _ kq_ k\Bigr )^*=P_\theta [u_1](u_0)=u_0. \]

Since $a_0$ is upper semicontinuous and dominates every $q_ k$, it dominates $(\sup _ kq_ k)^*=u_0$; hence $a_0=u_0$. Exchanging the roles of the endpoints, with the competitors $P_\theta (u_0+k,u_1)-k(1-t)$, gives $a_1=u_1$.

Let $[a,b]\subset [0,1]$. The restriction of the weak geodesic to $[a,b]$ is a subgeodesic between the slices $G_ a,G_ b\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. By the part already proved, the weak geodesic between $G_ a$ and $G_ b$ exists and dominates this restriction. By lemma 17.6.5, replacing the restriction by this weak geodesic gives a subgeodesic on $[0,1]$ with endpoints $u_0,u_1$. It is a competitor for the original Perron segment, so its slices are at most $G_ t$; hence the two coincide on $[a,b]$.

Finally, if $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $w\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by proposition 17.3.6, and $w\leq G_ t\preceq \phi $ gives $G_ t\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$ by corollary 17.2.10. If $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, then $\phi -C$ is a common minorant for some $C$, so $G_ t\geq w\geq \phi -C$, while the convex upper bound gives $G_ t\leq \phi +C$. $\square$

Proposition 17.6.9. Let $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. The weak geodesics of theorem 17.6.8 have the following properties.

  1. They increase with either endpoint: if $u_0',u_1'\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ satisfy $u_0\leq u_0'$ and $u_1\leq u_1'$, then $G_ t(u_0,u_1)\leq G_ t(u_0',u_1')$.

  2. For constants $c_0,c_1$, $G_ t(u_0+c_0,u_1+c_1)=G_ t(u_0,u_1)+(1-t)c_0+tc_1$, and $G_ t(u_1,u_0)=G_{1-t}(u_0,u_1)$.

  3. If $u_0^ k,u_1^ k\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ decrease to $u_0,u_1$ as $k\to \infty $, then $G_ t(u_0^ k,u_1^ k)$ decreases to $G_ t(u_0,u_1)$ for every $t$.

  4. If $u_1\leq u_0$, the slices $G_ t(u_0,u_1)$ decrease with $t\in [0,1]$.

  5. If $|u_1-u_0|\leq C$ quasi-everywhere, then on the common finite locus

    \[ |G_ t(u_0,u_1)-u_0|\leq Ct,\qquad |G_ t(u_0,u_1)-u_1|\leq C(1-t). \]

  6. For a constant $c$, $G_ t(u_0,u_0+c)=u_0+tc$.

Proof. Assertion (1) holds because enlarging the endpoints enlarges the family of competitors, and (2) because adding the affine function $(1-t)c_0+tc_1$, respectively reversing the parameter, is a bijection between the families of competitors.

For (3), the geodesics $G^ k=G(u_0^ k,u_1^ k)$ decrease with $k$ by (1) and dominate $G=G(u_0,u_1)$. Their complexifications decrease to a function which dominates the complexification of $G$ and so is not identically $-\infty $ on any chart; by theorem 8.3.1, applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $p^*\theta $, it is $p^*\theta $-psh. It is invariant under imaginary translations, and its slices $\widetilde G_ t$ are the pointwise limits of $G^ k_ t$. For every $k$ we have $G_ t\leq \widetilde G_ t\leq G^ k_ t\leq (1-t)u_0^ k+tu_1^ k$, so $G_ t\leq \widetilde G_ t\leq (1-t)u_0+tu_1$. As $t\to 0$, both outer functions converge to $u_0$ in $L^1(X)$, hence so does $\widetilde G_ t$; similarly at $t=1$. Thus $(\widetilde G_ t)$ is a competitor for the Perron segment between $u_0$ and $u_1$, so $\widetilde G\leq G$, and therefore $\widetilde G=G$.

For (4), let $u_1\leq u_0$ and $0<r<s<1$, and put $\delta =(s-r)/(1-r)$. The reparametrized curve $t\mapsto G_{\delta +(1-\delta )t}$ is a subgeodesic by lemma 17.6.5. Its value at $t=0$ is $G_\delta \leq (1-\delta )u_0+\delta u_1\leq u_0$, and its value at $t=1$ is $u_1$. It is therefore a competitor for the segment between $u_0$ and $u_1$, and at $t=r$, where $\delta +(1-\delta )r=s$, it gives $G_ s\leq G_ r$. For the endpoints, $G_ t\leq (1-t)u_0+tu_1\leq u_0$, and the constant curve $u_1$ is a competitor, so $G_ t\geq u_1$.

For (5), note that $u_1\leq u_0+C$ and $u_0\leq u_1+C$ everywhere, because inequalities between quasi-psh functions that hold quasi-everywhere hold everywhere (section 16.1). Hence the affine curves $u_0-Ct$ and $u_1-C(1-t)$ are competitors, so that $G_ t\geq \max \{ u_0-Ct,u_1-C(1-t)\} $. On the other hand $G_ t-u_0\leq t(u_1-u_0)\leq Ct$ and $G_ t-u_1\leq (1-t)(u_0-u_1)\leq C(1-t)$ by the upper bound of theorem 17.6.8. For (6), the upper bound $u_0+tc$ is itself an affine competitor. $\square$

Proposition 17.6.10. For $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ and $0<t<1$,

\[ G_ t(u_0,u_1) =\left(\sup _{s\in \mathbb R} \{ P_\theta (u_0,u_1-s)+ts\} \right)^*. \]

The regularization may equally be taken on $X\times S$: the slices of the regularized supremum of the complexifications are the functions displayed here.

Proof. For each $s$ the potential $u_1-s$ belongs to $\mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, so the rooftop $P_\theta (u_0,u_1-s)$ is not identically $-\infty $, by proposition 17.3.6, and the affine curve $P_\theta (u_0,u_1-s)+ts$ is a competitor: its value at $t=0$ is at most $u_0$ and its value at $t=1$ is at most $u_1$. Hence the right side is at most $G_ t$.

Conversely, let $(v_ t)$ be a competitor and $s\in \mathbb R$. By corollary 8.5.2, applied with the affine function $\ell (t)=ts$ in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $p^*\theta $, the function $q_ s=\inf _{0<t<1}(v_ t-ts)$ is $\theta $-psh or identically $-\infty $ on each chart. By lemma 17.6.3, for almost every $x$ the function $t\mapsto v_ t(x)$ is finite and convex, hence bounded below on $(0,1)$ by an affine function (lemma 17.6.2(2)), so $q_ s$ is finite almost everywhere and therefore $\theta $-psh. Letting $t\to 0$ and $t\to 1$ in $q_ s\leq v_ t-ts$, and using the $L^1$ convergence of $v_ t$ to its endpoint potentials, which are at most $u_0$ and $u_1$, we get $q_ s\leq u_0$ and $q_ s\leq u_1-s$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, and therefore $q_ s\leq P_\theta (u_0,u_1-s)$.

Now fix $x$ and $t_0\in (0,1)$. By lemma 17.6.3(1), the fiber $t\mapsto v_ t(x)$ is either identically $-\infty $, in which case $v_{t_0}(x)=-\infty $, or finite. In the second case lemma 17.6.2(2) gives $c\in \mathbb R$ with $v_ t(x)\geq v_{t_0}(x)+c(t-t_0)$ for all $t$, so that $q_ c(x)=\inf _ t(v_ t(x)-ct)=v_{t_0}(x)-ct_0$, and hence $v_{t_0}(x)=q_ c(x)+ct_0\leq \sup _ s(q_ s(x)+t_0s)$. In either case $v_{t_0}(x)\leq \sup _ s\{ P_\theta (u_0,u_1-s)(x)+t_0s\} $, since $q_ s\leq P_\theta (u_0,u_1-s)$. Taking the regularized supremum over all competitors and using lemma 17.6.6 proves the formula. The same lemma, applied to the affine competitors, gives the last assertion. $\square$

17.7 Energy along weak geodesics

We now show that the energy is affine along weak geodesics. The complexification of a weak geodesic lives on $X\times S$, which has one more complex dimension than $X$. We prove first that its local potentials are maximal plurisubharmonic functions, so that its top-degree non-pluripolar measure vanishes. To integrate by parts we then glue the complexification, over a smaller annulus, into a potential on the compact manifold $X\times \mathbb P^1$.

Lemma 17.7.1. Let $h$ be a maximal plurisubharmonic function on a domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ d$, $d\geq 1$, in the sense of definition 9.6.5. Then the top-degree non-pluripolar product $\langle (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^ d\rangle $ is locally finite and vanishes. Here $h$ need not be locally bounded.

Proof. Fix a ball $B\Subset \Omega $ and a concentric ball $B_1$ with $B\Subset B_1\Subset \Omega $. Put $h_ j=\max \{ h,-j\} $ and let $\widehat h_ j$ be the function supplied by corollary 9.6.4 for $h_ j$ and $B$. These are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions with $\widehat h_ j\geq h_ j\geq h$, equal to $h_ j$ outside $B$, with $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widehat h_ j)^ d=0$ on $B$; they decrease with $j$, because $\widehat h_ j$ is increasing in $h_ j$. Their decreasing limit $v$ is plurisubharmonic by theorem 8.3.1, since it dominates $h$, and it equals $h$ outside $B$. Maximality gives $v\leq h$, hence $v=h$.

Fix $C>0$ and $\varepsilon >0$, and put

\[ z_ j=\max \{ \widehat h_ j,-C\} ,\quad a_ j=\frac{z_ j+C}{z_ j+C+\varepsilon },\quad z=\max \{ h,-C\} ,\quad a=\frac{z+C}{z+C+\varepsilon }. \]

The functions $z_ j$ are plurisubharmonic, locally uniformly bounded (between $-C$ and $\widehat h_1$), and decrease to $z$, so they converge to $z$ locally in capacity on the bounded open set $B_1$ (proposition 9.4.6). The weights take values in $[0,1]$, and since $s\mapsto (s+C)/(s+C+\varepsilon )$ is Lipschitz with constant $\varepsilon ^{-1}$ on $[-C,\infty )$, we have $|a_ j-a|\leq \varepsilon ^{-1}|z_ j-z|$, so $a_ j\to a$ locally in capacity on $B_1$. The limit weight $a$ is quasi-continuous on $B_1$, because $z$ is (theorem 9.4.2) and $a$ is a continuous function of $z$. On $B$, the weight $a_ j$ vanishes where $\widehat h_ j\leq -C$, and on the plurifine open set $\{ \widehat h_ j>-C\} $ we have $z_ j=\widehat h_ j$; hence plurifine locality of Bedford–Taylor products (theorem 10.4.3) gives

\[ a_ j(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}z_ j)^ d=a_ j(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widehat h_ j)^ d=0\quad \text{on }B. \]

The last assertion of theorem 9.4.8, applied on $B_1$ with $d$ factors $z_ j$ and the weights $a_ j$, gives

\[ a_ j(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}z_ j)^ d\longrightarrow a(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}z)^ d\quad \text{weakly on }B_1, \]

so $a(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}z)^ d=0$ on $B$. Since $a>0$ exactly on $\{ h>-C\} $ and $z=\max \{ h,-C\} $, the measure $\mathbf1_{\{ h>-C\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}z)^ d$ vanishes on $B$. Taking $C=k$ for all integers $k$, the truncated products in the definition of $\langle (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^ d\rangle $ all vanish on $B$; hence the non-pluripolar product is locally finite and zero on $B$. A cover of $\Omega $ by such balls proves the lemma. $\square$

Lemma 17.7.2. Let $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$, let $D$ be a coordinate domain of $X\times S$ or of $X\times \{ \mathrm e^{-1}<|z|<1\} $, the latter with $t=-\log |z|$, and let $a$ be a smooth function on $D$ whose $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ is the pullback of $\theta $ (such functions exist on every coordinate ball whose closure lies in a chart, by lemma 13.1.2). Then the complexification of the weak geodesic between $u_0$ and $u_1$, plus $a$, is a maximal plurisubharmonic function on $D$.

Proof. Let $Z$ be either $X\times S$, on which the group $\Gamma =\mathbb R$ acts by the imaginary translations $(x,\zeta )\mapsto (x,\zeta +\mathrm iy)$, or $X\times A$ with $A=\{ \mathrm e^{-1}<|z|<1\} $, on which $\Gamma =\mathbb R$ acts by the rotations $(x,z)\mapsto (x,\mathrm e^{\mathrm iy}z)$, according to where $D$ lies. Put $\tau (x,\zeta )=\operatorname{Re}\zeta $ in the first case and $\tau (x,z)=-\log |z|$ in the second, and let $\theta _ Z$ be the pullback of $\theta $ to $Z$. The action of $\Gamma $ preserves $\theta _ Z$ and $\tau $, and the $\Gamma $-invariant functions on $Z$ are exactly the functions whose value at $(x,w)\in Z$ is $v_{\tau (x,w)}(x)$, for a family $(v_ t)_{0<t<1}$ of functions on $X$; by the discussion following definition 17.6.1, such a function is $\theta _ Z$-psh if and only if the complexification of $(v_ t)$ on $X\times S$ is $p^*\theta $-psh. Let $U$ be the $\Gamma $-invariant function on $Z$ defined by the weak geodesic $(G_ t)$; it is $\theta _ Z$-psh and bounded above by $\max \{ \sup _ Xu_0,\sup _ Xu_1\} $, since $G_ t\leq (1-t)u_0+tu_1$.

Now $D\subset Z$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a=\theta _ Z$ on $D$, so that $h=U+a$ is plurisubharmonic on $D$; we show that it is maximal. Let $v$ be plurisubharmonic on $D$ with $v\leq h$ outside a compact set $K\subset D$. Every boundary point of $D$ in $Z$ has a neighborhood disjoint from $K$, on whose intersection with $D$ we have $v-a\leq U$; since $U$ is upper semicontinuous, the boundary condition of proposition 8.2.5 holds, and the function $\widetilde U$ equal to $\max \{ U,v-a\} $ on $D$ and to $U$ elsewhere is $\theta _ Z$-psh (apply the proposition in charts after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta _ Z$). Moreover $\widetilde U\geq U$, and $\widetilde U=U$ outside $K$. Since $v-a$ is bounded above on the compact set $K$, the function $\widetilde U$ is bounded above.

The functions $\widetilde U\circ \gamma $, $\gamma \in \Gamma $, are $\theta _ Z$-psh and uniformly bounded above, so their regularized upper envelope $\breve U$ is $\theta _ Z$-psh (theorem 8.3.2, applied in charts after adding smooth local potentials); it is $\Gamma $-invariant and satisfies $\breve U\geq \widetilde U$. The compact set $\tau (K)$ lies in an interval $[r_1,r_2]$ with $0<r_1\leq r_2<1$, so the $\Gamma $-orbit of $K$ lies in $\{ r_1\leq \tau \leq r_2\} $. Outside this set every $\widetilde U\circ \gamma $ equals $U\circ \gamma =U$, so $\breve U=U$ on the open set $\{ \tau <r_1\} \cup \{ \tau >r_2\} $, because $U$ is upper semicontinuous. Therefore $\breve U$ is defined by a subgeodesic whose slices coincide with $G_ t$ for $t<r_1$ and for $t>r_2$; in particular its endpoint potentials are $u_0$ and $u_1$. It is a competitor for the Perron segment between $u_0$ and $u_1$, so $\breve U\leq U$. Consequently $\widetilde U\leq U$, so $v-a\leq U$ on $D$, that is, $v\leq h$. This is maximality in the sense of definition 9.6.5. $\square$

Theorem 17.7.3. For $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$,

\[ E_\theta ^\phi (G_ t(u_0,u_1)) =(1-t)E_\theta ^\phi (u_0)+tE_\theta ^\phi (u_1), \qquad 0\leq t\leq 1. \]

Proof. Step 1: setting up a compact extension. Assume first that $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$, and write $G_ t=G_ t(u_0,u_1)$. Let $A=\{ \mathrm e^{-1}<|z|<1\} \subset \mathbb C\subset \mathbb P^1$, and let $U(x,z)=G_{-\log |z|}(x)$ be the complexification on $X\times A$. Choose a constant $C$ with $\phi -C\leq u_0,u_1\leq \phi +C$. Then $\phi -C$ is a competitor for the rooftop envelope $P_\theta (u_0,u_1)$, so the bounds of theorem 17.6.8 give $\phi -C\leq U\leq \phi +C$. Fix concentric annuli $A_0\Subset A_1\Subset A$. On $Y=X\times \mathbb P^1$, with projections $p_1\colon Y\to X$ and $p_2\colon Y\to \mathbb P^1$, let $\beta =p_2^*\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$, where $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ is the Fubini–Study form of mass one, and put

\[ \Theta =p_1^*\theta +B\beta ,\qquad \Phi =p_1^*\phi , \]

with a constant $B>0$ chosen below.

To glue $U$ to $\Phi $ we use a smooth function $q$ on $\mathbb P^1$ that is small on $A_0$ and large near the boundary of $A_1$. Write $A_0=\{ r_0<|z|<r_0'\} $ and $A_1=\{ r_1<|z|<r_1'\} $, so that $\mathrm e^{-1}<r_1<r_0<r_0'<r_1'<1$, and choose $\varepsilon >0$ with $r_1+\varepsilon <r_0$ and $r_0'<r_1'-\varepsilon $. Let $\chi _0$ be a smooth function on $\mathbb R$ such that $1-\chi _0$ is a smooth cutoff function for $[r_0,r_0']$ in $(r_1+\varepsilon ,r_1'-\varepsilon )$ (lemma 1.4.1(4)); thus $\chi _0$ takes values in $[0,1]$, vanishes on $[r_0,r_0']$, and equals $1$ outside $(r_1+\varepsilon ,r_1'-\varepsilon )$. The function $q(z)=(2C+4)\chi _0(|z|)-(C+2)$, with $q(\infty )=C+2$, is smooth on $\mathbb P^1$, because $|z|$ is smooth on $\mathbb C\setminus \{ 0\} $ and $q$ is constant near $0$ and near $\infty $. It equals $-C-2$ on $A_0$ and $C+2$ on a neighborhood of $\partial A_1$. Choose $B$ so large that $B\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq 0$.

Then $\Phi +q$ is $\Theta $-psh, and so is $U$ on $X\times A_1$, since $\beta \geq 0$. Define $W=\max \{ U,\Phi +q\} $ on $X\times A_1$ and $W=\Phi +q$ on $Y\setminus (X\times A_1)$. Near $X\times \partial A_1$ we have $\Phi +q=\Phi +C+2>U$ outside the pole set of $\Phi $, on which both functions are $-\infty $; hence $W=\Phi +q$ on a collar inside $X\times A_1$. Thus, near every point of $Y$, $W$ is either the maximum of two $\Theta $-psh functions or equal to $\Phi +q$, and $W$ is $\Theta $-psh on $Y$. Moreover $W-\Phi $ is bounded, and $W=U$ on $X\times A_0$, since there $\Phi +q=\Phi -C-2<U$ outside the pole set of $\Phi $.

Step 2: a current on $\mathbb P^1$. Put $T=\Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W$ and $R=\Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi $, and define the current of order zero on $\mathbb P^1$

\[ H=\frac1{n+1}(p_2)_* \left((W-\Phi )\sum _{j=0}^ n\langle T^ j\wedge R^{n-j}\rangle \right). \]

Let $\chi $ be a smooth function on $\mathbb P^1$. By theorem 17.1.3 on the compact Kähler manifold $Y$, applied to the bounded quasi-psh differences $W-\Phi $ and $\chi \circ p_2$, we may move $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ from $\chi \circ p_2$ to $W-\Phi $. By (17.1), $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(W-\Phi )\wedge \langle T^ j\wedge R^{n-j}\rangle =\langle T^{j+1}\wedge R^{n-j}\rangle -\langle T^ j\wedge R^{n-j+1}\rangle $, and the sum over $j$ telescopes. Therefore

\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H=\frac1{n+1}(p_2)_* (\langle T^{n+1}\rangle -\langle R^{n+1}\rangle ). \end{equation}

On the other hand, corollary 15.5.1, with $Z=\mathbb P^1$ and $\gamma =B\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$, shows that $H$ is given by the function $z\mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (W(\cdot ,z))$. In particular, on $A_0$, $H$ is the function $z\mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (G_{-\log |z|})$.

Step 3: $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H$ vanishes on $A_0$. On $X\times A_0$ put $L=p_1^*\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U\geq 0$, so that $T=L+B\beta $ there. Locally $T$ has a plurisubharmonic potential which is the sum of a potential of $L$ and a smooth potential of $B\beta $. The product $\langle T^{n+1}\rangle $ is finite by theorem 15.4.3, so multilinearity (proposition 15.1.5), compatibility with smooth factors (proposition 15.3.3) and $\beta \wedge \beta =0$ give, on $X\times A_0$,

\[ \langle T^{n+1}\rangle =\langle L^{n+1}\rangle +(n+1)B\beta \wedge \langle L^ n\rangle , \qquad \beta \wedge \langle T^ n\rangle =\beta \wedge \langle L^ n\rangle , \]

with all terms locally finite. Every point of $X\times A_0$ has a coordinate ball $D\subset X\times A_0$ on which $p_1^*\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ for a smooth function $a$ (lemma 13.1.2); there $L=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(U+a)$, and lemma 17.7.2, lemma 17.7.1 give $\langle L^{n+1}\rangle =0$ on $D$. Hence $\langle L^{n+1}\rangle =0$ on $X\times A_0$, so that $\langle T^{n+1}\rangle =(n+1)B\beta \wedge \langle T^ n\rangle $ on $X\times A_0$. Every slice $W(\cdot ,z)$ has bounded difference from $\phi $ and hence mass $m$, so corollary 15.5.1 with weight one gives $(p_2)_*(\beta \wedge \langle T^ n\rangle )=m\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$.

The current $R$ is treated in the same way. We have $R=p_1^*\theta _\phi +B\beta $, and $\langle (p_1^*\theta _\phi )^{n+1}\rangle =0$. Indeed, the local potentials of $p_1^*\theta _\phi $ do not depend on the variable in $\mathbb P^1$, and so neither do their bounded truncations; each truncation is the decreasing limit of smooth plurisubharmonic functions of the $n$ variables of $X$ (proposition 8.2.2(2)), whose $(n+1)$st powers of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ vanish, so the bounded products of the truncations vanish by theorem 9.2.5, and with them the truncated products defining $\langle (p_1^*\theta _\phi )^{n+1}\rangle $. Hence $\langle R^{n+1}\rangle =(n+1)B\beta \wedge \langle R^ n\rangle $, and the same fiber formula, applied to $\Phi $ in place of $W$, gives $(p_2)_*\langle R^{n+1}\rangle =(n+1)Bm\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$. Hence the right side of (17.51) vanishes on $A_0$, that is, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H=0$ on $A_0$.

Step 4: the energy is affine. The function $\tau \mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (G_\tau )$ is locally Lipschitz on $(0,1)$. Indeed, let $0<\delta <\frac12$. On the common finite locus the convex functions $\tau \mapsto G_\tau (x)-\phi (x)$ take values in $[-C,C]$, so by lemma 17.6.2(4) their chord slopes on $[\delta ,1-\delta ]$ are bounded by $2C/\delta $. Thus $G_ s-(2C/\delta )|s-\tau |\leq G_\tau \leq G_ s+(2C/\delta )|s-\tau |$ for $s,\tau \in [\delta ,1-\delta ]$ on the common finite locus, hence everywhere, and monotonicity and the shift formula give $|E_\theta ^\phi (G_\tau )-E_\theta ^\phi (G_ s)|\leq (2Cm/\delta )|s-\tau |$.

Writing $z=\xi +\mathrm i\eta $, we have $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=(4\pi )^{-1}\Delta f\, \mathrm d\xi \wedge \mathrm d\eta $ for smooth functions $f$, and hence for distributions, so $\Delta H=0$ on $A_0$ by Step 3, and Weyl's lemma (theorem 2.1.4) shows that $H$ is represented on $A_0$ by a harmonic function. By Step 2, $H$ is also represented on $A_0$ by the function $z\mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (G_{-\log |z|})$, which is continuous by the Lipschitz bound just proved. Two locally integrable functions defining the same distribution agree almost everywhere (proposition 1.4.3(4)), and two continuous functions that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere, so this function is harmonic on $A_0$. A radial harmonic function on an annulus is an affine function of $\log |z|$: if $f(z)=g(\log |z|)$ is smooth, then $g$ is smooth and the Laplacian in polar coordinates gives $\Delta f=|z|^{-2}g''(\log |z|)$, so $g''=0$. Since $A_0$ was arbitrary, $\tau \mapsto E_\theta ^\phi (G_\tau )$ is affine on $(0,1)$.

Finally, $|u_1-u_0|\leq 2C$ on the common finite locus, so proposition 17.6.9(5) gives $|G_\tau -u_0|\leq 2C\tau $ and $|G_\tau -u_1|\leq 2C(1-\tau )$ there. By the Lipschitz bound of proposition 17.2.4, the energies converge to $E_\theta ^\phi (u_0)$ and $E_\theta ^\phi (u_1)$ as $\tau \to 0$ and $\tau \to 1$. This proves the theorem for $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^\infty (X,\theta ,\phi )$.

Step 5: finite energy. Let $u_0,u_1\in \mathcal E^1(X,\theta ,\phi )$. Adding the same constant $c$ to both endpoints adds $c$ to $G_ t$, by proposition 17.6.9(2), and adds $cm$ to both sides of the asserted identity. We may therefore assume $u_0,u_1\leq \phi $. Put $u_{j,k}=\max \{ u_ j,\phi -k\} $ for $j=0,1$. By proposition 17.6.9(3), $G_ t(u_{0,k},u_{1,k})\downarrow G_ t(u_0,u_1)$ as $k\to \infty $, and the limit has finite energy by theorem 17.6.8. The identity holds for $u_{0,k},u_{1,k}$ by the preceding steps, and proposition 17.2.7, applied at the endpoints and at each interior slice, passes it to the limit. $\square$

17.8 Extremal slopes and long Perron segments

We close the chapter with two estimates on how a weak geodesic depends on its endpoints. The first, proposition 17.8.1, shows that the essential supremum of $G_ t(u,v)-u$ is a linear function of $t$, and similarly for the essential infimum; essential suprema and infima are taken with respect to the volume form $\omega ^ n$. The second, proposition 17.8.2, concerns long segments from $\phi $ to the truncations of a singular potential.

Proposition 17.8.1. Let $u,v\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$ with $v\preceq u$, and put $C=\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_ X(v-u)$. Then $C$ is finite and

\[ \operatorname *{ess\, sup}_ X(G_ t(u,v)-u)=tC,\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1. \]

If moreover $u\sim v$ and $c=\operatorname *{ess\, inf}_ X(v-u)$, then $\operatorname *{ess\, inf}_ X(G_ t(u,v)-u)=tc$. These essential extrema equal the corresponding extrema over the common finite locus, and they do not change if a pluripolar set is removed.

Proof. The function $v-u$ is finite almost everywhere, and $v\preceq u$ gives $v-u\leq C'$ for some constant $C'$; hence $C$ is finite. The inequality $v\leq u+C$ holds almost everywhere, hence everywhere, as recalled in section 16.1. Thus the supremum of $v-u$ over the common finite locus is at most $C$; it is at least $C$ because the common finite locus has full measure. The same argument applies to an infimum, and to the complement of any pluripolar set, which still has full measure. The same remarks apply to $G_ t-u$.

By theorem 17.6.8, $G_ t\leq (1-t)u+tv\leq u+tC$. The affine curve $v-C(1-t)$ is a competitor, since $v-C\leq u$, so $G_ t\geq v-(1-t)C$. On the common finite locus $v-u=(v-G_ t)+(G_ t-u)$, and taking essential suprema gives $C\leq (1-t)C+\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_ X(G_ t-u)$. Together with the first bound, this proves the equality for the supremum.

Now let $u\sim v$. The affine curve $u+ct$ is a competitor, since $u+c\leq v$, so $G_ t-u\geq ct$. Applying the equality for the supremum to the reversed segment, $G_ t(u,v)=G_{1-t}(v,u)$, gives $\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_ X(G_ t-v)=(1-t)\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_ X(u-v)=-(1-t)c$, that is, $\operatorname *{ess\, inf}_ X(v-G_ t)=(1-t)c$. Taking essential infima in $v-u=(v-G_ t)+(G_ t-u)$ gives $c\geq (1-t)c+\operatorname *{ess\, inf}_ X(G_ t-u)$, which proves the opposite bound. $\square$

We now turn to long segments. Given a potential $u\leq \phi $ and $T>0$, we join $\phi $ to the truncation $\max \{ u,\phi -T\} $ by a weak geodesic parametrized by $[0,T]$. Changing $u$ by at most a constant $C$ changes this segment at time $s$ by at most $Cs/T$, an error that tends to zero as $T\to \infty $. Hence the limits of these segments as $T\to \infty $ depend only on the singularity type of $u$.

Proposition 17.8.2. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\leq \phi $. For $T>0$ and $0\leq s\leq T$ put

\[ u_ T=\max \{ u,\phi -T\} ,\qquad L_ s^{u,T}=G_{s/T}(\phi ,u_ T). \]

For fixed $s\geq 0$, the potentials $L_ s^{u,T}$ increase with $T\geq s$ and satisfy $\phi -s\leq L_ s^{u,T}\leq \phi $. Their regularized limit

\[ R_ s^ u=\left(\sup _{T>s}L_ s^{u,T}\right)^* \]

is $\theta $-psh. If $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfies $v\leq \phi $, and $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C\geq 0$, then

\[ L_ s^{u,T}\leq L_ s^{v,T}+\frac{Cs}{T},\qquad R_ s^ u\leq R_ s^ v. \]

In particular, if $u\sim v$, then $R_ s^ u=R_ s^ v$ for every $s\geq 0$. Consequently, for an arbitrary $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\preceq \phi $, the potentials $R_ s^{u-c}$ do not depend on the constant $c$ with $u-c\leq \phi $. Denoting them by $R_ s^ u$, we have $R_ s^ u\leq R_ s^ v$ whenever $u\preceq v\preceq \phi $.

Proof. Since $\phi -T\leq u_ T\leq \phi $, the potential $u_ T$ has bounded difference from $\phi $; in particular $u_ T\in \mathcal E(X,\theta ,\phi )$. By theorem 17.6.8, the Perron segment from $\phi $ to $u_ T$ is a weak geodesic. We regard it as a curve on $[0,T]$, so that its slice at time $s$ is $L_ s^{u,T}$.

Bounds. For $0\leq s\leq T$ put $q_ s=\max \{ u,\phi -s\} $. After the affine change of parameter, the complexification of the curve $(q_ s)_{0\leq s\leq T}$ is $(x,\zeta )\mapsto \max \{ u(x),\phi (x)-T\operatorname{Re}\zeta \} $. This is the maximum of two $p^*\theta $-psh functions, because $\operatorname{Re}\zeta $ is pluriharmonic, and hence it is $p^*\theta $-psh (proposition 8.2.5, applied after adding smooth local potentials of $p^*\theta $, which exist by lemma 7.3.1). Moreover $q_0=\phi $, since $u\leq \phi $, and $q_ T=u_ T$. As $|q_ s-q_ t|\leq |s-t|$ on the finite locus of $\phi $, the curve tends to $\phi $ and $u_ T$ in $L^1$ at the two ends. Thus $(q_ s)$ is a subgeodesic with endpoints $\phi $ and $u_ T$, and by the definition of the Perron segment, $L_ s^{u,T}\geq q_ s\geq \phi -s$. On the other hand, theorem 17.6.8 gives $L_ s^{u,T}\leq (1-s/T)\phi +(s/T)u_ T\leq \phi $.

Monotonicity in $T$. Let $T'>T$. The same subgeodesic, now on $[0,T']$, shows that $L_ T^{u,T'}\geq q_ T=u_ T$. By the last assertion of theorem 17.6.8, the restriction of the segment of length $T'$ to $[0,T]$ is the weak geodesic from $\phi $ to $L_ T^{u,T'}$. Since weak geodesics increase with their endpoints (proposition 17.6.9(1)), this restriction dominates the weak geodesic from $\phi $ to $u_ T$. Hence $L_ s^{u,T'}\geq L_ s^{u,T}$ for $0\leq s\leq T$.

The limit. Fix $s$. The family $(L_ s^{u,T})_{T>s}$ is increasing, bounded above by $\phi $ and bounded below by $\phi -s$. By theorem 8.3.2, applied after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $, its regularized supremum $R_ s^ u$ is $\theta $-psh, and $\phi -s\leq R_ s^ u\leq \phi $. Since the family is increasing, the supremum may be taken over $T\geq A$ for any $A>s$ without changing $R_ s^ u$. For $s=0$, all the potentials $L_0^{u,T}$ and $R_0^ u$ equal $\phi $.

Comparison. Suppose that $v\leq \phi $ and $u\leq v+C$ with $C\geq 0$. Then

\[ u_ T\leq \max \{ v+C,\phi -T\} \leq v_ T+C. \]

By the monotonicity of weak geodesics in their endpoints and the formula for constant shifts of the endpoints, proposition 17.6.9(1) and (2), we have $G_ t(\phi ,u_ T)\leq G_ t(\phi ,v_ T+C)=G_ t(\phi ,v_ T)+tC$ for $0\leq t\leq 1$. Taking $t=s/T$ gives the bound $L_ s^{u,T}\leq L_ s^{v,T}+Cs/T$. Now fix $s$ and $A>s$. For $T\geq A$ we get $L_ s^{u,T}\leq R_ s^ v+Cs/A$, and taking the regularized supremum over $T\geq A$ gives

\[ R_ s^ u\leq R_ s^ v+\frac{Cs}{A}. \]

Letting $A\to \infty $ yields $R_ s^ u\leq R_ s^ v$.

If $u\sim v$, then $u\leq v+C$ and $v\leq u+C$ for some $C\geq 0$, and the comparison applied in both directions gives $R_ s^ u=R_ s^ v$. If $u\preceq \phi $ is arbitrary, any two constants $c,c'$ with $u-c\leq \phi $ and $u-c'\leq \phi $ give potentials $u-c\sim u-c'$, so $R_ s^{u-c}=R_ s^{u-c'}$. Finally, if $u\preceq v\preceq \phi $, choose constants $c,c'$ with $u-c\leq \phi $ and $v-c'\leq \phi $. Then $u-c\leq v-c'+C$ for some $C\geq 0$, and the comparison gives $R_ s^ u=R_ s^{u-c}\leq R_ s^{v-c'}=R_ s^ v$. $\square$
