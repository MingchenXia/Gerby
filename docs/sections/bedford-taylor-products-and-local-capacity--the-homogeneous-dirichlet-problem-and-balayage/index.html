7.7 The homogeneous Dirichlet problem and balayage22EC1FF0BE9D

22EC1FF0BE9D

A plurisubharmonic function is maximal if it cannot be increased on a compact set without losing plurisubharmonicity (definition 7.7.6). We show in theorem 7.7.7 that, for locally bounded functions, maximality is equivalent to $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=0$. The proof replaces $u$ on a ball by a solution $H$ of the homogeneous equation $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=0$ with the same boundary values (corollary 7.7.5); in section 5.47 the same replacement shows that relative extremal functions have vanishing Monge–Ampère measure away from the sets on which they are constrained. We construct $H$ as an upper envelope. To show that it solves the equation, we bound its second differences by composing with automorphisms of the ball (lemma 7.7.1), which makes it twice differentiable almost everywhere (lemma 7.7.2).

Lemma 7.7.1.4521AF3E3E5C Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be the unit ball. For $a\in B$ and $h\in \mathbb C^ n$ with $|h|$ small, depending on $a$, there are biholomorphic automorphisms $S_{a,h}$ of $B$ such that $S_{a,0}$ is the identity and $S_{a,h}(a)=a+h$. Each $S_{a,h}$ extends to a holomorphic map on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ which maps $\partial B$ onto $\partial B$. For every compact set $K\subset B$ there is $h_0>0$ such that the map $(a,h,z)\mapsto S_{a,h}(z)$ is defined and smooth on an open subset of $\mathbb C^ n\times \mathbb C^ n\times \mathbb C^ n$ containing $K\times \overline{B(0,h_0)}\times \overline B$, and holomorphic in $z$ there. In particular, there is a constant $C$ such that, for $a\in K$, $|h|\le h_0$ and $z\in \overline B$, the first and second derivatives of $S_{a,h}(z)$ with respect to the real variable $h\in \mathbb C^ n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ are bounded by $C$.

Proof. Use the Hermitian product $\langle z,a\rangle =\sum z_ j\bar a_ j$, put $s_ a=\sqrt{1-|a|^2}$, and define

\[ T_ a(z)=\frac{s_ a z+\langle z,a\rangle a/(1+s_ a)-a}{1-\langle z,a\rangle }. \]

This expression depends smoothly on $(a,z)$ in the open set $\{ (a,z):|a|<1,\ |a||z|<1\} $, where the denominator does not vanish, and it is holomorphic in $z$; moreover $T_0$ is the identity. If $U$ is a unitary matrix, then $\langle Uz,Ua\rangle =\langle z,a\rangle $ and $s_{Ua}=s_ a$, so $T_{Ua}(Uz)=UT_ a(z)$. Given $a\ne 0$, choose $U$ with $Ua=(t,0,\ldots ,0)$, $t=|a|$; it therefore suffices to verify the following identities for $a=(t,0,\ldots ,0)$ with $0\le t<1$, and also for $-a=(-t,0,\ldots ,0)$. Put $s=s_ a=\sqrt{1-t^2}$. Then $\langle z,a\rangle =tz_1$, and the first component of the numerator of $T_ a(z)$ is $sz_1+t^2z_1/(1+s)-t=z_1-t$, because $s(1+s)+t^2=s+s^2+t^2=1+s$. Hence

\[ \begin{aligned} T_ a(z)& =\left(\frac{z_1-t}{1-tz_1}, \frac{s\, z_2}{1-tz_1},\ldots , \frac{s\, z_ n}{1-tz_1}\right),\\ T_{-a}(w)& =\left(\frac{w_1+t}{1+tw_1}, \frac{s\, w_2}{1+tw_1},\ldots , \frac{s\, w_ n}{1+tw_1}\right), \end{aligned} \]

the second formula by the same computation with $t$ replaced by $-t$. In particular $T_ a(a)=0$ and $T_{-a}(0)=a$. If $w=T_ a(z)$, then

\[ w_1+t=\frac{(1-t^2)z_1}{1-tz_1},\qquad 1+tw_1=\frac{1-t^2}{1-tz_1}, \]

so the first component of $T_{-a}(w)$ is $z_1$ and the others are $s\cdot sz_ k/(1-tz_1)\cdot (1-tz_1)/(1-t^2)=z_ k$; thus $T_{-a}\circ T_ a=\mathrm{id}$, and by symmetry $T_ a\circ T_{-a}=\mathrm{id}$. Finally,

\[ |1-tz_1|^2-|z_1-t|^2-(1-t^2)\sum _{k\ge 2}|z_ k|^2=(1-t^2)(1-|z_1|^2)-(1-t^2)\sum _{k\ge 2}|z_ k|^2, \]

because the terms $\mp 2t\operatorname{Re}z_1$ cancel; dividing by $|1-tz_1|^2$ gives

\[ 1-|T_ a(z)|^2=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\langle z,a\rangle |^2}. \]

Hence $T_ a$ maps $B$ into $B$ and the sphere $\partial B$ into itself, and the same holds for $T_{-a}$; since $T_{-a}$ is inverse to $T_ a$, the map $T_ a$ is a biholomorphic automorphism of $B$ which maps $\partial B$ onto itself.

Set $S_{a,h}=T_{-(a+h)}\circ T_ a$ for $|a+h|<1$. It maps $a$ to $T_{-(a+h)}(0)=a+h$ and is the identity for $h=0$. The map $T_ a$ is holomorphic on the neighborhood $\{ z:|a||z|<1\} $ of $\overline B$, and, as we have just seen, it maps $\overline B$ into $\overline B$ and $\partial B$ onto $\partial B$; the same holds for $T_{-(a+h)}$. Hence $S_{a,h}$ is holomorphic on the neighborhood $\{ z:|a||z|<1,\ |a+h||T_ a(z)|<1\} $ of $\overline B$, and it maps $\partial B$ onto $\partial B$. Let $K\subset B$ be compact (we may assume $K\neq \varnothing $), put $t_ K=\max _ K|a|<1$, and choose $h_0>0$ with $t_ K+3h_0<1$. On the open set $O$ of all $(a,h,z)$ with $|a|<t_ K+h_0$, $|h|<2h_0$, $|a||z|<1$ and $(t_ K+3h_0)|T_ a(z)|<1$, we have $|a+h|<1$ and $|a+h||T_ a(z)|<1$, so $(a,h,z)\mapsto S_{a,h}(z)$ is defined and smooth on $O$, and holomorphic in $z$. Since $|T_ a(z)|\le 1$ for $|z|\le 1$, the set $O$ contains the compact set $K\times \overline{B(0,h_0)}\times \overline B$, on which the continuous first and second derivatives of $S_{a,h}(z)$ with respect to $h$ are therefore bounded by a constant $C$. $\square$

A plurisubharmonic function whose second differences are bounded above is locally of class $C^{1,1}$, and its Monge–Ampère measure is then given by the determinant of its complex Hessian.

Lemma 7.7.2.0E5A3E632224 Let $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be open, $n\ge 1$, and let $H$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $U$. Suppose that for every compact set $K\subset U$ there are constants $C_ K\ge 0$ and $h_ K>0$ such that, for all $a\in K$ and all $h\in \mathbb C^ n$ with $|h|\le h_ K$, the points $a\pm h$ lie in $U$ and

332CD5FE501E 7.7.3
\begin{equation} H(a+h)+H(a-h)-2H(a)\le 2C_ K|h|^2\qquad (a\in K,\ |h|\le h_ K). \end{equation}

Then $H$ is continuous, $H\in C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$, and the distributional real Hessian of $H$ is a matrix $A=(A_{ab})$ of measurable functions which are bounded on compact subsets of $U$. Almost every $x\in U$ satisfies

\[ H(x+h)=H(x)+\nabla H(x)\cdot h+\tfrac 12h^ tA(x)h+o(|h|^2)\qquad (h\to 0). \]

Moreover, let $H_{j\bar k}$ be the coefficients of the distributional complex Hessian of $H$, which are fixed complex linear combinations of the $A_{ab}$. Then the matrix $(H_{j\bar k})$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere, and

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=\frac{n!}{\pi ^ n}\det (H_{j\bar k})\, \mathrm dV_{2n}. \]

Proof. Let $H_\varepsilon =H*\rho _\varepsilon $. Fix $a_0\in U$ and $r>0$ with $\overline{B(a_0,4r)}\subset U$, put $K=\overline{B(a_0,3r)}$, and let $M_0=\sup _ K|H|$, which is finite because $H$ is locally bounded. For $0<\varepsilon <r$, the regularization $H_\varepsilon $ is smooth and plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline{B(a_0,2r)}$ (proposition 5.9.2), and $|H_\varepsilon |\le M_0$ on $\overline{B(a_0,2r)}$, since its values there are averages of values of $H$ on $K$. For $x\in \overline{B(a_0,2r)}$ and $|h|\le h_ K$,

\[ \begin{aligned} & H_\varepsilon (x+h)+H_\varepsilon (x-h)-2H_\varepsilon (x)\\ & \qquad =\int \bigl (H(x-y+h)+H(x-y-h)-2H(x-y)\bigr )\rho _\varepsilon (y)\, \mathrm dy\le 2C_ K|h|^2, \end{aligned} \]

by (7.7.3), because $x-y\in K$ for $|y|\le \varepsilon $. Replacing $h$ by $th$ with $0<t\le 1$, dividing by $t^2$ and letting $t\to 0$, we get $D^2H_\varepsilon (x)[h,h]\le 2C_ K|h|^2$ for $|h|\le h_ K$, and hence, by homogeneity, for all $h\in \mathbb C^ n=\mathbb R^{2n}$. On the other hand, the complex Hessian of the smooth plurisubharmonic function $H_\varepsilon $ is positive semidefinite (proposition 5.9.2), and $D^2H_\varepsilon [h,h]+D^2H_\varepsilon [\mathrm ih,\mathrm ih]=4\sum _{j,k}(H_\varepsilon )_{j\bar k}h_ j\bar h_ k\ge 0$; hence $D^2H_\varepsilon (x)[h,h]\ge -2C_ K|h|^2$ as well. Thus the second derivatives of the $H_\varepsilon $ are bounded on $\overline{B(a_0,2r)}$ by a constant $\Lambda _0$ independent of $\varepsilon $. For $x\in \overline{B(a_0,r)}$ and a unit vector $e$, Taylor's formula

\[ H_\varepsilon (x+re)=H_\varepsilon (x)+r\, \partial _ eH_\varepsilon (x)+\int _0^ r(r-t)D^2H_\varepsilon (x+te)[e,e]\, \mathrm dt \]

gives $|\partial _ eH_\varepsilon (x)|\le 2M_0/r+\Lambda _0r=\Lambda $. By the mean value theorem on the convex set $B(a_0,r)$, $|H_\varepsilon (x)-H_\varepsilon (y)|\le \Lambda |x-y|$ for $x,y\in B(a_0,r)$. Since $H_\varepsilon \to H$ pointwise as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, by (3.4.13), the same inequality holds for $H$. Hence $H$ is continuous on $B(a_0,r)$, and, as $a_0$ was arbitrary, on $U$.

Moreover, $H_\varepsilon \to H$ in $L^1$ on compact subsets of $U$, by dominated convergence, and hence in the sense of distributions; and every ball $B_0\Subset U$ is covered by finitely many balls $B(a_0,2r)$ as above, so the second derivatives of the $H_\varepsilon $ are bounded on $B_0$ uniformly in small $\varepsilon $. By the converse part of proposition 2.8.5, the distributional real Hessian of $H$ has locally bounded measurable coefficients $A_{ab}$; hence $H\in C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$, and almost every $x\in U$ satisfies the second-order expansion of the statement.

The functions $H_{j\bar k}$ are bounded measurable functions on compact subsets of $U$. By proposition 2.5.3, the complex Hessian of $H_\varepsilon $ has the coefficients $(H_{j\bar k})_\varepsilon $; these are bounded on compact sets uniformly in small $\varepsilon $, and they converge to $H_{j\bar k}$ at every Lebesgue point of $H_{j\bar k}$, hence almost everywhere (theorem 2.8.3). In particular, the matrix $(H_{j\bar k})$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere, as an almost everywhere limit of the positive semidefinite complex Hessians of the smooth plurisubharmonic functions $H_\varepsilon $. Since $H$ is continuous, $H_\varepsilon \to H$ locally uniformly (lemma 2.5.1(1)), so $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_\varepsilon )^ n\to (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n$ weakly on every ball $B_0\Subset U$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ along a sequence, by proposition 7.2.6 applied on $B_0$. By (7.2.4), $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_\varepsilon )^ n=n!\pi ^{-n}\det ((H_{j\bar k})_\varepsilon )\, \mathrm dV_{2n}$, and the determinants converge almost everywhere and are bounded on compact sets uniformly in small $\varepsilon $. Hence, for every test function $\chi $ supported in such a ball $B_0$, dominated convergence gives

\[ \int \chi \, (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=\frac{n!}{\pi ^ n}\int \chi \det (H_{j\bar k})\, \mathrm dV_{2n}. \]

By lemma 2.5.2, every test function on $U$ is a finite sum of test functions supported in balls $B_0\Subset U$, so this identity holds for all test functions on $U$, and the two Radon measures coincide by proposition 2.5.3(4). $\square$

Theorem 7.7.4.9181532558D7 Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a ball and $\varphi $ a real continuous function on $\partial B$. There is a unique $H\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)\cap C(\overline B)$ such that

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=0\quad \text{on }B,\qquad H|_{\partial B}=\varphi . \]

It is the upper envelope of the class of all $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $\varlimsup _{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le \varphi (x)$ for every $x\in \partial B$. If $\varphi $ is the restriction of a $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\partial B$, then $H\in C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)$.

Proof. After an affine change of coordinates $z\mapsto z_0+Rz$ with $R>0$, we may assume that $B$ is the unit ball; plurisubharmonicity, the boundary conditions, $C^2$ data and the vanishing of the Monge–Ampère measure are preserved by such a change, by proposition 5.9.3 and proposition 7.2.2(5). We call the functions $v$ in the statement the subsolutions for $\varphi $ of the homogeneous equation. If $v$ is a subsolution for $\varphi $ and $\varphi \le \varphi '+c$ on $\partial B$ with a constant $c$, then $v-c$ is a subsolution for $\varphi '$. Every subsolution is subharmonic on $B$ (theorem 5.9.1), so the comparison principle (theorem 3.4.5 and the remark following it), applied with the harmonic function $P\varphi $, the Poisson integral of $\varphi $ given by theorem 3.1.18, gives $v\le P\varphi $, and $P\varphi \le \max _{\partial B}\varphi $ because the Poisson kernel is positive with integral $1$. Hence, as soon as the class of subsolutions is nonempty, its upper envelope $H$ is defined, and $H\le P\varphi $.

Step 1: the envelope for $C^2$ data. Suppose that $\varphi $ is the restriction of a $C^2$ function on a neighborhood of $\partial B$. Multiplying this function by a cutoff equal to $1$ near $\partial B$, we obtain a $C^2$ function $\tilde\varphi $ with compact support in $\mathbb {C}^ n$ and $\tilde\varphi =\varphi $ on $\partial B$. Its complex Hessian is continuous with compact support, so there is $\lambda >0$ with $\sum _{j,k}\tilde\varphi _{j\bar k}\xi _ j\bar\xi _ k\ge -\lambda |\xi |^2$ everywhere. The function $s=\tilde\varphi +\lambda (|z|^2-1)$ is then of class $C^2$ with positive semidefinite complex Hessian, so it is plurisubharmonic by proposition 5.9.2. As $s=\varphi $ on $\partial B$, it is a subsolution. Hence $s\le H\le P\varphi $. By theorem 5.10.2, the upper semicontinuous regularization $H^*$ is plurisubharmonic, and $s\le H^*\le P\varphi $ since $P\varphi $ is continuous. As $s$ and $P\varphi $ are continuous on $\overline B$ with boundary values $\varphi $, we have $H^*(z)\to \varphi (x)$ as $z\to x\in \partial B$; so $H^*$ is a subsolution, $H^*\le H$, and $H=H^*$ is plurisubharmonic. Moreover $H(z)\to \varphi (x)$ as $z\to x\in \partial B$, and $H$ is bounded.

Step 2: second differences. Let $K\subset B$ be compact, and let $S_{a,h}$, $h_0$ and $C$ be as in lemma 7.7.1. For $x\in \partial B$ and $a\in K$, the function $g(h)=\tilde\varphi (S_{a,h}(x))$, $|h|\le h_0$, is of class $C^2$, and by the chain rule its second derivatives are bounded by a constant $2C_ K$ independent of $a$ and $x$, since the first and second derivatives of $\tilde\varphi $ are bounded and those of $S_{a,h}(x)$ with respect to $h$ are bounded by $C$. Since $g(0)=\varphi (x)$, Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives $g(h)+g(-h)-2g(0)=\int _0^1(1-t)\bigl (D^2g(th)[h,h]+D^2g(-th)[h,h]\bigr )\, \mathrm dt\le 2C_ K|h|^2$. As $S_{a,\pm h}$ maps $\partial B$ to $\partial B$, where $\tilde\varphi =\varphi $, this reads

\[ \tfrac 12\bigl (\varphi \circ S_{a,h}+\varphi \circ S_{a,-h}\bigr ) \le \varphi +C_ K|h|^2\quad \text{on }\partial B\qquad (a\in K,\ |h|\le h_0). \]

The function $w=\frac12(H\circ S_{a,h}+H\circ S_{a,-h})-C_ K|h|^2$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$, by proposition 5.9.3, proposition 5.9.5, since $S_{a,\pm h}$ are automorphisms of $B$ and $H$ is bounded. If $y\in \partial B$ and $z\to y$ in $B$, then $S_{a,\pm h}(z)\to S_{a,\pm h}(y)\in \partial B$, so $H(S_{a,\pm h}(z))\to \varphi (S_{a,\pm h}(y))$ by Step 1, and the preceding inequality gives $\varlimsup _{z\to y}w(z)\le \varphi (y)$. Thus $w$ is a subsolution, so $w\le H$. Evaluating at $z=a$, where $S_{a,\pm h}(a)=a\pm h$, we obtain

\[ H(a+h)+H(a-h)-2H(a)\le 2C_ K|h|^2\qquad (a\in K,\ |h|\le h_0). \]

Since $K$ was arbitrary and the points $a\pm h=S_{a,\pm h}(a)$ lie in $B$, lemma 7.7.2 applies on $U=B$. Thus $H$ is continuous on $B$ and belongs to $C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)$, and its distributional real Hessian $A$ and complex Hessian $(H_{j\bar k})$ have the properties stated there: the second-order expansion holds almost everywhere, $(H_{j\bar k})$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=n!\, \pi ^{-n}\det (H_{j\bar k})\, \mathrm dV_{2n}$.

Step 3: the equation. Suppose that $\det (H_{j\bar k})>0$ on a set of positive measure, and choose a point $a$ of this set at which $(H_{j\bar k}(a))$ is positive semidefinite and the second-order expansion holds. The Taylor polynomial $Q(z)=H(a)+\nabla H(a)\cdot (z-a)+\frac12(z-a)^ tA(a)(z-a)$ is a real quadratic polynomial with real Hessian $A(a)$, so its complex Hessian is the constant matrix $(H_{j\bar k}(a))$; this matrix is positive semidefinite with positive determinant, hence positive definite. Let $\eta >0$ be smaller than its smallest eigenvalue; then $Q-\eta |z-a|^2$ has positive semidefinite complex Hessian and is plurisubharmonic (proposition 5.9.2). For sufficiently small $r>0$ we have $\overline{B(a,r)}\subset B$ and, by the expansion, $|H-Q|<\eta r^2/2$ on the sphere $|z-a|=r$. The polynomial $\tilde Q=Q-\eta |z-a|^2+\eta r^2/4$ is then strictly smaller than $H$ on that sphere, since there $\tilde Q<H+\eta r^2/2-3\eta r^2/4$, and $\tilde Q(a)=H(a)+\eta r^2/4>H(a)$. By proposition 5.9.5, applied with $D=B(a,r)$ and $v=\tilde Q$, whose boundary inequality holds because $\tilde Q$ and $H$ are continuous, the function equal to $\max \{ H,\tilde Q\} $ on $B(a,r)$ and to $H$ elsewhere is plurisubharmonic on $B$. It coincides with $H$ near $\partial B$, so it is a subsolution, and it exceeds $H$ at $a$, a contradiction. Hence $\det (H_{j\bar k})=0$ almost everywhere, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=0$. This proves the theorem for $C^2$ data, including the $C^{1,1}$ regularity, except for uniqueness.

Step 4: continuous data and uniqueness. For continuous $\varphi $, the function $z\mapsto \varphi (z/|z|)$ is continuous on the annulus $\{ 1/2<|z|<2\} $, and its mollifications $\varphi _ j$, $j\ge 4$, are smooth on the neighborhood $\{ 3/4<|z|<3/2\} $ of $\partial B$ and converge to $\varphi $ uniformly on $\partial B$ (lemma 2.5.1(1)). Let $H_ j$ be the envelope for $\varphi _ j|_{\partial B}$; by Steps 1–3 it is continuous on $\overline B$ with boundary values $\varphi _ j$, plurisubharmonic, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ j)^ n=0$. Put $c_ j=\max _{\partial B}|\varphi _ j-\varphi |$. Since $H_ j-c_ j$ is a subsolution for $\varphi $, the class of subsolutions for $\varphi $ is nonempty, and its envelope $H$ satisfies $H_ j-c_ j\le H$; symmetrically, $H-c_ j\le H_ j$. Therefore $\sup _ B|H_ j-H|\le c_ j\to 0$, so $H$ is a uniform limit of continuous functions on $B$; it is continuous, extends continuously to $\overline B$ with the boundary values $\varphi $, and is plurisubharmonic, being equal to its regularization $H^*$, which is plurisubharmonic by theorem 5.10.2. By proposition 7.2.6, $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=\lim _ j(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ j)^ n=0$. Finally, if $H'$ and $H''$ are two solutions, then $H'-H''\to 0$ at $\partial B$, and the last assertion of theorem 7.3.11, applied in both directions, gives $H'=H''$. $\square$

Corollary 7.7.5.2E8B947536C1 Let $u$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, and let $B\Subset \Omega $ be a ball. There is a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\widehat u$ on $\Omega $ which equals $u$ outside $B$, satisfies $\widehat u\ge u$ on $B$, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widehat u)^ n=0$ on $B$. The function $\widehat u$ is increasing in $u$, and its restriction to $B$ is the largest plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ with $\varlimsup _{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le u(x)$ for every $x\in \partial B$.

Proof. Let $u_ j=u*\rho _{\varepsilon _ j}$, with $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ smaller than the distance from $\overline B$ to $\mathbb C^ n\setminus \Omega $, be radial regularizations of $u$; they are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ decreasing to $u$ (proposition 5.9.2). Let $H_ j$ be the solution of theorem 7.7.4 with boundary values $u_ j|_{\partial B}$, that is, the upper envelope of the subsolutions for $u_ j|_{\partial B}$. Since $u_ j$ is such a subsolution, $u\le u_ j\le H_ j$ on $B$. Since $u_{j+1}\le u_ j$ on $\partial B$, every subsolution for $u_{j+1}|_{\partial B}$ is one for $u_ j|_{\partial B}$, so the envelopes decrease: $H_{j+1}\le H_ j$. Let $H=\lim _ jH_ j$. The function $H_1$ is subharmonic on $B$ and continuous on $\overline B$ with boundary values $u_1$, so $H_1\le \max _{\partial B}u_1$ by the comparison principle (theorem 3.4.5). Hence $\inf _{\overline B}u\le H\le \max _{\partial B}u_1$, so $H$ is bounded; it is plurisubharmonic by theorem 5.10.1, and theorem 7.3.7 gives $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=\lim _ j(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H_ j)^ n=0$. For $x\in \partial B$, since $H\le H_ j$ and $H_ j$ is continuous on $\overline B$ with $H_ j=u_ j$ on $\partial B$,

\[ \varlimsup _{B\ni z\to x}H(z)\le u_ j(x)\quad \text{for every }j, \qquad \text{hence}\quad \varlimsup _{B\ni z\to x}H(z)\le u(x). \]

By proposition 5.9.5, the function $\widehat u$ equal to $\max \{ u,H\} =H$ on $B$ and to $u$ outside $B$ is plurisubharmonic on $\Omega $. It is locally bounded, since $H$ is bounded and $u$ is locally bounded; it equals $u$ outside $B$, satisfies $\widehat u\ge u$ on $B$, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widehat u)^ n=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}H)^ n=0$ on $B$ by locality.

Let $v$ be plurisubharmonic on $B$ with $\varlimsup _{B\ni z\to x}v(z)\le u(x)$ for $x\in \partial B$. Since $u\le u_ j$, the function $v$ is a subsolution for the boundary values $u_ j|_{\partial B}$, so $v\le H_ j$ by theorem 7.7.4. Letting $j\to \infty $ gives $v\le H$. Conversely, $H$ belongs to this class by the displayed estimate, so $H$ is its largest member. If $u\le u'$, the class for $u$ is contained in the class for $u'$, which proves that $\widehat u\le \widehat{u'}$. $\square$

Definition 7.7.6.FD8547B922C9 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be open. A plurisubharmonic function $u$ on $\Omega $ is maximal if every plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $\Omega $ with $v\le u$ outside a compact subset of $\Omega $ satisfies $v\le u$ everywhere.

Theorem 7.7.7.BCFDDA1FCB7A A locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $u$ on an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ is maximal if and only if $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=0$. In particular, maximality is a local property for locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions.

Proof. Suppose that $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=0$, and let $v$ be plurisubharmonic on $\Omega $ with $v\le u$ outside a compact set $K$. Choose a bounded open set $D$ with $K\subset D\Subset \Omega $. By upper semicontinuity, $v$ is bounded above on $\overline D$; since $u$ is bounded on $\overline D$, we may choose $M$ with $-M<\inf _ Du$. The function $\max \{ v,-M\} $ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $D$, and it is at most $u$ on $D\setminus K$; since every point of $D$ near $\partial D$ lies in $D\setminus K$, we get $\varliminf _{D\ni z\to x}(u(z)-\max \{ v(z),-M\} )\ge 0$ for $x\in \partial D$. The last assertion of theorem 7.3.11 therefore gives $\max \{ v,-M\} \le u$ on $D$. Hence $v\le u$ everywhere, and $u$ is maximal.

Conversely, suppose that $u$ is maximal, and let $B\Subset \Omega $ be a ball. The function $\widehat u$ of corollary 7.7.5 is plurisubharmonic, satisfies $\widehat u\ge u$, and equals $u$ outside the compact set $\overline B$. Maximality gives $\widehat u\le u$, so $\widehat u=u$ and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widehat u)^ n=0$ on $B$. Since the balls $B\Subset \Omega $ cover $\Omega $, and a distribution vanishing on each member of a family of open sets vanishes on their union (lemma 2.5.2), $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=0$ on $\Omega $. For the last assertion, let every point of $\Omega $ have an open neighborhood $U$ such that $u|_ U$ is maximal on $U$. By the first part applied on $U$, and by locality of the products (proposition 7.2.2), $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ vanishes on each such $U$, hence on $\Omega $, and $u$ is maximal on $\Omega $. Conversely, if $u$ is maximal on $\Omega $, then $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=0$ on every open set $U\subset \Omega $, so $u|_ U$ is maximal on $U$. $\square$
