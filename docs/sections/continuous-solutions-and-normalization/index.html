14.19 Continuous solutions and normalization080A6052D542

080A6052D542

Throughout this chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, $\omega $ is a Hermitian form and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form on $X$. As in section 14.1, we write $\omega _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$; for a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function $u$, the power $\omega _ u^ n$ is the Bedford–Taylor product of proposition 5.28.2. We fix $p>1$ and a nonnegative $f\in L^ p(X,\mathrm{d}V)$ with $\int _ Xf\, \mathrm{d}V>0$. Norms and almost-everywhere statements refer to $\mathrm{d}V$ unless another measure is specified.

We seek continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic solutions of $\omega _ u^ n=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$, with an unknown constant $c>0$, and of $\omega _ u^ n=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}f\, \mathrm{d}V$, with $\lambda >0$. Smooth positive approximations of $f$ give solutions by section 14.1. To pass to a limit, we need uniform control of both their constants and their potentials. Integration against a Gauduchon metric bounds the constants and hence the $L^ p$ norms of the resulting densities. Modified comparison then turns normalized $L^1$ compactness into uniform compactness, which identifies the limiting Monge–Ampère measure.

For the exponential equation, a weighted Gauduchon mean also controls the additive constants. Letting $\lambda $ decrease to zero determines the normalization constant $c$ from $f$, even when the normalized potential is not unique. When a bounded solution has a smooth positive density depending on the point and the potential, proper exponential equations with a frozen smooth approximation of that potential give a trace estimate and prove that the solution is smooth.

On the Hopf threefold, the constant has a sharp lower bound, with equality exactly for densities pulled back from the projective plane. For densities of unit integral with a common $L^ p$ bound, $1<p<\infty $, near equality forces normalized potentials to approach their fiber averages uniformly and gives weak convergence in $L^ p$ for the density differences. Smooth oscillating examples show why strong $L^1$ convergence of the densities need not follow.

For $n\geq 2$, the final two sections fix a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\chi $ and study concave functions of the eigenvalues of $\omega ^{-1}(\chi +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)$. An admissible reference with a strict asymptotic gap controls an auxiliary Monge–Ampère comparison, giving stability as positive right-hand sides tend to zero. Equicontinuous data then give uniform compactness. Nearby smooth admissible functions yield a gradient bound if the first derivatives of the data are uniformly bounded.

14.19.1 Continuous solvability49F0FF1AED46

There are two natural ways to normalize the prescribed density: by an unknown positive constant, or by an exponential of the potential.

Theorem 14.19.2 (Kołodziej–Nguyen).C8D798C2D2F0 There are $c>0$ and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap C(X)$ such that

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=c f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

The potential can be normalized by $\sup _ Xu=0$.

The constant $c$ is uniquely determined by $f$, by proposition 14.19.34. The normalized potential, however, need not be unique, even for a smooth density; see theorem 14.26.1.

14.19.5 The modified comparison principle88B9A2247D18

For a Hermitian background, the total Monge–Ampère mass varies with the potential, and the ordinary comparison principle fails in general (see 14.25.3). A weaker form survives. Replacing the comparison potential $v$ by $(1-\varepsilon )v$ leaves the strictly positive form $\varepsilon \omega $ in reserve, and this reserve absorbs the torsion terms. On a sublevel set of height $s$, integration by parts makes the error proportional to $s$, and a finite induction controls the intermediate mixed products. The resulting multiplicative correction tends to one as $s$ tends to zero with $\varepsilon $ fixed.

Since $X$ is compact and $\omega $ is positive, we can choose $B_0\geq 1$ such that

\[ -B_0\omega ^2\leq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \leq B_0\omega ^2, \qquad -B_0\omega ^3\leq \mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \leq B_0\omega ^3. \]

These inequalities are understood after wedging with positive forms of complementary degree; forms of degree larger than $2n$ vanish, and the corresponding terms below are omitted.

Theorem 14.19.6.492E047184B3 Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$ and $0<\varepsilon <1$. For $s>0$, put

\[ m_\varepsilon =\inf _ X\bigl (u-(1-\varepsilon )v\bigr ),\qquad U_{\varepsilon ,s}=\{ u<(1-\varepsilon )v+m_\varepsilon +s\} . \]

If $0<s<\varepsilon ^3/(32B_0n^2)$, then, with $a=B_0n^2s\varepsilon ^{-3}$,

\begin{equation} (1-4a)^ n\int _{U_{\varepsilon ,s}}\omega _{(1-\varepsilon )v}^{\, n} \leq \int _{U_{\varepsilon ,s}}\omega _ u^ n. \end{equation}

In particular, the reverse factor $(1-4a)^{-n}$ tends to one as $s\downarrow 0$ for fixed $\varepsilon $.

Proof. Write $v_\varepsilon =(1-\varepsilon )v$, $U=U_{\varepsilon ,s}$ and

\[ \phi =\max (u,v_\varepsilon +m_\varepsilon +s),\qquad T_ k=\omega _ u^ k\wedge \omega _\phi ^{n-k},\qquad I_ k=\int _ U T_ k\quad (0\leq k\leq n). \]

Set $I_{-1}=I_{-2}=0$. Then $0\leq \phi -u\leq s$, this difference vanishes outside $U$, and on $U$ plurifine locality gives $\omega _\phi =\omega _{v_\varepsilon }\geq \varepsilon \omega $ in all bounded products.

By lemma 5.28.3, for every fixed positive mixed product $W$ of $n-1$ bounded-potential factors,

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u=\phi \} }\omega _ u\wedge W \leq \mathbf1_{\{ u=\phi \} }\omega _\phi \wedge W. \]

For $0\leq k\leq n-1$, apply this with $W=\omega _ u^ k\wedge \omega _\phi ^{n-k-1}$. Since $u=\phi $ on $X\setminus U$, the difference $T_ k-T_{k+1}=(\omega _\phi -\omega _ u)\wedge W$ is nonnegative there. Consequently

\begin{equation} I_ k-I_{k+1} \leq \int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\phi -u)\wedge W =\int _ X(\phi -u)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W. \end{equation}

This integration by parts is valid for bounded potentials. To see this, approximate $u$ and $\phi $ by decreasing sequences of smooth, uniformly bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions, using theorem 5.32.1. For smooth functions the identity is ordinary integration by parts. The form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W$ expands as below into bounded Hessian products and smooth forms, and bounded Bedford–Taylor convergence, also against bounded quasi-continuous coefficients converging in capacity, passes each term to the limit.

Set $r=n-k-1$. Since $\mathrm{d}\omega _ u=\mathrm{d}\omega _\phi =\mathrm{d}\omega $, expansion gives

\[ \begin{split} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}W={}& k\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \omega _ u^{k-1}\wedge \omega _\phi ^ r +r\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \omega _ u^ k\wedge \omega _\phi ^{r-1}\\ & +k(k-1)\mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \omega _ u^{k-2}\wedge \omega _\phi ^ r\\ & +2kr\, \mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \omega _ u^{k-1}\wedge \omega _\phi ^{r-1}\\ & +r(r-1)\mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \omega _ u^ k\wedge \omega _\phi ^{r-2}. \end{split} \]

Terms containing a negative power have coefficient zero and are omitted. On $U$ we may replace each $\omega ^ j$ in an upper bound by $\varepsilon ^{-j}\omega _\phi ^ j$, since $\omega _\phi \geq \varepsilon \omega $ there. Using $\phi -u\leq s$ and $\varepsilon ^{-2}\leq \varepsilon ^{-3}$ in (14.19.8), we obtain

\[ I_ k-I_{k+1}\leq B_0s\varepsilon ^{-3} \bigl (k(k-1)I_{k-2}+k(1+2r)I_{k-1}+r^2I_ k\bigr ). \]

Each coefficient is at most $n^2$, and $a=B_0n^2s\varepsilon ^{-3}$. Hence

\begin{equation} I_ k-I_{k+1}\leq a(I_{k-2}+I_{k-1}+I_ k). \end{equation}

Put $q=1-4a$; the assumption on $s$ gives $a<1/32$, so $q>7/8$. Suppose inductively that $qI_ j\leq I_{j+1}$ for $j<k$. Then $I_{k-1}\leq q^{-1}I_ k$ and $I_{k-2}\leq q^{-2}I_ k$ whenever the indices exist. Since

\[ 1+q^{-1}+q^{-2}\leq 1+8/7+64/49<4, \]

(14.19.9) gives $qI_ k\leq I_{k+1}$. The case $k=0$ follows from the same formula with the two absent terms set to zero. By induction, $q^ n I_0\leq I_ n$. Since $T_0=\omega _{v_\varepsilon }^ n$ on $U$ and $T_ n=\omega _ u^ n$, this is the theorem. In dimension one all torsion terms vanish and the same argument applies. $\square$

A strict multiplicative inequality between the Monge–Ampère measures on $\{ u<v\} $ absorbs the error in modified comparison. One first chooses the positivity reserve $\varepsilon $, and then a sublevel set thin enough to preserve the strict inequality.

Theorem 14.19.10.C0DEE578BC5D Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$, and let $0\leq c<1$. If

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\omega _ u^ n \leq c\, \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }\omega _ v^ n, \]

then $u\geq v$. In particular, $\omega _ u^ n(\{ u<v\} )=0$ implies $u\geq v$.

Proof. Suppose $m=\inf _ X(u-v)<0$. Choose $\varepsilon >0$ so small that

\[ (1-\varepsilon )^ n>c, \qquad 2\varepsilon \| v\| _\infty <-m/4, \]

omitting the second restriction if $v=0$. Let $m_\varepsilon $ and $U_{\varepsilon ,s}$ be defined as in theorem 14.19.6 for this pair $u,v$. Since $|m_\varepsilon -m|\leq \varepsilon \| v\| _\infty $, for all sufficiently small $s>0$ the set $U=U_{\varepsilon ,s}$ is contained in $\{ u<v\} $: there

\[ u-v<m_\varepsilon +s-\varepsilon v \leq m+2\varepsilon \| v\| _\infty +s<0. \]

The set $U$ is nonempty by the definition of the infimum and has positive volume by lemma 5.24.4.

Decrease $s$ further so that theorem 14.19.6 applies and $(1-4B_0n^2s\varepsilon ^{-3})^ n(1-\varepsilon )^ n>c$. Write the first factor as $q^ n$. Positivity of every mixed term gives

\[ \omega _{(1-\varepsilon )v}^ n \geq (1-\varepsilon )^ n\omega _ v^ n+\varepsilon ^ n\omega ^ n. \]

Modified comparison and the assumption would therefore imply

\[ q^ n(1-\varepsilon )^ n\int _ U\omega _ v^ n +q^ n\varepsilon ^ n\int _ U\omega ^ n \leq \int _ U\omega _ u^ n\leq c\int _ U\omega _ v^ n. \]

All integrals are finite, so the choice $q^ n(1-\varepsilon )^ n>c$ and the positive volume of $U$ make this impossible. Hence $m\geq 0$. $\square$

Corollary 14.19.11.7C5FAB3BA5DA Every bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function has strictly positive Monge–Ampère mass.

Proof. Let $u$ be a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function. If $\omega _ u^ n=0$, then theorem 14.19.10, applied with $c=0$ and the constant potential $v=\sup _ Xu+1$, would give $u\geq v$, a contradiction. $\square$

The positive lower bound in corollary 14.19.11 depends on the potential. A bound that is uniform over all bounded potentials is the condition $v_-(\omega )>0$ of definition 13.14.14.

Corollary 14.19.12.AB62A0258D5D If $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$ and $\lambda >0$, then

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda v}\omega _ v^ n\geq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda u}\omega _ u^ n \quad \Longrightarrow \quad v\leq u. \]

Proof. For every $t>0$, the assumption gives $\omega _ u^ n\leq \mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\omega _ v^ n$ on $\{ u<v-t\} $. Since $\omega _{v-t}=\omega _ v$, theorem 14.19.10, applied to $u$ and $v-t$, gives $u\geq v-t$. Letting $t\downarrow 0$ proves $v\leq u$. $\square$

14.19.13 Capacity estimates and uniform compactnessEF206AD1915F

Let $\operatorname {Cap}=\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ denote the capacity of section 5.29. Modified comparison bounds the capacity of a thin sublevel set by the Monge–Ampère mass of a slightly larger one. A bound by a power $1+\alpha $ of capacity, $\alpha >0$, makes the resulting iteration converge. For an $L^ p$ density, corollary 5.29.5 supplies the case $\alpha =1$. Iterating the two inequalities gives a lower bound for the capacity of every sublevel set of fixed height above the minimum. Since the capacity of deep sublevel sets of normalized potentials is small by the tail estimate (5.29.4), this bounds the depth of a normalized solution.

Lemma 14.19.14.370AD0860DAD Let $u,v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap L^\infty (X)$, with $-H\leq v\leq 0$ and $H\geq 1$. Fix $0<\varepsilon <1$, and put

\[ m=\inf _ X(u-(1-\varepsilon )v),\qquad U_ s=\{ u<(1-\varepsilon )v+m+s\} . \]

There are $\delta _\varepsilon >0$, depending only on $(X,\omega ,\varepsilon )$, and $C_ H>0$, depending only on $(n,H)$, such that

F937FDFBF165 14.19.15
\begin{equation} t^ n\operatorname {Cap}(U_ s)\leq C_ H\int _{U_{s+t}}\omega _ u^ n \qquad (0<s,t,\ s+t<\delta _\varepsilon ). \end{equation}

Every $U_ s$, $s>0$, has positive capacity.

Proof. Let $B_0$ be the constant of theorem 14.19.6, and set

\[ \delta _\varepsilon =\min \{ 1-\varepsilon ,\varepsilon ^3/(128B_0n^2)\} . \]

For a capacity test $0\leq \rho \leq 1$, put

\[ \tau =\frac{t}{2(1-\varepsilon )(H+1)},\quad w=(1-\tau )v+\tau \rho ,\quad m'=\inf _ X(u-(1-\varepsilon )w). \]

Then $0<\tau <1$ and $0\leq (1-\varepsilon )(w-v)\leq t/2$, so $m-t/2\leq m'\leq m$. Consequently

\[ U_ s\subset V:=\{ u<(1-\varepsilon )w+m'+s+t/2\} \subset U_{s+t}. \]

We apply modified comparison to $u,w$ at height $s+t/2<\delta _\varepsilon $; by the choice of $\delta _\varepsilon $, its inverse factor is at most $(8/7)^ n$. Moreover,

\[ \omega _{(1-\varepsilon )w} \geq (1-\varepsilon )\tau \omega _\rho =\frac{t}{2(H+1)}\omega _\rho . \]

Integrating over $U_ s\subset V$, applying modified comparison on $V$, and taking the supremum over $\rho $, we obtain (14.19.15) with $C_ H=[2(H+1)]^ n(8/7)^ n$. Finally, each $U_ s$ is nonempty by the definition of the infimum, so it has positive smooth volume by lemma 5.24.4, and hence positive capacity, as the constant test function shows. $\square$

Suppose that two uniformly bounded solutions are far apart at some point. The preceding estimates give a fixed positive lower bound for the capacity of a sublevel set of their difference. If their $L^1$ distance is small, this set has small volume, and the uniform $L^ p$ bound for the densities then makes its Monge–Ampère mass small. This contradicts the sublevel estimate (14.19.15), and so upgrades $L^1$ compactness to uniform compactness. Put $p'=p/(p-1)$.

Theorem 14.19.21.BF849D15DF41 Suppose $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap C(X)$, $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$, and

\[ \omega _{u_ j}^ n=F_ j\, \mathrm{d}V,\qquad F_ j\geq 0,\qquad \sup _ j\| F_ j\| _ p<\infty \]

for the fixed $p>1$. If $(u_ j)$ is Cauchy in $L^1(X,\mathrm{d}V)$, then it is Cauchy uniformly. In particular, every such family has a subsequence converging uniformly to a continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function.

\[ S:=\inf _ X(u_ k-u_ j)\leq -a. \]

\[ \begin{split} D^ n\operatorname {Cap}(U_ D) & \leq C_ H\int _{U_{2D}}F_ k\, \mathrm{d}V\\ & \leq C_ H\| F_ k\| _ p \left(\frac{2\| u_ k-u_ j\| _1}{a}\right)^{1/p'} \longrightarrow 0. \end{split} \]

This contradiction proves that the sequence is uniformly Cauchy. For the last assertion, the normalized compactness theorem, theorem 5.25.2, gives an $L^1$-Cauchy subsequence of any such family. Its uniform limit is continuous, and distributional convergence preserves $\omega $-plurisubharmonicity. $\square$

The capacity lower bound of lemma 14.19.16 turns an integral error against the solution measure into a uniform error. The argument uses only the power capacity bound on this measure and therefore applies to singular measures as well.

\[ \xi _ i=a(\underline U_ i-V_ i)+(-a+2\beta w)(U_ i-V_ i), \qquad |-a+2\beta w|\leq a+4. \]

Writing $D=|\partial (U-V)|_ B^2$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\begin{align*} \left|2\operatorname{Re}\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ iU_ i\xi _{\bar i}\right| & \leq \frac18\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ i^2+C_ aK_ V^4T+C_ a(Q+K_ V^2)D,\\ \left|2\operatorname{Re}\sum _{i,k}B_ i\chi _{i\bar k}U_ k\xi _{\bar i}\right| & \leq \tfrac 12QD+C_ aT+C_ aK_ VQ^{1/2}T. \end{align*}

For the first line, split $\xi _ i$ as above and use $\sum B_ i|U_ i|^2\leq 2D+2K_ V^2T$; then apply Young's inequality to the resulting three products, each absorbing at most $\frac1{24}\sum B_ i\lambda _ i^2$. For the second line, use $|\partial \xi |_ B\leq aK_ VT^{1/2}+(a+4)D^{1/2}$ and the bound for $\chi $. In both lines, $C_ a$ depends on $a$ but not on $\beta $ or on the derivatives of $V$.

By lemma 14.19.48 and $w\leq 0$,

\[ \mathcal L\xi \geq a\mathcal L(\underline U-U)-4H_0+2\beta D. \]

Choose $a$ so large that $(a\kappa -C_ g-2)\tau >4H_0+2H_1+1$, and then $\beta \geq 2C_ a+2$. Combining the preceding bounds with lemma 14.19.49, we obtain, at every point where $Q\geq C'(1+K_ V^4)$ for a suitable constant $C'$,

\begin{align*} \mathrm e^{-\xi }\mathcal L(\mathrm e^\xi Q) & \geq \frac18\sum _ iB_ i\lambda _ i^2\\ & \quad +Q\bigl [a\mathcal L(\underline U-U)-(C_ g+2)T +\beta D-(4H_0+2H_1)\bigr ]. \end{align*}

Indeed, the coefficient of $D$ before absorption is $(2\beta -C_ a-1/2)Q-C_ aK_ V^2$, which is at least $\beta Q$ once $C'$ is large enough. The other errors satisfy $C_ aK_ V^4\leq Q$ and $C_ a+C_ aK_ VQ^{1/2}\leq Q$, and they account for the additional term $-2T$ in the bracket.

Consider a maximum point of $\mathrm e^\xi Q$ at which $Q\geq C'(1+K_ V^4)$. In the first alternative of lemma 14.19.48, the bracket is positive by the choice of $a$, since $T\geq \tau $. In the second, $D\geq \kappa T|\partial (U-V)|^2$. For $Q\geq 4(1+K_ V^2)$, the triangle inequality gives $|\partial (U-V)|^2\geq c_*Q$, where $c_*=(\sqrt3-1)^2/4$. Since $\mathcal L(\underline U-U)\geq -H_0$, the bracket is then at least

\[ (\beta \kappa c_*Q-C_ g-2)T-(a+4)H_0-2H_1. \]

This is positive once $C'$ is large enough, because $T\geq \tau $. In both cases $\mathcal L(\mathrm e^\xi Q)>0$ at a maximum point, which contradicts the second derivative test. Thus $Q\leq C(1+K_ V^4)$ at the maximum point. The weight has uniformly bounded oscillation:

\[ \operatorname {osc}_ X\xi \leq a(\operatorname {osc}_ X\underline U+\operatorname {osc}_ XU)+4/\beta . \]

By the potential bound in proposition 14.19.44, this oscillation is bounded independently of $V$, so the estimate at the maximum point gives $Q\leq C(1+K_ V^4)$ everywhere. Taking square roots and setting $\eta =\beta ^{-1}$ proves the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 14.19.51.979B56319AA1 There is a constant $C$, depending only on the fixed data and $H_1$, such that $\| \mathrm du\| _{L^\infty (\omega )}\leq C$ for every normalized solution $u$ whose right-hand side $h$ lies in the family and satisfies $\| \partial h\| _\infty \leq H_1$. In particular, the bound is uniform as the positive right-hand sides tend to zero.

Proof. The right-hand sides in the family with $\| \partial h\| _\infty \leq H_1$ form an equicontinuous set. By corollary 14.19.46, the corresponding functions $U$ form a precompact subset of $C^0(X)$. Choose a finite $\eta $-net in this set, consisting of smooth admissible functions $V_1,\ldots ,V_ N$. Each solution is within $\eta $ of some $V_ j$, so proposition 14.19.50 bounds its gradient by $C(1+\max _ j\| \partial V_ j\| _\infty ^2)$. The set of solutions, and hence the chosen finite net, depends only on the fixed data and $H_1$. Finally, $u=2\pi U$, so the same conclusion holds for $\mathrm du$. $\square$
