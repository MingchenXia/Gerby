15.17 Local Hölder continuity in a big background1A5D83E43B0A

1A5D83E43B0A

We now combine the stability estimate above $\rho $ with the regularization of lemma 5.42.10, whose curvature error is linear in the regularization parameter. As in the proof of theorem 15.5.1, a small multiple of the strict barrier absorbs the curvature error of the transforms, and a lower bound for the minimizing radius converts the estimate for the transforms into an estimate for the smoothing on compact subsets of $\Omega $. This gives a local Hölder modulus for solutions with minimal singularities.

Theorem 15.17.1.D662421F3436 Fix $C_0>0$. Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy

\[ \sup _ Xu=0,\qquad V_\theta -C_0\leq u\leq V_\theta , \qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=f\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$ and $p>1$. Then $u$ is locally Hölder continuous on $\Omega $, with every exponent

\[ 0<\beta <\frac{2(p-1)}{np+3(p-1)}. \]

For fixed bounds on $C_0$ and $\| f\| _ p$, the Hölder constants are uniform on each fixed compact subset of $\Omega $.

Proof. Since $u\geq V_\theta -C_0\geq \rho -C_0$, we may subtract a constant from the strict barrier so that $\rho \leq u-1$; it still satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \kappa \omega $ for a fixed $\kappa >0$. Choose $0<\gamma <(p-1)/(np+p-1)$ and set $\alpha =2\gamma <1$. Apply lemma 5.42.10 with $a=u$, and let $A,K,C$ and $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ be the constants and transforms given there. For small $\delta >0$ set

\[ c=\delta ^\alpha ,\qquad e=Ac+C\delta ,\qquad s=e/\kappa , \qquad v_\delta =\frac{\Phi _{c,\delta }+s\rho }{1+s}. \]

By the curvature bound of lemma 5.42.10, $v_\delta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The lower bound for $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ in that lemma implies $\rho \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }$ when $\delta $ is small. Thus

\[ v_\delta \leq \Phi _{c,\delta }\leq R_\delta u\leq 0. \]

In particular $v_\delta \leq V_\theta $, so theorem 15.15.1 applies with the reference $V_\theta $ and the comparison potential $v_\delta $. Together with the $L^1$ smoothing estimate, it gives

\[ \sup _\Omega (v_\delta -u)_+ \leq C\| (R_\delta u-u)_+\| _1^\gamma \leq C\delta ^\alpha . \]

Fix a compact subset of $\Omega $ on which $\rho \geq -M$. Since $s\leq C\delta ^\alpha $ and $u\leq 0$, the identity

\[ \Phi _{c,\delta }-u=(1+s)(v_\delta -u)+s(u-\rho ) \]

gives $\Phi _{c,\delta }-u\leq C_ M\delta ^\alpha $ there.

At a radius $t$ where the infimum defining $\Phi _{c,\delta }$ is attained, the monotone lower bound yields

\[ -c\log (t/\delta )\leq \Phi _{c,\delta }-u+K(\delta ^2+\delta ) \leq C_ Mc. \]

Hence $t\geq a_ M\delta $ for a fixed $a_ M>0$. As in the proof of theorem 15.5.1, monotonicity of the smoothing now gives

\[ R_{a_ M\delta }u-u\leq C_ M\delta ^\alpha , \qquad R_{a_ M\delta }u-u\geq -K(a_ M\delta )^2 \]

on the chosen compact set. Since every small radius has the form $a_ M\delta $, we obtain $|R_ tu-u|\leq C_ Mt^\alpha $ there for all small $t>0$, with constants uniform on that compact set. We apply this to a slightly larger compact neighborhood, on which also $\| \nabla R_ tu\| \leq C_ M/t$. For nearby points $x,y$ at distance $d>0$, the smooth function $R_ tu$ and a short path in that neighborhood give

\[ |u(x)-u(y)|\leq C_ M(t^\alpha +d/t). \]

The choice $t=d^{1/(1+\alpha )}$ gives the exponent $\alpha /(1+\alpha )$, and letting $\gamma $ approach its upper endpoint gives the asserted range. Pairs of points farther apart are handled by the boundedness of $u$ on the compact neighborhood. $\square$

It suffices that the equation hold on $\Omega $. Suppose that $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V$ on $\Omega $, where $c>0$ and $0\leq f\in L^ p(X,\mathrm{d}V)$, and that $u$ satisfies the same bounds as in the theorem. The lower bound $u\geq V_\theta -C_0\geq \rho -C_0$ shows that $u$ is locally bounded on $\Omega $, so there $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$ is the local bounded product. Since $Z$ has zero volume and is not charged by $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)$, the equation holds on $X$, and the theorem applies with $cf$ in place of $f$. The uniform estimates then depend on a bound for $\| cf\| _ p$, which includes the constant $c$.

15.17.2 Limits of normalized exponential solutionsB0C9769ACDCD

For the normalized exponential family $(v_\lambda ,c_\lambda )$ of lemma 15.16.5, the reference interval $[V_\theta -B,V_\theta ]$ is uniform in $0<\lambda \leq 1$. Stability therefore controls differences on all of $\Omega $, and the local Hölder estimates strengthen the convergence on compact subsets. We fix the nonzero density $0\leq f\in L^ p(\mathrm{d}V)$, $p>1$, and use the constants $B,C,m$ of that lemma.

Proposition 15.17.3.D11439C1393F For the family $(v_\lambda ,c_\lambda )$ of lemma 15.16.5, the normalized potentials are relatively compact for uniform convergence of differences on $\Omega $. As $\lambda \downarrow 0$, every cluster limit $v$ is normalized, has minimal singularities, and satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=cf\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

where $c>0$ is the unique constant of theorem 15.16.7. Moreover,

\[ c_\lambda \longrightarrow c, \qquad c_\lambda \mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }f \longrightarrow cf\quad \text{in }L^ p(\mathrm{d}V). \]

Every sequence of parameters in $(0,1]$ has a subsequence for which the potentials converge in $C^\beta $ on each compact subset of $\Omega $, for all

\[ 0<\beta <\frac{2(p-1)}{np+3(p-1)}. \]

Proof. By lemma 15.16.5, all $v_\lambda $ lie in a common reference interval, and their densities are bounded in $L^ p$ by $C\| f\| _ p$. We may therefore apply corollary 15.15.3 with $\eta =V_\theta $. Given any sequence $\lambda _ j\downarrow 0$, we pass to a subsequence with $v_{\lambda _ j}\to v$ uniformly in differences on $\Omega $ and in $L^1(X)$, and with $c_{\lambda _ j}\to c_*\in [m,C]$. The limit satisfies the same reference bounds and is normalized. Off the pole set,

\[ 0\leq 1-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda } \leq 1-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda (V_\theta -B)}\longrightarrow 0 \]

as $\lambda \downarrow 0$. Multiplying by $f$ and using dominated convergence, we obtain $\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda }f\to f$ in $L^ p$ for the whole family. The compactness corollary therefore identifies the limit equation as $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=c_*f\, \mathrm{d}V$, and the uniqueness of the constant gives $c_*=c$. Since every subsequential limit of $c_\lambda $ as $\lambda \downarrow 0$ equals $c$, we conclude that $c_\lambda \to c$, and the stated convergence of the densities follows.

Finally, theorem 15.17.1 gives uniform local $C^\alpha $ bounds for every $\alpha $ below its endpoint, and the uniform limit inherits these bounds by pointwise convergence. For $0<\beta <\alpha $, uniform convergence and the elementary interpolation inequality on smaller coordinate balls give

\[ [v_{\lambda _ j}-v]_{C^\beta } \leq C\| v_{\lambda _ j}-v\| _\infty ^{1-\beta /\alpha } [v_{\lambda _ j}-v]_{C^\alpha }^{\beta /\alpha } \longrightarrow 0, \]

where the Hölder bounds are taken on slightly larger compact neighborhoods. The uniformly convergent subsequence given by compactness thus converges in $C^\beta $ on every compact subset of $\Omega $ and for every exponent below the endpoint; no further extraction depending on the exponent is needed. $\square$

Corollary 15.17.4.64893D9DABFE Suppose a group $G$ of biholomorphisms of $X$ preserves $\theta $ and the measure $f\, \mathrm{d}V$. Then every $v_\lambda $ and every cluster limit as $\lambda \downarrow 0$ is $G$-invariant. If the normalized zero-exponent equation has only one $G$-invariant solution with minimal singularities, then the whole family $v_\lambda $ converges to it as $\lambda \downarrow 0$, uniformly in differences on $\Omega $ and in each local $C^\beta $ topology of the proposition.

Proof. For $g\in G$, pullback by $g$ preserves $\theta $-plurisubharmonicity, and $V_\theta \circ g=V_\theta $ by the definition of the envelope. Pullback also preserves the positive-exponent equation, so its uniqueness gives $u_\lambda \circ g=u_\lambda $, and the normalization preserves this equality. Pullback by a fixed smooth biholomorphism is continuous on $L^1(X)$, since its Jacobian relative to the fixed smooth volume is bounded. Hence every cluster limit is invariant almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere for the canonical representatives. This argument does not require $g$ to preserve the barrier $\rho $ or the set $\Omega $.

If the invariant solution is unique, every cluster limit is the same potential. Relative compactness then implies convergence of the whole family for uniform convergence of differences on $\Omega $: a sequence staying at a fixed positive distance from that potential would have a convergent subsequence, a contradiction. The interpolation argument above upgrades this to the asserted local $C^\beta $ convergence. $\square$

The proposition gives compactness and identifies the equation of every cluster limit, and the symmetry criterion is one way to identify the limit itself. In general, theorem 11.44.2 shows that the whole family converges, and it describes the limit through the greatest subsolution of the zero-exponent equation that satisfies a family of integral normalizations determined by how the constant $c$ depends on the density. This holds even when the zero-exponent equation has several normalized solutions.
