14.3 Test curves

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

9 Test curves

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

A geodesic ray $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in [0,\infty )}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ records the asymptotic behavior of singularities along a path of potentials. In this chapter we encode the same information in the dual variable $\tau $. Instead of recording the potential at time $t$, the Legendre transform records, for each slope $\tau $, the model potential determined by the part of the ray growing at least linearly with slope $\tau $. This produces a concave decreasing family $\tau \mapsto \Gamma _\tau $ of model potentials; more precisely, a test curve $\Gamma $ is a concave decreasing family $(\Gamma _\tau )_{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}$ of model potentials in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

The central result is a correspondence initiated by Ross and Witt Nyström
Note. Witt and Nyström are both family names of a single person. Some Swedes have double family names. It should not be spelled as Witt-Nyström as some people do in the literature.
. It is the pluripotential-theoretic analogue of Legendre duality: a test curve gives a geodesic ray by taking a Legendre transform, and a suitable geodesic ray gives back a test curve by the inverse transform. Thus test curves provide an intrinsic language for the singularity data carried by geodesic rays.

We first develop test curves as self-contained objects. In section 9.1 we introduce their basic properties, including independence of the auxiliary form, behavior under bimeromorphic morphisms, closedness in the parameter $\tau $, log-concavity of the mass function and the $P$-equivalence relation on test curves. The Ross–Witt Nyström correspondence is proved in section 9.2, where the Legendre transform is shown to give a bijection between test curves and geodesic rays.

The later sections adapt this picture to the singularity types needed elsewhere in the book. In section 9.3 we develop the $\mathcal I$-model version of the theory, including the $\mathcal I$-envelope of a test curve, the transition from $P$-model to $\mathcal I$-model test curves, and the corresponding notion of maximal geodesic ray. Finally, section 9.4 studies sums, translations, maxima, suprema, infima and rescalings of test curves. These operations are needed in the non-Archimedean theory of chapter 13, where test curves provide the analytic input for the construction of non-Archimedean potentials.

Throughout the chapter we freely use the convex-analytic results of chapter A. Those results are stated for convex functions; when they are applied to concave functions here, they are applied to the negatives.

9.1 The notion of test curves

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class.

Recall that the notion of model potentials is defined in definition 3.1.10.

Definition 9.1.1. A test curve $\Gamma $ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ consists of a real number $\Gamma _{\max }$ together with a map $(-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ denoted by $\tau \mapsto \Gamma _{\tau }$ satisfying the following conditions:

  1. The map $\tau \mapsto \Gamma _{\tau }$ is concave and decreasing;

  2. $P_{\theta }[\Gamma _{\tau }]=\Gamma _{\tau }$;

  3. the potential

    9.1
    \begin{equation} \Gamma _{-\infty }\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma _{\tau } \end{equation}

    satisfies

    \[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{-\infty }\right)^ n>0. \]

Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be a model potential. The set of test curves $\Gamma $ with $\Gamma _{-\infty }=\phi $ is denoted by $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

The union of all $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$'s for various model potentials $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ is denoted by $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

By 2, $\sup _{X}\Gamma _{\tau }=0$ for each $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. So $\Gamma _{-\infty }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by proposition 1.2.2.

Thanks to corollary 2.4.9 and 3, there is $C<\Gamma _{\max }$ so that whenever $\tau <C$, we have $\Gamma _{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then it follows that $\Gamma _{-\infty }$ is a model potential by proposition 3.1.28.

Remark 9.1.2. Sometimes it is convenient to extend $\Gamma _{\tau }$ to $\tau \geq \Gamma _{\max }$ as well. This can be done as follows: for $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$, we set $\Gamma _{\tau }\equiv -\infty $. For $\tau =\Gamma _{\max }$, we set

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }\coloneqq \inf _{\tau '<\Gamma _{\max }}\Gamma _{\tau '}\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

Note that $\Gamma _{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by corollary 1.5.6.

In general, for any $x\in X$, the map $\tau \mapsto \Gamma _{\tau }(x)$ falls into one of the following three categories:

  1. $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)\equiv -\infty $;

  2. $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)>-\infty $ for all $\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }$;

  3. there is $\tau (x)\in (-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max }]$ so that $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)>-\infty $ for all $\tau \in (-\infty ,\tau (x))$, and $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)=-\infty $ for all $\tau \geq \tau (x)$.

The last two possibilities are illustrated in 9.1.

Note that 2 is the generic situation. It occurs whenever $x\notin \{ \Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}=-\infty \} $. In particular, when this situation occurs, $(-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })\ni \tau \mapsto \Gamma _{\tau }(x)$ is finite concave, and hence continuous. As a consequence, for any $\tau \in (-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })$, we have

9.2
\begin{equation} \Gamma _{\tau }=\inf _{\tau '<\tau }\Gamma _{\tau '},\quad \Gamma _{\tau }=\operatorname *{sup}_{\tau '\in (\tau ,\Gamma _{\max })}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma _{\tau '} \end{equation}

almost everywhere and hence everywhere due to proposition 1.2.6.

Recall that according to our general principle, we speak of model potentials only when a potential has positive mass.

Lemma 9.1.3. Assume that $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then for each $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

9.3
\begin{equation} \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{\tau }\right)^ n>0. \end{equation}

In particular, $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is a model potential in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

The notations in the proof below are summarized in 9.2.

Proof. Fix $\tau \in (-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })$.

By assumption, $\Gamma _{-\infty }$ has positive mass. By corollary 2.4.9, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{-\infty }}^ n=\lim _{\tau \to -\infty }\int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau }}^ n. \]

In particular, for a sufficiently small $\tau _0<\tau $, we have

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau _0}}^ n>0. \]

Now take $\tau '\in (\tau , \Gamma _{\max })$ and $t\in (0,1)$ so that

\[ \tau =(1-t)\tau '+t\tau _0. \]

From the concavity of $\Gamma $, we find that

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }\geq (1-t)\Gamma _{\tau '}+t\Gamma _{\tau _0}. \]

By the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 and the multi-linearity of the non-pluripolar products proposition 2.2.2 6,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau }}^ n\geq \int _ X \theta _{(1-t)\Gamma _{\tau '}+t\Gamma _{\tau _0}}^ n\geq t^ n \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau _0}}^ n>0 \]

and (9.3) follows. $\square$

Proposition 9.1.4. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then the map

\[ [-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })\rightarrow \mathbb {R},\quad \tau \mapsto \log \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau }}^ n \]

is continuous and concave on $(-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })$.

Proof. The concavity of this function follows from theorem 2.4.4 and theorem 2.4.8. The continuity at $-\infty $ is a consequence of corollary 2.4.9. The continuity on $(-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })$ follows from the fact that finite concave functions on open intervals are continuous. $\square$

Definition 9.1.5. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be a model potential.

A test curve $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ is said to be bounded if for $\tau $ small enough, $\Gamma _{\tau }=\phi $. The subset of bounded test curves in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ is denoted by $\mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )$. In this case, we write

9.4
\begin{equation} \Gamma _{\min }\coloneqq \sup \{ \tau \in \mathbb {R}:\Gamma _{\tau }=\phi \} . \end{equation}

A test curve $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ is said to have finite energy if

9.5
\begin{equation} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma )\coloneqq \Gamma _{\max } \int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n+\int _{-\infty }^{\Gamma _{\max }} \left(\int _ X\theta _{\Gamma _{\tau }}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n\right)\, \mathrm{d}\tau >-\infty . \end{equation}

When $\phi =V_{\theta }$, we write $\mathbf{E}$ instead of $\mathbf{E}^{\phi }$.

The subset of test curves with finite energy in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ is denoted by $\mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Example 9.1.6. Given $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, there is a canonically associated test curve $\Gamma ^{\varphi }\in \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;V_{\theta })$: Set $\Gamma ^{\varphi }_{\max }=0$ and

\[ \Gamma ^{\varphi }_{\tau }= \begin{cases} V_{\theta },& \textup{if }\tau \leq -1;\\ P_{\theta }\left[ (1+\tau )\varphi -\tau V_{\theta } \right],& \textup{if }-1<\tau <0. \end{cases} \]

Note that $\Gamma ^{\varphi }$ is indeed a test curve, as follows from proposition 3.1.24. Indeed, the concavity and monotonicity in $\tau $ follow from proposition 3.1.24, and $\Gamma ^\varphi _{-\infty }=V_\theta $ has positive mass.

We first observe that the notion of test curves does not really depend on the choice of $\theta $ within its cohomology class.

Proposition 9.1.7. Let $\theta '$ be another smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing the same cohomology class as $\theta $. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be a model potential. Let $\phi '\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$ be the unique model potential satisfying $\phi \sim \phi '$.

Then there is a canonical bijection

\[ \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ';\phi '). \]

This bijection induces the following bijections:

\[ \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim }\mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ';\phi '),\quad \mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim }\mathrm{TC}^{\infty }(X,\theta ';\phi '). \]

These bijections satisfy the obvious cocycle conditions.

Proof. Choose $g\in C^{\infty }(X)$ such that $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$. Given any $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we observe that $\Gamma '\colon (-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')$ defined as

\[ \tau \mapsto P_{\theta '}[\Gamma _{\tau }-g] \]

lies in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ';\phi ')$. In fact, the concavity and monotonicity of $\Gamma '$ follow from proposition 3.1.24. Moreover, applying proposition 3.1.28 to the increasing net obtained as $\tau \to -\infty $ gives

\[ \Gamma '_{-\infty }=P_{\theta '}[\Gamma _{-\infty }-g]=P_{\theta '}[\phi -g]=\phi '. \]

Thus $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ';\phi ')$.

Moreover, the choice of $g$ is irrelevant since for any other choice of $g$, say $g'$, we have

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }-g\sim \Gamma _{\tau }-g' \]

for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. The inverse construction is obtained by replacing $g$ with $-g$. The boundedness condition is clearly preserved. Finally, by proposition 3.1.6, for every $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$,

\[ \int _ X(\theta '+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma '_\tau )^ n=\int _ X(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _\tau )^ n, \]

and the same holds for $\phi $ and $\phi '$. Hence the finite energy condition is preserved. The cocycle condition is immediate from the uniqueness of the model representative in a fixed singularity type. $\square$

Proposition 9.1.8. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold $Y$. Then the pointwise pull-back induces a bijection

\[ \pi ^*\colon \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )\xlongrightarrow {\sim } \mathrm{TC}(Y,\pi ^*\theta ;\pi ^*\phi ). \]

Proof. The bijectivity on the underlying potentials follows from proposition 1.5.8. The compatibility with model potentials follows from proposition 3.1.23. These two facts also preserve concavity, monotonicity and the value of $\Gamma _{-\infty }$, hence give the asserted bijection of test curves. $\square$

Next we verify the closedness of a test curve as a family of concave functions, so that no pathology arises in the Legendre transforms considered below. The notion of closedness is recalled in definition A.1.13.

Proposition 9.1.9. Let $\Gamma $ be a test curve in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For each $x\in X$, the map $\mathbb {R} \ni \tau \mapsto \Gamma _{\tau }(x)$ is a closed concave function. Moreover, the map is proper as long as $\Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}(x)\neq -\infty $.

Proof. We prove closedness. Fix $x\in X$. Assume that $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)\neq -\infty $ for some $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. We only need to argue the upper semicontinuity of $\tau \mapsto \Gamma _{\tau }(x)$. The upper semicontinuity is clear at $\tau \geq \Gamma _{\max }$, so we are reduced to prove the following:

9.8
\begin{equation} \Gamma _{\tau }=\inf _{\tau '<\tau }\Gamma _{\tau '} \end{equation}

for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. Take $\tau ''\in (\tau ,\Gamma _{\max })$. Outside the polar locus of $\Gamma _{\tau ''}$, we know that (9.8) holds by continuity of real-valued concave functions. So (9.8) holds everywhere by proposition 1.2.6.

The final assertion follows from properness of a finite closed concave function on its effective domain. $\square$

Definition 9.1.10. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\omega $ be a smooth closed real positive $(1,1)$-form. Then we define $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\omega )_{>0}$ as follows:

  1. Define

    \[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\max }=\Gamma _{\max }; \]

  2. for each $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, define

    \[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\tau }=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma _{\tau }]. \]
It follows from proposition 3.1.24 that the family $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]$ is concave and decreasing. Moreover, since $\omega $ is positive,
\[ \int _ X(\theta +\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _\tau )^ n \geq \int _ X(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _\tau )^ n>0 \]

for every $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, and hence $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_\tau $ is a model potential by corollary 3.1.12. Thus $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\omega )_{>0}$.

Proposition 9.1.11. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\omega $ be a closed real smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{-\infty }=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma _{-\infty }]. \]

Proof. This follows from proposition 3.1.28. $\square$

9.2 Ross–Witt Nyström correspondence

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Fix a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

proposition 9.1.9 allows us to talk about the Legendre transforms of test curves in the expected way.

The general definition of the Legendre transform definition A.2.1 can be translated as follows:

Definition 9.2.1. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. We define its Legendre transform as the map $\Gamma ^*\colon (0,\infty )\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ given by

9.9
\begin{equation} \Gamma ^*_ t=\sup _{\tau \in \mathbb {R}} \left(t\tau + \Gamma _{\tau }\right)\footnotemark . \end{equation}
Note. There is no usc regularization in the preceding formula. This is not a typo.
Thanks to 9.1.2, (9.9) can be equivalently written as
\[ \Gamma ^*_ t=\sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }} \left(t\tau + \Gamma _{\tau }\right)=\sup _{\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }} \left(t\tau + \Gamma _{\tau }\right). \]

It is sometimes handy to define

9.10
\begin{equation} \Gamma ^*_0\coloneqq \phi \end{equation}

at $t=0$. But it is important to remember that, with this convention, (9.9) is not true at $t=0$ in general.

Remark 9.2.2. Here we do not discuss the case $t<0$ because its behavior is straightforward: take $x\in X$; if $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)=-\infty $ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, then $\Gamma _ t^*(x)=-\infty $; otherwise, $\Gamma _ t^*(x)=\infty $.

The information about $t>0$ suffices to characterize $\Gamma $.

Proposition 9.2.3. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

9.11
\begin{equation} \Gamma _{\tau }=\inf _{t>0} \left(\Gamma ^*_ t-t\tau \right) \end{equation}

for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$.

Due to our convention (9.10), in (9.11) we may equivalently take $t\geq 0$.

Proof. Fix $x\in X$. We want to establish (9.11) at $x$. We distinguish two cases. First suppose that $\Gamma _{\tau }(x)=-\infty $ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$ and hence all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. In this case, we have $\Gamma _ t^*(x)=-\infty $ for all $t>0$. Therefore, (9.11) follows trivially.

Otherwise, by 9.2.2, we know that $\Gamma ^*_ t(x)=\infty $ for all $t<0$. The relative interior of the domain of $t\mapsto \Gamma ^*_ t(x)$ is contained in $(0,\infty )$. Therefore, (9.11) follows from theorem A.2.2, proposition 9.1.9. $\square$

In definition 9.2.1, we made the nontrivial claim that $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for all $t>0$. Let us prove this.

Lemma 9.2.4. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for all $t>0$. In fact, $\Gamma ^*$ is upper semicontinuous as a function of $X\times (0,\infty )$.

Proof. We first observe that for each $x\in X$, we have

\[ \Gamma _ t^*(x)\leq t\Gamma _{\max }<\infty . \]

Let $R=\{ a+\mathrm{i}b\in \mathbb {C}:a>0,b\in \mathbb {R}\} $. We consider

\[ F\colon X\times R\rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad (x,a+\mathrm{i}b)\mapsto \Gamma _ a^*(x). \]

Let $\pi \colon X\times R\rightarrow X$ be the natural projection. Observe that the upper semicontinuous regularization $G$ of $F$ is $\pi ^*\theta $-psh by proposition 1.2.2. It suffices to show that $F=G$. We let

\[ E\coloneqq \left\{ (x,z)\in X\times R: F(x,z)<G(x,z) \right\} . \]

We want to argue that $E=\varnothing $. Since $F$ is invariant under translations in the imaginary direction of $R$, the same is true for its upper semicontinuous regularization $G$. So $E$ can be written as $B\times \mathrm{i}\mathbb {R}$ for some set $B\subseteq X\times (0,\infty )$. Since $E$ is a pluripolar set by proposition 1.2.5, it has zero Lebesgue measure. Hence, $B$ has zero Lebesgue measure. For each $x\in X$, write

\[ B_ x=\left\{ t\in (0,\infty ): (x,t)\in B \right\} . \]

By Fubini's theorem, $B_ x$ has vanishing $1$-dimensional Lebesgue measure for all $x\in X\setminus Z$, where $Z\subseteq X$ is a subset of measure $0$. We may assume that $Z\supseteq \{ \Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}=-\infty \} $ so that for $x\in X\setminus Z$, $\Gamma ^*_ t(x)\neq -\infty $ for all $t>0$.

For any $x\in X\setminus Z$, both $t\mapsto F(x,t)$ and $G(x,t)$ are convex functions with values in $\mathbb {R}$ on $(0,\infty )$. They agree almost everywhere, hence everywhere by their continuity. It follows that for $x\in X\setminus Z$, we have $B_ x=\varnothing $.

By proposition 9.2.3, for any $x\in X$, we have

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }(x)=\inf _{t>0} (F(x,t)-t\tau ),\quad \tau <\Gamma _{\max }. \]

On the other hand, let

9.12
\begin{equation} \chi _{\tau }(x)=\inf _{t>0} (G(x,t)-t\tau ),\quad \tau < \Gamma _{\max },\quad x\in X. \end{equation}

By Kiselman's principle proposition 1.2.11, $\chi _{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. But on $X\setminus Z$, we already know that $\Gamma _{\tau }=\chi _{\tau }$ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. By proposition 1.2.6,

\[ \Gamma _{\tau }=\chi _{\tau },\quad \tau <\Gamma _{\max }. \]

Now we conclude that $F(x,t)=G(x,t)$ by corollary A.2.4. $\square$

Corollary 9.2.5. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ for all $t>0$.

Proof. Fix $t>0$. We already know that $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by 9.2.4. It suffices to show that

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t\sim _ P \phi . \]

From (9.9) and proposition 6.1.20, we know that

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t\sim _ P \operatorname *{sup}_{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma _{\tau }=\phi . \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Lemma 9.2.6. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

\[ \sup _ X \Gamma _ t^*=t\Gamma _{\max } \]

for all $t>0$.

In particular, $t\mapsto \Gamma ^*_ t-t\Gamma _{\max }$ is a decreasing function in $t>0$.

Proof. Since $\Gamma _{\tau }\leq 0$ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

\[ \sup _ X \Gamma _ t^*\leq t\Gamma _{\max } \]

for all $t>0$. Conversely, for each $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$,

\[ \sup _ X\Gamma _ t^*\geq \sup _ X(t\tau +\Gamma _{\tau })=t\tau . \]

Letting $\tau \to \Gamma _{\max }$ gives the reverse inequality. The final assertion follows directly from the formula

\[ \Gamma _ t^*-t\Gamma _{\max }=\sup _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}\Bigl (\Gamma _\tau -t(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau )\Bigr ). \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

Lemma 9.2.7. Given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we have $\Gamma ^*\in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

See definition 4.2.8 for the notation $\mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

Proof. It follows from 9.2.4, (9.9) and proposition 1.2.2 that the restriction of $\Gamma ^*$ to each compact subinterval of $(0,\infty )$ is a subgeodesic. By corollary 9.2.5, for any $t>0$, $\Gamma ^*_ t\in \mathcal{E}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

First observe that as $t\to 0+$, we have

9.13
\begin{equation} \Gamma _ t^*\xrightarrow {L^1}\phi . \end{equation}

By 9.2.6 and proposition 1.5.4, it suffices to show each $L^1$-cluster point $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ of $\Gamma _ t^*$ as $t\to 0$ is equal to $\phi $.

To see this, first observe that by (9.9), for any fixed $t>0$,

\[ \Gamma _ t^*\leq t\Gamma _{\max }+\phi . \]

Therefore, $\psi \leq \phi $. On the other hand, for any fixed $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, by (9.9), we have

\[ \Gamma ^*_ t\geq \Gamma _{\tau }+t\tau \]

for any $t>0$. So $\psi \geq \Gamma _{\tau }$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. It follows that $\psi \geq \phi $. Therefore, $\psi =\phi $.

Assume that $\Gamma ^*$ is not a geodesic ray. Then we can find $0\leq a<b$ such that $(\Gamma ^*_ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$ differs from the geodesic $(\eta _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$ from $\Gamma ^*_ a$ to $\Gamma ^*_ b$. The existence of $(\eta _ t)_ t$ is guaranteed by proposition 4.2.4. We consider the subgeodesic $(\ell _ t)_{t>0}$ given by $\ell _ t=\eta _ t$ for $t\in (a,b)$ and $\ell _ t=\Gamma ^*_ t$ otherwise. Note that $\ell $ is a subgeodesic due to the gluing lemma 4.1.11.

Consider the Legendre transform

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }=\inf _{t>0} (\ell _ t-t\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \]

Then $\Gamma '_{\tau }\geq \Gamma _{\tau }$ and $\Gamma '_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $ by proposition 1.2.11 for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$.

We claim that

9.14
\begin{equation} \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _{\tau }+(b-a)(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau ),\quad \tau \in \mathbb {R}. \end{equation}

Observe that $\Gamma '_{\tau }\equiv -\infty $ when $\tau >\Gamma _{\max }$ by 9.2.6. Thus it suffices to consider $\tau \leq \Gamma _{\max }$. Put

\[ C_{\tau }\coloneqq (b-a)(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau )\geq 0. \]

For any $s\in [a,b]$, the monotonicity of $t\mapsto \Gamma _ t^*-t\Gamma _{\max }$ from 9.2.6 gives

\[ \Gamma _ b^*-b\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau }. \]

Since $\ell _ b=\Gamma _ b^*$, we get

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _ b^*-b\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau },\quad s\in [a,b]. \]

For $s\notin [a,b]$, we have $\ell _ s=\Gamma _ s^*$, hence

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau \leq \Gamma _ s^*-s\tau +C_{\tau }. \]

Taking the infimum over all $s>0$ gives

\[ \Gamma '_{\tau }\leq \Gamma _{\tau }+C_{\tau }. \]

Therefore, (9.14) follows. In particular, for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have $\Gamma '_{\tau }\sim \Gamma _{\tau }$. Moreover, $\sup _ X\ell _ t\leq t\Gamma _{\max }$ for all $t>0$: this is clear outside $(a,b)$ from 9.2.6, and on $(a,b)$ it follows from the convexity of subgeodesics applied to the geodesic $(\eta _ t)_{t\in (a,b)}$. Hence, for $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$,

\[ \sup _ X(\ell _ t-t\tau )\leq t(\Gamma _{\max }-\tau ). \]

Letting $t\to 0+$ gives $\Gamma '_{\tau }\leq 0$. It follows from the fact that $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is a model potential that $\Gamma _{\tau }=\Gamma '_{\tau }$ for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$. Therefore, by theorem A.2.2, we have $\Gamma ^*_ t=\ell _ t$ for all $t>0$, which is a contradiction. $\square$

\begin{equation} \sup _ X \ell ^*_{\tau }\leq 0. \end{equation}

\begin{equation} P_{\theta }\left[\ell ^*_{\tau }\right]=\ell ^*_{\tau }. \end{equation}

Therefore, $f$ lies in the right-hand side of (9.50). $\square$

The masses of potentials on a test curve can also be expressed in terms of the corresponding geodesic ray.

Proposition 9.2.14 (Hisamoto). Let $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Given any $\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }$, we have

9.55
\begin{equation} \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ell ^*_{\tau }\right)^ n=\int _{\{ \dot{\ell }_0\geq \tau \} } \theta _{\phi }^ n. \end{equation}
Here $\dot{\ell }_0$ denotes the right-derivative of $\ell _ t$ with respect to $t$ at $t=0$. It is well-defined quasi-everywhere by corollary 4.1.7, and hence the right-hand side of (9.55) makes sense, even though $\{ \dot{\ell }_0\geq \tau \} $ is not well-defined.

Proof. Fix $\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }$. We first observe that it suffices to show that

9.56
\begin{equation} \int _{\left\{ \dot{\ell }_0\geq \tau \right\} } \theta _{\phi }^ n=\int _{\left\{ \ell ^*_{\tau }=\phi \right\} } \theta _{\phi }^ n. \end{equation}

Assuming (9.56) for the moment. By theorem 9.2.9, $\ell ^*\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. In particular, $\ell ^*_\tau $ is a model potential and

\[ \ell ^*_\tau \leq \ell ^*_{-\infty }=\phi . \]

Since $\phi $ is model as well, proposition 3.1.21 gives

\[ \theta ^ n_{\ell ^*_{\tau }}=\mathds {1}_{\left\{ \ell ^*_{\tau }=0\right\} }\, \theta ^ n,\quad \theta ^ n_{\phi }=\mathds {1}_{\{ \phi =0\} }\, \theta ^ n. \]

Since $\ell ^*_{\tau }\leq \phi \leq 0$, we have

\[ \mathds {1}_{\left\{ \ell ^*_{\tau }=\phi \right\} }\, \theta _{\phi }^ n =\mathds {1}_{\left\{ \ell ^*_{\tau }=\phi =0\right\} }\, \theta ^ n =\mathds {1}_{\left\{ \ell ^*_{\tau }=0\right\} }\, \theta ^ n =\theta ^ n_{\ell ^*_{\tau }}. \]

Therefore, (9.55) follows from (9.56).

In order to prove (9.56), it suffices to show that

9.57
\begin{equation} \left\{ \dot{\ell }_0\geq \tau \right\} =\left\{ \ell ^*_{\tau }=\phi \right\} \quad \textup{outside a pluripolar set}. \end{equation}

Take $x\in X$ so that $(\ell _ t(x))_{t\geq 0}$ is finite and right-differentiable at $t=0$. Note that this condition holds quasi-everywhere. Suppose that $\ell ^*_{\tau }(x)=\phi (x)$. Then

\[ \dot{\ell }_0(x)=\inf _{t>0} \frac{\ell _ t(x)-\phi (x)}{t}\geq \inf _{t>0} \frac{\ell ^*_{\tau }(x)+t\tau -\phi (x)}{t}=\tau . \]

Therefore, the $\supseteq $ direction in (9.57) follows. Conversely, suppose that $\dot{\ell }_0(x)\geq \tau $, then

\[ \ell ^*_{\tau }(x)=\inf _{t>0} \left(\ell _ t(x)-t\tau \right)\geq \inf _{t>0} \left(\phi (x)+t\dot{\ell }_0(x)-t\tau \right)\geq \phi (x). \]

Together with $\ell ^*_\tau \leq \phi $, this gives $\ell ^*_\tau (x)=\phi (x)$. Therefore, the $\subseteq $ direction in (9.57) follows as well. $\square$

Corollary 9.2.15. Let $\ell \in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then $\dot{\ell }_0\in L^1(X,\theta _{\phi }^ n)$ and

9.58
\begin{equation} \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell )=\int _ X \dot{\ell }_0\, \theta _{\phi }^ n. \end{equation}

In particular, for every $t\geq 0$,

\[ E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell _ t)=t\int _ X \dot{\ell }_0\, \theta _{\phi }^ n. \]

Proof. Set $\Gamma =\ell ^*$. By theorem 9.2.9, we have $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}^1(X,\theta ;\phi )$ and

\[ \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell )=\mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\Gamma ). \]

By proposition 9.2.14, for every $\tau <\ell ^*_{\max }$,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\Gamma _{\tau }}^ n=\int _{\{ \dot{\ell }_0\geq \tau \} } \theta _{\phi }^ n. \]

Hence (9.5) gives

\[ \mathbf{E}^{\phi }(\ell ) =\ell ^*_{\max }\int _ X \theta _{\phi }^ n-\int _{-\infty }^{\ell ^*_{\max }} \int _{\{ \dot{\ell }_0<\tau \} }\theta _{\phi }^ n\, \mathrm{d}\tau . \]

For $\tau >\ell ^*_{\max }$, we have $\ell ^*_{\tau }\equiv -\infty $ by 9.2.8. Then (9.56) shows that

\[ \int _{\{ \dot{\ell }_0\geq \tau \} }\theta _{\phi }^ n=0. \]

The layer-cake formula therefore gives (9.58). The final assertion follows from proposition 4.3.16, since $E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\ell _0)=E_{\theta }^{\phi }(\phi )=0$. $\square$

9.3 -model test curves

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. Fix a model potential $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Definition 9.3.1. A test curve $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model if for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, the potential $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model.

The subset of $\mathcal{I}$-model test curves in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ is denoted by $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. When $\phi =V_{\theta }$, we omit $\phi $ and write $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ instead.

The union of the sets of $\mathcal{I}$-model test curves in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for all model potentials $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ is denoted by $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Note that $\Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}$ is automatically $\mathcal{I}$-model, since
\[ \Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}\leq P_{\theta }\left[\Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}\right]_{\mathcal{I}}\leq \inf _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}P_{\theta }\left[\Gamma _{\tau }\right]_{\mathcal{I}}=\inf _{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}\Gamma _{\tau }=\Gamma _{\Gamma _{\max }}. \]

Here we write $\mathrm{NA}$ with non-Archimedean in mind. The precise relation with non-Archimedean pluripotential theory will be clear in chapter 13. This section and the next can be skipped on a first reading; the necessary results can be consulted when reading chapter 13.

Proposition 9.3.2. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Then $\Gamma _{-\infty }$ is an $\mathcal{I}$-model potential.

Proof. This follows from proposition 3.2.19. $\square$

Proposition 9.3.3. Let $\theta '$ be another smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing the same cohomology class as $\theta $. Then there is a canonical bijection

\[ \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')_{>0}. \]

This bijection satisfies the obvious cocycle condition.

Proof. Let $\theta '=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$. By proposition 9.1.7, the canonical map sends $\Gamma $ to the test curve with levels

\[ \Gamma '_\tau =P_{\theta '}[\Gamma _\tau -g]. \]

If $\Gamma _\tau $ is $\mathcal I$-model, then it is $\mathcal I$-good by example 7.1.7. Note that $\Gamma _\tau -g$ is $\mathcal I$-good since $\Gamma _{\tau }$ is. Hence

\[ P_{\theta '}[\Gamma _\tau -g]=P_{\theta '}[\Gamma _\tau -g]_{\mathcal I}, \]

so $\Gamma '_\tau $ is $\mathcal I$-model. The same argument applied to $-g$ gives the inverse map, and the cocycle condition follows from the one in proposition 9.1.7. $\square$

Proposition 9.3.4. Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold $Y$. Then the pointwise pull-back induces a bijection

\[ \pi ^*\colon \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ;\phi )\xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(Y,\pi ^*\theta ;\pi ^*\phi ). \]

Proof. This is an immediate consequence of proposition 9.1.8 and proposition 3.2.7. $\square$

Definition 9.3.5. Given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$, we define its $\mathcal{I}$-envelope $P_{\theta }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ as the map

\[ (-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\quad \tau \mapsto P_{\theta }\left[\Gamma _{\tau }\right]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

More generally, for any closed real smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\omega $ on $X$, we define $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}$ as the map

\[ (-\infty ,\Gamma _{\max })\rightarrow \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\omega ),\quad \tau \mapsto P_{\theta +\omega }\left[\Gamma _{\tau }\right]_{\mathcal{I}}. \]

Proposition 9.3.6. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ;\phi )$. Then

\[ P_{\theta }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}\in \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ;P_{\theta }[\phi ]_{\mathcal{I}}). \]

More generally, for any closed real smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]_{\mathcal{I}}\in \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\omega ;P_{\theta +\omega }[\phi ]_{\mathcal{I}}). \]

Proof. We prove the first assertion; the second is identical, replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\omega $.

Set

\[ \widetilde\Gamma _\tau \coloneqq P_\theta [\Gamma _\tau ]_{\mathcal I}. \]

The family is decreasing by 3.2.17. If $\tau =(1-s)\tau _0+s\tau _1$, then the concavity of $\Gamma $ gives

\[ \Gamma _\tau \geq (1-s)\Gamma _{\tau _0}+s\Gamma _{\tau _1}. \]

Hence, by 3.2.17 and proposition 3.2.16,

\[ \widetilde\Gamma _\tau \geq (1-s)\widetilde\Gamma _{\tau _0}+s\widetilde\Gamma _{\tau _1}. \]

Thus the family is concave. Finally,

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{\tau <\Gamma _{\max }}{}\! {}^{*}\widetilde\Gamma _\tau = P_\theta [\phi ]_{\mathcal I} \]

follows from proposition 3.2.19. Since $P_\theta [\phi ]_{\mathcal I}\geq \phi $, its mass is positive by the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. This proves the assertion. $\square$

Definition 9.3.7. Let $\phi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ be a model potential. A geodesic ray $\ell \in \mathcal{R}(X,\theta ;\phi )$ is maximal if $\ell ^*$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model, namely, if $\ell ^*\in \mathcal E^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ;\phi )$.

An important class of $\mathcal{I}$-model test curves is given by filtrations. We briefly recall the corresponding terminology.

Definition 9.3.8. Let $L$ be a big line bundle. We write

\[ R(X,L)=\bigoplus _{k=0}^{\infty } \mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ k) \]
for the section ring

of $L$.

    \[ \mathcal{F}^{\lambda }_ k\cdot \mathcal{F}^{\lambda '}_{k'}\subseteq \mathcal{F}^{\lambda +\lambda '}_{k+k'}; \]

    \begin{equation} \mathcal{F}^{Cm}_{m}=0,\quad \mathcal{F}^{-Cm}_ m=\mathrm{H}^0(X,L^ m) \end{equation}

    for all $m\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$.

Example 9.3.9. Let $L$ be a big line bundle on $X$ and $\mathcal{F}$ be a filtration on $R(X,L)$. Fix a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $L$ and write $\theta =c_1(L,h)$.

We introduce a few auxiliary functions. For each $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we introduce

\[ \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau }\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\left\{ \log |s|_{h^ k}^2: s\in \mathcal{F}^{k\tau }_ k, |s|_{h^ k}^2\leq 1 \right\} . \]

When $k\tau \leq \tau _ k(\mathcal{F})$, we know that $\mathcal{F}_ k^{k\tau }\neq 0$. Moreover, proposition 1.8.4 and proposition 1.2.2 imply that

\[ \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,k\theta ),\quad \tau \leq k^{-1}\tau _ k(\mathcal{F}). \]

Observe that for $k,k'\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, we have

\[ \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k+k'}_{\tau }\geq \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau }+\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k'}_{\tau }. \]

In particular, by Fekete's lemma,

\begin{equation} \lim _{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}\frac{1}{k}\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau }=\sup _{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}\frac{1}{k}\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau } \end{equation}

exists for any $\tau <\tau (\mathcal{F})$.

We define $(\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau })_{\tau <\tau (\mathcal{F})}$ as follows:

\[ \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau }\coloneqq P_{\theta }\left[ \operatorname *{sup}_{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}{}\! {}^{*} \frac{1}{k} \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau }\right]\footnotemark . \]

We claim that $\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}\in \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$ and is bounded.

It is clear that $(-\infty ,\tau (\mathcal{F}))\ni \tau \mapsto \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau }$ is decreasing. We prove its concavity. By proposition 3.1.24, it suffices to show that

\[ \Bigl (-\infty ,\tau (\mathcal{F})\Bigr )\ni \tau \mapsto \operatorname *{sup}_{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}{}\! {}^{*} \frac{1}{k} \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau } \]

is concave. In other words, we need to prove the following: Given $\tau _0<\tau _1<\tau (\mathcal{F})$ and $t\in (0,1)$, we have

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}{}\! {}^{*} \frac{1}{k} \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{t\tau _1+(1-t)\tau _0}\geq t\operatorname *{sup}_{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}{}\! {}^{*} \frac{1}{k} \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau _1}+(1-t)\operatorname *{sup}_{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}}{}\! {}^{*} \frac{1}{k} \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau _0}. \]

But thanks to proposition 1.2.6 and proposition 1.2.5, it suffices to show that

\[ \sup _{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}} \frac{1}{k}\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{t\tau _1+(1-t)\tau _0}\geq t\sup _{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}} \frac{1}{k}\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau _1}+(1-t)\sup _{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}} \frac{1}{k}\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{\tau _0} \]

for all $t\in (0,1)$. Take $s_ i\in \mathcal{F}^{k_ i\tau _ i}_{k_ i}$ for $i=0,1$ with $|s_ i|_{h^{k_ i}}^2\leq 1$, where $k_0,k_1\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. We need to prove that

9.61
\begin{equation} \sup _{k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}} \frac{1}{k}\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F},k}_{t\tau _1+(1-t)\tau _0}\geq \frac{1-t}{k_0}\log |s_0|_{h^{k_0}}^2+\frac{t}{k_1}\log |s_1|_{h^{k_1}}^2. \end{equation}

Approximating $t$ by rational numbers $t_ m\searrow t$, and using the decreasingness of the filtration in the parameter $\lambda $, we reduce to the case $t\in \mathbb Q$. Write $t=p/q$ with $p,q\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$. Then

\[ s\coloneqq s_0^{k_1(q-p)}\otimes s_1^{k_0p}\in \mathcal{F}_{k_0k_1 q}^{k_0k_1\tau _0(q-p)+k_0k_1\tau _1 p}, \]

and

\[ \begin{aligned} & \frac{1}{k_0k_1 q} \log |s|_{h^{k_0k_1 q}}^2\\ =& \frac{1}{k_0k_1 q}\left(k_1(q-p)\log |s_0|^2+ k_0p\log |s_1|^2\right)\\ = & \frac{1-t}{k_0}\log |s_0|_{h^{k_0}}^2+\frac{t}{k_1}\log |s_1|_{h^{k_1}}^2. \end{aligned} \]

So (9.61) follows.

Note that for each $k\in \mathbb {Z}_{>0}$, $\tau \leq k^{-1}\tau _ k(\mathcal{F})$, we know that $k^{-1}\Gamma _{\tau }^{\mathcal{F},k}$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good by proposition 7.2.3. The family is bounded above by $0$, so it follows from the same proposition that the obstacle in the definition of $\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau }$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good. Hence the $P$- and $\mathcal{I}$-envelopes of this obstacle agree, so $\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau }$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model for each $\tau <\tau (\mathcal{F})$.

It remains to show that the test curve $\Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}$ is bounded and lies in $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )$. Fix $\tau \leq -C$, where $C$ is as in (9.59). We will show that

9.65
\begin{equation} \Gamma ^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau }=V_{\theta }. \end{equation}

This follows from the Bergman kernel technique. Based on the theory developed so far, we can also give an elementary argument.

Fix $k\in \mathbb Z_{>0}$. Since $\tau \leq -C$, we have $\mathcal F^{k\tau }_ k=\mathrm H^0(X,L^ k)$. Let $s\in \mathrm H^0(X,L^ k)$. After multiplying $s$ by a constant, we may assume that $|s|_{h^ k}^2\leq 1$. By the definition of $\Gamma ^{\mathcal F,k}_\tau $,

\[ \Gamma ^{\mathcal F,k}_\tau \geq \log |s|_{h^ k}^2. \]

Hence

\[ s\in \mathrm H^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal I(\Gamma ^{\mathcal F,k}_\tau )\right). \]

Since

\[ k\Gamma ^{\mathcal F}_\tau \geq \Gamma ^{\mathcal F,k}_\tau , \]

we get

\[ s\in \mathrm H^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal I(k\Gamma ^{\mathcal F}_\tau )\right). \]

Thus

\[ \mathrm H^0\left(X,L^ k\otimes \mathcal I(k\Gamma ^{\mathcal F}_\tau )\right)=\mathrm H^0(X,L^ k) \]

for every $k\in \mathbb Z_{>0}$. By theorem 7.4.2,

\[ \operatorname{vol}\left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\mathcal F}_\tau \right)=\operatorname{vol}L. \]

As $\Gamma ^{\mathcal F}_\tau $ is $\mathcal I$-model, this volume is equal to $\int _ X\left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{\mathcal F}_\tau \right)^ n$. Hence $\Gamma ^{\mathcal F}_\tau $ has full Monge–Ampère mass. Since it is also model, it must be equal to $V_\theta $. This proves (9.65).

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta ,\theta ',\theta ''$ be smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ representing big cohomology classes.

In this section, we develop several general operations on test curves, anticipating the applications in non-Archimedean geometry in chapter 13. One may read chapter 13 first and consult this section when necessary.

Definition 9.4.1. Given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$, we say $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$ if $\Gamma _{\max }\leq \Gamma '_{\max }$ and, for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

  2. $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]\leq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']$.

Proof. Let

\[ \eta _{\tau }=\sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}} \left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right)=\sup _{\tau -\Gamma '_{\max }<\delta <\Gamma _{\max }}\left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right) \]

when $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$.

Taking suprema and using proposition 3.1.24 gives the concavity of $\Gamma +\Gamma '$.

\[ \eta _\tau \geq \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }. \]

\[ \left\{ \left(\bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j\right)_ q:J\in \operatorname{Fin}(I)\right\} \subseteq \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

We obtain a countable subfamily $\mathcal J_ q\subseteq \operatorname{Fin}(I)$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{J\in \operatorname{Fin}(I)}{}\! {}^{*}\left(\bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j\right)_ q = \operatorname *{sup}_{J\in \mathcal J_ q}{}\! {}^{*}\left(\bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j\right)_ q. \]

Let

\[ I'\coloneqq \bigcup _{q\in \mathbb Q, q<M} \bigcup _{J\in \mathcal J_ q}J. \]

Then $I'$ is countable and, for every rational $q<M$,

\[ \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\right)_ q= \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\right)_ q. \]

Moreover, $\sup _{i\in I'}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }=M$, since the last equality is non-trivial for every rational $q<M$. Let $\Gamma '=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i$. Then clearly, $\Gamma '\leq \Gamma $. We claim that they are actually equal.

Thanks to proposition 6.1.4 and 9.1.3, it suffices to show that for any $\tau <\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }$, we have

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma '_{\tau }\right)^ n=\int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{\tau }\right)^ n. \]

The equality is already known for all rational $\tau <M$. Since both

\[ \tau \mapsto \log \int _ X\theta _{\Gamma '_\tau }^ n,\quad \tau \mapsto \log \int _ X\theta _{\Gamma _\tau }^ n \]

are continuous on $(-\infty ,M)$ by proposition 9.1.4, the equality of masses holds for every $\tau <M$. $\square$

Proposition 9.4.20. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Let $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} (\Gamma ^ i+C)=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^ i+C. \]

Suppose that $(\Gamma '^ i)_{i\in I}$ is another family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Suppose that $\Gamma ^ i\leq \Gamma '^ i$ for all $i\in I$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\leq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma '^ i. \]

Proof. This is immediate by definition. $\square$

Definition 9.4.21. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\lambda >0$, we define $\lambda \Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\lambda \theta )_{>0}$ as follows:

  1. We set

    \[ \left(\lambda \Gamma \right)_{\max }=\lambda \Gamma _{\max }; \]

  2. for any $\tau < \left(\lambda \Gamma \right)_{\max }$, we set

    \[ (\lambda \Gamma )_{\tau }=\lambda \Gamma _{\lambda ^{-1}\tau }. \]

Proposition 9.4.22. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\lambda >0$. Then $\lambda \Gamma $ as defined in definition 9.4.21 lies in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\lambda \theta )_{>0}$. Moreover, if $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, then $\lambda \Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\lambda \theta )_{>0}$.

We have

9.92
\begin{equation} (\lambda \Gamma )_{-\infty }=\lambda \Gamma _{-\infty }. \end{equation}

Proof. This is immediate by definition. $\square$

Proposition 9.4.23. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$, $C\in \mathbb {R}$ and $\lambda ,\lambda '>0$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda (\Gamma +\Gamma ')=& \lambda \Gamma +\lambda \Gamma ',\\ (\lambda \lambda ')\Gamma =& \lambda (\lambda '\Gamma ),\\ \lambda (\Gamma +C)=& \lambda \Gamma +\lambda C. \end{aligned} \]

Suppose that $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ is a non-empty family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Then

\[ \lambda \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i \right)=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}(\lambda \Gamma ^ i). \]

Proof. This is immediate by definition. $\square$

Lemma 9.4.24. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\lambda >0$. Then for any closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\omega $ on $X$, we have

\[ P_{\lambda \theta +\lambda \omega }[\lambda \Gamma ]=\lambda P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]. \]

Proof. This is clear by definition. $\square$

Definition 9.4.25. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Assume that

9.96
\begin{equation} \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{\max }>-\infty , \end{equation}

and

9.97
\begin{equation} \inf _{i\in I} \int _ X \left( \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right)^ n>0,\quad \textup{for some }\tau < \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{\max }. \end{equation}

Then we define $\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i}\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ as follows:

  1. We set

    \[ \left( \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{\max }=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{\max }; \]

  2. for any $\tau < \left( \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{\max }$, we let

    \[ \left( \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{\tau }\coloneqq \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i}_{\tau }. \]

Proposition 9.4.26. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.96) and (9.97). Then $\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i}\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Moreover, if $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ for all $i\in I$, then so is $\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i}$.

Proof. The first assertion is an immediate consequence of proposition 3.1.25, except for the positivity of the mass at $-\infty $. Choose $\tau _0$ as in (9.97). Applying proposition 3.1.26 gives

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\tau _0}\right)^ n =\lim _{i\in I}\int _ X\left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{i}_{\tau _0}\right)^ n>0. \]

Hence the value at $-\infty $ has positive mass by monotonicity. The last assertion follows from proposition 3.2.18. $\square$

In general, it is not true that

\[ \left( \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i} \right)_{-\infty }=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{-\infty }. \]

Lemma 9.4.27. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.96) and (9.97). Assume that $\omega $ is a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right]= \inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]. \]

Proof. First observe that

\[ \left( P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right] \right)_{\max }= \left(\inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]\right)_{\max }. \]

Let $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ be less than this common value. Then we need to show the following:

9.98
\begin{equation} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]\sim _ P \inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]. \end{equation}

Applying proposition 3.1.26 gives the mass convergence required in corollary 6.2.11. Hence $\Gamma ^ i_{\tau }\xrightarrow {d_ S}\inf _{j\in I} \Gamma ^ j_{\tau } $. Thanks to corollary 6.2.19 and corollary 6.2.11, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]\xrightarrow {d_ S} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{j\in I} \Gamma ^ j_{\tau } \right],\quad P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]\xrightarrow {d_ S} \inf _{j\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ j_{\tau } \right]. \]

Hence, (9.98) follows from proposition 6.2.4. $\square$
