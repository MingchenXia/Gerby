10.3 Normalized potentials and minimal singularitiesF0B52F4D634F

F0B52F4D634F

Fix a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $ on $X$. A function $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is determined by its current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ up to an additive constant, which we fix by normalizing the supremum. The local compactness and integrability theorems then become uniform statements for normalized potentials, and the upper envelope of the nonpositive potentials has minimal singularities. We also compare potentials on $X$ and on a modification of $X$.

Integrals over $X$ are taken with respect to $\mathrm dV$, or equivalently $\omega ^ n$, unless stated otherwise. A $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function is integrable on the compact manifold $X$, being locally integrable. If $\varphi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic and $\psi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic or continuous, an inequality between $\varphi $ and $\psi $ that holds almost everywhere holds everywhere. Indeed, by lemma 6.14.4(1) the value of a quasi-plurisubharmonic function at a point is the limit of its averages over small coordinate balls centered at that point, a continuous function has the same property, and the inequality holds between these averages. In particular, a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function is determined by its class in $L^1(X)$, and we regard $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ as a subset of $L^1(X)$.

Proposition 10.3.1.2984F6438B4B Let $a\leq b$ be real numbers. The set of $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $a\leq \sup _ X\varphi \leq b$ is compact in $L^1(X)$, and the map $\varphi \mapsto \sup _ X\varphi $ is continuous on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ for the $L^1$ topology. In particular, the functions $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\sup _ X\varphi =0$ are uniformly bounded in $L^1(X)$. The same statements hold for a sequence of backgrounds: if smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms $\theta _ j$ converge smoothly to $\theta $ and $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j)$ satisfy $a\leq \sup _ X\varphi _ j\leq b$, then a subsequence converges in $L^1(X)$ to some $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $a\leq \sup _ X\varphi \leq b$, and $\sup _ X\varphi _ j\to \sup _ X\varphi $ whenever $\varphi _ j\to \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ in $L^1(X)$. The assertions for a fixed background hold more generally on every connected compact complex manifold $X'$ of positive dimension, for every smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form $\theta '$ on $X'$, with $L^1(X')$ taken with respect to any smooth positive volume form.

Proof. Choose finitely many coordinate balls $B_ k$ covering $X$, smaller concentric balls $B'_ k$ still covering $X$, and smooth functions $h_ k$ near $\overline{B_ k}$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ k=\theta $ (lemma 6.14.2, applied on slightly larger concentric balls). For $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ the functions $\varphi +h_ k$ are plurisubharmonic on $B_ k$.

Let $(\varphi _ j)$ be a sequence with $a\leq \sup _ X\varphi _ j\leq b$. The plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi _ j+h_ k$ are uniformly bounded above on each $B_ k$. Since $\varphi _ j$ is upper semicontinuous on the compact manifold $X$, it attains its supremum at a point $x_ j$, and after passing to a subsequence $x_ j\to x$, since $X$ is compact and metrizable. Choose $k$ with $x\in B'_ k$. On $B_ k$ the functions $\varphi _ j+h_ k$ cannot converge locally uniformly to $-\infty $, because their values at $x_ j$ are bounded below by $a+\inf _{B'_ k}h_ k$ for large $j$. By theorem 5.10.4, a further subsequence converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_ k)$. If $B_ l$ meets $B_ k$, the functions $\varphi _ j+h_ l$ cannot tend to $-\infty $ locally uniformly on $B_ l$, since on the nonempty open set $B_ k\cap B_ l$ they differ by the smooth function $h_ l-h_ k$ from a sequence that converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$; so again a subsequence converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_ l)$. Since $X$ is connected, every ball is joined to $B_ k$ by a finite chain of overlapping balls, and after finitely many extractions the sequence converges in $L^1(B'_ l)$ for every $l$, hence in $L^1(X)$, to some $\varphi $. The limit satisfies $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq 0$ as a distributional limit of positive currents, and its canonical representative belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Now let $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ in $L^1(X)$, all in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$; we show that $\sup _ X\varphi _ j\to \sup _ X\varphi $. The submean inequality for $\varphi _ j+h_ k$ on small balls shows that the $\varphi _ j$ are uniformly bounded above. Let $x_ j\to x$; we claim that $\varlimsup _ j\varphi _ j(x_ j)\leq \varphi (x)$. Indeed, choose $k$ with $x\in B_ k$, let $c>\varphi (x)$ be real, and use upper semicontinuity to choose a closed ball $K\subset B_ k$ around $x$ on which $\varphi <c$. The function $c+h_ k$ is continuous near $K$ and $\varphi +h_ k<c+h_ k$ on $K$, so the second part of theorem 5.10.4 gives $\varphi _ j+h_ k<c+h_ k$ on $K$ for large $j$; hence $\varphi _ j(x_ j)<c$ for large $j$.

Choose a subsequence along which $\sup _ X\varphi _ j$ tends to $\varlimsup _ j\sup _ X\varphi _ j$ and points $x_ j$ with $\varphi _ j(x_ j)=\sup _ X\varphi _ j$ converge to some $x$; the claim gives $\varlimsup _ j\sup _ X\varphi _ j\leq \varphi (x)\leq \sup _ X\varphi $. Conversely, choose a subsequence along which $\sup _ X\varphi _ j$ tends to $\varliminf _ j\sup _ X\varphi _ j$ and $\sum _ j\| \varphi _ j-\varphi \| _{L^1}<\infty $; by monotone convergence $\sum _ j|\varphi _ j-\varphi |$ is integrable, hence finite almost everywhere, so $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ almost everywhere along it. We get $\varphi \leq \varliminf _ j\sup _ X\varphi _ j$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, as noted at the beginning of this section. The two inequalities prove that the supremum is continuous; in particular, the limit in the previous paragraph satisfies $a\leq \sup _ X\varphi \leq b$. Thus the set in question is sequentially compact, hence compact, in the metric space $L^1(X)$. The continuous function $\| \cdot \| _{L^1}$ is bounded on the compact set of normalized functions.

The arguments so far use only that $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of positive dimension and that the volume form defining $L^1(X)$ is smooth and positive; hence they also prove the last assertion of the proposition.

For smoothly convergent backgrounds, choose $C\geq 0$ with $\theta _ j\leq C\omega $ for all $j$. Then $C\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j\geq 0$, so all $\varphi _ j$ lie in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,C\omega )$, and the first part, applied to the closed form $C\omega $, gives an $L^1$-convergent subsequence and the convergence of the suprema. Since $\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j\to \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ as distributions, the limit belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. $\square$

Corollary 10.3.2.384717733620 For every $1\leq p<\infty $ there is a constant $M_ p$, depending only on $X$, $\theta $, $\omega $ and $p$, such that

\[ \| u\| _{L^ p(X,\omega ^ n)}\leq M_ p \quad \text{for all }u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\text{ with }\sup _ Xu=0. \]

Proof. (If $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is empty, there is nothing to prove.) Choose finitely many coordinate polydiscs $D_ k$ such that the polydiscs $\frac14D_ k$ with the same centers and a quarter of the radii cover $X$, and smooth functions $h_ k$ near $\overline{D_ k}$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h_ k=\theta $. For $u$ as in the statement, $v_ k=u+h_ k-\sup _{D_ k}h_ k$ is a nonpositive plurisubharmonic function on $D_ k$. By proposition 10.3.1 and the boundedness of the $h_ k$ and of the coordinate volume densities, the norms $\| v_ k\| _{L^1(D_ k)}$ are bounded independently of $u$. After rescaling $D_ k$ to the unit polydisc, (5.10.9) bounds $\| v_ k\| _{L^ p(\frac14D_ k)}$ independently of $u$. Since $u=v_ k-h_ k+\sup _{D_ k}h_ k$ on $D_ k$, the bounded functions $h_ k$ and the bounded volume densities give the assertion. $\square$

Corollary 10.3.3.C7609FD8FC8C There are $\alpha ,C>0$, depending only on $X$, $\theta $ and $\omega $, such that

6B3D5F5D04A5 10.3.4
\begin{equation} \int _ X\mathrm e^{-\alpha u}\omega ^ n\leq C \quad \text{for all }u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\text{ with }\sup _ Xu=0. \end{equation}

Proof. (If $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is empty, any $\alpha ,C>0$ will do.) Choose finitely many coordinate unit balls $B_ j$ such that the balls $\frac14B_ j$ with the same centers and a quarter of the radii cover $X$, and smooth functions $\rho _ j$ near $\overline{B_ j}$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho _ j=\theta $. For $u$ as in the statement put $v_ j=u+\rho _ j-\sup _{B_ j}\rho _ j$ on $B_ j$; then $v_ j$ is plurisubharmonic and $v_ j\leq u\leq 0$. By proposition 10.3.1 and comparison of the coordinate volume densities, $\| v_ j\| _{L^1(B_ j)}\leq M_ j$ for a constant $M_ j\geq 1$ independent of $u$. Applying proposition 5.10.12 to $v_ j/M_ j$, we see that $\int _{\frac14B_ j}\mathrm e^{-\alpha _ jv_ j}$ is bounded independently of $u$, where $\alpha _ j=\alpha _ n/M_ j$. Put $\alpha =\min _ j\alpha _ j>0$. Since $v_ j\leq u\leq 0$ and $\alpha \leq \alpha _ j$, we have $\mathrm e^{-\alpha u}\leq \mathrm e^{-\alpha _ ju}\leq \mathrm e^{-\alpha _ jv_ j}$ on $B_ j$. Summing over $j$ and comparing the coordinate volume densities with $\omega ^ n$ gives (10.3.4). $\square$

Recall from definition 5.47.2 that a subset of $X$ is pluripolar if it is locally contained in the pole set of a plurisubharmonic function.

Corollary 10.3.5.B2C28BB645D3 Let $K\subset X$ be a compact set that is not pluripolar. Then there is a constant $C$ such that

\[ \sup _ X\varphi \leq \sup _ K\varphi +C\qquad \text{for all }\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

Consequently, every subset of $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ on which $\sup _ K\varphi $ is bounded above and below is relatively compact in $L^1(X)$.

Proof. Suppose that no such constant exists. Since $\sup _ X\varphi -\sup _ K\varphi $ does not change when a constant is added to $\varphi $, there are $\varphi _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\sup _ X\varphi _ j=0$ and $\sup _ K\varphi _ j\leq -2^ j$. Choose $C\geq 0$ with $\theta \leq C\omega $ (lemma 6.14.1) and put $\psi _ N=\sum _{j=1}^ N2^{-j}\varphi _ j$. Since $\sum _{j\leq N}2^{-j}\leq 1$, we have

\[ C\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ N=\Bigl (1-\sum _{j\leq N}2^{-j}\Bigr )C\omega +\sum _{j\leq N}2^{-j}(C\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\geq \sum _{j\leq N}2^{-j}(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j)\geq 0. \]

The sequence $(\psi _ N)$ is decreasing because $\varphi _ j\leq 0$, and $\| \psi _ N\| _{L^1}\leq \sup _ j\| \varphi _ j\| _{L^1}<\infty $ by proposition 10.3.1. By monotone convergence the limit $\psi $ is integrable, so it is not identically $-\infty $, and theorem 5.10.1, applied on coordinate balls after adding local potentials of $C\omega $, shows that $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,C\omega )$. On $K$ every term $2^{-j}\varphi _ j$ is at most $-1$, so $\psi =-\infty $ on $K$. On every coordinate ball $U$ with a smooth function $h$ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=C\omega $, the function $\psi +h$ is plurisubharmonic and equals $-\infty $ on $K\cap U$. Hence $K$ is pluripolar, a contradiction. The last assertion follows: bounds for $\sup _ K\varphi $ give bounds for $\sup _ X\varphi $, and proposition 10.3.1 applies. $\square$

Corollary 10.3.6.134C98DC2772 Let $(\varphi _ i)$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ whose suprema $\sup _ X\varphi _ i$ are bounded below. Then its pointwise infimum $\varphi $ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $\varphi _ i\to \varphi $ in $L^1(X)$, and $\sup _ X\varphi _ i\to \sup _ X\varphi $.

Proof. Fix an index $i_0$. For $i\geq i_0$ the suprema are bounded above by $\sup _ X\varphi _{i_0}$, so the tail of the net lies in a compact subset of $L^1(X)$ by proposition 10.3.1, and a subnet converges in $L^1$ to some $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. For each fixed $i$, the members of the subnet are eventually below $\varphi _ i$, so $\psi \leq \varphi _ i$ almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere, as noted at the beginning of this section. Hence the pointwise infimum $\varphi $ is at least $\psi $ and is not identically $-\infty $. Applying theorem 5.10.1 on the coordinate balls of a finite cover, after adding local potentials of $\theta $, we find that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi _ i\to \varphi $ in $L^1(X)$. The continuity of the supremum in proposition 10.3.1 gives the last assertion. $\square$

Definition 10.3.7.9499229CD425 Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Recall from section 6.18 that $\varphi $ is more singular than $\psi $, written $\varphi \preceq \psi $, if $\varphi \leq \psi +C$ on $X$ for some constant $C$; we then also say that $\psi $ is less singular than $\varphi $. A potential $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has minimal singularities if every $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is more singular than $\psi $. If $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\ne \varnothing $, put

\[ V_\theta =\bigl (\sup \{ \varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):\varphi \leq 0\} \bigr )^*, \]

where ${}^*$ denotes the upper semicontinuous regularization.

Lemma 10.3.8.5755B2D3C604 Suppose that $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\neq \varnothing $. Then $V_\theta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, $\sup _ XV_\theta =0$, and $\varphi \leq V_\theta +\sup _ X\varphi $ for every $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$; in particular, $V_\theta $ has minimal singularities. Any two potentials with minimal singularities differ by a bounded function on the set where both are finite, and the notion of minimal singularities depends only on the class $\{ \theta \} $.

Proof. The family whose upper envelope defines $V_\theta $ in definition 10.3.7 is nonempty, because $\varphi -\sup _ X\varphi $ belongs to it for every $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, and it is bounded above by $0$. Theorem 5.10.2, applied on coordinate balls after adding smooth local potentials of $\theta $, therefore gives $V_\theta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $V_\theta \leq 0$; and $\sup _ XV_\theta =0$, because $V_\theta \geq \varphi -\sup _ X\varphi $ for every $\varphi $. The same inequality reads $\varphi \leq V_\theta +\sup _ X\varphi $, so $V_\theta $ has minimal singularities. Any two potentials with minimal singularities are each more singular than the other, so their difference is bounded on the set where both are finite. Replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ with $h$ smooth replaces $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ by $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )-h$; since $h$ is bounded, the notion of minimal singularities depends only on the class $\{ \theta \} $. $\square$

Lelong numbers of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are defined pointwise as in corollary 6.9.3: if $\varphi =v+g$ near $y$, with $v$ plurisubharmonic and $g$ smooth, then $\nu (\varphi ,y)=\nu (v,y)$, which does not depend on the decomposition. Hence the rules of proposition 6.7.9, the characterization of $\nu (\varphi ,y)$ as the largest $c\geq 0$ with $\varphi \leq c\log |z-z(y)|^2+O(1)$ near $y$ (proposition 6.7.1), and the upper semicontinuity (6.7.10) along sequences $\varphi _ j=v_ j-g$ with a common smooth $g$, all carry over.

Recall from proposition 6.9.7 the generic Lelong number of a plurisubharmonic function along an irreducible analytic subset, for instance along a prime divisor (an irreducible hypersurface). For a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $\varphi $ on a complex manifold and an irreducible analytic subset $Z$, we define in the same way the generic Lelong number $\nu (\varphi ,Z)=\inf _{y\in Z}\nu (\varphi ,y)$ of $\varphi $ along $Z$. The infimum is attained at every point of $Z$ outside a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$. Indeed, $\lambda =\nu (\varphi ,Z)$ is finite, and for every integer $j\geq 1$ the set $A_ j=\{ y\in Z:\nu (\varphi ,y)\geq \lambda +1/j\} $ is an analytic subset of $Z$: near each point of $Z$ it is the intersection of $Z$ with the set $\{ \nu (v,\cdot )\geq \lambda +1/j\} $ for a local plurisubharmonic function $v=\varphi -g$ as above, which is analytic by theorem 6.9.5. It is a proper subset of $Z$ by the definition of $\lambda $, and $\nu (\varphi ,y)=\lambda $ at every point $y\in Z\setminus \bigcup _ jA_ j$. By proposition 6.9.7, the complement in $Z$ of a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $Z$ is dense in $Z$; in particular, $\nu (\varphi ,Z)$ is attained on a dense subset of $Z$. If $\pi \colon Y\to X$ is a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$ and $E\subset Y$ is a prime divisor, we put $\nu (\varphi ,E)=\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,E)$ whenever $\varphi \circ \pi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, as in proposition 6.9.7.

Proposition 10.3.9.4C3B45D704A9 Let $\pi \colon Y\to X$ be a proper bimeromorphic holomorphic map from a complex manifold $Y$. Then $Y$ is compact and connected, and $\varphi \mapsto \varphi \circ \pi $ is a bijection from $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ onto $\mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$, which is continuous for convergence in $L^1(X)$ and $L^1(Y)$, the latter taken with respect to any smooth positive volume form. For every prime divisor $E$ on $Y$, the generic Lelong numbers $\nu (\varphi ,E)=\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,E)$ are bounded above uniformly for $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. The manifold $Y=\pi ^{-1}(X)$ is compact, because $\pi $ is proper. By the definition of bimeromorphic maps (section 9.4), there is a nowhere dense analytic subset $E_0\subset X$ such that $\pi ^{-1}(E_0)$ is nowhere dense and $\pi $ restricts to an isomorphism of $Y\setminus \pi ^{-1}(E_0)$ onto $X\setminus E_0$. The set $X\setminus E_0$ is connected by lemma 9.2.9, hence so is its dense preimage, and therefore $Y$ is connected. The image $\pi (Y)$ is closed, since proper maps are closed, and it contains the dense set $X\setminus E_0$; so $\pi $ is surjective. By proposition 9.8.3 there is an analytic subset $A\subset X$ of codimension at least two such that $\pi $ is an isomorphism over $X\setminus A$. Then $\pi ^{-1}(A)$ is an analytic subset of $Y$ different from $Y$, because $\pi $ is surjective. Since $Y$ is connected, $\pi ^{-1}(A)$ is locally contained in the zero set of a holomorphic function that does not vanish identically on a connected neighborhood (section 9.2), so it is nowhere dense and a null set (lemma 5.5.1). The same holds for $A$ in $X$.

If $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, let $B$ be a coordinate ball and $h$ a smooth function on $B$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $ (lemma 6.14.2). On every connected coordinate neighborhood $W\subset \pi ^{-1}(B)$ the function $(\varphi +h)\circ \pi $ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $, by proposition 5.9.3, and the second case is excluded because $\varphi +h$ is finite almost everywhere on the image under the biholomorphism $\pi $ of the nonempty open set $W\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$. Hence $\varphi \circ \pi =(\varphi +h)\circ \pi -h\circ \pi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, with $\pi ^*\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi \circ \pi )=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}((\varphi +h)\circ \pi )\geq 0$ on $\pi ^{-1}(B)$. Thus $\varphi \circ \pi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$. If $\varphi \circ \pi =\varphi '\circ \pi $, then $\varphi =\varphi '$ on $X\setminus A$, hence almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere, as noted at the beginning of this section; this proves injectivity.

Conversely, let $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$. Over $X\setminus A$ it descends to the function $\psi \circ \pi ^{-1}$, which is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic there. It is bounded above, because $\psi $ is upper semicontinuous on the compact manifold $Y$. On a coordinate ball $B$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=\theta $ on $B$, the plurisubharmonic function $\psi \circ \pi ^{-1}+h$ on $B\setminus A$ is therefore locally bounded above near $A$, and it extends to a plurisubharmonic function on $B$ by theorem 5.11.1. The extensions on two balls agree outside the null set $A$, hence everywhere, so they define $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. The pullback $\varphi \circ \pi $ equals $\psi $ outside the null set $\pi ^{-1}(A)$, hence everywhere. This proves surjectivity.

For continuity, we use proposition 10.3.1 on $X$ and on $Y$; by its last assertion, its assertions for a fixed background hold on $Y$. Let $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ in $L^1(X)$. By the continuity of the supremum, it suffices to treat normalized functions, $\sup _ X\varphi _ j=\sup _ X\varphi =0$, since subtracting the suprema preserves $L^1$ convergence. The pullbacks have the same suprema, because $\pi $ is surjective, so they are relatively compact in $L^1(Y)$, and every cluster value lies in $\mathrm{PSH}(Y,\pi ^*\theta )$. On compact subsets of $Y\setminus \pi ^{-1}(A)$, a change of variables shows that every $L^1$ cluster value of $(\varphi _ j\circ \pi )$ coincides almost everywhere with $\varphi \circ \pi $; since $\pi ^{-1}(A)$ is a null set, every cluster value is $\varphi \circ \pi $. A relatively compact sequence in a metric space with a single cluster value converges to it; hence $\varphi _ j\circ \pi \to \varphi \circ \pi $ in $L^1(Y)$.

Finally, $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,E)$ is the infimum over $y\in E$ of the point Lelong numbers $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,y)$. Near $y$ choose a smooth $g$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=\pi ^*\theta $, so that the functions $\varphi \circ \pi +g$ are plurisubharmonic. By (6.7.10), applied to them, each $\nu (\varphi \circ \pi ,y)$ is upper semicontinuous for $L^1$ convergence of $\varphi \circ \pi $, hence, by the continuity just proved, for $L^1$ convergence of $\varphi $; and an infimum of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous. Since Lelong numbers do not change when a constant is added, we may restrict to the normalized functions, which form a compact set by proposition 10.3.1. An upper semicontinuous function with finite values attains its maximum on a compact set, which gives the uniform bound. $\square$

Corollary 10.3.10.E6419D4591F9 Let $B'$ be a coordinate ball in $X$ and $B\Subset B'$ a ball in the same coordinates, and let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be bounded near $\overline{B'}$. There is $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $\psi \geq \varphi $, $\psi =\varphi $ on $X\setminus B$, $\psi $ bounded near $\overline B$, and $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )^ n=0$ on $B$. It is the largest function in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with these properties, and any two functions with these properties that are continuous on $\overline B$ coincide.

Proof. Choose a ball $B''$ in the same coordinates with $\overline B\subset B''$ and $\overline{B''}\subset B'$. Write $\theta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ on $B''$, with $h$ smooth (lemma 6.14.2), and apply corollary 7.7.5 to the locally bounded plurisubharmonic function $\varphi +h$ on $B''$. Subtracting $h$ from the result on $B''$ and keeping $\varphi $ outside $B''$ gives $\psi $, since the result equals $\varphi +h$ on $B''\setminus B$; here $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi )^ n=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\psi +h))^ n$ is the Bedford–Taylor product of the bounded plurisubharmonic function $\psi +h$. If $\psi '$ is another function with the stated properties, then by upper semicontinuity $\varlimsup _{z\to \zeta ,\, z\in B}\psi '(z)\leq \psi '(\zeta )=\varphi (\zeta )$ for every $\zeta \in \partial B$. Hence $\psi '+h$ belongs to the family whose upper envelope is $\psi +h$ in corollary 7.7.5, and $\psi '\leq \psi $ on $B$. If $\psi '$ is continuous on $\overline B$, then its boundary values $\varphi |_{\partial B}$ are continuous, and $\psi '+h$ is the solution of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem with the continuous boundary values $(\varphi +h)|_{\partial B}$; by the uniqueness in theorem 7.7.4, any two such continuous functions coincide. $\square$
