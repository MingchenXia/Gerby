7.2 Complex Hessian equations

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

12 Complex Hessian equations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, let $\omega $ be a smooth positive Hermitian form, and fix $2\leq m<n$. The complex Hessian operator $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m}$ replaces the determinant by an intermediate symmetric polynomial. Its admissible cone allows negative eigenvalues, so the product calculus for plurisubharmonic potentials from chapter 1 no longer applies directly.

For a continuous admissible weak solution with smooth positive density depending on the potential, the difficulty is to obtain derivative estimates before smoothness is known. Proper exponential equations associate smooth solutions to its smooth admissible approximations. The complex-Hessian estimate is independent of derivatives of the prescribed approximation, so it survives passage to the limit. Uniform convergence defines the continuous mixed products, while obstacle envelopes give the domination principle that identifies the limit with the original solution. A concave uniformly elliptic extension then supplies classical interior regularity. The determinant case is treated in theorem 10.5.2; the argument here concerns the intermediate Hessian degrees. Uniform stability also constructs solutions for strictly increasing exponents, by contraction between constant barriers.

Bounded admissible potentials require decreasing approximation. The recursion first gives currents with measure coefficients below degree $m$; local Hessian capacities control multiplication by their discontinuous potentials. Quasicontinuity and weighted convergence make the products symmetric and allow all approximation indices to tend to their limits together. At degree $m$, the positive product after wedging with $\omega ^{n-m}$ is a Radon measure.

These weighted limits pass locality and the maximum inequality to bounded potentials. Truncation above the infimum then proves that the Hessian measure of each potential is nonzero on an arbitrary Hermitian background. In global comparison, the maximum inequality leaves a signed total-mass difference. The complex Hessians of the background powers determine when this difference vanishes for every bounded admissible list. On small sublevel sets, a modified comparison estimate absorbs the torsion error on arbitrary Hermitian backgrounds. It yields strict domination, bounded stability for proper equations and smoothness for their solutions with smooth data between constant barriers.

12.1 The admissible cone

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For $\lambda \in \mathbb R^ n$, let $S_ j(\lambda )$ be the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $j$, put $\sigma _ j=S_ j/\binom nj$ and $\sigma _0=1$, and set

\[ \Gamma _ m=\{ \lambda :S_ j(\lambda )>0,\ 1\leq j\leq m\} . \]

A real $(1,1)$-form belongs to $\Gamma _ m(\omega )$ if its eigenvalues relative to $\omega $ belong to $\Gamma _ m$. Write $C_{n,m}$ for the corresponding closed cone of Hermitian matrices. The algebraic assertions below also apply when $m=n$.

We use the classical Gårding facts: the closed spectral cone is convex, contains the nonnegative matrices, and is preserved by adding them; adding a positive matrix puts a closed-cone matrix in its interior. The normalized root $F=\sigma _ m^{1/m}$ is concave and increasing there. Polarized products of $k$ forms in $\Gamma _ k(\omega )$ with $\omega ^{n-k}$ are positive, and Maclaurin's inequalities give $\sigma _ j^{1/j}\geq \sigma _ k^{1/k}$ for $j\leq k$. These algebraic facts will serve as inputs to the analytic arguments.

A continuous real function $v$ on $X$ is admissible if

12.1
\begin{equation} (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)\wedge \gamma _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \gamma _{m-1} \wedge \omega ^{n-m}\geq 0 \end{equation}

in distributions for every smooth collection $\gamma _1,\ldots ,\gamma _{m-1}\in \Gamma _ m(\omega )$. For smooth $v$ this is equivalent to the pointwise closed-cone condition; strict admissibility means membership in the open cone. Maxima and convex combinations preserve continuous admissibility. To check the maximum property, fix one smooth positive linear test in (12.1). Locally a smooth solution of its inhomogeneous linear equation reduces the assertion to the maximum property for subsolutions of a smooth elliptic operator.

For a bounded upper semicontinuous function $v$, admissibility means that every local $C^2$ upper test $q$ at $x$ satisfies

12.2
\begin{equation} \omega (x)+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q(x)\in \overline{\Gamma _ m(\omega (x))}. \end{equation}

For continuous $v$, this agrees with (12.1): Gårding duality reduces both conditions to subsolution inequalities for smooth strictly elliptic linear operators. We use their classical viscosity–distributional equivalence, with the usual subharmonic representative in the bounded class.

The first estimate controls a scalar shift of the cone. It will give lower bounds for every coefficient of the linearized Hessian operator.

Lemma 12.1.1. For $\lambda \in \Gamma _ m$, put

\[ e_ m=\frac{\sigma _ m(\lambda )}{m\sigma _{m-1}(\lambda )}. \]

Then $\lambda -t\mathbf1\in \Gamma _ m$ for $0\leq t<e_ m$. If $m\geq 2$, the conclusion also holds for $t=e_ m$. For $m=1$, the endpoint lies on the boundary.

Proof. The polynomial

\[ P(x)=\sigma _ m(\lambda +x\mathbf1) =\sum _{j=0}^ m\binom mj\sigma _ j(\lambda )x^{m-j} \]

is a positive multiple of the $(n-m)$th derivative of $\prod _ i(x+\lambda _ i)$. Hence all its roots are real, by Rolle's theorem. Its coefficients are positive, so all its roots are strictly negative. Write $P(x)=\prod _{i=1}^ m(x+r_ i)$ with $r_ i>0$. Logarithmic differentiation at zero gives

\[ \sum _{i=1}^ m r_ i^{-1} =\frac{m\sigma _{m-1}(\lambda )}{\sigma _ m(\lambda )} =e_ m^{-1}. \]

Thus $\min _ i r_ i\geq e_ m$, with strict inequality if $m\geq 2$. For $x> -\min _ i r_ i$, every derivative of $P$ is positive: differentiating its product expansion produces sums of products of positive factors. Since

\[ P^{(m-j)}(x)=\frac{m!}{j!}\sigma _ j(\lambda +x\mathbf1), \qquad 1\leq j\leq m, \]

all the required symmetric polynomials are positive at $x=-t$. When $m=1$, the sole root is $-e_1$. $\square$

Lemma 12.1.2. If $\alpha ,\gamma \in \Gamma _ m(\omega )$, then

\[ \frac{\alpha ^{m-1}\wedge \gamma \wedge \omega ^{n-m}}{\omega ^ n} \frac{\alpha \wedge \omega ^{n-1}}{\omega ^ n} \geq \frac1{m!} \frac{\alpha ^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m}}{\omega ^ n} \frac{\gamma \wedge \omega ^{n-1}}{\omega ^ n}. \]

The assertion extends to $\gamma \in \overline{\Gamma _ m(\omega )}$ by continuity.

Proof. Write $\sigma _ k=\alpha ^ k\wedge \omega ^{n-k}/\omega ^ n$, and $M_ k=\alpha ^{k-1}\wedge \gamma \wedge \omega ^{n-k}/\omega ^ n$. For $1\leq k<m$, the cone shift with index $k+1$ and Gårding positivity give

\[ M_{k+1}\geq tM_ k \quad \left(0\leq t<\frac{\sigma _{k+1}}{(k+1)\sigma _ k}\right). \]

Let $t$ increase to the displayed endpoint. Iterating, starting with $M_1=\gamma \wedge \omega ^{n-1}/\omega ^ n$, yields

\[ M_ m\geq \frac{\sigma _ m}{m!\sigma _1}M_1. \]

All denominators are positive. Approximating a form in the closed cone by $\gamma +\delta \omega $, $\delta \downarrow 0$, gives the last assertion. $\square$

At a diagonal Hermitian matrix $\Lambda $ in the open cone, order its eigenvalues as $\lambda _1\geq \cdots \geq \lambda _ n$ and write

\[ f=F(\Lambda ),\quad F_ i=\partial _{\lambda _ i}F,\quad S=\operatorname {tr}\Lambda ,\quad \mathcal F=\sum _ iF_ i, \quad Q=\sum _ iF_ i\lambda _ i^2. \]

The next inequalities use these derivatives of the normalized root, rather than derivatives of the polynomial $S_ m$.

Lemma 12.1.3. If $M$ belongs to the closed Hermitian $m$-cone, then

\[ W:=\sum _ iF_ iM_{i\bar i}\geq \frac{f}{m!S}\operatorname {tr}M, \qquad F_ i\geq \frac{f}{m!S},\qquad |M_{1\bar1}|\leq \operatorname {tr}M. \]

No simultaneous diagonalization of $M$ and $\Lambda $ is required.

Proof. In the wedge notation of the mixed-trace estimate, differentiation gives $DF_\Lambda (M)=fM_ m/\sigma _ m$. The estimate $M_ m\geq \sigma _ m M_1/(m!\sigma _1)$, with $M_1=\operatorname {tr}M/n$ and $\sigma _1=S/n$, proves the first inequality. The positive semidefinite rank-one coordinate projections belong to the closed $m$-cone; taking $M$ to be each projection proves the second inequality. The closed $m$-cone is contained in the closed $2$-cone. Its eigenvalues $\mu $ satisfy

\[ \operatorname {tr}M\geq 0,\qquad \sum _ i\mu _ i^2=(\operatorname {tr}M)^2-2S_2(\mu ) \leq (\operatorname {tr}M)^2. \]

Thus the operator norm of $M$ is at most its trace, which proves the last inequality. The wedge pairing computes the derivative in the diagonal frame of $\Lambda $, regardless of the eigenvectors of $M$. $\square$

Lemma 12.1.4. If $0<f_0\leq f\leq f_1$, then

\[ Q:=\sum _ iF_ i\lambda _ i^2 \geq \frac{f_0}{m}S-\frac{n-m}{m}f_1^2, \qquad \mathcal F\geq 1. \]

Proof. The derivative of the normalized root is $F_ i=fS_{m-1}(\lambda |i)/(mS_ m)$. The splitting identity gives

\[ Q=\frac fm S-\frac{(m+1)f}{mS_ m}S_{m+1}. \]

For $m=n$, set $S_{n+1}=0$. If $S_{m+1}\leq 0$, the second term is nonnegative. Otherwise $\lambda \in \Gamma _{m+1}$ and Maclaurin gives $S_{m+1}\leq \binom n{m+1}f^{m+1}$. Since $S_ m=\binom nm f^ m$, the subtracted term is at most $(n-m)f^2/m$. This proves the first assertion. Summing the derivatives gives $\mathcal F=f\sigma _{m-1}/\sigma _ m\geq 1$, again by Maclaurin. $\square$

When $m<n$, admissible forms need not be semipositive. Positivity of a mixed Hessian measure therefore does not directly bound every coefficient of its factors. For degrees below $m$, deleted-coordinate positivity supplies the required absolute bound: if $S_{j;I}$ means $S_ j$ with the coordinates indexed by $I$ omitted, then $S_{j;I}(\lambda )>0$ for $j+|I|\leq m$, by polarized Gårding positivity.

Lemma 12.1.5. If $0\leq r<m$, $\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ r\in \Gamma _ m(\omega )$, and $\Xi $ is a smooth complementary form, then

\[ |\alpha _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha _ r\wedge \Xi | \leq C_{n,m,\Xi }(\alpha _1+\cdots +\alpha _ r)^ r\wedge \omega ^{n-r}. \]

For $r=0$ the right-hand side means $C_{n,m,\Xi }\omega ^ n$.

Proof. First take a single form $\alpha $, diagonal in an $\omega $-unitary frame, with ordered eigenvalues $\lambda \in \Gamma _ m$. For $1\leq r<m$, repeated splitting and deleted-coordinate positivity give $S_ r(\lambda )\geq \lambda _1\cdots \lambda _ r$. The sum of the last $n-r$ eigenvalues is positive, so each negative eigenvalue has modulus at most $(n-r)\lambda _{r+1}$. Thus every product of $r$ distinct eigenvalues has modulus at most

\[ (n-r)^ r\lambda _1\cdots \lambda _ r\leq (n-r)^ r S_ r(\lambda ). \]

Indeed, replace its negative factors by this bound and its positive factors by the corresponding largest eigenvalues. In the diagonal frame only complementary diagonal coefficients of $\Xi $ contribute to $\alpha ^ r\wedge \Xi $. This proves $|\alpha ^ r\wedge \Xi |\leq C_{n,m,\Xi }\alpha ^ r\wedge \omega ^{n-r}$.

Apply this bound to the polynomial $(\alpha _1+t_2\alpha _2+\cdots +t_ r\alpha _ r)^ r\wedge \Xi $, with $0\leq t_ i\leq 1$. Its absolute value is bounded by the stated right-hand side, by polarized positivity and monotonicity on the cone. A fixed invertible interpolation matrix on this finite-dimensional polynomial space bounds each coefficient by its supremum on the cube. The coefficient of $t_2\cdots t_ r$ is a fixed positive multiple of $\alpha _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha _ r\wedge \Xi $. This proves the mixed bound. The closed-cone case follows by adding $\varepsilon \omega $ to each form and taking the limit. $\square$

The next coefficient bound also controls a torsion form whose diagonal coefficients can have either sign. It compares each two-coordinate coefficient with the positive coefficients of the associated gradient pairing.

Lemma 12.1.6. If $\lambda \in \overline{\Gamma _ m}$, $1\leq q\leq \min \{ m-1,n-2\} $, and $i\ne j$, then, with $N=n-2$ and

\[ c_{N,q}=\frac{q(N-q)}{(q+1)(N-q+1)}<1, \]

one has

\[ |S_{q;ij}(\lambda )|^2 \leq \frac{S_{q;i}(\lambda )S_{q;j}(\lambda )}{1-c_{N,q}}. \]

This includes $q=N$, when $c_{N,N}=0$.

Proof. First prove the unnormalized Newton inequality for an arbitrary real list $\mu $ of length $N$. For $1\leq q<N$, put $R(x)=\prod _{a=1}^ N(x+\mu _ a)$ and $G=R^{(N-q-1)}$. Repeated Rolle's theorem makes $G$ a real-rooted polynomial of degree $d=q+1$. Away from its roots $r_ a$, logarithmic differentiation and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\[ G'^2-GG''=G^2\sum _{a=1}^ d\frac1{(x-r_ a)^2} \geq \frac1dG^2\left(\sum _{a=1}^ d\frac1{x-r_ a}\right)^2 =\frac1dG'^2. \]

Continuity extends this polynomial inequality to every real $x$. At zero,

\[ G=(N-q-1)!S_{q+1}(\mu ),\quad G'=(N-q)!S_ q(\mu ),\quad G''=(N-q+1)!S_{q-1}(\mu ). \]

Consequently $S_{q-1}(\mu )S_{q+1}(\mu )\leq c_{N,q}S_ q(\mu )^2$. No sign restriction on $\mu $ or on these coefficients was used. For $q=N$, its left side is zero and the same assertion holds with $c_{N,N}=0$.

Apply this to the list with $i,j$ deleted. Put $A=S_{q-1;ij}$, $B=S_{q;ij}$, $C=S_{q+1;ij}$ and $D_ i=S_{q;i}$, $D_ j=S_{q;j}$. Deleted-coordinate positivity gives $A,D_ i,D_ j\geq 0$: the required degrees are $q+1\leq m$. Splitting in the two omitted coordinates gives the exact identity

\[ D_ iD_ j=A S_{q+1}(\lambda )+B^2-AC. \]

Since $S_{q+1}(\lambda )\geq 0$ and $AC\leq c_{N,q}B^2$, this is at least $(1-c_{N,q})B^2$. There is no division by $A$ or $D_ i$; all degenerate endpoints are therefore included. Equivalently one may first add $\epsilon \mathbf1$ and pass to the closed cone. $\square$

A smooth maximum preserves the cone condition and can be chosen monotone in its smoothing scale as well as in its two arguments.

Lemma 12.1.7. If smooth uniformly bounded admissible $u_ j\downarrow u$ and $v_ j\downarrow v$ are given, there are smooth uniformly bounded admissible $w_ j\downarrow \max \{ u,v\} $. For any chosen $\epsilon _ j>0$ decreasing to zero, they can be chosen to satisfy $w_ j=u_ j$ on $\{ u_ j-v_ j>\epsilon _ j\} $ and $w_ j=v_ j$ on $\{ v_ j-u_ j>\epsilon _ j\} $. For smooth functions $f,g$ on an open subset of $X$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f,\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\in \overline{\Gamma _ m(\omega )}$, the regularized maximum $M_\epsilon (f,g)$ constructed below satisfies the same unshifted cone condition.

Proof. Choose a nonnegative even smooth probability density $\zeta $ supported in $[-1,1]$, and set

\[ r_\epsilon (t)=\int |t-\epsilon s|\zeta (s)\, \mathrm ds, \qquad M_\epsilon (x,y)=\tfrac 12(x+y+r_\epsilon (x-y)). \]

The function $r_\epsilon $ is smooth and convex, $|r_\epsilon '|\leq 1$, and $r_\epsilon (t)=|t|$ for $|t|\geq \epsilon $. Jensen's inequality and the triangle inequality give

\[ \max \{ x,y\} \leq M_\epsilon (x,y) \leq \max \{ x,y\} +\epsilon /2. \]

Its partial derivatives in $x,y$ are nonnegative. For fixed $t$, $r_\epsilon (t)$ is an even convex function of $\epsilon $, since $\zeta $ is even. It is therefore nondecreasing for $\epsilon \geq 0$. Consequently $w_ j=M_{\epsilon _ j}(u_ j,v_ j)$ decreases pointwise to $\max \{ u,v\} $ and has the asserted equalities and bounds.

Put $h_ j=u_ j-v_ j$ and $a_ j=(1+r_{\epsilon _ j}'(h_ j))/2$. The chain rule gives

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j =a_ j(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)+(1-a_ j)(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j) +\tfrac 12r_{\epsilon _ j}''(h_ j) \mathrm dh_ j\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}h_ j. \]

Here $0\leq a_ j\leq 1$, and the last form is semipositive. Convexity of the cone and its stability under semipositive addition prove admissibility. For smooth local functions satisfying the unshifted cone condition, omit $\omega $ from this chain rule; the same argument proves preservation of that condition. The pointwise maximum is itself admissible: an upper test for it at a point is an upper test for a potential attaining the maximum there. Its canonical representative is thus the indicated pointwise maximum. $\square$

Lemma 12.1.8. If bounded admissible $u,v$ satisfy $u\leq v$ almost everywhere with respect to $\omega ^ n$, then $u\leq v$ everywhere.

Proof. By 12.1.7, $w=\max \{ u,v\} $ is bounded admissible with its indicated canonical representative. Since $w=v$ almost everywhere, they represent the same distribution. The canonical subharmonic representative used in (12.2) is determined by that distribution. Thus $w=v$ pointwise, which is the assertion. $\square$

12.2 Liouville and gradient estimates

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The gradient estimate comes from rescaling a sequence at points of large gradient. The background then disappears, leaving a maximal function for a constant Euclidean cone. We first establish the Liouville theorem that rules out such a nonconstant limit.

For this Euclidean argument allow $1\leq m\leq n$. Write $H(v)=(v_{i\bar j})$. A continuous real function $v$ on a Euclidean domain is admissible for $C_{n,m}$ if

\[ \operatorname {tr}(QH(v))\geq 0 \quad \text{in distributions for every }Q\in C_{n,m}^*, \]

where $C_{n,m}^*=\{ Q:\operatorname {tr}(QA)\geq 0\text{ for all }A\in C_{n,m}\} $. For smooth functions this says $H(v)\in C_{n,m}$. Call $v$ maximal if every smooth lower test has Hessian outside the interior of $C_{n,m}$ at the contact point. Admissibility implies subharmonicity and is preserved by nonnegative convolution. The cone polynomial $S_ m$ increases under nonnegative matrix addition in the interior.

Lemma 12.2.1. Local uniform limits of admissible maximal functions are admissible and maximal. Suppose $v$ is admissible and maximal on a bounded open set $D$, and $h$ is smooth and admissible there. If both are continuous on $\overline D$ and $h\leq v$ on $\partial D$, then $h\leq v$ on $D$.

Proof. The distributional inequalities pass to local uniform limits. If a lower test $h$ for the limit at $z_0$ has Hessian in the interior, subtract $\delta |z-z_0|^2$, for sufficiently small $\delta >0$, to make the contact strict while retaining interior admissibility near $z_0$. Interior minimum points for the approximants then tend to $z_0$. Adding the minimum value produces lower tests for them with Hessian in the interior, contradicting their maximality.

For comparison choose $R$ with $\overline D\subset \{ |z|\leq R\} $. If $h>v$ somewhere, the function $h_\delta =h+\delta (|z|^2-R^2)$ still exceeds $v$ somewhere for small $\delta >0$ and is at most $v$ on the boundary. The negative minimum of $v-h_\delta $ is attained inside $D$. Since $H(h_\delta )=H(h)+\delta I$ belongs to the interior, adding the minimum value gives a forbidden lower test. This argument requires no regularity of the boundary. $\square$

Theorem 12.2.2. Every bounded globally Lipschitz admissible function on $\mathbb C^ n$ that is maximal for $C_{n,m}$ is constant.

Proof. For $m=1$, the distributional subharmonicity and the lower-test condition say that the function is a viscosity solution of the Laplace equation. The elementary equivalence with harmonicity and bounded harmonic Liouville give the conclusion. For $m=n$, admissibility is distributional plurisubharmonicity. The continuous representative is plurisubharmonic, and bounded plurisubharmonic Liouville applies. For a fixed $m\geq 2$, we therefore argue by induction on $n\geq m$.

The reduction in dimension is particularly simple for this cone. For a Hermitian $(n-1)$-by-$(n-1)$ matrix $A$ and $m<n$,

\[ S_ j(A\oplus 0)=S_ j(A),\qquad 1\leq j\leq m. \]

Consequently $A\oplus 0$ belongs to $C_{n,m}$, respectively its interior, exactly when $A$ belongs to $C_{n-1,m}$, respectively its interior. For the closed cones, the nonnegative inequalities characterize the closure: the expansion of $S_ j(\lambda +\delta \mathbf1)$ has nonnegative coefficients and a strictly positive $\delta ^ j$ term. If an admissible function is independent of $z_ n$, convolving it and using the block identity shows that the function in the remaining variables is admissible. Extending its lower tests independently of $z_ n$ shows that maximality also passes to dimension $n-1$.

Suppose $v$ is nonconstant. Normalize $\inf v=0$, $\sup v=1$, and let $L$ be its Lipschitz constant. Choose a smooth radial nonnegative probability kernel $\eta $, supported in the closed unit ball and positive in its interior. Put

\[ [f]_ r(x)=\int f(x+y)\eta _ r(y)\, \mathrm dy, \qquad \eta _ r(y)=r^{-2n}\eta (y/r),\qquad v_\epsilon =[v]_\epsilon . \]

Both $v$ and $v^2$ are subharmonic: for the second assertion use $\Delta (v_\epsilon ^2)=2v_\epsilon \Delta v_\epsilon +2|\nabla v_\epsilon |^2\geq 0$ and pass to the uniform limit. Their radial averages increase with $r$, by monotonicity of spherical means. For either $f=v$ or $f=v^2$,

\[ |[f]_ r(x)-[f]_ r(y)| \leq \| f\| _\infty \| \eta _ r(\cdot -x)-\eta _ r(\cdot -y)\| _{L^1} \leq C\frac{|x-y|}{r}. \]

Thus the limiting average is independent of its center. The submean inequality makes it at least $f(x)$ for every $x$, and the upper bound is $\sup f=1$. Hence

\[ [v]_ r(x)\longrightarrow 1,\qquad [v^2]_ r(x)\longrightarrow 1. \]

First suppose there are a fixed $\rho >0$, numbers $\epsilon _ k\downarrow 0$, $r_ k\to \infty $, points $x_ k$, and unit complex directions $\xi _ k$, such that

\[ [v^2]_{r_ k}(x_ k)+[v]_\rho (x_ k)-2v(x_ k)\geq \frac43, \qquad \int _{B(x_ k,r_ k)}|\partial _{\xi _ k}v_{\epsilon _ k}|^2\, \mathrm dy\longrightarrow 0. \]

Translate and rotate to make $x_ k$ the origin and $\xi _ k$ the last complex direction. Compactness of uniformly bounded Lipschitz functions and the preceding lemma give a locally uniform limit $a$ that is admissible and maximal. The transformed mollifications have the same limit. Their last derivatives tend to zero in $L^2$ on every fixed ball, so $\partial _{z_ n}a=0$ in distributions. Since $a$ is real and continuous, it is independent of $z_ n$. The induction hypothesis gives $a=c\in [0,1]$. In the displayed gap inequality the first average is at most one, while the fixed-radius average and the value at the origin tend to $c$. This would give $4/3\leq 1-c\leq 1$, a contradiction.

If this case does not occur, then for every fixed $\rho >0$ there is $C_\rho \geq 1$ such that

\[ \int _{B(x,r)}|\partial _\xi v_\epsilon |^2\, \mathrm dy\geq C_\rho ^{-1} \]

for every unit complex direction $\xi $, whenever $0<\epsilon <C_\rho ^{-1}$, $r>C_\rho $ and $[v^2]_ r(x)+[v]_\rho (x)-2v(x)\geq 4/3$. Indeed, failure for every integer choice $C_\rho =k$ produces the sequences excluded above. The dependence on the fixed radius $\rho $ is retained. Translate so that $v(0)<1/12$. Choose $\rho $ with $[v]_\rho (0)>3/4$, then $r>C_\rho $ with $[v^2]_ r(0)>3/4$. The open set

\[ D=\{ x:2v(x)<[v^2]_ r(x)+[v]_\rho (x)-4/3\} \]

contains the origin.

On this set, $[v^2]_ r-c|z|^2$ is smooth and admissible for some fixed $c>0$. To prove this, decompose

\[ H([v_\epsilon ^2]_ r) = [2v_\epsilon H(v_\epsilon )]_ r +[2(\partial _ i v_\epsilon \overline{\partial _ jv_\epsilon })_{ij}]_ r =A_\epsilon +B_\epsilon . \]

The matrix $A_\epsilon $ belongs to $C_{n,m}$ by convexity and $v_\epsilon \geq 0$. For every complex direction the unweighted energy on $B(x,r)$ is at least $C_\rho ^{-1}$. Since $|\nabla v_\epsilon |\leq L$, a sufficiently thin boundary annulus carries at most half that amount, uniformly in $x,\epsilon ,\xi $. On the remaining smaller closed ball $\eta _ r$ has a positive minimum. Therefore $B_\epsilon \geq c_0I$ on $D$, with $c_0>0$ independent of small $\epsilon $. Choose $0<c\leq c_0$. Then $A_\epsilon +B_\epsilon -cI\in C_{n,m}$. Uniform convergence of $v_\epsilon ^2$ and convolution at the fixed radius give convergence of every derivative to those of $[v^2]_ r$, proving the assertion.

The smooth function

\[ h=\tfrac 12\bigl ([v^2]_ r-c|z|^2+[v]_\rho -4/3\bigr ) \]

is admissible on $D$. The set $D'=\{ v<h\} $ contains the origin, is bounded, and lies in $D$. Continuity gives $v=h$ on its boundary. The comparison lemma gives $h\leq v$ on $D'$, a contradiction. This completes the induction. $\square$

A trace bound now suffices to bound the gradient. The following statement permits a smooth background different from the metric.

Proposition 12.2.3. Fix a compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n$, a smooth Hermitian form $\omega $, a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\theta $, an integer $2\leq m\leq n$, and constants $M,A,B>0$. There is a uniform bound for $\sup _ X|\partial u|_\omega $ over all smooth real $u$ such that $\theta _ u=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\in \Gamma _ m(\omega )$ and

\[ |u|\leq M,\qquad \frac{\theta _ u^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m}}{\omega ^ n}\leq B, \qquad \operatorname {tr}_\omega \theta _ u \leq A\bigl (1+\sup _ X|\partial u|_\omega ^2\bigr ). \]

The bound requires no derivatives of the Hessian density.

Proof. In local holomorphic coordinates write $\omega =\mathrm i g_{i\bar j}\, \mathrm dz_ i\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$ and $\theta _ u=\mathrm i(b_{i\bar j}+\kappa u_{i\bar j})\, \mathrm dz_ i\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$, where the fixed constant $\kappa >0$ accounts for the convention for $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$. For eigenvalues $\lambda \in \Gamma _ m$, $m\geq 2$, one has

\[ \sum _ i\lambda _ i^2 =\left(\sum _ i\lambda _ i\right)^2-2S_2(\lambda ) <\left(\sum _ i\lambda _ i\right)^2. \]

Thus the assumed trace estimate bounds every eigenvalue in absolute value by $A(1+\sup |\partial u|^2)$.

Suppose contrary to the claim that a sequence $u_ j$ has maxima $R_ j=\sup |\partial u_ j|_\omega \to \infty $, attained at $x_ j$. Pass to $x_ j\to x_\infty $ and work in a fixed chart, changing coordinates linearly so that $g(x_\infty )=I$. If $y_ j$ is the coordinate of $x_ j$, set

\begin{align*} v_ j(z)& =u_ j(y_ j+z/R_ j),\qquad g_ j(z)=g(y_ j+z/R_ j),\\ E_ j(z)& =R_ j^{-2}b(y_ j+z/R_ j)+\kappa H(v_ j)(z). \end{align*}

These are defined on balls with radii tending to infinity. On each fixed ball $g_ j\to I$, the smooth background term tends to zero, and $E_ j$ is uniformly bounded by the eigenvalue estimate. In particular $H(v_ j)$ and its Euclidean trace are uniformly bounded. Interior estimates for the fixed Euclidean Laplacian give local uniform $W^{2,q}$ bounds for every finite $q$. A diagonal subsequence converges in $C^1$ on compact subsets to a bounded globally Lipschitz $v$, with $|\partial v(0)|=1$.

The Hermitian matrices $g_ j^{-1/2}E_ jg_ j^{-1/2}$ belong to the interior of $C_{n,m}$ and differ from $\kappa H(v_ j)$ by a term tending uniformly to zero on fixed balls. Pairing with any fixed matrix in the dual cone and passing in distributions proves that $v$ is admissible. Its equation is

\[ \frac{S_ m(g_ j^{-1/2}E_ jg_ j^{-1/2})}{\binom nm} =R_ j^{-2m}\frac{\theta _{u_ j}^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m}}{\omega ^ n} (y_ j+z/R_ j)\leq BR_ j^{-2m}. \]

We show maximality by lower tests, rather than by an unproved convergence assertion for variable-background Hessian measures. If a smooth lower test $\psi $ at $z_0$ had $H(\psi )(z_0)$ in the interior, choose a small $\delta >0$ so that $H(\psi )-\delta I$ remains there near $z_0$. Strictifying the contact by subtracting $\delta |z-z_0|^2$ gives interior minimum points $z_ j\to z_0$ for $v_ j$ minus this test. At those points

\[ E_ j\geq R_ j^{-2}b+\kappa (H(\psi )-\delta I). \]

The matrix on the right, measured with $g_ j$, is uniformly inside the cone. Monotonicity of $S_ m$ under nonnegative addition gives a fixed positive lower bound for the left side of the preceding equation. This contradicts $BR_ j^{-2m}\to 0$. Hence $v$ is maximal. The Liouville theorem makes it constant, contradicting $|\partial v(0)|=1$. $\square$

On a fixed rescaled ball the metric varies by order $R_ j^{-1}$, whereas the background form enters with factor $R_ j^{-2}$. Thus this argument uses fixed smooth backgrounds and an upper bound for the Hessian density, without derivatives of that density.

Lemma 12.3.1. For $\epsilon >0$, let $\chi (t)=-(1/12)\log (2\epsilon -t)$ on $|t|\leq \epsilon $. Suppose $q=\chi '(t)\geq 8$. For any $a,b\in \mathbb C$,

Proof. Young's inequality gives $|a-b|^2\geq (7/8)|a|^2-7|b|^2$. Since $\chi ''=12q^2$, the coefficient of $|a|^2$ after subtracting $9q^2$ is $3q^2/2$. 12.4 Bounded complex Hessians and elliptic extensions

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Uniform complex-Hessian bounds give enough compactness both to pass the nonlinear products to a limit and to make the Hessian root uniformly elliptic. In coordinates use the metric matrix $g$ of the preceding section and write $H(u)=(u_{i\bar j})$. Coefficient products with bounded measurable factors will be identified with currents by convolution.

Lemma 12.4.1. Suppose $u_ j$ is smooth,

\[ \sup _ j\bigl (\| u_ j\| _\infty +\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\| _{L^\infty }\bigr )<\infty , \qquad u_ j\longrightarrow u\quad \hbox{uniformly on }X. \]

Then $u\in W^{2,p}(X)$ for every $1<p<\infty $, its distributional complex Hessian has bounded coefficients, and $u_ j\to u$ in $C^{1,\alpha }(X)$ for every $0<\alpha <1$. The same hypotheses give uniform $W^{2,p}$ bounds for the sequence.

Proof. On a coordinate ball the Euclidean Laplacian is $\Delta u_ j=4\sum _ i (u_ j)_{i\bar i}$, so it is uniformly bounded. The interior Laplace estimate on two nested balls gives

\[ \| u_ j\| _{W^{2,p}(B')}\leq C_ p \bigl (\| \Delta u_ j\| _{L^ p(B)}+\| u_ j\| _{L^ p(B)}\bigr ). \]

A finite cover gives the global bound. Weak $W^{2,p}$ limits coincide with the uniform limit. The bounded complex Hessians have weak-star limits in $L^\infty $; testing against compactly supported smooth functions identifies them with $H(u)$. Choose $p$ with $1-2n/p>\alpha $. The compact Sobolev embedding into $C^{1,\alpha }$ gives convergence of subsequences. Every such limit is $u$, hence the whole sequence converges. This estimates the full real Hessian in every finite $L^ p$ space, without asserting that it is bounded. $\square$

Lemma 12.4.2. For $1\leq a\leq k\leq n$, suppose $u_ j^ a$ and $u^ a$ satisfy the preceding lemma. Let $\theta _ a$ be fixed smooth real $(1,1)$-forms. Then

\[ \bigwedge _{a=1}^ k(\theta _ a+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j^ a) \longrightarrow \bigwedge _{a=1}^ k(\theta _ a+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u^ a) \quad \hbox{as currents}. \]

The current on the right is the product of the almost-everywhere coefficients. No closedness or positivity of the $\theta _ a$ is required. For each compactly supported smooth complementary test form $\eta $, the difference of its pairings is bounded by $C_\eta \max _ a\| u_ j^ a-u^ a\| _\infty $.

Proof. Set $D_ j^ a=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j^ a$ and $D^ a=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u^ a$. On a smaller coordinate ball, convolution gives smooth $u_\epsilon ^ a$ whose complex Hessians are uniformly bounded and converge to $H(u^ a)$ in every finite $L^ p$ space. Their coefficient products consequently converge in finite $L^ p$ spaces. In particular the products of the $D^ a$ are closed currents, since their smooth approximants are closed.

Expand the smooth background factors and telescope each remaining product. Every difference has the form

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ j^ a-u^ a)\wedge T_ j, \]

where $T_ j$ is a product of $D_ j^ b$ and $D^ b$ with $b\ne a$. It has uniformly bounded coefficients and is closed. Local convolution, followed by strong convergence of its finite coefficient products, justifies integration by parts:

\[ \left\langle \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ j^ a-u^ a)\wedge T_ j,\xi \right\rangle =\int (u_ j^ a-u^ a)\, T_ j\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\xi . \]

Here $\xi $ includes the chosen smooth background factors and the test form, so their derivatives are retained. The right-hand side has the asserted uniform bound. A finite partition of unity proves the claim on $X$. This argument also proves that the coefficient product is the distributional limit; it does not identify a separately defined bounded Hessian operator for all nonsmooth admissible functions. $\square$

Lemma 12.4.3. For $f_0>0$ and $L>0$, assume the set

\[ K=\{ A\in C_{n,m}:\operatorname {tr}A\leq L, \ \sigma _ m(A)\geq f_0^ m\} \]

is nonempty. It is a compact subset of the interior, and there are $\vartheta ,\Theta >0$, depending only on $n,m,f_0,L$, such that

\[ \vartheta I\leq DF_ A\leq \Theta I\qquad (A\in K). \]

The function on all Hermitian matrices defined by

\[ \widehat F(B)=\inf _{A\in K} \bigl (F(A)+\operatorname {tr}(DF_ A(B-A))\bigr ) \]

is concave, agrees with $F$ on $K$, and satisfies, for $P\geq 0$,

\[ \vartheta \operatorname {tr}P\leq \widehat F(B+P)-\widehat F(B) \leq \Theta \operatorname {tr}P. \]

Proof. In the closed $2$-cone,

\[ \sum _ i\lambda _ i^2=(\operatorname {tr}A)^2-2S_2(A) \leq L^2. \]

Thus $K$ is bounded and closed. Maclaurin gives $\sigma _ j(A)\geq \sigma _ m(A)^{j/m}\geq f_0^ j$ for $j\leq m$, so every matrix in $K$ is in the interior. In a unitary eigenframe,

\[ F_ i=\frac{F^{1-m}S_{m-1}(\lambda |i)}{m\binom nm}, \qquad \frac{f_0}{m!L}\leq F_ i\leq \frac{L^{m-1}}{n f_0^{m-1}}. \]

The lower bound is 12.1.3; the upper bound uses $|\lambda _ i|\leq L$ in the polynomial numerator and its positivity. These bounds give possible values of $\vartheta $ and $\Theta $. Concavity places each tangent plane above $F$ on the cone; the plane at $B$ gives equality when $B\in K$. An infimum of affine functions is concave. Each plane increases under $P\geq 0$ by a number between the two displayed trace bounds; taking infima preserves these bounds. The same estimates give global Lipschitz continuity of $\widehat F$. $\square$

To interpret this as a real elliptic operator, let $\mathcal C(R)$ be the complex Hessian of the real quadratic function $q(x)=\frac12 x^{\mathsf T}Rx$, where $x\in \mathbb R^{2n}$. For $R\geq 0$, one has $\mathcal C(R)\geq 0$ and $\operatorname {tr}\mathcal C(R)=\operatorname {tr}R/4$. If $\mu I\leq g(x)\leq MI$, then

\[ \mathcal G(x,R)=\widehat F \bigl (I+g(x)^{-1/2}\mathcal C(R)g(x)^{-1/2}\bigr ) \]

is uniformly elliptic on real symmetric matrices, with constants $\vartheta /(4M)$ and $\Theta /(4\mu )$. Its dependence on $x$ satisfies on compact coordinate balls

\[ |\mathcal G(x,R)-\mathcal G(y,R)| \leq C|x-y|(1+\| R\| ). \]

These follow respectively from the trace bound and the smooth dependence of the positive matrix square root of the fixed metric.

We use the classical interior Evans–Krylov–Caffarelli estimate for a concave operator on all real symmetric matrices with fixed positive ellipticity constants and coefficient oscillation bounded by $C|x-y|^\gamma (1+\| R\| )$, where $\gamma >0$. A Hölder right-hand side then gives an interior $C^{2,\beta }$ estimate, for some $\beta >0$. This estimate applies also to continuous viscosity solutions. The real extension just constructed satisfies these conditions. Once its solution is $C^{2,\beta }$ and the attained complex Hessians remain in a compact subset of the open cone, the original smooth Hessian operator and Schauder estimates give the higher derivatives.

We next identify the limit of the proper auxiliary equations that will be used for regularity.

Proposition 12.4.4. Suppose $v_ j\to \varphi $ uniformly on $X$, $h(x,t)$ is smooth on a fixed bounded interval containing their values, and smooth admissible $u_ j$ satisfy

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m} =e^{u_ j+h(x,v_ j)}\omega ^ n. \]

Assume $u_ j\to u$ uniformly and the complex Hessians of $u_ j$ are uniformly bounded. Then $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is admissible almost everywhere and its coefficient product satisfies

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m} =e^{u+h(x,\varphi )}\omega ^ n. \]

On every coordinate ball $u$ also solves the corresponding uniformly elliptic concave extension in the viscosity sense.

Proof. The first lemma gives $u\in W^{2,p}$ for every finite $p$ and a bounded complex Hessian. The matrices $A_ j=I+g^{-1/2}H(u_ j)g^{-1/2}$ converge weakly in $L^2$ to the analogous matrix $A$ for $u$. Mazur's lemma gives convex combinations of tails converging strongly in $L^2$, hence almost everywhere along a subsequence. The pointwise closed cone is convex, so $A\in C_{n,m}$ almost everywhere. The product lemma passes the equation to the limit as currents, since its right-hand side converges uniformly. Equality of the bounded densities then gives the equation almost everywhere.

The right-hand sides are bounded above and away from zero. The complex Hessian bound gives a uniform upper bound $L$ for $\operatorname {tr}A_ j$. Choose $f_0>0$ below all their normalized roots. Thus every $A_ j$, and almost every $A$, belongs to the compact set $K$ of the preceding lemma. Every $u_ j$ is a classical solution of

\[ \mathcal G(x,D^2u_ j)=r_ j(x),\qquad r_ j(x)=e^{(u_ j+h(x,v_ j))/m}. \]

For completeness, if a smooth test $q$ touches $u$ from above at an interior point $x_0$, add a small positive quartic term to make the local contact strict. Local maximum points of $u_ j-q$ tend to $x_0$. At these points $D^2u_ j\leq D^2q$, and ellipticity gives $\mathcal G(x,D^2q)\geq r_ j(x)$. Pass to the limit, then remove the quartic term. A test from below gives the reverse inequality. The extension is defined on every real Hessian, so no admissibility of a lower test has been assumed. This proves the viscosity statement. $\square$

12.5 Proper equations and admissible approximation

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a smooth potential write $H_ m(w)=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ m\wedge \omega ^{n-m}$. The positive exponential coefficient controls additive constants by the maximum principle. The resulting smooth equations will also construct admissible approximations of bounded potentials, before any continuous Hessian product is defined.

Lemma 12.5.1. For every $a>0$ and smooth real $H$ on $X$, there is a unique smooth strictly admissible solution of

\[ H_ m(w)=e^{aw+H}\omega ^ n. \]

It satisfies

\[ -\frac{\max _ X H}{a}\leq w\leq -\frac{\min _ X H}{a}. \]

Proof. At a maximum of $w$, the admissible form $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ is at most $\omega $, hence $e^{aw+H}\leq 1$ there. At a minimum it is at least $\omega $, and the reverse inequality holds. These give the bounds. For two solutions, a positive maximum of their difference would imply $H_ m(w_1)\leq H_ m(w_2)$ there, contradicting the exponential factor. This proves uniqueness.

First take $a=1$ and use the path $H_ m(w_ t)=e^{w_ t+tH}\omega ^ n$, starting with $w_0=0$. The trace estimate of 12.3.6 applies with prescribed potential $v=0$, fixed smooth comparison $0$, and $h(x,v)=tH(x)$; its constants are uniform for $t\in [0,1]$. 12.2.3 then gives a uniform gradient and complex-Hessian bound. 12.4.1 gives uniform $W^{2,p}$ and hence $C^{1,\alpha }$ bounds, for any fixed $\alpha <1$. Consequently $r_ t=e^{(w_ t+tH)/m}$ has a uniform Hölder bound.

The extension of 12.4.3 is uniformly elliptic and concave on all real Hessians, with coefficient oscillation bounded by $C|x-y|(1+\| R\| )$. The analytic estimate recalled in section 12.4 therefore gives uniform $C^{2,\beta }$ bounds on a finite cover. The original smooth Hessian operator is uniformly elliptic near the attained compact cone set. Differentiating and applying Schauder estimates gives bounds for every higher derivative.

The linearization of $\log (H_ m(w)/\omega ^ n)-w-tH$ is $L_ w-1$, where

\[ L_ w\xi =\frac{m\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\xi \wedge (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^{m-1} \wedge \omega ^{n-m}}{H_ m(w)}. \]

Its principal coefficients are positive definite. The maximum principle gives zero kernel; smooth elliptic Fredholm theory gives surjectivity and Schauder inversion. The implicit-function theorem proves openness. The uniform estimates prove closedness, so the path reaches $t=1$. For general $a$, use the metric $a\omega $ and potential $\widehat w=aw$. Both sides of its coefficient-one equation acquire the same factor $a^ n$, so it is exactly the stated equation after division by that factor. $\square$

An ordinary smooth approximation need not be admissible. To obtain an admissible one, choose a smooth obstacle above the continuous potential and penalize the solution's distance from that obstacle.

Lemma 12.5.2. Every continuous admissible $\varphi $ on $X$ is the uniform limit of smooth strictly admissible functions.

Proof. Fix a smooth $h\geq \varphi $ and a constant

\[ b>\max \left\{ 1,\left\| H_ m(h)/\omega ^ n\right\| _\infty \right\} . \]

For each positive integer $N$ solve, by the preceding lemma,

\[ H_ m(w_ N)=b e^{N(w_ N-h)}\omega ^ n. \]

At a positive maximum of $w_ N-h$, the form $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ lies above the strictly admissible form $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ N$, hence is admissible there. Monotonicity would give $b e^{N(w_ N-h)}\leq H_ m(h)/\omega ^ n<b$, a contradiction. Thus $w_ N\leq h$. The preceding zero-order estimate also gives

\[ \| w_ N\| _\infty \leq M_ N:=\| h\| _\infty +\frac{\log b}{N}. \]

Let $x_ N$ maximize $\varphi -(1+N^{-1})w_ N$. The smooth positive form $T_ N=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ N)^{m-1}\wedge \omega ^{n-m}$ defines a strictly elliptic linear operator. Admissibility gives $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )\wedge T_ N\geq 0$ in distributions. Its linear maximum principle, applied to the upper test $(1+N^{-1})w_ N$ plus the maximum value, gives at $x_ N$

\[ 0\leq \bigl (\omega +(1+N^{-1})\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ N\bigr )\wedge T_ N. \]

This application is to a continuous distributional subsolution of a smooth strictly elliptic operator. It does not assume a second derivative of $\varphi $. Writing $d_ N=H_ m(w_ N)/\omega ^ n$, polarized Gårding positivity yields

\[ (N+1)d_ N\geq \omega \wedge T_ N/\omega ^ n \geq d_ N^{(m-1)/m}. \]

Therefore, at this maximum point,

\[ w_ N-h\geq -E_ N,\qquad E_ N=\frac{m\log (N+1)+\log b}{N}. \]

Since $\varphi \leq h$, the maximum of $\varphi -(1+N^{-1})w_ N$ is at most $E_ N+M_ N/N$. It follows on all of $X$ that

\[ \varphi -E_ N-2M_ N/N\leq w_ N\leq h. \]

Given $\delta >0$, choose an ordinary smooth majorant $\varphi \leq h\leq \varphi +\delta $, and then $N$ so large that $E_ N+2M_ N/N\leq \delta $. The resulting $w_ N$ is strictly admissible and within $\delta $ of $\varphi $. Let $\delta \downarrow 0$. No monotonicity of the chosen approximants is needed. $\square$

12.5.1 Decreasing approximation of bounded potentials

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Upper semicontinuity supplies smooth obstacles decreasing to a bounded potential. The same penalty equation gives admissible functions between the potential and each obstacle, up to an error tending to zero. Choosing successive obstacles below the preceding smooth approximation makes the admissible approximations decrease as well.

Lemma 12.5.3. Every bounded upper semicontinuous real function $u$ on $X$ is the pointwise decreasing limit of smooth functions $h_ j>u$.

Proof. Fix a Riemannian distance $d$ on $X$ and put

\[ c_ j(x)=\sup _{y\in X}\{ u(y)-j d(x,y)\} . \]

Each $c_ j$ is Lipschitz, $c_ j\geq u$, and $c_{j+1}\leq c_ j$. The supremum is attained. If $y_ j$ attains it at a fixed $x$, then $j d(x,y_ j)\leq \sup _ X u-u(x)$, so $y_ j\to x$. Upper semicontinuity gives $\limsup _ j c_ j(x)\leq u(x)$, and hence $c_ j\downarrow u$ pointwise.

Set $\delta _ j=4^{-j}$. Smooth approximation of continuous functions on a compact manifold gives $s_ j\in C^\infty (X)$ with $\| s_ j-c_ j\| _\infty \leq \delta _ j/4$. Take $h_ j=s_ j+\delta _ j$. Then

\[ u+\tfrac 34\delta _ j\leq h_ j\leq c_ j+\tfrac 54\delta _ j, \qquad h_{j+1}\leq c_ j+\tfrac 5{16}\delta _ j < c_ j+\tfrac 34\delta _ j\leq h_ j. \]

The bounds also imply $h_ j(x)\to u(x)$ at every point. $\square$

Lemma 12.5.4. Let $u$ be bounded admissible and $h\geq u$ smooth. Choose

\[ b>\max \{ 1,\| H_ m(h)/\omega ^ n\| _\infty \} . \]

For each positive integer $N$, let $w_ N$ be the smooth strictly admissible solution of

\[ H_ m(w_ N)=b e^{N(w_ N-h)}\omega ^ n. \]

With

\[ M_ N=\| h\| _\infty +\frac{\log b}{N},\qquad E_ N=\frac{m\log (N+1)+\log b}{N}, \]

one has $\| w_ N\| _\infty \leq M_ N$ and

\[ u-E_ N-2M_ N/N\leq w_ N\leq h. \]

Proof. Existence follows from 12.5.1. The maximum comparison with $h$ in the proof of 12.5.2 gives $w_ N\leq h$; it involves only the smooth obstacle and solution. The zero-order bound from 12.5.1 gives $\| w_ N\| _\infty \leq M_ N$.

The upper semicontinuous function $u-(1+N^{-1})w_ N$ attains its maximum at some $x_ N$. The smooth function $(1+N^{-1})w_ N$ plus this maximum is an upper test for $u$ there. Put

\[ T_ N=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ N)^{m-1}\wedge \omega ^{n-m}, \qquad d_ N=H_ m(w_ N)/\omega ^ n>0. \]

Condition (12.2) and polarized positivity yield, at $x_ N$,

\[ (N+1)d_ N\geq \omega \wedge T_ N/\omega ^ n \geq d_ N^{(m-1)/m}. \]

Consequently $d_ N\geq (N+1)^{-m}$ and $w_ N-h\geq -E_ N$ there. Using $u\leq h$, the maximum is at most $E_ N+M_ N/N$. On all of $X$ this gives

\[ u\leq (1+N^{-1})w_ N+E_ N+M_ N/N \leq w_ N+E_ N+2M_ N/N. \]
$\square$

Theorem 12.5.5. Every bounded admissible potential $u$ on $X$ is the pointwise decreasing limit of smooth strictly admissible potentials. The sequence may be chosen uniformly bounded.

Proof. Choose smooth $h_ k\downarrow u$ by 12.5.3 and put $\varepsilon _ j=8^{-j}$. We construct smooth strictly admissible $u_ j$ with $u_ j\geq u+\varepsilon _ j$. For $j=1$, take $k_1=1$. At a subsequent step choose $k_ j\geq \max \{ j,k_{j-1}+1\} $ so large that

\[ h_{k_ j}<u_{j-1}-\tfrac 12\varepsilon _{j-1}\quad \hbox{on }X. \]

Such an index exists: the open sets $\{ h_ k<u_{j-1}-\varepsilon _{j-1}/2\} $ increase with $k$ and cover $X$, since $h_ k\downarrow u\leq u_{j-1}-\varepsilon _{j-1}$. Compactness gives a single index for all of $X$.

For this fixed obstacle $h_{k_ j}$ choose $b_ j$ as in 12.5.4, and then choose $N_ j$ so large that its error $E_{N_ j}+2M_{N_ j}/N_ j$ is at most $\varepsilon _ j$. The resulting smooth solution $w_ j$ satisfies

\[ u-\varepsilon _ j\leq w_ j\leq h_{k_ j}. \]

Put $u_ j=w_ j+2\varepsilon _ j$. Adding a constant preserves strict admissibility, and $u_ j\geq u+\varepsilon _ j$. For $j\geq 2$,

\[ u_ j\leq h_{k_ j}+2\varepsilon _ j <u_{j-1}-\tfrac 12\varepsilon _{j-1}+2\varepsilon _ j =u_{j-1}-\tfrac 14\varepsilon _{j-1}. \]

Finally, $u\leq u_ j\leq h_{k_ j}+2\varepsilon _ j$ and $k_ j\geq j$ give pointwise convergence to $u$. The bounds $\inf _ X u\leq u_ j\leq \sup _ X u_1$ give a common uniform bound. $\square$

For continuous $u$, Dini's theorem makes these decreasing approximations converge uniformly on $X$. This is the convergence used for the continuous mixed products in the next section.

12.6 Continuous products, envelopes and domination

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Uniform admissible approximation allows us to define the mixed Hessian products. Their local bounds retain the derivatives of the Hermitian background; closedness of the background is not required.

Proposition 12.6.1. For continuous admissible $u_1,\ldots ,u_ k$, $1\leq k\leq m$, the current

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1)\wedge \cdots \wedge (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ k) \wedge \omega ^{n-m} \]

is well defined as the limit of uniform smooth admissible approximations. It is positive, local, and continuous when its potentials converge uniformly. Its local masses are bounded in terms of uniform bounds on the potentials. In particular $H_ m(u)$ is a positive Radon measure.

Proof. 12.5.1, 12.5.2 provide the smooth approximations without using any continuous Hessian product or domination input. For a smooth admissible $u$ put $\sigma =\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. Since $\mathrm d\sigma =\mathrm d\omega $, the coefficients of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi \sigma ^{r-1}\wedge \omega ^{n-r})$ are sums of smooth test factors paired with powers $\sigma ^ q$, $q\leq r-1$, where $\chi $ is a fixed cutoff. The absolute-form lemma and integration by parts give the Chern–Levine–Nirenberg bound by induction on $r\leq m$:

\[ \int _ K\sigma ^ r\wedge \omega ^{n-r} \leq C_{K,U,\omega }(1+\| u\| _{L^\infty (U)})^ r. \]

At the induction step write $\sigma ^ r=\omega \wedge \sigma ^{r-1} +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \sigma ^{r-1}$, integrate the second term against $\chi $, and move $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ to $\chi \sigma ^{r-1}\omega ^{n-r}$. The resulting powers have degrees $r-1,r-2,r-3$, with negative powers absent, and the induction applies on nested slightly larger compact sets. Mixed masses reduce to the single potential average, by positivity of the expansion of its power. Positive mixed currents have total variation controlled by these trace masses.

To prove independence, telescope the difference between two smooth mixed products whose potentials have a common uniform bound. Each term paired with a fixed smooth complementary test is

\[ \int \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ a-v_ a)\wedge T\wedge \eta =\int (u_ a-v_ a)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(T\wedge \eta ), \]

where $T$ is a product of the remaining admissible factors and $\omega ^{n-m}$. The identity $\mathrm d(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)=\mathrm d\omega $ and its $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ analogue show that derivatives of $T$ introduce only fixed metric derivatives; every term has at most $k-1<m$ admissible factors. The absolute mixed-form bound and the mass estimate just proved bound the variation of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(T\wedge \eta )$ uniformly. Consequently the pairing difference is at most $C_\eta \max _ a\| u_ a-v_ a\| _\infty $. The same construction with an arbitrary complementary smooth test also defines the signed products of fewer than $m$ factors without the factor $\omega ^{n-m}$: their variation is bounded by the absolute-form lemma. These products justify the metric-derivative terms above. This proves that all approximations have the same limit and that the products are continuous in the uniform topology. Positivity passes from the smooth mixed products. Using a test supported in an open set on which two limiting potentials coincide proves locality by the same estimate on a smaller neighborhood of its support. $\square$

Write $H_ m(u)$ for the measure with every potential equal to $u$. Locality and uniform continuity of these products give comparison on a contact set.

Lemma 12.6.2. If $u\leq v$ are continuous admissible functions and the remaining $m-1$ factors are fixed continuous admissible functions, then on $\{ u=v\} $ the mixed measure with first factor $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is at most the one with first factor $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$. In particular $\mathbf1_{\{ u=v\} }H_ m(u)\leq \mathbf1_{\{ u=v\} }H_ m(v)$.

Proof. Write $M(w)$ for the mixed measure with first potential $w$. Positivity and locality give, for $t>0$,

\[ M(\max (v,u+t))\geq \mathbf1_{\{ v>u+t\} }M(v)+\mathbf1_{\{ u\geq v\} }M(u). \]

The first indicators increase to $\mathbf1_{\{ v>u\} }$ as $t\downarrow 0$, and the potentials on the left converge uniformly to $v$. Integrate against nonnegative continuous tests and pass to the limit. Subtract the first restricted measure to obtain the claimed contact inequality. Replacing one factor at a time proves the pure-power claim and the analogous contact inequalities for all mixed powers. $\square$

For a continuous real obstacle $h$, define $P(h)$ to be the supremum of continuous admissible functions bounded above by $h$. Constants make the family nonempty. The penalty construction also proves that this supremum is continuous and its Hessian measure lies on the contact set.

Lemma 12.6.3. The function $P(h)$ is continuous and admissible, and $H_ m(P(h))$ is supported on $\{ P(h)=h\} $. Moreover $\| P(h_1)-P(h_2)\| _\infty \leq \| h_1-h_2\| _\infty $.

Proof. For smooth $h$, use the penalty solutions $w_ N$ in 12.5.2. Its maximum argument applies to every continuous admissible competitor $v\leq h$, with an error depending on $h$ and $N$, and not on $v$. Taking their supremum gives

\[ P(h)-E_ N-2M_ N/N\leq w_ N\leq P(h), \]

where the error tends to zero. Thus $P(h)$ is their uniform limit and is continuous and admissible. On every compact subset of $\{ P(h)<h\} $ the penalty densities $b e^{N(w_ N-h)}$ tend uniformly to zero. Product continuity implies the contact support assertion. Adding constants to competitors gives the stated Lipschitz bound. Approximate a continuous $h$ uniformly by smooth obstacles. Their envelopes converge uniformly to $P(h)$, proving continuity and admissibility. Any compact subset of $\{ P(h)<h\} $ also lies outside the contact sets of all sufficiently close smooth obstacles; product continuity gives the support assertion for $h$. $\square$

The obstacle also bounds discontinuous competitors. Their upper-test admissibility suffices for the penalty estimate, so allowing them does not change the continuous envelope of a smooth obstacle.

Proposition 12.6.4. For every smooth real obstacle $h$, the envelope $P(h)$ equals the pointwise supremum of all bounded admissible functions below $h$.

Proof. The solutions $w_ N$ of 12.5.4 are continuous admissible competitors, so $w_ N\leq P(h)$. For each bounded admissible $u\leq h$, the same lemma gives

\[ u\leq w_ N+E_ N+2M_ N/N\leq P(h)+E_ N+2M_ N/N. \]

Let $N\to \infty $ for this fixed obstacle and fixed $b$. Thus $P(h)$ dominates every bounded competitor. Since $P(h)$ itself is a bounded competitor, it equals their supremum. $\square$

The trace test in (12.1) gives $L_\omega u\geq -n$, where $L_\omega u=n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \omega ^{n-1}/\omega ^ n$. We use the classical normalized Green estimate

\begin{equation} \int _ X(\sup _ Xu-u)\omega ^ n\leq C_\omega \end{equation}

To use envelopes in domination, we must first exclude a potential with zero total Hessian mass.

Lemma 12.6.5. Every continuous admissible $u$ satisfies $\int _ X H_ m(u)>0$.

Proof. Suppose this integral is zero. For $s>0$ put $w=\max (u,\min _ Xu+s)$ and $U=\{ u<\min _ Xu+s\} $. The open set $U$ is nonempty, $0\leq w-u\leq s$, and $w=u$ outside it. Set $\sigma _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$, $\sigma _ w=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$, and $\mu _ q=\sigma _ u^ q\wedge \sigma _ w^{m-q}\wedge \omega ^{n-m}$. The contact lemma gives $\mathbf1_{X\setminus U}\mu _ q\geq \mathbf1_{X\setminus U}\mu _ m$, for $0\leq q\leq m$. For $q<m$ the telescoping identity is

\[ \mu _ q-\mu _ m=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w-u)\wedge T_ q,\qquad T_ q=\sigma _ u^ q\wedge \sum _{r=0}^{m-q-1}\sigma _ w^ r\wedge \sigma _ u^{m-q-1-r} \wedge \omega ^{n-m}. \]

Integration by parts on $X$ therefore gives

\[ \int _ U(\mu _ q-\mu _ m)\leq \int _ X(\mu _ q-\mu _ m) =\int _ X(w-u)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ q. \]

This identity follows first for smooth approximants and then by uniform product continuity. The derivatives of $T_ q$ contain only fixed metric derivatives and products of at most $m-1$ admissible factors, so the absolute-form lemma makes $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ q$ a measure of uniformly controlled variation. Uniform convergence also justifies its continuous weight. On $U$, locality gives $\sigma _ w=\omega $ and thus

\[ |\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_ q|\leq C\sum _{r=0}^{m-1}\mu _ r. \]

The constant depends only on the metric and $m,n$, and not on $u$ or $s$. Consequently

\[ \sum _{q=0}^ m\int _ U\mu _ q \leq (m+1)\int _ U H_ m(u)+Cms\sum _{r=0}^{m-1}\int _ U\mu _ r. \]

For sufficiently small $s$, absorption and $H_ m(u)=0$ force all these nonnegative integrals to vanish. But $\mu _0=\omega ^ n$ on the nonempty open set $U$, a contradiction. $\square$

The envelope of a stretched difference now converts vanishing of a restricted measure into an order relation. The linear bound (12.3) prevents these envelopes from tending to $-\infty $ on an open set.

Proposition 12.6.6. If continuous admissible $u,v$ satisfy $\mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(u)=0$, then $u\geq v$.

Proof. Replace $v$ by $\max (u,v)$, so $u\leq v$. For $b>1$ put $p_ b=P(bu-(b-1)v)$ and $z_ b=b^{-1}p_ b+(1-b^{-1})v\leq u$. On the contact set $C_ b=\{ p_ b=bu-(b-1)v\} $ one has $z_ b=u$; the contact inequality and polarized positivity give

\[ \mathbf1_{C_ b}b^{-m}H_ m(p_ b) \leq \mathbf1_{C_ b}H_ m(z_ b) \leq \mathbf1_{C_ b}H_ m(u). \]

The envelope measure is supported on $C_ b$ and has positive mass. It vanishes on $C_ b\cap \{ u<v\} $ by the hypothesis, so some point of $C_ b\cap \{ u=v\} $ exists. Hence $\sup _ Xp_ b\geq \inf _ Xv$, while $p_ b\leq u$. The estimate (12.3) is consequently uniform for $p_ b$. On the open set $E_ a=\{ u<v-a\} $, for $a>0$, one has $p_ b\leq v-ba$, and therefore

\[ C_\omega \geq \int _ X(\sup _ Xp_ b-p_ b)\omega ^ n \geq (ba-\operatorname {osc}_ Xv)\int _{E_ a}\omega ^ n. \]

Letting $b\to \infty $ gives zero volume for $E_ a$. Since it is open it is empty, and $a\downarrow 0$ gives $u\geq v$. $\square$

Proposition 12.6.7. If continuous admissible $u,v$ satisfy

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(u)\leq c\mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(v),\qquad 0\leq c<1, \]

then $u\geq v$.

Proof. Choose $b>1$ so that $(1-b^{-1})^ m>c$, and put $p=P(bu-(b-1)v)$ and $z=b^{-1}p+(1-b^{-1})v\leq u$. On $C=\{ p=bu-(b-1)v\} $ the contact comparison gives

\[ \mathbf1_ C\bigl (b^{-m}H_ m(p)+(1-b^{-1})^ mH_ m(v)\bigr ) \leq \mathbf1_ C H_ m(u). \]

Restricting to $\{ u<v\} $ and using the hypothesis shows that $H_ m(p)$ vanishes on $C\cap \{ u<v\} $. Its support lies in $C$, where $p<v$ is equivalent to $u<v$. Thus $H_ m(p)$ vanishes on $\{ p<v\} $. The preceding domination proposition gives $p\geq v$. Since $p\leq bu-(b-1)v$, it follows that $u\geq v$. $\square$

Strict domination yields the comparison needed for the proper equations.

Lemma 12.6.8. For continuous admissible $u,v$ and $a>0$, if

\[ e^{-av}H_ m(v)\geq e^{-au}H_ m(u), \]

then $u\geq v$.

Proof. Fix $\delta >0$. On $\{ u<v-\delta \} $, the assumed inequality gives

\[ H_ m(u)\leq e^{a(u-v)}H_ m(v) \leq e^{-a\delta }H_ m(v-\delta ). \]

The constant $e^{-a\delta }$ is strictly smaller than one, so 12.6.7 applied to $u$ and $v-\delta $ gives $u\geq v-\delta $. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ proves the assertion. $\square$

12.7 Continuous equations and regularity

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The proper equation is stable in the uniform norm, so its smooth solutions extend to continuous data. This also identifies the limit of the auxiliary equations with a prescribed continuous weak solution.

Proposition 12.7.1. Let $a>0$ and let smooth $H_ j$ converge uniformly to a continuous $H$. The smooth solutions of $H_ m(w_ j)=e^{aw_ j+H_ j}\omega ^ n$ converge uniformly to the unique continuous admissible solution of $H_ m(w)=e^{aw+H}\omega ^ n$. For two continuous data $H_1,H_2$, the corresponding solutions satisfy

\[ \| w_1-w_2\| _\infty \leq a^{-1}\| H_1-H_2\| _\infty . \]

They also satisfy $-\max _ XH/a\leq w\leq -\min _ XH/a$.

Proof. Smooth solutions exist by 12.5.1. At a maximum of $w_1-w_2$, monotonicity of the smooth Hessian operator gives $e^{aw_1+H_1}\leq e^{aw_2+H_2}$. Hence $a(w_1-w_2)\leq H_2-H_1$ there. Exchanging the functions gives the stated norm estimate for smooth data. Thus a uniformly convergent sequence of smooth data gives a uniformly Cauchy sequence of solutions. Its limit is admissible by the linear distributional inequalities, and 12.6.1 passes the equation to the limit. The bounds follow from those for the smooth solutions. Taking limits in the smooth norm estimate proves it for continuous data. Finally, 12.6.8 in both directions gives uniqueness among continuous admissible solutions. $\square$

For smooth positive densities depending on the potential, the estimate of section 12.3 gives complex-Hessian bounds independent of derivatives of the prescribed approximation. The elliptic extension then upgrades the continuous solution to a classical one.

Theorem 12.7.2. If $\varphi $ is continuous and admissible on $X$, $f(x,t)$ is smooth on a neighborhood of its bounded range, and

\[ H_ m(\varphi )=e^{f(x,\varphi )}\omega ^ n, \]

then $\varphi $ is smooth.

Proof. Choose smooth admissible $v_ j\to \varphi $ uniformly, by 12.5.2. Their values lie in a fixed compact interval on which $f$ is smooth. Put $h(x,t)=-t+f(x,t)$ and solve

\[ H_ m(u_ j)=e^{u_ j+h(x,v_ j)}\omega ^ n. \]

The preceding stability proposition shows that $u_ j$ converges uniformly to a continuous admissible $u$ satisfying

\[ e^{-u}H_ m(u)=e^{-\varphi +f(x,\varphi )}\omega ^ n =e^{-\varphi }H_ m(\varphi ). \]

12.6.8, applied in both directions, gives $u=\varphi $.

The zero-order bounds for $u_ j$ are uniform. Fix $M$ bounding them and choose $B>\| \varphi \| _\infty +1$. Extend $h$ smoothly away from a neighborhood of the range of $\varphi $ to $X\times [-B-1,B+1]$; this does not change the equations for large $j$. The tolerance $\varepsilon $ in 12.3.6 depends on these bounds and on $h$, without derivatives of the smooth comparison function. Choose a fixed smooth $\widetilde\varphi $ with $\| \widetilde\varphi -\varphi \| _\infty <\varepsilon /2$ and $\| \widetilde\varphi \| _\infty \leq B$. For large $j$, $\| v_ j-\widetilde\varphi \| _\infty <\varepsilon $. 12.3.7 then gives uniform gradient and complex-Hessian bounds for $u_ j$, independent of derivatives of $v_ j$. 12.4.1 yields $\varphi \in W^{2,p}$ for every finite $p$, and $\varphi \in C^{1,\alpha }$ for every $\alpha <1$.

12.4.4 makes $\varphi $ a continuous viscosity solution of the concave uniformly elliptic extension, with right-hand side $e^{f(x,\varphi )/m}$. This right-hand side is Hölder. The analytic estimate in section 12.4 therefore gives $\varphi \in C^{2,\beta }$, for some $\beta >0$. The operator agrees with the original Hessian root on the attained compact cone set; the now classical equation thus has smooth dependence on $x,\varphi $ and its complex Hessian, and is uniformly elliptic there. Difference quotients and interior Schauder estimates give $C^{3,\beta }$; iteration proves smoothness. The estimate applies directly to the viscosity limit. $\square$

The proper equation can now be solved when its exponent depends smoothly on the unknown potential, provided that constant barriers bound the desired range and the dependence is strictly increasing.

Theorem 12.7.3. Let $A<B$ and let $f$ be smooth on a neighborhood of $X\times [A,B]$. Suppose that, for every $x\in X$,

\[ f(x,A)\leq 0\leq f(x,B),\qquad 0<\kappa \leq \partial _ t f(x,t)\leq L\quad (A\leq t\leq B). \]

There is a unique continuous admissible solution with values in $[A,B]$ of

\[ H_ m(w)=e^{f(x,w)}\omega ^ n. \]

This solution is smooth and strictly admissible.

Proof. Choose $a\geq L$ and let $\mathcal C=\{ v\in C(X):A\leq v\leq B\} $, a complete subset of $C(X)$ in the uniform norm. For $v\in \mathcal C$ put $H_ v(x)=f(x,v(x))-av(x)$ and let $T(v)$ be the continuous proper solution with exponent $aT(v)+H_ v$, furnished by 12.7.1. The function $f(x,t)-at$ is nonincreasing in $t$, so

\[ -aB\leq f(x,B)-aB\leq H_ v(x) \leq f(x,A)-aA\leq -aA. \]

The zero-order bounds in that proposition give $A\leq T(v)\leq B$. The mean-value theorem also gives

\[ \| H_ v-H_ z\| _\infty \leq (a-\kappa )\| v-z\| _\infty , \qquad \| T(v)-T(z)\| _\infty \leq (1-\kappa /a)\| v-z\| _\infty . \]

Since $0\leq 1-\kappa /a<1$, the contraction theorem gives a unique fixed point $w\in \mathcal C$. It is admissible because it equals $T(w)$, and its equation is exactly the stated equation. Every continuous admissible solution in this interval is a fixed point, by uniqueness of the continuous proper equation with its prescribed $H_ w$. This proves uniqueness in the stated class. 12.7.2 makes $w$ smooth. Its form lies in the closed $m$-cone and has positive $m$th symmetric polynomial; Maclaurin's inequalities then put it in the open cone. Thus it is strictly admissible. $\square$

12.8 Bounded potentials and recursive Hessian currents

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The decreasing approximation of bounded admissible potentials supplies limits that uniform approximation cannot reach. A bounded potential can multiply a current with measure coefficients, so one can apply $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ to the resulting current. Repeating this operation requires bounds on every coefficient, rather than only on the trace of a positive mixed measure. The absolute-form estimate provides these bounds below degree $m$; at degree $m$, positivity is obtained after wedging with $\omega ^{n-m}$.

All bounded potentials have their canonical upper semicontinuous representatives from (12.2). Their pointwise values will be used when multiplying measure coefficients. For a real current $T$ of order zero, write $\| T\| _ K$ for the sum of the total variations of its coefficients on a compact coordinate set $K$. Fix $K\Subset U$ in a coordinate chart. Different fixed coordinate frames give equivalent local bounds.

Lemma 12.8.1. If $u$ is bounded admissible, then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ has signed Radon measure coefficients. For $K\Subset U$ contained in a coordinate chart,

\[ \| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\| _ K\leq C_{K,U,\omega }(1+\| u\| _{L^\infty (X)}). \]

Proof. Choose smooth strictly admissible $u_ j\downarrow u$ by 12.5.5, and put $\sigma _ j=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$. After discarding finitely many terms, $\| u_ j\| _\infty \leq \| u\| _\infty +1$. Indeed, the increasing open sets $\{ u_ j<\sup _ Xu+1\} $ cover $X$, and compactness gives a single index whose open set is all of $X$; the lower bound follows from $u_ j\geq u$.

The closed $m$-cone lies in the closed $2$-cone. In an $\omega $-unitary frame, its eigenvalues satisfy

\[ \sum _ i\lambda _ i^2\leq (\operatorname {tr}\sigma _ j)^2, \qquad \operatorname {tr}\sigma _ j\geq 0. \]

Thus every coefficient of $\sigma _ j$ is bounded by its trace. The degree-one Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate in 12.6.1, applied to these smooth functions, bounds the integral of that trace on $K$ by $C(1+\| u\| _\infty )$. Subtracting the fixed smooth $\omega $ gives the same bound for the coefficient variations of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$. Bounded convergence gives $u_ j\to u$ in local $L^1$, and therefore $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\to \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ in distributions. The uniform coefficient bounds show that this limit has measure coefficients with the asserted bound. $\square$

Set $D_0=1$. For an ordered list $u_1,\ldots ,u_ p$ of bounded admissible potentials, define recursively

12.4
\begin{equation} D_ p(u_1,\ldots ,u_ p) =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl (u_ pD_{p-1}(u_1,\ldots ,u_{p-1})\bigr ). \end{equation}

The next result justifies the bounded multiplication at every step up to $p=m$.

Proposition 12.8.2. For $1\leq p<m$, the recursion defines a real closed current of order zero and bidegree $(p,p)$. If $M=\max _ a\| u_ a\| _{L^\infty (X)}$, then

\[ \| D_ p(u_1,\ldots ,u_ p)\| _ K \leq C_{K,U,\omega ,p}(1+M)^ p. \]

For $p=m$, it defines a real closed distributional current. For any smooth admissible sequences $u_ a^{j_ a}\downarrow u_ a$ with a common uniform bound, one has

12.5
\begin{equation} D_ p(u_1,\ldots ,u_ p) =\lim _{j_ p\to \infty }\cdots \lim _{j_1\to \infty } \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1^{j_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ p^{j_ p} \end{equation}

in distributions. The index $j_1$ tends to infinity first. Below degree $m$, all these limits have locally uniformly bounded coefficient variations.

Proof. The case $p=1$ is the preceding lemma. Suppose the assertions hold through degree $p-1<m$. Since $u_ p$ is bounded Borel and $D_{p-1}$ has measure coefficients, their product is well defined. Applying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ gives a real current that is both $\mathrm d$- and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$-closed.

For a fixed smooth complementary test form $\eta $, dominated convergence against the fixed signed measures gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \langle D_ p,\eta \rangle & =\langle u_ pD_{p-1},\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta \rangle \\ & =\lim _{j_ p\to \infty } \langle u_ p^{j_ p}D_{p-1},\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta \rangle . \end{aligned} \]

With $j_ p$ fixed, the inductive iterated limit for $D_{p-1}$ can be paired with the smooth form $u_ p^{j_ p}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\eta $. Integration by parts for the smooth closed factors identifies the resulting pairing with the smooth product on the right of (12.5). This proves that identity also when $p=m$.

For $p<m$, expand the smooth product using $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ a^{j_ a}=\sigma _ a^{j_ a}-\omega $. A term with $q$ admissible factors, $0\leq q\leq p$, has the form

\[ \sigma _{a_1}^{j_{a_1}}\wedge \cdots \wedge \sigma _{a_ q}^{j_{a_ q}}\wedge \omega ^{p-q}. \]

Every such $q$ is strictly less than $m$. Apply 12.1.5 to each coefficient test, and then the smooth mass estimate from 12.6.1 to the potential sum. The term $q=0$ is the fixed smooth form $\omega ^ p$. For $q\geq 1$, the sum of its admissible forms is $q(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bar u)$, where $\bar u$ is their average and $\| \bar u\| _\infty \leq M+1$. The result is a coefficient variation bound $C(1+M)^ p$, after choosing the approximations with norm at most $M+1$ as in the preceding proof. For arbitrary uniformly bounded approximations the same argument uses their common bound. The bounds survive each distributional limit, proving that $D_ p$ has order zero and the stated estimate. This completes the induction. No coefficient bound for the unwedged degree-$m$ current is used or asserted. $\square$

For a subset $I=\{ i_1<\cdots <i_ q\} \subset \{ 1,\ldots ,p\} $, write $D_ I=D_ q(u_{i_1},\ldots ,u_{i_ q})$, with $D_\varnothing =1$. Define the shifted current and its mixed positive current by

12.8
\begin{equation} R_ p(u_1,\ldots ,u_ p) =\sum _{I\subset \{ 1,\ldots ,p\} }\omega ^{p-|I|}\wedge D_ I, \qquad Q_ p=R_ p\wedge \omega ^{n-m}. \end{equation}

Below degree $m$, $R_ p$ has measure coefficients. At degree $m$ it is initially a distributional current. Its positive wedge $Q_ m$ is nevertheless a measure.

Put $M=\max _ a\| u_ a\| _{L^\infty (X)}$.

Theorem 12.8.3. For $1\leq p\leq m$, $Q_ p$ is a positive current of order zero, and

\[ \int _ KQ_ p\wedge \omega ^{m-p} \leq C_{K,U,\omega ,p}(1+M)^ p. \]

It is the iterated limit, in the order of (12.5), of

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1^{j_1})\wedge \cdots \wedge (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ p^{j_ p})\wedge \omega ^{n-m}. \]

In particular $Q_ m$ is a positive Radon measure. These currents are local in the canonical potentials and independent of the chosen approximations for the fixed ordering. When all potentials are continuous, $Q_ p$ equals the continuous mixed product of 12.6.1.

Proof. Expand the smooth shifted product over its subsets. Applying (12.5) to each subset, in its inherited ordering, gives (12.8). The absent indices have no effect on a subset's limit.

Each smooth product is positive: pairing with a decomposable strongly positive complementary test appends $m-p$ nonnegative rank-one $(1,1)$-forms, and polarized Gårding positivity applies. The smooth mixed mass bound is uniform in all indices. Distributional limits preserve positivity and the local trace bound at every step. Positive currents have order zero, so this gives the assertions for $Q_ p$, including the top-degree measure.

The recursion uses only bounded multiplication of canonical values and local distributional differentiation. It is therefore local. Its definition contains no approximation choices, so the proved limit is independent of those choices while the ordering is fixed. For continuous potentials, the approximations converge uniformly by Dini's theorem. The telescoping estimate in 12.6.1 is uniform in all remaining indices and shows that their iterated limit is the same continuous mixed product. This also proves permutation invariance in the continuous case. $\square$

The shifted currents are not closed. Their derivatives can be stated without multiplying a discontinuous function by a distribution of positive order. This is the torsion identity needed before considering integration by parts with bounded weights.

Proposition 12.8.4. Write $R_{p-1}^{\widehat a}$ for the shifted current obtained by omitting $u_ a$, in the inherited order, and use two hats for two omissions. Put $R_0=1$. For $1\leq p\leq m$,

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm dR_ p& =\mathrm d\omega \wedge \sum _{a=1}^ pR_{p-1}^{\widehat a},\\ \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}R_ p& =\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \sum _{a=1}^ pR_{p-1}^{\widehat a},\\ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ p& =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \sum _{a=1}^ pR_{p-1}^{\widehat a} +2\, \mathrm d\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \sum _{a<b}R_{p-2}^{\widehat a\widehat b}. \end{aligned} \]

The last sum is absent for $p=1$. All currents on the right have order zero. If all but at most one potential are smooth, $R_ p$ is independent of the ordering and is the ordinary product of the smooth factors with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$.

Proof. Every $D_ I$ is both $\mathrm d$- and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$-closed. Differentiating (12.8) therefore gives

\[ \mathrm dR_ p=\mathrm d\omega \wedge \sum _ I(p-|I|)\omega ^{p-|I|-1}\wedge D_ I, \]

with zero terms omitted. The coefficient $p-|I|$ counts the omitted indices outside $I$, which proves the first formula; the second is identical with $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ in place of $\mathrm d$. Apply $\mathrm d$ to the second formula. Each pair of omitted indices occurs twice, and $-\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \mathrm d\omega =\mathrm d\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega $ because both forms have odd degree three. This gives the displayed $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ formula, with every background derivative retained.

If all but one potential are smooth, applying $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ to the bounded potential times a smooth closed product gives its $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ wedged with that product. At later smooth steps, closedness again makes the recursion ordinary smooth multiplication. This is independent of the position of the bounded potential, and the subset expansion gives the last assertion. $\square$

Local capacity arguments naturally concern functions whose unshifted Hessian lies in the cone. On a coordinate ball $U$, call a bounded canonical function $f$ $m$-subharmonic relative to $\omega $ if

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge \gamma _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \gamma _{m-1} \wedge \omega ^{n-m}\geq 0 \]

for all smooth $\gamma _ i\in \Gamma _ m(\omega )$. Equivalently, its local upper tests $q$ satisfy $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\in \overline{\Gamma _ m(\omega )}$. The same Hermitian form $\omega $ is used here and in the chapter's shifted admissibility.

Lemma 12.8.5. Let $U$ be a coordinate ball compactly contained in a holomorphic chart. There is a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic $\rho $ on a neighborhood of $\overline U$ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq 2\omega $ and, for every bounded admissible $u$ on $X$, $u+\rho $ is $m$-subharmonic relative to $\omega $ on $U$. Adding $\rho $ to the approximations in 12.5.5 gives uniformly bounded smooth decreasing approximations in this local class.

Proof. Take $\rho =C|z|^2$ in the chart. On the fixed compact set $\overline U$, a sufficiently large $C$ gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq 2\omega $. An upper test $q$ for $u+\rho $ at $x$ gives the upper test $q-\rho $ for $u$ there. Hence

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(q-\rho ))+(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho -\omega ) \in \overline{\Gamma _ m(\omega )}, \]

since the second summand is positive and the cone is preserved by adding nonnegative forms. Smooth addition preserves the canonical representative. The same identity applies pointwise to every smooth admissible approximation. Adding a fixed smooth function preserves pointwise decrease and convergence, and its bounded norm on $\overline U$ preserves the common local bound. $\square$

The correction is a local inclusion of classes. It leaves the metric in the cone tests unchanged. Although $\tau =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ is closed, $\tau -\omega $ has derivative $-\mathrm d\omega $, so the background terms in the preceding torsion identity remain present. In particular, $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u+\rho )+(\omega -\tau )$. Comparison of the unshifted Hessians of the corrected functions must therefore account for this smooth remainder.

Example 12.8.6. On the unit ball in $\mathbb C^3$, put $\beta _ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z_ j|^2$, $\beta =\sum _{j=1}^3\beta _ j$ and

\[ u=-\tfrac 12\sum _{j=1}^3|z_ j|^2, \qquad v=-\tfrac 45|z_1|^2. \]

Both $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ are strictly positive, but

\[ (\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^2\wedge \beta > (\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^2\wedge \beta , \qquad (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^2\wedge \beta <(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^2\wedge \beta . \]

Thus shifted Hessian order does not imply unshifted Hessian order, even for smooth potentials with positive perturbed forms.

Proof. The eigenvalues of the two perturbed forms relative to $\beta $ are $(\tfrac 12,\tfrac 12,\tfrac 12)$ and $(\tfrac 15,1,1)$. For a diagonal form with relative eigenvalues $\lambda _1,\lambda _2, \lambda _3$, its second Hessian is $(\lambda _1\lambda _2+\lambda _1\lambda _3+\lambda _2\lambda _3)\beta ^3/3$. Consequently the shifted measures of $u,v$ are $\tfrac 14\beta ^3$ and $\tfrac 7{15}\beta ^3$, while their unshifted expressions are $\tfrac 14\beta ^3$ and $0$. Their unshifted traces are negative, so neither original function belongs to the local unshifted class. Adding $\sum _ j|z_ j|^2$ gives an exact correction in this constant closed background. An exact correction $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho =\omega $ is impossible where $\mathrm d\omega \ne 0$. $\square$

12.9 Sobolev capacity and two Hessian factors

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

To multiply a varying coefficient measure by a bounded potential, weak convergence of the measures is not enough. We first control the sets on which a potential can be discontinuous. The linear trace of an admissible form supplies this control through Sobolev capacity. Its Gauduchon multiplier must be chosen in the conformal class of $\omega $, since an admissible form need not be positive in every tangent direction.

Choose $\rho >0$ by theorem 4.1.2 and put

\[ T=\rho \omega ^{n-1},\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0,\qquad A=\int _ X\omega \wedge T>0. \]

Write $H^1(X)=W^{1,2}(X,\mathbb R)$ and use the equivalent Sobolev norm

\[ \| f\| _{H^1}^2=\int _ Xf^2\omega ^ n+ \int _ X\mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge T. \]

For an open set $G\subset X$ define

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_1(G) =\inf \{ \| f\| _{H^1}^2:f\in H^1(X),\ f\geq 1 \text{ almost everywhere on }G\} , \]

and for an arbitrary set $E$ take the infimum over open $G\supset E$. Thus this capacity is defined by Sobolev energy; it is distinct from an $m$-Hessian capacity. Truncation allows $0\leq f\leq 1$ in the infimum for open sets.

Lemma 12.9.1. The capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_1$ is countably subadditive. For a smooth real $f$ and $a>0$,

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_1(\{ |f|>a\} )\leq a^{-2}\| f\| _{H^1}^2. \]

If a positive Radon measure $\mu $ satisfies $|\int _ X\phi \, \mathrm d\mu |\leq C\| \phi \| _{H^1}$ for every smooth real $\phi $, then for every Borel set $E$,

\[ \mu (E)\leq C\operatorname {Cap}_1(E)^{1/2}. \]

Proof. For open $G_ i$ choose Sobolev functions $0\leq f_ i\leq 1$ equal to one almost everywhere on $G_ i$, with a summably small excess in their squared norms over the capacity infima. The Sobolev chain rule gives

\[ \| \max _{i\leq N}f_ i\| _{H^1}^2\leq \sum _{i\leq N}\| f_ i\| _{H^1}^2. \]

Indeed, almost everywhere the gradient of the maximum is the gradient of an active function, and the squared value is bounded by the sum of the squared values. If the sum is finite, these maxima converge in $L^2$, are bounded in $H^1$, and have a weak $H^1$ limit equal to one almost everywhere on $\bigcup _ iG_ i$. Lower semicontinuity proves subadditivity for open sets. Taking open supersets proves it for arbitrary sets. If the sum is infinite there is nothing to prove. The level-set estimate follows by testing with $\min \{ 1,|f|/a\} $ and the same Sobolev chain rule.

Fix a compact $K\subset G$ and a Sobolev competitor $0\leq f\leq 1$ for the open set $G$. Positive local mollification and a partition of unity give smooth nonnegative $f_\epsilon \to f$ in $H^1$ that satisfy $f_\epsilon \geq 1$ on $K$ for small $\epsilon $: every convolution ball meeting $K$ then lies in $G$, where $f=1$ almost everywhere. The assumed estimate gives $\mu (K)\leq C\lim _\epsilon \| f_\epsilon \| _{H^1}=C\| f\| _{H^1}$. Take the infimum over $f$ and then the supremum over compact $K\subset G$ to obtain the estimate for $G$. Outer regularity and open supersets give it for every Borel $E$. $\square$

Lemma 12.9.2. Every bounded admissible $u$ belongs to $H^1(X)$. Its trace

\[ \mu _ u=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T \]

is a positive Radon measure of total mass $A$. If $\| u\| _\infty \leq M$, then

\[ \| u\| _{H^1}\leq C(1+M),\qquad \mu _ u(E)\leq C(1+M)\operatorname {Cap}_1(E)^{1/2} \]

for every Borel $E$. On each fixed relatively compact coordinate chart, the total variation of every coefficient of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is also bounded on $E$ by $C(1+M)\operatorname {Cap}_1(E)^{1/2}$.

Proof. First let $u$ be smooth admissible. The first-cone inequality gives $\mu _ u\geq 0$, since $T$ is conformal to $\omega ^{n-1}$, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0$ gives $\mu _ u(X)=A$. Applying distributional Stokes to $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u^2)$ yields the energy identity

\[ \int _ X\mathrm du\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u\wedge T =-\int _ Xu\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge T =-\int _ Xu\, \mathrm d\mu _ u+\int _ Xu\omega \wedge T \leq 2MA. \]

Together with the $L^2$ bound this controls $\| u\| _{H^1}$. For bounded admissible $u$, choose smooth admissible $u_ j\downarrow u$ by 12.5.5, discard a finite initial segment, and take $\| u_ j\| _\infty \leq M+1$ as in 12.8.1. Bounded convergence gives strong $L^2$ convergence. The energy bound gives weak $H^1$ compactness, and every weak cluster point agrees with $u$ in $L^2$; thus the entire sequence converges weakly to $u$ in $H^1$. This proves the Sobolev bound. The trace measures converge weakly to $\mu _ u$, preserving positivity and total mass.

For any smooth real $\phi $, the first-order Stokes identity is

\[ \int _ X\phi \, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge T =-\int _ X\mathrm d\phi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u\wedge T +\int _ X\phi \, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u\wedge \mathrm dT. \]

It remains valid for $u\in H^1$ by smooth Sobolev approximation. Both terms are bounded by $C\| u\| _{H^1}\| \phi \| _{H^1}$; the background term $\int \phi \omega \wedge T$ is bounded by $C\| \phi \| _{H^1}$. The preceding capacity lemma gives the bound for $\mu _ u$. In particular the derivative $\mathrm dT$ has not been discarded.

For a smooth admissible approximant, write the eigenvalues of $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$ in an $\omega $-unitary frame as $\lambda _ i$. Since $m\geq 2$,

\[ \sum _ i\lambda _ i^2\leq \left(\sum _ i\lambda _ i\right)^2, \qquad \sum _ i\lambda _ i\geq 0. \]

Consequently every coefficient of this form is dominated by its trace. On a fixed coordinate chart this bounds the coefficient variations of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$ by $C(\mu _{u_ j}+\omega ^ n)$. Passing to the weak measure limits of 12.8.1 gives the same domination by $C(\mu _ u+\omega ^ n)$. Finally $\int _ E\omega ^ n\leq \operatorname {Cap}_1(E)$ and $\operatorname {Cap}_1(E)\leq \| 1\| _{H^1}^2$ absorb the smooth term into the claimed square-root estimate. $\square$

Lemma 12.9.3. Every bounded canonical admissible potential is continuous on the complement of an open set of arbitrarily small $\operatorname {Cap}_1$. The same open set can be chosen for any countable family of such potentials.

Proof. Choose smooth admissible $u_ j\downarrow u$. By the preceding lemma these functions converge weakly to $u$ in $H^1$. Mazur's lemma gives finite convex combinations $w_ N$ of terms with $j\geq N$ such that

\[ \| w_ N-u\| _{H^1}\leq 8^{-N}. \]

Because $u\leq w_ N\leq u_ N$, they converge pointwise to the canonical function $u$, not just to an unspecified almost-everywhere representative. Put

\[ G_{N_0}=\bigcup _{N\geq N_0} \{ |w_{N+1}-w_ N|>2^{-N}\} . \]

The capacity tools imply

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_1(G_{N_0}) \leq \sum _{N\geq N_0}4^ N\| w_{N+1}-w_ N\| _{H^1}^2 \leq C\sum _{N\geq N_0}16^{-N}\longrightarrow 0. \]

Outside $G_{N_0}$, the successive differences form a uniformly summable series. Thus $w_ N$ converge uniformly there and their limit $u$ is continuous on that closed set. For a countable family, choose the exceptional open sets with capacities less than $\epsilon 2^{-i}$ and take their union. Countable subadditivity proves the last assertion. $\square$

Proposition 12.9.4. Suppose $u_ j\downarrow u$ and $v_ j\downarrow v$ are bounded canonical admissible potentials with a common uniform bound. Then

\[ v_ j\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\longrightarrow v\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u \]

weakly as currents with signed measure coefficients. The same conclusion holds for $v_ j\mu _{u_ j}\to v\mu _ u$.

Proof. Bounded convergence gives $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1$. Their complex Hessians therefore converge in distributions. The preceding coefficient bounds are uniform, so this is weak convergence of each signed coefficient measure against continuous compactly supported tests. The trace measures also converge weakly. Every one of these measures has total variation bounded by $C_ M\operatorname {Cap}_1(E)^{1/2}$ on a Borel set $E$.

Fix $\epsilon >0$. Quasicontinuity supplies an open $G$ with $\operatorname {Cap}_1(G)<\epsilon $ on whose complement $v$ and every $v_ j$ are continuous. Dini's theorem on the compact set $X\setminus G$ gives uniform convergence there. Extend $v|_{X\setminus G}$ to a continuous function $F$ on $X$ with the same uniform bound, by Tietze's theorem. For any one of the signed coefficient measures $\nu _ j\to \nu $ and a fixed continuous compactly supported test $\psi $, subtract $F\nu _ j$ and $F\nu $. The resulting error is at most

\[ C_{M,\psi }\bigl (\| v_ j-v\| _{X\setminus G} +\epsilon ^{1/2}\bigr ). \]

The term with $F$ converges by weak measure convergence. First let $j\to \infty $ and then $\epsilon \downarrow 0$. This proves the assertion for the Hessian coefficients and for the positive trace measures. $\square$

Theorem 12.9.5. For two bounded admissible potentials $u,v$, the recursive current $D_2(u,v)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)$ is symmetric in its arguments. If $u_ j\downarrow u$ and $v_ j\downarrow v$ are bounded admissible sequences with a common uniform bound, then

\[ D_2(u_ j,v_ j)\longrightarrow D_2(u,v) \]

in distributions. Their shifted positive currents

\[ Q_2(u,v)=\bigl (\omega ^2+\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u +\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v+D_2(u,v)\bigr )\wedge \omega ^{n-m} \]

converge weakly as positive currents. For $m=2$ these are positive Radon measures. For $m>2$, the unwedged $D_2$ also converges weakly as a current with measure coefficients.

Proof. For smooth admissible approximations $u_ k\downarrow u$ and $v_ k\downarrow v$, the weighted convergence proposition gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v_ k\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ k)\longrightarrow \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u). \]

The smooth current on the left is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ k\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ k$ and is symmetric. Applying the same argument in the opposite order proves $D_2(u,v)=D_2(v,u)$. For the bounded sequences in the statement, weighted convergence followed by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ gives the asserted simultaneous distributional limit.

The displayed subset expansion is the current of 12.8.3 with $p=2$. Its lower-degree terms converge weakly, so its whole distributional limit is $Q_2(u,v)$. Positivity and the uniform local mass bounds turn this into weak convergence of currents of order zero. When $m>2$, the same conclusion for $D_2$ follows from the uniform coefficient bounds below degree $m$ in 12.8.2. $\square$

The capacity estimate depends on using a first Hessian. A product of two Hessians can already charge a set of zero Sobolev capacity.

Lemma 12.9.6. Let $E$ be a compact subset of a smooth real submanifold of codimension two contained in a coordinate chart. Then $\operatorname {Cap}_1(E)=0$.

Proof. It suffices to treat a compact subset of a submanifold chart, since finitely many such subsets cover $E$ and the capacity is subadditive. Choose real coordinates $(s,t)$ there, with $t\in \mathbb R^2$ and the submanifold given by $t=0$. Choose $0\leq \eta \leq 1$ smooth, supported in this chart and equal to one near the compact subset. Put $r=|t|$. For $0<\epsilon <1/2$ define

\[ h_\epsilon (r)= \begin{cases} 1,& 0\leq r\leq \epsilon ^2,\\ \displaystyle \frac{\log (\epsilon /r)}{\log (1/\epsilon )}, & \epsilon ^2<r<\epsilon ,\\ 0,& r\geq \epsilon . \end{cases} \]

The Sobolev function $f_\epsilon =\eta h_\epsilon $ equals one on an open neighborhood of that subset. On the fixed compact coordinate set the Sobolev norm is equivalent to the Euclidean one. Integration in the two normal variables gives

\[ \| f_\epsilon \| _{H^1}^2 \leq C\left(\epsilon ^2+ \int _{\epsilon ^2}^{\epsilon } \frac{\mathrm dr}{r\log (1/\epsilon )^2}\right) =C\left(\epsilon ^2+\frac1{\log (1/\epsilon )}\right) \longrightarrow 0. \]

The derivatives of $\eta $ contribute at most $C\epsilon ^2$. Taking the infimum over open neighborhoods proves the assertion. $\square$

Example 12.9.7. There are bounded continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic potentials $u,v$ and a compact Borel set $E$ such that

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_1(E)=0,\qquad \int _ E Q_2(u,v)\wedge \omega ^{m-2}>0. \]

Consequently the first-Hessian capacity estimate does not extend to this positive two-factor measure, even within the continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic subclass.

Proof. Choose holomorphic coordinates $z_ j=x_ j+\mathrm i y_ j$ and a smooth nonnegative cutoff $\chi $ supported in the chart and equal to one on a smaller coordinate box. Extend

\[ u=a\chi |x_1|,\qquad v=a\chi |x_2| \]

by zero outside the chart. These functions are globally continuous and bounded. To check their curvature, replace $|x_ j|$ by $g_\delta (x_ j)=(x_ j^2+\delta ^2)^{1/2}$, with $0<\delta \leq 1$. The Hessian of $\chi g_\delta $ has the nonnegative term $\chi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_\delta $; all its remaining terms are uniformly bounded forms, since $g_\delta $ and $g_\delta '$ are uniformly bounded on the fixed support. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi g_\delta )\geq -C\omega $ with $C$ independent of $\delta $. Choosing $a>0$ sufficiently small gives $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a\chi g_\delta )\geq \omega /2$ for both coordinates. Their decreasing limits $u,v$ are therefore $\omega $-plurisubharmonic and admissible.

On the box where $\chi =1$, set $\alpha _ j=\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi }\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$. The book's normalization gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_\delta (x_ j)=\tfrac 12g_\delta ''(x_ j)\alpha _ j, \qquad g_\delta ''(x_ j) =\frac{\delta ^2}{(x_ j^2+\delta ^2)^{3/2}} \longrightarrow 2\delta _{\{ x_ j=0\} }. \]

The measures $g_\delta ^{\prime \prime }(t)\, \mathrm dt$ have mass two, while their mass outside any fixed neighborhood of zero tends to zero. The two smooth factors depend on distinct real variables, so their product is an approximate identity in $(x_1,x_2)$ and converges to the tensor-product measure. By 12.9.5, locally

\[ D_2(u,v)=a^2\delta _{\{ x_1=0\} }\delta _{\{ x_2=0\} } \alpha _1\wedge \alpha _2. \]

Choose $E$ to be a compact piece of $\{ x_1=x_2=0\} $ inside this box with nonempty relative interior. The preceding lemma gives $\operatorname {Cap}_1(E)=0$. On this box, the positive measure $D_2(u,v)\wedge \omega ^{n-2}$ has strictly positive mass on $E$, since $\omega $ is strictly positive. The smooth term $\omega ^ n$ and the two single-Hessian terms in the expansion

\[ Q_2(u,v)\wedge \omega ^{m-2} =\omega ^ n+\omega ^{n-1}\wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v) +D_2(u,v)\wedge \omega ^{n-2} \]

have zero mass on $E$: each single-Hessian term is absolutely continuous in the other normal real variable. Hence the asserted mass is strictly positive. A bound by $C\operatorname {Cap}_1(F)^ b$ for all Borel $F$, with any $b>0$, would contradict this choice of $E$. $\square$

The preceding Sobolev-capacity estimate therefore cannot be iterated to control higher-degree coefficient measures. A local Hessian capacity supplies that control in the next section.

12.10 Local capacities and decreasing products

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The codimension-two example shows why Sobolev capacity cannot control a product of Hessians. Local capacities built from smooth Hessian measures give the stronger control needed for the recursive currents. Their quasicontinuity estimate comes from an energy variation in which the derivatives of the background remain present.

Work on a coordinate ball $U$ with compact closure in a holomorphic chart, with the restriction of $\omega $. For a smooth function in the local unshifted class of 12.8.5, put

\[ \Lambda _ s(f)=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ s\wedge \omega ^{n-s},\qquad 0\leq s\leq m, \]

where $\Lambda _0(f)=\omega ^ n$. The local mass and gradient estimates needed for the capacity use ordinary smooth products.

Lemma 12.10.1. Fix $K\Subset U$ and $M<\infty $. For smooth local $m$-subharmonic $f_1,\ldots ,f_ q$ with $|f_ i|\leq M$, $0\leq q\leq m$, their mixed trace has mass at most a constant depending only on $K,U,\omega ,m,M$. If also $f$ is smooth in this class and $|f|\leq M$, then, for $q\leq m-1$,

\[ \int _ K\mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ q \wedge \omega ^{n-q-1}\leq C. \]

Proof. For one potential and a nonnegative compactly supported cutoff $\chi $, closedness of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ gives

\[ \int \chi (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ q\omega ^{n-q} =\int f(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^{q-1}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\chi \omega ^{n-q}). \]

For $q\geq 1$, the absolute-form estimate 12.1.5 applies at degree $q-1<m$. Nested cutoffs and induction, starting with volume, therefore bound the left side by $C M^ q$; replacing $M$ by $1+M$ includes $M=0$. For mixed factors, positivity of every term in the expansion of $(\sum _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ i)^ q\omega ^{n-q}$ bounds the mixed trace by a fixed multiple of the single trace for $\sum _ i f_ i$. This proves the first assertion, including its degree-zero convention.

Replace $f$ by $f+M$, so that $0\leq f\leq 2M$ without changing its gradient or Hessian. The identity

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(f^2)=2f\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f+2\mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f \]

also shows that $f^2$ belongs to the local cone. Wedge with the remaining $q$ Hessians and $\omega ^{n-q-1}$. The first term on the right is nonnegative, and the mixed mass estimate applied to $f^2$ and the other potentials bounds the second term. The degree used is $q+1\leq m$. A cutoff equal to one on $K$ gives the stated local estimate. $\square$

Definition 12.10.2. For Borel $E\subset U$ define the smooth capacity

\[ c_ s(E)=\sup \left\{ \int _ E \Lambda _ s(a): a\in C^\infty (U),\ -1\leq a\leq 0, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\in \overline{\Gamma _ m(\omega )}\right\} , \]

for $0\leq s\leq m$. Its outer-open version is

\[ c_ s^*(E)=\inf \{ c_ s(G):E\subset G\subset U,\ G\text{ open}\} . \]

A function on a compact $K\Subset U$ is quasicontinuous for this capacity if, for every $\epsilon >0$, it is continuous on $K\setminus G$ for some open $G\subset U$ with $c_ s(G)<\epsilon $.

In particular $c_0(E)=\int _ E\omega ^ n$. Each $c_ s$ is monotone, countably subadditive and continuous under increasing unions. For the last assertion, the supremum over competitors commutes with the increasing supremum over sets. Local smooth mass bounds make it finite on compact subsets. The outer capacity $c_ s^*$ is monotone and countably subadditive and agrees with $c_ s$ on open sets.

The capacities control mixed traces for smooth local $m$-subharmonic functions $f_1,\ldots ,f_ q$ with a common bound. Choose a fixed smooth strictly plurisubharmonic $b$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\geq \omega $ on $U$. For $q\leq s$, choose constants $B$ and $L\geq 1$ so that $(f_1+\cdots +f_ q+b-B)/L$ lies in $[-1,0]$. Both constants depend only on the common bound and the fixed data. Positivity of the smooth binomial expansion gives

12.15
\begin{equation} (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_1+\cdots +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ q)^ q\omega ^{n-q} \leq C \Lambda _ s\bigl ((\textstyle \sum _ i f_ i+b-B)/L\bigr ). \end{equation}

For $q=0$ the left side is $\omega ^ n$; the same argument applies. Indeed, replacing any of the $s-q$ factors $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b$ by $\omega $ subtracts a nonnegative mixed trace. Thus every such mixed trace, and every coefficient of a smooth product of $q<m$ Hessians, is dominated on Borel sets by $C c_ s$. The latter assertion follows from the absolute mixed-form estimate.

Lemma 12.10.3. Let $0\leq q\leq m-1$ and let $S$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form in $\overline{\Gamma _ m(\omega )}$ pointwise. Put $T=S^ q\omega ^{n-q-2}$. For smooth real $f$ and nonnegative Borel weights $a$, supported on a fixed compact set,

\[ \left|\int a\, \mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge T\right| \leq C\left(\int a\, \mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge \omega \wedge T\right)^{1/2} \left(\int a\, \omega ^2\wedge T\right)^{1/2}. \]

The corresponding inequality with $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge \mathrm d\omega $ holds as well. For a mixed product of $q$ cone forms in place of $S^ q$, it suffices to sum these bounds over the finitely many subset sums of its factors.

Proof. For $q\leq m-2$, diagonalize $S$ in an $\omega $-unitary frame. Every diagonal coefficient of $S^ q\omega ^{n-q-2}$ is a positive multiple of $S_{q;ij}(\lambda )$ for two omitted coordinates. This is nonnegative by deleted-coordinate positivity, because $q+2\leq m$. The form is a nonnegative sum of simple positive coordinate forms.

For one such simple form, only the remaining complex two-plane contributes to a complementary wedge. The coefficients of $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega $ are uniformly bounded in unitary frames on the fixed compact set. Its wedge with $\mathrm df$ is consequently bounded by a constant times the gradient norm on that plane. The wedges with $\omega ^2$ and with $\mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge \omega $ give, up to fixed positive constants, one and the square of that norm. Pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz over the simple summands, followed by integral Cauchy–Schwarz with weight $a$, proves the estimate. Conjugation gives the other estimate.

For $q=m-1$, at one point choose an $\omega $-unitary coframe $e_ i$ that diagonalizes $S$. Put $\epsilon _ i=\mathrm i e_ i\wedge \bar e_ i$ and $\Omega =\epsilon _1\cdots \epsilon _ n$, so that $\omega =\sum _ i\epsilon _ i$ and $S=\sum _ i\lambda _ i\epsilon _ i$. Writing $B_{ij}=S_{q;ij}(\lambda )$ and $D_ i=S_{q;i}(\lambda )$, ordinary wedge expansion gives

\[ S^ q\omega ^{n-q-2} =q!(n-q-2)!\sum _{i<j}B_{ij} \prod _{k\notin \{ i,j\} }\epsilon _ k. \]

Only the remaining two-plane contributes to its wedge with $\mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega $. If $\partial f=\sum _ i f_ i e_ i$, its absolute density relative to $\Omega $ is bounded by

\[ C\sum _{i<j}(|f_ i|+|f_ j|)|B_{ij}|. \]

The constant bounds the tensor coefficients of $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega $ in all unitary frames on the fixed compact set. Diagonalization is only pointwise; no derivative of this moving frame is taken.

12.1.6 and finite Cauchy–Schwarz bound this sum by

\[ C\sum _ i|f_ i|\sqrt{D_ i}\sum _ j\sqrt{D_ j} \leq C\left(\sum _ i|f_ i|^2D_ i\right)^{1/2} \left(\sum _ iD_ i\right)^{1/2}. \]

Here $\sum _ iD_ i=(n-q)S_ q(\lambda )$. Moreover

\[ \begin{aligned} S^ q\omega ^{n-q}& =q!(n-q)!S_ q(\lambda )\Omega ,\\ \mathrm df\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge S^ q\omega ^{n-q-1} & =\kappa q!(n-q-1)!\sum _ i|f_ i|^2D_ i\, \Omega , \end{aligned} \]

where $\kappa =1/(2\pi )$ by the book's $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ normalization. These two densities are nonnegative. Thus the pointwise torsion density is bounded by a constant times their geometric mean. Multiplication by $a$ and integral Cauchy–Schwarz give the assertion, including the cases where either density vanishes. The other wedge is conjugate up to a fixed sign, which does not affect its absolute value. For $q\geq 1$, use the exact polarization identity

\[ q!\, \alpha _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha _ q =\sum _{J\subset \{ 1,\ldots ,q\} } (-1)^{q-|J|}\left(\sum _{i\in J}\alpha _ i\right)^ q. \]

Every nonempty subset sum remains in the cone. Applying the single power estimate to each term and taking absolute values proves the mixed assertion. At $q=0$ use the single term $S^0=1$. No Cauchy–Schwarz assertion for an arbitrary weakly positive $(n-2,n-2)$-form is needed. $\square$

Lemma 12.10.4. Let $f_ j$ be uniformly bounded smooth local $m$-subharmonic functions with $f_ j\downarrow f$, and fix a smaller concentric ball $V\Subset U$. There are uniformly bounded smooth local functions $v_ j\downarrow v$ which equal $f_ j$ on a neighborhood of $\overline V$ and equal one fixed strictly plurisubharmonic $\psi $ on one fixed boundary collar. Moreover $v=f$ near $\overline V$ and $v=\psi $ on that collar.

Proof. Write $U=\{ |z|<R\} $ and $|f_ j|\leq M$. Take $\psi =A(|z|^2-R^2)+b$, with $b>M+2$ and then $A$ so large that $\psi <-M-2$ on a neighborhood of $\overline V$. It exceeds $M+2$ on a fixed sufficiently thin boundary collar.

Use the regularized maximum $M_\epsilon $ constructed in 12.1.7. Choose $0<\epsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ with $\epsilon _ j<1$, and put $v_ j=M_{\epsilon _ j}(f_ j,\psi )$. The lemma preserves the local unshifted cone condition. Its two monotonicities give $v_ j\downarrow \max (f,\psi )=v$, and the fixed gaps give the two asserted regions of exact equality. The bounds

\[ \max (f_ j,\psi )\leq v_ j\leq \max (f_ j,\psi )+\epsilon _ j/2 \]

give a common bound. In particular all differences $v_ k-v_ j$ have support in one fixed compact subset of $U$. $\square$

Proposition 12.10.5. For the localized sequence in the preceding lemma and $0\leq s\leq m$,

\[ c_ s\{ v_ k-v>\delta \} \longrightarrow 0 \qquad (\delta >0). \]

Consequently a uniformly bounded smooth decreasing local sequence has a quasicontinuous pointwise limit on every compact subset of $U$, for each $c_ s^*$ as well as $c_ s$, $0\leq s\leq m$.

Proof. The assertion for $s=0$ follows by bounded convergence for volume. Induct on $s$, assuming it for $s-1$, and put $q=s-1$ and $\theta =\omega ^{n-s}$. For $j\geq k$ let $h=v_ k-v_ j\geq 0$. The support of $h$ lies in one fixed compact set $K_0\Subset U$. Smooth capacity domination and the induction hypothesis give, uniformly over all bounded local smooth mixed potentials,

12.18
\begin{equation} \int h\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_1\cdots \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_ q\omega ^{n-q} \longrightarrow 0\quad (k\to \infty ). \end{equation}

To see this, split at $h=\delta $. On $\{ h\leq \delta \} $ the mass bound gives $C\delta $. On the remaining set use $h\leq v_ k-v$, the capacity bound and the induction hypothesis. Let $k$ tend to infinity before $\delta \downarrow 0$. For $q=0$ this is the volume estimate. Constants are uniform in $j\geq k$ and all potentials with the fixed common bound.

Consider the real smooth energy

\[ E_ s(w)=\int _ U w(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ s\theta , \qquad w_ t=v_ j+t h,\quad 0\leq t\leq 1. \]

The energies $E_ s(v_ j)$ are bounded: on the fixed collar the integrand is fixed, and the remaining compact set has a uniform smooth mass bound. Since $h$ vanishes near the boundary, integration by parts and closedness of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ t$ give the exact identity

12.19
\begin{equation} \begin{split} \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}E_ s(w_ t) & =(s+1)\int h(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ t)^ s\theta \\ & \quad +s\int h(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ t)^ q \bigl (\mathrm dw_ t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\theta -\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w_ t\wedge \mathrm d\theta +w_ t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta \bigr ). \end{split} \end{equation}

Here

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm d\theta & =(n-s)\mathrm d\omega \wedge \omega ^{n-s-1},\\ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\theta & =(n-s)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega \wedge \omega ^{n-s-1}\\ & \quad +(n-s)(n-s-1)\mathrm d\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega \wedge \omega ^{n-s-2}, \end{aligned} \]

and the analogous $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ formula holds. A zero scalar coefficient means that its term is absent. The two first-order terms in (12.19) tend uniformly to zero by 12.10.3, the uniform local gradient bound, and (12.18). The last term has the same property by the absolute-form estimate at degree $q<m$. More explicitly, the first torsion integral is bounded by

\[ C\left(\int h\, \mathrm dw_ t\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w_ t \wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ t)^ q\omega ^{n-q-1}\right)^{1/2} \left(\int h(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ t)^ q\omega ^{n-q}\right)^{1/2}. \]

The first factor is bounded since $h$ has a common bound and 12.10.1 applies; the second tends to zero by (12.18). Conjugation handles the other first-order term. Thus, with $e_ k\downarrow 0$ after taking a tail supremum,

\[ E_ s(v_ k)-E_ s(v_ j)=(s+1)\Delta _{kj}+r_{kj}, \quad \Delta _{kj}=\int _0^1\int h\Lambda _ s(w_ t)\, \mathrm dt\geq 0, \quad |r_{kj}|\leq e_ k. \]

It follows that $E_ s(v_ j)\leq E_ s(v_ k)+e_ k$ for $j\geq k$. Taking a subsequence $k$ approaching the liminf shows that the bounded energy sequence converges. Hence $\Delta _{kj}\to 0$ uniformly for $j\geq k$. Positivity of the binomial expansion $\Lambda _ s(w_ t)\geq (1-t)^ s \Lambda _ s(v_ j)$ then gives

12.25
\begin{equation} \int h\Lambda _ s(v_ j)\longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{uniformly for }j\geq k. \end{equation}

It remains to replace each Hessian factor by a capacity competitor. For any smooth $-1\leq a\leq 0$ in the local cone, put

\[ I_\ell =\int h(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^\ell (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a)^{s-\ell }\theta , \qquad \eta _\ell =(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j)^\ell (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a)^{s-\ell -1}, \quad 0\leq \ell <s. \]

First-order Stokes, with every metric derivative retained, gives

\[ I_\ell =-\int \mathrm dh\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}a\wedge \eta _\ell \theta +\int h\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}a\wedge \eta _\ell \mathrm d\theta . \]

The second term is $o(1)$ uniformly in $a$ and $j\geq k$. Use finite polarization in 12.10.3; each subset sum has a uniform local gradient bound and its lower trace is controlled by (12.18).

The form $\eta _\ell \theta $ is positive of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$: a complementary nonnegative rank-one form completes a polarized product of $s\leq m$ cone forms. Its associated Hermitian gradient pairing is nonnegative, so Cauchy–Schwarz and the uniform gradient bound for $a$ give

\[ I_\ell \leq C F_\ell (h)^{1/2}+o(1), \qquad F_\ell (h)=\int \mathrm dh\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}h\wedge \eta _\ell \theta . \]

Another exact first-order Stokes identity gives

\[ F_\ell (h)= I_{\ell +1} -\int h\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ k\wedge \eta _\ell \theta +\int h\, \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}h\wedge \eta _\ell \mathrm d\theta \leq I_{\ell +1}+o(1). \]

The dropped term is nonnegative. For the last term the same polarized torsion estimate applies: its first gradient factor is uniformly bounded because $h=v_ k-v_ j$, and its second factor tends to zero by (12.18); here $h^2\leq C h$ and the gradient energy of the difference is at most twice the sum of those of $v_ k$ and $v_ j$ for every positive single-power gradient pairing. Starting with (12.25) at $I_ s$, finite backward induction gives $I_0\to 0$ uniformly in $a$ and $j\geq k$.

Chebyshev's inequality now yields $c_ s\{ v_ k-v_ j>\delta \} \leq \delta ^{-1}\sup _ a I_0$. These sets increase to $\{ v_ k-v>\delta \} $ as $j\to \infty $. Continuity of $c_ s$ under increasing unions proves the induction.

For any one $0\leq s\leq m$, choose $k_ N$ so that the open sets $G_ N=\{ v_{k_ N}-v>2^{-N}\} $ satisfy $c_ s(G_ N)<\epsilon 2^{-N}$, $N\geq 1$. They are open because $v$ is upper semicontinuous and $v_{k_ N}$ is smooth. On $U\setminus \bigcup _ NG_ N$ these smooth functions converge uniformly to the pointwise limit $v$. Hence $v$ is continuous there. The union has capacity less than $\epsilon $. Boundary localization transfers this conclusion to $f$ on each smaller compact set. Open sets have the same outer capacity, which also proves the assertion for $c_ s^*$. $\square$

Lemma 12.10.6. Every bounded admissible potential on $X$ is locally quasicontinuous for each smooth capacity $c_ s^*$, $0\leq s\leq m$. For $0\leq p<m$, each coefficient measure $\nu $ of the ordered $D_ p$ satisfies

\[ |\nu |(E)\leq C c_{m-1}^*(E),\qquad E\subset K\Subset U, \]

uniformly when its global potentials have one common bound. The assertion at $p=0$ means the volume coefficient of $D_0=1$.

Proof. The global decreasing approximation and 12.8.5 provide bounded smooth local approximations to $u+\rho $. The preceding proposition makes its canonical pointwise limit quasicontinuous. Subtracting the smooth $\rho $ preserves quasicontinuity.

\[ \int _ X H_ m(u)>0. \]

For bounded admissible $u,V$, use the notation

\[ \sigma _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u,\qquad M_{p,q}=\sigma _ u^ p\sigma _ V^ q\omega ^{n-p-q} \quad (0\leq p+q\leq m). \]

These expressions denote the positive mixed measure $Q_ m$ with $p$ slots equal to $u$, $q$ slots equal to $V$ and the remaining slots equal to the constant zero. In particular $M_{0,0}=\omega ^ n$. For $p+q\leq m-1$, put

\[ T_{p,q}=\sigma _ u^ p\sigma _ V^ q\omega ^{n-p-q-1}. \]

This denotes $R_{m-1}$ with the same nonconstant slots and zero in its other slots, wedged with $\omega ^{n-m}$.

Lemma 12.12.5. Let $u,V$ be bounded admissible, $b$ a real constant and $s>0$. Suppose $0\leq \max \{ u,V+b\} -u\leq s$. Set $E=\{ u<V+b\} $ and $b_{p,q}=M_{p,q}(E)$. There is a constant $C$, depending only on $X,\omega ,m,n$, such that

\[ b_{p,q+1}\leq b_{p+1,q} +Cs\sum _{a+d\leq p+q}b_{a,d} \qquad (p+q\leq m-1). \]

For $q=0$, the error can instead be bounded by $Cs(b_{p,0}+b_{p-1,0}+b_{p-2,0})$, with negative indices zero.

Proof. First estimate the torsion of $T_{p,q}$, writing $r=p+q$. For smooth admissible potentials, the product rule expresses $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_{p,q}$ as a sum of fixed smooth metric forms wedged with products of $\sigma _ u,\sigma _ V$ of degrees $r,r-1,r-2$. The terms of degree $r$ come from differentiating the background power; they cannot be discarded. Indeed, $\mathrm d\sigma _ u=\mathrm d\sigma _ V=\mathrm d\omega $, $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\sigma _ u=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\sigma _ V=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega $ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\sigma _ u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\sigma _ V=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $. Every term therefore contains either $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $ or $\mathrm d\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega $, with its product-rule multiplicity and the appropriate remaining background power. Terms with negative numbers of factors are absent.

All variable degrees are at most $r<m$. Apply 12.1.5 to each term. Expanding the resulting positive power of the sum of its variable factors gives the pointwise bound

\[ |\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_{p,q}|\leq C\sum _{a+d\leq r}M_{a,d}. \]

For $q=0$ every variable factor is $\sigma _ u$, so the sharper bound is $C(M_{p,0}+M_{p-1,0}+M_{p-2,0})$. The constant bounds fixed metric coefficients on a finite coordinate cover and the finitely many multiplicities; it is independent of the potentials and their bounds.

Choose uniformly bounded smooth decreasing approximations to $u,V$. By 12.11.4, the torsion measures converge weakly; by 12.10.8, all positive measures in these bounds also converge weakly. Against nonnegative continuous tests, lower semicontinuity of total variation preserves both bounds. They are consequently inequalities of measures and may be restricted to the Borel set $E$.

Put $w=\max \{ u,V+b\} $ and $h=w-u$. The mixed maximum theorem 12.11.2, with one moving slot and the fixed slots defining $T_{p,q}$, gives

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)T_{p,q} \geq \mathbf1_ E\sigma _ VT_{p,q} +\mathbf1_{X\setminus E}\sigma _ uT_{p,q}. \]

All products here are the corresponding positive mixed measures. After integration, this implies

\[ b_{p,q+1}\leq b_{p+1,q} +\int _ X\bigl ((\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)-\sigma _ u\bigr )T_{p,q}. \]

Regularized maxima of the smooth decreasing approximations give a uniformly bounded decreasing smooth list $w_ j\downarrow w$. Stokes' theorem on compact $X$ gives for these lists

\[ \int _ X\bigl ((\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j)-\sigma _{u_ j}\bigr )T_{p,q}^ j =\int _ X(w_ j-u_ j)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_{p,q}^ j. \]

Joint decreasing convergence passes the positive mixed measures on the left to their limits. The continuous two-variable weight $F(x,y)=y-x$ in 12.11.4 passes the right side to $\int _ Xh\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_{p,q}$. No monotonicity of $w_ j-u_ j$ is needed. Since $h$ is zero outside $E$ and $0\leq h\leq s$,

\[ \int _ Xh\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_{p,q}\leq s|\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T_{p,q}|(E). \]

The torsion bounds prove both assertions. The argument never passes a varying measure to a weak limit after restriction to $E$. $\square$

Theorem 12.12.6. There is $C\geq 1$, depending only on $X,\omega ,m,n$, with the following property. For bounded admissible $u,v$ and $0<\alpha <1$, put $\eta =1-\alpha $, $V=\alpha v$ and $S=\inf _ X(u-V)$. For $0<s<\eta ^3/C$, let $E_ s=\{ u<V+S+s\} $. Then

\[ \int _{E_ s}H_ m(V) \leq \left(1+\frac{Cs}{\eta ^ m}\right) \int _{E_ s}H_ m(u). \]

No differential identity for a power of $\omega $ is assumed.

Proof. Use the mixed measures above on $E_ s$, writing $b_{p,q}=M_{p,q}(E_ s)$ and $a_ k=b_{k,0}$. The definition of $S$ gives $0\leq \max \{ u,V+S+s\} -u\leq s$, so 12.12.5 applies. Repeatedly moving a $V$ slot to a $u$ slot gives, for $p+q=r$,

\[ b_{p,q}\leq a_ r+C_1s\sum _{d=0}^{r-1}A_ d, \qquad A_ d=\max _{a+b=d}b_{a,b},\qquad 1\leq r\leq m. \]

Here there are at most $m$ moves, and the multiplicity $d+1$ in the lower-degree sum is absorbed in $C_1$. For $0<s\leq 1$, induction on $r$ shows

\[ A_ r\leq a_ r+K_ rs\sum _{k<r}a_ k. \]

In fact $A_0=a_0$, and one may take $K_ r=C_1(1+\sum _{1\leq d<r}K_ d)$: insert the earlier bounds in the displayed recurrence and use $s^2\leq s$. Thus, for a constant $C_2$ depending only on the setting,

\[ b_{0,m}\leq a_ m+C_2s\sum _{k<m}a_ k. \]

It remains to control the lower diagonal masses by $a_ m$. The identity $\sigma _ V=\alpha \sigma _ v+\eta \omega $ and mixed positivity give $b_{k,1}\geq \eta a_ k$; this identity and inequality pass from smooth approximations by joint convergence. The diagonal assertion of 12.12.5 gives

\[ \eta a_ k\leq a_{k+1} +C_3s(a_ k+a_{k-1}+a_{k-2})\qquad (0\leq k<m), \]

with $a_{-1}=a_{-2}=0$. Set $B_ k=\eta ^{m-k}a_ k$ for $0\leq k\leq m$, and set $B_{-1}=B_{-2}=0$. Multiplying by $\eta ^{m-k-1}$ and using $0<\eta <1$ gives

\[ B_ k\leq B_{k+1} +\delta (B_ k+B_{k-1}+B_{k-2}), \qquad \delta =C_3s\eta ^{-3}. \]

For $M=\max _{0\leq k<m}B_ k$, summing from any $j<m$ through $m-1$ gives $B_ j\leq B_ m+3m\delta M$. If $s<\eta ^3/(6mC_3)$, it follows that $M\leq 2B_ m$. Consequently

\[ a_ k\leq 2\eta ^{-(m-k)}a_ m\quad (k<m),\qquad \sum _{k<m}a_ k\leq 2m\eta ^{-m}a_ m. \]

Choose $C\geq \max \{ 1,6mC_3,2mC_2\} $. Then the asserted range for $s$ supplies every preceding restriction, and substitution in the bound for $b_{0,m}$ proves the theorem. No division by $a_ m$ occurs, so zero top mass on $E_ s$ is included. $\square$

12.12.2 Domination and proper equations

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The weak modified comparison estimate makes strict domination possible on an arbitrary Hermitian background. A convex rescaling supplies a positive volume term, while choosing a sufficiently small sublevel set absorbs the torsion error. Canonical representatives detect its positive volume even when the set is not open.

Theorem 12.12.7. Let $u,v$ be bounded admissible. If, for some $0\leq c<1$,

\[ \mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(u) \leq c\mathbf1_{\{ u<v\} }H_ m(v), \]

then $u\geq v$ everywhere, on an arbitrary Hermitian background. In particular, $H_ m(u)(\{ u<v\} )=0$ implies $u\geq v$.

Proof. Add the same constant to $u,v$ so that $v\geq 1$. This preserves the measure hypothesis and the desired conclusion. Choose $0<\alpha <1$ with $\alpha ^ m>c$ and put $V=\alpha v$, $\eta =1-\alpha $. Suppose $S=\inf _ X(u-V)<0$. For $0<s<-S$, the set $E_ s=\{ u<V+S+s\} $ is contained in $\{ u<V\} $ and hence in $\{ u<v\} $. It has positive volume. Indeed, if its volume were zero, then $u\geq V+S+s$ almost everywhere. Both functions are bounded admissible with their canonical representatives, so 12.1.8 would give this order everywhere, contradicting the definition of $S$. The argument does not require $E_ s$ to be open or the infimum to be attained.

Choose also $s<\eta ^3/C$, where $C$ is from 12.12.6. By 12.11.3 and the measure hypothesis,

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha ^ m H_ m(v)(E_ s)+\eta ^ m\int _{E_ s}\omega ^ n & \leq H_ m(V)(E_ s)\\ & \leq (1+Cs\eta ^{-m})H_ m(u)(E_ s)\\ & \leq c(1+Cs\eta ^{-m})H_ m(v)(E_ s). \end{aligned} \]

If $c>0$, take $s$ still smaller so that $c(1+Cs\eta ^{-m})<\alpha ^ m$. If $c=0$, this strict inequality already holds. All measures are finite and positive, so the display forces the positive-volume term to be zero, a contradiction. Hence $S\geq 0$ and $u\geq \alpha v$. Let $\alpha $ increase to one, with $\alpha ^ m>c$. Since $v$ is bounded, $\alpha v\to v$ uniformly, and therefore $u\geq v$. The final assertion is $c=0$. $\square$

Corollary 12.12.8. Fix $a>0$. If bounded admissible $u,v$ satisfy

\[ e^{-av}H_ m(v)\geq e^{-au}H_ m(u), \]

then $u\geq v$. Consequently, for any fixed finite positive Radon measure $\nu $, the equation $H_ m(u)=e^{au}\nu $ has at most one bounded admissible solution.

Proof. For $\delta >0$, put $E_\delta =\{ u<v-\delta \} $. Multiplying the assumed measure inequality by the positive bounded Borel function $e^{au}$ gives on $E_\delta $

\[ H_ m(u)\leq e^{a(u-v)}H_ m(v) \leq e^{-a\delta }H_ m(v-\delta ). \]

The last identity uses invariance under constants. Since $e^{-a\delta }<1$, 12.12.7 applied to $u,v-\delta $ gives $u\geq v-\delta $ everywhere. Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$ gives $u\geq v$. Two solutions of the stated equation have equal weighted measures $e^{-au}H_ m(u)=\nu $. Applying the order assertion in both directions proves uniqueness. $\square$

A strictly increasing exponent also controls changes in logarithmic data for a fixed Radon right-hand measure.

Proposition 12.12.9. Let $A<B$ and $f$ be smooth near $X\times [A,B]$, with $\partial _ t f\geq \kappa >0$ there, and let $\nu $ be a finite positive Radon measure. Suppose bounded admissible $u,v$ have values in $[A,B]$ and satisfy

\[ H_ m(u)=e^{f(x,u)+h_1}\nu ,\qquad H_ m(v)=e^{f(x,v)+h_2}\nu , \]

where $h_1,h_2$ are bounded Borel functions. Then

\[ \| u-v\| _\infty \leq \kappa ^{-1}\| h_1-h_2\| _{L^\infty (\nu )}. \]

Proof. Put $K=\| h_1-h_2\| _{L^\infty (\nu )}$ and fix $s>K/\kappa $. On $E_ s=\{ v+s<u\} $, the actual values $u,v$ lie in $[A,B]$, so $f(x,u)-f(x,v)\geq \kappa s$. The equations and the essential bound on $h_2-h_1$ therefore give, as restricted measures,

\[ \mathbf1_{E_ s}H_ m(v+s) =\mathbf1_{E_ s}H_ m(v) \leq e^{K-\kappa s}\mathbf1_{E_ s}H_ m(u). \]

The constant is smaller than one. Applying 12.12.7 to $v+s,u$ gives $u\leq v+s$ pointwise. Let $s\downarrow K/\kappa $ and exchange $u,v$ to obtain the norm bound. The exponent is evaluated only at $u,v$; the shifted comparison potential need not take its values in $[A,B]$. $\square$

Corollary 12.12.10. Under the hypotheses of 12.7.3, every bounded admissible solution with values in $[A,B]$ is its smooth solution. In particular, for every $a>0$ and smooth real $H$, the equation $H_ m(u)=e^{au+H}\omega ^ n$ has exactly one bounded admissible solution, and that solution is smooth and strictly admissible.

Proof. For a continuous admissible potential, smooth decreasing admissible approximations converge uniformly on compact $X$ by Dini's theorem. Thus 12.10.8, 12.6.1 define the same Hessian measure on the continuous class. The smooth solution of 12.7.3 therefore solves the bounded equation as well. Apply 12.12.9 with $\nu =\omega ^ n$ and $h_1=h_2=0$ to identify any other bounded solution in $[A,B]$ with it. For the final assertion, smooth existence is already 12.5.1, and 12.12.8 with $\nu =e^ H\omega ^ n$ gives uniqueness among all bounded solutions. $\square$
