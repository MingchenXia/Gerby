15.19 Degenerating semipositive backgrounds28A495F29AAD

28A495F29AAD

Adding a small multiple of a Hermitian form makes a semipositive background strictly positive, and the smooth theory then solves the normalized equation with a smooth positive density. We study these smooth solutions as the added multiple tends to zero. Bounded subsolutions give uniform bounds for the potentials and the normalizing constants, and a strict analytic barrier gives uniform bounds for the trace on compact sets away from its poles. Smooth local convergence then identifies the limiting equation, without any global continuity property of the Monge–Ampère operator.

The main example is a small resolution of a threefold with nodes (15.19.8). There the background is the pullback of a positive form from the singular threefold, and it degenerates exactly along the exceptional curves. When this background is closed, the limit is unique and the whole family converges smoothly away from the exceptional curves. Convergence of the associated metric spaces then reduces to controlling the diameters of neighborhoods of these curves, in the limiting metric and in the approximating metrics (15.19.18).

Throughout this section $n\geq 2$. The background $\theta $, the barrier $\rho $ and its pole set $Z$ are new data, independent of those fixed in section 15.13. Let $\theta \geq 0$ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, and fix a Hermitian form $\eta $ and a smooth positive probability volume form $\mu $ on $X$. Suppose that there are $\delta >0$ and $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with analytic singularities such that

C397B905A723 15.19.1
\begin{equation} \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \eta , \qquad \sup _ X\rho =0. \end{equation}

Write $Z=\{ \rho =-\infty \} $ and assume that $\rho $ is smooth on $X\setminus Z$. For $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$, the form $\theta +\varepsilon \eta $ is Hermitian, and theorem 14.2.1 gives $u_\varepsilon \in C^\infty (X)$ and $c_\varepsilon >0$ with

8C6C5AF468B2 15.19.2
\begin{equation} \omega _\varepsilon :=\theta +\varepsilon \eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon >0, \qquad \omega _\varepsilon ^ n=c_\varepsilon \mu , \qquad \sup _ Xu_\varepsilon =0. \end{equation}

Lemma 15.19.3.4A0FCD6D9FCD There are constants $b,B,C>0$, independent of $\varepsilon $, such that

\[ b\leq c_\varepsilon \leq B, \qquad -C\leq u_\varepsilon \leq 0. \]

Proof. Write $\mu =f\, \mathrm{d}V$, where $f$ is smooth and positive. The strict barrier makes $\theta $ big. By lemma 15.3.1, there is a bounded $s\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $-1\leq s\leq 0$ and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (s)\geq b\mu $ for some fixed $b>0$. If $c_\varepsilon <b$, positivity gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_{\theta +\varepsilon \eta }(u_\varepsilon ) \leq (c_\varepsilon /b)\mathrm{MA}_{\theta +\varepsilon \eta }(s+A) \]

for every constant $A$. Strict bounded domination would imply $u_\varepsilon \geq s+A$ for every $A$, which is absurd. Hence $c_\varepsilon \geq b$.

For an upper bound, choose a fixed Gauduchon form $\gamma $. The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality and Stokes give

\[ c_\varepsilon ^{1/n} \int _ X\left(\frac{\mu }{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n \leq \int _ X\omega _\varepsilon \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =\int _ X(\theta +\varepsilon \eta )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1} \leq \int _ X(\theta +\eta )\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}. \]

The first integral is fixed and positive, giving $c_\varepsilon \leq B$.

For the lower bound on $u_\varepsilon $, note that all $u_\varepsilon $ are normalized $(\theta +\eta )$-plurisubharmonic functions. Uniform exponential integrability, lemma 5.26.2, gives $a>0$ such that $g_\varepsilon =\mathrm{e}^{-a u_\varepsilon }f$ has a common $L^2$ bound. Also $g_\varepsilon \geq f$. By theorem 15.3.3, the solutions

\[ w_\varepsilon \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ), \qquad \mathrm{MA}_\theta (w_\varepsilon ) =\mathrm{e}^{a w_\varepsilon }g_\varepsilon \, \mathrm{d}V \]

are uniformly bounded. Put $B_0=\max \{ 1,B\} $ and $z_\varepsilon =w_\varepsilon -a^{-1}\log B_0$. Then

\[ \mathrm{MA}_{\theta +\varepsilon \eta }(z_\varepsilon ) \geq \mathrm{e}^{a z_\varepsilon }c_\varepsilon g_\varepsilon \, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad \mathrm{MA}_{\theta +\varepsilon \eta }(u_\varepsilon ) =\mathrm{e}^{a u_\varepsilon }c_\varepsilon g_\varepsilon \, \mathrm{d}V. \]

The comparison principle for the exponential equation in the background $\theta +\varepsilon \eta $ gives $z_\varepsilon \leq u_\varepsilon \leq 0$, and $z_\varepsilon $ is bounded below uniformly in $\varepsilon $. $\square$

Since $\theta $ may degenerate along $Z$, we do not estimate the trace by local arguments. Instead we apply the maximum principle on all of $X\setminus Z$ to the logarithm of the trace minus a multiple of $u_\varepsilon -\rho $. This function tends to $-\infty $ at $Z$, so its maximum is attained off $Z$, where $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \eta $ provides positivity. An additional logarithmic term in the weight absorbs the torsion term without a gradient bound for $\rho $.

Lemma 15.19.4.C390030D09AE For every compact $K\Subset X\setminus Z$, there is $C_ K>1$, independent of $\varepsilon $, such that

\[ C_ K^{-1}\eta \leq \omega _\varepsilon \leq C_ K\eta \quad \hbox{on }K. \]

Proof. For the differentiated calculation write $U=u_\varepsilon /(2\pi )$ and $P=\rho /(2\pi )$, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon =\mathrm{i}\partial \bar\partial U$. Put

\[ S=\operatorname {tr}_\eta \omega _\varepsilon , \quad R=\operatorname {tr}_{\omega _\varepsilon }\eta , \quad D=\omega _\varepsilon ^ n/\eta ^ n. \]

The previous lemma and the smooth positive density give $0<d_1\leq D\leq d_2$. Choose a fixed $C_0$ so that $v=U-P+C_0>1$, and set

\[ H=\log S-\gamma (v),\qquad \gamma (v)=Av+\log v, \]

where $A$ will be fixed below. For each $\varepsilon $, $S$ is smooth on $X$ and $v$ tends to infinity at $Z$. Thus $H\to -\infty $ there, and $H$ attains its maximum on $X\setminus Z$. We bound this maximum independently of $\varepsilon $.

In local holomorphic coordinates, $h$ and $g$ denote the coefficient matrices of $\eta $ and $\omega _\varepsilon $ with respect to $\mathrm{i}\, \mathrm{d}z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_ k$, and commas denote coordinate derivatives, as in $h_{i\bar j,k}=\partial _ kh_{i\bar j}$ and $h_{i\bar j,\bar k}=\partial _{\bar k}h_{i\bar j}$. At a maximum point choose holomorphic coordinates in which

\[ h_{i\bar j}=\delta _{ij},\qquad h_{i\bar i,k}=0, \qquad g_{i\bar j}=\lambda _ i\delta _{ij}. \]

A linear change first normalizes $h$ and diagonalizes $g$. In these coordinates, the quadratic change $z_ i=w_ i+\frac12\sum _{j,k}a^ i_{jk}w_ jw_ k$, with symmetric coefficients chosen so that $a^ i_{ik}=-h_{i\bar i,k}$, then kills $h_{i\bar i,k}$. Its coefficients are uniformly bounded on a fixed finite cover. Write

\[ \Delta _ g=\sum _ i\lambda _ i^{-1}\partial _ i\partial _{\bar i}, \quad T_{ij\bar k}=g_{j\bar k,i}-g_{i\bar k,j}, \quad E_ i=\sum _ jT_{ij\bar j}. \]

The lowered torsion coefficients $T_{ij\bar k}$ and their first derivatives are uniformly bounded: the potential Hessian cancels, leaving only derivatives of $\theta +\varepsilon \eta $.

Let $\mu _{\mathrm{loc}}$ be the density of $\mu $ with respect to the coordinate volume form. Differentiating $S=h^{k\bar\ell }g_{k\bar\ell }$ and the equation $\log \det g=\log c_\varepsilon +\log \mu _{\mathrm{loc}}$ gives

279BD7D5BB42 15.19.5
\begin{equation} \Delta _ g S\geq Q-CRS-CR, \qquad Q=\sum _{i,j}\frac{|g_{i\bar j,j}|^2}{\lambda _ i\lambda _ j}. \end{equation}

The second derivatives of $g$ contribute $\sum _{i,j,k}|g_{i\bar j,k}|^2/(\lambda _ i\lambda _ j)$, up to $-CR-C$, by commuting the potential derivatives. The first derivatives of $h$ contribute $-2\operatorname {Re}\sum _{i,j,k}\lambda _ i^{-1}h_{j\bar k,\bar i}g_{k\bar j,i}$. Its terms with $j=k$ vanish. Replace $g_{k\bar j,i}$ by $g_{i\bar j,k}+T_{ik\bar j}$ and apply weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. This consumes exactly the terms of the preceding square with $j\ne k$ and costs at most $CRS+CR$. The second derivatives of $h^{-1}$ cost $CRS$. Finally the fixed density term $-C$ is absorbed using $SR\geq n^2$.

Since $\partial _ iS=\sum _ jg_{i\bar j,j}+E_ i$ at the maximum point, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz also gives

\[ |\partial S|_ g^2 \leq SQ+2\operatorname {Re}\langle E,\partial S\rangle _ g. \]

Indeed expand around $\partial S-E$ and discard the resulting term $-|E|_ g^2$. Combining with (15.19.5) yields

\[ \Delta _ g\log S\geq -CR-CR/S -\frac2S\operatorname {Re}\langle E,\partial \log S\rangle _ g. \]

At the maximum, $\partial \log S=\gamma '\partial v$. Moreover (15.19.1) gives $\Delta _ gv\leq n-\delta R$, and $|E|_ g^2\leq CR$. Young's inequality with the positive coefficient $-\gamma ''=v^{-2}$ therefore gives

\[ 0\geq \Delta _ g H \geq (\delta \gamma '-C)R-n\gamma '-CR/S -\frac{CR}{S^2}\frac{\gamma '^2}{-\gamma ''}. \]

Since $\gamma '=A+v^{-1}$, choose $A$ with $\delta A-C\geq 1$. The resulting inequality is

2D587C24F80B 15.19.6
\begin{equation} 0\geq R-C\left(1+\frac RS+\frac{R(1+v^2)}{S^2}\right). \end{equation}

All constants are independent of $\varepsilon $.

If the maxima of $H$ were unbounded, then at a sequence of maximizing points

\[ \log (S/v)=H+Av\longrightarrow +\infty . \]

Thus $S/v\to \infty $ and $S\to \infty $. Inequality (15.19.6) would imply $R\leq 2C$ along this sequence. But

\[ S=D\sum _ i\prod _{j\ne i}\lambda _ j^{-1} \leq nDR^{n-1}\leq nd_2(2C)^{n-1}, \]

a contradiction. Hence $H$ is uniformly bounded. On $K$, both $U$ and $P$ are bounded, so $S\leq C_ K$. The determinant lower bound then gives $\lambda _ i\geq d_1/C_ K^{n-1}$, completing the proof. $\square$

Theorem 15.19.7.E00C93820852 In the preceding setting, $c_\varepsilon $ decreases to a positive number $c_0$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. This number is independent of the perturbing Hermitian form $\eta $. For every sequence $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, a subsequence satisfies

\[ u_{\varepsilon _ j}\longrightarrow u_0 \quad \hbox{in }L^1(X)\cap C^\infty _{\mathrm{loc}}(X\setminus Z), \]

where $u_0\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cap L^\infty (X)$, $\sup _ Xu_0=0$, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_0)=c_0\mu . \]

If $\theta $ is closed, then $c_0=\int _ X\theta ^ n$.

Proof. Monotonicity of the constants. Let $\alpha \leq \beta $ be Hermitian forms, and let $(u_\alpha ,c_\alpha )$ and $(u_\beta ,c_\beta )$ be the normalized smooth solutions of $(\alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\alpha )^ n=c_\alpha \mu $ and $(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\beta )^ n=c_\beta \mu $. At a maximum point of $u_\alpha -u_\beta $,

\[ \alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\alpha \leq \beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\beta , \]

so $c_\alpha \leq c_\beta $. Hence $c_\varepsilon $ decreases as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, and lemma 15.19.3 gives $c_0\geq b>0$. Write $c_\eta (\varepsilon )$ for the constant $c_\varepsilon $ defined with the perturbation $\eta $. For two perturbations with $M^{-1}\eta _1\leq \eta _2\leq M\eta _1$, the same comparison gives

\[ c_{\eta _1}(\varepsilon /M) \leq c_{\eta _2}(\varepsilon ) \leq c_{\eta _1}(M\varepsilon ). \]

Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ shows that $c_0$ does not depend on $\eta $.

Local estimates. The trace bound of lemma 15.19.4 gives uniform ellipticity on compact subsets of $X\setminus Z$. The smooth coefficients of $\theta +\varepsilon \eta $ and all derivatives of $\log \mu _{\mathrm{loc}}$ have common bounds there. Interior complex Evans–Krylov and Schauder estimates, as in the ellipticity argument of proposition 15.18.5, give uniform $C^ k$ bounds on smaller compact subsets for every $k$.

Convergence and the limiting equation. Normalized compactness in the fixed background $\theta +\eta $, followed by a diagonal extraction over an exhaustion of $X\setminus Z$, gives the stated convergence. The uniform bound $-C\leq u_\varepsilon \leq 0$ passes to the canonical limit, and normalized compactness preserves the supremum zero. Current convergence gives $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_0\geq 0$, and smooth convergence identifies the equation on $X\setminus Z$. The bounded non-pluripolar measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_0)$ and the smooth measure $\mu $ both vanish on the analytic set $Z$. This proves the equation on all of $X$. For closed $\theta $, bounded closed-background Stokes gives $\int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u_0)=\int _ X\theta ^ n$, proving the last assertion. $\square$

Since $\theta \geq 0$ is nef, theorem 12.11.1 identifies $c_0$ with the Gauduchon variational volume $\mathcal V_\mu (\theta )$. The proof uses no bound on Monge–Ampère masses: all estimates concern the solutions for the fixed smooth density $\mu $. If the bounded normalized limiting equation has a unique solution, all cluster limits coincide, and the whole family converges in $L^1(X)\cap C^\infty _{\mathrm{loc}}(X\setminus Z)$.

15.19.8 Small resolutions of nodes8C7192C73C68

The pullback of a positive form from a singular space loses positivity along the contracted curves. In the standard small resolution of a node, the logarithm of the norm in the normal bundle of the exceptional curve provides the missing curvature along the curve. Cutting this logarithm off where the pullback is already positive gives a global strict barrier, so the results above apply.

Assume now $n=3$. Let $Y$ be a connected compact normal complex threefold, smooth outside a finite set $\Sigma $ of nodes. A node is locally the hypersurface

\[ \{ x_1x_4-x_2x_3=0\} \subset \mathbb C^4. \]

Let $\pi :X\to Y$ be a proper holomorphic map that is biholomorphic over $Y\setminus \Sigma $. Assume each local resolution is the standard small resolution: a neighborhood of its exceptional curve is identified with a neighborhood of the zero section of

\[ \mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\oplus \mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1), \]

and the contraction, in bundle coordinates $(z,y_1,y_2)$, is

5C6AB7F124F9 15.19.9
\begin{equation} \pi (z,y_1,y_2)=(y_1,zy_1,y_2,zy_2). \end{equation}

Interchanging the two rulings gives the other standard model. Write $E=\pi ^{-1}(\Sigma )$; its components $E_ j$ are disjoint copies of $\mathbb P^1$.

Fix a positive form $\omega _ Y$ on $Y$ in the local embedding sense: in each ambient chart it is induced by a smooth strictly positive real $(1,1)$-form. Set $\theta =\pi ^*\omega _ Y$, and let $\eta $ be an arbitrary Hermitian form on $X$.

Lemma 15.19.10.C6428C798D5F The form $\theta $ is smooth and semipositive, positive on $X\setminus E$. There are $\delta >0$ and $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with analytic poles precisely on $E$, smooth on $X\setminus E$, such that

\[ \sup _ X\rho =0,\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \eta . \]

Proof. Smoothness and positivity off $E$ follow from the pullback and the biholomorphism. In one standard model put

\[ r^2=(1+|z|^2)(|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2),\qquad b=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+|z|^2). \]

The function $r^2$ is the sum of the squared absolute values of the four contracted coordinates. It is therefore globally defined on the bundle; $b$ is the pullback of the Fubini–Study form from its base. In the chart (15.19.9),

AC4599B20814 15.19.11
\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log r^2 =b+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2)\geq b. \end{equation}

This is an inequality of currents across the zero section as well as a smooth inequality off it.

Ambient positivity gives $\theta \geq c\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r^2$ on a sufficiently small tube, for some $c>0$. The smooth form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r^2+b$ is positive definite there. Indeed the contraction is an immersion off the zero section, and on that section $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r^2$ is positive in both fiber directions while $b$ is positive along the base. Compactness of the zero section and a smaller closed tube give a uniform lower bound by a positive multiple of $\eta $. Thus, for every fixed $a>0$,

\[ \theta +a\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log r^2 \geq c\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r^2+ab \geq \delta _ a\eta \]

near the zero section, with $\delta _ a>0$.

Choose disjoint model tubes with nonempty complement and numbers $0<t_1<t_2<1$ in each. Let $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ be a smooth cutoff of $r^2$, equal to one for $r^2\leq t_1$ and zero for $r^2\geq t_2$. Extend $\chi (r^2)\log r^2$ by zero outside its tube. On the compact transition annuli, the pullback $\theta $ is uniformly positive and the second derivatives of this cutoff potential are bounded. A sufficiently small positive rational $a$ therefore makes

\[ \rho =a\sum _ j\chi _ j(r_ j^2)\log r_ j^2 \]

satisfy $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \delta \eta $ there and near every $E_ j$. On the remaining compact set, $\rho =0$ and $\theta $ is positive, so decreasing $\delta $ proves the global inequality. Near $E_ j$ the function is $a\log (|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2)+a\log (1+|z|^2)$, hence has analytic singularities with rational coefficient and pole set exactly $E_ j$. It is smooth elsewhere and nonpositive; its zero extension gives supremum zero. If $E$ is empty, take $\rho =0$ and use the positivity of $\theta $ on compact $X$. $\square$

Corollary 15.19.12.A1536B3C6F39 For the preceding geometric data and every smooth positive probability volume form $\mu $ on $X$, the solutions of (15.19.2) have the bounds and subsequential convergence of theorem 15.19.7, with $Z=E$. Moreover, for each exceptional curve,

\[ \int _{E_ j}\omega _\varepsilon =\varepsilon \int _{E_ j}\eta \longrightarrow 0. \]

Proof. Apply the theorem using lemma 15.19.10. Since $\pi $ contracts $E_ j$, the restriction of $\theta $ to it is zero. Stokes on the compact smooth curve gives $\int _{E_ j}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon =0$, proving the area identity. $\square$

When $K_ X$ is torsion, the density can be chosen so that these metrics are Chern–Ricci flat. Recall that the Chern–Ricci form $\operatorname {Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega )$ of a Hermitian metric $\omega $ is $-\mathrm{i}\partial \bar\partial $ of the logarithm of the density of $\omega ^ n$ with respect to a coordinate volume form. Choose a nowhere vanishing holomorphic section of $K_ X^{\otimes m}$ for some $m\geq 1$. If its local expression is $f(\mathrm{d}z_1\wedge \mathrm{d}z_2\wedge \mathrm{d}z_3)^{\otimes m}$, use $|f|^{2/m}$ as the density relative to the standard coordinate volume form. The coordinate Jacobians cancel, so this defines a smooth positive volume form on $X$; normalize it to total mass one and take it as $\mu $. Its logarithmic density is pluriharmonic. Applying $-\mathrm{i}\partial \bar\partial \log $ to $\omega _\varepsilon ^3=c_\varepsilon \mu $ therefore gives $\operatorname {Ric}^{\mathrm{Ch}}(\omega _\varepsilon )=0$, and the same holds for the smooth limiting metric on $X\setminus E$.

15.19.13 Measures and bounded potentials at a nodeDA6098A35E46

We keep the standard small resolution $\pi :X\to Y$ and a smooth positive probability volume form $\mu $ on $X$. Write $\mu _ Y=\pi _*\mu $, and let $\nu _ Y$ be $\omega _ Y^3$ on the regular locus, extended by zero at the nodes. The pullback of the ambient volume form $\omega _ Y^3$ vanishes along the exceptional curves, and its order of vanishing determines the integrability of the density of $\mu _ Y$ with respect to $\nu _ Y$.

Lemma 15.19.14.9225EE111CC9 The measures $\mu _ Y$ and $\nu _ Y$ are finite and give the nodes zero mass. There is a positive density $F$ with $\mu _ Y=F\nu _ Y$. If $\Sigma $ is nonempty, then for $1\leq p<\infty $,

\[ F\in L^ p(Y,\nu _ Y)\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad p<3. \]

At each node, $F$ is comparable to $r^{-2}$, where $r^2=\sum _{j=1}^4|x_ j|^2$ in the local model $\{ x_1x_4-x_2x_3=0\} $ of the node. There are $c,C,T_0>0$ such that

\[ cT^{-3}\leq \nu _ Y(\{ F>T\} )\leq CT^{-3} \qquad (T\geq T_0). \]

Thus the endpoint is weak $L^3$, with $F\notin L^3$. If $\Sigma $ is empty, $F$ is smooth and bounded.

Proof. The map is biholomorphic off $E$, and both smooth measures $\mu $ and $\theta ^3$ give $E$ zero mass. Hence $\nu _ Y=\pi _*\theta ^3$ is finite, $\mu _ Y$ has no node atom, and on the regular locus

\[ F\circ \pi =\mu /\theta ^3. \]

In the chart (15.19.9), put $h=1+|z|^2$ and $s=|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2$, so $r^2=hs$. The fiber block of the complex Hessian of $r^2$ is $h$ times the identity, and its Schur complement is $s/h$. Consequently

DE65B074D310 15.19.15
\begin{equation} \det \bigl ((r^2)_{j\bar k}\bigr )=hs=r^2, \qquad \frac{(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r^2)^3}{3!} =\frac{r^2}{\pi ^3}\, \mathrm{d}\lambda _6, \end{equation}

where $\lambda _6$ is Euclidean volume in $(z,y_1,y_2)$. Ambient positivity bounds $\theta $ above and below by positive multiples of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r^2$ on a smaller tube. The smooth density of $\mu $ is bounded above and below there. On compact subcharts covering the base $\mathbb P^1$, it follows that

\[ \theta ^3\asymp r^2\, \mathrm{d}\lambda _6, \qquad F\circ \pi \asymp r^{-2}. \]

The other bundle chart gives the same comparison, since $r$ is the norm of the contracted coordinates.

On each bounded base subchart, $r$ is comparable to the Euclidean radius $R$ in the two complex fiber variables. Up to fixed positive constants, the contribution to $\int F^ p\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ Y$ is therefore

\[ \int _0^{R_0}R^{2-2p}R^3\, \mathrm{d}R =\int _0^{R_0}R^{5-2p}\, \mathrm{d}R. \]

Two compact base subcharts give the upper estimate; a fixed open base subchart gives the lower estimate. The integral is finite precisely when $p<3$, and diverges logarithmically at $p=3$. Away from the finitely many nodes the density is smooth. The same comparisons give $\nu _ Y(\{ r<t\} )\asymp t^6$ near a node. The two bounds for $F$ sandwich $\{ F>T\} $ between such tubes of radii comparable to $T^{-1/2}$, proving the endpoint estimate. $\square$

The descent theorem theorem 9.11.5 applies to the normal space $Y$ and its resolution $\pi $, also when the ambient background is not closed. 15.19.18 Distances across finitely many contracting regionsF6016CBBF3C5

On the small resolutions above, the potential limit for a closed background gives smooth convergence of the metrics away from the exceptional curves. Global distances, however, are also affected by paths through neighborhoods of these curves, so their convergence requires control of the diameters of such neighborhoods. The criterion of proposition 15.19.22 separates the two controls: uniform convergence on compact subsets of the regular part, and small diameters of neighborhoods of the contracted sets, in the limiting metric and uniformly in the approximating metrics. We first recall the Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

Definition 15.19.19.0EB9DF723176 For nonempty subsets $E,F$ of a metric space $(W,d_ W)$, their Hausdorff distance is

\[ d_{\mathrm H}^ W(E,F)=\max \left\{ \sup _{x\in E}\inf _{y\in F}d_ W(x,y), \sup _{y\in F}\inf _{x\in E}d_ W(x,y)\right\} . \]

For nonempty bounded metric spaces $E,F$, put

\[ d_{\mathrm{GH}}(E,F) =\inf _{W,\iota ,\jmath }d_{\mathrm H}^ W(\iota (E),\jmath (F)), \]

where $\iota ,\jmath $ are isometric embeddings into a common metric space $W$. A correspondence is a subset $R\subset E\times F$ whose two coordinate projections are surjective. Its distortion is

\[ \operatorname {dis}R =\sup _{(x,y),(x',y')\in R} |d_ E(x,x')-d_ F(y,y')|. \]

Lemma 15.19.20.022849340F28 For any correspondence $R$ between nonempty bounded metric spaces,

\[ \frac12|\operatorname {diam}E-\operatorname {diam}F| \leq d_{\mathrm{GH}}(E,F) \leq \frac12\operatorname {dis}R. \]

Proof. Choose $\varepsilon >0$ with $2\varepsilon \geq \operatorname {dis}R$. Give the disjoint union $E\sqcup F$ its original distances within each space, and use the symmetric cross distance

\[ D(x,y)=\inf _{(x_0,y_0)\in R} \bigl (d_ E(x,x_0)+\varepsilon +d_ F(y_0,y)\bigr ), \qquad x\in E,\ y\in F. \]

It is finite and at least $\varepsilon $. The triangle inequalities involving one cross distance follow from the triangle inequality within the corresponding space and the infimum. For two cross distances, take $(x_0,y_0),(x_1,y_1)\in R$. The distortion bound gives

\[ \begin{split} d_ E(x,x') & \leq d_ E(x,x_0)+d_ F(y_0,y_1) +2\varepsilon +d_ E(x_1,x')\\ & \leq d_ E(x,x_0)+d_ F(y_0,y)+\varepsilon +\varepsilon +d_ F(y,y_1)+d_ E(x_1,x'). \end{split} \]

Taking the two infima gives $d_ E(x,x')\leq D(x,y)+D(y,x')$. The same argument with $E,F$ exchanged treats the other case. Hence $D$ is a metric extending both metrics. Each point has a correspondent at cross distance $\varepsilon $, so the Hausdorff distance between $E$ and $F$ in $(E\sqcup F,D)$ is at most $\varepsilon $. Letting $\varepsilon $ decrease to half the distortion proves the upper bound, including zero distortion.

For the lower bound, suppose two isometric images have Hausdorff distance $h$. For every $\delta >0$, two points in the first image have points in the second within $h+\delta $. Their distance is at most $\operatorname {diam}F+2h+2\delta $. Taking the supremum and then $\delta \downarrow 0$ gives $\operatorname {diam}E\leq \operatorname {diam}F+2h$. Exchanging the images and taking the infimum over embeddings proves the lower bound. $\square$

We now work in a purely metric setting, which contains the nodal one. Let $Y$ be a compact metrizable space and let $S=\{ s_1,\ldots ,s_ N\} \subset Y$ be finite. Assume $Y^\circ =Y\setminus S$ is a dense connected smooth manifold. Let $g_0$ be a smooth Riemannian metric on $Y^\circ $, and denote its intrinsic length distance by $d_0$.

Lemma 15.19.21.E3C9C96E314E Suppose every $s_ a$ has a neighborhood basis of open sets $V_{a,r}$ such that

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\operatorname {diam}_{d_0}(V_{a,r}\setminus S)=0. \]

Then the metric completion $(\widehat Y,\widehat d_0)$ of $(Y^\circ ,d_0)$ is compact, and the identity on $Y^\circ $ extends to a homeomorphism $\iota :Y\to \widehat Y$.

Proof. The neighborhoods can be chosen nested, with closures avoiding the other points of $S$. Every sequence of regular points converging to $s_ a$ is $d_0$-Cauchy: its tail lies in each prescribed neighborhood, whose punctured diameter tends to zero. Interleaving two such sequences shows that they define the same completion point, denoted by $\widehat s_ a$. Density of $Y^\circ $ guarantees the existence of these sequences.

For each prescribed positive diameter, remove sufficiently small neighborhoods of all the points of $S$. Their complement is a compact subset of $Y^\circ $ and hence is totally bounded for $d_0$. The removed punctured neighborhoods each have the prescribed small diameter. Thus $Y^\circ $ is totally bounded, and its completion is compact.

The points $\widehat s_ a$ are distinct from one another and from the regular points. To see this, fix $s_ a$ and a different point $y\in Y$. Choose an open neighborhood $V$ of $s_ a$ whose closure avoids $y$ and $S\setminus \{ s_ a\} $, and choose $W\Subset V$ containing $s_ a$. There is a smooth function $\chi $ on $Y^\circ $, equal to one on $W\setminus S$ and zero outside $V$, whose derivative has compact support in $Y^\circ $. Indeed the transition takes place in the compact regular set $\overline V\setminus W$, where smooth cutoffs exist. Therefore $|\mathrm{d}\chi |_{g_0}\leq C$ for some finite $C$. Integration along curves gives

\[ |\chi (p)-\chi (q)|\leq C d_0(p,q),\qquad p,q\in Y^\circ . \]

The Lipschitz extension of $\chi $ to the completion has value one at $\widehat s_ a$ and zero at the regular point $y$, or at $\widehat s_ b$ if $y=s_ b$. This proves the assertion.

Define $\iota $ by the identity on the regular locus and by $\iota (s_ a)=\widehat s_ a$. The intrinsic distance induces the smooth topology at each regular point. At $s_ a$, adjoining $\widehat s_ a$ to a punctured neighborhood does not increase its diameter, so the diameter hypothesis proves continuity of $\iota $. Every completion point is represented by a Cauchy sequence in $Y^\circ $. Compact metrizability of $Y$ gives a subsequence converging topologically either to a regular point or to some $s_ a$; the preceding arguments give its metric limit. Hence $\iota $ is surjective as well as injective. A continuous bijection from compact $Y$ to Hausdorff $\widehat Y$ is a homeomorphism. $\square$

Let $X$ be a compact connected smooth manifold and let $\pi :X\to Y$ be a continuous surjection, with $E=\pi ^{-1}(S)$ and with $\pi :X\setminus E\to Y^\circ $ a diffeomorphism. Assume that the identity of $Y^\circ $ extends to a homeomorphism $\iota :Y\to \widehat Y$, for instance under the hypothesis of lemma 15.19.21, and write $G=\iota \circ \pi $. For a smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on $X$ and an open subset $U$, let $\operatorname {diam}^{\mathrm{int}}(U,g)$ denote the intrinsic diameter of $U$, computed with the lengths of piecewise smooth curves contained in $U$.

Proposition 15.19.22.65049DB7855E Let $g_ j$ be smooth Riemannian metrics on $X$ such that $g_ j\to \pi ^*g_0$ uniformly on every compact subset of $X\setminus E$. Suppose each $s_ a$ has a nested neighborhood basis $V_{a,r}$, with $U_{a,r}=\pi ^{-1}(V_{a,r})$, satisfying

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\operatorname {diam}_{\widehat d_0}(\iota (V_{a,r}))=0, \qquad \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\limsup _{j\to \infty } \operatorname {diam}^{\mathrm{int}}(U_{a,r},g_ j)=0. \]

\[ \lim _{j\to \infty }\sup _{x,x'\in X} |d_ j(x,x')-\widehat d_0(G(x),G(x'))|=0. \]

\[ \operatorname {diam}_{\widehat d_0}(\iota (V_ a))<\tau , \qquad \operatorname {diam}^{\mathrm{int}}(U_ a,g_ j)<\tau \]

for all sufficiently large $j$, where $U_ a=\pi ^{-1}(V_ a)$. Pick $q_ a\in V_ a\setminus S$, and then choose open neighborhoods $W_ a$ of $s_ a$ with $\overline W_ a\subset V_ a$ and $q_ a\notin \overline W_ a$. The regular cores

\[ K_ Y=Y\setminus \bigcup _ a W_ a, \qquad K_ X=\pi ^{-1}(K_ Y) \]

are compact and identified by $\pi $. Uniform convergence and positivity of $g_0$ on this core give numbers $A_ j\geq 1$, with $A_ j\to 1$, such that lengths of curves contained in the cores compare in both directions by the factor $A_ j$.

At its first visit to any such set, delete the portion from that visit through its last visit to the same set. Resume with the remaining tail. That set is never visited again; thus there are at most $N$ deleted portions. Each retained segment lies in $K_ Y$, and each deleted portion has endpoints in a single $\overline W_ a\subset V_ a$. The lifts of those endpoints can be joined in $U_ a$ with $g_ j$-length less than $\tau $, up to an arbitrarily small error. Applying this procedure to almost minimizing paths, comparing retained lengths, and letting the errors tend to zero gives, for $x,x'\in K_ X$,

\[ d_ j(x,x')\leq A_ j\widehat d_0(G(x),G(x'))+N\tau . \]

Conversely start with an almost minimizing $g_ j$-path in $X$ and apply the same deletion procedure to the closed sets $\pi ^{-1}(\overline W_ a)$. Project its retained segments, which avoid $E$, to $K_ Y$. The two images of each deleted portion have completion distance less than $\tau $. The triangle inequality in $\widehat Y$ therefore gives

\[ \widehat d_0(G(x),G(x'))\leq A_ j d_ j(x,x')+N\tau , \qquad x,x'\in K_ X. \]

For any $x\notin K_ X$ with $\pi (x)\in W_ a$, replace $x$ by $p=\pi ^{-1}(q_ a)\in U_ a\cap K_ X$. Then $d_ j(x,p)<\tau $ and $\widehat d_0(G(x),G(p))<\tau $. Leave points of $K_ X$ fixed. Replacing both endpoints extends the two inequalities to all $x,x'\in X$, with the same error

\[ C_ j=(N+2+2A_ j)\tau . \]

If $D=\operatorname {diam}(\widehat Y,\widehat d_0)$, the first inequality gives $\operatorname {diam}(X,d_ j)\leq A_ jD+C_ j$. The positive and negative distance errors are consequently bounded by

\[ (A_ j-1)D+C_ j, \qquad (A_ j-1)(A_ jD+C_ j)+C_ j, \]

respectively. Their upper limits are at most $(N+4)\tau $. Letting $\tau \downarrow 0$ proves uniform convergence.

The graph of $G$ is a correspondence between $X$ and $\widehat Y$, and its distortion $b_ j$ for the distances $d_ j$ and $\widehat d_0$ is the supremum in the statement. By lemma 15.19.20,

\[ d_{\mathrm{GH}}((X,d_ j),(\widehat Y,\widehat d_0))\leq b_ j/2\longrightarrow 0. \]

The approximating metrics give each exceptional curve an area tending to zero, by corollary 15.19.12. Along the curve, small area forces small diameter as long as the metrics are bounded above; in the normal directions, an integrable bound for the speed of radial rays has the same effect.

Let $C$ be a connected compact Riemann surface. Fix a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ of area one on $C$, and use the associated Riemannian metrics for lengths and areas.

Lemma 15.19.24.240B8EBF50C3 There is $K_ h>0$ such that every smooth Hermitian metric $g\leq M h$, with $M>0$ and $A=\operatorname {Area}_ g(C)$, satisfies

\[ \operatorname {diam}(C,g) \leq K_ h\sqrt{A\bigl (1+\log (M/A)\bigr )}. \]

Here $0<A\leq M$. In particular, for a fixed upper bound $M$, area tending to zero implies diameter tending to zero.

Proof. Replace $g$ by $g/M$. Its area is $a=A/M\leq 1$, and it is bounded above by $h$. We prove the estimate for this normalized metric.

Choose a finite cover by holomorphic coordinate disks $U_ i$ represented by $\{ |z|<1\} $, with each coordinate defined on the larger disk $\{ |z|<2\} $. Write $g=\rho _ i|\mathrm{d}z|^2$ there. There is a common $L>0$ such that $0<\rho _ i\leq L$ on all the closed unit disks, and

\[ \int _{U_ i}\rho _ i\, \mathrm{d}\lambda _2\leq a, \]

where $\lambda _2$ is Euclidean area. For $p,z\in U_ i$, let $\ell _ p(z)$ be the $g$-length of their straight coordinate segment, which stays in $U_ i$. Polar coordinates about $p$ and Fubini give

\[ \int _{U_ i}\ell _ p(z)\, \mathrm{d}\lambda _2(z) \leq 2\int _{U_ i}\frac{\sqrt{\rho _ i(w)}}{|w-p|} \, \mathrm{d}\lambda _2(w). \]

For $0<a\leq 1/4$, put $t=\sqrt a$. The integral on the right, without its factor two, is bounded by

\[ \begin{split} \sqrt L\int _{\{ |w-p|<t\} }\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda _2(w)}{|w-p|} & +\left(\int _{U_ i}\rho _ i\, \mathrm{d}\lambda _2\right)^{1/2} \left(\int _{\{ t<|w-p|<2\} }\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda _2(w)}{|w-p|^2}\right)^{1/2}\\ & \leq 2\pi \sqrt L\, t+\sqrt{2\pi a\log (2/t)} \leq K\sqrt{a\bigl (1+\log (1/a)\bigr )}. \end{split} \]

Thus for any $p,q\in U_ i$, averaging $\ell _ p(z)+\ell _ q(z)$ over $z\in U_ i$ gives a two-segment path of length at most $K\sqrt{a(1+\log (1/a))}$. The constant is common to the finite cover.

The overlap graph of this cover is connected because $C$ is connected. Choose points in consecutive overlaps along a simple path in this finite graph. The local estimates join any two points of $C$ through at most the number of covering disks, proving the global bound for $a\leq 1/4$. For $1/4\leq a\leq 1$, the comparison $g\leq h$ gives $\operatorname {diam}(C,g)\leq \operatorname {diam}(C,h)$, and increasing $K_ h$ gives the same estimate. Restoring the factor $M$ scales lengths by $\sqrt M$ and proves the assertion. $\square$

Choose $0<\varepsilon _ j<1$ tending to zero, with $2\varepsilon _ j^2<\delta _0$, and a smooth function $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ on $[0,\infty )$, equal to zero on $[0,1]$ and to one on $[4,\infty )$. Set

\[ \begin{aligned} f_ j(r)& =\varepsilon _ j^2+(1-\varepsilon _ j^2) \chi (r^2/\varepsilon _ j^4),\\ g_ j& =f_ jq^*h+f_ j^{-1}k, \end{aligned} \]

on $U_{\delta _0}$, and set $g_ j=g_*$ elsewhere. The function $f_ j$ is smooth at $E$, is at least $\varepsilon _ j^2$, and equals one for $r\geq 2\varepsilon _ j^2$. Hence the metrics glue smoothly and are positive. They equal $g_*$ eventually on every compact subset of $X\setminus E$.

Let $\beta ,\kappa $ be the Hermitian forms of $q^*h,k$. Their complex rank one gives $\beta ^2=\kappa ^2=0$. On the tube,

\[ (f_ j\beta +f_ j^{-1}\kappa )^2 =2\beta \wedge \kappa =(\beta +\kappa )^2. \]

Outside it the metrics coincide. This proves the global volume identity. On $E$, the restriction is $g_ j|_ E=\varepsilon _ j^2h$, so its area is $\varepsilon _ j^2$.

For any unit fiber vector $a$, a radial ray $\gamma (s)=sa$ has $k$-squared speed one and zero base component. Thus its $g_ j$-squared speed is $f_ j(s)^{-1}$. If $s<2\varepsilon _ j^2$, this is at most $\varepsilon _ j^{-2}\leq 2/s$; otherwise it is one, still at most $2/s$ for $s<\delta _0$. This is the uniform integrable radial bound with exponent $1/2$.

A radial segment of radius $r<\delta $ has length at most $r+2\varepsilon _ j$: its portion below $2\varepsilon _ j^2$ has speed at most $\varepsilon _ j^{-1}$ and length at most $2\varepsilon _ j$, and its remaining speed is one. Connect two points by their radial segments and an almost minimizing path on $E$. All three segments stay inside $U_\delta $, giving

\[ \operatorname {diam}^{\mathrm{int}}(U_\delta ,g_ j) \leq 2\delta +\varepsilon _ j\bigl (4+\operatorname {diam}(\mathbb P^1,h)\bigr ). \]

Taking $j\to \infty $ before $\delta \downarrow 0$ proves the asserted shrinking condition. The affine balls

\[ V_\delta =\{ [1:z_1:z_2]:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2<\delta ^2\} \]

have precisely these tubes as their preimages.

Let $d_*$ be the global distance of the fixed smooth metric $g_*$. Choose distinct points $a,b\in E$, and put $c=d_*(a,b)>0$. Choose points $a_ r,b_ r$ of fiber norm $r>0$ over these base points. They tend to $a,b$ as $r\downarrow 0$. Every path in $Y\setminus \{ p\} $ lifts to a path in $X\setminus E$ with the same $g_0$-length as its $g_*$-length. Therefore

\[ d_0(\pi (a_ r),\pi (b_ r))\geq d_*(a_ r,b_ r)\longrightarrow c. \]

For any fixed $\delta >0$, these two images lie in $V_\delta $ when $r<\delta $. Taking the supremum and then $r\downarrow 0$ gives its punctured diameter at least $c$. $\square$
