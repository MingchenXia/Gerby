10.8 Extension from a compact submanifold9264FE392E91

9264FE392E91

In theorem 10.7.9 the error $\bar\partial (\chi f)$ of the cutoff is supported away from the interpolation point. To extend a section from a submanifold $Y$ of positive dimension, we must glue local extensions, and the gluing error then meets $Y$ itself. We control it in two steps. First, local extensions with a logarithmic gain (lemma 10.8.6) make the gluing error integrable against the critical weight $R_ Y^{-q}$, where $q$ is the codimension of $Y$ and $R_ Y$ is a smooth function vanishing to second order exactly along $Y$. Second, for weights with gentle analytic singularities, a computation on a resolution identifies the limit of the cutoff error with the norm of the prescribed section on $Y$ (lemma 10.8.15). This yields an estimate whose constant depends only on the geometry, and decreasing approximation then gives the result for arbitrary singular metrics (theorem 10.8.18). In subsection 10.8.23 a related gluing construction, by maxima instead of sums, extends potentials of Kähler currents from $Y$ to $X$.

We solve the $\bar\partial $-equations of this section with the following twisted form of lemma 10.7.6. The auxiliary function $a$ contributes the term $-\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial a$ to the curvature, at the price of the weight $1/(a+b)$ in the estimate for the solution.

Lemma 10.8.1.D973E523027E Let $M$ be a complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$ with a Hermitian form $\omega $ and the volume form $\mathrm dV=\omega ^ n/n!$, carrying a Kähler form $\sigma \geq \omega /2$ and functions $\chi _ R$ with the properties stated in lemma 10.7.5. Let $F\to M$ be a holomorphic line bundle with a smooth quadratic metric $h$, and let $a,b$ be smooth functions on $M$ that are bounded above and below by positive constants. Suppose that the real $(1,1)$-form

\[ H=a\, 2\pi c_1(F,h)-\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial a -\frac{\mathrm i}{b}\partial a\wedge \bar\partial a \]

is positive definite, and put $B_\omega =[H,\Lambda _\omega ]$ on $(n,1)$-forms. Let $g$ be an $F$-valued $(n,1)$-form with $\bar\partial g=0$ in the sense of distributions, $g\in L^2_{(n,1)}(\omega ,h)$, and

\[ M_ g=\int _ M\langle B_\omega ^{-1}g,g\rangle _{\omega ,h}\, \mathrm dV<\infty . \]

Then there is an $F$-valued $(n,0)$-form $u$ with $\bar\partial u=g$ and

C5A4CDD14D8E 10.8.2
\begin{equation} \int _ M\frac{|u|_{\omega ,h}^2}{a+b}\, \mathrm dV\leq M_ g. \end{equation}

Proof. The Bochner–Kodaira identity (10.7.3) of lemma 10.7.2 holds for every smooth metric on $F$; we apply it to the metric $ah$. Let $\delta _ h=D''{}^*$ be the formal adjoint for the metric $h$ and the Kähler form $\sigma $, and $C_ a=(\bar\partial a\wedge )^*$ the contraction with the dual of $\bar\partial a$. In a frame, the weight of $ah$ is $p-\log a$, so

\[ \delta _{ah}=\delta _ h-a^{-1}C_ a,\qquad a\, 2\pi c_1(F,ah)=a\, 2\pi c_1(F,h) -\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial a +\frac{\mathrm i}{a}\partial a\wedge \bar\partial a. \]

On $(n,1)$-forms, the commutator with the last term is $[\frac{\mathrm i}{a}\partial a\wedge \bar\partial a,\Lambda _\sigma ]=a^{-1}C_ a^*C_ a$. Indeed, where $\partial a\neq 0$, choose a $\sigma $-unitary coframe with $\partial a=|\partial a|\theta _1$, so that $\bar\partial a=|\partial a|\bar\theta _1$, and put $e=\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _ n$. By lemma 10.7.1(1), the left side multiplies the coefficient $v_1$ of $e\wedge \bar\theta _1$ by $|\partial a|^2/a$ and annihilates the other coefficients; so does the right side, because $\bar\partial a\wedge fe=(-1)^ n|\partial a|f\, e\wedge \bar\theta _1$, whence $C_ av=(-1)^ n|\partial a|v_1e$. Where $\partial a=0$, both sides vanish. Writing (10.7.3) for $ah$ in terms of $h$, expanding $a|\delta _ hv-a^{-1}C_ av|^2$, cancelling the term $a^{-1}|C_ av|^2$ that appears on both sides, and dropping the nonnegative term $\| D'{}^*v\| ^2$, we obtain for compactly supported smooth $v$, with all integrals taken with respect to $h$, $\sigma $ and $\mathrm dV_\sigma $,

\[ \int _ M a\bigl (|\delta _ hv|^2+|D''v|^2\bigr ) \geq \int _ M\langle [a\, 2\pi c_1(F,h) -\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial a,\Lambda _\sigma ]v,v\rangle +2\operatorname{Re}\int _ M\langle \delta _ hv,C_ av\rangle . \]

The inequality $2\operatorname{Re}\langle A,B\rangle \geq -b|A|^2-b^{-1}|B|^2$ and the identity $b^{-1}|C_ av|^2=\langle [\frac{\mathrm i}{b}\partial a\wedge \bar\partial a,\Lambda _\sigma ]v,v\rangle $ consequently give

82B8AA567C14 10.8.3
\begin{equation} \int _ M\langle [H,\Lambda _\sigma ]v,v\rangle \leq \int _ M(a+b)|\delta _ hv|^2+a|D''v|^2. \end{equation}

Consider the Hilbert spaces of $(n,0)$-, $(n,1)$- and $(n,2)$-forms with the respective weights $h/(a+b)$, $h$ and $ah$, and the operators $T=D''$ and $S=D''$ with maximal domains between them. Testing against compactly supported forms shows that $T^*=(a+b)\delta _ h$, so $\| T^*v\| ^2$ is the first term on the right of (10.8.3), and $\| Sv\| ^2$ is the second. Compactly supported smooth forms are dense in $\operatorname{Dom}T^*\cap \operatorname{Dom}S$ for the graph norm, by lemma 10.7.4, applied with the metrics $h/(a+b)$, $h$ and $ah$, whose ratios $a+b$ and $a$ are bounded.

The form $g$ lies in $\ker S$, and in the second space by lemma 10.7.1(3), because $\sigma \geq \omega /2$. The operator $[H,\Lambda _\sigma ]$ is positive definite on $(n,1)$-forms by lemma 10.7.1(1). As in the proof of lemma 10.7.6, the pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for this operator, (10.8.3) and the density of compactly supported smooth forms give the hypothesis of theorem 6.2.4, which yields a solution of $\bar\partial u=g$ whose squared norm in the first space is at most $\int \langle [H,\Lambda _\sigma ]^{-1}g,g\rangle \, \mathrm dV_\sigma $. This integral, and the squared norm of $(n,0)$-forms times the volume form, do not depend on the Hermitian form used, by lemma 10.7.1(3); hence the bound is $M_ g$, and we obtain (10.8.2). $\square$

Lemma 10.8.6.833820F8A53D Let $m\geq 0$ and $q\geq 1$ be integers, and let $D=B'\times B''\Subset \mathbb C^{m}\times \mathbb C^ q$ be a product of balls centered at the origin, of radii $r'$ and $r''$, with $r''<\mathrm e^{-2}$; when $m=0$, $B'=\{ 0\} $. We write points of $D$ as $(z',w)$. Let $p$ be plurisubharmonic on a neighborhood of $\overline D$, and let $f$ be holomorphic near $\overline{B'}$ with $\int _{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p(z',0)}\, \mathrm dV_{B'}<\infty $, where $|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p(z',0)}$ is taken to be $0$ where $f=0$. Then there is a holomorphic function $F$ on $D$ with $F(z',0)=f(z')$ and

27D0BD8514EB 10.8.7
\begin{equation} \int _ D\frac{|F|^2\mathrm e^{-p}}{|w|^{2q}(-\log |w|^2)^2}\, \mathrm dV \leq C\int _{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p(z',0)}\, \mathrm dV_{B'}, \end{equation}

where both volumes are Euclidean and $C$ depends only on $m$, $q$, $r'$ and $r''$. When $m=0$, the right side is $C|f|^2\mathrm e^{-p(0)}$.

Proof. If $f=0$, take $F=0$. Put $n=m+q$, and let $\beta $ be the Euclidean Kähler form on $\mathbb C^ n$. We first treat smooth weights: we cut off the trivial extension of $f$ near $\{ w=0\} $, correct it with lemma 10.8.1 on $D\setminus \{ w=0\} $, and let the width $\varepsilon $ of the cutoff tend to zero. So assume that $p$ is smooth near $\overline D$, and set $R=|w|^2$, $\psi =\log R$ and $\psi _\varepsilon =\log (\varepsilon +R)$ for small $\varepsilon >0$. The function $P=p+\pi |z|^2$, where $z=(z',w)$, satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P\geq \beta $, and replacing $p$ by $P$ changes both sides of (10.8.7) by factors bounded in terms of $r'$ and $r''$ only; we prove the estimate for $P$.

The twisted form. On $D\setminus \{ w=0\} $ consider the trivial line bundle with the metric $\mathrm e^{-P-q\psi }$, and let $\mu $, $a_\varepsilon $ and $b_\varepsilon $ be the functions of lemma 10.8.4, for $0<\varepsilon <\mathrm e^{-4}$; that lemma applies because $0<R<\mathrm e^{-4}$ on $D\setminus \{ w=0\} $, since $r''<\mathrm e^{-2}$. By its part (1), $a_\varepsilon \geq 1$ and $-\mu '(\psi _\varepsilon )\geq 1$, and for fixed $\varepsilon $ the functions $a_\varepsilon $ and $b_\varepsilon $ are bounded above and below by positive constants. By its part (2), applied with $\Theta =\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial (P+q\psi )$, the curvature form of this metric times $2\pi $, the gradient terms cancel in the form $H$ of lemma 10.8.1, and

\[ H_\varepsilon =a_\varepsilon \bigl (\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial P +q\, \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \psi \bigr ) -\mu '(\psi _\varepsilon )\, \mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon . \]

Both logarithmic Hessians $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \psi $ and $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon $ are semipositive, so (10.8.5) gives $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon \geq (\varepsilon /R)\, \mathrm i\partial \psi _\varepsilon \wedge \bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon $. Since also $\mathrm i\partial \bar\partial P=2\pi \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P\geq 2\pi \beta $, the bounds $a_\varepsilon \geq 1$ and $-\mu '(\psi _\varepsilon )\geq 1$ give $H_\varepsilon \geq 2\pi \beta +(\varepsilon /R)\, \mathrm i\partial \psi _\varepsilon \wedge \bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon $.

Complete cutoffs. The manifold $D\setminus \{ w=0\} $ carries a Kähler form and cutoffs as required in lemma 10.8.1. Indeed, put $\tau _0=-\log (r'^2-|z'|^2)-\log (r''^2-|w|^2)$, omitting the first term if $m=0$, and $\tau _1=-\log (-\psi )$. Each of the three functions $\tau $ formed by the two terms of $\tau _0$ and by $\tau _1$ is of the form $\tau =-\log g$ with $g>0$ smooth and $-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\geq 0$, namely $g=r'^2-|z'|^2$, $g=r''^2-|w|^2$ and $g=-\psi $; hence

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau =-\frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g}{g}+\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi g^2}\partial g\wedge \bar\partial g\geq \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \tau \wedge \bar\partial \tau \geq 0. \]

Thus $\tau _0$ and $\tau _1$ are plurisubharmonic, and $\sigma =\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau _1\geq \beta $ is a Kähler form. Moreover $\sigma \geq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\tau \geq \frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \tau \wedge \bar\partial \tau $ for each of the three functions; the eigenvalue of the rank-one form $\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\partial \tau \wedge \bar\partial \tau $ with respect to $\sigma $ is $|\partial \tau |_\sigma ^2/(2\pi )$, so $|\partial \tau |_\sigma ^2\leq 2\pi $, and these functions have bounded gradients for $\sigma $. The function $\tau _0-\tau _1$ tends to $+\infty $ at the boundary of $D$ and along $\{ w=0\} $, and its sublevel sets are compact in $D\setminus \{ w=0\} $. If $\lambda \in C^\infty (\mathbb R)$ equals one on $(-\infty ,1]$ and zero on $[2,\infty )$, the functions $\chi _ j=\lambda ((\tau _0-\tau _1)/j)$ are compactly supported, tend to one, and have gradients $O(1/j)$. The canonical bundle of $\mathbb C^ n$ is trivialized by $\mathrm dz_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dz_ n$, whose norm for $\beta $ is constant, so we identify functions with $(n,0)$-forms, as in the proof of theorem 10.7.9; this multiplies all the pointwise norms below by the same constant factor.

The correction. Let $G(z',w)=f(z')$. Choose $\rho \in C^\infty (\mathbb R)$ with $0\leq \rho \leq 1$, equal to one on $(-\infty ,1/2]$ and to zero on $[1,\infty )$, and put $G_\varepsilon =\rho (R/\varepsilon )G$. Since $\bar\partial R/\varepsilon =(1+R/\varepsilon )\bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon $,

\[ g_\varepsilon =\bar\partial G_\varepsilon =\rho '(R/\varepsilon )(1+R/\varepsilon ) \bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon \, G, \]

which is supported in $\{ \varepsilon /2\leq R\leq \varepsilon \} $. For an $(n,1)$-form $v$, the rank-one part of the lower bound for $H_\varepsilon $ and lemma 10.7.1(1),(2), applied with $\alpha =\partial \psi _\varepsilon $, give

\[ |\langle \bar\partial \psi _\varepsilon \wedge G,v\rangle |^2 \leq \frac R\varepsilon \, |G|^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}\langle [H_\varepsilon ,\Lambda _\beta ]v,v\rangle , \]

where $|G|$ is the modulus of the function $G$ and the inner products are those of $\beta $ and the metric $\mathrm e^{-P-q\psi }=\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}$. Hence, on the support of $g_\varepsilon $, where $R/\varepsilon \leq 1$, we have $\langle [H_\varepsilon ,\Lambda _\beta ]^{-1}g_\varepsilon ,g_\varepsilon \rangle \leq C|G|^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}$, and the integral $M_{g_\varepsilon }$ of lemma 10.8.1 is at most

\[ C\int _{\{ \varepsilon /2\leq R\leq \varepsilon \} } |G|^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}\, \mathrm dV. \]

In polar coordinates in $w$, the integral of $R^{-q}$ over the shell $\varepsilon /2\leq |w|^2\leq \varepsilon $ is a fixed multiple of $\int _{\sqrt{\varepsilon /2}}^{\sqrt\varepsilon }\mathrm dt/t=\frac12\log 2$. Since $P$ is uniformly continuous on $\overline D$ and $G$ does not depend on $w$, the last integral tends to a fixed multiple of $\int _{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-P(z',0)}\, \mathrm dV_{B'}$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$. Lemma 10.8.1 gives $u_\varepsilon $ on $D\setminus \{ w=0\} $ with $\bar\partial u_\varepsilon =g_\varepsilon $ and

\[ \int _{D}\frac{|u_\varepsilon |^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}}{a_\varepsilon +b_\varepsilon }\, \mathrm dV\leq C\int _{\{ \varepsilon /2\leq R\leq \varepsilon \} } |G|^2\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}\, \mathrm dV. \]

For fixed $\varepsilon $ the denominator is bounded and $\mathrm e^{-P}R^{-q}$ is bounded below, so $u_\varepsilon $ is square integrable, and $F_\varepsilon =G_\varepsilon -u_\varepsilon $, which is holomorphic off $\{ w=0\} $, extends holomorphically to $D$ by lemma 10.7.8. The difference $u_\varepsilon =G_\varepsilon -F_\varepsilon $ is then smooth near $\{ w=0\} $, and it vanishes there: a nonzero value at a point $(z'_0,0)$ would make $\int |u_\varepsilon |^2|w|^{-2q}$ diverge near that point, since $\int |w|^{-2q}\, \mathrm dV(w)$ diverges logarithmically at $w=0$ in $\mathbb C^ q$. Hence $F_\varepsilon (z',0)=G_\varepsilon (z',0)=f(z')$.

The limit $\varepsilon \to 0$. By lemma 10.8.4(1), $a_\varepsilon +b_\varepsilon \leq 5(-\log (\varepsilon +R))^2$, so the correction estimate bounds $u_\varepsilon $ in the weaker weight $(\varepsilon +R)^{-q}(-\log (\varepsilon +R))^{-2}\mathrm e^{-P}$. In this weight the squared norm of $G_\varepsilon $ is at most $C_ P(-\log \varepsilon )^{-2}$, because its support has normal volume $O(\varepsilon ^ q)$ and $(\varepsilon +R)^{-q}\leq \varepsilon ^{-q}$ there; the constant $C_ P$ is finite for the fixed smooth weight $P$. Consequently the squared norms of $F_\varepsilon $ in this weight have upper limit, as $\varepsilon \to 0$, at most a constant, depending only on $m,q,r',r''$, times $\int _{B'}|f|^2\mathrm e^{-P(z',0)}$. Since $t^{-q}(-\log t)^{-2}$ is bounded below on $(0,\mathrm e^{-3}]$, the $F_\varepsilon $ are bounded in $L^2(D)$, and by lemma 10.6.2(1) a sequence $\varepsilon _ k\to 0$ gives functions converging locally uniformly to a holomorphic $F$ with $F(z',0)=f(z')$. By Fatou's lemma, $F$ satisfies (10.8.7) with $P$ in place of $p$, and removing the bounded factor $\mathrm e^{-\pi |z|^2}$ gives (10.8.7) for smooth $p$.

General $p$. Take smooth plurisubharmonic functions $p_ l\downarrow p$ on a neighborhood of $\overline D$, obtained by convolution: by (3.4.13) the convolutions $p*\rho _{1/l}$ decrease to $p$, they are smooth, and they are plurisubharmonic because $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(p*\rho _{1/l})=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)*\rho _{1/l}\geq 0$ (theorem 5.9.1). Their restrictions $p_ l(z',0)$ decrease to $p(z',0)$, so the right sides of (10.8.7) for $p_ l$ are at most the right side for $p$, and the smooth case gives extensions $F_ l$ satisfying (10.8.7) for $p_ l$ with the same constant. Since $p_ l\leq p_1$, the weights have a common positive lower bound, so the $F_ l$ are bounded in $L^2(D)$ and, by lemma 10.6.2(1), a subsequence converges locally uniformly on $D$, including on $\{ w=0\} $. The limit $F$ satisfies $F(z',0)=f(z')$, and Fatou's lemma gives (10.8.7). $\square$

Indeed, $R\geq 0$ attains its minimum $0$ at such a point, so its first derivatives vanish there, and Taylor's formula gives $R(z',t\xi )=\frac{t^2}2D_ w^2R(z',0)[\xi ,\xi ]+O(t^3)$ as $t\to 0$, for every $\xi $; comparing with $R(z',t\xi )\geq c_0t^2|\xi |^2$ for small $t$, we obtain $D_ w^2R(z',0)[\xi ,\xi ]\geq 2c_0|\xi |^2$.

Step 2. Local extensions. Every point of $Y$ has holomorphic coordinates $(z',w)\in \mathbb C^ m\times \mathbb C^{n-m}$, centered at that point, in which $Y=\{ w=0\} $ on the coordinate domain, where $m<n$ is the dimension of the component of $Y$ through the point. A product of two small balls centered at the origin is then a chart $\widehat W=\widehat B'\times \widehat B''$ with $Y\cap \widehat W=\widehat B'\times \{ 0\} $. The products $B'\times B''$ of the concentric balls of half the radii cover $Y$, and since $Y$ is compact, finitely many of them suffice. We thus obtain finitely many charts $W_ j=B'_ j\times B''_ j$, relatively compact in charts $\widehat W_ j=\widehat B'_ j\times \widehat B''_ j$ of the same form, with $Y\cap W_ j=B'_ j\times \{ 0\} $ and with dimensions $m_ j<n$. Let $\pi _ j\colon \widehat W_ j\to Y$, $\pi _ j(z',w)=(z',0)$, be the holomorphic projection.

Below we shrink the normal factors $B''_ j$ several times, keeping them balls centered at the origin; this does not change $Y\cap W_ j$, so the charts keep covering $Y$. If a property holds on an open set $O\subset \widehat W_ j$ containing the compact set $\overline{B'_ j}\times \{ 0\} $, then this compact set has positive distance from the complement of $O$ in the coordinates, so $O$ contains $W_ j$ after the normal factor has been shrunk. We claim that there is $A>0$ such that, after such a shrinking,

\[ a_ j=\varphi \circ \pi _ j+AR,\qquad b_ j=F+AR \]

belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(W_ j,(1-2\varepsilon )\omega )$ for every $j$.

Since $4\varepsilon \leq \delta $, the hypothesis gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi \geq -(1-\delta )\omega |_ Y\geq -(1-4\varepsilon )\omega |_ Y$. Choose a ball $\widetilde B'_ j$, concentric with $B'_ j$, with $\overline{B'_ j}\subset \widetilde B'_ j\Subset \widehat B'_ j$, and a smooth function $s$ on $\widetilde B'_ j\times \{ 0\} $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s=(1-4\varepsilon )\omega |_ Y$ there (lemma 6.14.2; if $m_ j=0$, take $s=0$). Then $\varphi +s$ is plurisubharmonic on $\widetilde B'_ j\times \{ 0\} $, by lemma 6.14.4(2). By proposition 5.9.3, $(\varphi +s)\circ \pi _ j$ is plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $ on the connected set $\widetilde B'_ j\times \widehat B''_ j$, and the second alternative is excluded because $\pi _ j$ maps this set onto $\widetilde B'_ j\times \{ 0\} $, where $\varphi +s$ is not identically $-\infty $. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(s\circ \pi _ j)=\pi _ j^*\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s$ for the smooth function $s$, we obtain that $\varphi \circ \pi _ j$ is quasi-plurisubharmonic on $\widetilde B'_ j\times \widehat B''_ j\supset \overline{W_ j}$ and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi \circ \pi _ j)\geq -(1-4\varepsilon )\pi _ j^*(\omega |_ Y). \]

For $a_ j$ it therefore suffices that the smooth form

\[ Q_ j=(1-2\varepsilon )\omega -(1-4\varepsilon )\pi _ j^*(\omega |_ Y)+A\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R \]

be positive on $W_ j$.

We first examine $Q_ j$ at the points of $Y$. Write a real $(1,1)$-form on $W_ j$ as $\mathrm i\sum _{\mu ,\nu }g_{\mu \nu }\, \mathrm d\zeta _\mu \wedge \mathrm d\bar\zeta _\nu $, where $\zeta =(z',w)$, and split its Hermitian coefficient matrix $(g_{\mu \nu })$ into blocks with respect to the tangential coordinates $z'$ and the normal coordinates $w$; the form is positive definite at a point if and only if this matrix is. The form $\pi _ j^*(\omega |_ Y)$ involves only $\mathrm dz'$ and $\mathrm d\bar z'$, and at a point $(z',0)$ its coefficients are those of $\omega |_ Y$ at that point. Hence its mixed and normal blocks vanish, and at points of $Y$ its tangential block is the tangential block $\Omega _ T$ of $\omega $.

The function $R$ vanishes on $Y$ together with its first derivatives. Differentiating the identities $R(z',0)=0$ and $\partial R/\partial \bar w_\nu (z',0)=0$ with respect to $z'$ and $\bar z'$ shows that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R$ has zero tangential and mixed blocks along $Y$. Its normal block is $(2\pi )^{-1}$ times the matrix $N_ R=(\partial ^2R/\partial w_\mu \partial \bar w_\nu )$, which is positive definite at points of $Y$: for $\xi \in \mathbb C^{n-m_ j}\setminus \{ 0\} $, applying the identity $\Delta =4\partial _ t\partial _{\bar t}$ to $t\mapsto R(z',t\xi )$ at $t=0$ gives

\[ \sum _{\mu ,\nu }\frac{\partial ^2R}{\partial w_\mu \partial \bar w_\nu }(z',0)\xi _\mu \bar\xi _\nu =\frac14\bigl (D_ w^2R(z',0)[\xi ,\xi ]+D_ w^2R(z',0)[\mathrm i\xi ,\mathrm i\xi ]\bigr )>0 \]

by Step 1.

Consequently, at a point of $Y$, the tangential block of $Q_ j$ is $2\varepsilon \Omega _ T$, which is positive definite, its mixed block is the mixed block $\Omega _ M$ of $(1-2\varepsilon )\omega $, and its normal block is $\Omega _ N+AN_ R/(2\pi )$, where $\Omega _ N$ is the normal block of $(1-2\varepsilon )\omega $. By the Schur complement criterion for Hermitian block matrices whose upper left block is positive definite, $Q_ j$ is positive definite at a point of $Y$ if and only if

\[ \Omega _ N+\frac A{2\pi }N_ R-\Omega _ M^*\bigl (2\varepsilon \, \Omega _ T\bigr )^{-1}\Omega _ M \]

is positive definite there. (If $m_ j=0$, there is no tangential block, and the condition reads $\Omega _ N+AN_ R/(2\pi )>0$.) All blocks depend continuously on the point, $N_ R$ is positive definite, and the sets $\overline{B'_ j}\times \{ 0\} $ are compact and finite in number. Hence the smallest eigenvalue of $N_ R$ on them has a positive lower bound and the other two terms are bounded, so one $A$ makes this matrix positive definite at every point of every $\overline{B'_ j}\times \{ 0\} $. Fix such an $A$. Since the coefficients of $Q_ j$ are continuous on $\widehat W_ j$ and positive definiteness is an open condition, $Q_ j$ is positive definite on an open set containing $\overline{B'_ j}\times \{ 0\} $, and we shrink the normal factor $B''_ j$ so that $W_ j$ lies in this set. This proves the claim for $a_ j$.

Along $Y$ the form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R$ is positive semidefinite, so $A\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R+\varepsilon \omega $ is positive definite on an open set containing $\overline{B'_ j}\times \{ 0\} $; after shrinking the normal factors once more we have $A\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R\geq -\varepsilon \omega $ on every $W_ j$. Together with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F\geq -(1-4\varepsilon )\omega $ this gives

\[ (1-2\varepsilon )\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ j\geq (1-2\varepsilon )\omega -(1-4\varepsilon )\omega -\varepsilon \omega =\varepsilon \omega \geq 0, \]

which proves the claim for $b_ j$.

By lemma 10.8.25, applied with $\theta =(1-2\varepsilon )\omega $, the functions

\[ \varphi _ j=\log (\mathrm e^{a_ j}+\mathrm e^{b_ j}) =\log (\mathrm e^{\varphi \circ \pi _ j}+\mathrm e^ F)+AR \]

belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(W_ j,(1-2\varepsilon )\omega )$. On $Y$ we have $F=-\infty $, $R=0$ and $\varphi \circ \pi _ j=\varphi $, so $\varphi _ j=\varphi $ on $Y\cap W_ j$, also at the poles of $\varphi $. Off $Y$ the function $\mathrm e^ F$ is positive and continuous and $\mathrm e^{\varphi \circ \pi _ j}$ is continuous, so $\varphi _ j$ is continuous on $W_ j\setminus Y$. Finally $\mathrm e^{\varphi _ j}=(\mathrm e^\varphi \circ \pi _ j+\mathrm e^ F)\, \mathrm e^{AR}$ is Hölder continuous on $W_ j$ by lemma 10.8.24(3),(4).

Step 3. Comparison on overlaps. We claim that for every $p\in Y\cap W_ i\cap W_ j$ and every $\eta >0$ there is a neighborhood of $p$ on which

\begin{equation} \varphi _ i\leq \varphi _ j+\eta . \end{equation}

Work in the coordinates $(z',w)$ of $W_ i$, and write $p=(z'_ p,0)$. The coordinate $z'$ restricts to coordinates of $Y$ on $Y\cap W_ i$, so by Step 0 there are a neighborhood $V_ p$ of $p$ in $Y\cap W_ i$ and a constant $C_ H$ with $|\mathrm e^{\varphi (y_1)}-\mathrm e^{\varphi (y_2)}|\leq C_ H|z'(y_1)-z'(y_2)|^\alpha $ for $y_1,y_2\in V_ p$. Since $\pi _ i$ and $\pi _ j$ are continuous and fix $p$, there is a closed ball $K\subset W_ i\cap W_ j$ centered at $p$ such that $\pi _ i(K)\cup \pi _ j(K)\subset V_ p$ and (10.8.27) holds on $K$ in these coordinates. The map $z'\circ \pi _ j$ is smooth on the convex compact set $K$, so it is Lipschitz there with some constant $L$. Let $x=(z',w)\in K$, and put $y=(z',0)=\pi _ i(x)$. Then $|y-p|\leq |x-p|$, so $y\in K\cap Y$, and $\pi _ j(y)=y$ because $\pi _ j$ is the identity on $Y\cap W_ j$. Hence

\[ |z'(\pi _ i(x))-z'(\pi _ j(x))|=|z'(\pi _ j(y))-z'(\pi _ j(x))|\leq L|y-x|=L|w|, \]

and therefore

\[ \bigl |\mathrm e^{\varphi \circ \pi _ i}(x)-\mathrm e^{\varphi \circ \pi _ j}(x)\bigr |\leq C_ HL^\alpha |w|^\alpha . \]

Since $c<\alpha $, we have $C_ HL^\alpha |w|^\alpha \leq (\mathrm e^\eta -1)c_0^{c/2}|w|^ c$ whenever $|w|$ is sufficiently small, and then (10.8.27) gives $C_ HL^\alpha |w|^\alpha \leq (\mathrm e^\eta -1)\mathrm e^{F(x)}$. At such points $x\in K$, including the points of $Y$,

\[ \mathrm e^{\varphi \circ \pi _ i}+\mathrm e^ F \leq \mathrm e^{\varphi \circ \pi _ j}+\mathrm e^\eta \mathrm e^ F \leq \mathrm e^\eta \bigl (\mathrm e^{\varphi \circ \pi _ j}+\mathrm e^ F\bigr ). \]

The logarithm is increasing on $[0,\infty )$, with $\log 0=-\infty $, so taking logarithms and adding $AR$ to both sides gives (10.8.28) on the neighborhood of $p$ formed by the interior points $x$ of $K$ with $|w(x)|$ sufficiently small.

Step 4. Gluing near $Y$. Every point of $Y$ has an open neighborhood whose closure is contained in some $W_ j$, and finitely many of these neighborhoods cover $Y$; let $W'_ j$ be the union of those chosen inside $W_ j$. Then $W'_ j\Subset W_ j$ and the $W'_ j$ cover $Y$. Choose open sets $U_ j$ and $T_ j$ with $W'_ j\Subset U_ j\Subset T_ j\Subset W_ j$, for instance sets of points whose coordinate distance from $\overline{W'_ j}$ is less than suitable small constants. The compact set $\overline{T_ j}\setminus U_ j$ is contained in the open set $W_ j\setminus \overline{W'_ j}$, so by proposition 5.2.6 there is a smooth function $f_ j\colon X\to [0,1]$ with compact support in $W_ j\setminus \overline{W'_ j}$ which equals one on a neighborhood of $\overline{T_ j}\setminus U_ j$; in particular $f_ j=0$ on the neighborhood $X\setminus \operatorname{Supp}f_ j$ of $\overline{W'_ j}$. Fix $\eta >0$ so small that $\eta \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j\leq \varepsilon \omega $ for every $j$. Since $(1-\varepsilon )\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j\geq \varepsilon \omega $, the functions

\[ \widehat\varphi _ j=\varphi _ j-\eta f_ j \]

belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(W_ j,(1-\varepsilon )\omega )$. On $W=\bigcup _ jT_ j$ define

\[ \psi (x)=\max \{ \widehat\varphi _ j(x):x\in T_ j\} . \]

The set of indices in this maximum changes with $x$. We show that near $Y$ the maximum is nevertheless taken over a fixed set of indices. Let $p\in Y$, and choose $j$ with $p\in W'_ j$; then $f_ j=0$ near $p$, and $j$ occurs in the maximum near $p$. Let $l$ be an index with $p\in \partial T_ l$. Then $p\in \overline{T_ l}\setminus U_ l$, so $f_ l=1$ near $p$. Since $p\in Y\cap W_ l\cap W_ j$, the inequality (10.8.28) with $\eta /2$ in place of $\eta $ gives, near $p$,

\[ \widehat\varphi _ l=\varphi _ l-\eta \leq \varphi _ j-\eta /2\leq \widehat\varphi _ j. \]

These are inequalities between functions with values in $[-\infty ,\infty )$, valid also at common poles. As there are finitely many indices, $p$ has a neighborhood $O_ p$ on which the indices $l$ with $p\in \partial T_ l$ never affect the maximum, the indices with $p\notin \overline{T_ l}$ do not occur, and the indices with $p\in T_ l$ occur at every point. Thus, on $O_ p$, the function $\psi $ is the maximum of the $\widehat\varphi _ l$ over the fixed finite set of indices $l$ with $p\in T_ l$.

Let $W_0=\bigcup _{p\in Y}O_ p$. A finite maximum of functions in $\mathrm{PSH}(O_ p,(1-\varepsilon )\omega )$ belongs to this class, by lemma 6.14.4(3). Hence $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(W_0,(1-\varepsilon )\omega )$; moreover $\psi $ is continuous on $W_0\setminus Y$, and $\mathrm e^\psi $, which is locally a finite maximum of the functions $\mathrm e^{\widehat\varphi _ l}=\mathrm e^{\varphi _ l}\mathrm e^{-\eta f_ l}$, is Hölder continuous on $W_0$ by lemma 10.8.24(3). On $Y$ every $\widehat\varphi _ l$ restricts to $\varphi -\eta f_ l\leq \varphi $, while $\widehat\varphi _ j=\varphi $ near $p$ for the index $j$ chosen above. Consequently $\psi |_ Y=\varphi $.

Step 5. The global extension. Choose an open neighborhood $G$ of $Y$ with $\overline G\subset W_0$, for instance a finite union of coordinate balls with closures in $W_0$ covering the compact set $Y$. Its boundary $\partial G$ is compact and disjoint from $Y$, and both $\psi $ and $F$ are finite and continuous near $\partial G$. Choose a constant $M$ with $M>\max _{\partial G}(\psi -F)$ (if $\partial G=\varnothing $, every $M$ will do); since $F+M-\psi $ is continuous on $W_0\setminus Y$ and positive on $\partial G$, it is positive on an open neighborhood $G_1\subset W_0\setminus Y$ of $\partial G$. Set

\[ \widetilde\varphi = \begin{cases} \max \{ \psi ,F+M\} & \text{on }G,\\ F+M& \text{on }X\setminus G. \end{cases} \]

The two expressions agree on $G_1$, so $\widetilde\varphi =F+M$ on the open set $G_1\cup (X\setminus \overline G)$, which contains $X\setminus G$.

The function $F+M$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,(1-4\varepsilon )\omega )\subset \mathrm{PSH}(X,(1-\varepsilon )\omega )$, and $\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(W_0,(1-\varepsilon )\omega )$. Let $B$ be a coordinate ball whose closure lies in a chart, and $h$ a smooth function on $B$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h=(1-\varepsilon )\omega $ (lemma 6.14.2). Then $F+M+h\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ and $\psi +h\in \mathrm{PSH}(G\cap B)$. Every point $x$ of the boundary of $G\cap B$ in $B$ lies in $\partial G$, and $\varlimsup _{G\ni y\to x}(\psi +h)(y)\leq F(x)+M+h(x)$, because $\psi <F+M$ on $G_1$ and $F+M+h$ is continuous at $x$. By proposition 5.9.5, $\widetilde\varphi +h$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$. Since such balls cover $X$, $\widetilde\varphi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic and $(1-\varepsilon )\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde\varphi \geq 0$, that is, $\widetilde\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde\varphi \geq \varepsilon \omega $. Since $F=-\infty $ on $Y$, we have $\widetilde\varphi |_ Y=\psi |_ Y=\varphi $. The function $\widetilde\varphi $ is continuous on $X\setminus Y$, because $\psi $ and $F$ are continuous on $W_0\setminus Y$ and $X\setminus Y$, respectively. Its exponential equals $\max \{ \mathrm e^\psi ,\mathrm e^ M\mathrm e^ F\} $ on $G$ and $\mathrm e^ M\mathrm e^ F$ on the open set $G_1\cup (X\setminus \overline G)$; these two open sets cover $X$, so $\mathrm e^{\widetilde\varphi }$ is Hölder continuous on $X$ by lemma 10.8.24(3).

Step 6. Analytic singularities. Finally suppose that $\varphi $ has analytic singularities (definition 6.14.5). By proposition 6.14.8, so does the holomorphic pullback $\varphi \circ \pi _ j$, which is not identically $-\infty $, and the function $F$ has analytic singularities by Step 1. By the same proposition, $\max \{ \varphi \circ \pi _ j,F\} $ has analytic singularities; adding the smooth function $AR$ changes the bounded remainder in its local presentations (6.14.6) by a locally bounded function, so $\max \{ \varphi \circ \pi _ j,F\} +AR$ has analytic singularities. By lemma 10.8.25,

\[ \max \{ \varphi \circ \pi _ j,F\} +AR\leq \varphi _ j\leq \max \{ \varphi \circ \pi _ j,F\} +AR+\log 2. \]

Hence a local presentation (6.14.6) of $\max \{ \varphi \circ \pi _ j,F\} +AR$ yields one of $\varphi _ j$, with the same holomorphic functions and a bounded remainder changed by at most $\log 2$; so $\varphi _ j$ has analytic singularities. Subtracting the smooth function $\eta f_ j$ changes the remainders by bounded functions, and the locally fixed finite maxima of Step 4 preserve analytic singularities by proposition 6.14.8; so $\psi $ has analytic singularities on $W_0$. On $G$, the function $\widetilde\varphi $ is the maximum of $\psi $ and $F+M$, and on the open set $G_1\cup (X\setminus \overline G)$ it equals $F+M$; both functions have analytic singularities. Therefore $\widetilde\varphi $ has analytic singularities on $X$. $\square$
