12.10 Integrable densities in the compact Gauduchon problem5CB8CFC32A19

5CB8CFC32A19

We keep the Hermitian background $\omega $ and replace the continuous logarithmic density by an integrable density. For an unbounded density, weak compactness of the test currents alone does not pass the determinant integral to the limit; uniform control of the weighted tails provides the missing upper semicontinuity.

We use the following notation from section 15.28, section 15.29, with $\omega $ in place of $h$. For a nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, the subsolution threshold $b_\omega (f)\geq 0$ is, by theorem 15.28.4, the largest $b\geq 0$ for which some $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V$. For $p\in L^ n$, the root norm is $\rho _\omega (p)=b_\omega (|p|^ n)^{-1/n}$ if $p\ne 0$, and $\rho _\omega (0)=0$. The space $\mathcal D_\omega $ of proposition 15.32.5 consists of the $p\in L^ n$ with $\rho _\omega (p)<+\infty $, and $\mathcal D_\omega ^0$ is the closure of the bounded functions in $\mathcal D_\omega $, characterized by vanishing tails in proposition 15.32.9. For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, $L_{\omega ,\gamma }(p)$ denotes the test quotient of section 15.29, and theorem 15.29.1 gives $\rho _\omega (p)=\sup _\gamma L_{\omega ,\gamma }(|p|)$. Finally, $k_ T$ is the determinant density of $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ from theorem 12.9.1.

If $p\geq 0$ is measurable, define

\[ D_ p(T)=\int _ Xp k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V\qquad (T\in \mathscr G_\omega ), \]

allowing the value $+\infty $. If $T=\gamma ^{n-1}/\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ for a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, then $D_ p(T)=L_{\omega ,\gamma }(p)$.

Theorem 12.10.1.41D17749328B For every nonnegative $p\in L^ n$,

025E3287544B 12.10.2
\begin{equation} \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_\omega }D_ p(T)=\rho _\omega (p), \end{equation}

with the value $+\infty $ allowed. If $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$, the functional $D_ p$ is finite, concave and weakly upper semicontinuous on $\mathscr G_\omega $, its maximum is attained, and the densities $p k_ T$, for $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, are uniformly integrable.

Proof. For a smooth strictly positive $p$, we have $D_ p=\mathfrak D_{n\log p}$ and $\mathrm{e}^{-B_0(n\log p)/n}=\rho _\omega (p)$, so theorem 12.7.3, applied to $F=n\log p$, proves (12.10.2). By Hölder's inequality and theorem 12.9.1, for every real $r>n$ there is a constant $C_ r$ such that, for every $q\in L^ r$,

B5DC9A4ABEEC 12.10.3
\begin{equation} \sup _{T\in \mathscr G_\omega }\int _ X|q| k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \leq C_ r\| q\| _{L^ r}. \end{equation}

Next, approximate a bounded nonnegative $p$ in $L^{2n}$ by smooth strictly positive functions. This estimate and (15.32.8) give uniform convergence on $\mathscr G_\omega $ of the corresponding functionals $D$ and convergence of the root norms, so the supremum formula holds for bounded $p$. For such $p$, the functional $D_ p$ is also upper semicontinuous and concave, by lemma 12.7.2 and uniform convergence. For general $p\in L^ n$, we use the truncations $\min (p,K)$ at positive integer levels $K$. Monotone convergence and the definition of $b_\omega $ through truncations give

\[ \sup _ T D_ p(T)=\sup _ T\sup _ K D_{\min (p,K)}(T) =\sup _ K\rho _\omega (\min (p,K))=\rho _\omega (p). \]

This step only interchanges two suprema and gives no attainment.

Now suppose $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$. The supremum formula applied to the tail gives

\[ 0\leq D_ p(T)-D_{\min (p,K)}(T) \leq \rho _\omega (p\mathbf1_{\{ p>K\} })\longrightarrow 0 \]

uniformly in $T$, by proposition 15.32.9. Hence $D_ p$ is finite and is a uniform limit of upper semicontinuous concave functionals, and compactness of $\mathscr G_\omega $ gives a maximizer. Finally, fix $1<q<n/(n-1)$. By Hölder's inequality and theorem 12.9.1, for every measurable set $E$,

\[ \sup _ T\int _ Ep k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \rho _\omega (p\mathbf1_{\{ p>K\} }) +K C_ q\, \mathrm{d}V(E)^{1-1/q}. \]

Choosing first $K$ large and then the volume of $E$ small proves uniform absolute continuity. Since the total masses are bounded by $\rho _\omega (p)$, the densities are uniformly integrable. $\square$

For a nonzero density, a maximizing current gives a supporting probability for the threshold, as we show below. Whether the threshold is attained by an exact equation depends on the regularity of the maximizing current, which enters through the trace inequalities of 12.10.10.

12.10.4 The tail condition and perturbations of the density5210ECD5227F

Vanishing tails are equivalent to continuity of the threshold under uniformly bounded multiplicative perturbations of the density that converge in measure. This explains the role of the tail condition: if it fails, a perturbation supported on sets of shrinking volume can lower the threshold by a fixed factor.

Proposition 12.10.5.249445C21482 Let $0\leq p\in \mathcal D_\omega $ be nonzero. The following are equivalent:

  1. $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$;

  2. the densities $p k_ T$, for $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, are uniformly integrable;

  3. whenever $F_ j$ are smooth real functions, uniformly bounded and tending to zero in measure, one has

    \[ b_\omega (\mathrm{e}^{F_ j}p^ n)\longrightarrow b_\omega (p^ n). \]

If the first condition holds, the convergence in the third also holds for bounded measurable $F_ j$ with the same uniform bound and convergence in measure. If it fails, there are smooth $F_ j\geq 0$, uniformly bounded and tending to zero in measure, such that

\[ \mathrm{e}^{F_ j}p^ n\longrightarrow p^ n\quad \text{in }L^1, \qquad b_\omega (\mathrm{e}^{F_ j}p^ n)\leq 2^{-n}b_\omega (p^ n). \]

Proof. The first condition implies the second by theorem 12.10.1. Conversely, $\mathrm{d}V(\{ p>K\} )\to 0$ as $K\to \infty $, so uniform integrability and (12.10.2) give

\[ \rho _\omega (p\mathbf1_{\{ p>K\} }) =\sup _{T\in \mathscr G_\omega }\int _{\{ p>K\} }p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \longrightarrow 0. \]

This is the first condition by proposition 15.32.9.

If $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$ and $|F_ j|\leq A$, then $p(\mathrm{e}^{F_ j/n}-1)$ tends to zero in measure and its absolute value is at most $(\mathrm{e}^{A/n}+1)p$. The dominated-convergence criterion of proposition 15.32.9 makes its root norm tend to zero, and the triangle inequality gives $\rho _\omega (p\mathrm{e}^{F_ j/n})\to \rho _\omega (p)>0$. Taking negative $n$th powers proves the third condition, also for bounded measurable $F_ j$.

Suppose now that the tails do not tend to zero, and put $R=\rho _\omega (p)>0$. The formula of theorem 15.29.1, which uses only smooth tests, yields $\delta >0$, numbers $K_ j\to \infty $ and smooth strictly positive $T_ j\in \mathscr G_\omega $ such that

\[ \int _{\{ p>K_ j\} }p k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V>\delta . \]

Since the finite measure $p k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V$ is regular, we can choose a compact set $C_ j\subset \{ p>K_ j\} $ of mass greater than $\delta /2$ for this measure. By outer regularity of $\mathrm{d}V$, choose an open set $O_ j\supset \{ p>K_ j\} $ with $\mathrm{d}V(O_ j)\leq \mathrm{d}V(\{ p>K_ j\} )+1/j$, and a smooth cutoff $0\leq \chi _ j\leq 1$ that equals one on $C_ j$ and has support in $O_ j$. Fix $A>0$ with $\mathrm{e}^{A/n}\delta /2>2R$, and set $F_ j=A\chi _ j$. Then $F_ j\to 0$ in measure, while

\[ \rho _\omega (p\mathrm{e}^{F_ j/n}) \geq \int _ Xp\mathrm{e}^{F_ j/n}k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V >2R. \]

Since $b_\omega (\mathrm{e}^{F_ j}p^ n)=\rho _\omega (p\mathrm{e}^{F_ j/n})^{-n}$, this gives the asserted uniform gap. Finally, $|\mathrm{e}^{F_ j}-1|p^ n\leq (\mathrm{e}^ A-1)p^ n\mathbf1_{O_ j}$, whose integral tends to zero by absolute continuity of the integral. This proves the last assertion and the converse to the third condition. $\square$

For $f=p^ n$, the perturbed densities satisfy $\mathrm{e}^{-A}f\leq \mathrm{e}^{F_ j}f\leq \mathrm{e}^ Af$ with a fixed $A$, so the continuity statement concerns only such order intervals. The instability under general $L^1$ approximation in section 15.32 involves approximations without these bounds.

For attainment at one density, uniform integrability along a single maximizing sequence is enough, and this weaker condition also identifies the limit of its weighted measures.

Proposition 12.10.6.B62E802ED631 Let $0\leq p\in \mathcal D_\omega $ be nonzero and put $R=\rho _\omega (p)$. Suppose $T_ j\in \mathscr G_\omega $ converge weakly to $T$ and satisfy $D_ p(T_ j)\to R$. If the densities $p k_{T_ j}$ are uniformly integrable, then

\[ D_ p(T)=R,\qquad p k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V\rightharpoonup p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Consequently, a maximizing sequence for $D_ p$ with uniformly integrable densities $pk_{T_ j}$ has a maximizing current among its weak cluster limits.

Proof. For positive integers $K$, put $p_ K=\min (p,K)$. Since $p$ is finite almost everywhere, uniform integrability gives

\[ \varepsilon _ K:=\sup _ j\int _{\{ p>K\} }p k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V \longrightarrow 0. \]

For every nonnegative continuous function $h$, the weight $hp_ K$ is bounded and measurable, so the upper semicontinuity of $D_{hp_ K}$ proved in theorem 12.10.1 gives

\[ \limsup _ j\int _ Xhp k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \int _ Xhp_ K k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V +\| h\| _\infty \varepsilon _ K. \]

Now let $K\to \infty $. For $h=1$ this yields $R\leq D_ p(T)$, and the supremum formula (12.10.2) gives the reverse inequality. If $\Lambda $ is a weak cluster limit of the measures $p k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V$, the same estimate for all nonnegative continuous $h$ gives $\Lambda \leq p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$. Both measures have mass $R$, so they agree. Since these measures have bounded masses, they form a weakly relatively compact family, and every cluster limit has now been identified. The final assertion follows from the compactness of $\mathscr G_\omega $. $\square$

12.10.7 Supporting probabilities for an integrable density516A524E7A2A

Under the vanishing-tail condition, we can also perturb the density by continuous multiplicative factors $\mathrm{e}^ F$. The logarithm of the threshold is then a concave function of $F$, and its supporting probabilities are given by the maximizing currents, just as for the continuous densities in section 12.8.

Let $f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero and nonnegative, and assume $p=f^{1/n}\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$. For a continuous real $F$, set

\[ B_ f(F)=\log b_\omega (\mathrm{e}^ Ff) =-n\log \rho _\omega (p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}),\qquad M_ f(F)=\rho _\omega (p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}). \]

Both quantities are finite and $M_ f(F)>0$, since $\mathcal D_\omega ^0$ is an order ideal. Let $\mathscr M_ f(F)$ be the set of currents in $\mathscr G_\omega $ maximizing $D_{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}}$, which is nonempty by theorem 12.10.1, and put

\[ \nu _{T,F}=\frac{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n} k_ T}{M_ f(F)}\, \mathrm{d}V \quad (T\in \mathscr M_ f(F)). \]

These are probability measures. Let $\mathcal A_ f(F)$, the set of supporting probabilities of $B_ f$ at $F$, consist of all Borel probability measures $\nu $ satisfying

C174F8B6FDCF 12.10.8
\begin{equation} B_ f(G)\leq B_ f(F)-\int _ X(G-F)\, \mathrm{d}\nu \qquad (G\in C(X,\mathbb R)). \end{equation}

Proposition 12.10.9.CCE6CFE41CBF The functional $B_ f$ is concave, decreasing and $1$-Lipschitz for the uniform norm, and $B_ f(F+a)=B_ f(F)-a$ for constants $a$. Moreover,

\[ \mathcal A_ f(F)=\{ \nu _{T,F}:T\in \mathscr M_ f(F)\} . \]

This set is nonempty, convex and weakly compact, and its measures are absolutely continuous. For each $R>0$, the densities of all these measures, for all $F$ with $\| F\| _\infty \leq R$, are uniformly integrable. For $H\in C(X,\mathbb R)$, the one-sided directional derivatives of $B_ f$ at $F$ are

\[ (B_ f)'_+(F;H)=-\max _{\nu \in \mathcal A_ f(F)}\int _ XH\, \mathrm{d}\nu , \qquad (B_ f)'_-(F;H)=-\min _{\nu \in \mathcal A_ f(F)}\int _ XH\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

Proof. For each $T$, Hölder's inequality makes $F\mapsto \int _ Xp \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$ logarithmically convex, and taking suprema preserves the inequality defining logarithmic convexity. Thus $M_ f$ is logarithmically convex and $B_ f=-n\log M_ f$ is concave. Monotonicity and homogeneity of $\rho _\omega $ give the translation rule and, by comparison with constant translates, the Lipschitz estimate.

For fixed $F$, the weight $p\mathrm{e}^{F/n}$ belongs to $\mathcal D_\omega ^0$, so the densities $p\mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_ T$, $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, are uniformly integrable by theorem 12.10.1. If $T_ j\to T$ and $D_{p\mathrm{e}^{F/n}}(T_ j)\to M_ f(F)$, proposition 12.10.6 makes $T$ a maximizer and gives convergence of the weighted measures. For $T_1,T_2\in \mathscr G_\omega $ and real $0\leq t\leq 1$, matrix determinant concavity gives

\[ p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_{tT_1+(1-t)T_2}\, \mathrm{d}V \geq t p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_{T_1}\, \mathrm{d}V +(1-t)p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}k_{T_2}\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

For two maximizing currents, the current on the left also maximizes, and equal total masses turn this inequality into an equality. The set $\mathscr N_ f(F)=\{ \nu _{T,F}:T\in \mathscr M_ f(F)\} $ is therefore nonempty, compact and convex.

A maximizing $T$ gives

\[ M_ f(G)\geq M_ f(F)\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{(G-F)/n}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _{T,F}. \]

Taking $-n$ times the logarithm and applying Jensen's inequality proves (12.10.8), so $\mathscr N_ f(F)\subset \mathcal A_ f(F)$. For the reverse inclusion, a Taylor expansion of the exponential gives

\[ D_{p \mathrm{e}^{(F+tH)/n}}(T) =D_{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}}(T)+\frac tn\int _ X H p \mathrm{e}^{F/n} k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V+O(t^2), \]

where the error is uniform in $T$ since $D_{p \mathrm{e}^{F/n}}(T)\leq M_ f(F)$. Maximizers at $F+tH$ are near-maximizers at $F$, by the multiplicative bounds $\mathrm{e}^{-t\| H\| _\infty /n}\leq M_ f(F+tH)/M_ f(F)\leq \mathrm{e}^{t\| H\| _\infty /n}$. The convergence of their weighted measures proved above and this uniform expansion are exactly the two inputs of the one-sided differentiation argument in the proof of theorem 12.8.2, which gives

\[ (B_ f)'_+(F;H)=-\max _{\nu \in \mathscr N_ f(F)}\int _ XH\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

The separation argument of that proof then identifies all supporting probabilities with $\mathscr N_ f(F)$, and replacing $H$ by $-H$ gives the left derivative.

Finally, $M_ f(F)\geq \mathrm{e}^{-R/n}\rho _\omega (p)$ when $\| F\| _\infty \leq R$, so the densities of the probabilities $\nu _{T,F}$ are at most $\mathrm{e}^{2R/n}\rho _\omega (p)^{-1}p k_ T$. The uniform integrability of the densities $pk_ T$ in theorem 12.10.1 proves the remaining assertions. $\square$

When $f=1$, we have $B_ f=B_0$, and the proposition reduces to theorem 12.8.2. For general $f$, the functional $B_ f$ is defined through the subsolution threshold and does not presuppose that the corresponding equation has a solution. If $v_-(\omega )>0$, every nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^1$ satisfies $f^{1/n}\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$ by proposition 15.32.9, and corollary 15.37.32 identifies $b_\omega (f)$ as the unique constant for which the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (v)=b\, f\, \mathrm{d}V$ has a solution $v$ in the envelope class $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\omega ,0)$ of that corollary. Without positive lower mass, the uniform tail condition is an explicit sufficient hypothesis for this compact dual description.

12.10.10 Trace inequalities at the subsolution threshold07A010666AF5

Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero with $b=b_\omega (f)>0$. Recall from section 15.28 that a threshold subsolution is a function $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V$, and a threshold solution is one for which equality holds; threshold subsolutions exist by theorem 15.28.4. A smooth Gauduchon test can be paired with the full current $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ of a threshold subsolution, and by lemma 13.14.12 the resulting trace measure is bounded below by the determinant-weighted density. The nonnegative difference has a mass that depends only on the test. Along a maximizing sequence this mass tends to zero, uniformly over all threshold subsolutions. We write $\| \cdot \| _{\mathrm{TV}}$ for the total variation of a finite signed measure.

Proposition 12.10.11.08C2A578ACE2 Let $0\leq f\in L^1$ be nonzero and $b=b_\omega (f)>0$, and write $p=f^{1/n}$. Suppose $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ satisfies $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (u)\geq bf\, \mathrm{d}V$. For every smooth strictly positive $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, the measure

\[ R_{u,T}=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T-b^{1/n}p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V \]

is positive and has mass

\[ R_{u,T}(X)=1-b^{1/n}D_ p(T). \]

In particular, if $(T_ j)$ is a sequence of smooth strictly positive members of $\mathscr G_\omega $ with $D_ p(T_ j)\to \rho _\omega (p)$, then $\| R_{u,T_ j}\| _{\mathrm{TV}}\to 0$, uniformly over all threshold subsolutions $u$.

If also $p\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$ and $T_ j\to T$ as currents, then $T$ maximizes $D_ p$ and

\[ (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T_ j \rightharpoonup b^{1/n}p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

In particular, for a fixed sequence $(T_ j)$ with limit $T$, this limiting probability is the same for all threshold subsolutions $u$.

Proof. A smooth strictly positive $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $ is $\gamma ^{n-1}$ for a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $ normalized by $\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=1$. Lemma 13.14.12 proves positivity of $R_{u,T}$, and Stokes' theorem for currents gives $((\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T)(X)=\int _ X\omega \wedge T=1$. This proves the mass formula. Since $\rho _\omega (p)=b^{-1/n}$, the nonnegative mass tends to zero along a maximizing sequence; it equals the total variation norm and does not depend on $u$.

Under the tail hypothesis, theorem 12.10.1 gives uniform integrability of the densities $p k_{T_ j}$. Thus proposition 12.10.6 makes $T$ a maximizer and gives $p k_{T_ j}\, \mathrm{d}V\rightharpoonup p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$. Adding the error measures $R_{u,T_ j}$, which tend to zero in total variation, proves the limit formula. $\square$

The limit formula concerns smooth maximizing sequences. It does not define the product $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)\wedge T$ for a singular current $T$, nor does it show that the limit is the same for different maximizing currents.

If a maximizing test is itself a smooth strictly positive form, the trace inequality becomes an equality of currents, and since $n\geq 2$ this forces an exact smooth equation. The local regularity needed for this comes from lemma 5.22.4.

Corollary 12.10.12.F5E70269636C Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero with $b=b_\omega (f)>0$. If $D_{f^{1/n}}$ has a smooth strictly positive maximizer $T\in \mathscr G_\omega $, then $f^{1/n}$ agrees almost everywhere with a smooth nonnegative function, and every threshold subsolution $u$ is smooth and satisfies

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\omega (u)=bf\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

In particular a smooth threshold solution exists, and any two threshold subsolutions differ by a constant.

Proof. Let $u$ be any threshold subsolution; one exists by theorem 15.28.4. Write $T=\gamma ^{n-1}$, where $\gamma $ is the smooth Gauduchon metric given by the positive $(n-1)$st root of $T$, and set

\[ a=\left(\frac{bf\, \mathrm{d}V}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}. \]

Put $A=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$. Since $T$ maximizes, the positive measure $R_{u,T}$ of proposition 12.10.11 has mass zero, which gives the equality

\[ A\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=a\gamma ^ n. \]

The coefficient measures of the positive current $A$ are controlled by this trace measure, which is absolutely continuous. Thus $A$ has a matrix density that is positive semidefinite almost everywhere, with coefficients in $L^ n$ because $a\in L^ n$.

For every smooth Hermitian form $\beta $, lemma 13.14.12 also gives

\[ A\wedge \beta ^{n-1} \geq \left(\frac{bf\, \mathrm{d}V}{\beta ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\beta ^ n. \]

The resulting pointwise matrix inequalities hold almost everywhere, simultaneously for all positive definite matrices: on each coordinate chart, first use a countable dense family of constant positive matrices, realized on smaller charts by global smooth Hermitian forms, and then use continuity in the matrix. The variational formula for $(\det P)^{1/n}$ in the proof of lemma 12.7.2 therefore yields

\[ \left(\frac{A^ n}{\gamma ^ n}\right)^{1/n}\geq a \quad \text{almost everywhere}. \]

Here $A^ n$ denotes only the determinant of the measurable coefficient matrix of $A$; no nonlinear product of currents is used. The trace equality gives the reverse inequality, by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality. Equality then forces all eigenvalues of $A$ relative to $\gamma $ to equal $a$, also where $a=0$. Consequently $A=a\gamma $ as currents.

By lemma 5.22.4, applied with $\theta =\omega $, both $a$ and $u$ are smooth, and $f^{1/n}=b^{-1/n}a(\gamma ^ n/\mathrm{d}V)^{1/n}$ has a smooth nonnegative representative. \[ 1\geq c_\lambda ^{1/n}d_ j \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{\lambda v_\lambda /n}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \geq c_\lambda ^{1/n}d_ j\mathrm{e}^{-C\lambda /n}. \]

The second inequality follows from Jensen's inequality and (12.10.44). Letting $j\to \infty $ and using $R=b^{-1/n}$, we obtain the upper bound for $c_\lambda $. The lower bound and the comparison with every normalized threshold subsolution were proved in corollary 15.35.3; applying that comparison to $\Phi _ f$, we obtain

\[ v_\lambda \geq \Phi _ f-\lambda ^{-1}\log (c_\lambda /b) \geq \Phi _ f-C. \]

Now let $v_{\lambda _ k}\to v$ in $L^1$. By proposition 15.28.6, the limit $v$ lies in $\mathcal S_ f$, hence below $\Phi _ f$. The lower bound passes to the limit, so $\Phi _ f-C\leq v\leq \Phi _ f$; in particular $v\sim \Phi _ f$ in the sense of definition 16.7.1. For all $k$ with $\lambda _ k\leq 1$,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (v_{\lambda _ k}) =c_{\lambda _ k}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda _ k v_{\lambda _ k}}f\, \mathrm{d}V \leq b\mathrm{e}^{C}f\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

The functions $|\mathrm{e}^{v_{\lambda _ k}}-\mathrm{e}^ v|$ are uniformly bounded and tend to zero in $\mathrm{d}V$-measure, so their integrals against the fixed $L^1$ density $f$ tend to zero. Hence the error term in (15.9.2) tends to zero, as required by proposition 15.9.3 with $\varphi =\Phi _ f$. The additional hypothesis $v\preceq \Phi _ f$ of that proposition holds since $v\leq \Phi _ f$, and the condition on $P_{\delta \omega }(\Phi _ f)$ is assumed. That proposition therefore gives convergence in capacity.

The continuity of damped measures under convergence in capacity, theorem 13.15.9, now identifies the equation without a mass-conservation argument. Write $g_ k=c_{\lambda _ k}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda _ k v_{\lambda _ k}}f$. We know $g_ k\to bf$ in $L^1$ and $0\leq g_ k\leq b\mathrm{e}^ Cf$ for large $k$. Hence

\[ \mathrm{e}^{v_{\lambda _ k}}\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (v_{\lambda _ k}) =\mathrm{e}^{v_{\lambda _ k}}g_ k\, \mathrm{d}V \longrightarrow \mathrm{e}^ v bf\, \mathrm{d}V \]

in total variation, while theorem 13.15.9 gives the weak limit $\mathrm{e}^ v\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (v)$. Dividing by $\mathrm{e}^ v$ off the pole set of $v$, which neither measure charges, gives the equation and the finiteness of the mass. Finally, compactness of normalized $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions provides an $L^1$ cluster limit along any sequence $\lambda _ k\downarrow 0$, which proves existence. $\square$

The moment condition holds if the densities of the $\nu _ j$ are uniformly bounded in $L^ q(\mathrm{d}V)$ for some $q>1$, by Hölder's inequality and the uniform bounds for the moments of normalized $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions. More generally, a uniform entropy bound suffices. Write $a_ j=\mathrm{d}\nu _ j/\mathrm{d}V$ and suppose $\int _ Xa_ j\log a_ j\, \mathrm{d}V\leq H$ for all $j$. (By proposition 11.38.1, a uniform entropy bound is equivalent to a uniform bound for the dual entropy $H_0(\nu _ j)$ with zero reference defined there.) Choose $\alpha >0$ and $A$ from lemma 5.26.2, so that $\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha v}\, \mathrm{d}V\leq A$ for every $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $\sup _ Xv=0$. The elementary entropy inequality, applied first to the truncations $v_ T=\max (v,-T)$ at levels $T>0$, gives

\[ \alpha \int _ X(-v_ T)a_ j\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \int _ Xa_ j\log a_ j\, \mathrm{d}V +\log \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha v_ T}\, \mathrm{d}V \leq H+\log A. \]

To prove this inequality, apply $t\log t-t+1\geq 0$ to $t=a_ j/q$, integrate against $q\, \mathrm{d}V$, where $q=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha v_ T}/\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha v_ T}\, \mathrm{d}V$ is a probability density, and rearrange. Monotone convergence as $T\to \infty $ proves (12.10.44) with $C=(H+\log A)/\alpha $.

The same entropy bound also gives attainment of the dual supremum along this sequence. Indeed, $t\log t\geq -\mathrm{e}^{-1}$ and, for $K>1$,

\[ \int _{\{ a_ j>K\} }a_ j\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \frac{H+\mathrm{e}^{-1}}{\log K}. \]

This proves uniform integrability of the $a_ j$, and since $d_ j\to R>0$, also of the densities $p k_{T_ j}=d_ ja_ j$. By proposition 12.10.6, every weak cluster limit $T$ of $(T_ j)$ maximizes $D_ p$, and along a subsequence converging to $T$ the probabilities $\nu _ j$ converge to $R^{-1}p k_ T\, \mathrm{d}V$. For each normalized $v$, the function $-v$ is nonnegative and lower semicontinuous, so the lower semicontinuity of its integral under weak convergence of measures passes the moment bound to this limiting probability. These conclusions give no regularity of $T$.

The envelope condition holds if $\Phi _ f$ is bounded. In general, it and the moment bound are additional hypotheses; the tail condition on $f^{1/n}$ alone does not provide them in this argument. The theorem identifies the equation satisfied by each cluster limit. Uniqueness, which gave convergence of the whole family in proposition 12.10.20, is not available here.
