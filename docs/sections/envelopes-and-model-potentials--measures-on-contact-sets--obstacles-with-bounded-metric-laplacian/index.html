16.14 Obstacles with bounded metric Laplacian74F4AD99CB8E

74F4AD99CB8E

The results above show that the Monge–Ampère measure of an envelope is carried by the contact set, but they do not identify the measure there. For obstacles with bounded Laplacian we now compute the measure on the contact set from the second derivatives of the obstacle. Recall from section 10.2 that $L=\mathrm d^*\mathrm d$ is the Laplace–Beltrami operator of the Riemannian metric defined by the fixed Kähler form $\omega $; with our sign convention it is nonnegative, and for the Euclidean metric it equals $-\Delta $. We call $L$ the metric Laplacian. A function $f$ on an open set $U\subset X$ has (locally) bounded metric Laplacian if the distribution $Lf$ on $U$ is a (locally) bounded measurable function.

By the trace identity $\Lambda \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=-Lf/(4\pi )$ of lemma 10.2.12, where $\Lambda $ is the trace with respect to $\omega $, a bound for $Lf$ is a bound for the trace of the complex Hessian of $f$. In local holomorphic coordinates, write $\omega =\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{i,j}h_{i\bar j}\, \mathrm dz_ i\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j$ and let $(h^{i\bar j})$ be the inverse matrix, normalized by $\sum _ jh^{i\bar j}h_{l\bar j}=\delta _{il}$; then (10.2.14), applied with $\omega '=\omega $, gives

B47D0EFD195A 16.14.1
\begin{equation} Lf=-4\pi \sum _{i,j}h^{i\bar j}f_{i\bar j} \end{equation}

for every distribution $f$ in the coordinate domain. For a $\theta $-psh function $u$ the current $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is positive, and positivity turns this trace bound into bounds for all complex Hessian coefficients. A general obstacle has no such positivity, and if $n\geq 2$ its complex Hessian need not be bounded (example 16.14.2). Nevertheless it has square-integrable weak second derivatives (lemma 16.14.4), and on the set where it agrees with a potential that has bounded metric Laplacian, these coincide almost everywhere with the second derivatives of the potential. This gives the contact formula of proposition 16.14.6. The $L^ p$ estimate of proposition 16.14.9 shows moreover that such an obstacle has weak second derivatives in every $L^ p$ with $p<\infty $, so that wedge products of the corresponding weak forms are integrable on all of $X$ (corollary 16.14.11).

Example 16.14.2.74EA4AAF7D98 Let $n\geq 2$ and $x_0\in X$. Choose holomorphic coordinates $z$ centered at $x_0$ in which $h_{i\bar j}(0)=\delta _{ij}$, which is possible after a complex-linear change of coordinates, and choose $0<r_0<\mathrm e^{-1}$ such that the coordinates are defined on a neighborhood of $\{ |z|\leq r_0\} $. On $0<|z|<1$ put

\[ \ell (z)=\log (-\log |z|),\qquad P(z)=|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2,\qquad \phi =P\ell , \]

and $\phi (0)=0$. Write $r=|z|$. Since $\partial _{z_ i}\log r=\bar z_ i/(2r^2)$ and $\partial _{\bar z_ j}(r^2\log r)=z_ j(\log r+\frac12)$, we have

\[ \partial _{z_ i}\ell =\frac{\bar z_ i}{2r^2\log r},\qquad \partial _{z_ i}\partial _{z_ j}\ell =-\frac{(\log r+\frac12)\bar z_ i\bar z_ j}{2r^4(\log r)^2}, \]
\[ \ell _{i\bar j}=\frac{\delta _{ij}}{2r^2\log r}-\frac{(\log r+\frac12)\bar z_ iz_ j}{2r^4(\log r)^2}. \]

For $0<r<r_0$ we have $\log r<-1$, hence $|\log r+\frac12|\leq |\log r|$, and these formulas give $|\nabla \ell |\leq C/(r|\log r|)$ and $|D^2\ell |\leq C/(r^2|\log r|)$ for the real gradient and Hessian, with a constant $C$ depending only on $n$. Moreover $|P|\leq r^2$, $|\nabla P|\leq 2r$, the real Hessian of $P$ is constant, $P_{1\bar1}=1$, $P_{2\bar2}=-1$, and all other $P_{i\bar j}$ vanish. By the Leibniz rule, on $0<r<r_0$,

\[ |\nabla \phi |\leq 2r\ell +\frac{Cr}{|\log r|},\qquad |D^2\phi -\ell D^2P|\leq \frac{C}{|\log r|},\qquad \Bigl |\phi _{i\bar j}-\ell P_{i\bar j}\Bigr |\leq \frac{C}{|\log r|}. \]

In particular $\phi $ is continuously differentiable on $\{ |z|<r_0\} $, with $\nabla \phi (0)=0$, since $|\phi |\leq r^2\ell $ and $\nabla \phi \to 0$ as $z\to 0$; and its pointwise second derivatives off the origin are bounded by $C(1+\ell )\leq C(1-\log |z|)$, hence locally integrable.

These pointwise derivatives are the distributional second derivatives of $\phi $. Indeed, let $\eta $ be a smooth function on $\mathbb R$ with $\eta =0$ on $(-\infty ,1]$ and $\eta =1$ on $[2,\infty )$, put $\eta _\varepsilon (z)=\eta (|z|/\varepsilon )$, and let $\psi $ be a test function on $\{ |z|<r_0\} $. Since $\phi $ is $C^1$, we have $\int \phi \, \partial _ a\partial _ b\psi =-\int \partial _ a\phi \, \partial _ b\psi $ for real coordinate derivatives $\partial _ a,\partial _ b$. Splitting $\psi =\eta _\varepsilon \psi +(1-\eta _\varepsilon )\psi $ and integrating by parts once more in the first term, where $\phi $ is smooth, we get

\[ \int \phi \, \partial _ a\partial _ b\psi =\int \partial _ b\partial _ a\phi \, \eta _\varepsilon \psi -\int \partial _ a\phi \, \partial _ b\bigl ((1-\eta _\varepsilon )\psi \bigr ). \]

The last integral is bounded by $\sup _{|z|\leq 2\varepsilon }|\nabla \phi |\cdot C_\psi (\varepsilon ^{-1}+1)\varepsilon ^{2n}$, which tends to zero, and the first tends to $\int \partial _ b\partial _ a\phi \, \psi $ by dominated convergence.

Choose a smooth function $\chi $ on $X$ with support in $\{ |z|<r_0\} $ that equals $1$ on $\{ |z|\leq r_0/2\} $, and put $f=\chi \phi $, extended by zero. Then $f$ is continuous. On $\{ |z|<r_0/2\} $, (16.14.1) and the estimates above give

\[ \Bigl |\frac{Lf}{4\pi }\Bigr |\leq \ell \, |h^{1\bar1}-h^{2\bar2}|+\frac{C'}{|\log r|}\leq C''\bigl (r\ell +1\bigr ), \]

because $h^{1\bar1}-h^{2\bar2}$ is smooth and vanishes at the origin; the right side is bounded, since $r\ell \to 0$ as $r\to 0$, and elsewhere $f$ is smooth. Hence $f$ has bounded metric Laplacian. On the other hand $f_{1\bar1}=\phi _{1\bar1}\geq \ell -C/|\log r|$ near $x_0$, which is unbounded. Thus the complex Hessian of $f$ is not locally bounded.

We use the Sobolev spaces $W^{k,p}$ of subsection 2.9.9. For an open set $U\subset X$, a function on $U$ belongs to $W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ if it belongs to $W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ in every holomorphic chart of $U$. Since membership in $W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is a local property, lemma 2.9.14(2), applied to the coordinate changes, shows that it suffices to check this in the charts of one atlas of $U$. To see that the property is local, let $D\subset \mathbb R^ d$ be open, covered by open sets $D_1,D_2,\ldots $ (an open cover of $D$ has a countable subcover), and let $v\in W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(D_ i)$ for every $i$. For $|\alpha |\leq k$, the $L^ p_{\mathrm{loc}}$ functions representing $\partial ^\alpha v$ on $D_ i$ and on $D_ j$ agree almost everywhere on $D_ i\cap D_ j$ (proposition 2.5.3(4)), so they define a measurable function $g_\alpha $ on $D$. It belongs to $L^ p_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, because every compact subset of $D$ is covered by finitely many compact balls, each contained in some $D_ i$. Finally, the distribution $\partial ^\alpha v-g_\alpha $ vanishes on every $D_ i$, hence on $D$ (lemma 2.5.2).

Let $(U_ i,\varphi _ i)$ and $(\chi _ i)$ be the charts and the partition of unity that define the norm of $W^{k,p}(X)$. For an open set $U'\subset X$ we put $\| f\| _{W^{k,p}(U')}=\sum _ i\| (\chi _ if)\circ \varphi _ i^{-1}\| _{W^{k,p}(\varphi _ i(U'\cap U_ i))}$. Every $v\in W^{k,p}(X)$ belongs to $W^{k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)$, with a bound: for every holomorphic chart $\psi \colon U_0\to \mathbb C^ n$ and every open set $U'\Subset U_0$,

0304C463D49F 16.14.3
\begin{equation} \| v\circ \psi ^{-1}\| _{W^{k,p}(\psi (U'))}\leq C\| v\| _{W^{k,p}(X)}, \end{equation}

with $C$ independent of $v$. Indeed, let $\eta $ be a smooth function on $X$ with compact support in $U_0$ that equals $1$ on $U'$ (proposition 5.2.6). The charts $\psi ,\varphi _1,\varphi _2,\ldots $ with the partition of unity $\eta ,(1-\eta )\chi _1,(1-\eta )\chi _2,\ldots $ are another admissible choice in subsection 2.9.9, so the norm they define is at most a constant times $\| v\| _{W^{k,p}(X)}$; one of its terms is $\| (\eta v)\circ \psi ^{-1}\| _{W^{k,p}(\psi (U_0))}$, which dominates the left side of (16.14.3).

Finally, the Hilbert spaces $H^ r$, $r\geq 0$, of section 10.2 are contained in $W^{r,2}(X)$, with $\| u\| _{W^{r,2}(X)}\leq C\| u\| _{H^ r}$. Indeed, the charts and the cutoff functions that define $H^ r$ are an admissible choice in subsection 2.9.9, so, up to a constant factor, the norm of $W^{r,2}(X)$ is the sum of the $W^{r,2}$ norms of the products of $u$ with these cutoff functions, written in the corresponding charts. By lemma 10.2.1(2), for $u\in H^ r$ these products have square-integrable distributional derivatives of order at most $r$, and this sum is at most $C\| u\| _{H^ r}$.

The following lemma is the variable-coefficient analogue of lemma 7.13.2.

Lemma 16.14.4.75E3F7AC3FA0 Let $U\subset X$ be open, and let $f\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ be such that its distributional metric Laplacian $Lf$ belongs to $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. Then $f\in W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. More precisely, for open sets $U'\Subset V\Subset U$ there is a constant $C$, depending only on $U'$, $V$ and $\omega $ (and on the fixed charts defining the norms), such that

\[ \| f\| _{W^{2,2}(U')}\leq C\bigl (\| f\| _{L^2(V)}+\| Lf\| _{L^2(V)}\bigr ). \]

In particular, every continuous function on $U$ with locally bounded metric Laplacian belongs to $W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$.

Proof. Fix $c>0$. By lemma 10.2.5(3), applied to functions, on which $\mathcal L=L$, the operator $P=L+c$ is a bijection of the space of distributions on $X$ onto itself, and its inverse maps $H^{-1}$ boundedly into $H^1$ and $H^0=L^2$ (lemma 10.2.1(2)) boundedly into $H^2$. Since no derivative of $f$ is known in advance, we gain regularity in two steps, using two cutoff functions.

For a smooth function $\chi $ on $X$, the Leibniz rules $\mathrm d(\chi u)=\chi \, \mathrm du+u\, \mathrm d\chi $ and $\mathrm d^*(\chi \alpha )=\chi \, \mathrm d^*\alpha -\langle \alpha ,\mathrm d\chi \rangle $, for smooth functions $u$ and real $1$-forms $\alpha $, give

D02E2F9A7872 16.14.5
\begin{equation} L(\chi u)=\chi Lu+[L,\chi ]u,\qquad [L,\chi ]u=-2\langle \mathrm d\chi ,\mathrm du\rangle +(L\chi )u, \end{equation}

where $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle $ is the pointwise inner product of real $1$-forms, extended bilinearly. Since $L$ and multiplication by $\chi $ are formally self-adjoint, the transpose of $[L,\chi ]$ is $-[L,\chi ]$, and (16.14.5) extends to distributions $u$, with $\langle [L,\chi ]u,\phi \rangle =-\langle u,[L,\chi ]\phi \rangle $ for smooth $\phi $.

By proposition 5.2.6, choose smooth functions $\chi _0,\chi _1$ on $X$ with compact supports in $V$ such that $\chi _1=1$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{U'}$ and $\chi _0=1$ on a neighborhood of $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _1$. Extend $\chi _0f$ by zero to a function on $X$. By (16.14.5),

\[ P(\chi _0f)=\chi _0(Lf+cf)+[L,\chi _0]f. \]

The first term lies in $L^2$, with norm at most $\| Lf\| _{L^2(V)}+c\| f\| _{L^2(V)}$, hence in $H^{-1}$ with a comparable norm. For smooth $\phi $, the function $[L,\chi _0]\phi $ is a first-order expression in $\phi $ with smooth coefficients supported in $V$, so

\[ |\langle [L,\chi _0]f,\phi \rangle |=|\langle f,[L,\chi _0]\phi \rangle |\leq C\| f\| _{L^2(V)}\| \phi \| _{H^1}, \]

because the norm of $H^1$ dominates the $L^2$ norms of the first derivatives on compact subsets of the charts (lemma 10.2.1(2)). Hence $[L,\chi _0]f\in H^{-1}$, and the right side of the last identity has $H^{-1}$ norm at most $C(\| Lf\| _{L^2(V)}+\| f\| _{L^2(V)})$. Applying $P^{-1}$, we obtain $\chi _0f\in H^1$ with the same bound.

In particular $f$ has square-integrable first derivatives on the interior of $\{ \chi _0=1\} $, which contains $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _1$, with $L^2$ norm bounded in the same way. Therefore the distribution $[L,\chi _1]f=-2\langle \mathrm d\chi _1,\mathrm df\rangle +(L\chi _1)f$ is a square-integrable function, and in the identity

\[ P(\chi _1f)=\chi _1(Lf+cf)+[L,\chi _1]f \]

the right side lies in $L^2$, with norm at most $C(\| Lf\| _{L^2(V)}+\| f\| _{L^2(V)})$. Since $P^{-1}$ is bounded from $L^2$ to $H^2$, it follows that $\chi _1f\in H^2\subset W^{2,2}(X)$, with this bound for its norm in $W^{2,2}(X)$. Since $\chi _1=1$ on $U'$, this proves the estimate.

Finally, let $\psi \colon U_0\to \mathbb C^ n$ be a holomorphic chart with $U_0\subset U$, and let $U''\Subset U_0$ be open. Applying the preceding argument with $U'=U''$ and some open $V$ with $U''\Subset V\Subset U$, we find $\chi _1f\in W^{2,2}(X)$ with $\chi _1=1$ on $U''$, so $f\circ \psi ^{-1}\in W^{2,2}(\psi (U''))$ by (16.14.3). Hence $f\in W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. The last assertion holds because continuous functions and locally bounded measurable functions are locally square-integrable. $\square$

For the contact formula we work in a coordinate ball. Recall that $D(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)$ denotes the mixed discriminant of Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices, defined in section 5.3 (see lemma 5.3.3 for its basic properties), that $\mathrm dV_{2n}$ is Lebesgue measure in the coordinates, and that $(u_{a\bar b})$, with $u_{a\bar b}=\partial _{z_ a}\partial _{\bar z_ b}u$, is the complex Hessian matrix of $u$ (section 5.2).

Proposition 16.14.6.CB0B256B92D7 Let $U\subset X$ be open. For $1\leq j\leq n$, let $\theta _ j$ be a closed smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $U$, let $u_ j$ be a continuous $\theta _ j$-psh function on $U$ with locally bounded metric Laplacian, and let $f_ j$ be a real Borel function on $U$ that belongs to $W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. By lemma 16.14.4, this holds, for instance, if $f_ j$ is continuous and has locally bounded metric Laplacian. Put

\[ E=\bigcap _{j=1}^ n\{ u_ j=f_ j\} . \]

Let $W\Subset U$ be a coordinate ball, choose smooth real functions $\rho _ j$ on $W$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho _ j=\theta _ j$, and let

\[ A_ j=\bigl ((\rho _ j+u_ j)_{a\bar b}\bigr )_{a,b}, \qquad B_ j=\bigl ((\rho _ j+f_ j)_{a\bar b}\bigr )_{a,b} \]

be the weak complex Hessian matrices, defined almost everywhere on $W$. Then each $A_ j$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere and locally bounded, and on $W$

\[ \begin{aligned} \bigwedge _{j=1}^ n(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j) & =\frac{n!}{\pi ^ n}D(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)\, \mathrm dV_{2n},\\ \mathbf1_ E\bigwedge _{j=1}^ n(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j) & =\frac{n!}{\pi ^ n}\mathbf1_ E D(B_1,\ldots ,B_ n)\, \mathrm dV_{2n}. \end{aligned} \]

In particular the density $\mathbf1_ ED(B_1,\ldots ,B_ n)$ is nonnegative and locally bounded, although the $f_ j$ need not be $\theta _ j$-psh.

Proof. The potentials $\rho _ j$ exist by lemma 6.14.2. By lemma 16.14.4, all $u_ j$ belong to $W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)\subset W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$, so the matrices $A_ j$ and $B_ j$ are defined almost everywhere, and the positive current $T_ j=\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ j+u_ j)$ has coefficients in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

We first show that $A_ j$ is positive semidefinite almost everywhere. For each $\xi \in \mathbb C^ n$ the distribution $\sum _{a,b}(\rho _ j+u_ j)_{a\bar b}\xi _ a\bar\xi _ b$ is positive, because $T_ j$ is a positive current; since it is a locally integrable function, it is nonnegative almost everywhere, by proposition 2.5.3(3),(4) and lemma 2.5.1(2) (its mollifications are nonnegative and converge to it in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$). We apply this to the countably many $\xi $ with rational real and imaginary parts. Outside the union of the corresponding null sets, $\sum _{a,b}(A_ j)_{ab}\xi _ a\bar\xi _ b\geq 0$ for all such $\xi $, hence for all $\xi \in \mathbb C^ n$, because these $\xi $ are dense and a quadratic form on $\mathbb C^ n$ is continuous.

Next we bound the coefficients. By the trace identity (10.2.13),

\[ \Lambda T_ j=\Lambda \theta _ j-\frac{Lu_ j}{4\pi }, \]

which is bounded on $W$, since $Lu_ j$ is locally bounded and $\overline W$ is compact. Since $T_ j=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{a,b}(A_ j)_{ab}\, \mathrm dz_ a\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ b$, the function $\Lambda T_ j$ is the trace $\sum _{a,b}h^{a\bar b}(A_ j)_{ab}=\operatorname {tr}(H^{-1}A_ j)$ of $A_ j$ relative to $h$, as in (10.2.14), where $H=(h_{a\bar b})$. As $\overline W$ is compact, $H\leq CI$ on $W$ for a constant $C$, so $H^{-1}\geq C^{-1}I$; since $A_ j$ is positive semidefinite, $\operatorname {tr}(H^{-1}A_ j)=\operatorname {tr}(A_ j^{1/2}H^{-1}A_ j^{1/2})\geq C^{-1}\operatorname {tr}A_ j$ almost everywhere on $W$. For a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix every diagonal entry is at most the trace, and every entry satisfies $|a_{ab}|^2\leq a_{aa}a_{bb}$, by the positivity of the principal $2\times 2$ minors. Therefore $A_ j$ is bounded on $W$.

The functions $\rho _ j+u_ j$ are continuous and plurisubharmonic on $W$, and they have bounded complex Hessian in the sense of section 7.13. The first identity is therefore the first part of proposition 7.13.4. The functions $\rho _ j+f_ j$ are real Borel functions in $W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(W)$, and adding $\rho _ j$ to both $u_ j$ and $f_ j$ does not change the contact set $\{ u_ j=f_ j\} $. Hence the second part of proposition 7.13.4 gives the second identity. Moreover proposition 7.13.3, applied to $\rho _ j+u_ j$ and $\rho _ j+f_ j$, shows that $A_ j=B_ j$ almost everywhere on $\{ u_ j=f_ j\} $, hence on $E$. Consequently $\mathbf1_ ED(B_1,\ldots ,B_ n)=\mathbf1_ ED(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)$ almost everywhere. This density is nonnegative, since the mixed discriminant of positive semidefinite matrices is nonnegative (lemma 5.3.3(3)), and it is bounded, since $D$ is a polynomial in the entries of the bounded matrices $A_ j$. $\square$

The contact formula involves the weak complex Hessians of an obstacle only on the contact set. Off this set the second derivatives of an obstacle with bounded metric Laplacian may be unbounded, and to give a meaning to the wedge products of the forms $\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j$ on all of $X$ we need more integrability. We now prove that all weak second derivatives of such an obstacle lie in $L^ p$ for every $p<\infty $. This belongs to the $L^ p$ theory of the elliptic operator $L$ on the compact manifold $X$, and we derive it from the Euclidean estimate (2.9.13) by freezing coefficients. We write $C^{1,\alpha }(X)$ for the space of functions on $X$ that belong to $C^{1,\alpha }_{\mathrm{loc}}$ in every chart (subsection 2.9.9). As there, matrices carry the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and for a matrix $A=(A^{\mu \sigma })$ of functions and a function $w$ we write $A:D^2w=\sum _{\mu ,\sigma }A^{\mu \sigma }\partial _\mu \partial _\sigma w$.

Lemma 16.14.7.A64885BC3406 Let $d\geq 1$, $1<p<\infty $ and $\lambda >0$. There are constants $\delta _0>0$ and $C$, depending only on $d$, $p$ and $\lambda $, with the following property. Let $B\subset \mathbb R^ d$ be a ball with center $x_ B$, and let $A$ be a measurable function on $B$ with values in the real symmetric $d\times d$ matrices such that

\[ A(x_ B)\geq \lambda I,\qquad |A(x)-A(x_ B)|\leq \delta _0\quad \text{for }x\in B. \]

Then every $w\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb R^ d)$ that vanishes almost everywhere outside a compact subset of $B$ satisfies

\[ \| D^2w\| _ p\leq C\| A:D^2w\| _{L^ p(B)}. \]

Proof. First let $A_0$ be a constant real symmetric matrix with $A_0\geq \lambda I$, and put $M=A_0^{-1/2}$, a symmetric matrix of operator norm at most $\lambda ^{-1/2}$. For $v\in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R^ d)$ let $\tilde v(y)=v(M^{-1}y)$, so that $v(x)=\tilde v(Mx)$. Then $D^2v(x)=M\, D^2\tilde v(Mx)\, M$ and $A_0:D^2v(x)=\operatorname {tr}(MA_0M\, D^2\tilde v(Mx))=\Delta \tilde v(Mx)$. Since $|MNM|\leq \lambda ^{-1}|N|$ for every matrix $N$, the change of variables $y=Mx$ and (2.9.13) give

\[ \begin{aligned} \| D^2v\| _ p& \leq \lambda ^{-1}|\det M|^{-1/p}\| D^2\tilde v\| _ p\\ & \leq \lambda ^{-1}C_{d,p}|\det M|^{-1/p}\| \Delta \tilde v\| _ p =\lambda ^{-1}C_{d,p}\| A_0:D^2v\| _ p. \end{aligned} \]

This estimate extends to every $w\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb R^ d)$ that vanishes almost everywhere outside a compact set $K$. Indeed, for $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$ the mollifications $w*\rho _\varepsilon $ are test functions supported in the compact set $K'=\{ x:\operatorname {dist}(x,K)\leq 1\} $, their second derivatives are $(\partial _\mu \partial _\sigma w)*\rho _\varepsilon $, by proposition 2.5.3(1), and these converge to $\partial _\mu \partial _\sigma w$ in $L^ p(K')$, by lemma 2.5.1(2), hence in $L^ p(\mathbb R^ d)$, since all these functions vanish almost everywhere outside $K'$. Applying the estimate to $v=w*\rho _\varepsilon $ and letting $\varepsilon \to 0$, we obtain it for $w$.

Now let $A$ and $w$ be as in the statement, put $C_1=\lambda ^{-1}C_{d,p}$, $\delta _0=1/(2C_1)$, and $A_0=A(x_ B)$. Since $|\sum _{\mu ,\sigma }m_{\mu \sigma }m'_{\mu \sigma }|\leq |m|\, |m'|$ for matrices $m$ and $m'$, we have $|A_0:D^2w-A:D^2w|\leq \delta _0|D^2w|$ almost everywhere on $B$, and both sides vanish outside $B$. Hence

\[ \| D^2w\| _ p\leq C_1\| A_0:D^2w\| _ p\leq C_1\| A:D^2w\| _{L^ p(B)}+\tfrac 12\| D^2w\| _ p, \]

and since $\| D^2w\| _ p<\infty $ we obtain the lemma with $C=2C_1$. $\square$

We apply the lemma to complex Hessian operators. Let $U\subset \mathbb C^ n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ be open, with real coordinates $x_{2i-1}=\operatorname{Re}z_ i$, $x_{2i}=\operatorname{Im}z_ i$, and let $(a^{i\bar j})$ be a function on $U$ with values in $n\times n$ matrices such that $\overline{a^{i\bar j}}=a^{j\bar i}$. Since $\partial _{z_ i}=\frac12(\partial _{2i-1}-\mathrm i\partial _{2i})$ and $\partial _{\bar z_ j}=\frac12(\partial _{2j-1}+\mathrm i\partial _{2j})$, the operator $\sum _{i,j}a^{i\bar j}\partial _{z_ i}\partial _{\bar z_ j}$ equals $\sum _{\mu ,\sigma }c^{\mu \sigma }\partial _\mu \partial _\sigma $ with complex coefficients $c^{\mu \sigma }$. For $\xi \in \mathbb R^{2n}$ they satisfy $\sum _{\mu ,\sigma }c^{\mu \sigma }\xi _\mu \xi _\sigma =\frac14\sum _{i,j}a^{i\bar j}\zeta _ i\bar\zeta _ j$, where $\zeta _ i=\xi _{2i-1}-\mathrm i\xi _{2i}$. The operator maps real functions to real functions, because $\overline{a^{i\bar j}\partial _{z_ i}\partial _{\bar z_ j}w}=a^{j\bar i}\partial _{\bar z_ i}\partial _{z_ j}w$ for real $w$. Hence, on real functions,

487F3CA23BA4 16.14.8
\begin{equation} \sum _{i,j}a^{i\bar j}\partial _{z_ i}\partial _{\bar z_ j}w=A:D^2w,\qquad A\xi \cdot \xi =\frac14\sum _{i,j}a^{i\bar j}\zeta _ i\bar\zeta _ j, \end{equation}

where $A$ is the symmetric part of the real part of $(c^{\mu \sigma })$. Thus $A$ is real symmetric, $|A(x)-A(y)|\leq |a(x)-a(y)|$, and since $|\zeta |=|\xi |$, a lower bound $\sum a^{i\bar j}\zeta _ i\bar\zeta _ j\geq \lambda |\zeta |^2$ gives $A\geq (\lambda /4)I$. By (16.14.1), in a holomorphic chart the metric Laplacian acts on real functions as $L=-4\pi \, A:D^2$, where $A$ is obtained in this way from $a^{i\bar j}=h^{i\bar j}$; in particular $L$ has no terms of order less than two.

Proposition 16.14.9.F9586D89B24F Let $1<p<\infty $, and let $f\in L^ p(X)$ be such that its distributional metric Laplacian $Lf$ belongs to $L^ p(X)$. Then $f\in W^{2,p}(X)$, and

F8F2AE7A2F29 16.14.10
\begin{equation} \| f\| _{W^{2,p}(X)}\leq C_{X,\omega ,p} \bigl (\| Lf\| _{L^ p(X)}+\| f\| _{L^ p(X)}\bigr ), \end{equation}

where the constant $C_{X,\omega ,p}$ depends only on $X$, $\omega $ and $p$ (and on the fixed charts defining the norm). In particular, a continuous function on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian belongs to $W^{2,p}(X)$ for every finite $p\geq 1$ and to $C^{1,\alpha }(X)$ for every $0<\alpha <1$.

Proof. We first treat smooth functions and then pass to the general case through the inverse of $L+c$.

Step 1: the estimate for smooth real functions. Let $d=2n$. Fix finitely many holomorphic charts and compact subsets $K_\iota $ of their domains whose interiors cover $X$. By (16.14.8), in these charts $L=-4\pi \, A:D^2$ on real functions, with smooth real symmetric matrices $A$; by compactness there is $\lambda >0$ with $A\geq \lambda I$ on every $K_\iota $. Let $\delta _0$ and $C$ be the constants of lemma 16.14.7 for $d$, $p$ and $\lambda $. Since $A$ is uniformly continuous on a compact neighborhood of each $K_\iota $, there is $r>0$ such that, for every $x\in K_\iota $, the ball $B(x,r)$ lies in that neighborhood and $|A(y)-A(x)|\leq \delta _0$ for $y\in B(x,r)$. Cover $X$ by finitely many such balls $B_\nu $, and take a smooth partition of unity $(\chi _\nu )$ with $\operatorname{Supp}\chi _\nu $ a compact subset of $B_\nu $ (proposition 5.2.6).

Let $f$ be smooth and real, and let $v_\nu =\chi _\nu f$, written in the chart of $B_\nu $ and extended by zero. By lemma 16.14.7, $\| D^2v_\nu \| _ p\leq C\| A:D^2v_\nu \| _ p=(C/4\pi )\| Lv_\nu \| _ p$. By (16.14.5), $Lv_\nu =\chi _\nu Lf+[L,\chi _\nu ]f$, where the commutator involves only $f$ and its first derivatives. Summation therefore gives

\[ S_2:=\sum _\nu \| D^2v_\nu \| _ p \leq C\bigl (\| Lf\| _ p+\| \nabla f\| _ p+\| f\| _ p\bigr ). \]

Here and below the $L^ p$ norms of $f$ and $\nabla f$ on $X$ may be computed in the charts or with the metric of $\omega $; on the fixed finite cover these choices give equivalent norms. Since $f=\sum _\nu v_\nu $, the norm $\| \nabla f\| _ p$ is at most a fixed multiple of $\sum _\nu \| \nabla v_\nu \| _ p$. By lemma 2.9.15, for every $\varepsilon >0$,

\[ \sum _\nu \| \nabla v_\nu \| _ p\leq \varepsilon S_2+C_\varepsilon \| f\| _ p. \]

Combining the last three estimates and choosing $\varepsilon $ small, we can absorb $S_2$, which is finite because $f$ is smooth. This gives $S_2\leq C(\| Lf\| _ p+\| f\| _ p)$ and then $\| \nabla f\| _ p\leq C(\| Lf\| _ p+\| f\| _ p)$. Since the norm of $W^{2,p}(X)$ may be computed, up to equivalence, with the charts of the balls $B_\nu $ and the partition of unity $(\chi _\nu )$ (subsection 2.9.9), this proves (16.14.10) for smooth real $f$.

Step 2: an $L^ p$ bound for the inverse. Fix $c>0$ and put $P=L+c$. For a smooth real function $\psi $, lemma 10.2.5(3) gives a unique distributional solution $u=P^{-1}\psi $, which lies in every $H^ r$ and is therefore smooth, by lemma 10.2.1(3); it is real since $P$ is a real operator. For $\eta >0$ put

\[ \phi _\eta (s)=s(s^2+\eta )^{(p-2)/2}, \qquad \phi _\eta '(s)= \bigl ((p-1)s^2+\eta \bigr )(s^2+\eta )^{(p-4)/2}\geq 0, \qquad s\in \mathbb R. \]

The function $\phi _\eta $ is smooth, so we may multiply the equation $Lu+cu=\psi $ by $\phi _\eta (u)$ and integrate. Since $\mathrm d^*$ is the formal adjoint of $\mathrm d$, we have $\int _ X(Lu)\phi _\eta (u)\, \mathrm dV=\int _ X\phi _\eta '(u)|\mathrm du|^2\, \mathrm dV\geq 0$, and therefore

\[ c\int _ Xu\phi _\eta (u)\, \mathrm dV\leq \int _ X\psi \phi _\eta (u)\, \mathrm dV. \]

As $\eta \downarrow 0$, $u\phi _\eta (u)\to |u|^ p$ and $\phi _\eta (u)\to u|u|^{p-2}$ pointwise, the latter being $0$ where $u=0$. For $p\geq 2$ and $\eta \leq 1$ both functions are bounded by $(1+u^2)^{p/2}$, which is bounded since $u$ is continuous on the compact manifold $X$; for $1<p<2$ we have $(u^2+\eta )^{(p-2)/2}\leq |u|^{p-2}$ where $u\neq 0$, so they are bounded by $|u|^ p$ and $|u|^{p-1}$. Dominated convergence and Hölder's inequality therefore give $c\| u\| _ p^ p\leq \| \psi \| _ p\| u\| _ p^{p-1}$, that is (if $u\neq 0$; otherwise there is nothing to prove),

\[ \| P^{-1}\psi \| _ p\leq c^{-1}\| \psi \| _ p. \]

Applying Step 1 to $u$, for which $Lu=\psi -cu$, we conclude that $\| P^{-1}\psi \| _{W^{2,p}}\leq C\| \psi \| _ p$.

Step 3: the general case. Let $f$ be real, put $\psi =Lf+cf\in L^ p(X)$, and choose smooth real functions $\psi _ j\to \psi $ in $L^ p(X)$, using chart cutoffs and mollification (lemma 2.5.1). By Step 2 the solutions $u_ j=P^{-1}\psi _ j$ form a Cauchy sequence in $W^{2,p}(X)$. This space is complete: in each chart the derivatives of order at most two converge in $L^ p$, and testing against smooth functions identifies the limits with the weak derivatives of the limit function. Let $u$ be the limit. Then $Pu_ j\to Pu$ in the sense of distributions, so $Pu=\psi $. Thus $P(u-f)=0$, and since $P$ is injective on distributions (lemma 10.2.5(3)), $u=f$. Passing to the limit in the estimate of Step 2 gives $\| f\| _{W^{2,p}}\leq C\| \psi \| _ p\leq C(\| Lf\| _ p+\| f\| _ p)$. For complex $f$ we apply this to the real and imaginary parts, since $L$ is a real operator.

Step 4: continuous functions with bounded Laplacian. If $f$ is continuous and $Lf$ is bounded, then $f$ and $Lf$ lie in every $L^ p(X)$ with $p<\infty $, which proves the assertion for $1<p<\infty $; the case $p=1$ follows from the case $p=2$, since $X$ has finite volume. Given $0<\alpha <1$, choose $p>d/(1-\alpha )$. In every chart, $f$ belongs to $W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ by (16.14.3), so Morrey's inequality (lemma 2.9.16) gives a representative of $f$ in $C^{1,1-d/p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, hence in $C^{1,\alpha }_{\mathrm{loc}}$. This representative agrees with the continuous function $f$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere. $\square$

Consequently the Hessian forms of obstacles with bounded metric Laplacian can be multiplied on all of $X$, the products being taken pointwise almost everywhere.

Corollary 16.14.11.E3B684F226D1 Let $\theta _1,\ldots ,\theta _ n$ be closed smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$, and let $f_1,\ldots ,f_ n$ be continuous real functions on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian. Then each $\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j$ is a real $(1,1)$-form whose coefficients, computed from the weak second derivatives of $f_ j$, lie in $L^ p$ for every $p<\infty $. Their pointwise wedge product is defined almost everywhere and satisfies

\[ \bigwedge _{j=1}^ n(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j) =F\, \mathrm dV,\qquad F\in L^ q(X)\quad \text{for every }1\leq q<\infty ; \]

in particular it is a finite signed Radon measure. On a coordinate ball, with smooth real $\rho _ j$ such that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho _ j=\theta _ j$, it equals $n!\pi ^{-n}D(B_1,\ldots ,B_ n)\, \mathrm dV_{2n}$, where $B_ j=((\rho _ j+f_ j)_{a\bar b})_{a,b}$. If moreover $u_ j$ are continuous $\theta _ j$-psh functions on $X$ with bounded metric Laplacian, then on $E=\bigcap _ j\{ u_ j=f_ j\} $ this signed measure agrees with the positive measure $\mathbf1_ E\bigwedge _ j(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)$ of proposition 16.14.6.

Proof. By proposition 16.14.9 and (16.14.3), every $f_ j$ belongs to $W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ in every chart, for every finite $p$. In a chart, the current $\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j$ is therefore given by integration against the form $\theta _ j+\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{a,b}(f_ j)_{a\bar b}\, \mathrm dz_ a\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ b$, where the $(f_ j)_{a\bar b}$ are the weak derivatives, as in (5.2.9); its coefficients lie in $L^ p_{\mathrm{loc}}$. On the overlap of two charts, the forms obtained in the two charts represent the same current. For a current with locally integrable coefficients, (5.3.6), in which $\delta $ is the Jacobian determinant relating the two Lebesgue measures, says that the coefficients in the two charts are related, as distributions and hence almost everywhere (proposition 2.5.3(4)), by the pointwise change of variables of forms. Thus the two forms agree almost everywhere as pointwise forms. Hence the pointwise wedge product of the forms $\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j$ is a well-defined $(n,n)$-form, almost everywhere on $X$, and we write it as $F\, \mathrm dV$.

A coefficient of the wedge product in a chart is a sum of products of $n$ coefficients of the factors and of smooth functions; by Hölder's inequality, with each factor in $L^{nq}$ on compact subsets of the chart, these products lie in $L^ q$ there, for every $1\leq q<\infty $. Covering $X$ by finitely many compact subsets of charts, we obtain $F\in L^ q(X)$. A real function in $L^1(X)$ defines a finite signed Radon measure through its positive and negative parts.

On a coordinate ball, $\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ j+f_ j)=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{a,b}(B_ j)_{ab}\, \mathrm dz_ a\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ b$ almost everywhere, and the identity $\omega _{A_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \omega _{A_ n}=D(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)\, \omega _ I^ n$ of lemma 5.3.3(2), applied pointwise with $A_ j=B_ j/(2\pi )$, together with $\omega _ I^ n=n!\, 2^ n\, \mathrm dV_{2n}$, gives $\bigwedge _ j(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_ j)=n!\pi ^{-n}D(B_1,\ldots ,B_ n)\, \mathrm dV_{2n}$. Finally, on every coordinate ball $W$ the second identity of proposition 16.14.6, applied with $U=X$, shows that $\mathbf1_ E\bigwedge _ j(\theta _ j+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)=\mathbf1_ EF\, \mathrm dV$ on $W$. Since coordinate balls cover $X$, the two measures agree on $X$. $\square$
