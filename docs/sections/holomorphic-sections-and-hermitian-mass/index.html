19.8 Holomorphic sections and Hermitian mass725C2F97B643

725C2F97B643

On a projective manifold, holomorphic sections of line bundles connect singularities with geometry: a singular weight selects the sections whose vanishing compensates for its poles, and the growth of the selected section spaces is governed by Monge–Ampère masses. On a compact Hermitian manifold the curvature of a Hermitian line bundle is still closed, whereas the background form of the Monge–Ampère operator need not be. This part of the book asks what section spaces, and the geometric models attached to sections and singularities, can still detect. Gauduchon degrees often take the place of intersection numbers: they bound the vanishing orders of sections, the valuations along a flag and the divisorial parts of positive currents on modifications.

Two kinds of geometric models appear. A complete flag turns vanishing orders into lattice points and section growth into the volume of a convex body, and the elliptic fibration of a Hopf manifold over projective space reduces its sections and its descended potentials to the base. Compact modifications carry the divisorial part of a singularity: on each model, the divisorial Lelong numbers or trace numbers sum to a closed positive current, and subtracting it from the pulled-back background gives residual classes that form b-divisor classes. Finally, positive Hermitian perturbations of a background organize multiplier-ideal envelopes into compatible systems; the limit of such a system in the original background need not recover it.

In this chapter, $X$ is a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, $L$ is a holomorphic line bundle on $X$ with a smooth Hermitian metric $h$, $u\in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$ is a fixed weight, and $\mathrm{d}V$ is a smooth positive volume form. In a local holomorphic frame $e$ of $L$, write $|e|_ h^2=\mathrm{e}^{-\chi }$. The form $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $ does not depend on the frame; it is the smooth closed curvature form of $h$.

The weight selects the global sections of $L^ m$ that are square integrable against $h^ m\mathrm{e}^{-mu}$, and the Bergman kernels of these finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces produce closed positive currents in the curvature class of $L$. Pairing the Poincaré–Lelong formula with a Gauduchon metric bounds the vanishing that a nonzero section can carry; on a submanifold, this gives a necessary condition for realizing the trace data of section 20.3 by restricted sections. When $X$ is a curve, $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$ and $u$ has finitely many logarithmic poles, Riemann–Roch identifies the leading growth of the section spaces with the extended Monge–Ampère mass of $u$. In higher dimensions this identification depends on the background. Homogeneous sections on a Hopf manifold grow only like the sections on its projective base, and a potential of infinite Hermitian mass with trivial multiplier ideals leaves every section space unchanged. Section counts therefore cannot recover a mass computed in a Hermitian background unrelated to the curvature of $L$.

19.8.1 Weighted sections and Bergman currents5F8226DC1A97

The multiplier ideals of $mu$ determine which sections of $L^ m$ have finite weighted $L^2$ norm. Evaluation at points of the resulting Hilbert space defines a Bergman kernel, and its extremal characterization turns comparisons of norms into comparisons of the associated potentials.

For every positive integer $m$, set

\[ \mathcal H_ m(u)=H^0(X,L^ m\otimes \mathcal I(mu)),\qquad \| s\| _{m,u}^2=\int _ X|s|_{h^ m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mu}\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

The integrand is defined off the pole set $\{ u=-\infty \} $, which has volume zero. A section $s\in H^0(X,L^ m)$ belongs to $\mathcal H_ m(u)$ if and only if $\| s\| _{m,u}<\infty $. Indeed, if the local coefficients of $s$ lie in $\mathcal I(mu)$, the integrand is locally integrable, and a finite covering of the compact manifold $X$ gives a finite integral; conversely, a finite integral gives local integrability on every coordinate ball. Thus $\mathcal H_ m(u)$, a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $H^0(X,L^ m)$, is a Hilbert space for this norm. Write $N_ m=\dim \mathcal H_ m(u)$.

Let $v\in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$. If $u\preceq _{\mathcal I}v$, that is, $\mathcal I(ku)\subseteq \mathcal I(kv)$ for every real $k>0$ (section 5.33), then $\mathcal H_ m(u)\subseteq \mathcal H_ m(v)$ for every $m$. In particular, weights of the same ordinary singularity type (definition 16.7.1) have the same section spaces, by proposition 5.33.2. When the two weights differ by a bounded function, their Hilbert norms are also comparable. Below, $\| s\| _{m,v,\mathrm{d}V'}$ denotes the norm defined with $v$ and $\mathrm{d}V'$ in place of $u$ and $\mathrm{d}V$.

Proposition 19.8.2.F2503E6C53F2 Let $v\in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)$, let $D,E\geq 0$, and let $F\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$. Suppose that $|u-v|\leq D$ outside the union of the pole sets of $u$ and $v$, and that $\mathrm{d}V'=\mathrm{e}^ F \mathrm{d}V$ with $|F|\leq E$. Then $u$ and $v$ have the same section spaces, and for every positive integer $m$ and every $s$ in the common space $\mathcal H_ m(u)=\mathcal H_ m(v)$,

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-mD-E}\| s\| _{m,u}^2 \leq \| s\| _{m,v,\mathrm{d}V'}^2 \leq \mathrm{e}^{mD+E}\| s\| _{m,u}^2. \]

Proof. Off the pole sets, the integrand of $\| s\| _{m,v,\mathrm{d}V'}^2$ is that of $\| s\| _{m,u}^2$ multiplied by $\mathrm{e}^{-m(v-u)+F}$, a function with values between $\mathrm{e}^{-mD-E}$ and $\mathrm{e}^{mD+E}$. Hence one norm is finite if and only if the other is, so the section spaces coincide, and integration gives both estimates. $\square$

Fix $m\geq 1$ and choose an orthonormal basis $s_1,\ldots ,s_{N_ m}$ of $\mathcal H_ m(u)$. Define $K_{m,u}$ on $X$ and $B_{m,u}$ off the pole set by

\[ K_{m,u}(x)=\sum _{j=1}^{N_ m}|s_ j(x)|_{h^ m}^2, \qquad B_{m,u}(x)=K_{m,u}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-mu(x)}, \]

and set $B_{m,u}=0$ on the pole set. When $N_ m=0$, both functions are zero. Since two orthonormal bases differ by a unitary matrix, $K_{m,u}$ does not depend on the choice of basis. We call $\Phi _ m=m^{-1}\log K_{m,u}$ the Bergman potential; when $N_ m>0$, $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ m$ is its Bergman current. For $N_ m=0$, we have $\Phi _ m\equiv -\infty $.

Proposition 19.8.3.9E7A6E9D52A6 If $N_ m>0$, then

\[ K_{m,u}(x)=\sup _{0\neq s\in \mathcal H_ m(u)} \frac{|s(x)|_{h^ m}^2}{\| s\| _{m,u}^2}, \qquad \int _ X B_{m,u}\, \mathrm{d}V=N_ m. \]

Moreover, the Bergman potential $\Phi _ m=m^{-1}\log K_{m,u}$ is not identically $-\infty $, has analytic singularities, and satisfies

\[ \beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ m\geq 0. \]

In particular, the Bergman current $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ m$ is a closed positive current.

Proof. Write a section as $s=\sum _ ja_ js_ j$ and trivialize the fiber of $L^ m$ at $x$ by a unit vector. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, $|s(x)|_{h^ m}^2\leq \| s\| _{m,u}^2K_{m,u}(x)$, with equality when the coefficient vector $(a_ j)$ is proportional to the complex conjugate of $(s_ j(x))$. If $K_{m,u}(x)=0$, both sides of the extremal formula vanish. This proves the extremal formula. Integrating the finite sum defining $B_{m,u}$ gives $\sum _ j\| s_ j\| _{m,u}^2=N_ m$.

Locally $s_ j=f_ j e^{\otimes m}$ with holomorphic functions $f_ j$, so

\[ \Phi _ m=\frac1m\log \sum _ j|f_ j|^2-\chi , \qquad \beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ m=\frac1m\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \sum _ j|f_ j|^2\geq 0. \]

Since $X$ is connected, a nonzero section cannot vanish identically on an open set; hence the sum $\sum _ j|f_ j|^2$ does not vanish identically. The local expression therefore shows that $\Phi _ m$ is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities, not identically $-\infty $. It also shows directly that $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ m$ is closed. $\square$

Write $\Phi _ m(u,\mathrm{d}V)$ when the dependence on the weight and the volume form matters. Under the hypotheses of proposition 19.8.2, and provided that the common section space is nonzero, the extremal formula gives

\[ |\Phi _ m(u,\mathrm{d}V)-\Phi _ m(v,\mathrm{d}V')|\leq D+E/m \]

outside the common base locus. Both potentials equal $-\infty $ on that locus. In particular, the two potentials have the same ordinary singularity type, and for a fixed weight a change of the smooth volume form changes the Bergman potential by $O(m^{-1})$.

A simultaneous change of metric and weight leaves the weighted norm unchanged. Let $a\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$, replace the metric by $h'=h\mathrm{e}^{-a}$, so that $|\cdot |_{h'}^2=|\cdot |_ h^2\mathrm{e}^{-a}$, and replace the weight by $u'=u-a$. Then $h'^ m\mathrm{e}^{-mu'}=h^ m\mathrm{e}^{-mu}$, so the Hilbert space, its norm and the density $B_{m,u}\, \mathrm{d}V$ are unchanged, while

\[ \beta '=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a,\qquad \Phi '_ m=\Phi _ m-a. \]

Consequently the Bergman current $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _ m$ does not change.

19.8.4 Gauduchon degree and vanishingE5AAFD4B6553

The zero divisor of a holomorphic section is effective, and the Poincaré–Lelong formula expresses its integration current as the curvature form of the line bundle plus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ of a global function. Pairing with a Gauduchon metric removes the $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ term, so the size of the divisor is bounded by a degree of the line bundle. Recall that a Hermitian form $\gamma $ is Gauduchon if $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\gamma ^{n-1})=0$ (definition 11.4.1); every Hermitian form on a curve is Gauduchon. Write $h^0(X,L^ m)=\dim H^0(X,L^ m)$.

For a Gauduchon metric $\gamma $ on $X$, define the degree

\[ \deg _\gamma L=\int _ X\beta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}. \]

A change of the smooth metric on $L$ changes $\beta $ by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ for a smooth real function $a$, and the integral of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ against $\gamma ^{n-1}$ vanishes by Stokes' theorem. Hence the degree does not depend on the smooth metric on $L$.

Theorem 19.8.5.1DFDCCEF325D If $\deg _\gamma L<0$, then $H^0(X,L^ m)=0$ for every $m\geq 1$. If $\deg _\gamma L=0$ and $H^0(X,L^ m)\neq 0$, then $L^ m$ is holomorphically trivial and $h^0(X,L^ m)=1$. At degree zero, each nonzero weighted subspace $\mathcal H_ m(u)$ is also one-dimensional.

Proof. Let $s$ be a nonzero section of $L^ m$ and let $D$ be its zero divisor. The function $\log |s|_{h^ m}^2$ is locally integrable, and the Poincaré–Lelong formula gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |s|_{h^ m}^2=[D]-m\beta . \]

Since $\gamma $ is Gauduchon, integration by parts against $\gamma ^{n-1}$ annihilates the left-hand side, and therefore

\[ m\deg _\gamma L=\int _ D\gamma ^{n-1}\geq 0, \]

where the components of $D$ are counted with multiplicities. If $D\neq 0$, the right-hand side is strictly positive, because $\gamma $ is positive on the regular locus of each component. Hence negative degree is impossible. At degree zero we obtain $D=0$, so $s$ has no zeros and trivializes $L^ m$. Every other section of $L^ m$ is then a holomorphic function times $s$, and holomorphic functions on the connected compact manifold $X$ are constant. Thus $h^0(X,L^ m)=1$, and every nonzero subspace $\mathcal H_ m(u)\subseteq H^0(X,L^ m)$ is one-dimensional. $\square$

The degree constrains positive curvature currents in the same way. If $T=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$, the trace identity of proposition 11.5.1 gives

\[ \deg _\gamma L=\int _ X T\wedge \gamma ^{n-1}\geq 0. \]

If the degree is zero, the positivity of $T$ and the strict positivity of $\gamma $ imply $T=0$.

19.8.6 Restricted sections and trace dataB7AD9A465882

Let $Y\subset X$ be a connected compact complex submanifold of dimension $r\geq 1$, and suppose that the generic Lelong number $\nu (u,Y)=\inf _{y\in Y}\nu (u,y)$ vanishes. Recall from subsection 20.3.1 the image ideals $\mathfrak a_ m=\mathfrak a_ m(u;Y)=\mathcal I(mu)\mathcal O_ Y$ and the divisorial trace data $\tau _{Y,u}(E)$ of a prime divisor $E$ on a smooth compact modification of $Y$, defined in (20.3.3) as the limit of $\operatorname {ord}_ E\mathfrak a_ m/m$. Put

\[ V_ m=\operatorname {im}\bigl (\mathcal H_ m(u)\longrightarrow H^0(Y,L^ m|_ Y)\bigr ). \]

The local coefficients of a restricted section lie in $\mathfrak a_ m$, so $V_ m\subseteq H^0(Y,L^ m|_ Y\otimes \mathfrak a_ m)$; the right-hand space can contain sections that do not extend to $X$.

On a modification of $Y$, the pullback of a nonzero restricted section vanishes along each prime divisor at least to the order imposed by the image ideal. Subtracting finitely many of these orders still leaves an effective divisor, which the Poincaré–Lelong formula turns into a positive current. If such sections exist in arbitrarily large degrees, normalized compactness produces a positive current in the class obtained by subtracting the trace data from the curvature class, and pairing it with a Gauduchon metric gives a degree inequality. Below, $c_1$ denotes the first Chern class in real Bott–Chern cohomology (subsection 20.4.9); it is represented by the curvature form of any smooth Hermitian metric.

Theorem 19.8.7.165328A043C0 Suppose that $V_ m\neq 0$ for arbitrarily large integers $m$. Let $\pi :Z\to Y$ be any smooth compact modification, let $E_1,\ldots ,E_ q$ be distinct prime divisors on $Z$, and let $\gamma $ be a Gauduchon metric on $Z$. Then

\[ \deg _\gamma (\pi ^*(L|_ Y)) \geq \sum _{i=1}^ q\tau _{Y,u}(E_ i)\int _{E_ i}\gamma ^{r-1}. \]

Moreover, the real closed $(1,1)$ class

\[ c_1(\pi ^*(L|_ Y))- \sum _{i=1}^ q\tau _{Y,u}(E_ i)c_1(\mathcal O_ Z(E_ i)) \]

is pseudoeffective: it contains a closed positive current.

Proof. Choose an unbounded set of degrees $m$ and nonzero sections $s_ m\in V_ m$, and put $b_ i(m)=\operatorname {ord}_{E_ i}\mathfrak a_ m$. The pullback $\pi ^*s_ m$ is nonzero, and its local coefficients belong to the pulled-back ideal $\mathfrak a_ m\mathcal O_ Z$, so it vanishes to order at least $b_ i(m)$ along $E_ i$. Thus

\[ D_ m:=\operatorname {div}(\pi ^*s_ m)-\sum _{i=1}^ q b_ i(m)E_ i \geq 0. \]

Choose smooth Hermitian metrics on the line bundles $\mathcal O_ Z(E_ i)$, with curvature forms $\beta _ i$, and set

\[ \Theta _ m=\pi ^*(\beta |_ Y)-\sum _{i=1}^ q\frac{b_ i(m)}m\beta _ i, \qquad \Theta =\pi ^*(\beta |_ Y)-\sum _{i=1}^ q\tau _{Y,u}(E_ i)\beta _ i. \]

Dividing $\pi ^*s_ m$ by the corresponding powers of the canonical sections of the bundles $\mathcal O_ Z(E_ i)$ gives a nonzero holomorphic section of $\pi ^*(L^ m|_ Y)\otimes \mathcal O_ Z(-\sum _ i b_ i(m)E_ i)$ with divisor $D_ m$. Equip this line bundle with the metric induced by $h$ and the chosen metrics. One $m$th of the logarithm of the squared norm of this section is a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $w_ m$ on $Z$, and the Poincaré–Lelong formula gives

\[ \Theta _ m+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ m=\frac1m[D_ m]\geq 0. \]

By theorem 20.3.2, $b_ i(m)/m\to \tau _{Y,u}(E_ i)$ as $m\to \infty $ through all integers, hence also along the chosen degrees, so $\Theta _ m\to \Theta $ smoothly. Consequently a single Hermitian form $\Lambda $ on $Z$ dominates all the forms $\Theta _ m$, and every $w_ m$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(Z,\Lambda )$. Normalize $\sup _ Zw_ m=0$. By theorem 5.25.2, a subsequence converges in $L^1$, and its limit $w$ satisfies

\[ \Theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\geq 0. \]

Since $\Theta $ is closed and represents the class in the statement, this positive current proves the asserted pseudoeffectivity.

Finally, pair this current with $\gamma ^{r-1}$, where $\gamma ^{r-1}=1$ if $r=1$. Integration by parts removes $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ because $\gamma $ is Gauduchon, and the Poincaré–Lelong formula for the canonical section of $\mathcal O_ Z(E_ i)$ gives $\int _ Z\beta _ i\wedge \gamma ^{r-1}=\int _{E_ i}\gamma ^{r-1}$. The nonnegativity of the pairing is exactly the stated degree inequality. $\square$

Equivalently, if one of these inequalities fails for some choice of $Z$, $E_1,\ldots ,E_ q$ and $\gamma $, then $V_ m=0$ for all sufficiently large $m$.

The converse of the theorem fails in general. Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathrm{i}\mathbb Z)$ be the square elliptic curve, choose an irrational real number $a$, and let $L$ be the flat line bundle on $E$ with multipliers $\mathrm{e}^{2\pi \mathrm{i} a}$ and $1$ along the two generators: its sections lift to holomorphic functions $f$ on $\mathbb C$ with $f(z+1)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi \mathrm{i} a}f(z)$ and $f(z+\mathrm{i})=f(z)$. The constant metric on $L$ has curvature zero. Take $u=0$ and $Y=X=E$. Then every trace number is zero, and the class in the theorem is zero, hence pseudoeffective. Nevertheless no positive tensor power of $L$ has a nonzero holomorphic section. Indeed, a nonzero section of $L^ m$ would have a zero divisor of degree zero and would therefore be nowhere zero. The logarithm of its norm would be harmonic on $E$, hence constant, so its lift $f$ would be a holomorphic function of constant modulus on $\mathbb C$, and thus constant. Since $ma\notin \mathbb Z$, the relation $f(z+1)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi \mathrm{i} ma}f(z)$ then forces $f=0$, a contradiction.

19.8.8 Bounded weighted sectionsC9CFDB0867F5

The spaces $\mathcal H_ m(u)$ need not be closed under multiplication: the product of two sections with integrable weighted squared norms need not have an integrable weighted squared norm, as the following logarithmic example shows. Requiring the weighted pointwise norm to be bounded rather than integrable gives spaces that do multiply. They form the graded algebra used in section 19.9.

Example 19.8.9.D1A2270A67A3 Let $X=\mathbb P^1$ with affine coordinate $z$, and let $\delta _0$ be the Dirac mass at the origin. Take $L=\mathcal O(1)$ with its Fubini–Study metric, whose curvature form is $\beta $, and let

\[ u(z)=\frac35\log \frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2}. \]

Then $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\frac25\beta +\frac35\delta _0\geq 0$, $\mathcal I(u)=\mathcal O$ and $\mathcal I(2u)=\mathcal O(-[0])$. A section of $L$ that does not vanish at $0$ belongs to $\mathcal H_1(u)$, but its square does not belong to $\mathcal H_2(u)$.

Proof. The curvature formula follows from the Poincaré–Lelong formula $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z|^2=\delta _0$ and from $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log (1+|z|^2)$ on $\mathbb C$. The weight is bounded near infinity and equals $\frac35\log |z|^2+O(1)$ near zero, its only singularity. The one-variable calculation of example 5.33.3 therefore gives the two stated ideals: $\mathcal I(u)$ is trivial, while $\mathcal I(2u)$ consists of the functions vanishing at the origin. A section of $L$ with $s(0)\neq 0$ thus lies in $\mathcal H_1(u)$, whereas $s^2$ does not vanish at the origin and so does not lie in $\mathcal H_2(u)$. $\square$

We return to the general data $(X,L,h,u)$. Define $\mathcal W_0=\mathbb C$ and, for $m\geq 1$,

\[ \mathcal W_ m(u)=\{ s\in H^0(X,L^ m): |s|_{h^ m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mu}\text{ is bounded off }\{ u=-\infty \} \} . \]

These are vector spaces, since $|s+t|^2\leq 2|s|^2+2|t|^2$; they are finite-dimensional and do not depend on the smooth metric, because the ratio of two smooth metrics is bounded above and below on $X$. Since $X$ has finite volume, they are contained in $\mathcal H_ m(u)$. For positive integers $m,k$, $s\in \mathcal W_ m(u)$ and $t\in \mathcal W_ k(u)$, the identity

\[ |st|_{h^{m+k}}^2\mathrm{e}^{-(m+k)u} =\bigl (|s|_{h^ m}^2\mathrm{e}^{-mu}\bigr ) \bigl (|t|_{h^ k}^2\mathrm{e}^{-ku}\bigr ) \]

proves $\mathcal W_ m(u)\mathcal W_ k(u)\subseteq \mathcal W_{m+k}(u)$. Thus $\bigoplus _ m\mathcal W_ m(u)$ is a graded subalgebra of the section ring $\bigoplus _{m\geq 0}H^0(X,L^ m)$ of $L$.

On a smooth projective variety, for big line bundles with metrics of positive curvature current and positive multiplier-ideal volume, the almost-semigroup construction of [Section 10.3, SGPT] works with the integrable section spaces themselves.

19.8.10 Section growth on a curve25780F9C24E0

On a curve, a weight with logarithmic poles imposes vanishing orders at the poles. Integrability rounds the required order down and boundedness rounds it up, so the two resulting divisors differ by a bounded amount, and Riemann–Roch gives the same leading growth for both section spaces.

Throughout this section, $X$ is a compact Riemann surface and $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$. Let $p_1,\ldots ,p_ r$ be distinct points such that $u$ is locally bounded on $X\setminus \{ p_1,\ldots ,p_ r\} $ and, in coordinates $z_ i$ centered at $p_ i$,

\[ u=c_ i\log |z_ i|^2+O(1),\qquad c_ i>0. \]

Set $\ell =\deg L=\int _ X\beta $ and $d=\sum _ i c_ i$. Recall that $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta (u)$ denotes the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 13.14.3.

Theorem 19.8.11.1E6D5AA611CC In this situation $0\leq d\leq \ell $, and

\[ \begin{split} \mathcal H_ m(u)& =H^0\left(X,L^ m\Bigl (-\sum _ i\lfloor mc_ i\rfloor p_ i\Bigr )\right),\\ \mathcal W_ m(u)& =H^0\left(X,L^ m\Bigl (-\sum _ i\lceil mc_ i\rceil p_ i\Bigr )\right). \end{split} \]

Moreover,

\[ \lim _{m\to \infty }\frac{\dim \mathcal H_ m(u)}m =\lim _{m\to \infty }\frac{\dim \mathcal W_ m(u)}m =\ell -d =\int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta (u). \]

Proof. Step 1: the section spaces. A germ of a section of $L^ m$ vanishing to order $k$ at $p_ i$ has weighted squared norm comparable to $|z_ i|^{2(k-mc_ i)}$. As in example 5.33.3, it is locally integrable if and only if $k-mc_ i>-1$, that is, $k\geq \lfloor mc_ i\rfloor $; it is bounded if and only if $k\geq mc_ i$, that is, $k\geq \lceil mc_ i\rceil $. Away from the points $p_ i$ the weight is locally bounded and imposes no condition. Since $X$ is compact, this gives the displayed global identifications.

Step 2: the mass. The positive measure $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ has total mass $\ell $, and it has an atom of mass $c_ i$ at $p_ i$. Subtracting these atoms leaves a positive measure, which proves $d\leq \ell $. This remaining measure does not charge polar sets: after subtraction of the logarithmic singularity, its local potential has a bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic representative, and by proposition 5.28.2 its curvature gives polar sets zero mass. Thus the remaining measure is exactly the extended measure, and its mass is $\ell -d$.

Step 3: growth. If $\ell >d$, the degrees of both line bundles in the display equal $m(\ell -d)+O(1)$ and tend to infinity. Let $g$ be the genus of $X$. By Riemann–Roch and Serre duality, $h^0=\deg +1-g$ once the degree exceeds $2g-2$. Dividing by $m$ proves both limits.

If $\ell =d$, both degrees remain bounded. A line bundle of negative degree has no nonzero section, and one of nonnegative degree $k$ has at most $k+1$ independent sections: the sections vanishing at $k+1$ given distinct points are sections of a line bundle of negative degree, hence zero. Therefore both dimensions are uniformly bounded, and both limits vanish. These arguments include the case $r=0$. $\square$

19.8.12 Homogeneous sections on a Hopf manifold82379C382500

On a scalar Hopf manifold, a holomorphic section of a line bundle lifts to a function on $\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $ with an exact homogeneity property under the contraction. Hartogs extension reduces the section spaces to spaces of homogeneous polynomials, and the invariance of the metric makes the Bergman kernel constant. The resulting growth is of order $m^{n-1}$, reflecting the dimension of the projective base rather than that of the manifold.

Let $n\geq 2$, let $z=(z_1,\ldots ,z_ n)$ be the coordinates on $\mathbb C^ n$, and set

\[ X_ n=(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto \mathrm{e}^{-1}z\rangle . \]

On the covering space put

\[ s=\log |z|^2,\qquad \alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s,\qquad \zeta =\mathrm{d}s\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s,\qquad \omega =\alpha +\zeta . \]

The contraction changes $s$ by the constant $-2$, so these forms descend to $X_ n$. The calculation proving (23.1.3), written in section 23.1 for $n=3$ and with $\beta $ in place of $\zeta $, applies in every dimension and gives

\[ \alpha ^ n=0,\qquad \zeta ^2=0,\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\zeta =-\alpha ^2, \qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-1})=-(n-1)\alpha ^ n=0. \]

Thus $\omega $ is a Gauduchon form. Projectivization defines a holomorphic map $\pi :X_ n\to \mathbb P^{n-1}$ with elliptic fibers, and $\alpha $ is the pullback of the Fubini–Study form normalized to have total mass one. Since $\zeta $ restricts to twice the normalized area form on each fiber, as in the proof of proposition 23.1.4, and $\omega ^ n=n\alpha ^{n-1}\wedge \zeta $, we obtain

\[ \int _{X_ n}\alpha ^{n-1}\wedge \zeta =2, \qquad \int _{X_ n}\omega ^ n=2n. \]

Set $\mathrm{d}V=\omega ^ n/(2n)$, a probability measure.

For an integer $k\geq 0$, let $L_ k$ be the line bundle on $X_ n$ obtained from $(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )\times \mathbb C$ through the identification

\[ (z,t)\sim (\mathrm{e}^{-1}z,\mathrm{e}^{-k}t). \]

Give it the metric $|t|_{h_ k}^2=|t|^2|z|^{-2k}$. Under the identification the two factors change by $\mathrm{e}^{-2k}$ and $\mathrm{e}^{2k}$, so this metric descends. In the frame $t=1$ the squared norm is $|z|^{-2k}$, and the curvature form is $k\alpha $. We apply the constructions of subsection 19.8.1 with $X=X_ n$, $L=L_ k$, $h=h_ k$ and this probability volume form.

Proposition 19.8.13.4ABE18AC08D2 For $m\geq 1$ and the weight $u=0$,

\[ N_ m:=h^0(X_ n,L_ k^ m)=\binom {km+n-1}{n-1}, \qquad B_{m,0}=N_ m. \]

The Bergman potential is the constant $m^{-1}\log N_ m$, and the Bergman current is $k\alpha $. Moreover,

\[ \deg _\omega L_ k=2k(n-1),\qquad \lim _{m\to \infty }m^{-n}N_ m=0, \qquad (k\alpha )^ n=0, \]

whereas $\int _{X_ n}\omega ^ n=2n>0$.

Proof. A section of $L_ k^ m$ lifts to a holomorphic function $f$ on $\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $ satisfying

\[ f(\mathrm{e}^{-1}z)=\mathrm{e}^{-km}f(z). \]

Since $n\geq 2$, Hartogs' theorem extends $f$ holomorphically to $\mathbb C^ n$. In its Taylor series, a monomial of total degree $j$ is multiplied by $\mathrm{e}^{-j}$ under the contraction, so comparing coefficients shows that only monomials of degree $km$ occur. Conversely, every homogeneous polynomial of that degree has the required equivariance. Counting monomials gives the binomial coefficient; when $k=0$, only the constant polynomials remain.

The inner product defined by $h_ k^ m$ and $\mathrm{d}V$ is invariant under the unitary group $U(n)$, so the basis-independent function $K_{m,0}$ is $U(n)$-invariant. It is also invariant under positive radial dilations: every section is homogeneous of degree $km$, and the factor $|z|^{-2km}$ in its squared norm cancels the effect of a dilation. Unitary transformations and positive radial dilations act transitively on $\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} $, so $K_{m,0}$ is constant. By proposition 19.8.3 its integral is $N_ m$, and $\mathrm{d}V$ has mass one. Thus $K_{m,0}=B_{m,0}=N_ m$.

The statements about the Bergman potential and current follow immediately. Since $\alpha ^ n=0$ and $\zeta ^2=0$,

\[ \int _{X_ n}k\alpha \wedge \omega ^{n-1} =k(n-1)\int _{X_ n}\alpha ^{n-1}\wedge \zeta =2k(n-1). \]

The binomial coefficient grows at most like $m^{n-1}$, which proves the normalized limit, and $\alpha ^ n=0$ proves the vanishing of the curvature mass. $\square$

For $n=3$, $X_3$ is the Gauduchon threefold of theorem 23.1.1, on which Hermitian Monge–Ampère masses are unbounded; for $k>0$, the line bundles $L_ k$ nevertheless have positive degree and quadratic section growth. In the notation of example 11.21.4, $L_ k$ is the line bundle $E_ a$ on $X_ q$ with $q=\mathrm e^{-1}$ and $a=\mathrm e^{-k}$. Hence, for every $n\geq 2$ and $k>0$, the complement $\mathbb P(\mathcal O\oplus L_ k)\setminus \mathbb P(\mathcal O)$ of 11.21 has least smooth convexity index two, that is, it is $2$-convex but not $1$-convex in the sense of 11.17. When $k=0$, the line bundle is trivial and the least smooth convexity index of its complement is $n+1$, by the same example.

19.8.14 Section counts and infinite Hermitian massB32BAEE663D4

We return to a connected compact complex manifold $X$ of dimension $n$ and fix a Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$. A weight with trivial multiplier ideals leaves every integrable section space unchanged. When $X$ fails the bounded mass property of definition 13.14.14, theorem 13.14.18 provides such a weight $U\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with infinite extended mass. For the trivial line bundle with constant metric, $\mathcal H_ m(U)$ consists of constants and every Bergman current vanishes. Thus section growth in a closed curvature class does not determine the mass in an unrelated Hermitian background.

Proposition 19.8.15.6400AAE4BA1E Let $\omega $ be a Hermitian metric on $X$ with $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $. There is a potential $U\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ such that, with $L=\mathcal O_ X$ and its constant metric,

\[ \dim \mathcal H_ m(U)=1\quad \hbox{for every }m\geq 1, \qquad \int _ X\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\omega (U)=+\infty . \]

All Bergman currents of $U$ are identically zero.

Proof. Take $U$ from theorem 13.14.18; its extended mass is infinite, and $\mathcal I(tU)=\mathcal O_ X$ for every $t>0$. Global holomorphic functions on $X$ are constant, and the constant function $1$ lies in $\mathcal H_ m(U)$ because $\mathcal I(mU)=\mathcal O_ X$ and $X$ is compact. Hence every weighted section space is one-dimensional, and its orthonormal basis consists of a nonzero constant. Each Bergman potential $\Phi _ m$ is therefore constant. Since the trivial metric has curvature zero, every Bergman current is zero. $\square$
