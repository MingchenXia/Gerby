5.42 Chern-connection regularization001730C9ED64

001730C9ED64

Convolution in local coordinates does not directly produce global approximants with a controlled lower curvature bound. Demailly's regularization instead averages over the fibers of the tangent bundle, using the Chern connection of $\omega $, and a Kiselman-type minimum of these averages controls the loss of positivity. The classical curvature estimate for this minimum holds in a Hermitian background, while the $L^1$ and gradient bounds for the averages are elementary.

Fix $C_0>0$ and $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $-C_0\leq u\leq 0$. Distances, gradients and norms of tensors are taken with respect to the Riemannian metric associated with $\omega $.

For small $t>0$, let $R_ tu$ be Demailly's regularization of $u$ formed with the Chern connection of $\omega $. It averages $u$ over the image of the ball of radius $t$ in each tangent space, with a fixed smooth nonnegative radial kernel of integral one. The map used for this purpose is a smooth realization of the holomorphic part of the formal fiberwise expansion of the exponential map; it need not be holomorphic in the fiber. The following classical estimate controls the Kiselman-type transform of these averages, including its mixed base–fiber terms; see section 25.3 for the sources.

Lemma 5.42.1.D38324EEBF88 There are constants $A,K>0$ and $t_*>0$, depending only on $(X,\omega )$ and $C_0$, such that $R_ tu$ is smooth for $0<t<t_*$,

\[ -C_0\leq R_ tu\leq 0,\qquad R_ tu+Kt^2\downarrow u\quad \text{as }t\downarrow 0. \]

For $0<\delta <t_*$ and $c>0$, the bounded function

\[ \Phi _{c,\delta } =\inf _{0<t\leq \delta } \left\{ R_ tu+K(t^2-\delta ^2)+K(t-\delta ) -c\log (t/\delta )\right\} \]

is quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _{c,\delta }\geq -(Ac+2K\delta )\omega . \]

At every point, the infimum is attained at some $t>0$.

We use only this curvature estimate from the classical theory. The linear term $K(t-\delta )$ is needed in the Hermitian case, where it absorbs the mixed base–fiber terms in the complex Hessian of the regularization. Since $-c\log (t/\delta )$ tends to $+\infty $ as $t\downarrow 0$ while $u$ is bounded, the infimum is attained away from zero. We need neither smoothness of the minimizing radius nor smoothness of the transform across the region where this radius equals $\delta $.

Next we prove two elementary estimates for the averaging operators. They do not rely on any volume identity from Kähler geometry.

Lemma 5.42.2.490E5EB01AE4 For all sufficiently small $t>0$,

\[ \| R_ tu-u\| _{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq Ct^2, \qquad \| \nabla R_ tu\| _\infty \leq C/t. \]

The constants are uniform for $-C_0\leq u\leq 0$.

Proof. Let $z$ be local coordinates on $X$ and $\xi \in \mathbb C^ n$ the fiber variable with respect to a smooth orthonormal frame of the tangent bundle. Write $\rho (|\xi |^2)$ for the fixed radial averaging kernel, normalized with respect to Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{d}\xi $ and supported in $|\xi |\leq 1$. In these coordinates the averaging map $F$ gives

\[ R_ tu(z)=\int u(F(z,t\xi ))\rho (|\xi |^2)\, \mathrm{d}\xi , \qquad F(z,0)=z. \]

The map $F$ is smooth in these variables, and its fiber differential at zero is an isomorphism. Let $R_ t^*$ be the formal adjoint of $R_ t$ with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$, so that $\int _ X(R_ ta)b\, \mathrm{d}V=\int _ Xa\, R_ t^*b\, \mathrm{d}V$. We first claim that

\[ R_ t^*1=1+O(t^2) \]

uniformly. To see this, insert a smooth partition of unity in the variable $z$, each term being compactly supported in a chart on which the frame is defined. For fixed $\xi $, change variables $x=F(z,t\xi )$ on a neighborhood of the support of a term. The inverse map and its Jacobian have uniform Taylor expansions in $t$. The term of order zero in the transformed density is the chosen partition function at $x$, and the term of order one is linear in $\xi $, so it integrates to zero against the even radial kernel. The remaining density is bounded by $Ct^2\mathrm{d}V(x)$, since the second derivatives of all maps, partition functions and volume coefficients are bounded on these finitely many compact charts. Summing over the partition functions proves the claim.

Consequently,

\[ \left|\int _ X(R_ tu-u)\, \mathrm{d}V\right| =\left|\int _ Xu(R_ t^*1-1)\, \mathrm{d}V\right|\leq Ct^2. \]

By lemma 5.42.1, $R_ tu-u\geq -Kt^2$. A real function $a\geq -Kt^2$ satisfies $|a|\leq a+2Kt^2$; applying this to $a=R_ tu-u$ and integrating gives the first estimate.

For the derivative bound, change variables in the fiber to write $R_ tu(z)=\int _ Xu(x)k_ t(z,x)\, \mathrm{d}V(x)$. In local coordinates the smooth kernel $k_ t$ is supported where $|x-z|\leq Ct$ and satisfies

\[ |\nabla _ z k_ t(z,x)|\leq Ct^{-2n-1}. \]

Indeed, the inverse fiber map and its Jacobian have uniformly bounded derivatives, and differentiating the rescaled radial argument introduces at most one factor $t^{-1}$ in addition to $t^{-2n}$. Since the kernel is smooth with compact support, there are no boundary terms. Integration over a ball of volume at most $Ct^{2n}$ gives $\int |\nabla _ zk_ t|\, \mathrm{d}V\leq C/t$, and multiplication by the uniform bound for $u$ proves the assertion. $\square$

The first estimate relies on the cancellation of the odd first-order term against the even kernel, and not on conservation of Monge–Ampère mass.

5.42.3 The general curvature modulus45B2E87FDE47

Let $\theta $ now be any smooth real $(1,1)$-form and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cap L^\infty (X)$. We use the same averaging operators and the same metric.

We first state the classical input for this background. The classical Chern-connection regularization theorem applies to the closed current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ with the smooth, possibly nonclosed, lower bound $-\theta $; the curvature of the Chern connection on the tangent bundle is bounded by a fixed multiple of $\omega $. The theorem gives a constant $K_0>0$ such that

\[ R_ tu+K_0t^2\downarrow u. \]

To state its curvature estimate, use local coordinates $z$ on $X$ and a complex radius variable $w$, and put $t=|w|$. Write $\zeta \in T_ z^{1,0}X$ and $\eta \in \mathbb C$ for the base and radius components of a tangent vector, and set $\lambda (z,t)=t\partial _ t(R_ tu(z)+K_0t^2)$. Then $\lambda $ is nonnegative and increasing in $t$, and there are constants $A,C>0$ and a function $\epsilon _0(t)\geq 0$ with $\epsilon _0(t)\downarrow 0$ as $t\downarrow 0$ such that the complex Hessian of $(z,w)\mapsto R_{|w|}u(z)$ is bounded below by the base form $-\theta -A\lambda (z,t)\omega -\epsilon _0(t)\omega $, evaluated on $\zeta $, minus mixed and vertical error terms bounded by $C(|\zeta ||\eta |+|\eta |^2)$. This is the classical regularization estimate for a smooth lower bound.

Lemma 5.42.4.D0B76A103405 For $K$ sufficiently large, there is an increasing function $\epsilon (\delta )\geq 0$ tending to zero at zero such that, for every small $\delta >0$ and every $c>0$, the function

\[ \Phi _{c,\delta } =\inf _{0<t\leq \delta } \{ R_ tu+K(t^2-\delta ^2)+K(t-\delta )-c\log (t/\delta )\} \]

is bounded and quasi-plurisubharmonic and satisfies

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _{c,\delta } \geq -(Ac+\epsilon (\delta ))\omega . \]

The infimum is attained away from zero. Moreover, $R_ tu+Kt^2\downarrow u$ as $t\downarrow 0$.

Proof. Take $K\geq K_0$ and put $H(z,t)=R_ tu(z)+Kt^2+Kt$. As a function of $\log t$, $H$ is increasing and strictly convex, because $R_ tu+K_0t^2$ has nonnegative increasing logarithmic derivative and the added terms have positive second logarithmic derivative. Its logarithmic derivative $\widetilde\lambda =t\partial _ tH$ is at least $\lambda $.

The complex Hessian of $K|w|$ has a positive vertical term proportional to $K/|w|$. For sufficiently small $|w|=t$, this term absorbs the vertical error and, by the inequality

\[ C|\zeta ||\eta | \leq C' t|\zeta |^2+\frac{K}{8t}|\eta |^2, \]

also the mixed error. After increasing the constants and replacing $\lambda $ by the larger $\widetilde\lambda $, we find that the complex Hessian of $(z,w)\mapsto H(z,|w|)$ is bounded below by the base form

\[ -\theta -(A\widetilde\lambda +\epsilon (t))\omega , \]

where $\epsilon $ is an increasing majorant of $\epsilon _0(t)+C't$.

Since $u$ is bounded, the term $-c\log (t/\delta )$ tends uniformly to $+\infty $ as $t\downarrow 0$, so the minimum occurs on a compact interval of radii away from zero. Strict convexity in $\log t$ makes the minimizing radius unique. At an interior minimum $\widetilde\lambda =c$, while at the upper endpoint $t=\delta $ one has $\widetilde\lambda \leq c$. No differentiability of the minimizing radius is needed.

In more detail, localize near a point of $X$ and fix $a>0$. By continuity of the minimizers and of $\widetilde\lambda $, there is an interval of radii containing the minimizers at all nearby points on which $\widetilde\lambda \leq c+a$. If the minimum is attained at the upper endpoint, keep the upper endpoint $\delta $; an open interval approaching it has the same infimum. Because of the uniform logarithmic term, the lower endpoint can be chosen below all minimizers, and since $\widetilde\lambda $ is increasing, the bound $\widetilde\lambda \leq c+a$ holds throughout the chosen interval. The local Kiselman minimum principle for functions depending only on $|w|$, applied after a local quadratic correction, now gives the lower curvature bound with error $A(c+a)+\epsilon (\delta )$, up to an arbitrarily small error from freezing the smooth base forms. Letting these errors and $a$ tend to zero proves the asserted current inequality and the quasi-plurisubharmonicity. Finally, $K\geq K_0$ preserves the monotone convergence of $R_ tu+Kt^2$ to $u$. $\square$

The classical theorem only gives some modulus $\epsilon $; we do not claim that $\epsilon (\delta )$ is linear in $\delta $. The cancellation and kernel arguments in the proof of lemma 5.42.2 still give

\[ \| R_ tu-u\| _{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq Ct^2, \qquad \| \nabla R_ tu\| _\infty \leq C/t, \]

because the averaging map is unchanged, $u$ is bounded, and these arguments use only the monotone lower bound $R_ tu-u\geq -Kt^2$.

For continuous potentials, the curvature loss of the averages becomes uniformly small.

Lemma 5.42.5.A340B19CD0CC Let $\alpha $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\alpha )\cap C^0(X)$. There are smooth real functions $u_ j$ and numbers $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ such that $u_ j\to u$ uniformly and

\[ \alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\geq -\varepsilon _ j\omega . \]

Proof. Use the Chern averaging operators and the classical estimate stated before lemma 5.42.4, with $\alpha $ in place of $\theta $. For some $K_0,A>0$ and $\epsilon _0(t)\to 0$, put $\lambda (x,t)=t\partial _ t(R_ tu(x)+K_0t^2)$. The estimate with vanishing radius component gives

\[ \alpha +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ tu\geq -(A\lambda (\cdot ,t)+\epsilon _0(t))\omega . \]

The function $\lambda $ is nonnegative, continuous for $t>0$, and increasing in $t$. For fixed $x$, the convex function $s\mapsto R_{\mathrm{e}^ s}u(x)+K_0\mathrm{e}^{2s}$ has the finite limit $u(x)$ as $s\to -\infty $. Its nonnegative derivative must therefore tend to zero, since a positive limit of the derivative would make the function tend to $-\infty $. Thus $\lambda (x,t)\to 0$ as $t\to 0$, and for any sequence $t_ j\downarrow 0$, Dini's theorem gives $\| \lambda (\cdot ,t_ j)\| _\infty \to 0$.

The averaging kernels have mass one and only sample points at distance at most $Ct$ from the base point. Uniform continuity of $u$ therefore gives $\| R_ tu-u\| _\infty \to 0$. Set $u_ j=R_{t_ j}u$, and let $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$ be a decreasing positive majorant of $A\| \lambda (\cdot ,t_ j)\| _\infty +\epsilon _0(t_ j)$. $\square$

5.42.6 Hölder reference approximation and weighted smoothing91A88ECF3F18

The Chern averages preserve a Hölder norm uniformly. Their small lower-curvature loss can therefore be absorbed by rescaling, without changing the limiting potential.

Lemma 5.42.7.3AB7626C8759 If $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap C^{0,\alpha }(X)$ with $0<\alpha \leq 1$, there are smooth $u_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ tending uniformly to $u$, with a common $C^{0,\alpha }$ bound. If $\sup _ Xu=0$, the approximants can also be normalized by $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$.

Proof. Use the Chern averages $R_ tu$ in lemma 5.42.5. For a sequence $t_ j\downarrow 0$ there are $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ such that

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_{t_ j}u\geq -\varepsilon _ j\omega , \qquad R_{t_ j}u\longrightarrow u\quad \text{uniformly}. \]

We also have a uniform Hölder bound. In a coordinate chart with a smooth orthonormal tangent frame, the averaging formula is

\[ R_ tu(x)=\int u(F(x,t\xi ))\rho (|\xi |^2)\, \mathrm d\xi . \]

On a smaller chart, smoothness of $F$ bounds $\operatorname {dist}(F(x,t\xi ),F(y,t\xi ))$ by $C\operatorname {dist}(x,y)$, uniformly for small $t$ and $|\xi |\leq 1$. Integrating the Hölder inequality for $u$ gives the same bound for $R_ tu$. A finite cover and the common supremum bound handle pairs outside a common smaller chart.

Set $u_ j=R_{t_ j}u/(1+\varepsilon _ j)$. Then

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j =\frac{\varepsilon _ j\omega +(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_{t_ j}u)}{1+\varepsilon _ j}\geq 0. \]

These functions converge uniformly with a common Hölder bound. Subtracting their suprema gives the asserted normalization when $\sup _ Xu=0$. $\square$

Lemma 5.42.8.AEDE3F4F53EC Let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )\cap C^{0,\alpha }(X)$, $0<\alpha \leq 1$, and let $\mu $ be a positive measure with $\mu \leq A\omega _ u^ n$. For bounded $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $-M\leq \varphi \leq 0$, the Chern averages satisfy

\[ \| R_ t\varphi -\varphi \| _{L^1(\mu )} \leq C t^{2\alpha /(\alpha +2n)} \]

for small $t>0$. The constant is uniform under fixed bounds for $A,M$ and $\| u\| _{C^{0,\alpha }}$.

Proof. First we check a common lower curvature bound for the averages. The base part of the classical Chern curvature estimate stated before lemma 5.42.4 gives

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ t\varphi \geq -(A_0\lambda (\cdot ,t)+\epsilon _0(t))\omega , \qquad \lambda (x,t)=t\partial _ t(R_ t\varphi (x)+K_0t^2). \]

Its constants are fixed under the stated bounded range. In the local kernel form used in lemma 5.42.2,

\[ k_ t(x,y)=t^{-2n}a\bigl (x,(y-x)/t,t\bigr ), \]

with uniformly bounded first derivatives and compact support in its rescaled second variable. Differentiating with respect to $t$ at fixed $x,y$ and changing variables shows that

\[ \int |t\partial _ t k_ t(x,y)|\, \mathrm dy\leq C. \]

Thus $|t\partial _ tR_ t\varphi |\leq CM$, so $\lambda $ is uniformly bounded and the displayed curvature estimate supplies one fixed lower bound $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R_ t\varphi \geq -C\omega $. Both $R_ t\varphi $ and $\varphi $ also lie in $[-M,0]$.

Apply corollary 5.29.9 to this pair, whose common lower bound has just been established. By lemma 5.42.2, its smooth-volume $L^1$ error is at most $Ct^2$. Domination by $A\omega _ u^ n$ therefore gives

\[ \int |R_ t\varphi -\varphi |\, \mathrm d\mu \leq C\| R_ t\varphi -\varphi \| _{L^1(\mathrm dV)}^{\alpha /(\alpha +2n)} \leq Ct^{2\alpha /(\alpha +2n)}. \]
$\square$

5.42.9 A linear error for a smooth background23459D626290

Throughout this subsection, $\theta $ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form that is big. By theorem 5.36.5 and the remark following it, there is a strict potential $\rho \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, that is, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \varepsilon \omega $ for some $\varepsilon >0$, which is smooth outside an analytic pole set and has a smooth remainder after resolution. Put $\Omega =X\setminus \{ \rho =-\infty \} $. For normalized potentials that are locally bounded on $\Omega $, the next lemma improves the curvature error of lemma 5.42.4 to a linear one.

Lemma 5.42.10.96444124D09C Let $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $\sup _ Xa=0$, and suppose that $a$ is locally bounded on $\Omega $. There are constants $A,K,C>0$ such that the averaging operators $R_ t$ of lemma 5.42.1 satisfy

\[ R_ ta+Kt^2\downarrow a,\qquad \| R_ ta-a\| _{L^1(\mathrm{d}V)}\leq Ct^2. \]

For small $\delta >0$ and $c>0$, the transform on $\Omega $

\[ \Phi _{c,\delta } =\inf _{0<t\leq \delta } \{ R_ ta+K(t^2-\delta ^2)+K(t-\delta )-c\log (t/\delta )\} \]

extends canonically to $X$ and satisfies

E762B356717B 5.42.11
\begin{equation} a-K(\delta ^2+\delta )\leq \Phi _{c,\delta }\leq R_\delta a\leq 0, \qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi _{c,\delta }\geq -(Ac+C\delta )\omega . \end{equation}

At each point of $\Omega $ the infimum is attained at a positive radius. On every compact set $K_1\Subset \Omega $, for sufficiently small $t$, $\| \nabla R_ ta\| _{L^\infty (K_1)}\leq C_{K_1}/t$.

Proof. Step 1: the classical expansion. Apply the classical Chern-connection regularization expansion to the closed current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ and the smooth lower bound $-\theta $. It gives monotonicity and logarithmic convexity: for a constant $K_0>0$, the function $\lambda (x,t)=t\partial _ t(R_ ta(x)+K_0t^2)$ is nonnegative and increasing in $t$. Instead of inferring a rate from the unspecified modulus in that theorem, we estimate the error terms of the expansion directly, using the smoothness of $\theta $.

Choose the auxiliary curvature form to be $A\omega $, with $A$ large enough that the Chern curvature plus $A\omega \otimes \operatorname {Id}$ is nonnegative on every decomposable tensor. Equivalently, after absorbing the normalization constants, the Chern curvature coefficients $R_{j\bar k\ell \bar m}$ in a unitary frame satisfy

\[ \sum R_{j\bar k\ell \bar m}\xi _ j\bar\xi _ k\zeta _\ell \bar\zeta _ m \geq -A|\xi |^2|\zeta |^2 \]

for all tangent vectors $\xi ,\zeta $. Such an $A$ exists by compactness. Pairing this inequality with the nonnegative eigenvalues of a positive complex Hessian bounds its curvature contraction from below by $-A$ times its trace, with no further error term.

Step 2: a frozen quadratic correction. Fix $x\in X$ and a complex radius variable $w\ne 0$, and put $t=|w|$. Write $\xi \in T_ x^{1,0}X$ and $\tau \in \mathbb C$ for the base and radius components of a tangent vector. In a coordinate chart of uniform size about $x$, write $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and choose a quadratic function $q_{x,t}$ with

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_{x,t}=\theta (x)+Bt\beta , \]

where the constant $B$ is chosen so large that $a+q_{x,t}$ is plurisubharmonic on a ball of radius of order $t$ containing the support of the averages at $x$ and at nearby points. This is possible by the bound $\| \theta (y)-\theta (x)\| \leq C|y-x|$ on such a ball. For the Hessian calculation at $(x,w)$, the quadratic function is held fixed; its coefficients and all needed derivatives are uniformly bounded.

Step 3: the error terms. By normalized compactness, $\| a\| _{L^1}$ is bounded. On each fixed larger chart, add one fixed strictly plurisubharmonic quadratic function $Q$ to $a$. For the trace measure $\mu =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a+Q)\wedge \beta ^{n-1}$, the local Lelong mass inequality and a bound for the mass of a fixed larger ball give $\mu (B(x,t))\leq Ct^{2n-2}$. The coefficients of $\partial \bar\partial (a+q_{x,t})$ are controlled in total variation by those of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(a+Q)$ plus a uniformly bounded smooth form. Write $|\partial \bar\partial (a+q_{x,t})|$ for the sum of the total variations of its coefficient measures. The rescaled averaging kernels are supported in $B(x,Ct)$ and bounded by a constant times $t^{-2n}$, so their integrals against this measure are bounded by

\[ C t^{-2n}|\partial \bar\partial (a+q_{x,t})|(B(x,Ct)) \leq C't^{-2}. \]

Thus the terms of the expansion with a coefficient $O(t^3)$ contribute $O(t)$, and the remainder of the expansion of order two is $O(t)$ as well. The mixed and vertical contributions are bounded by $C(|\xi ||\tau |+|\tau |^2)$. By the choice of $A$, the curvature term is bounded below by $-A\lambda (x,t)|\xi |^2$; here the radial term of the same expansion identifies the relevant mass with $\lambda $ up to $O(t^2)$. Subtracting the regularization of $q_{x,t}$ restores $-\theta (x)$: its base Hessian differs from $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_{x,t}$ by $O(t)$, its mixed and vertical Hessians are bounded, and its radial logarithmic derivative is $O(t^2)$. The extra term $Bt\beta $ also costs only $O(t)$. For a real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha $, write $\alpha [v]^2$ for its associated Hermitian quadratic form evaluated on $v$. Consequently, for a fixed constant $C_1>0$, the complex Hessian of $(x,w)\mapsto R_{|w|}a(x)$ satisfies

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_{x,w}R_{|w|}a[\xi ,\tau ]^2 \geq -\theta _ x[\xi ]^2-(A\lambda (x,t)+Ct)|\xi |^2 -C_1(|\xi ||\tau |+|\tau |^2). \]

All constants are independent of $x,t,c,\delta $ and uniform for normalized $a$. In particular, setting $\xi =0$ in the same expansion gives a uniform lower bound for the vertical Hessian. Adding a fixed multiple of $|w|^2$ makes the function subharmonic in $w$, which gives the logarithmic convexity and monotonicity above with a uniform constant $K_0$.

Step 4: the minimum. Take $K\geq K_0$ and add $K(t^2+t)$ to the regularization. The positive vertical Hessian of $K|w|$ absorbs the mixed term by the inequality

\[ C_1|\xi ||\tau |\leq C't|\xi |^2+\frac{K}{8t}|\tau |^2, \]

and it absorbs the remaining bounded vertical error for small $t$. The logarithmic derivative of the corrected function is at least $\lambda $. The Kiselman minimum argument with an upper endpoint, as in the proof of lemma 5.42.4, therefore gives the last inequality in (5.42.11), now with error $C\delta $. On compact subsets of $\Omega $, local boundedness of $a$ makes the term $-c\log (t/\delta )$ dominate uniformly as $t\downarrow 0$, so that the minimum is attained away from zero. As before, minimizers at the upper endpoint are handled without differentiating their radius.

Step 5: the remaining bounds. The other bounds in (5.42.11) follow from the monotonicity of $R_ ta+Kt^2$ and from evaluation at $t=\delta $. In particular, the transform is bounded above near the analytic set $X\setminus \Omega $. The removable singularity theorem therefore extends it across this set, and local freezing and passage to the limit preserve the exact smooth lower bound for its Hessian. Its lower bound $a-K(\delta ^2+\delta )$ also extends, since quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are determined by their values off an analytic set.

The adjoint computation in the proof of lemma 5.42.2 gives $R_ t^*1=1+O(t^2)$. It only needs $a\in L^1$, since

\[ \left|\int _ X(R_ ta-a)\, \mathrm{d}V\right| \leq Ct^2\| a\| _1. \]

Together with $R_ ta-a\geq -Kt^2$, this proves the stated $L^1$ estimate. Finally, near $K_1$ the smoothing kernel only samples a fixed larger compact subset of $\Omega $, on which $a$ is bounded. The kernel derivative estimate in the proof of lemma 5.42.2 then gives the bound $C_{K_1}/t$. $\square$
