18.44 Anisotropic point jets and isolated poles on fibrations44BF9B7D0141

44BF9B7D0141

Vanishing to a given order at a point couples the variables through the total degree, even when the metric weight splits as a sum. On a compact product of curves, this coupling becomes a half-space condition on independent monomial indices, and the transition on each curve factor determines the local law in higher dimensions. An isolated pole on a submersion with connected fibers gives a further application: its multiplier ideal does not descend to the base, although the condition it imposes on pulled-back sections does.

Let $q\geq 2$. For $1\leq j\leq q$, choose integers $r_ j\geq 2$, at least two of them unequal, and numbers $\varepsilon _ j>0$. Put

\[ Y=(\mathbb P^1)^ q,\qquad x=(0,\ldots ,0),\qquad d_ j=r_ j+1,\qquad L=\boxtimes _ j\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(d_ j). \]

In the affine coordinates $z_ j$ and the product frame $e$, put $t_ j=|z_ j|^2$ and define the smooth metric and volume by

\[ \begin{gathered} |e|_ h^2=\mathrm e^{-\phi },\qquad \phi =\sum _ j\psi _ j(t_ j),\qquad \psi _ j(t)=\log (1+t^{r_ j}+\varepsilon _ jt^{r_ j+1}),\\ \nu _ Y=a(t)\prod _ j\mathrm dA_ j,\qquad a(t)=\prod _ j a_ j(t_ j),\qquad a_ j(t)=(1+t)^{-2}, \end{gathered} \]

where $\mathrm dA_ j$ is Euclidean area in $z_ j$. In particular $\nu _ Y(Y)=\pi ^ q$ and $a(0)=1$. Define

\[ H_ j(t)=t\psi _ j'(t),\qquad G_ j(t)=tH_ j'(t),\qquad g_ j(t)=G_ j(t)/t,\qquad \theta =c_1(L,h). \]

The metric extends across infinity: there its weight, in terms of $u=|z_ j|^{-2}$, is $\log (\varepsilon _ j+u+u^{d_ j})$, which has positive curvature at infinity. For $t>0$, $H_ j(t)$ and $G_ j(t)$ are the mean and the variance of the exponents $0,r_ j,d_ j$ taken with probabilities proportional to $1,t^{r_ j},\varepsilon _ jt^{d_ j}$. Hence $G_ j>0$, and $H_ j$ increases from $0$ to $d_ j$. Near zero,

7FB2622EF514 18.44.1
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} H_ j(t)& =r_ jt^{r_ j}+(r_ j+1)\varepsilon _ jt^{r_ j+1}+O(t^{2r_ j}),\\ G_ j(t)& =r_ j^2t^{r_ j}+(r_ j+1)^2\varepsilon _ jt^{r_ j+1}+O(t^{2r_ j}). \end{aligned} \end{equation}

Thus $\theta $ is semipositive, and its rank drops along the coordinate divisors $\{ z_ j=0\} $.

For integers $k_ m\to \infty $ with $k_ m=o(m)$, let $B_ m$ be the density of

\[ V_ m=H^0(Y,L^ m\otimes \mathfrak m_ x^{k_ m}),\qquad \| s\| ^2=\int _ Y|s|_{h^ m}^2\nu _ Y. \]

The ideal $\mathfrak m_ x^{k_ m}$ of the point $x$ imposes vanishing of total order $k_ m$, rather than separate orders in the individual variables. As before, $\Phi $ is the standard Gaussian distribution function.

Theorem 18.44.2.7840E247F9D5 Let $z=z^{(m)}$ be points of the affine chart, with $t_ j=|z_ j|^2$, such that $t_ j\to 0$ and $\mu _ j:=mH_ j(t_ j)$ satisfies $\mu _ j/k_ m\to \alpha _ j>0$, where $\sum _ j\alpha _ j=1$. Set $V=\sum _ jmG_ j(t_ j)$. If $(\sum _ j\mu _ j-k_ m)/\sqrt V\to \tau \in \mathbb R$, then

1BC32B08A00F 18.44.3
\begin{equation} \frac{\pi ^ q a(t)B_ m(z)}{m^ q\prod _ jg_ j(t_ j)}\longrightarrow \Phi (\tau ). \end{equation}

The arguments of the coordinates $z_ j$ are arbitrary. For all sufficiently large $m$,

4219416A5981 18.44.4
\begin{equation} N_ m:=\dim V_ m=\prod _ j(d_ jm+1)-\binom {k_ m+q-1}{q}, \qquad N_ m/m^ q\longrightarrow D:=\prod _ jd_ j. \end{equation}

Moreover $m^{-q}B_ m\nu _ Y\to \theta ^ q/q!$ in total variation.

Proof. The proof reduces the kernel to a probability for a sum of independent variables, one for each factor, and applies the one-variable transition of section 18.42 to each of them. In each variable, the affine coefficient of a global section of $L^ m$ is entire, and its expression in the frame at infinity must be holomorphic, so it is a polynomial of degree at most $d_ jm$. Hence the monomials $z^\ell e^ m$, $0\leq \ell _ j\leq d_ jm$, form a basis of the global sections. A polynomial vanishes to order at least $k_ m$ at $x$ exactly when all its monomials have total degree at least $k_ m$, so the monomials with $\sum _ j\ell _ j\geq k_ m$ form a basis of $V_ m$. Once $k_ m\leq \min _ j(d_ jm)+1$, which holds for large $m$, all monomials of total degree below $k_ m$ are global, and counting them gives (18.44.4).

Invariance under the rotation of each coordinate makes these monomials orthogonal. Their squared norms are $\prod _ jJ_{j,m,\ell _ j}$, where

\[ J_{j,m,\ell }=\pi \int _0^\infty t^\ell \mathrm e^{-m\psi _ j(t)}a_ j(t)\, \mathrm dt, \qquad w_{j,m,\ell }(t)=\frac{t^\ell \mathrm e^{-m\psi _ j(t)}}{J_{j,m,\ell }}. \]

These integrals converge even for $\ell =d_ jm$. Write $K_{j,m}=\sum _{\ell =0}^{d_ jm}w_{j,m,\ell }$, and let $\mathsf L_1,\ldots ,\mathsf L_ q$ be independent random variables with $\Pr \{ \mathsf L_ j=\ell \} =w_{j,m,\ell }(t_ j)/K_{j,m}(t_ j)$ for $0\leq \ell \leq d_ jm$. Then

238CE4ED10CD 18.44.5
\begin{equation} B_ m(z)=\prod _ jK_{j,m}(t_ j)\, \Pr \left\{ \sum _ j\mathsf L_ j\geq k_ m\right\} . \end{equation}

Each factor $(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(d_ j))$, with the metric $\mathrm e^{-\psi _ j(|z_ j|^2)}$ and the volume $a_ j\, \mathrm dA_ j$, satisfies the hypotheses of theorem 18.42.15, and $K_{j,m}$ is its full density. Put $V_ j=mG_ j(t_ j)$. Since $\partial _{z_ j}\partial _{\bar z_ j}\psi _ j(|z_ j|^2)=g_ j(t_ j)$, the full density (18.42.25) gives $K_{j,m}=mg_ j(t_ j)/(\pi a_ j(t_ j))(1+o(1))$. For any fixed $y\in \mathbb R$, apply the radial formula (18.42.33) to the integer order

\[ b_{j,m}=\lfloor \mu _ j+y\sqrt{V_ j}\rfloor +1. \]

This order tends to infinity and is sublinear. The selected kernel of the factor, divided by its full kernel, is $\Pr \{ \mathsf L_ j\geq b_{j,m}\} $, and it tends to $\Phi (-y)$. Hence

\[ \frac{\mathsf L_ j-\mu _ j}{\sqrt{V_ j}}\ \Longrightarrow \ N(0,1). \]

That is, these normalized variables converge in distribution to a standard Gaussian variable. Here $\mu _ j$ and $\sqrt{V_ j}$ serve only as centering and scale; we do not need the exact mean or variance of $\mathsf L_ j$. Since $V_ j/V\to r_ j\alpha _ j/\sum _ i r_ i\alpha _ i>0$, independence gives a standard Gaussian limit for $(\sum _ j\mathsf L_ j-\sum _ j\mu _ j)/\sqrt V$. To see this, use the product of the limiting Gaussian laws on bounded rectangles, and then tightness and the fact that the boundary of a half-space has zero Gaussian measure. Consequently the probability in (18.44.5) tends to $\Phi (\tau )$, which proves the local assertion for every sequence $k_ m\to \infty $ with $k_ m=o(m)$.

For the global assertion we also need the full kernel at a fixed $t>0$, where the formula for shrinking radii does not apply. We suppress the index of the factor and set $F(u)=\psi (\mathrm e^ u)$. Then $F'(u)=H(\mathrm e^ u)$ and $F''(u)=G(\mathrm e^ u)>0$. For $\ell /m$ in a compact subinterval of $(0,d)$, the saddle $s=H^{-1}(\ell /m)$ stays compact, and Laplace expansion gives uniformly

\[ J_{m,\ell }=\pi a(s)s\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }{mG(s)}} \mathrm e^{\ell \log s-m\psi (s)}(1+o(1)). \]

Indeed, the phase in the logarithmic variable has a uniformly positive second derivative there, and outside a fixed neighborhood of the saddle point, convexity and the slopes at the endpoints bound the tails exponentially. For $\ell =mH(t)+O(\sqrt m)$, evaluation yields

\[ w_{m,\ell }(t)=\frac{\sqrt{mG(t)}}{\pi a(t)t\sqrt{2\pi }} \mathrm e^{-(\ell -mH(t))^2/(2mG(t))}(1+o(1)). \]

For indices with saddle points in a compact interval, the loss in the phase, as a function of $\ell $, has second derivative $1/(mG(s))\geq c/m$, which gives summable Gaussian tails. For the remaining indices, fix small numbers $\delta ,\sigma >0$, and bound the norm $J_{m,\ell }$ from below by the integral over an interval of length $1/m$ about $t\mathrm e^{-\sigma }$ or $t\mathrm e^{\sigma }$. If $|\ell /m-H(t)|\geq \delta $, the appropriate choice gains at least $m(\delta \sigma -C\sigma ^2)\geq cm$ in the exponent. Each such weight $w_{m,\ell }(t)$ is then at most $Cm\mathrm e^{-cm}$, and there are $dm+1$ indices. Gaussian summation therefore gives $K_ m(t)/m\to g(t)/(\pi a(t))$.

In the product chart, $\theta ^ q/q!=\prod _ j(g_ j(t_ j)/\pi )\mathrm dA_ j$, and its mass is $D$ because $\int _0^\infty g_ j(t)\, \mathrm dt=H_ j(\infty )-H_ j(0)=d_ j$. The normalized full density $m^{-q}\prod _ jK_{j,m}$ therefore tends to the density of $\theta ^ q/q!$ with respect to $\nu _ Y$ almost everywhere; the coordinate divisors have zero volume. The total mass of the normalized full kernel measure is $m^{-q}\prod _ j(d_ jm+1)\to D$. For the nonnegative densities $f_ m,f$ of these measures with respect to $\nu _ Y$, dominated convergence applied to $\min (f_ m,f)$ gives

\[ \int |f_ m-f|\nu _ Y =\int f_ m\nu _ Y+\int f\nu _ Y-2\int \min (f_ m,f)\nu _ Y\longrightarrow 0. \]

Finally, the difference between the full and the selected kernel is nonnegative and has integral $\binom {k_ m+q-1}{q}=o(m^ q)$. This proves the total-variation convergence. $\square$

18.44.6 Corrected and homogeneous centers39F4BF549DB7

The transition in theorem 18.44.2 is centered at the radii determined by the functions $H_ j$. Replacing them by the radii of the homogeneous leading terms of the weight introduces a shift, which is negligible exactly in a range of orders determined by the largest exponent $r_ j$.

Fix positive numbers $\alpha _ j$ with sum one and a real $\tau $, and set

452067A980A7 18.44.7
\begin{equation} R=\sum _ j\alpha _ jr_ j,\qquad T_ m=k_ m+\tau \sqrt{Rk_ m},\qquad t_ j=H_ j^{-1}(\alpha _ jT_ m/m). \end{equation}

Then $\sum _ j\mu _ j=T_ m$ and $V\sim Rk_ m$, so (18.44.3) holds for every sublinear growing order. The radii $|z_ j|=\sqrt{t_ j}$ are asymptotic to $(\alpha _ jk_ m/(r_ jm))^{1/(2r_ j)}$.

Proposition 18.44.8.443408C4E29A Keep $\alpha _ j,\tau ,R,T_ m$ as above, but use instead the homogeneous radii given by $t_ j=(\alpha _ jT_ m/(r_ jm))^{1/r_ j}$, and put $r_*:=\max _ jr_ j$. For the metrics of this section, the law

\[ \frac{\pi ^ qB_ m(z)}{m^ q\prod _ jr_ j^2|z_ j|^{2r_ j-2}}\longrightarrow \Phi (\tau ) \]

holds for every fixed $\tau $ if and only if $k_ m=o(m^{2/(r_*+2)})$. If $k_ m/m^{2/(r_*+2)}\to c\in (0,\infty )$, the left side instead tends to

\[ \Phi \bigl (\tau +C_\alpha c^{(r_*+2)/(2r_*)}\bigr ),\qquad C_\alpha =\frac1{\sqrt R}\sum _{j:r_ j=r_*} (r_*+1)\varepsilon _ j(\alpha _ j/r_*)^{1+1/r_*}. \]

If that ratio tends to infinity while $k_ m=o(m)$, the limit is $1$.

Proof. By (18.44.1), $\sum _ j\mu _ j=T_ m+\Delta _ m$ with

\[ \Delta _ m=\sum _ j(r_ j+1)\varepsilon _ jmt_ j^{r_ j+1} +O\left(\sum _ jmt_ j^{2r_ j}\right),\qquad V\sim Rk_ m. \]

Since $r_ j\geq 2$, each error term is negligible compared with the corresponding positive correction. Consequently

\[ \frac{\Delta _ m}{\sqrt{Rk_ m}}=(1+o(1))\sum _ j \frac{(r_ j+1)\varepsilon _ j(\alpha _ j/r_ j)^{1+1/r_ j}}{\sqrt R} k_ m^{1/2+1/r_ j}m^{-1/r_ j}. \]

At the critical scale only the indices with $r_ j=r_*$ contribute in the limit. Below it every term tends to zero, and above it a positive term diverges. The Gaussian limit of the normalized sum proved above applies in all three cases; in the last case its tightness gives probability one. Moreover, $a(t)\to 1$ and $g_ j(t_ j)/(r_ j^2t_ j^{r_ j-1})\to 1$, so the two normalizations agree. If $k_ m/m^{2/(r_*+2)}$ does not tend to zero, a subsequence has a positive finite or infinite limit, and the law fails at $\tau =0$. This proves both directions of the equivalence for these metrics. $\square$

18.44.9 An isolated selecting potential on a submersion68C553A45799

Let $\pi :X\to Y$ be a surjective holomorphic submersion from a compact complex manifold $X$, with connected fibers of dimension $f\geq 1$, and put $N=q+f=\dim X$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega _ X$ on $X$. Use $E=\pi ^*L$ with the metric $h_ E=\pi ^*h$, and a smooth positive volume form $\nu _ X$ satisfying

A73831DBE999 18.44.10
\begin{equation} \pi _*\nu _ X=\nu _ Y. \end{equation}

Choose $p\in \pi ^{-1}(x)$ and submersion coordinates $(z,w)$ centered at $p$, in which $\pi (z,w)=z$. Let $\chi _ p$ be a function equal to $\log (|z|^2+|w|^2)$ near $p$, smooth on $X\setminus \{ p\} $, and zero outside a larger coordinate ball. Define the selecting potentials, which depend on $m$,

\[ \varphi _ m=\frac{k_ m+N-1}{m}\chi _ p,\qquad \mathcal I_ m=\mathcal I(m\varphi _ m). \]

As in definition 6.8.5, $\mathcal I_ m$ consists of the germs $F$ for which $|F|^2\mathrm e^{-m\varphi _ m}$ is locally integrable. The selected sections are measured with the smooth norm defined by $h_ E^ m$ and $\nu _ X$.

Theorem 18.44.11.154EDD25787A For all large $m$, $\varphi _ m\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega _ X)$ and $\mathcal I_ m=\mathfrak m_ p^{k_ m}$. Neither $\varphi _ m$ nor $\mathcal I_ m$ is pulled back from $Y$, but pullback gives an isometry of Hilbert spaces and an identity of densities

\[ H^0(X,E^ m\otimes \mathcal I_ m)=\pi ^*V_ m,\qquad B_{X,m}=B_ m\circ \pi . \]

Thus the local laws above hold for every sequence of points of $X$ whose projections satisfy their hypotheses. Moreover $\log N_ m/\log m\to q<\dim X$, and

1D6E0EF28851 18.44.12
\begin{equation} m^{-q}B_{X,m}\nu _ X\longrightarrow \left(\frac{\theta ^ q/q!}{\nu _ Y}\circ \pi \right)\nu _ X \quad \hbox{in total variation}. \end{equation}

Normalizing by $N_ m$ instead gives the limit divided by $D$, a probability measure.

Proof. The function $\chi _ p$ satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ p\geq -C\omega _ X$, because it is plurisubharmonic near its pole and smooth elsewhere. Since its coefficient $(k_ m+N-1)/m$ tends to zero, $\varphi _ m$ is $\omega _ X$-plurisubharmonic for large $m$. A germ of order $\ell $ at $p$ has a nonzero initial homogeneous term, so the integral of its squared modulus over the sphere of radius $s$ is $cs^{2\ell }(1+o(1))$ with $c>0$. Local integrability is therefore equivalent to

\[ \int _0^{s_0}s^{2\ell -2(k_ m+N-1)+2N-1}\, \mathrm ds =\int _0^{s_0}s^{2\ell -2k_ m+1}\, \mathrm ds<\infty , \]

that is, to $\ell \geq k_ m$. Away from $p$ the ideal is the unit ideal. This proves $\mathcal I_ m=\mathfrak m_ p^{k_ m}$. The ideal is proper at $p$ but trivial at the other points of the same fiber, which is impossible for an ideal pulled back from $Y$; likewise, the pole of $\varphi _ m$ occurs only at $p$.

By the maximum modulus principle, every section of $\pi ^*L^ m$, written in a trivialization pulled back from the base, is constant on each compact connected fiber. Local holomorphic sections of the submersion show that the resulting coefficient on the base is holomorphic, and constancy along the fibers makes these coefficients glue. In the submersion coordinates, a pulled-back coefficient depends only on $z$, so its order at $p$ equals its order at $x$. This proves the identity of the selected spaces. Fiber integration and (18.44.10) give

\[ \int _ X|\pi ^*s|_{h_ E^ m}^2\nu _ X=\int _ Y|s|_{h^ m}^2\nu _ Y. \]

Pulling back an orthonormal basis proves the identity of densities. If $b_ m$ and $b$ are the densities, with respect to $\nu _ Y$, of the measures in the total-variation limit of theorem 18.44.2, then the corresponding densities on $X$ are $b_ m\circ \pi $ and $b\circ \pi $, and their $L^1(\nu _ X)$ distance equals $\int _ Y|b_ m-b|\nu _ Y$. This proves (18.44.12). The dimension formula (18.44.4) gives $\log N_ m/\log m\to q$ and the final normalization. $\square$

Volume forms satisfying (18.44.10) exist on every such submersion. For any smooth positive volume form $\mu _ X$, write $\pi _*\mu _ X=b\nu _ Y$. Submersion charts and a finite partition of unity express $b$ as a sum of integrals of smooth compactly supported densities in the fiber variables, and differentiation under the integral sign shows that $b$ is smooth. Since the fibers are nonempty, $b>0$. Thus $\nu _ X=\mu _ X/(b\circ \pi )$ satisfies (18.44.10). For a product $X=Y\times F$, the fiber $F$ may be any compact connected complex manifold, Kähler or not. On a product, both the volumes $\nu _ Y\otimes \nu _ F$ with $\nu _ F(F)=1$ and the nonproduct volumes $W(y,u)\nu _ Y\nu _ F$, with $W>0$ and $\int _ FW(y,u)\nu _ F=1$, satisfy (18.44.10).

For an arbitrary smooth positive volume $\nu '_ X$, the pulled-back norm becomes the norm on the base for the volume $\pi _*\nu '_ X$, whereas the local laws above were proved for the specific volume $\nu _ Y$. Since the selecting potentials vary with $m$, theorem 18.44.11 does not settle 24.10.2 for fixed singularities. Weights with mixed or angular remainder terms, observation points with some $\alpha _ j=0$, and other ideals on the base are not treated here. The lifted measure keeps the chosen conditional fiber volume; it is not a Monge–Ampère measure in the dimension of $X$.
