10.5 Normalization and weak adjoint measures

Definitions, results, and proofs on this subject.

15 Normalization and weak adjoint measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$ with a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$. Function spaces are real, and Lebesgue norms are taken with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$. Until section 15.8, $\theta $ is a smooth semipositive big real $(1,1)$-form and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is a model reference in the sense of (13.26): it is a fixed point of the ordinary singularity envelope $P_\theta [\cdot ]$ of definition 1.19.4, and its envelope $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ in a smaller background is not identically $-\infty $ for some $0<\varepsilon <1$. We write $u\sim \varphi $ when $u$ has the ordinary singularity type of $\varphi $ (definition 1.12.1), and $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta $ for the extended non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 6.1.2; when its total mass is finite, it is the usual non-pluripolar measure, which we also denote by $\mathrm{MA}_\theta $.

In the ordinary type of $\varphi $, the equation $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=c\, \mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V$ determines the constant $c>0$ uniquely, even when it has several normalized solutions. As a function of the logarithmic density $F$, the logarithm of this constant is concave. Its supporting functionals are probability measures that describe the first-order response of the constant to a change of density; for smooth Hermitian equations they are the Gauduchon adjoint probabilities of the linearized operator. The convex dual of the functional controls the concentration of these measures through entropy, and this gives compactness when the density, the reference or the positive background varies. The same measures provide the integral normalizations that single out the limit of the exponential equations as the exponent tends to zero. The scalar functional does not, however, determine the singularities of the solutions: examples with moving divisors have identical functionals but different limiting potentials. On closed projective backgrounds, convex geometry gives quantitative criteria for varying references, including references with nonanalytic poles or degenerate residuals.

15.1 The scalar functional

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The domination principle makes the constant decrease as the density increases, and mixed Monge–Ampère products strengthen this monotonicity to concavity in the logarithmic density. Compactness of the solutions then gives continuity under uniformly bounded $L^1$ perturbations.

For a bounded real measurable function $F$, part (2) of theorem 14.11.1, applied to the bounded density $\mathrm{e}^ F$, gives $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $ and a unique constant $c_\varphi (F)>0$ such that

15.1
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) =c_\varphi (F)\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V. \end{equation}

The normalization scalar is

\[ B_\varphi (F)=\log c_\varphi (F). \]

Since the constant is unique, $B_\varphi $ is well defined; it depends on $\varphi $ only through its ordinary singularity type.

Proposition 15.1.1. Let $F,G\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)$ be real. The functional $B_\varphi $ is decreasing, and for every constant $a\in \mathbb R$,

\[ B_\varphi (F+a)=B_\varphi (F)-a. \]

Consequently

15.2
\begin{equation} -\mathop{\rm ess\, sup}_ X(G-F) \leq B_\varphi (G)-B_\varphi (F) \leq -\mathop{\rm ess\, inf}_ X(G-F), \end{equation}

and $|B_\varphi (G)-B_\varphi (F)|\leq \| G-F\| _\infty $.

Proof. Suppose that $F\leq G$ almost everywhere and, to reach a contradiction, that $c_\varphi (F)<c_\varphi (G)$. Let $u,v\sim \varphi $ solve the two equations. Since $F\leq G$ and the extended measure is unchanged by adding constants, for every constant $a$,

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \leq \frac{c_\varphi (F)}{c_\varphi (G)} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (v+a), \]

and the ratio is strictly less than one. As $u\sim \varphi $, the envelope $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(u)$ dominates a translate of $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$ and is therefore not identically $-\infty $; moreover $v+a\preceq u$. The singular strict domination principle, theorem 14.1.2, gives $u\geq v+a$ for every $a$, which is impossible at any point where both potentials are finite. Thus $B_\varphi (F)\geq B_\varphi (G)$.

Replacing $F$ by $F+a$ and the constant by $\mathrm{e}^{-a}c_\varphi (F)$ leaves the right-hand side of (15.1) unchanged, so uniqueness of the constant proves the translation rule. Finally, $G$ lies between the constant translates $F+\mathop{\rm ess\, inf}_ X(G-F)$ and $F+\mathop{\rm ess\, sup}_ X(G-F)$; monotonicity and the translation rule give (15.2). $\square$

The scalar does not depend on the smooth representative of the background: for a smooth real $h$, replacing $\theta $ by $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ and each potential $u$, including $\varphi $, by $u-h$ preserves the currents and the prescribed ordinary type, and hence leaves the equation and its constant unchanged.

Concavity comes from mixed products. A convex combination of two solutions stays in the prescribed ordinary type, and lemma 6.4.1 bounds its measure below by the corresponding mixed density.

Theorem 15.1.2. The functional $B_\varphi $ is concave on the real space $L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)$: for $F,G$ in that space,

\[ B_\varphi (tF+(1-t)G) \geq tB_\varphi (F)+(1-t)B_\varphi (G),\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1. \]

Proof. Let $u,v\sim \varphi $ solve the equations for $F$ and $G$, and set $H=tF+(1-t)G$ and $D=c_\varphi (F)^ t c_\varphi (G)^{1-t}$. Lemma 6.4.1 and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality give

\[ \begin{split} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (tu+(1-t)v) & \geq \bigl (t(c_\varphi (F)\mathrm{e}^ F)^{1/n} +(1-t)(c_\varphi (G)\mathrm{e}^ G)^{1/n}\bigr )^ n\, \mathrm{d}V\\ & \geq D \mathrm{e}^ H\, \mathrm{d}V. \end{split} \]

The averaged potential $tu+(1-t)v$ has the ordinary type of $\varphi $. If $c_\varphi (H)<D$, its measure dominates the measure of a solution $w\sim \varphi $ for $H$ by the factor $D/c_\varphi (H)>1$. Applying the singular strict domination principle to $w$ and to every constant translate of the averaged potential, as in the proof of proposition 15.1.1, leads to the same contradiction. Hence $c_\varphi (H)\geq D$, and taking logarithms proves concavity. $\square$

For smooth Hermitian data, this concavity agrees with the second-variation formula of theorem 9.7.1.

Uniformly bounded logarithmic densities give the solutions a common lower barrier, and compactness of the solutions identifies the limiting scalar even when the densities converge only in $L^1$.

Theorem 15.1.3. If $F_ j,F$ are uniformly bounded real measurable functions and $F_ j\to F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, then

\[ B_\varphi (F_ j)\longrightarrow B_\varphi (F). \]

Proof. Choose solutions $u_ j\sim \varphi $ normalized by $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$, and write $c_ j=c_\varphi (F_ j)$. If $|F_ j|\leq M$, monotonicity and the translation rule of proposition 15.1.1 give

\[ \mathrm{e}^{-M}c_\varphi (0)\leq c_ j\leq \mathrm{e}^ M c_\varphi (0). \]

In particular, the Monge–Ampère measures of the $u_ j$ are dominated by a fixed multiple of $\mathrm{d}V$, and theorem 14.11.3 gives a constant $C$ such that

\[ \varphi -C\leq u_ j\leq \varphi . \]

From any subsequence, extract a further subsequence with $c_ j\to c>0$ and $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1(X)$. By corollary 14.8.3, the convergence also holds in capacity, $u\sim \varphi $, and the measures converge weakly. On the other hand, the exponential function is Lipschitz on the common range of the $F_ j$, so $c_ j\mathrm{e}^{F_ j}\to c\mathrm{e}^ F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. Therefore

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u)=c\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V, \]

and uniqueness of the constant gives $c=c_\varphi (F)$. Since every subsequence has a further subsequence along which $c_ j\to c_\varphi (F)$, the whole sequence converges, and so do the logarithms. $\square$

15.2 Supporting probability measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

A supporting functional of the concave function $B_\varphi $ describes the first-order response of the scalar to a change of logarithmic density. Monotonicity and the translation rule force such a functional to be minus the integral against a probability measure. We work first on the space $C(X)$ of continuous real functions, with the uniform norm. For $F\in C(X)$, let $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ be the set of Borel probability measures $\nu $ on $X$ satisfying

15.5
\begin{equation} B_\varphi (G)\leq B_\varphi (F)-\int _ X(G-F)\, \mathrm{d}\nu \qquad \text{for every }G\in C(X). \end{equation}

Thus $G\mapsto -\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $ is a supergradient of $B_\varphi $ at $F$. We call the members of $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ the supporting probabilities at $F$.

Theorem 15.2.1. For every $F\in C(X)$, the set $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is nonempty, convex and weakly compact. Every one of its measures is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$.

Proof. The function $C_\varphi =-B_\varphi $ is convex and Lipschitz. Fix $F\in C(X)$. For a direction $h\in C(X)$, the limit

\[ p(h)=\lim _{t\downarrow 0} \frac{C_\varphi (F+th)-C_\varphi (F)}{t} \]

exists and is finite: convexity makes the difference quotients monotone in $t>0$, and their absolute values are at most $\| h\| _\infty $. Clearly $p$ is positively homogeneous. Given another direction $k\in C(X)$, apply convexity at the midpoint of $F+2th$ and $F+2tk$ and let $t\downarrow 0$; this gives $p(h+k)\leq p(h)+p(k)$. Thus $p$ is sublinear.

By the Hahn–Banach theorem, the zero functional on $\{ 0\} $ extends to a linear functional $\ell \leq p$. Since $p(h)\leq \| h\| _\infty $, applying this bound to $h$ and $-h$ shows that $\ell $ is continuous. Monotonicity of the convex difference quotients gives

\[ C_\varphi (F+h)\geq C_\varphi (F)+\ell (h), \]

which is (15.5) once $\ell $ is represented by a probability. Testing the translation rule with positive and negative constants gives $\ell (1)=1$. If $h\geq 0$, monotonicity of $B_\varphi $ gives $C_\varphi (F-th)\leq C_\varphi (F)$ for $t>0$, whereas the inequality just proved gives the lower bound $C_\varphi (F)-t\ell (h)$. Hence $\ell (h)\geq 0$. By the Riesz representation theorem, $\ell (h)=\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\nu $ for a probability measure $\nu $, and $\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)$. Each inequality in (15.5) defines a weakly closed half-space in the compact space of probability measures, so their intersection is closed and convex, hence weakly compact.

It remains to prove absolute continuity. Let $K$ be compact with $\mathrm{d}V(K)=0$, and choose continuous functions $0\leq \chi _ j\leq 1$, equal to one on $K$, whose supports have volumes tending to zero. For any $\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)$, the supporting inequality with $G=F+\chi _ j$ gives

\[ 0\leq \nu (K)\leq \int _ X\chi _ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq B_\varphi (F)-B_\varphi (F+\chi _ j). \]

The right-hand side tends to zero by theorem 15.1.3, so $\nu (K)=0$. By inner regularity of finite Borel measures on $X$, the same holds for every Borel set of zero volume. The Radon–Nikodym theorem now gives $\mathrm{d}\nu =h_\nu \mathrm{d}V$ with $h_\nu \geq 0$ and $\int _ Xh_\nu \mathrm{d}V=1$. $\square$

Proposition 15.2.2. Let $F_ j,F\in C(X)$ with $F_ j\to F$ uniformly, and let $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F_ j)$. Then every weak cluster point of $(\nu _ j)$ belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$. In particular, if the latter set consists of a single measure, $\nu _ j$ converges weakly to it.

Proof. Take a weakly convergent subsequence and fix $G\in C(X)$. In the inequality

\[ B_\varphi (G)\leq B_\varphi (F_ j)-\int _ X(G-F_ j)\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j, \]

the Lipschitz continuity of $B_\varphi $, the uniform convergence of $F_ j$ and the weak convergence of the probabilities allow passage to the limit. The limiting inequality holds for every $G$, so the limit measure belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$. If that set is a singleton, every convergent subsequence has the same limit, and compactness of the space of probability measures proves the final assertion. $\square$

15.2.1 Directional derivatives

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

As $\nu $ runs through the supporting probabilities at $F$, the integral $-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $ ranges exactly between the two one-sided derivatives of $B_\varphi $ at $F$ in the direction $G$. Consequently, differentiability is equivalent to uniqueness of the supporting probability. For $F,G\in C(X)$, write $D^+B_\varphi (F)[G]$ and $D^-B_\varphi (F)[G]$ for the limits of the difference quotient $(B_\varphi (F+tG)-B_\varphi (F))/t$ as $t\downarrow 0$ and $t\uparrow 0$, respectively.

Proposition 15.2.3. For $F,G\in C(X)$, the one-sided derivatives exist, and

\begin{align*} D^+B_\varphi (F)[G]& =-\max _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu ,\\ D^-B_\varphi (F)[G]& =-\min _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \end{align*}

Consequently $B_\varphi $ is Gâteaux differentiable at $F$ if and only if $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is a singleton. Its derivative is then $G\mapsto -\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $.

Proof. The one-sided limits exist and are finite by concavity and the Lipschitz bound. For $t>0$, every supporting probability $\nu $ at $F$ gives

\[ \frac{B_\varphi (F+tG)-B_\varphi (F)}{t} \leq -\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

Conversely, choose $\nu _ t\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F+tG)$ and apply its supporting inequality with the test function $F$; this bounds the same quotient below by $-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ t$. Along a sequence $t\downarrow 0$, the measures $\nu _ t$ have a weak limit point, which belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ by proposition 15.2.2. The two bounds identify the right derivative with minus the maximum. Applying this identity to $-G$ gives the formula for the left derivative.

If $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is a singleton $\{ \nu \} $, the two derivatives coincide for every $G$ and equal $-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $, a continuous linear functional of $G$. Conversely, if the two one-sided derivatives agree for every $G$, all supporting probabilities have the same integral against every continuous function, and the Riesz representation theorem shows that they coincide. $\square$

15.2.2 Generic uniqueness

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

On every affine line in $C(X)$, the concave function $B_\varphi $ is differentiable outside a countable set, so the gap between its two directional derivatives along the line vanishes on a dense subset. Upper semicontinuity of this gap and separability of $C(X)$ turn the one-dimensional fact into uniqueness of the supporting probability on a dense $G_\delta $ subset of $C(X)$.

Theorem 15.2.4. The set

\[ \mathscr U_\varphi =\{ F\in C(X,\mathbb R):\mathcal A_\varphi (F) \text{ is a singleton}\} \]

is a dense $G_\delta $ subset of $C(X)$ with the uniform norm. At every $F\in \mathscr U_\varphi $, the functional $B_\varphi $ is Gâteaux differentiable, and its derivative is minus the integral against the unique supporting probability. If $F_ j\to F$ uniformly and $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F_ j)$, then $\nu _ j$ converges weakly to that probability.

Proof. For $h\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ define the width

\[ w_ h(F)=\max _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\nu -\min _{\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (F)}\int _ Xh\, \mathrm{d}\nu \geq 0. \]

The extrema exist by weak compactness. We claim that the width is upper semicontinuous in $F$. Indeed, let $F_ j\to F$ and choose maximizing and minimizing probabilities at each $F_ j$. Along a subsequence realizing the upper limit of the widths, compactness of the space of probability measures gives weak limits of both choices. By proposition 15.2.2, both limits belong to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$, so the difference of their integrals against $h$ is at most $w_ h(F)$.

Consequently, for every $\epsilon >0$ the set $\{ F:w_ h(F)<\epsilon \} $ is open. It is also dense. If $h=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise fix $F$ and an open ball about it. The function

\[ b(t)=B_\varphi (F+th) \]

is finite and concave on the real line, so it is differentiable outside an at most countable set: its one-sided derivatives are finite and monotone, and the nonempty open intervals between unequal one-sided derivatives at distinct points are disjoint, so each contains a different rational number. Choose a point of differentiability $t$ so close to zero that $F+th$ lies in the given ball. The derivative formulas of proposition 15.2.3 give $w_ h(F+th)=0$, which proves density.

The compact manifold $X$ is metrizable, so $C(X)$ is separable. Choose a countable uniformly dense family $(h_ j)$ in $C(X)$. By the Baire category theorem in the complete metric space $C(X)$, the set

\[ \bigcap _{j,k\geq 1}\{ F:w_{h_ j}(F)<1/k\} \]

is a dense $G_\delta $. At every point of this intersection, all supporting probabilities have the same integral against every $h_ j$. Since they have unit mass, uniform approximation gives equal integrals against every continuous function, and the Riesz representation theorem shows that the probabilities coincide. Conversely, a singleton has zero width in every direction, so the intersection is exactly $\mathscr U_\varphi $. The formula for the derivative follows from proposition 15.2.3, and the approximation assertion from proposition 15.2.2. $\square$

15.2.3 Gauduchon adjoints

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a smooth Hermitian equation, differentiating the scalar identifies the supporting probability with the adjoint probability of the linearized operator.

Theorem 15.2.5. Let $\theta =\omega $ be a Hermitian form, let $\varphi =0$, and let $F$ be a smooth real function. Let $u$ be the smooth solution of (15.1), unique up to an additive constant by corollary 11.4.6, and let $\mu _ u$ be its Gauduchon adjoint probability from proposition 4.8.1; for $n=1$, this is $\mu _ u=(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)/\int _ X\omega $. Then

\[ \mathcal A_0(F)=\{ \mu _ u\} . \]

Proof. Suppose first that $n\geq 2$. By uniqueness of the constant, the normalization scalar of the smooth equation in theorem 9.7.1 is $B_0$, and that theorem gives, for every smooth real $G$,

\[ \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}B_0(F+tG) =-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ u. \]

Let $\nu \in \mathcal A_0(F)$. Testing the supporting inequality at $F+tG$ for both signs of $t$ and letting $t\to 0$ shows that $G$ has the same integral against $\nu $ and $\mu _ u$. Since smooth functions are uniformly dense in $C(X)$, it follows that $\nu =\mu _ u$, and the assertion follows from nonemptiness of $\mathcal A_0(F)$. In dimension one, Stokes' theorem gives $c_0(F)=\int _ X\omega /\int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F \mathrm{d}V$; differentiating its logarithm in the same way yields the stated measure, since $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=c_0(F)\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V$. $\square$

Consequently, in the Hermitian case the supporting probabilities at a continuous $F$ can be approached through smooth data. If smooth $F_ j$ converge uniformly to $F$, then by proposition 15.2.2 every weak cluster point of the Gauduchon adjoint probabilities of the solutions for $F_ j$ belongs to $\mathcal A_0(F)$, and is therefore absolutely continuous. If $\mathcal A_0(F)$ is a singleton, which holds in particular for every $F\in \mathscr U_0$ and under the continuous positive Hessian hypothesis of theorem 15.2.6, these probabilities converge to its unique member, independently of the approximation. We do not know whether $\mathcal A_0(F)$ is a singleton for every continuous $F$, even in this Hermitian setting; the same question is open for the singular references considered above.

For $n\geq 2$, the Hermitian zero-reference case admits a quantitative strengthening: corollary 26.8.3 bounds the densities of all supporting probabilities uniformly in $L^ q$, for $1<q<n/(n-1)$, when $\| F\| _\infty $ is bounded, and also for $q=n/(n-1)$ when $\omega $ has positive lower mass $v_-(\omega )>0$ in the sense of definition 6.5.1. The proof uses the Gauduchon-current formulation of chapter 26, and we do not claim such a bound for an arbitrary singular reference.

A weak linearized operator requires more than a supporting probability. In the same Hermitian zero-reference setting, theorem 26.7.2 represents each supporting probability by a determinant-weighted positive $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed current. Identifying that current with a scalar multiple of a weak power $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{n-1}$ would require a well-defined product and its compatibility with approximation.

A similar representation holds for a functional defined by subsolutions rather than by an equation in a prescribed singularity type. Let $0\leq f\in L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ be nonzero with $f^{1/n}\in \mathcal D_\omega ^0$, the space of densities with uniformly vanishing Gauduchon tails from 14.31.2. Then proposition 26.9.4 describes the supporting probabilities of $F\mapsto \log b_\omega (\mathrm{e}^ Ff)$, where $b_\omega (\cdot )$ is the density threshold of theorem 14.27.2.

15.2.4 Continuous positive complex Hessians

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The smooth recovery theorem extends to a bounded solution whose complex Hessian is continuous and everywhere positive. In this case one can vary the potential in both directions, and the resulting densities force every supporting probability to annihilate the same elliptic operator. The Sobolev range theorem then gives uniqueness.

Theorem 15.2.6. Let $n\geq 2$, let $\theta =\omega $ be a Hermitian form, and let $\varphi =0$. Suppose $F\in C(X)$ and a bounded solution $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ of (15.1) has distributional complex Hessian represented by a continuous form

\[ \sigma =\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0 \]

everywhere on $X$. Then $\mathcal A_0(F)$ consists of one probability $\nu _ F$. It satisfies

\begin{align*} \int _ X L_\sigma h\, \mathrm d\nu _ F& =0\quad (h\in C^\infty (X)),\\ DB_0(F)[H]& =-\int _ XH\, \mathrm d\nu _ F\quad (H\in C(X)). \end{align*}

The derivative is taken in the Gâteaux sense.

Proof. The continuous trace of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ is the Euclidean Laplacian of $u$, up to the fixed normalization. Local constant-coefficient elliptic regularity gives $u\in W^{2,p}$ for every finite $p$. Indeed, subtract the Newtonian potential of a cutoff of this trace. Its second high-frequency derivatives are order-zero multipliers, and the low-frequency kernels are locally bounded; the remaining distribution is harmonic. Sobolev embedding gives a $C^{1,\alpha }$ representative for every $\alpha <1$, which agrees with the quasi-psh representative.

On a coordinate ball choose smooth $\rho $ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \omega $. The radial regularizations of the bounded psh function $u+\rho $ decrease to it, and their complex Hessians converge uniformly on smaller balls. Continuity of bounded products and the finite smooth-background expansion identify $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ with $\sigma ^ n$. For smooth real $h$, the same argument applies to $u+th$, which is admissible for all sufficiently small signed $t$ by uniform positivity. Define

\[ F_ t=F+\log \frac{(\sigma +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^ n}{\sigma ^ n}. \]

Its equation has the same normalization scalar as the equation for $u$, so $B_0(F_ t)=B_0(F)$. The supporting inequality gives $\int _ X(F_ t-F)\, \mathrm d\nu \leq 0$ for each $\nu \in \mathcal A_0(F)$. Since $F_ t-F=tL_\sigma h+O(t^2)$ uniformly, using both signs yields stationarity.

Choose $p=4n+1$ and $q=p/(p-1)=1+1/(4n)$. By corollary 26.8.3, every supporting probability has an $L^ q$ density, since $q<n/(n-1)$. Smooth density in $W^{2,p}$ extends its stationarity to every $W^{2,p}$ test. By lemma 4.9.1, all these densities lie in a one-dimensional space. Their integrals equal one, so they coincide. Nonemptiness is theorem 15.2.1, and the derivative formula follows from proposition 15.2.3. $\square$

15.2.5 Products with a Hermitian factor

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

An explicit product construction exhibits the criterion for densities with an arbitrary continuous modulus. Let $(Y,\kappa )$ be a connected compact Hermitian $k$-fold, $k\geq 1$, with $\int _ Y\kappa ^ k=1$. Denote by $\nu _\kappa $ the smooth stationary probability of $L_\kappa =\operatorname {tr}_\kappa \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$, given by the Gauduchon multiplier when $k\geq 2$; for $k=1$ it is $\kappa $. Write $w$ for the Fubini–Study area of mass one on $\mathbb P^1$. All factor forms and functions below are pulled back to $X=\mathbb P^1\times Y$.

Proposition 15.2.7. Let $a>0$, let $b$ be smooth and positive on $\mathbb P^1$, and let $A$ be continuous and positive there, with $\int _{\mathbb P^1}Aw=a$. For

\[ \omega =aw+b\kappa ,\qquad \mathrm dV=w\wedge \kappa ^ k, \qquad F=\log ((k+1)Ab^ k), \]

one has

\[ c_0(F)=1,\qquad \mathcal A_0(F)=\left\{ \frac Aa\, w\otimes \nu _\kappa \right\} . \]

Proof. The Green solution of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=(A-a)w$ on $\mathbb P^1$ has continuous complex Hessian and is bounded. Its pullback yields $\sigma =Aw+b\kappa >0$, and $\sigma ^{k+1}=(k+1)Ab^ kw\wedge \kappa ^ k$. Thus scalar uniqueness gives $c_0(F)=1$, and theorem 15.2.6 gives a unique support. For every smooth $h$ depending on all variables, the block decomposition

\[ L_\sigma h=A^{-1}L_ wh+b^{-1}L_\kappa h \]

shows that $(A/a)w\otimes \nu _\kappa $ is stationary: the first term integrates to zero by Stokes on $\mathbb P^1$, and the second by stationarity on $Y$ and Fubini. This candidate has a bounded density with respect to $\mathrm dV$. The one-dimensional annihilator of lemma 4.9.1 and probability normalization therefore identify it with the supporting probability. $\square$

Example 15.2.8. There is a non-Kähler threefold with a nonclosed Hermitian background and a positive continuous density whose logarithm is neither Dini continuous nor Hölder continuous of any positive order, but whose supporting probability is explicit and unique.

Proof. Take the Hopf surface $Y=(\mathbb C^2\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto 2z\rangle $ and its unit-volume metric $\kappa =c|z|^{-2}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$, where $c>0$ is chosen so that $\int _ Y\kappa ^2=1$. Put $s=\log |z|^2$, $\alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s$ and $\beta =\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s$, so that $\kappa =c(\alpha +\beta )$. The differentiation in (8.1) applies in this dimension. Here $\alpha $ has rank one, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\kappa =-c\alpha ^2=0$ and $\nu _\kappa =\kappa ^2$. Polar coordinates identify $Y$ with $S^3\times S^1$, hence $H^2(Y,\mathbb R)=0$. A closed positive form would be exact and have positive square integral, contradicting Stokes. Thus $Y$ is non-Kähler, and so is $\mathbb P^1\times Y$, by restriction.

Let $\tau \in [-1,1]$ be the height on $\mathbb P^1$, set $b=2+\tau $, and put

\[ f(t)=\begin{cases} 1/\log (e/|t|),& 0<|t|\leq 1,\\ 0,& t=0, \end{cases} \]
\[ A=a+\varepsilon \left(f(\tau )-\int _{\mathbb P^1}f(\tau )w\right), \quad 0<\varepsilon <a. \]

Since $0\leq f\leq 1$, this is a positive continuous function of mean $a$. Along a normal segment to the equator, $|\tau |$ is comparable to the distance $r$. The oscillation of $A$ is therefore bounded below by a positive multiple of $1/\log (e/r)$. Its Dini integral $\int _0^{r_0}\mathrm dr/(r\log (e/r))$ diverges, and this modulus is larger than every $r^\gamma $, $\gamma >0$, as $r\downarrow 0$. The logarithm of $A$ has the same lower bound, because $A$ is uniformly positive and bounded. The smooth term $2\log b$ contributes only $O(r)$, so $F=\log (3Ab^2)$ has neither regularity. The mixed component $\mathrm db\wedge \kappa $ shows that $\omega =aw+b\kappa $ is nonclosed. By proposition 15.2.7, its supporting probability is $(A/a)w\wedge \kappa ^2$. $\square$

The theorem assumes a continuous positive complex Hessian for a bounded solution. It does not establish that hypothesis for every continuous $F$; the uniqueness question for arbitrary continuous data remains open.

15.2.6 Divisorial smoothing

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a singular reference, the supporting probability can be unique while smoothings of the solution have different limiting adjoint probabilities. The reason is that the non-pluripolar product discards the mass on a divisor that the smoothings keep. On a product of two projective lines, explicit formulas exhibit both the lost mass and its dependence on the smoothing.

In this subsection, $X=\mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1$. Let $\omega _1$ and $\omega _2$ be the pullbacks of the Fubini–Study forms of the two factors, normalized to have integral one on their factors, and put

\[ \theta =2\omega _1+2\omega _2,\qquad \beta =\omega _1+2\omega _2,\qquad \omega =\omega _1+\omega _2,\qquad \mathrm dV=\omega _1\wedge \omega _2. \]

Thus $\mathrm dV$ is a probability volume form, $\theta ^2=8\mathrm dV$ and $\beta ^2=4\mathrm dV$. Let $z$ be the affine coordinate on the first factor, and put

\[ r=\frac{|z|^2}{1+|z|^2},\qquad \psi =\log r,\qquad D=\{ 0\} \times \mathbb P^1. \]

The function $r$ extends smoothly to the first factor, with value one at infinity, and $\psi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic with value $-\infty $ exactly on $D$. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]-\omega _1$, so $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\beta +[D]$.

For a smooth positive $(1,1)$-form $\Omega $ on the surface $X$, the linearization $v\mapsto 2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \Omega /\Omega ^2$ of the logarithmic Monge–Ampère operator has the adjoint probability $\rho \Omega ^2$, where the Gauduchon multiplier $\rho >0$ satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \Omega )=0$ and $\int _ X\rho \Omega ^2=1$ (proposition 4.8.1). We call $Q=\rho \Omega $ the adjoint cofactor of $\Omega $: it is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-closed, and $\Omega \wedge Q$ is the adjoint probability.

Lemma 15.2.9. Write $x=\log |z|^2$ and $\chi (x)=\log (1+\mathrm e^ x)$. The envelope $\varphi =P_\theta [\psi ]$ is

15.8
\begin{equation} \varphi =W(x)-2\chi (x),\qquad W(x)=\begin{cases} x+2\log 2,& x\leq 0,\\ 2\chi (x),& x\geq 0.\end{cases} \end{equation}

It satisfies $\psi \leq \varphi \leq 0$, $0\leq \varphi -\psi \leq 2\log 2$, and the model condition (13.26) with $\varepsilon =3/4$.

Proof. The function $W$ is convex: both one-sided derivatives at zero equal one, and the derivative of $2\chi $ increases from one to two on $[0,\infty )$. Since $\omega _1=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi (x)$ on the affine chart, $W-2\chi $ is $2\omega _1$-plurisubharmonic there. Since $W$ has slope one for $x\leq 0$ and agrees with $2\chi $ for $x\geq 0$, the function $W-2\chi $ extends to a global $2\omega _1$-plurisubharmonic function, with a logarithmic pole along $D$ and equal to zero where $|z|\geq 1$. The asserted bounds follow from $\psi =x-\chi $: the difference $\varphi -\psi $ equals $2\log 2-\chi $ for $x\leq 0$ and $\chi -x$ for $x\geq 0$.

For $C>0$ write $v_ C=P_\theta (\min \{ \psi +C,0\} )$. Rotations of the first factor and the transitive unitary action on the second preserve the whole competitor class, so the envelope is radial and independent of the second factor. The function $V_ C=2\chi +v_ C$ is then convex in $x$ and satisfies $V_ C\leq \chi +x+C$. This upper bound as $x\to -\infty $ forces the slope of $V_ C$ at $-\infty $ to be at least one, and by convexity all its slopes are at least one. For $x<0$,

\[ V_ C(x)\leq V_ C(0)+x\leq 2\log 2+x=W(x). \]

For $x\geq 0$, the zero obstacle gives $V_ C\leq 2\chi =W$. Consequently $v_ C\leq \varphi $. If $C\geq 2\log 2$, the bounds already proved make $\varphi $ a competitor, so $v_ C=\varphi $. Letting $C\to \infty $ proves $P_\theta [\psi ]=\varphi $. Since $\varphi \sim \psi $ and the ordinary envelope depends only on the singularity type, also $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $.

Finally

\[ \tfrac 34\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =\tfrac 12\omega _1+\tfrac 32\omega _2+[D]\geq 0. \]

Since $\psi \leq \varphi $, the function $\psi $ is a competitor for $P_{(3/4)\theta }(\varphi )$, which is therefore not identically $-\infty $. $\square$

Proposition 15.2.10. Every $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $v\sim \varphi $ is uniquely of the form $v=\psi +w$, where $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$ is bounded. Its non-pluripolar measure is

15.11
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^2,\qquad \int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)=4. \end{equation}

For every real continuous $F$,

\[ B_\varphi (F)=\log 4-\log \int _ X\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV, \qquad \mathcal A_\varphi (F)= \left\{ \frac{\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV}{\int _ X\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV}\right\} . \]

At $v=\psi $, the non-pluripolar part of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi $ is $\beta $, whose adjoint cofactor is $Q=\beta /4$, with adjoint probability $\beta \wedge Q=\mathrm dV$.

Proof. On $X\setminus D$, the function $w=v-\psi $ is bounded, because $v\sim \varphi \sim \psi $, and satisfies $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\geq 0$. After adding local potentials of the closed form $\beta $, lemma 1.3.7 extends $w$ across $D$; the extensions agree on overlaps and give a bounded $\beta $-plurisubharmonic function on $X$. Conversely, for every bounded $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$, the function $\psi +w$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic with current $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w+[D]$, and it has the ordinary type of $\varphi $. The decomposition is unique, since two such functions $w$ agree off $D$ and hence everywhere.

Put $T=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$. The bounded-product construction gives

\[ T^2-\beta ^2=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl (w(T+\beta )\bigr ), \]

and Stokes' theorem against the constant function yields $\int _ XT^2=4$. The product gives no mass to $D$ by proposition 1.7.1; we also give a direct cutoff estimate, which will be used again below. Shift $w$ so that $0\leq w\leq M$, and set $f_ t=\max \{ \psi /t,-1\} $ for $t>0$. Choose smooth convex functions $\sigma _\epsilon $ with derivative in $[0,1]$, equal to $-1$ below $-1-\epsilon $ and to the identity above $-1+\epsilon $, converging uniformly to $\max \{ \cdot ,-1\} $. Then $f_{t,\epsilon }=\sigma _\epsilon (\psi /t)$ is globally smooth, constant near $D$, and

\[ f_{t,\epsilon }\longrightarrow f_ t\ \text{uniformly},\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f_{t,\epsilon }\geq -\omega _1/t. \]

Testing the distributional identity against $f_{t,\epsilon }$, using positivity of $T+\beta $, and letting $\epsilon \to 0$, we obtain

15.12
\begin{equation} \int _ X(-f_ t)T^2 \leq \int _ X(-f_ t)\beta ^2 +\frac Mt\int _ X\omega _1\wedge (T+\beta ) =\int _ X(-f_ t)\beta ^2+\frac{4M}{t}. \end{equation}

Here the last integral equals four by Stokes' theorem. The right-hand side tends to zero as $t\to \infty $, since $f_ t\to 0$ off $D$ and $\beta ^2(D)=0$; as $-f_ t=1$ on $D$, this gives $T^2(D)=0$. Off $D$ the currents $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ and $T$ agree, so locality and the fact that the extended measure puts no mass on the pole set prove (15.11).

For the Hermitian background $\beta $, theorem 10.1.1 with density $\mathrm e^ F$ gives a bounded solution $w$ and a constant $c>0$, and the correspondence above turns it into a solution $\psi +w$ in the ordinary type of $\varphi $. Every potential of that type has mass four, so integration forces $c=4/\int _ X\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV$, which is the formula for $B_\varphi $. Jensen's inequality for the probability $\nu _ F=\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV/\int _ X\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV$ gives

\[ B_\varphi (G)\leq B_\varphi (F)-\int _ X(G-F)\, \mathrm d\nu _ F. \]

Thus $\nu _ F$ is a supporting probability. For any supporting probability, testing its inequality at $G=F\pm th$ and differentiating at $t=0$ shows that its integral against every $h\in C(X)$ equals that of $\nu _ F$.

For a smooth real $h$ and sufficiently small $|t|$, we have $\beta +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h>0$, so $\psi +th$ stays in the same ordinary type and its residual measure is $(\beta +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^2$, with derivative $2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \beta $ at $t=0$. Since $\beta $ is closed, the constant weight $\rho =1/4$ annihilates this derivative after integration, and $Q=\rho \beta $ gives the asserted probability. This normalization uses the residual mass: since $\int _ X\theta \wedge Q=3/2$, the cofactor $Q$ is not a normalized Gauduchon current for $\theta $ in the sense of section 4.4. $\square$

Theorem 15.2.11. Let $g$ be a smooth real function on the second factor with $\gamma =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g\geq -\omega _2$, regarded as a function on $X$. For $0<\delta \leq 1$ put

\[ u_{\delta ,g}=\log (r+\delta \mathrm e^ g),\qquad \Omega _{\delta ,g}=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{\delta ,g},\qquad F_{\delta ,g}=\log \frac{\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2}{4\mathrm dV}. \]

The forms satisfy $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\geq \omega $ and $\int _ X\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2=8$. For each fixed $g$, as $\delta \downarrow 0$ the smooth potentials $u_{\delta ,g}$ decrease pointwise to $\psi $, converge in $L^1$ and in the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $, and converge smoothly off $D$. Moreover $F_{\delta ,g}\to 0$ in $L^ p(\mathrm dV)$ for every $1\leq p<\infty $. The adjoint cofactors and adjoint probabilities of the forms $\Omega _{\delta ,g}$ are

\[ Q_{\delta ,g}=\Omega _{\delta ,g}/8,\qquad \nu _{\delta ,g}=\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2/8, \]

and they have the weak limits

15.13
\begin{equation} \begin{split} Q_{\delta ,g}& \longrightarrow (\beta +[D])/8,\\ \nu _{\delta ,g}& \longrightarrow \tfrac 12\mathrm dV+\tfrac 12[D]\wedge \omega _2 +\tfrac 18[D]\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g. \end{split} \end{equation}

The limiting probability has mass $1/2$ on $D$ and depends on $g$, although $\mathcal A_\varphi (0)=\{ \mathrm dV\} $. The cofactors do not approach $\beta /4$, and their limit is not $\rho \beta $ for any locally integrable scalar $\rho $.

Proof. Smoothness and monotone convergence follow directly from the defining formula, also on $D$, where $\psi =-\infty $. Off $D$, set

\[ \lambda =\frac{r}{r+\delta \mathrm e^ g},\qquad h=\psi -g-\log \delta . \]

The chain rule gives $\Omega _{\delta ,g}=A_\delta +R_\delta $, where

\[ A_\delta =(2-\lambda )\omega _1+2\omega _2+(1-\lambda )\gamma , \qquad R_\delta =\lambda (1-\lambda )\mathrm dh\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}h. \]

Thus $A_\delta \geq \omega _1+\omega _2$ and $R_\delta \geq 0$, and the inequality $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\geq \omega $ extends across $D$ by continuity. Closedness of $\theta $ and Stokes' theorem give the mass eight. Since $\Omega _{\delta ,g}$ is closed, the Gauduchon condition $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho \Omega _{\delta ,g})=0$ reduces to the elliptic equation $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \wedge \Omega _{\delta ,g}=0$ on the connected surface, whose smooth solutions are constant by the maximum principle. Normalization against $\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2$ gives $\rho =1/8$ and the stated adjoint cofactors and probabilities.

The rank-one form $R_\delta $ has square zero, hence $\Omega _{\delta ,g}^2=A_\delta ^2+2A_\delta \wedge R_\delta $. The bounded coefficients of $A_\delta $ converge off $D$ to those of $\beta $, which gives $A_\delta ^2\to 4\mathrm dV$. Write $N=\mathrm d\psi \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\psi $. In $A_\delta \wedge R_\delta $, the part in which $R_\delta $ contributes only derivatives along the first factor (the normal part) is

\[ \lambda (1-\lambda )N\wedge \bigl (2\omega _2+(1-\lambda )\gamma \bigr ). \]

Terms mixing a derivative along the first factor with one along the second vanish for degree reasons. The remaining tangential term is $\lambda (1-\lambda )(2-\lambda )\omega _1\wedge \mathrm dg\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}g$, which tends to zero by dominated convergence.

The normal coefficients can be computed in polar coordinates. Put $s=\delta \mathrm e^{g}$ and $t=|z|^2$. Then

\[ \lambda =\frac{t}{s+(1+s)t},\qquad N=\frac{\mathrm i\, \mathrm dz\wedge \mathrm d\bar z}{2\pi t(1+t)^2}. \]

After angular integration the radial measure of $\lambda (1-\lambda )N$ is

\[ \frac{s\, \mathrm dt}{(1+t)(s+(1+s)t)^2} =\frac{\mathrm dy}{(1+sy)(1+(1+s)y)^2},\qquad t=sy. \]

It is bounded by $\mathrm dy/(1+y)^2$ and tends to that measure, whose integral is one. With an additional factor $1-\lambda $, the rescaled measure becomes $\mathrm dy/(1+(1+s)y)^3$, which is bounded by $\mathrm dy/(1+y)^3$ and has limiting integral $1/2$. The mass of both measures outside every fixed tube around $D$ tends to zero, uniformly in the point of the second factor because $g$ is bounded. Testing against continuous functions therefore gives

\[ \lambda (1-\lambda )N\longrightarrow [D],\qquad \lambda (1-\lambda )^2N\longrightarrow \tfrac 12[D]. \]

Combining the normal and the remaining terms proves

15.16
\begin{equation} \Omega _{\delta ,g}^2\longrightarrow 4\mathrm dV+[D]\wedge (4\omega _2+\gamma ). \end{equation}

Dividing by eight gives the second line of (15.13). The limit is positive since $4\omega _2+\gamma \geq 3\omega _2$, and its mass on $D$ is $4/8=1/2$ by Stokes' theorem on the second factor. Taking $g=0$ and a small nonconstant smooth $g$ gives different limits, since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g$ vanishes identically only for constant $g$.

The difference of the potentials satisfies

\[ 0\leq u_{\delta ,g}-\psi \leq \log \bigl (1+\delta \mathrm e^{\max g}/r\bigr ) \]

off $D$. The right-hand side tends to zero and is dominated by an integrable logarithmic pole, which proves $L^1$ convergence. Continuity of distributional differentiation then gives the limit of the cofactors. A locally integrable function times the smooth form $\beta $ has absolutely continuous coefficients, so it cannot produce the singular coefficients of $[D]$.

For convergence in capacity, use the capacity $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $ of section 1.8; after subtracting one, its test potentials range over $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with $-1\leq v\leq 0$. For such $v$, apply the cutoff argument of (15.12) with $\beta $ replaced by $\omega $ and $w=v+1$. Stokes' theorem gives $\int _ X\omega _1\wedge (2\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)=2$, so

\[ \operatorname {Cap}_\omega \{ \psi <-t\} \leq \int _ X(-f_ t)\omega ^2+2/t\longrightarrow 0. \]

The same bound shows that $D$ has capacity zero. For $\eta >0$, the set of points off $D$ where $u_{\delta ,g}-\psi >\eta $ is contained in

\[ \left\{ r<\frac{\delta \mathrm e^{\max g}}{\mathrm e^\eta -1}\right\} . \]

This is a sublevel set of $\psi $ whose level tends to $-\infty $ as $\delta \downarrow 0$. The capacity bound for such sublevel sets, together with $\operatorname {Cap}_\omega (D)=0$, proves convergence in capacity.

Finally, $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\geq \omega $ gives $F_{\delta ,g}\geq -\log 2$. In the affine chart $|\mathrm d\psi |_\omega ^2\leq C/r$, while $g$ and its first two derivatives are bounded. The decomposition above yields $\Omega _{\delta ,g}\leq C_ g(1+r^{-1})\omega $, hence

\[ |F_{\delta ,g}|\leq C_ g+2|\log r|. \]

Every finite power of this logarithmic bound is integrable. Smooth convergence off $D$ gives $F_{\delta ,g}\to 0$ there, and dominated convergence proves convergence in $L^ p$. $\square$

The exponential densities satisfy $\int _ X\mathrm e^{F_{\delta ,g}}\mathrm dV=2$ for every $\delta $, whereas their pointwise limit has integral one; they are therefore not uniformly integrable. The smoothings leave the ordinary type of $\varphi $ and keep the mass eight of bounded potentials instead of the mass four of that type. The example thus shows which concentration a recovery theorem must exclude. The hypotheses of proposition 15.2.2 (uniform convergence) and of theorem 15.1.3 (uniformly bounded logarithmic densities converging in $L^1$) are stronger than the convergence in every $L^ p$ obtained here. In the fixed divisorial type, a common bound on the residual potentials gives a direct recovery estimate instead.

Corollary 15.2.12. Let $w_ j,w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$ satisfy $|w_ j|,|w|\leq M$ for a fixed $M\geq 1$, and suppose that $w_ j\to w$ in capacity. Put

\[ \begin{gathered} T_ j=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j,\qquad T=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w,\\ Q_ j=T_ j/4,\quad Q=T/4,\qquad \nu _ j=T_ j^2/4,\quad \nu =T^2/4. \end{gathered} \]

Then $Q_ j\rightharpoonup Q$ as currents and $\nu _ j\rightharpoonup \nu $ as probability measures. More precisely, for real $f\in C^\infty (X)$ choose $C_ f\geq 0$ with $-C_ f\beta \leq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\leq C_ f\beta $. For every $\eta >0$, writing $E_{j,\eta }=\{ |w_ j-w|>\eta \} $, one has

15.17
\begin{equation} \left|\int _ X f\, (\mathrm d\nu _ j-\mathrm d\nu )\right| \leq C_ f\bigl (2\eta +2M^2\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E_{j,\eta })\bigr ). \end{equation}

If $T_ j$ is smooth and strictly positive, then $Q_ j$ and $\nu _ j$ are its adjoint cofactor and adjoint probability. If $\psi +w$ solves (15.1) for some $F\in C(X)$, the limit $\nu $ is the unique member of $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$.

Proof. The capacity is the one of section 1.8, and $\beta \leq 2\omega $ gives $\operatorname {Cap}_\beta \leq 4\operatorname {Cap}_\omega $. Set $a_ j=(w_ j+M)/(2M)$ and $S_ j=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ j$. Then $0\leq a_ j\leq 1$ and

\[ \begin{gathered} S_ j=\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)\beta +\frac1{2M}T_ j,\\ S_ j^2\geq \left(\frac1M-\frac1{2M^2}\right)\beta \wedge T_ j \geq \frac1{2M}\beta \wedge T_ j. \end{gathered} \]

Positivity of bounded mixed products justifies these inequalities. Thus $\int _ E\beta \wedge T_ j\leq 2M\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E)$ for every Borel set $E$, and the same bound holds for $T$. By closedness and Stokes' theorem, $\int _ X\beta \wedge (T_ j+T)=8$. Now test the identity

\[ T_ j^2-T^2=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl ((w_ j-w)(T_ j+T)\bigr ) \]

against $f$. On $X\setminus E_{j,\eta }$ the potential difference has absolute value at most $\eta $, and on $E_{j,\eta }$ at most $2M$. Consequently

\[ \begin{aligned} \left|\int _ Xf(T_ j^2-T^2)\right| & \leq C_ f\left(8\eta +2M\int _{E_{j,\eta }}\beta \wedge (T_ j+T)\right)\\ & \leq C_ f\left(8\eta +8M^2\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E_{j,\eta })\right). \end{aligned} \]

Dividing by four proves (15.17). Letting first $j\to \infty $ with $\eta $ fixed and then $\eta \downarrow 0$ shows that $\int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu _ j\to \int _ Xf\, \mathrm d\nu $. All $\nu _ j$ and $\nu $ have mass one by proposition 15.2.10, and smooth functions are uniformly dense in $C(X)$, so $\nu _ j\rightharpoonup \nu $.

The constant test potential in the definition of capacity gives $4\mathrm dV(E)\leq \operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E)$. Splitting the potential difference over the same sets yields

\[ \| w_ j-w\| _{L^1(\mathrm dV)} \leq \eta +\tfrac M2\operatorname {Cap}_\beta (E_{j,\eta }). \]

Taking the limits in the same order proves $L^1$ convergence, and hence convergence of the currents $Q_ j$ by continuity of distributional differentiation. The limiting cofactor satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}Q=0$, $\int _ X\beta \wedge Q=1$, and $T\wedge Q=\nu $ as a bounded product. For smooth real $h$, the signed measure $2\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge T$ is the formal linearization of the residual operator at $w$ in the direction $h$, and its integral against the constant $1/4$ vanishes by Stokes' theorem. It is the derivative of the residual measure along $w+th$ whenever these functions remain $\beta $-plurisubharmonic.

If $T_ j$ is smooth and strictly positive, the maximum principle on the connected surface makes its Gauduchon multiplier constant, and the mass four normalizes it to $1/4$. Finally, if $\psi +w$ solves the equation at $F$, then $\nu =T^2/4=\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV/\int _ X\mathrm e^ F\mathrm dV$, which is the unique supporting probability by proposition 15.2.10. $\square$

In the corollary, the potentials $\psi +w_ j$ keep their pole along $D$, and the adjoint objects refer to the smooth residual forms $T_ j$. We do not know whether a similar recovery holds for more general singular references, for unbounded residual sequences, or when the Gauduchon multipliers are not constant.

15.3 A convex functional on probability measures

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

To pass to the limit in the supporting inequality, we need to control the mass that supporting probabilities can put on small sets. The convex dual of $B_\varphi $ provides this control: the measures in each of its sublevel sets are uniformly absolutely continuous. This allows the logarithmic density and the supporting measure to vary simultaneously.

Let $\mathcal P(X)$ denote the set of Borel probability measures on $X$, with the weak topology. Define

15.20
\begin{equation} H_\varphi (\nu ) =\sup _{G\in C(X)}\left(B_\varphi (G)+\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu \right), \qquad \nu \in \mathcal P(X). \end{equation}

The value $+\infty $ is allowed, while taking $G=0$ shows that $H_\varphi \geq B_\varphi (0)>-\infty $.

Proposition 15.3.1. The functional $H_\varphi $ is convex and weakly lower semicontinuous. For every $F\in C(X)$,

15.21
\begin{equation} B_\varphi (F) =\min _{\nu \in \mathcal P(X)} \left(H_\varphi (\nu )-\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu \right). \end{equation}

The minimizers are exactly $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$.

Proof. Each function of $\nu $ in the supremum defining $H_\varphi $ is affine and weakly continuous, so their supremum is convex and lower semicontinuous. By definition, $H_\varphi (\nu )-\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu \geq B_\varphi (F)$, with equality precisely when

\[ B_\varphi (G)+\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq B_\varphi (F)+\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu \quad \text{for every }G\in C(X). \]

This is the defining inequality of $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$, and the minimum is attained because $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$ is nonempty by theorem 15.2.1. $\square$

Theorem 15.3.2. For every finite real $L$, the set

\[ \mathcal K_ L=\{ \nu \in \mathcal P(X):H_\varphi (\nu )\leq L\} \]

is weakly compact. Its measures are uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$: for every $\epsilon >0$ there is a $\delta >0$ such that

15.22
\begin{equation} \mathrm{d}V(E)<\delta \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sup _{\nu \in \mathcal K_ L}\nu (E)<\epsilon \end{equation}

for every Borel set $E$. In particular, writing $\mathrm{d}\nu =h_\nu \mathrm{d}V$, the densities in this set are uniformly integrable in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. On $\mathcal K_ L$, weak convergence of measures also implies weak convergence of their densities in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$.

Proof. Weak compactness follows from lower semicontinuity and compactness of $\mathcal P(X)$. We may assume that the sublevel set is nonempty, so that $L\geq B_\varphi (0)$. Fix $t>0$. By theorem 15.1.3, there is $\delta _ t>0$ such that

\[ 0\leq \chi \leq 1,\quad \chi \in C(X),\quad \int _ X\chi \, \mathrm{d}V<\delta _ t \quad \Longrightarrow \quad B_\varphi (t\chi )>B_\varphi (0)-1. \]

Indeed, otherwise there would be a sequence of such functions $\chi $ tending to zero in $L^1$ with $B_\varphi (t\chi )\leq B_\varphi (0)-1$, contrary to that theorem. For $\nu \in \mathcal K_ L$, the defining supremum gives

\[ t\int _ X\chi \, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq L-B_\varphi (t\chi ) <L-B_\varphi (0)+1. \]

Choose $t$ so large that $(L-B_\varphi (0)+1)/t<\epsilon /2$. If $\mathrm{d}V(E)<\delta _ t/2$, outer regularity gives an open neighborhood $O$ of $E$ with $\mathrm{d}V(O)<\delta _ t$. For any compact $K\subset E$, choose a continuous cutoff $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ equal to one on $K$ and supported in $O$. The last inequality gives $\nu (K)<\epsilon /2$, uniformly in $\nu \in \mathcal K_ L$, and inner regularity yields $\nu (E)\leq \epsilon /2<\epsilon $. This proves (15.22).

In particular, every measure in the sublevel set is absolutely continuous, with a density of integral one. Together with the uniform small-set estimate, this is exactly uniform integrability on the finite measure space $(X,\mathrm{d}V)$.

Finally, suppose that $\nu _ j\to \nu $ weakly within the sublevel set. For a bounded measurable $G$, choose continuous $G_ k$ with $|G_ k|\leq \| G\| _\infty $ and $G_ k\to G$ in $\mathrm{d}V$-measure; such approximations exist by Lusin's theorem. Uniform absolute continuity gives

\[ \sup _{\sigma \in \mathcal K_ L}\int _ X|G_ k-G|\, \mathrm{d}\sigma \longrightarrow 0. \]

To see this, split $X$ into $\{ |G_ k-G|\leq a\} $ and its complement; the integral over the first set is at most $a$, and the integral over the second is at most $2\| G\| _\infty $ times a uniformly small measure. For fixed $k$, weak convergence applies to $G_ k$. Letting first $j\to \infty $ and then $k\to \infty $ proves convergence of the integrals against $G$, which is exactly weak $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ convergence of the densities. In particular, every sequence in the sublevel set has a subsequence whose densities converge weakly in $L^1$. $\square$

15.4 Bounded measurable densities and stability

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The supporting probabilities of continuous approximations of a bounded measurable logarithmic density lie in one sublevel set of $H_\varphi $. Uniform integrability then allows both the density and the supporting measure to vary when we pass to the limit in the supporting inequality.

Theorem 15.4.1. For every $F\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)$ there is a nonempty set of absolutely continuous probability measures, again denoted $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$, characterized by

15.23
\begin{equation} B_\varphi (G)\leq B_\varphi (F)-\int _ X(G-F)\, \mathrm{d}\nu \qquad \text{for every }G\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V). \end{equation}

For continuous $F$, this set agrees with the one defined by (15.5). Formula (15.21) also holds for bounded measurable $F$, with these measures as its minimizers; there the objective is interpreted as $+\infty $ whenever $H_\varphi (\nu )=+\infty $, and otherwise $\nu $ is absolutely continuous, so that the integral of the $L^\infty $ class $F$ is well defined.

Suppose that $F_ j,F\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)$ are uniformly bounded and $F_ j\to F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, and let $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F_ j)$. Then the densities of the $\nu _ j$ are uniformly integrable, and every weak $L^1$ cluster point belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (F)$. If the latter is a singleton, the whole sequence converges to it weakly in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$.

Proof. First, if $H_\varphi (\nu )<\infty $, the measure $\nu $ is absolutely continuous by theorem 15.3.2. A bounded measurable $G$ admits uniformly bounded continuous approximations $G_ k\to G$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, and absolute continuity together with the common bound gives $\int _ XG_ k\, \mathrm{d}\nu \to \int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu $. Combined with theorem 15.1.3, this proves

15.24
\begin{equation} H_\varphi (\nu ) =\sup _{G\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)} \left(B_\varphi (G)+\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu \right) \end{equation}

for such $\nu $.

To construct supporting probabilities at $F$, choose continuous $F_ j$ with $|F_ j|\leq M=\| F\| _\infty $ and $F_ j\to F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, and choose $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_\varphi (F_ j)$. The minimum formula of proposition 15.3.1 and the Lipschitz bound give

\[ H_\varphi (\nu _ j) =B_\varphi (F_ j)+\int _ XF_ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \leq B_\varphi (0)+2M. \]

By theorem 15.3.2, a subsequence converges weakly in $L^1$ to a probability measure $\nu $. Uniform absolute continuity gives

\[ \int _ X|F_ j-F|\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j\longrightarrow 0, \]

by splitting again at a fixed small positive threshold: the sets where $|F_ j-F|$ exceeds it have $\mathrm{d}V$-measure tending to zero. Since $F$ is bounded and measurable, weak $L^1$ convergence applies to its integral. Lower semicontinuity of $H_\varphi $ and continuity of $B_\varphi $ yield

\[ H_\varphi (\nu ) \leq \liminf _ j H_\varphi (\nu _ j) =B_\varphi (F)+\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

The reverse inequality follows from (15.24) with $G=F$. Equality, combined with the same supremum formula, proves (15.23) for $\nu $ and the minimum formula.

For continuous $F$, every measure satisfying (15.5) has finite dual value by proposition 15.3.1, so (15.24) extends its supporting inequality to all bounded measurable tests; the converse inclusion is trivial. Thus the two definitions agree. Finally, an absolutely continuous probability satisfying (15.23) has finite dual value and attains the minimum, so the characterization is exact.

For the stability assertion, we start from the given $F_ j$ and $\nu _ j$ instead of an approximating construction. The common uniform bound again places all $\nu _ j$ in a single finite sublevel set of $H_\varphi $, and the same compactness and limiting argument shows that every weak $L^1$ cluster point attains the minimum for $F$. If the set of minimizers is a singleton, compactness forces the whole sequence to converge to its member. $\square$

15.5 Entropy and the closed case

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In a closed background, all potentials of an ordinary singularity type have the same mass, and integrating the equation determines the scalar explicitly; its convex dual is then the relative entropy. The variational formula for the entropy also gives estimates in nonclosed backgrounds.

For a Borel probability measure $\nu $ absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathrm{d}V$, let $h$ be its nonnegative density and define the relative entropy

\[ \operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu )=\int _ Xh\log h\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad 0\log 0=0, \]

and set $\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu )=+\infty $ if $\nu $ is not absolutely continuous. The integral is well defined in $(-\infty ,+\infty ]$, since $h\log h\geq -\mathrm{e}^{-1}$; it may be negative when $\mathrm{d}V(X)\ne 1$.

Lemma 15.5.1. For every $\nu \in \mathcal P(X)$,

\[ \operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu ) =\sup _{F\in C(X)}\left(\int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu -\log \int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V\right). \]

Proof. If $\nu $ is not absolutely continuous, choose a compact set $K$ with $\mathrm{d}V(K)=0$ and $\nu (K)>0$. For $t>0$, regularity gives a continuous cutoff $0\leq \chi _ t\leq 1$, equal to one on $K$, whose support has volume at most $\mathrm{e}^{-2t}$. For $F=t\chi _ t$, the expression in the supremum is at least

\[ t\nu (K)-\log \bigl (\mathrm{d}V(X)+\mathrm{e}^{-t}\bigr ), \]

which tends to infinity with $t$.

Suppose now that $\nu =h\, \mathrm{d}V$. If its entropy is finite, Jensen's inequality for the logarithm gives

\[ \int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu -\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu ) \leq \log \int _{\{ h>0\} }\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V \leq \log \int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

For infinite entropy this upper bound is automatic. For the opposite inequality, set

\[ F_ k=\log \bigl (\max \{ k^{-1},\min \{ h,k\} \} \bigr ),\qquad k\geq 1. \]

Then $\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{F_ k}\, \mathrm{d}V\to 1$ and $\int _ XF_ k\, \mathrm{d}\nu \to \operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu )$, with the value $+\infty $ allowed: the positive parts of the integrands $hF_ k$ increase to that of $h\log h$, and their negative parts are dominated by the integrable negative part of $h\log h$. For each fixed $k$, approximate $F_ k$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V+\nu )$ by continuous functions with the same bounds; both integrals, including the exponential one, converge to those of $F_ k$. Hence the supremum over continuous functions is at least the value of the expression at $F_ k$, and letting $k\to \infty $ completes the proof. $\square$

Suppose now that $\theta $ is a Kähler form. By the closed-background theory of [SGPT], all potentials of the ordinary type of $\varphi $ have the same non-pluripolar mass $m_\varphi $, which is positive under the standing hypotheses. For $F\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)$, integrating the equation gives

15.25
\begin{equation} B_\varphi (F)=\log m_\varphi -\log \int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V, \qquad \mathcal A_\varphi (F) =\left\{ \frac{\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V}{\int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V}\right\} . \end{equation}

For the second identity, Jensen's inequality shows, as in proposition 15.2.10, that the normalized exponential measure is a supporting probability; differentiating in bounded directions and testing the supporting inequality with both signs of the parameter shows that it is the only one.

Proposition 15.5.2. If $\theta $ is Kähler, then

\[ H_\varphi (\nu )=\log m_\varphi +\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu ). \]

Thus the minimum formula (15.21) becomes the usual variational formula for the logarithmic integral in (15.25).

Proof. Substitute (15.25) into the defining supremum and apply lemma 15.5.1. $\square$

The dual functional depends on the reference volume. If $\mathrm{d}V$ is replaced by $\mathrm{d}V'=\mathrm{e}^ a \mathrm{d}V$ for a continuous real $a$, the corresponding functionals satisfy

\[ B'_\varphi (F)=B_\varphi (F+a),\qquad H'_\varphi (\nu )=H_\varphi (\nu )-\int _ Xa\, \mathrm{d}\nu . \]

The first identity follows from the equation, and the second, which also holds when the values are infinite, from the change of variable $G\mapsto G+a$ in the defining supremum. In a nonclosed background, $H_\varphi $ is still defined by the same supremum, but the total mass need not be constant on an ordinary type, so the entropy computation no longer determines it.

15.6 Entropy bounds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a Hermitian background and the zero reference, a lower bound for the $L^ q$ norm of the density in the equation and an upper bound coming from a Gauduchon trace place $B_0$ between two logarithmic integrals. Dualizing gives two-sided entropy estimates, which identify the set where $H_0$ is finite. Throughout this section, $\mathrm{d}V$ is normalized by $\mathrm{d}V(X)=1$.

Proposition 15.6.1. Let $\theta =\omega $ be a Hermitian form, and let $\varphi =0$. There are real constants $A$ and, for every $q>1$, $a_ q$ such that every Borel probability measure $\nu $ satisfies

\[ a_ q+\frac1q\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu ) \leq H_0(\nu ) \leq A+n\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu ). \]

The inequalities include the value $+\infty $. Thus $H_0(\nu )<\infty $ if and only if $\nu $ has finite entropy, and a family of probability measures has uniformly bounded values of $H_0$ if and only if its entropies are uniformly bounded.

Proof. In dimension one, $\omega $ is a Kähler form, and proposition 15.5.2, which rests on (15.25), gives $H_0=\log \int _ X\omega +\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}$, so the assertions follow. Suppose that $n\geq 2$.

For $G\in C(X)$, apply the lower bound for $\| F\| _ q$ in theorem 10.3.3 to a bounded solution with density $F=\mathrm{e}^{B_0(G)+G}$. It gives a constant $c_ q>0$, independent of $G$, such that

\[ B_0(G)\geq \log c_ q-\frac1q\log \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{qG}\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

For the upper bound, fix a smooth Gauduchon metric $\gamma $, rescaled so that $\int _ X\omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}=1$, and write

\[ k_\gamma =\left(\frac{\gamma ^ n}{\mathrm{d}V}\right)^{(n-1)/n}, \qquad m=\min _ X k_\gamma >0. \]

For smooth $G$, the trace bound (4.3) gives

\[ \mathrm{e}^{B_0(G)/n}\int _ X\mathrm{e}^{G/n}k_\gamma \, \mathrm{d}V\leq 1. \]

Uniform approximation and continuity of $B_0$ extend this inequality to continuous $G$. Consequently

\[ B_0(G)\leq -n\log m-n\log \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{G/n}\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

Taking the defining suprema in the two scalar bounds and applying lemma 15.5.1 with $F=qG$ and $F=G/n$, respectively, proves both inequalities for every probability measure, including infinite values, with $a_ q=\log c_ q$ and $A=-n\log m$. The finite-domain and bounded-family assertions follow by taking $q=2$. $\square$

15.6.1 The mass bounds as entropy differences

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In a closed background the difference between $H_0$ and the entropy is constant. In general it measures how much the total Monge–Ampère mass of bounded potentials varies: its infimum and supremum recover the lower and upper masses $v_-(\omega )$ and $v_+(\omega )$ of definition 6.5.1.

Let $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ent}}(X)$ denote the set of probability measures of finite entropy relative to the fixed smooth probability volume $\mathrm{d}V$. By proposition 15.6.1, the difference $H_0(\nu )-\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu )$ is a finite real number for every $\nu $ in this set.

Proposition 15.6.2. For every Hermitian background $\omega $,

\[ \begin{split} \inf _{\nu \in \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ent}}(X)} \bigl (H_0(\nu )-\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu )\bigr ) & =\log v_-(\omega ),\\ \sup _{\nu \in \mathcal P_{\mathrm{ent}}(X)} \bigl (H_0(\nu )-\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu )\bigr ) & =\log v_+(\omega ). \end{split} \]

The same extrema are obtained over smooth strictly positive probability measures. Here $\log 0=-\infty $ and $\log (+\infty )=+\infty $. Thus the difference is bounded above if and only if $X$ has the bounded mass property, and bounded below if and only if $\omega $ has positive lower mass.

Proof. Write $Z_ G=\int _ X\mathrm{e}^ G\, \mathrm{d}V$. A bounded solution for $G\in C(X)$ has mass $\mathrm{e}^{B_0(G)}Z_ G$. If $v_-(\omega )>0$, the lower mass bound gives

\[ B_0(G)\geq \log v_-(\omega )-\log Z_ G. \]

Taking the defining supremum for $H_0$ and using lemma 15.5.1 gives $H_0-\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}\geq \log v_-(\omega )$ on $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ent}}(X)$. In the same way, if $v_+(\omega )<\infty $, the upper mass bound gives $H_0-\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}\leq \log v_+(\omega )$. These bounds hold trivially when $v_-(\omega )=0$ or $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $.

To prove sharpness, first take a smooth real $G$ and its Gibbs probability $\nu _ G=Z_ G^{-1}\mathrm{e}^ G\, \mathrm{d}V$, which has finite entropy. Testing the supremum defining $H_0$ with $G$ gives

\[ H_0(\nu _ G)-\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu _ G) \geq B_0(G)+\log Z_ G. \]

In the other direction, let $\sigma _ G$ be the smooth strictly positive Gauduchon adjoint probability of theorem 15.2.5. It lies in $\mathcal A_0(G)$, so its dual value is $B_0(G)+\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\sigma _ G$, and lemma 15.5.1 yields

\[ H_0(\sigma _ G)-\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\sigma _ G) \leq B_0(G)+\log Z_ G. \]

As $G$ runs over smooth real functions, the masses $\mathrm{e}^{B_0(G)}Z_ G$ run over the masses $\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ of all smooth $u$ with $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0$, since such a $u$ solves the equation for $G=\log ((\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n/\mathrm{d}V)$. By theorem 1.11.1, the mass of every bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function is a limit of such masses. Hence the infimum and supremum of $\mathrm{e}^{B_0(G)}Z_ G$ over smooth $G$ are $v_-(\omega )$ and $v_+(\omega )$. Since $\nu _ G$ and $\sigma _ G$ are smooth and strictly positive, the last two inequalities prove sharpness on this smaller domain, including the cases $v_-(\omega )=0$ and $v_+(\omega )=+\infty $. The two equivalences follow. $\square$

The difference does not depend on the smooth positive reference volume. For smooth real $a$, replacing $\mathrm{d}V$ by $\mathrm{d}V'=\mathrm{e}^ a\mathrm{d}V$ changes both $H_0(\nu )$ and $\operatorname {Ent}(\nu )$ by $-\int _ Xa\, \mathrm{d}\nu $, by the transformation formula preceding proposition 15.6.1, and finiteness of the entropy is unchanged because $a$ is bounded. Similarly, if $h$ is a smooth real function and $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ is also Hermitian, transporting bounded solutions by $u\mapsto u-h$ preserves $B_0$, and hence this difference. Finally, the difference is constant on $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{ent}}(X)$ exactly when $v_-(\omega )=v_+(\omega )$, that is, when all bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions have the same mass.

15.6.2 Entropy for a prescribed ordinary type

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Return to the standing setting: $\theta $ is semipositive and big, and $\varphi $ is a model reference, with $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ as in (13.26). The part $(1-\varepsilon )\theta $ of the background left over by the smaller-background envelope gives a uniform lower bound for the density norm, while comparison in a larger Hermitian background gives the upper bound. Together they extend the entropy estimates to the prescribed type.

Corollary 15.6.3. For every $q>1$ there is a finite real $a_ q$, and there is a finite real $A$, such that

\[ a_ q+\frac1q\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu ) \leq H_\varphi (\nu ) \leq A+n\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu ) \]

for every Borel probability measure $\nu $, including infinite values. Thus $H_\varphi (\nu )$ is finite exactly when $\nu $ has finite entropy, and uniformly bounded dual values are equivalent to uniformly bounded entropies. For fixed $\theta $ and $\varepsilon $, the constants can be chosen independently of the model reference $\varphi $.

Proof. For $G\in C(X)$, let $u\sim \varphi $ solve the equation with density $c\mathrm{e}^ G$, where $c=\mathrm{e}^{B_\varphi (G)}$. The lower bound $b_ q>0$ of lemma 14.11.2 for the $L^ q$ norm of the density gives $c\| \mathrm{e}^ G\| _ q\geq b_ q$, hence

\[ B_\varphi (G)\geq \log b_ q-\frac1q \log \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{qG}\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

For the upper bound, choose a Hermitian form $\Omega \geq \theta $, and let $B_{\Omega ,0}$ and $H_{\Omega ,0}$ denote its normalization scalar and dual functional for the zero reference. Let $v$ be a bounded solution for $G$ in this background; being bounded, it has a smaller-background envelope $P_{\delta \Omega }(v)\geq \inf _ Xv$ for every $0<\delta <1$. Moreover $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\Omega )$, $u\preceq v$, and positivity gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\Omega (u) \geq \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) =c\mathrm{e}^ G\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

If $c>\mathrm{e}^{B_{\Omega ,0}(G)}$, the singular strict domination principle, applied in the background $\Omega $ to $v$ and $u+t$ for every real $t$, would give $v\geq u+t$ for all $t$, which is impossible. Hence $B_\varphi \leq B_{\Omega ,0}$, and therefore $H_\varphi \leq H_{\Omega ,0}$; proposition 15.6.1 now gives the upper bound.

Taking the defining supremum in the lower scalar estimate and applying lemma 15.5.1 with $F=qG$ gives the lower bound for every probability measure, including infinite values, with $a_ q=\log b_ q$. The finite-domain and bounded-family assertions follow by taking $q=2$. Finally, lemma 14.11.2 is uniform in the reference for fixed $\theta $ and $\varepsilon $, and the upper bound uses only $\Omega $, which proves the last assertion. $\square$

This gives compactness when the reference varies. Let the model references $\varphi _ j$ satisfy the smaller-background condition with the same $\varepsilon $, let $G_ j\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)$ satisfy $\| G_ j\| _\infty \leq M$ for a fixed constant $M$, and choose $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_{\varphi _ j}(G_ j)$. With $\Omega $ as in the proof,

\[ H_{\varphi _ j}(\nu _ j) =B_{\varphi _ j}(G_ j)+\int _ XG_ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \leq B_{\Omega ,0}(0)+2M. \]

The uniform lower estimate of corollary 15.6.3 bounds their entropies, and the Hermitian upper estimate then places all $\nu _ j$ in a single finite sublevel set of $H_{\Omega ,0}$. Hence theorem 15.3.2, applied in the background $\Omega $, gives weak compactness and uniform integrability of their densities, and every weak limit has finite entropy. Identifying such a limit as a supporting probability for a limiting reference requires further information.

15.7 Changing the prescribed singularities

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

A more singular reference has a smaller normalization scalar. A competitor built from the smaller-background envelope also gives a quantitative comparison with the zero reference, uniform over all logarithmic densities. In this section the reference varies among the model potentials satisfying (13.26) for the fixed semipositive big background $\theta $, and $B_0,H_0$ denote the functionals of the zero reference in the same background.

Proposition 15.7.1. Let $\varphi ,\psi $ satisfy (13.26). If $\varphi \preceq \psi $, then $B_\varphi \leq B_\psi $ and $H_\varphi \leq H_\psi $. Moreover, if $0<\varepsilon <1$ and $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )\not\equiv -\infty $, then

\[ B_0(G)+n\log (1-\varepsilon ) \leq B_\varphi (G)\leq B_0(G) \qquad (G\in C(X)), \]

and

\[ H_0(\nu )+n\log (1-\varepsilon ) \leq H_\varphi (\nu )\leq H_0(\nu ) \qquad (\nu \in \mathcal P(X)). \]

The dual inequalities include infinite values.

Proof. Fix $G$, and let $u_\varphi ,u_\psi $ solve the equations in the ordinary types of $\varphi $ and $\psi $, with constants $c_\varphi ,c_\psi $. Then $u_\varphi \preceq u_\psi $. If $c_\varphi >c_\psi $, the singular strict domination principle applied to $u_\psi $ and $u_\varphi +t$ would give $u_\psi \geq u_\varphi +t$ for every real $t$, which is impossible at a point where both are finite. This proves the monotonicity of $B$, and taking the defining supremum gives the monotonicity of $H$. Since $\varphi \preceq 0$ and the zero potential is itself a model reference, this also proves both upper bounds.

For the lower bound, let $v$ be a bounded solution for $G$ with the zero reference, with constant $c_0$, and put

\[ h=P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )+(1-\varepsilon )v. \]

The two summands are $\varepsilon \theta $- and $(1-\varepsilon )\theta $-plurisubharmonic, respectively, so positivity and homogeneity give

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (h) \geq (1-\varepsilon )^ n\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v) =(1-\varepsilon )^ nc_0\mathrm{e}^ G\, \mathrm{d}V. \]

As in the proof of lemma 14.11.2, this inequality is justified first on finite truncation levels and then off the pole set of $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\varphi )$, which has zero smooth volume. Also $h\preceq u_\varphi $. If $c_\varphi <(1-\varepsilon )^ nc_0$, strict domination would force $u_\varphi \geq h+t$ for every $t$. Thus $c_\varphi \geq (1-\varepsilon )^ nc_0$. Taking logarithms proves the scalar bound, and taking the defining suprema proves the dual bound. $\square$

15.7.1 Uniform comparison of the scalar functionals

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

When two scalar functionals are uniformly close, their supporting probabilities are close as well. To make this precise, define for two model references $\varphi ,\psi $ in the preceding setting

15.28
\begin{equation} \Delta (\varphi ,\psi ) =\sup _{G\in C(X)}|B_\varphi (G)-B_\psi (G)|. \end{equation}

The comparison with $B_0$ in proposition 15.7.1 makes this number finite for every such pair. It is a pseudometric: symmetry, vanishing on the diagonal and the triangle inequality follow from the properties of the uniform norm. Distinct singularity types can have distance zero, as example 15.10.2 shows.

Proposition 15.7.2. For these model references,

\[ \Delta (\varphi ,\psi ) =\sup _{\operatorname {Ent}_{\mathrm{d}V}(\nu )<\infty } |H_\varphi (\nu )-H_\psi (\nu )|, \]

where the supremum is over probability measures of finite entropy. The same value is obtained if (15.28) uses all bounded measurable $G$, and it does not depend on the smooth positive reference volume.

Proof. By corollary 15.6.3, both duals are finite exactly on the probability measures of finite entropy; rescaling the reference volume to have mass one does not affect finiteness. A uniform bound for $|B_\varphi -B_\psi |$ passes to the defining suprema, so the supremum in the statement is at most $\Delta (\varphi ,\psi )$. Conversely, denote that supremum by $D$. If $\nu \in \mathcal A_\psi (G)$, then $\nu $ has finite entropy, and the dual minimum formula gives

\[ B_\varphi (G) \leq H_\varphi (\nu )-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq B_\psi (G)+D. \]

Interchanging the references gives the opposite inequality, so $\Delta (\varphi ,\psi )\leq D$.

For bounded measurable $G$, take continuous approximations with the same uniform bound converging in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$; the continuity theorem theorem 15.1.3, applied to each of the two references, extends the scalar bound to $G$. Finally, a change of volume $\mathrm{d}V'=\mathrm{e}^ a\mathrm{d}V$ replaces each $B(G)$ by $B(G+a)$, and translation by $a\in C(X)$ is a bijection of the space of tests, so the supremum is unchanged. $\square$

Proposition 15.7.3. Let $\varphi _ j,\varphi $ be model references in the same background with $\Delta (\varphi _ j,\varphi )\to 0$. Let $G_ j,G$ be uniformly bounded measurable functions with $G_ j\to G$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$, and choose $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_{\varphi _ j}(G_ j)$. Then the densities of the $\nu _ j$ are uniformly integrable, every weak cluster point of $(\nu _ j)$ belongs to $\mathcal A_\varphi (G)$, and a weakly convergent subsequence also converges weakly in $L^1$. If $\mathcal A_\varphi (G)$ is a singleton, the whole sequence converges to it in both senses.

Proof. Write $\delta _ j=\Delta (\varphi _ j,\varphi )$. The bounds $|B_{\varphi _ j}-B_\varphi |\leq \delta _ j$ and $|H_{\varphi _ j}-H_\varphi |\leq \delta _ j$, together with the identity $H_{\varphi _ j}(\nu _ j)=B_{\varphi _ j}(G_ j)+\int _ XG_ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j$, give

\[ 0\leq H_\varphi (\nu _ j)-\int _ XG_ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j-B_\varphi (G_ j) \leq 2\delta _ j. \]

If $\| G_ j\| _\infty \leq M$, then $H_\varphi (\nu _ j)\leq B_\varphi (0)+2M+2\delta _ j$, so all the measures lie in one finite sublevel set of $H_\varphi $. By theorem 15.3.2, their densities are uniformly integrable, and a weakly convergent subsequence also converges weakly in $L^1$.

Let $\nu $ be the limit of such a subsequence. Uniform integrability, the common bound and convergence in measure give $\int _ X|G_ j-G|\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j\to 0$; weak $L^1$ convergence applies to the bounded function $G$, and theorem 15.1.3 gives $B_\varphi (G_ j)\to B_\varphi (G)$. Lower semicontinuity of $H_\varphi $ in the last inequality yields

\[ H_\varphi (\nu )-\int _ XG\, \mathrm{d}\nu -B_\varphi (G)\leq 0. \]

The minimum formula for bounded measurable densities in theorem 15.4.1 gives the reverse inequality, and equality means exactly that $\nu \in \mathcal A_\varphi (G)$. When this set is a singleton, compactness gives convergence of the whole sequence. $\square$

The smaller-background comparison gives an explicit family of references converging to the zero reference.

Corollary 15.7.4. Let the model references $\varphi _ j$ satisfy $P_{\varepsilon _ j\theta }(\varphi _ j)\not\equiv -\infty $, where $0<\varepsilon _ j<1$ and $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$. Then $B_{\varphi _ j}\to B_0$ uniformly on $C(X)$. Let $G_ j,G\in C(X)$ with $G_ j\to G$ uniformly, and let $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_{\varphi _ j}(G_ j)$. Then every weak cluster point of $(\nu _ j)$ belongs to $\mathcal A_0(G)$, the densities of the $\nu _ j$ are uniformly integrable, and a weakly convergent subsequence also converges weakly in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$. If $\mathcal A_0(G)$ is a singleton, the whole sequence converges to it in both senses.

Proof. Put $\delta _ j=-n\log (1-\varepsilon _ j)\to 0$. Proposition 15.7.1 gives $\Delta (\varphi _ j,0)\leq \delta _ j$, which is the uniform convergence of $B_{\varphi _ j}$ to $B_0$ on $C(X)$. The assertions about supporting probabilities follow from proposition 15.7.3 with the zero reference as limit. $\square$

For ordinary divisorial types, a comparison of positive residual forms gives a geometric criterion for this convergence of functionals, as the next two sections show. We do not know general convergence criteria for references with degenerate residuals or with arbitrary singularities.

15.8 Varying positive backgrounds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

A two-sided inequality between two Hermitian forms compares their normalization scalars uniformly in the logarithmic density. This turns geometric convergence of backgrounds into the uniform comparison of functionals used above.

From this section on, each section specifies its own background and reference. Here we vary a Hermitian form $\beta $ on $X$ and use the zero reference. Write $B_\beta $, $H_\beta $ and $\mathcal A_\beta $ for the normalization scalar, its dual functional and the supporting sets, defined as above with $\theta =\beta $ and $\varphi =0$. For two such backgrounds, set

\[ d(B_\beta ,B_{\beta '})=\sup _{F\in L^\infty (\mathrm{d}V)}|B_\beta (F)-B_{\beta '}(F)|. \]

The supremum ranges over all bounded measurable tests, with no common bound on their norms.

Theorem 15.8.1. Suppose $0<\varepsilon <1$ and $(1-\varepsilon )\beta \leq \beta '\leq (1+\varepsilon )\beta $. For every bounded measurable $F$,

15.29
\begin{equation} n\log (1-\varepsilon )\leq B_{\beta '}(F)-B_\beta (F)\leq n\log (1+\varepsilon ). \end{equation}

In particular, $d(B_\beta ,B_{\beta '})\leq -n\log (1-\varepsilon )$. The same conclusion holds if the two inequalities between forms are satisfied by positive representatives $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$ and $\beta '+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h'$, with $h,h'$ smooth real functions.

Proof. First let $F$ be smooth, and let $v,v'$ be smooth solutions in the two backgrounds. At a maximum point of $v'-(1+\varepsilon )v$,

\[ 0<\beta '+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v'\leq (1+\varepsilon )(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v). \]

Taking determinants and canceling $\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V$ gives the upper bound. At a minimum point of $v'-(1-\varepsilon )v$, the reverse inequality between forms gives the lower bound. For bounded measurable $F$, choose smooth uniformly bounded approximations converging in $L^1$ and apply theorem 15.1.3 in each of the two backgrounds. Finally, the correspondence $v\mapsto v-h$ preserves both boundedness and the current, so $B_{\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h}=B_\beta $ whenever both backgrounds are positive. This proves the assertion about representatives. $\square$

Corollary 15.8.2. Let $\beta _ j$ be Hermitian forms with $(1-\varepsilon _ j)\beta \leq \beta _ j\leq (1+\varepsilon _ j)\beta $, where $0<\varepsilon _ j<1$ and $\varepsilon _ j\to 0$. Suppose that $F_ j\to F$ in $L^1(\mathrm{d}V)$ with a common $L^\infty $ bound. Then $B_{\beta _ j}(F_ j)\to B_\beta (F)$. For any $\nu _ j\in \mathcal A_{\beta _ j}(F_ j)$, the densities are uniformly integrable, and every subsequence has a further subsequence converging weakly in $L^1$ to an element of $\mathcal A_\beta (F)$. If this set is a singleton, which is the case in particular when $F$ is smooth, the whole sequence converges to it weakly in $L^1$ and as probability measures.

Proof. Put $\delta _ j=-n\log (1-\varepsilon _ j)$. The scalar assertion follows from (15.29) and theorem 15.1.3 in the fixed background $\beta $. Taking suprema over tests gives the inequalities, valid also for infinite values,

\[ H_{\beta _ j}\leq H_\beta +\delta _ j,\qquad H_\beta \leq H_{\beta _ j}+\delta _ j. \]

In particular, the two duals are finite on the same measures, and the identity $H_{\beta _ j}(\nu _ j)=B_{\beta _ j}(F_ j)+\int _ XF_ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j$ gives

\[ 0\leq H_\beta (\nu _ j)-B_\beta (F_ j)-\int _ XF_ j\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j\leq 2\delta _ j. \]

If $\| F_ j\| _\infty \leq M$, this puts the measures in the fixed sublevel set $\{ H_\beta \leq B_\beta (0)+2M+2\sup _ j\delta _ j\} $, to which theorem 15.3.2 applies. For a weak $L^1$ cluster point, uniform integrability gives $\int _ X|F_ j-F|\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j\to 0$, and lower semicontinuity in the last inequality, together with the minimum formula of theorem 15.4.1, identifies the limit as a supporting probability at $F$. Compactness gives convergence of the whole sequence when that supporting probability is unique, and theorem 15.2.5 gives uniqueness for smooth $F$. $\square$

Smooth changes of the background also control the potentials, because the inverse of the linearized equation used in proposition 9.3.1 remains available when the form itself is included among the parameters.

Proposition 15.8.3. Let $\beta _ j,\beta $ be Hermitian forms and $F_ j,F$ smooth real functions with $\beta _ j\to \beta $ and $F_ j\to F$ in $C^\infty $. Then the solutions $v_ j$ for $(\beta _ j,F_ j)$ normalized by $\int _ Xv_ j\, \mathrm{d}V=0$ converge in $C^\infty $ to the similarly normalized solution $v$ for $(\beta ,F)$, and their scalars converge as well.

Proof. Fix $k\geq 0$ and $0<\alpha <1$. On the open set where $\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w>0$, consider the smooth map

\[ (\eta ,G,w,a)\longmapsto \log \frac{(\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n}{\mathrm{d}V}-a-G \]

defined for $C^{k,\alpha }$ forms $\eta $ and functions $G$, mean-zero $C^{k+2,\alpha }$ potentials $w$ and $a\in \mathbb R$, with values in $C^{k,\alpha }$. At the limiting solution its derivative in $(w,a)$ is $(h,q)\mapsto \Delta _\Omega h-q$, where $\Omega =\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$ and $\Delta _\Omega h=n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h\wedge \Omega ^{n-1}/\Omega ^ n$. Given a target $g$, choose $q=-\int _ Xg\, \mathrm{d}\mu _ v$, where $\mu _ v$ is the adjoint probability of proposition 4.8.1. The Fredholm alternative then gives a unique mean-zero $h$ with $\Delta _\Omega h=g+q$, and the inverse is bounded as in proposition 9.3.1. In dimension one this is the Fredholm alternative for the Poisson equation.

The implicit function theorem gives a local branch of solutions depending continuously on the parameters in these spaces. For smooth parameters, uniqueness of bounded solutions modulo constants, corollary 11.4.6, identifies this branch with the global smooth solution. Thus $v_ j\to v$ in $C^{k+2,\alpha }$ for every $k$, which proves the assertion. $\square$

The smooth adjoint probabilities determine the functional up to its value at zero. If $\nu _{\beta ,F}$ denotes the unique supporting probability at a smooth $F$, integrating the derivative of theorem 15.2.5 along the path $s\mapsto sF$ gives

15.30
\begin{equation} B_\beta (F)=B_\beta (0)-\int _0^1\! \int _ XF\, \mathrm{d}\nu _{\beta ,sF}\, \mathrm{d}s. \end{equation}

Here the derivative is continuous along the path by the implicit function theorem. Consequently, two Hermitian backgrounds on the same $X$, with the same $\mathrm{d}V$, have the same functional on bounded measurable tests if and only if their values at zero and all their adjoint probabilities at smooth tests agree. One direction follows by differentiation; the other follows from (15.30) for smooth tests and from bounded $L^1$ approximation for the remaining tests.

15.9 Divisorial types and moving sections

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a reference given by logarithms of holomorphic sections, removing its divisorial part identifies its ordinary singularity type with the bounded potentials of the residual background. The normalization scalar therefore depends only on the residual form, whereas convergence of the full potentials also requires control of the sections defining the divisors.

In this section, $\theta $ is a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Fix finitely many holomorphic line bundles $L_ r$ on $X$ with smooth Hermitian metrics $h_ r$, nonzero holomorphic sections $s_ r$, and weights $t_ r>0$. Put

\[ \varphi =\sum _ r t_ r\log |s_ r|_{h_ r}^2,\qquad \Gamma =\sum _ r t_ r\operatorname {div}(s_ r),\qquad \beta =\theta -\sum _ r t_ rc_1(L_ r,h_ r), \]

where $c_1(L_ r,h_ r)$ is the Chern curvature form, and assume that the residual form $\beta $ is positive. The empty family is allowed, with $\varphi =0$ and $\beta =\theta $.

Theorem 15.9.1. The ordinary singularity type of $\varphi $ is described by

\[ \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\sim \varphi \} =\{ \varphi +w:w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )\cap L^\infty (X)\} , \]

and for each such $w$,

15.31
\begin{equation} \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\varphi +w)=(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n. \end{equation}

For every bounded measurable $F$, the equation (15.1) in this ordinary type has a unique constant and a solution unique modulo constants. Its normalization scalar $B_{\theta ,[\varphi ]}$ satisfies

\[ B_{\theta ,[\varphi ]}(F)=B_\beta (F), \]

so its dual functional and supporting sets coincide with $H_\beta $ and $\mathcal A_\beta $.

Proof. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi =[\Gamma ]+\beta $, so every function on the right is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic and has the type of $\varphi $. Conversely, let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $u\sim \varphi $. Then $u$ has the same generic Lelong numbers as $\varphi $ along the components of $\Gamma $, namely the coefficients of $\Gamma $, and lemma 1.17.1 gives

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-[\Gamma ]\geq 0. \]

The locally integrable difference $w=u-\varphi $ therefore has a $\beta $-plurisubharmonic representative. It is bounded off the divisor because $u\sim \varphi $, and the local submean property preserves both bounds across the divisor. Since $u$ and $\varphi +w$ agree almost everywhere, they are equal as quasi-plurisubharmonic functions.

Off the divisor, the currents $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi +w)$ and $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$ agree, and locality identifies their Monge–Ampère products there. The extended measure on the left gives the divisor zero mass, and so does the bounded product on the right, which is finite and charges no analytic set by section 1.7. This proves (15.31). In the background $\beta $, the equation with bounded density has a continuous solution and a unique constant by theorem 10.1.1, proposition 10.6.1. The density $\mathrm{e}^ F$ is bounded above and below by positive constants, so corollary 11.4.6 gives uniqueness modulo constants. The correspondence transfers these assertions to the whole ordinary type and identifies the functionals. $\square$

In particular, the comparison of backgrounds and the stability of supporting probabilities in theorem 15.8.1, corollary 15.8.2 apply whenever the positive residual forms satisfy the two-sided inequalities there; the location of the divisors does not enter these estimates. This statement concerns ordinary types. To interpret it in terms of model references, one also needs the envelope $P_\theta [\varphi ]$ to differ from $\varphi $ by a bounded function. For a Hermitian $\theta $, lemma 15.9.3 below proves this bounded difference and the smaller-background model condition.

For a fixed positive residual $\beta $, uniformly bounded $F_ j\to F$ in $L^1$ give uniform convergence of the residual solutions normalized by $\sup _ Xv_ j=0$. Indeed, continuity of the scalar gives uniformly bounded densities $\mathrm{e}^{B_\beta (F_ j)+F_ j}$ converging in every $L^ p$, $p<\infty $. The compactness theorem theorem 10.3.4 gives uniform convergence of subsequences, continuity of bounded products identifies the limiting equation, and corollary 11.4.6 identifies every limit. Subtracting the means gives the same conclusion for solutions normalized to have mean zero.

Theorem 15.9.2. Keep the line bundles and metrics fixed. Let nonzero sections $s_{jr}$ converge to $s_ r\ne 0$ in the finite-dimensional spaces of global sections, and let positive weights satisfy $t_{jr}\to t_ r>0$. Given Hermitian forms $\beta _ j$, define $\varphi _ j$ and $\theta _ j=\beta _ j+\sum _ rt_{jr}c_1(L_ r,h_ r)$ by the preceding formulas. Suppose either that $\beta _ j\to \beta $ in $C^\infty $ for a Hermitian form $\beta >0$ and $F_ j\to F$ in $C^\infty $, or that $\beta _ j=\beta $ for all $j$ and the tests $F_ j$ converge to $F$ in $L^1$ with a common $L^\infty $ bound. Then $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ in $L^1$, and the solutions $u_ j\sim \varphi _ j$ for $(\theta _ j,F_ j)$, normalized by $\sup _ Xu_ j=0$, converge in $L^1$ to the similarly normalized solution $u\sim \varphi $. For the residual solutions $v_ j$ normalized to have mean zero, one also has

\[ \sup _ X(\varphi _ j+v_ j)\longrightarrow \sup _ X(\varphi +v). \]

The residuals converge smoothly in the first regime and uniformly in the second.

Proof. The convergence of the residual solutions follows from proposition 15.8.3 in the first case and from the preceding compactness argument in the second. The fixed metrics and bounded weights give a common smooth lower bound for $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ j$. The convergent sections give a common upper bound for $\varphi _ j$ and convergence at a point outside all limiting divisors, so the suprema of the $\varphi _ j$ are bounded above and below. By theorem 1.4.2, every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in $L^1$. Off the limiting divisors, convergence of the sections and weights gives local uniform convergence to $\varphi $; since those divisors have zero volume, every $L^1$ cluster point equals $\varphi $. Thus $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ in $L^1(X)$.

Put $C_ j=\sup _ X(\varphi _ j+v_ j)$ and $C=\sup _ X(\varphi +v)$. The sums converge in $L^1$ and have a common smooth lower bound for their complex Hessians, since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j\geq -\beta _ j$ and the residual forms are uniformly bounded. Applying lemma 1.4.1 at maximum points gives $\limsup _ jC_ j\leq C$. A maximum point of $\varphi +v$ lies off the poles of $\varphi $, and evaluation there, using local uniform convergence, gives $\liminf _ jC_ j\geq C$.

It follows that $u_ j=\varphi _ j+v_ j-C_ j\to \varphi +v-C=u$ in $L^1$. For an empty family of divisors, the assertion reduces to the convergence of the residual solutions and of their suprema. $\square$

15.9.1 One-sided divisorial classes and sharp thresholds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The ordinary-type correspondence extends to potentials with additional poles. It then identifies the largest subsolution coefficient in the whole one-sided class, even for merely integrable data. For smooth positive data, the prescribed divisor causes a strict loss relative to the global coefficient.

Keep the divisorial setting above, assume also that $\theta $ is a Hermitian form and $n\ge 2$, and subtract a constant from $\varphi $ so that $\sup _ X\varphi =0$. Write $S=\operatorname {Supp}\Gamma $ and $\psi =P_\theta [\varphi ]$. For a nonempty class $\mathcal A\subset \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and nonzero $0\le f\in L^1(\mathrm dV)$, define

\[ b_{\theta ,\mathcal A}(f)=\sup \{ c\ge 0:\text{some }u\in \mathcal A \text{ satisfies }\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (u) \ge cf\, \mathrm dV\} . \]

The three classes considered here are

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal A_{\preceq }& =\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\preceq \varphi \} ,\\ \mathcal A_{\sim }& =\{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\sim \varphi \} ,\\ \mathcal A_{\mathrm{env}}& =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\psi ). \end{aligned} \]

The following lemma supplies the genuine reference $\psi $ used in the last definition.

Lemma 15.9.3. The map $w\mapsto \varphi +w$ is a bijection from $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$ onto $\mathcal A_{\preceq }$, and

\[ \mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\theta (\varphi +w) =\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_\beta (w). \]

Its bounded members correspond to $\mathcal A_{\sim }$. The envelope $\psi $ is genuine, satisfies $\varphi \le \psi \le \varphi +K$ for some finite $K$, meets the model condition (13.26), and

\[ \mathcal A_{\sim }\subset \mathcal A_{\mathrm{env}} \subset \mathcal A_{\preceq }. \]

Proof. If $u\preceq \varphi $, its generic Lelong numbers dominate the coefficients of $\Gamma $. By lemma 1.17.1, $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-[\Gamma ]\ge 0$. The locally integrable distribution $w=u-\varphi $ therefore has a canonical $\beta $-psh representative. Off $S$ it equals the difference and is bounded above. The identity $u=\varphi +w$ holds almost everywhere, hence everywhere for the canonical quasi-psh functions. Conversely, $w\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\beta )$ has finite supremum, and

\[ \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\varphi +w)=\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w+[\Gamma ]\ge 0, \qquad \varphi +w\preceq \varphi . \]

These maps are inverse. The bounded correspondence is theorem 15.9.1. Off $S$ the currents agree; locality of the extended products identifies their measures there. Both measures give the analytic set $S$ zero mass by theorem 6.1.2, even when $w$ has additional poles or infinite mass. This proves the measure identity on $X$ without subtracting infinite pointwise values.

For an envelope competitor $v\le 0$ with $v\preceq \varphi $, the corresponding $w=v-\varphi $ satisfies $w\le -\varphi $ off $S$. Hence

\[ \int _ Xw^+\, \mathrm dV\le \int _ X(-\varphi )\, \mathrm dV<\infty , \]

uniformly in $v$. On a fixed finite cover by inner and outer coordinate balls, add smooth quadratic corrections making $w$ locally psh. Submean at a point attaining $\sup _ Xw$, with uniform correction and volume-ratio bounds, gives $\sup _ Xw\le C_1\int _ Xw^+\, \mathrm dV+C_2\le K$. Thus $v\le \varphi +K$ canonically. The function $\varphi $ itself is a competitor, so the ordinary envelope construction gives a genuine $\theta $-psh function between $\varphi $ and $\varphi +K$. The upper bound survives upper regularization because $\varphi +K$ is upper semicontinuous. Ordinary equivalence gives $P_\theta [\psi ]=P_\theta [\varphi ]=\psi $. For $\varepsilon <1$ sufficiently close to one, $\beta -(1-\varepsilon )\theta >0$, so $\varphi $ is an $\varepsilon \theta $-psh competitor below $\psi $. Hence $P_{\varepsilon \theta }(\psi )$ is not identically $-\infty $, as required in (13.26).

If $u\sim \varphi \sim \psi $, choose $K_ u\ge 0$ with $\psi -K_ u\le u$. For each $t\ge 1$, the $\theta $-psh function $a_ t=\psi -tK_ u$ obeys

\[ t^{-1}a_ t+(1-t^{-1})\psi =\psi -K_ u\le u. \]

This proves relative envelope-class membership in definition 13.4.1, including the convention at $t=1$ and common poles. The other containment follows from its defining order $u\preceq \psi \sim \varphi $. $\square$

Theorem 15.9.4. For every nonzero nonnegative $f\in L^1(\mathrm dV)$,

\[ b_{\theta ,\mathcal A_{\preceq }}(f)=b_\beta (f), \]

and this coefficient is attained. If $f$ is smooth and strictly positive, then

\[ b_{\theta ,\mathcal A_{\sim }}(f) =b_{\theta ,\mathcal A_{\mathrm{env}}}(f) =b_{\theta ,\mathcal A_{\preceq }}(f)=c_\beta (f), \]

with an exact finite equation attained in the ordinary type of $\varphi $. If also $\Gamma \ne 0$, then

\[ 0<c_\beta (f)<c_\theta (f)=b_\theta (f). \]

Proof. The correspondence lemma and theorem 14.27.2 for the Hermitian form $\beta $ prove the first assertion, including attainment. This assertion allows additional poles and excess or infinite mass. For smooth positive $f$, theorem 9.1.1 gives a smooth $w$ with $\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w>0$ and $(\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n=c_\beta (f)f\, \mathrm dV$. The potential $\varphi +w$ belongs to the ordinary type and hence to all three classes. The class containments and the first assertion give equality of their coefficients; bounded-data normalization identifies $b_\beta (f)=c_\beta (f)$. The exact measure is finite.

It remains to prove strictness. Let $v$ be the smooth solution for $\theta $, and put $\sigma =\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$, $\eta =\beta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w$. The one-sided subsolution gives $c_\beta (f)\le b_\theta (f)=c_\theta (f)$. If equality held, then on $X\setminus S$ the smooth function $a=\varphi +w-v$ would satisfy

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a=\eta -\sigma ,\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge Q=0,\qquad Q=\sum _{j=0}^{n-1}\eta ^ j\wedge \sigma ^{n-1-j}. \]

The form $Q$ is smooth and strictly positive, so it defines a real second-order operator $L$ with positive definite principal matrix and no zeroth-order term. Also $a\to -\infty $ near $S$, so its maximum is attained inside a component $U$ of $X\setminus S$. The strong maximum principle makes $a$ constant on $U$. To recall its local argument, put $z=\max _ Ua-a\ge 0$. If a positive region of $z$ had a boundary zero inside $U$, choose a ball in that region touching the zero set, with closure in a coordinate chart. On its outer half-annulus, the barrier $e^{-A|x-y|^2}-e^{-AR^2}$ has strictly positive $L$ for large $A$. A sufficiently small multiple lies below $z$ on the two boundary spheres. A negative interior minimum of the difference would have nonnegative $L$, whereas its equation gives strictly negative $L$. Thus the comparison holds throughout the annulus and gives a positive inward derivative at the touching zero, contradicting $\nabla z=0$. Connectedness propagates the zero set to all of $U$. The boundary of $U$ is nonempty and lies in $S$: otherwise $U$ would be both open and closed in connected $X$, although $S\ne \varnothing $. Constancy then contradicts $a\to -\infty $ at that boundary. This proves strictness; no semipositivity of the divisor's smooth curvature representative is required. $\square$

Corollary 15.9.5. For smooth strictly positive $f$, no threshold subsolution has a nonzero divisorial component in $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$.

Proof. If such a component existed, retain a sufficiently small positive multiple $E$ of it. Choose a canonical logarithm $\ell _ E$ and a smooth closed representative $\gamma _ E$ with $[E]=\gamma _ E+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\ell _ E$. The multiple can be chosen so that $\theta -\gamma _ E>0$. \[ b_\theta (f)\le c_{\theta -\gamma _ E}(f) <c_\theta (f)=b_\theta (f), \]

Example 15.9.6. Let $(X_0,h_0)$ be a connected compact Hermitian $m$-fold, let $k\ge 2$, and let $\kappa $ be the mass-one Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ k$. On $X_0\times \mathbb P^ k$, choose a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm dV$. With pullbacks implicit, put

Proof. The Poincaré–Lelong formula gives $[D]=\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\ell _ E/\lambda )$. Dimensions and the mass-one projective factor give

\[ H^ N=CV_0R\, \mathrm dV,\qquad H_ E^ N=CV_0(1-\lambda )^ kR\, \mathrm dV. \]

Thus the zero residual solves each smooth positive equation and fixes its unique scalar. The theorem identifies all three restricted coefficients, and the correspondence gives $\mathrm{MA}^{\mathrm{ext}}_ H(\ell _ E)=H_ E^ N$. Under the factor hypothesis, pullbacks of bounded $h_0$-psh potentials with mass tending to zero have masses multiplied respectively by $C$ and $C(1-\lambda )^ k$. Bounded submersion pullback and the product expansion therefore give zero lower mass for both backgrounds. A closed positive $h_0$ would instead have every bounded mass equal to $V_0$ by Stokes, so both backgrounds are nonclosed. $\square$

15.10 The mass determined by the functional

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

In a closed background, the information lost by the scalar functional can be described exactly: for the model references below, two functionals agree precisely when the masses agree. This also gives examples in which the supporting probabilities stay fixed while the potentials have different limits.

In this section $X$ is a compact Kähler manifold and $\theta $ is any smooth closed big real $(1,1)$-form. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfy $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $, have small unbounded locus in the sense of section 1.12, and have positive mass

\[ m_\varphi =\int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (\varphi )>0. \]

Write $B_{\theta ,\varphi }$ and $\mathcal A_{\theta ,\varphi }$ for the normalization scalar and the supporting sets of the equation (15.1) in the ordinary type of $\varphi $, and put $Z_ F=\int _ X\mathrm{e}^ F\, \mathrm{d}V$.

Theorem 15.10.1. For every such pair $(\theta ,\varphi )$ and every bounded measurable $F$, the equation in the ordinary type of $\varphi $ has a unique constant and a solution unique modulo constants. Its normalization scalar is

\[ B_{\theta ,\varphi }(F)=\log m_\varphi -\log Z_ F. \]

For continuous real $F$, its supporting set is

\[ \mathcal A_{\theta ,\varphi }(F)=\{ \mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V/Z_ F\} . \]

For two such pairs on the same $X$, with the same $\mathrm{d}V$ and possibly in different classes,

15.37
\begin{equation} \sup _{F\in L^\infty }|B_{\theta ,\varphi }(F)-B_{\theta ',\psi }(F)| =|\log m_\varphi -\log m_\psi |. \end{equation}

Thus an invariant of these pairs is determined by the whole functional if and only if it is constant on every fiber of the map $(\theta ,\varphi )\mapsto m_\varphi $.

Proof. Existence follows from corollary 32.8.3. Indeed, a Kähler manifold has the bounded mass property by lemma 21.4.1, and since in a closed background all potentials of the ordinary type of $\varphi $ have the same mass $m_\varphi >0$, the reference satisfies the positive-volume model condition (30.10). Uniqueness modulo constants is the classical theorem on equations with prescribed singularities in closed big classes, which applies under the model and small-unbounded-locus hypotheses; see the notes in section 34.11.

Every potential of the ordinary type of $\varphi $ has mass $m_\varphi $, so integrating the equation forces $cZ_ F=m_\varphi $, which is the scalar formula. For continuous $F$, Jensen's inequality shows, as for (15.25), that the normalized exponential measure is a supporting probability, and differentiating at zero in both signs of every continuous direction shows that it is the only one. Subtracting the scalar formulas gives (15.37). Finally, $m_\varphi =\mathrm{d}V(X)\exp B_{\theta ,\varphi }(0)$, so the functional and the mass determine each other, and an invariant factors through either one exactly when it is constant on the fibers of the mass map. $\square$

For an ordinary divisorial type whose residual form $\beta $ is positive and closed, theorem 15.9.1 gives the same formula with mass $\int _ X\beta ^ n$, since Stokes' theorem for bounded products in a closed background fixes the mass of every bounded $\beta $-plurisubharmonic function. This computation does not require the real weighted sum of divisors to have a model representative. If $\beta _ j\to \beta $ uniformly, the masses converge by continuity of $\beta \mapsto \int _ X\beta ^ n$; under the two-sided bounds $(1-\varepsilon _ j)\beta \leq \beta _ j\leq (1+\varepsilon _ j)\beta $ one has the sharper inequalities $(1-\varepsilon _ j)^ n\int \beta ^ n\leq \int \beta _ j^ n\leq (1+\varepsilon _ j)^ n\int \beta ^ n$.

Example 15.10.2. Let $X=\mathbb P^ n$ with $n\geq 1$, let $\theta $ be the Fubini–Study form, normalized by $\int _ X\theta ^ n=1$, and let $D$ be a hyperplane. Let $s_ D$ be a section of $\mathcal O(1)$ defining $D$, measured with the Fubini–Study metric and rescaled so that $a_ D=\log |s_ D|^2\leq 0$, and choose $t\in (0,1)$. Then

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ D=[D]-\theta ,\qquad \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(ta_ D)=(1-t)\theta +t[D]. \]

Put $\varphi _{t,D}=P_\theta [ta_ D]$. By proposition 16.2.7, this envelope fixed point has the ordinary type of $ta_ D$. Moreover $ta_ D\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,t\theta )$ and $ta_ D\leq \varphi _{t,D}$, so $\varphi _{t,D}$ satisfies (13.26) with $\varepsilon =t$. Off $D$, the potential $ta_ D$ has Monge–Ampère measure $(1-t)^ n\theta ^ n$, and its non-pluripolar measure puts no mass on $D$.

\[ m_{\varphi _{t,D}}=(1-t)^ n,\qquad B_{\varphi _{t,D}}(G)-B_0(G)=n\log (1-t)\quad (G\in C(X,\mathbb R)). \]

Thus the constant in proposition 15.7.1 is sharp.

Now let $n=1$, and take for $D$ two distinct points $p,q\in \mathbb P^1$. The corresponding references have distance zero, although $\varphi _{t,p}$ is unbounded near $p$ and bounded near $q$, while $\varphi _{t,q}$ behaves in the opposite way; in particular they are not ordinarily equivalent. For every positive integer $m$,

\[ \mathcal I(m\varphi _{t,p})=\mathcal O(-\lfloor mt\rfloor p),\qquad \mathcal I(m\varphi _{t,q})=\mathcal O(-\lfloor mt\rfloor q), \]

by lemma 1.16.1, and for $mt\geq 1$ these ideals differ. Thus the distance $\Delta $ of (15.28) does not even separate multiplier-ideal types, and it determines neither the ordinary nor the ideal singularity type.

Fix a smooth real function $F$ on $\mathbb P^1$, and let $w_ F$ be the solution of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ F=\mathrm{e}^ F\mathrm{d}V/Z_ F$ with $\int _ Xw_ F\, \mathrm{d}V=0$. The normalized solutions for $D=p$ and $D=q$ are

\[ u_{t,D}=ta_ D+(1-t)w_ F-\sup _ X\bigl (ta_ D+(1-t)w_ F\bigr ). \]

Their difference satisfies $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_{t,p}-u_{t,q})=t([p]-[q])\ne 0$. Hence the sequence of solutions obtained by alternating between $D=p$ and $D=q$ has two different $L^1$ cluster points, although the scalar functional and all its supporting sets are the same at every step. Example 15.10.3. On $\mathbb P^ n$, $n\geq 2$, let $\omega $ be the Fubini–Study form, normalized as in example 15.10.2, and let $f$ be a nonconstant smooth real function. Fix $0<t<1$ and put

\[ \beta =\mathrm{e}^ f\omega ,\qquad \theta =\beta +t\omega ,\qquad \varphi _ D=t\log |s_ D|^2. \]

Here $s_ D$ defines a hyperplane $D$ as in example 15.10.2, and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _ D=\beta +t[D]$, so every hyperplane has the same positive residual form $\beta $. This form is not closed: $\mathrm{d}\beta =\mathrm{e}^ f\mathrm{d}f\wedge \omega \not\equiv 0$, since $\mathrm{d}f$ is nonzero somewhere and wedging a one-form with $\omega ^{n-1}$ is injective. By theorem 15.9.1, the ordinary types of all $\varphi _ D$ have the same functional $B_\beta $ on bounded tests, hence the same supporting sets. At a smooth test, the common supporting probability is the Gauduchon adjoint probability of theorem 15.2.5.

If $v_ F$ is the common smooth residual solution, the normalized solutions are the functions $\varphi _ D+v_ F-\sup _ X(\varphi _ D+v_ F)$. Two distinct hyperplanes $D_1,D_2$ give different poles, different divisorial coefficients and, for sufficiently large $k$, different multiplier ideals $\mathcal O(-\lfloor kt\rfloor D_ i)$, by the calculation in normal coordinates of lemma 1.16.1. The potentials differ by more than a constant, since the difference of their complex Hessians is $t([D_1]-[D_2])$, and alternating between the two hyperplanes again produces two distinct $L^1$ limits. These conclusions come from the common residual form; for general nonclosed backgrounds we do not claim that the functional is determined by a mass.

15.11 Convex-body criteria for varying references

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

When the singularities of a torus-invariant reference are described by a convex body, the closed mass formula of theorem 15.10.1 turns convergence of the convex bodies into convergence of the functionals. A translated simplex contained in all the bodies keeps the limiting reference solvable, and the Hausdorff distance then controls the functionals uniformly over all continuous tests, even though the ordinary types vary.

Let $n\geq 2$, and let $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ n$, normalized by $\int _{\mathbb P^ n}\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}^ n=1$. In homogeneous coordinates $[Z_0:\cdots :Z_ n]$, consider the torus $T=\{ Z_0\cdots Z_ n\ne 0\} $, and put

\[ x_ i=\log |Z_ i/Z_0|^2,\qquad L(x)=\log \bigl (1+\textstyle \sum _ i\mathrm{e}^{x_ i}\bigr ),\qquad \Sigma =\{ p_ i\geq 0:\textstyle \sum _ i p_ i\leq 1\} , \]

where $1\leq i\leq n$ and $\Sigma \subset \mathbb R^ n$ is the standard simplex. Functions of $x=(x_1,\ldots ,x_ n)$ are regarded as functions on $T$; thus $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}L$ on $T$. Define the continuous function $\ell $ on $\Sigma $ by

\[ \ell (p)=\sum _ i p_ i\log p_ i+ (1-\textstyle \sum _ i p_ i)\log (1-\textstyle \sum _ i p_ i), \qquad 0\log 0=0. \]

For a convex body $K\subset \Sigma $, that is, a compact convex set with nonempty interior, set

\[ h_ K(x)=\sup _{p\in K}p\cdot x,\qquad g_ K(x)=\sup _{p\in K}\{ p\cdot x-\ell (p)\} . \]

15.11.1 Canonical models and their mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Lemma 15.11.1. The functions $h_ K-L$ and $g_ K-L$ on $T$ extend uniquely to $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic functions $\psi _ K,\Phi _ K$ on $\mathbb P^ n$. They satisfy

\[ \begin{aligned} & \psi _ K\leq \Phi _ K\leq \psi _ K+\log (n+1),\qquad \sup \Phi _ K=0,\\ & P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\psi _ K]=\Phi _ K =P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\Phi _ K]. \end{aligned} \]

If $p_*+\delta \Sigma \subset K$ for some $p_*\in \mathbb R^ n$ and $0<\delta <1$, then $P_{(1-\delta )\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\Phi _ K)\not\equiv -\infty $.

Proof. The Legendre transform of $L$ is $L^*=\ell $ on $\Sigma $ and $+\infty $ outside it. Since $-\log (n+1)\leq \ell \leq 0$,

\[ h_ K\leq g_ K\leq h_ K+\log (n+1),\qquad g_ K\leq L,\qquad h_ K\leq h_\Sigma \leq L. \]

A finite convex function of the logarithmic moduli is plurisubharmonic: for a smooth convex function the complex Hessian is positive, and convolution gives the general case. On $T\cap \{ Z_ j\ne 0\} $, add the local Fubini–Study potential $\log \sum _ i|Z_ i/Z_ j|^2$; the resulting functions are again convex functions of the logarithmic moduli of the affine coordinates, and they are bounded above by that smooth potential because $h_ K,g_ K\leq L$. Lemma 1.3.7 therefore gives the extensions across the coordinate hyperplanes and the stated bounds. If $p\in \operatorname {int}K$, then $p\in \operatorname {int}\Sigma $, and at $x_ i=\log (p_ i/(1-\sum _ k p_ k))$ the supremum defining $L=\ell ^*$ is attained at $p\in K$, so $g_ K(x)=L(x)$. This proves $\sup \Phi _ K=0$.

For $C\geq \log (n+1)$, let $v_ C=P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\psi _ K+C,0)$, the envelope of the obstacle $\min (\psi _ K+C,0)$. Since $\Phi _ K$ is a competitor, $\Phi _ K\leq v_ C\leq 0$. The obstacle is upper semicontinuous, so $v_ C\leq \psi _ K+C$ everywhere. The compact torus acting by rotations of the coordinates preserves the obstacle, so $v_ C$ is invariant, and on $T$ the function $f=v_ C+L$ is a finite convex function of $x$ with $f\leq L$ and $f\leq h_ K+C$. The second inequality shows that the Legendre transform $f^*$ is $+\infty $ outside $K$, and the first gives $f^*\geq \ell $. Thus

\[ f(x)=\sup _{p\in \operatorname {dom}f^*}\{ p\cdot x-f^*(p)\} \leq g_ K(x). \]

Hence $v_ C\leq \Phi _ K$ on $T$, and since both functions are quasi-plurisubharmonic and $T$ is the complement of an analytic set, $v_ C=\Phi _ K$ on $\mathbb P^ n$. Letting $C\to \infty $ gives $P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\psi _ K]=\Phi _ K$. Since $\psi _ K\leq \Phi _ K\leq \psi _ K+\log (n+1)$, the envelopes of $\min (\Phi _ K+C,0)$ and $\min (\psi _ K+C,0)$ interlace as $C\to \infty $, so also $P_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}[\Phi _ K]=\Phi _ K$.

For the final assertion put $\varepsilon =1-\delta $, $b=\sum _ i p_{*,i}$ and $\chi =p_*\cdot x-\varepsilon L$ on $T$. The inclusion $p_*+\delta \Sigma \subset \Sigma $ implies $p_{*,i}\geq 0$ and $b\leq \varepsilon $, and the extension of $\chi $ to $\mathbb P^ n$ satisfies

\[ \varepsilon \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi =\sum _ i p_{*,i}[Z_ i=0]+(\varepsilon -b)[Z_0=0]\geq 0. \]

Since $h_ K\geq p_*\cdot x+\delta h_\Sigma $ and $L-h_\Sigma \leq \log (n+1)$, one has $\chi -\delta \log (n+1)\leq \Phi _ K$. Thus $\chi -\delta \log (n+1)$ is an $\varepsilon \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$-plurisubharmonic competitor for $P_{\varepsilon \omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\Phi _ K)$, which proves the claim. $\square$

Indeed, off $E_ q$ add a local potential of $\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}$; the resulting plurisubharmonic function is bounded above near $q$, because the original function is bounded above near the compact fiber $E_ q$, and therefore extends across $q$. \[ \mathrm{d}^{\mathrm c}g=\frac1{2\pi }\sum _ i g_ i\, \mathrm{d}\vartheta _ i, \qquad \int _ T(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g)^ n=n!\int _{\mathbb R^ n}\det (D^2g)\, \mathrm{d}x. \]

15.11.3 A family with nonanalytic isolated poles

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The hypothesis of a common simplex allows ordinary singularity types that contain no potential with analytic singularities. Let $o=[1:0:\cdots :0]$ be the origin of the affine chart $Z_0\ne 0$, with affine coordinates $z_ i=Z_ i/Z_0$. For $0<a\leq 1$, define

\[ P_ a=\{ p\in \Sigma :\textstyle \sum _ i\sqrt{p_ i}\geq \sqrt a\} , \qquad \kappa _ n=\frac{2^ n n!}{(2n)!}=\frac1{(2n-1)!!}. \]

Proposition 15.11.4. The function $\Phi _{P_ a}$ is locally bounded outside the single point $o$, where it has a pole, and it does not have the ordinary singularity type of any function with analytic singularities. Its generic Lelong number along every prime divisor of $\mathbb P^ n$ is zero, and

15.43
\begin{equation} \int _{\mathbb P^ n}\mathrm{MA}_{\omega _{\mathrm{FS}}}(\Phi _{P_ a}) =1-\kappa _ n a^ n>0. \end{equation}

The same assertions about the pole, nonanalyticity and generic Lelong numbers hold for $\pi ^*\Phi _{P_ a}$ on the blowup $\operatorname {Bl}_ q\mathbb P^ n$ at a fixed point $q\ne o$.

Proof. The function $p\mapsto \sum _ i\sqrt{p_ i}$ is concave, so $P_ a$ is convex. Set

\[ b=\frac{n+1}{2n},\qquad p_{*,i}=\frac bn, \qquad \delta =\frac{n-1}{4n}. \]

Then $b+\delta <1$ and $\sum _ i\sqrt{p_{*,i}}=\sqrt{(n+1)/2}>1$. Adding a point of $\delta \Sigma $ preserves the last inequality and keeps the sum of the coordinates below one, so $p_*+\delta \Sigma $ lies in every $P_ a$.

All the standard basis vectors $e_ i$ belong to $P_ a$, and therefore

\[ \psi _{P_ a}\geq \log \frac{\max _{1\leq i\leq n}|Z_ i|^2}{\sum _{i=0}^ n|Z_ i|^2}. \]

Hence the reference is locally bounded away from $o$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $\sum _ ip_ i\geq a/n$ on $P_ a$. When all $x_ i<0$, this yields $h_{P_ a}(x)\leq (a/n)\max _ i x_ i$, which tends to $-\infty $ as $z\to o$, and lemma 15.11.1 gives the pole at $o$. Local boundedness outside one point implies that all generic Lelong numbers along prime divisors vanish, and pulling back to the blowup at a different point introduces no pole.

To test analyticity, fix positive $\lambda _1,\ldots ,\lambda _ n$ and approach $o$ along $|z_ i|^2=\mathrm{e}^{-R\lambda _ i}$, $R\to \infty $. Writing $y_ i=\sqrt{p_ i}$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

15.44
\begin{equation} \min _{p\in P_ a}\sum _ i\lambda _ i p_ i =\frac a{\sum _ i\lambda _ i^{-1}}=:d_ a(\lambda ),\qquad y_ i=\frac{\sqrt a}{\lambda _ i\sum _ k\lambda _ k^{-1}}, \end{equation}

where the minimizer satisfies $\sum _ ip_ i\leq a\leq 1$, so that it lies in $P_ a$. Since $L(-R\lambda )\to 0$ and $\Phi _{P_ a}-\psi _{P_ a}$ is bounded, $-\Phi _{P_ a}/R\to d_ a(\lambda )$ along these curves.

Suppose that the type were that of $c\log \sum _ k|f_ k|^2$ near $o$, with $c>0$ and holomorphic germs $f_ k=\sum _\alpha c_{k,\alpha }z^\alpha $. Exponentiating the two inequalities expressing this equivalence and averaging over the arguments of the $z_ i$ gives the positive series

\[ \sum _{k,\alpha }|c_{k,\alpha }|^2\mathrm{e}^{-R\alpha \cdot \lambda }, \]

whose exponential decay rate in $R$ must be $d_ a(\lambda )/c$. Near any $\lambda $ with positive coordinates, only finitely many monomials can attain the minimum, because these coordinates have a common positive lower bound and any one nonzero coefficient bounds the degree of the minimizing monomials. The rate is therefore locally piecewise linear in $\lambda $. But

\[ d_ a(s,1,\ldots ,1)=\frac{as}{1+(n-1)s},\qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}s^2}d_ a(s,1,\ldots ,1) =-\frac{2a(n-1)}{(1+(n-1)s)^3}<0. \]

This contradiction proves nonanalyticity. The assertion persists on a blowup at a point different from $o$, which does not change a neighborhood of the pole.

15.12.1 A norm defined by the critical scalar

Mixed products turn the comparison of critical scalars into a triangle inequality for $n$th roots of densities, and the subsolution estimate associated with the strict barrier gives continuity in the corresponding Lebesgue norm.

Fix $1<q<p$ and set $\ell =nq$. On real $L^\ell (\mathrm{d}V)$ define

\[ N(a)= \begin{cases} c(|a|^ n)^{-1/n},& a\ne 0,\\ 0,& a=0. \end{cases} \]

Proposition 15.12.2. The function $N$ is a lattice norm on $L^\ell (\mathrm{d}V)$, that is, a norm satisfying $N(a)\leq N(b)$ whenever $|a|\leq |b|$. There is $C_ q<\infty $, depending only on $X,\theta ,\mathrm{d}V$ and $q$, such that

15.52
\begin{equation} N(a)\leq C_ q\| a\| _\ell ,\qquad |N(a)-N(b)|\leq C_ q\| a-b\| _\ell . \end{equation}

Consequently, for nonzero $g,h\geq 0$ in $L^ q$,

\[ |c(g)^{-1/n}-c(h)^{-1/n}| \leq C_ q\| g-h\| _ q^{1/n}. \]

Proof. If a potential $v$ of minimal type satisfies $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)\geq d g\, \mathrm{d}V$, then $d\leq c(g)$, since otherwise comparison with a critical potential for $g$ contradicts part (1) of corollary 14.12.3. Hence $g\leq h$ implies $c(g)\geq c(h)$. Uniqueness of the critical scalar also gives $c(tg)=c(g)/t$ for $t>0$. These facts prove that $N$ is monotone and absolutely homogeneous, and positivity follows from $0<c(g)<\infty $.

For the triangle inequality, let $a,b\geq 0$ be nonzero, put $A=N(a)$ and $B=N(b)$, and choose critical potentials $v_ a,v_ b$ with measures $(a/A)^ n\mathrm{d}V$ and $(b/B)^ n\mathrm{d}V$. On every relatively compact subset of $\Omega $, (1.23) gives

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\theta \left(\frac{A v_ a+B v_ b}{A+B}\right) \geq \left(\frac{a+b}{A+B}\right)^ n\mathrm{d}V, \]

where both potentials are locally bounded. Exhausting $\Omega $ gives the inequality on $X$, since $X\setminus \Omega $ has zero smooth volume. The averaged potential has minimal type, so the first paragraph gives $N(a+b)\leq A+B$. The cases where $a$ or $b$ vanishes are trivial, and for real functions we use $|a+b|\leq |a|+|b|$ and monotonicity.

Next, apply lemma 14.13.1 with $\eta =V_\theta $ to nonnegative densities of $L^ q$ norm at most one. It gives a uniform $m>0$ and subsolutions of minimal type, hence $c(g)\geq m$ for each such nonzero density. For $a\ne 0$, take $g=|a|^ n/\| a\| _\ell ^ n$, which has $L^ q$ norm one. Homogeneity gives $N(a)\leq m^{-1/n}\| a\| _\ell $, which is the first bound in (15.52), and the reverse triangle inequality gives the second. Applying the second bound to $g^{1/n},h^{1/n}$ and using $|g^{1/n}-h^{1/n}|^ n\leq |g-h|$ proves the last assertion. $\square$

Set $a=f^{1/n}\in L^\ell (\mathrm{d}V)$ and $A=N(a)=c^{-1/n}$. With $\ell '=\ell /(\ell -1)$, define

\begin{equation} \mathcal H_ a=\left\{ \eta \in L^{\ell '}(\mathrm{d}V): \eta \geq 0,\ \int _ Xa\eta \, \mathrm{d}V=A, \int _ Xb\eta \, \mathrm{d}V\leq N(b)\text{ for every }b\in L^\ell \right\} . \end{equation}

Proposition 15.12.3. The set $\mathcal H_ a$ is nonempty and weakly compact in $L^{\ell '}$. The function $\kappa $ of (15.46) is given by

\[ \kappa (r)=n\log \frac{N(a\mathrm{e}^{r/n})}{A}, \]

and it is convex, increasing and $1$-Lipschitz for the uniform norm on $C(X)$, with $\kappa (0)=0$ and $\kappa (r+b)=\kappa (r)+b$ for constants $b$. Its subdifferential at zero is

15.54
\begin{equation} \mathcal P_ f=\{ \nu _\eta :\eta \in \mathcal H_ a\} ,\qquad \mathrm{d}\nu _\eta =A^{-1}a\eta \, \mathrm{d}V. \end{equation}

15.55
\begin{equation} \sup _{\nu \in \mathcal P_ f}\int _ X|z|\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq C_ q A^{-1}\| az\| _\ell . \end{equation}

Proof. The formula for $\kappa $ follows from $N(a\mathrm{e}^{r/n})=c(\mathrm{e}^ rf)^{-1/n}$. The Hahn–Banach theorem gives a linear functional that norms $N$ at $a$. By (15.52) it is continuous on $L^\ell $, and hence is represented by some $\eta _0\in L^{\ell '}$. For $b\geq 0$, testing on $\operatorname {sgn}(\eta _0)b$ gives

\[ \int _ Xb|\eta _0|\, \mathrm{d}V\leq N(b). \]

Since $a\geq 0$ and $\int _ Xa\eta _0\, \mathrm{d}V=A=N(a)$, the same equality holds with $|\eta _0|$ in place of $\eta _0$. Monotonicity of $N$ extends the domination to real $b$, so $|\eta _0|\in \mathcal H_ a$. The defining conditions of $\mathcal H_ a$ are weakly closed and give $\| \eta \| _{\ell '}\leq C_ q$, so reflexivity proves weak compactness.

For nonzero nonnegative $b_0,b_1$, the weighted arithmetic–geometric mean inequality applied to $b_ j/N(b_ j)$, together with monotonicity and the triangle inequality, gives

\[ N(b_0^{1-t}b_1^ t)\leq N(b_0)^{1-t}N(b_1)^ t,\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1. \]

Taking $b_ j=a\mathrm{e}^{r_ j/n}$ proves convexity of $\kappa $. Monotonicity and the translation rule follow from the corresponding properties of $N$, and together they imply the Lipschitz bound.

Every $\nu _\eta $ is a probability and satisfies

\[ \frac{N(a\mathrm{e}^{r/n})}{A} \geq \int _ X\mathrm{e}^{r/n}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _\eta . \]

Jensen's inequality gives $\kappa (r)\geq \int _ Xr\, \mathrm{d}\nu _\eta $, which proves one inclusion in (15.54). The map $\eta \mapsto \nu _\eta $ is weakly continuous, since $ar\in L^\ell $ for $r\in C(X)$.

Conversely, let $\nu \in \mathcal P_ f$ and put $L(r)=\int _ Xr\, \mathrm{d}\nu $. Differentiating the curve $t\mapsto a\mathrm{e}^{tr/n}$ in $L^\ell $ at $t=0$ gives

\[ N(a\mathrm{e}^{tr/n}) =N(a)+\frac{t}{n}N'_+(a;ar)+o(t),\qquad t\downarrow 0. \]

Here the one-sided derivative $N'_+(a;\cdot )$ of the continuous convex function $N$ exists, and the error estimate follows from its Lipschitz bound. The inequality $L\leq \kappa $, applied to $tr$ and $-tr$, therefore gives

\begin{equation} |A L(r)|\leq C_ q\| ar\| _\ell ,\qquad A L(r)\leq N'_+(a;ar)\leq N(a+ar)-A. \end{equation}

Thus $T(ar)=A L(r)$ defines a continuous linear functional on the space of products $ar$, $r\in C(X)$. These products are dense in

\[ S_ a=\{ b\in L^\ell :b=0\text{ almost everywhere on }\{ a=0\} \} : \]

indeed, it suffices to approximate $b/a$ in $L^\ell (a^\ell \mathrm{d}V)$ by continuous functions. Approximating nonnegative elements of $S_ a$ by products $ar$ with $r\geq 0$ continuous shows that the continuous extension of $T$ to $S_ a$ is positive.

\[ \int _ Xb\eta \, \mathrm{d}V \leq N(b\mathbf1_{\{ a>0\} })\leq N(b). \]

Step 4: $z$ is the greatest element of the class. For any $s$ in the class and fixed $x\in \Omega $, pass to a further subsequence with $\nu _{\lambda _ j,x}\rightharpoonup \nu $, so that $\nu \in \mathcal P_ f$ by Step 1. The potential $s$ is upper semicontinuous and bounded above on $X$, so

\[ \limsup _ j\int _ Xs\, \mathrm{d}\nu _{\lambda _ j,x} \leq \int _ Xs\, \mathrm{d}\nu \leq 0. \]

For an unbounded $s$, this follows from the portmanteau theorem applied to $\max (s,-M)$, followed by $M\to \infty $; the final integral is finite by (15.55). Passing to the limit in (15.62) gives $z(x)\geq s(x)$. This holds for every $x\in \Omega $, and hence on $X$ by lemma 1.3.3. Thus every cluster potential is the greatest element $w$ of the class, and precompactness gives convergence of the whole family.

Step 5: the normalizations. The maximum of $\int _ Xw\, \mathrm{d}\nu $ over $\mathcal P_ f$ is attained, because under (15.54) it is the maximum of the weakly continuous functional $\eta \mapsto A^{-1}\int _ Xaw\eta \, \mathrm{d}V$ on $\mathcal H_ a$; note that $aw\in L^\ell $. If the maximum were negative, a small positive constant translate of $w$ would still satisfy (15.49), contrary to greatestness. Thus it is zero.

Finally, $k_\lambda \to \sup _ Xw$. Indeed, from any subsequence choose a further one with $k_\lambda \to k$. Then $v_\lambda =w_\lambda -k_\lambda $ converges in $L^1$ to $w-k$, and theorem 1.4.2 gives $\sup _ X(w-k)=0$. Thus $k=\sup _ Xw$ for every cluster point of $(k_\lambda )$. Subtracting the constants proves the convergence of $u_\lambda -\sup _ Xu_\lambda $. If $\theta $ is Hermitian, we may take $\rho =0$ and $\Omega =X$, so that local uniform convergence is uniform convergence. $\square$

Together with proposition 14.16.2, the theorem also gives convergence of the normalized potentials in the topology of uniform convergence of differences on $\Omega $ and in every local $C^\beta $ topology stated there: compactness in those topologies and uniqueness of the $L^1$ limit force every subsequence to have the same limit. The fact that the limit is selected does not imply that the critical equation has a unique normalized solution.

\[ \int \left(\Delta _1f-\lambda _ j\frac Ab f\right)\, \mathrm{d}T_ j=0. \]

For $j=0$, testing on spherical harmonics of the first sphere shows that $T_0=C\omega _1$. Their finite span is uniformly dense, because restrictions of polynomials decompose into harmonics and polynomials are dense on the sphere. The first marginal of $\mu $ is therefore $CA\omega _1$, and probability normalization gives $C=1/a$. For every nonconstant mode, lemma 15.13.5 with $V=\lambda _ jA/b>0$ gives $T_ j=0$. As these are identities of measures, their test functions may then be multiplied by the continuous function $A$. They determine all spherical harmonic moments of $\mu $. Uniform density of harmonics and of finite product tests gives $\mu =(A/a)\omega _1\wedge \omega _2$.

Supporting probabilities and weights. Let $\mu \in \mathcal A_\phi (F)$ and, for smooth real $h$, set

\[ F_ t=F+\log \frac{(\Omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h)^2}{\Omega ^2}. \]

This is continuous, and $u+th$ solves the ordinary-type equation with density $\mathrm{e}^{F_ t}$ and scalar $c_0$. Thus $B_\phi (F_ t)=B_\phi (F)$, by uniqueness of the scalar. Uniform positivity gives $F_ t-F=tL_ uh+O(t^2)$ in the uniform norm. Apply (15.5) for both signs of $t$ to obtain $\int L_ uh\, \mathrm{d}\mu =0$. The perturbed density need not depend on the first curve alone: the inequality is the one on all of $C(X)$. Invariant uniqueness and nonemptiness of $\mathcal A_\phi (F)$ from theorem 15.2.1 now prove (15.79).

Finally, $\Omega ^2/\mathrm{d}W$ is bounded above and below by positive constants, so a normalized $r$ as in the statement belongs to $L^1(\mathrm{d}W)$ and $r\Omega $ is an order-zero current. Distributional Stokes makes $r\Omega ^2$ invariant. The probability uniqueness just proved gives $r\Omega ^2=\rho \Omega ^2$, hence $r=\rho $ almost everywhere. $\square$

Proposition 15.13.7. Let $w_ j\in C^\infty (X)$ be real and suppose

\[ \Omega _ j=\eta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_ j>0,\qquad \Omega _ j\longrightarrow \Omega \quad \text{uniformly as }(1,1)\text{-forms}. \]

Put $u_ j=\psi +w_ j$ and $F_ j=\log (\Omega _ j^2/(c_0\mathrm{d}V))$. Let $\rho _ j>0$ be the smooth residual adjoint weight, normalized by

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\rho _ j\Omega _ j)=0,\qquad \int _ X\rho _ j\Omega _ j^2=1. \]

Then $F_ j\to F$ uniformly, and the entire sequence satisfies

\[ \rho _ j\Omega _ j^2\rightharpoonup \nu ,\qquad \rho _ j\Omega _ j\rightharpoonup Q,\qquad \rho _ j\, \mathrm{d}W\rightharpoonup \rho \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

Such approximations exist with $w_ j\to w_0$ uniformly and can depend on both curve variables.

Proof. Existence of the smooth positive weights is theorem 4.1.2, applied to the residual metrics $\Omega _ j$ on the surface. Put $\nu _ j=\rho _ j\Omega _ j^2$ and $L_ jh=2\Omega _ j\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h/\Omega _ j^2$. Distributional Stokes gives $\int L_ jh\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j=0$. Uniform convergence to the positive form $\Omega $ supplies a common determinant lower bound and gives $L_ jh\to L_ uh$ uniformly for every fixed smooth $h$. Every weak cluster point of the probabilities $\nu _ j$ is therefore invariant for $L_ u$. By theorem 15.13.6, it equals $\nu $, proving convergence of the whole sequence. The same determinant convergence gives $F_ j\to F$ uniformly.

For a smooth real $(1,1)$ test form $\xi $, the ratios $\Omega _ j\wedge \xi /\Omega _ j^2$ converge uniformly to $\Omega \wedge \xi /\Omega ^2$. Consequently,

\[ \int _ X\rho _ j\Omega _ j\wedge \xi =\int _ X\frac{\Omega _ j\wedge \xi }{\Omega _ j^2}\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \longrightarrow \int _ X\rho \Omega \wedge \xi . \]

Similarly, the positive densities $p_ j=\Omega _ j^2/\mathrm{d}W$ converge uniformly to $p=\Omega ^2/\mathrm{d}W$. For every $g\in C(X)$,

\[ \int _ Xg\rho _ j\, \mathrm{d}W =\int _ X(g/p_ j)\, \mathrm{d}\nu _ j \longrightarrow \int _ X(g/p)\, \mathrm{d}\nu =\int _ Xg\rho \, \mathrm{d}W. \]

These arguments require no pointwise control of the weights.

For existence, heat-smooth $A$ on the first sphere to positive smooth functions $A_ j$ of the same mean $a$, converging uniformly to $A$. Proof. The height $t$ is uniform on $[-1,1]$, so $\int A\omega _1=1$ and $\int tA\omega _1=0$. Since $\Omega ^2=2bA\, \mathrm{d}W$, its mass is four and its normalized height moment is

15.14 Residual regularity in radial singularity types

Subtracting a prescribed singularity separates regularity of the residual from the pole of the solution. On projective space, central radial mass gives an exact inverse for the equation. 15.14.1 The prescribed ordinary type

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $k\geq 2$, let $\kappa $ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ k$ with $\int \kappa ^ k=1$, and put $o=[1:0:\cdots :0]$. On the chart $Z_0\ne 0$ write $z_ i=Z_ i/Z_0$ and set

\[ r=\sum _{j=1}^ k|z_ j|^2,\qquad P=\frac r{1+r},\qquad T=1-\log P, \qquad u_\alpha =1-T^\alpha \quad (0<\alpha <1). \]

The ratio $P=\sum _{i=1}^ k|Z_ i|^2/\sum _{i=0}^ k|Z_ i|^2$ is global. Give $u_\alpha $ its canonical value $-\infty $ at $o$ and value zero on the hyperplane $Z_0=0$. Primes denote differentiation in $r$, and $b_\alpha =k(1-\alpha )>0$ throughout this section.

Lemma 15.14.1. The canonical $u_\alpha $ is $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic and smooth off $o$. Its non-pluripolar measure has a positive density $q_\alpha $ of mass one, in $L^1$ and in no $L^ p$, $p>1$. Distinct $\alpha $ give distinct ordinary types, although all their multiplier ideals are trivial.

Proof. Plurisubharmonicity, smoothness off the pole, unit mass and the $L^ p$ assertions are those of lemma 14.29.1. To fix the profile used in the inverse below, put

15.80
\begin{equation} \Phi _\alpha =\log (1+r)+u_\alpha ,\qquad Q_\alpha =r\Phi _\alpha '=P+\alpha (1-P)T^{\alpha -1}. \end{equation}

The tangential and radial eigenvalues are $Q_\alpha /r$ and $Q_\alpha '$, both positive. Radial determinant integration, with the constant fixed by $\Phi =\log (1+r)$, gives

15.81
\begin{equation} \int _{a<|z|^2<b}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi )^ k=Q(b)^ k-Q(a)^ k, \qquad Q=r\Phi '. \end{equation}

Since $Q_\alpha (0)=0$ and $Q_\alpha (\infty )=1$, the density is

15.82
\begin{equation} q_\alpha =\frac{(Q_\alpha ^ k)'}{(P^ k)'}>0. \end{equation}

Smoothness on the hyperplane and the exclusion of the canonical pole in the product leave no further measure. Near $r=0$, with $L=1-\log r$, direct differentiation gives

15.83
\begin{align} Q_\alpha & \sim \alpha L^{\alpha -1},& Q_\alpha '& \sim \frac{\alpha (1-\alpha )}rL^{\alpha -2},\\ (Q_\alpha ^ k)'& \sim \frac{k\alpha ^ k(1-\alpha )}rL^{-1-k(1-\alpha )}.\nonumber \end{align}

In particular $q_\alpha \sim \alpha ^ k(1-\alpha )r^{-k}L^{-1-b_\alpha }$; the radial volume has order $r^{k-1}\, \mathrm{d}r$, recovering these integrability assertions.

The types for different $\alpha $ are ordinarily distinct, since their difference is unbounded at $o$. Yet every $\mathcal I(tu_\alpha )$ is trivial for finite $t>0$: integrability of the constant function reduces near $o$ to $\int ^\infty \mathrm{e}^{-kL+tL^\alpha }\, \mathrm{d}L<\infty $. Ordinary type information here is strictly finer than this ideal information. $\square$

15.14.2 Density criterion and exact inverse

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Here “radial” means depending only on $r$ in the displayed chart. The smoothness assumption along the hyperplane includes a smooth profile in $t=1/r$ near $t=0$.

Theorem 15.14.2. Let $q\ge 0$ be radial, smooth on $\mathbb P^ k\setminus \{ o\} $, including the hyperplane at infinity, and satisfy $\int q\kappa ^ k=1$. Suppose that for some $\delta ,A,r_0>0$,

15.84
\begin{equation} \left|\frac{q(r)}{q_\alpha (r)}-1\right|\le Ar^\delta \quad (0<r<r_0). \end{equation}

Then there is a genuine radial $\kappa $-psh potential $U$, normalized to have value zero on the hyperplane, with

\[ \mathrm{MA}_\kappa (U)=q\kappa ^ k, \qquad U\sim u_\alpha . \]

Its residual $R=U-u_\alpha $ off $o$ extends continuously across $o$, with value $R_0$, and for small $r$ satisfies

15.85
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} |R-R_0|& \le Cr^\delta L^{\alpha -2},\\ |R'|& \le Cr^{\delta -1}L^{\alpha -2},\qquad |R''|\le Cr^{\delta -2}L^{\alpha -2}. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

It is locally $C^{0,\min \{ 1,2\delta \} }$. For $\delta \ge \frac12$ it extends $C^1$ with zero gradient at $o$; for $\delta \ge 1$ it extends $C^2$ with zero Hessian at $o$. No derivative hypothesis on the error in (15.84) is needed for these conclusions. The potential and its residual are smooth on $\mathbb P^ k\setminus \{ o\} $, including every density zero there. The tail condition ensures positive cumulative central mass; local density smoothness alone is not the criterion.

Proof. Let

15.88
\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} M(r)& =\int _{|z|^2<r}q\kappa ^ k,& Q(r)& =M(r)^{1/k},\\ U'(r)& =\frac{Q(r)-P(r)}r,& U(\infty )& =0. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

The tail condition makes $q$ positive near $o$, so $M(r)>0$ for every $r>0$. One has $M'=q(P^ k)'\ge 0$, $Q'\ge 0$ and $M(\infty )=1$. Thus the positive root defining $Q$ is smooth for every finite $r>0$, even where $q$ vanishes. This proves smoothness across all finite density zeros without a vanishing-order assumption. In $t=1/r$, the tail $1-M$ is the integral from $0$ to $t$ of the smooth density profile times $-\mathrm{d}(1+t)^{-k}/\mathrm{d}t$. Thus $M$ and $Q$ are smooth there, with $Q(0)=1$ in that coordinate. The equation $\mathrm{d}U/\mathrm{d}t=-(Q-P)/t$ is smooth there, since $Q-P$ vanishes at $t=0$. This both defines the normalization and proves smoothness along the full hyperplane.

From (15.84) and (15.83),

15.91
\begin{equation} |M-Q_\alpha ^ k| \le C\int _0^ r s^{\delta -1}(1-\log s)^{-1-b_\alpha }\, \mathrm{d}s \le Cr^\delta L^{-1-b_\alpha }. \end{equation}

The last inequality follows by writing $s=r\mathrm{e}^{-y}$ and bounding $\int _0^\infty \mathrm{e}^{-\delta y}(L+y)^{-1-b_\alpha }\, \mathrm{d}y$. Dividing by $Q_\alpha ^ k\asymp L^{-b_\alpha }$ shows that $Q/Q_\alpha $ tends to one; the mean value theorem for the positive $k$th root then gives

15.92
\begin{equation} |Q-Q_\alpha |\le Cr^\delta L^{\alpha -2}. \end{equation}

Moreover the exact identity

\[ \frac{Q'}{Q_\alpha '}= \frac q{q_\alpha }\left(\frac{Q_\alpha }{Q}\right)^{k-1} \]

gives $|Q'-Q_\alpha '|\le Cr^{\delta -1}L^{\alpha -2}$. Since $R'=(Q-Q_\alpha )/r$, integration to the origin gives a finite $R_0$ and the first two inequalities in (15.85). Differentiating this formula gives the last inequality.

The local potential $\Phi =\log (1+r)+U$ has semipositive Hessian away from $o$ because $Q/r>0$ and $Q'\ge 0$. Also $U=u_\alpha +R_0+o(1)$ near $o$, so $\Phi $ tends to $-\infty $. The local profile has nonnegative increasing slope in $\log r$, so its maxima with its value at $r=\varepsilon $ are plurisubharmonic. They decrease to the locally integrable profile; the local decreasing-limit property gives its genuine plurisubharmonic extension with the canonical pole retained. Thus $U$ is globally $\kappa $-psh. Formula (15.81) or the local determinant ratio gives its measure $q\kappa ^ k$ away from $o$; the defining non-pluripolar truncations exclude $o$ and are locally identical to this smooth product elsewhere. The measure has exactly total mass one. The bounded global residual proves the ordinary type.

In real coordinates, with $\rho =|z|=\sqrt r$,

\[ |\nabla R|\le C\rho ^{2\delta -1}L^{\alpha -2}, \qquad |\nabla ^2R|\le C\rho ^{2\delta -2}L^{\alpha -2}. \]

For $2\delta <1$, integration along radial radii bounds differences by $C|\rho _1-\rho _2|^{2\delta }$ and hence by $C|z_1-z_2|^{2\delta }$. For $2\delta \ge 1$ the gradient is bounded, giving local Lipschitz regularity. At $\delta =\frac12$ its bound tends to zero and $R-R_0=o(\rho )$, which give the claimed $C^1$ extension. At $\delta =1$, the Hessian bound tends to zero and $\nabla R=o(\rho )$, giving the $C^2$ extension. Larger $\delta $ only improves these bounds. These arguments justify crossing the pole for $R$; they do not assign a finite value to $U(o)$. $\square$

Among radial genuine solutions smooth off $o$ with this density, no central flux and no hyperplane singularity, the potential is unique up to constants: its flux satisfies $Q^ k=M$, and (15.88) then fixes its derivative. No nonsymmetric uniqueness is asserted.

Relative envelope-class membership follows from an explicit stretching family. If $a_* =\inf R$, the genuine $\kappa $-psh functions $v_ t=u_\alpha +t a_*$ satisfy, for every $t\ge 1$,

\[ t^{-1}v_ t+(1-t^{-1})u_\alpha =u_\alpha +a_*\le U. \]

At $t=1$ the term with zero coefficient is omitted, as in definition 13.4.1. Hence $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(\mathbb P^ k,\kappa ,u_\alpha )$ in the stretching definition, canonically at the common pole.

15.14.3 Smooth residuals from flat errors

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Proposition 15.14.3. Under the hypotheses of Theorem 15.14.2, suppose $g=q/q_\alpha -1$ satisfies

\[ (r\partial _ r)^ jg=O(r^ N) \quad \text{for every }N,j\ge 0. \]

Then $R-R_0$ extends smoothly across $o$, with every derivative zero there.

Proof. Put $x=-\log r$, $a(x)=Q_\alpha (\mathrm{e}^{-x})^ k$ and $E(x)=M(\mathrm{e}^{-x})-a(x)$. The exact central integral is

\[ E(x)=\int _ x^\infty g(\mathrm{e}^{-y})[-a'(y)]\, \mathrm{d}y, \qquad E'(x)=g(\mathrm{e}^{-x})a'(x). \]

Every derivative of $a$ has at most polynomial growth in $1+x$, and $a\asymp (1+x)^{-b_\alpha }$. The integral bounds $E$ by an exponential of arbitrarily large rate. Repeated differentiation of $E'$ expresses each derivative as a finite Leibniz sum of flat derivatives of $g$ and polynomially bounded derivatives of $a$; all derivatives of $E$ are therefore exponentially flat. Every derivative of $1/a$ has at most polynomial growth, by (15.80). Thus $E/a$ has the same flatness. Applying smooth composition near one to

\[ Q-Q_\alpha =Q_\alpha \bigl [(1+E/a)^{1/k}-1\bigr ] \]

proves flatness of every $x$ derivative of $Q-Q_\alpha $. Since $\mathrm{d}R/\mathrm{d}x=-(Q-Q_\alpha )$, integration from $x$ to infinity proves the same for $R-R_0$, including its zeroth derivative. An ordinary $j$th derivative in $r$ is $r^{-j}$ times a polynomial in $\partial _ x$; arbitrarily strong exponential decay absorbs that factor. All ordinary derivatives extend continuously as zero at $r=0$, and repeated one-dimensional integration verifies smoothness there. Composition with $r=|z|^2$ gives the conclusion. $\square$

15.14.4 Compact examples and sharp derivative thresholds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Proposition 15.14.4. The $C^1$ threshold $\delta =1/2$ and $C^2$ threshold $\delta =1$ are sharp. When $2\delta <1$, the stated Hölder exponent is also sharp. Nontrivial probability-density perturbations with smoothly flat residuals exist.

Proof. Choose smooth nonnegative radial cutoffs $\chi _0,\chi _1$ with disjoint supports: $\chi _0=1$ near $r=0$ and vanishes outside a finite small ball; $\chi _1$ is nonzero in a finite annulus. Put

15.93
\begin{equation} B=\frac{\int r^\delta \chi _0q_\alpha \kappa ^ k}{\int \chi _1q_\alpha \kappa ^ k},\qquad q_ c=q_\alpha \bigl [1+c(r^\delta \chi _0-B\chi _1)\bigr ]. \end{equation}

For sufficiently small $c>0$, $q_ c$ is positive, smooth off $o$ and along the hyperplane, and has total mass exactly one. It is comparable to $q_\alpha $ near $o$, hence is in no $L^ p$, $p>1$. Theorem 15.14.2 gives a compact solution in the prescribed ordinary type. Near $o$ its central flux difference is exactly $c\int _0^ r s^\delta (Q_\alpha ^ k)'\, \mathrm{d}s$. The substitution $s=r\mathrm{e}^{-y}$ and dominated convergence give

\[ \int _0^ r s^{\delta -1}(1-\log s)^{-1-b_\alpha }\, \mathrm{d}s \sim \delta ^{-1}r^\delta L^{-1-b_\alpha }. \]

Using (15.83) and the $k$th-root expansion yields

\begin{align*} R_ c'(r)& \sim \frac{c\alpha (1-\alpha )}\delta r^{\delta -1}L^{\alpha -2},\\ R_ c(r)-R_ c(0)& \sim \frac{c\alpha (1-\alpha )}{\delta ^2} r^\delta L^{\alpha -2}. \end{align*}

For $\delta <\frac12$, the radial gradient $2\sqrt r\, R_ c'$ diverges, ruling out $C^1$ extension. For $\delta <1$, a tangential real Hessian eigenvalue $2R_ c'$ diverges, ruling out $C^2$ extension. The annular compensation changes no central flux. For $2\delta <1$ the displayed asymptotic also rules out any larger Hölder exponent. The critical derivative thresholds in Theorem 15.14.2 are therefore sharp for this family.

Replacing $r^\delta \chi _0$ in (15.93) by $\mathrm{e}^{-1/r}\chi _0$ and adjusting $B$ by the same integral formula gives a nonzero mass-preserving perturbation satisfying every flat-error hypothesis. Proposition 15.14.3 then supplies a smoothly flat residual across the pole. $\square$

15.14.5 Smooth residuals with an open density-zero annulus

15.14.6 Products with a zero-lower-mass factor

15.15 Envelopes for singular radial obstacles

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

The radial inverse of section 15.14 starts with a density. One can instead perturb the singular reference as an obstacle and let the envelope select its measure. The resulting total profile is a convex minorant. Its affine intervals give open regions of zero density, while its contact with the reference at both ends controls the residual across the pole and the hyperplane at infinity.

Keep $k\geq 2$, $0<\alpha <1$, the normalized Fubini–Study form $\kappa $ on $\mathbb P^ k$ and the potential $u_\alpha $ of lemma 15.14.1. Write $r=|z|^2$ on the chart $Z_0\ne 0$, put $x=\log r$, and define

\[ A(x)=\log (1+\mathrm e^ x)+u_\alpha (x). \]

Here $u_\alpha (x)$ denotes its radial profile. Throughout this section primes denote derivatives in $x$. For $F\in C_ c^2(\mathbb R;\mathbb R)$, extend $F(\log r)$ by zero near the pole $o$ and the hyperplane at infinity, and set

15.94
\begin{equation} E_ F=\left(\sup \{ v\in \mathrm{PSH}(\mathbb P^ k,\kappa ): v\leq u_\alpha +F\ \text{everywhere},\ v\preceq u_\alpha \} \right)^*. \end{equation}

The obstacle takes the value $-\infty $ at $o$. Since $F$ is bounded, its pointwise constraint already implies ordinary domination for each competitor.

Lemma 15.15.1. The profile $A$ is smooth and strictly convex, with $0<A'<1$, bounded $A''$ and asymptotic slopes $0,1$. Its tangents tend uniformly to $-\infty $ on each compact interval as their point of tangency tends to $-\infty $, and uniformly to the line $y\mapsto y$ as that point tends to $+\infty $. Moreover, $A(x)-x\searrow 0$ at the right end.

Proof. Put $p=\mathrm e^ x/(1+\mathrm e^ x)$, $T=1-\log p$ and $a=\alpha T^{\alpha -1}$. Differentiating (15.80) gives

\[ A'=p+(1-p)a,\qquad A''=p(1-p)(1-a)+\alpha (1-\alpha )(1-p)^2T^{\alpha -2}. \]

Since $T\geq 1$ and $0<a\leq \alpha <1$, these formulas give the asserted positivity and bounds. On the left, $A(x)=1-(1-x)^\alpha +o(1)$ and $A-xA'$ has leading term $-(1-\alpha )(-x)^\alpha $. On the right, $A=x+(1-\alpha )\mathrm e^{-x}+O(\mathrm e^{-2x})$ and $A'=1-(1-\alpha )\mathrm e^{-x}+O(\mathrm e^{-2x})$. The tangent at $x$ is $y\mapsto A'(x)y+A(x)-xA'(x)$, so both uniform limits follow. Finally $A'-1<0$ gives the decreasing right-end limit. $\square$

15.15.1 The convex minorant and its residual

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Put $g=A+F$, $K_ F=\| F\| _\infty $, and let $C_ F$ be the supremum of all affine functions below $g$ on $\mathbb R$. Since $A-K_ F$ is convex and is the supremum of its supporting lines,

15.95
\begin{equation} A-K_ F\leq C_ F\leq A+F\leq A+K_ F. \end{equation}

In particular $C_ F$ is a finite convex function.

Theorem 15.15.2. The envelope (15.94) is

\[ E_ F=C_ F(\log r)-\log (1+r) \]

on the punctured affine chart, with its canonical extensions at both ends. It has ordinary type $u_\alpha $. The residual $R_ F=E_ F-u_\alpha $ off $o$ extends to $C^{1,1}(\mathbb P^ k)$, is zero near $o$ and is smooth near infinity. The function $C_ F$ is affine on each component of $\{ C_ F<g\} $, and, with $L_2=\sup _{\mathbb R}\max (g'',0)$,

15.96
\begin{equation} 0\leq C_ F'(b)-C_ F'(a)\leq L_2(b-a)\qquad (a<b). \end{equation}

Proof. We first identify the full envelope, then examine contact and the two ends. Every supporting slope of $C_ F$ lies in $[0,1]$, by the left growth $g\to -\infty $ and the right growth $g=x+o(1)$. The sandwich forces its asymptotic slopes to be $0,1$.

The competitor family is invariant under the unitary group $U(k)$ acting on the affine coordinates. Its regularized supremum is therefore radial. Adding smooth local potentials of $\kappa $ gives a locally upper-bounded family of plurisubharmonic functions, so the envelope is $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic. The competitor $u_\alpha -K_ F$ prevents collapse, and upper semicontinuity of the obstacle preserves the everywhere bound after regularization, including at $o$. A radial plurisubharmonic total potential $B(\log r)$ has convex profile $B$: restriction to a complex line gives this from radial subharmonicity. Positivity in a direction orthogonal to $z$ gives $B'\geq 0$. Conversely a convex nondecreasing profile has nonnegative tangential and radial Hessian eigenvalues $B'/r$ and $B''/r$, first for smooth profiles and then by convex smoothing on annuli. The envelope's total profile is consequently a convex minorant of $g$, and is at most $C_ F$.

The same criterion makes $C_ F(\log r)$ plurisubharmonic on the punctured chart. Its upper bound near $o$ and (15.95) give a removable extension with value $-\infty $. Since $C_ F'\leq 1$, the function $C_ F-x$ is nonincreasing and, by the sandwich, has a finite right-end limit. The proposed $E_ F$ is thus locally bounded above near infinity. Add a local smooth potential of $\kappa $ and apply lemma 1.3.7 across the hyperplane. This gives a global competitor, hence the formula. The sandwich proves ordinary equivalence without subtracting the infinite values at $o$.

On a compact interval in the free set the gap $g-C_ F$ is positive. Replacing $C_ F$ on a sufficiently short subinterval by its chord preserves convexity at both endpoints and stays below $g$. Unless $C_ F$ is locally affine this would give a larger minorant. It follows that it is affine on each free component. At a contact point $y$, domination by $g$ gives $C'_{F,-}(y)\geq g'(y)\geq C'_{F,+}(y)$, and convexity reverses the outer inequality. Thus $C_ F$ is differentiable at contacts, with derivative $g'$, and it is differentiable on free intervals as well. Its derivative is continuous. For $a<b$ outside one common free interval, move $a$ to its right contact endpoint and $b$ to its left contact endpoint whenever necessary. These points $a',b'\in [a,b]$ have the same respective derivatives. An unbounded free interval lacking the needed endpoint contains both original points, in which case the difference is zero. Otherwise

\[ 0\leq C_ F'(b)-C_ F'(a)=g'(b')-g'(a') \leq L_2(b'-a')\leq L_2(b-a), \]

which proves (15.96).

For all sufficiently negative $x$, the tangent to $A$ at $x$ lies below $g$ on $\operatorname {supp}F$, by lemma 15.15.1, and below $A=g$ elsewhere. Hence $C_ F=A$ on a left tail. The residual is zero near $o$.

For the other end, put $L_ F=\inf _{\mathbb R}(g(x)-x)\leq 0$. If this infimum is attained at $y$, the affine minorant $x+L_ F$ touches $g$ there. The bound $C_ F'\leq 1$ forces $C_ F=x+L_ F$ for $x\geq y$. A negative infimum is always attained, since $g-x$ tends to $+\infty $ on the left and to zero on the right. With $R=1/r$, the envelope on this tail is

15.97
\begin{equation} E_ F=L_ F-\log (1+R). \end{equation}

If $L_ F=0$ is not attained and $F\ne 0$, the continuous function $g(y)-y$ has a positive minimum on $\operatorname {supp}F$. The right tangents of $A$ approach the line $y$ uniformly there, so every sufficiently late tangent lies below $g$ and gives $C_ F=A$ at its contact point. Thus $C_ F=A$ on a right tail; for $F=0$ this holds everywhere. Both alternatives give a smooth residual near infinity, because $R$ and $u_\alpha $ are smooth there.

On the intervening finite annulus,

\[ |C_ F-A|\leq K_ F,\qquad |C_ F'-A'|\leq 1, \qquad |(C_ F-A)''|\leq L_2+\| A''\| _\infty \quad \text{a.e.} \]

Composing with the smooth coordinate function $x=\log r$ gives $C^{1,1}$ regularity. The exact end formulas extend it over the whole projective space. $\square$

Corollary 15.15.3. The non-pluripolar measure of $E_ F$ has mass one and satisfies

\[ \operatorname{MA}_\kappa (E_ F)(\{ a<\log r<b\} )=C_ F'(b)^ k-C_ F'(a)^ k \qquad (-\infty <a<b<\infty ). \]

It vanishes on every free interval. For any $F,G\in C_ c^2(\mathbb R;\mathbb R)$,

15.98
\begin{equation} \| R_ F-R_ G\| _{C^0(\mathbb P^ k)}\leq \| F-G\| _\infty . \end{equation}

Proof. On an annulus, the radial flux formula (15.81), followed by convex smoothing and bounded Bedford–Taylor continuity, gives the displayed measure. Continuity of $C_ F'$ excludes boundary atoms. The non-pluripolar truncations discard $o$, and smoothness at infinity excludes hyperplane mass. The asymptotic slopes $0,1$ therefore give mass one. An affine profile has zero radial eigenvalue and hence zero top-degree measure. Finally, if $\delta =\| F-G\| _\infty $, subtracting $\delta $ from a competitor for $F$ gives a competitor for $G$, and conversely. The resulting value bound for the envelopes gives (15.98) off $o$, and continuity of the residuals extends it across both ends. $\square$

For example, take a nonnegative smooth compactly supported bump $\chi $ with $\chi (x_0)=1$ and $F=-M\chi $, where $M>A(x_0)-x_0$. Then $L_ F<0$, and (15.97) gives an open free region of zero density. The envelope still has its sublogarithmic pole, while its residual is globally $C^{1,1}$.

15.15.2 Uniform bounds through moving contact

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

For a fixed interval $[a,b]$ and $M>0$, let $\mathcal F_{a,b,M}$ consist of real $C^2$ functions supported in $[a,b]$ with $\| F\| _\infty +\| F''\| _\infty \leq M$. Take all norms in one fixed finite smooth projective atlas.

Theorem 15.15.4. There is $K=K(k,\alpha ,a,b,M)$ such that

\[ \| R_ F\| _{C^{1,1}}\leq K\qquad (F\in \mathcal F_{a,b,M}). \]

For every $0<\gamma <1$ and $F,G\in \mathcal F_{a,b,M}$ with $\delta =\| F-G\| _\infty \leq 1$,

\[ \| R_ F-R_ G\| _{C^{1,\gamma }} \leq K_\gamma \delta ^{(1-\gamma )/2}, \]

where $K_\gamma $ has the same fixed-parameter dependence. These bounds hold across changes of the infinity alternative in theorem 15.15.2.

Proof. The left tangents of $A$ lie below $A-M$ on $[a,b]$ once their contact point is less than a common $a_0<a$. They are minorants of every $A+F$, so all residuals vanish on the same neighborhood of $o$. The sandwich and slope bound give uniform real Hessian bounds on any fixed finite annulus, since $L_2\leq \| A''\| _\infty +M$. It remains to control infinity, where the right contact point need not stay bounded.

Put $B=\max (b,0)+1$. For $x>B$, the obstacle is $A$. At contact $C_ F'=A'$. On a free interval with finite right contact endpoint $d$, its constant slope is $A'(d)\geq A'(x)$; an unbounded right free interval has slope one. Thus

\[ 0\leq d_ F(x):=1-C_ F'(x)\leq 1-A'(x)\leq \mathrm e^{-x}. \]

The locally Lipschitz functions $C_ F',A'$ agree on the contact set, so their derivatives agree almost everywhere there. Indeed, a differentiable Lipschitz function has derivative zero at almost every density point of its zero set. On the free set $C_ F''=0$. The reference formula consequently gives

15.99
\begin{equation} 0\leq C_ F''(x)\leq A''(x) \leq (1+\alpha (1-\alpha ))\mathrm e^{-x} \quad \text{a.e. on }x>B. \end{equation}

In a chart $Z_ j\ne 0$, $j\geq 1$, use $\zeta =Z_0/Z_ j$ and $y=(Z_ i/Z_ j)_{i\geq 1,i\ne j}$. Then

\[ R=\frac{|\zeta |^2}{1+\sum |y_ i|^2},\qquad x=-\log R. \]

On fixed relatively compact chart pieces, $|\nabla R|\leq c\sqrt R$ and $|\nabla ^2R|\leq c$. Write the envelope as $\mathcal E_ F(R)=C_ F(-\log R)+\log R-\log (1+R)$ for $R>0$. Its derivatives are

\[ (\mathcal E_ F)_ R=\frac{d_ F}{R}-\frac1{1+R},\qquad (\mathcal E_ F)_{RR}=\frac{C_ F''-d_ F}{R^2}+\frac1{(1+R)^2} \quad \text{a.e.} \]

The tail bounds give $|(\mathcal E_ F)_ R|\leq 2$ and $|(\mathcal E_ F)_{RR}|\leq c_\alpha /R+1$. The real chain rule therefore bounds its Hessian uniformly, and its gradient tends to zero as $\zeta \to 0$. Also $L_ F=\lim (C_ F-x)\in [-M,0]$, and integrating $d_ F\leq \mathrm e^{-x}$ gives $|C_ F(x)-x-L_ F|\leq \mathrm e^{-x}$. Hence $\mathcal E_ F-L_ F=O(R)$, so the extended envelope is differentiable on the hyperplane with zero gradient. The Hessian bound makes the gradient Lipschitz off the hyperplane. Apply it along straight coordinate segments and use the continuous zero gradient at a crossing; on segments contained in the hyperplane the gradient is zero. This proves a uniform $C^{1,1}$ extension. Subtract the fixed smooth $u_\alpha $ near infinity and join this estimate to the common pole neighborhood and a fixed annulus.

For the stability estimate put $Z=R_ F-R_ G$. By (15.98), $\| Z\| _\infty \leq \delta $, and its gradient has a uniform Lipschitz bound. On a smaller fixed chart, a real directional increment of length $h$ gives $|\partial _ e Z|\leq 2\delta /h+Kh$. Taking $h$ of order $\sqrt\delta $, or enlarging the constant when limited by the chart radius, yields $\| \nabla Z\| _\infty \leq c\sqrt\delta $. For points a distance $t$ apart,

\[ |\nabla Z(x)-\nabla Z(y)|\leq \min (c\sqrt\delta ,2Kt). \]

Dividing by $t^\gamma $ and splitting at $t=\sqrt\delta $ gives the estimate in a finite atlas. When $\delta =0$, the value bound gives equality. This argument does not assert convergence in $C^{1,1}$. $\square$

15.15.3 The full envelope on a Hermitian product

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory

Let $(Y,h_0)$ be a connected compact Hermitian manifold of dimension $m\geq 1$, and on $X=\mathbb P^ k\times Y$ put $H=\operatorname {pr}_1^*\kappa +\operatorname {pr}_2^*h_0$, $N=k+m$ and $V_0=\int _ Yh_0^ m$. Pull $u_\alpha $ and $F$ back to $X$, and define $E_ F^ H$ by (15.94) using the full family $\mathrm{PSH}(X,H)$.

Proposition 15.15.5. One has $E_ F^ H=\operatorname {pr}_1^*E_ F$ and

\[ \operatorname{MA}_ H(E_ F^ H)=\binom Nk\, \operatorname{MA}_\kappa (E_ F)\otimes h_0^ m, \qquad \operatorname{MA}_ H(E_ F^ H)(X)=\binom Nk V_0. \]

The pulled-back residual is globally $C^{1,1}$, and $E_ F^ H$ belongs to $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,H,u_\alpha )$. On each fixed product the uniform bounds and derivative stability of theorem 15.15.4 hold for these residuals.

Proof. Every competitor restricts to a projective slice as a $\kappa $-plurisubharmonic function or as $-\infty $ identically. To check the exact lower bound when $H$ is nonclosed, choose a smooth quadratic correction $q$ with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq H$ on a coordinate polydisk and let $v_\epsilon =(v+q)*\eta _\epsilon -q$ on a smaller polydisk, where $\eta _\epsilon $ is the rescaling of a fixed smooth nonnegative radial probability kernel. Radial plurisubharmonic convolutions decrease to the canonical $v+q$, and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\epsilon \geq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q-H)*\eta _\epsilon -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q \geq -H-o(1)\omega _{\mathrm{Eucl}}. \]

Restrict this smooth inequality to a fixed slice. The corrected restrictions decrease either to $-\infty $ or to a plurisubharmonic function in local $L^1$; in the latter case the background error tends to zero distributionally. Subtracting $q$ gives the exact $\kappa $ bound, and these canonical restrictions patch on the connected slice. The everywhere obstacle constraint now gives $v\leq \operatorname {pr}_1^*E_ F$ on every slice. Upper regularization preserves this bound. Conversely every base competitor lifts, since $h_0>0$, proving equality of the full envelopes.

The bounded truncations $\max (E_ F^ H,-j)$ are pullbacks. By proposition 1.7.1, lemma 1.7.3, dimensional multilinearity leaves only the term with $k$ projective factors and $m$ factor entries. Restrict to strict superlevel sets and let $j\to \infty $ to obtain the stated non-pluripolar measure and its finite mass. The residual regularity and uniform estimates pull back in a fixed smooth product atlas. Finally $E_ F^ H\geq u_\alpha -K_ F$. At $t=1$ take the stretching witness $v_1=E_ F^ H$; for $t>1$ take $v_ t=u_\alpha -tK_ F$, so that

\[ t^{-1}v_ t+(1-t^{-1})u_\alpha =u_\alpha -K_ F\leq E_ F^ H. \]

Together with ordinary domination this is definition 13.4.1, including the canonical values at the common pole. $\square$

Example 15.15.6. There is a nonclosed positive background on a non-Kähler fourfold for which the preceding full envelope formula and uniform residual estimates hold.

Proof. Take $Y=(\mathbb C^2\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle \zeta \mapsto \mathrm e\zeta \rangle $. With $s=\log |\zeta |^2$, the form $h_0=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s+\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s$ is invariant under the quotient. Its two summands are positive on the tangential and radial complex directions, respectively, so it is Hermitian. Moreover $\mathrm dh_0=-\mathrm ds\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s\ne 0$. The Hopf surface is diffeomorphic to $S^3\times S^1$ and has odd first Betti number. Thus $X=\mathbb P^2\times Y$ is a non-Kähler fourfold with $\mathrm dH\ne 0$, and proposition 15.15.5 applies. $\square$
