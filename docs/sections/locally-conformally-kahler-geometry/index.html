23.2 Locally conformally Kähler geometry9B4394B392ED

9B4394B392ED

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$. A locally conformally Kähler (LCK) metric is a Hermitian metric that becomes Kähler after local conformal changes, and its Lee form measures the obstruction to making this change globally. On a covering on which the Lee form is exact, the Kähler metric and its admissible potentials change by the same character under deck transformations. Descending this potential calculus to $X$ gives a twisted complex Hessian with a zeroth-order term, so that even adding a constant to a potential changes the metric. The standard Vaisman metric on the Hopf manifold illustrates the difference between the two potential theories: although it is locally conformally Kähler, its ordinary potentials have unbounded Monge–Ampère masses.

Prescribing the volume while keeping the Lee class and the Gauduchon gauge couples the determinant equation with an equation for the conformal factor. For a volume invariant under the Lee and anti-Lee flows of a Vaisman metric, uniqueness for the ordinary smooth equation gives a potential that is constant along these flows. Stokes' theorem in the transverse directions fixes the normalizing constant, and changing the Lee form reconstructs the required Vaisman metric. On scalar Hopf manifolds, radial symmetry reduces the coupled equations to a periodic ordinary differential equation. Its positive solutions are ordered by their Lee periods, so the metric is determined by its Lee period and its volume; for a nonconstant radial volume, this metric is not Vaisman. A different compatibility condition gives rigidity: when an LCK metric is both Gauduchon and Chern–Ricci flat, the Bianchi and Bochner identities force its harmonic Lee form to be parallel.

The Lee form also produces a closed curvature class that can be nonzero in Bott–Chern cohomology while vanishing in de Rham cohomology. Pairing these classes with all Gauduchon metrics defines the Lee–Gauduchon cone, which detects their pseudoeffectivity and is invariant under bimeromorphic maps. Strongly Gauduchon metrics exclude all nonzero positive de Rham-exact currents and bound the linear mass of closed positive currents by their de Rham classes. The Calabi–Eckmann example separates this condition from the presence of zero in the Lee–Gauduchon cone, and a balanced elliptic fibration shows that it still allows zero lower Monge–Ampère mass.

23.2.1 The Lee form and the Kähler coveringE0F12265EE84

A Hermitian form is locally conformally Kähler when its exterior derivative is controlled by a closed one-form.

Definition 23.2.2.48F6C713AFE3 A Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$ is locally conformally Kähler, or LCK, if there is a smooth real one-form $\lambda $ on $X$ such that

\[ \mathrm{d}\omega =\lambda \wedge \omega , \qquad \mathrm{d}\lambda =0. \]

The form $\lambda $ is called its Lee form. The metric is strictly LCK if $\lambda $ is not exact. A strictly LCK metric is Vaisman if its Lee form is parallel for the Levi–Civita connection of the associated Riemannian metric.

Fix an LCK metric $\omega $ with Lee form $\lambda $. On a compact manifold a nonzero parallel one-form cannot be exact: the differential of a function vanishes at a maximum, whereas a parallel form has constant length. Thus a nonzero parallel Lee form is automatically nowhere zero and not exact.

The Lee form is determined by $\omega $, because the map $a\mapsto a\wedge \omega $ is injective on one-forms: after wedging with $\omega ^{n-2}$, this follows from the bijectivity of the Lefschetz map $a\mapsto a\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$ on one-forms.

Locally write $\lambda =\mathrm{d}f$. Then

\[ \Omega =\mathrm{e}^{-f}\omega >0,\qquad \mathrm{d}\Omega =0, \]

so $\Omega $ is a Kähler form. Choose a connected regular covering $\pi :\widetilde X\to X$ on which $\pi ^*\lambda =\mathrm{d}F$, for example the universal covering. The quotient of $\widetilde X$ by its deck group is $X$. The same construction gives a Kähler form

2E90E918DF55 23.2.3
\begin{equation} \widetilde\omega =\mathrm{e}^{-F}\pi ^*\omega . \end{equation}

For a deck transformation $\sigma $, the difference $\sigma ^*F-F=c_\sigma $ is constant, and

\[ \sigma ^*\widetilde\omega =\chi (\sigma )\widetilde\omega , \qquad \chi (\sigma )=\mathrm{e}^{-c_\sigma }. \]

Thus $\chi $ is a homomorphism from the deck group to the multiplicative group of positive real numbers, which records how the Kähler metric is rescaled. Forms on $\widetilde X$ transforming by $\chi $ may equivalently be viewed as forms on $X$ with values in the flat real line bundle defined by $\chi $, the weight bundle. In this sense an LCK form is closed as a form with values in the weight bundle [Section 3.4, OV24LCK].

Let $g$ be the Riemannian metric of $\omega $, let $J$ be the complex structure, and let $\lambda ^\sharp $ be the vector field dual to $\lambda $ with respect to $g$. On a chart where $\lambda =\mathrm df$, the metric $\widetilde g=\mathrm{e}^{-f}g$ is Kähler. For real vector fields $U,V$, the Levi–Civita connections $\nabla $ of $g$ and $\widetilde\nabla $ of $\widetilde g$ are related by

\[ \widetilde\nabla _ UV=\nabla _ UV-\tfrac 12\bigl (\lambda (U)V+\lambda (V)U-g(U,V)\lambda ^\sharp \bigr ). \]

Substituting $JV$ for $V$ and using $\widetilde\nabla J=0$ gives

0F9BDB92C6B1 23.2.4
\begin{equation} (\nabla _ UJ)V=\tfrac 12\bigl (\lambda (JV)U-\lambda (V)JU+g(U,V)J\lambda ^\sharp +g(JU,V)\lambda ^\sharp \bigr ). \end{equation}

Both sides are global tensors, so this identity holds on all of $X$.

For $a\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)$, the conformal change $\omega '=\mathrm{e}^ a\omega $ replaces the Lee form by $\lambda +\mathrm{d}a$. In particular, the de Rham class $[\lambda ]$ depends only on the conformal class of $\omega $. If $\lambda $ is exact, the construction above gives a Kähler form on $X$ itself.

23.2.5 Gauduchon representatives and the Lee classA8F518936B0A

In the conformal class of an LCK metric, the Gauduchon representative is the one whose Lee form is harmonic. A closed $(1,1)$-form built from the Lee form pairs positively with this Gauduchon metric when the structure is strictly LCK, even though the form is de Rham exact. It therefore defines a nonzero Bott–Chern class, which obstructs Kähler modifications.

Set

\[ \lambda ^{\mathrm c} =\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi }(\lambda ^{0,1}-\lambda ^{1,0}), \]

so that $\lambda ^{\mathrm c}=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f$ wherever $\lambda =\mathrm{d}f$. The closed real $(1,1)$-form

29D45DC05342 23.2.6
\begin{equation} \kappa _\lambda :=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\lambda =-\mathrm{d}\lambda ^{\mathrm c} \end{equation}

connects the Lee class with Bott–Chern cohomology. It is de Rham exact, but it need not be $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-exact.

Proposition 23.2.7.11EBB7AD1165 An LCK metric is Gauduchon if and only if its Lee form is co-closed for that metric. Consequently, the Gauduchon representative in its conformal class has harmonic Lee form. Moreover, for every LCK metric,

35851308DD2B 23.2.8
\begin{equation} \int _ X\kappa _\lambda \wedge \omega ^{n-1} =(n-1)\int _ X\lambda \wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \omega ^{n-1}. \end{equation}

If $\lambda \not\equiv 0$, the right side is strictly positive.

Proof. On a chart where $\omega $ is conformally Kähler,

\[ \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(\omega ^{n-1}) =(n-1)\lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \omega ^{n-1}. \]

Let $*$ be the real Hodge star of the Riemannian metric associated with $\omega $, and let $\mathrm d^*$ be the formal adjoint of the exterior derivative. By the pointwise Hodge-star identities for Hermitian metrics, $\lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$ is a fixed nonzero constant multiple of $*\lambda $. Consequently, the Gauduchon condition $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-1})=0$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{d}*\lambda =0$, that is, to $\mathrm{d}^*\lambda =0$. Since $\mathrm{d}\lambda =0$, co-closedness is equivalent to harmonicity. The Gauduchon representative exists by theorem 11.4.2, and conformal changes preserve the LCK condition.

Stokes' theorem and $\mathrm{d}(\omega ^{n-1})=(n-1)\lambda \wedge \omega ^{n-1}$ give

\[ \begin{split} -\int _ X\mathrm{d}\lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \omega ^{n-1} & =-\int _ X\lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \mathrm{d}(\omega ^{n-1})\\ & =(n-1)\int _ X\lambda \wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \omega ^{n-1}. \end{split} \]

For a real one-form, $\lambda \wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c} =\mathrm i\lambda ^{1,0}\wedge \lambda ^{0,1}/(2\pi )$ is semipositive, and its trace with respect to $\omega $ is positive wherever $\lambda \ne 0$. This proves the last assertion. $\square$

The same construction applies to every closed real one-form and defines a linear map

624B1D55AC6E 23.2.9
\begin{equation} j_ X:H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)\longrightarrow H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R), \qquad [\lambda ]\longmapsto \{ \kappa _\lambda \} , \end{equation}

which we call the Lee map. It is well defined, since replacing $\lambda $ by $\lambda +\mathrm{d}a$ replaces $\kappa _\lambda $ by $\kappa _\lambda -\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$. Consequently, the pairing of $\{ \kappa _\lambda \} $ with any Gauduchon form $\gamma ^{n-1}$ is well defined. For a strictly LCK structure, the Gauduchon representative in its conformal class gives a strictly positive pairing by (23.2.8). In particular $\{ \kappa _\lambda \} \ne 0$, although its image in de Rham cohomology is zero.

Corollary 23.2.10.F6950D5704C9 If $X$ carries a strictly LCK metric, then no class in $H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb R)$ is big. In particular, $X$ does not belong to Fujiki's class $\mathcal C$.

Proof. If there were a big class, the modification criterion recalled in section 10.10 would give a modification $\pi :Y\to X$ with $Y$ compact Kähler. The form $\pi ^*\kappa _\lambda $ is closed and de Rham exact, so the $\partial \bar\partial $-lemma on $Y$ gives a smooth real function $h$ on $Y$ with $\pi ^*\kappa _\lambda =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}h$. Since $\pi $ has degree one, pushing forward gives the current identity $\kappa _\lambda =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\pi _*h)$. By Stokes' theorem, the pairing of this current with a Gauduchon form is zero, which contradicts (23.2.8) for the Gauduchon representative of the LCK structure. $\square$

For the Hopf manifold considered below, $\kappa _\lambda =\alpha $ is a smooth semipositive form, so its class is nef. Its non-nef locus is empty, whereas its non-Kähler locus is all of $X$, by the convention for classes that are not big in section 10.10. These statements concern the closed class $\{ \alpha \} $; the Monge–Ampère equations on the Hopf manifold use instead the strictly positive, nonclosed form $\omega $.

Proposition 23.2.11.26993B1C8C74 Suppose that $X$ carries a strictly LCK metric with Lee form $\lambda $ and that $\{ \kappa _\lambda \} $ is pseudoeffective. Then $X$ admits no balanced metric.

Proof. Let $T=\kappa _\lambda +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$ be a closed positive current representing the class. It is nonzero, since the class is nonzero, and it is de Rham exact. If a Hermitian metric $b$ were balanced, then $\mathrm{d}(b^{n-1})=0$, and Stokes' theorem for currents would give

\[ \int _ XT\wedge b^{n-1}=0. \]

This is impossible, since the mass of a nonzero positive $(1,1)$-current against a smooth strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form is strictly positive. $\square$

The balanced obstruction motivates the following conjecture.

Conjecture 23.2.12.4EA8A1D43A8C If $X$ carries a strictly LCK metric with Lee form $\lambda $, then the Bott–Chern class $\{ \kappa _\lambda \} $ is pseudoeffective.

The positivity in (23.2.8) tests the class only against the Gauduchon representative of the LCK metric. By theorem 11.6.1, pseudoeffectivity requires a nonnegative pairing with every Gauduchon metric.

23.2.13 The least eigenvalue of Lee curvature942B6FCFDC1A

Fix a strictly LCK metric $\omega $ with Lee form $\lambda $. The volume along $\omega +t\kappa _\lambda $ increases as long as this form is positive. If the least relative eigenvalue of $\kappa _\lambda $ is a negative constant, the first boundary form has a kernel everywhere and its total volume is zero. This contradiction gives a pointwise positivity criterion.

Set $Q=\lambda \wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c}\geq 0$. The form $Q$ is nonzero on an open set, since $\lambda $ is not exact and hence is not identically zero.

Proposition 23.2.14.E5F423FF7707 On the interval where $\eta _ t=\omega +t\kappa _\lambda >0$, the polynomial $V(t)=\int _ X\eta _ t^ n$ has degree at most $n-1$ and

\[ V'(t)=n(n-1)\int _ XQ\wedge \omega \wedge \eta _ t^{n-2}>0. \]

Proof. Since $\mathrm d\eta _ t=\lambda \wedge \omega $ and $\mathrm d\lambda ^{\mathrm c}=-\kappa _\lambda $, Stokes' theorem gives

\[ 0=\int _ X\mathrm d(\lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \eta _ t^{n-1}) =-\int _ X\kappa _\lambda \wedge \eta _ t^{n-1} +(n-1)\int _ XQ\wedge \omega \wedge \eta _ t^{n-2}. \]

Differentiating the wedge polynomial proves the formula. The integrand on the right is positive wherever $\lambda \ne 0$, including when $n=2$. Its integral is therefore strictly positive. Finally, $\kappa _\lambda $ is closed and de Rham exact, so

\[ \int _ X\kappa _\lambda ^ n =-\int _ X\mathrm d(\lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge \kappa _\lambda ^{n-1})=0. \]

This removes the coefficient of degree $n$. $\square$

Theorem 23.2.15.B8583A0E17A6 If the least eigenvalue of $\kappa _\lambda $ relative to $\omega $ is constant on $X$, then it is zero and $\kappa _\lambda $ is semipositive. Its ordinary Bott–Chern class is nonzero and pseudoeffective.

If all relative eigenvalues are constant, the rank is constant and lies between $1$ and $n-1$. In particular, the conclusion holds on a compact quotient $\Gamma \backslash G$ with left-invariant complex structure and strictly LCK tensors.

Proof. Let $r$ be the constant least relative eigenvalue. If $r<0$, put $t_0=-1/r$. Then $\eta _ t>0$ for $0\leq t<t_0$, whereas $\eta _{t_0}$ is semipositive with a kernel at every point. Thus $V(t_0)=0$. The preceding proposition and polynomial continuity instead give $V(t_0)\geq V(0)>0$. This proves $r\geq 0$ and semipositivity.

The nonnegative smooth form $\kappa _\lambda ^ n$ has integral zero by the same proposition, hence vanishes everywhere. Thus the least eigenvalue is zero at every point. Applying the Lee degree (23.2.8) to a conformal Gauduchon representative, as above, proves that the class is nonzero. The form $\kappa _\lambda $ itself represents it positively.

Constant spectrum gives constant rank. The positive pairing (23.2.8) excludes rank zero, and the vanishing top power excludes rank $n$. On the stated compact quotient, the relative matrix is constant in a left-invariant frame, which proves the last assertion. $\square$

The class is also nef: the same representative is bounded below by $-\epsilon h$ for every Hermitian form $h$ and every $\epsilon >0$. Thus proposition 23.2.11 excludes balanced metrics under the least-eigenvalue hypothesis.

The hypothesis concerns the chosen LCK metric. The other eigenvalues may vary, as the following examples show. They also explain why first degeneration of a volume ray need not force its total volume to vanish without the constant-minimum condition.

Example 23.2.16.BBCE95D98A62 On the scalar Hopf manifold $H_ n=(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto z/2\rangle $, for every $n\geq 2$, there is a strictly LCK metric with indefinite Lee curvature and a smooth semipositive representative of its ordinary Bott–Chern class. The first boundary reached for increasing $t>0$ in $\omega +t\kappa _\lambda $ has positive total volume.

Proof. Fix $0<\epsilon <1$ and put

\[ \rho =\sum _ j|z_ j|^2,\qquad \Phi =\rho +\epsilon \operatorname {Re}(z_1^2),\qquad \omega =\frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho }{\Phi },\qquad \lambda =-\mathrm d\log \Phi . \]

The bounds $(1-\epsilon )\rho \leq \Phi \leq (1+\epsilon )\rho $ give positivity. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Phi =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ and both $\Phi $ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ scale by $1/4$ under $z\mapsto z/2$, the metric and Lee form descend and satisfy the LCK equation. Polar coordinates identify the quotient with $S^{2n-1}\times (\mathbb R/(\log 2)\mathbb Z)$, so it is compact and connected. The Lee period from $z$ to $z/2$ is $2\log 2$, which proves strictness.

Here $\kappa _\lambda =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \Phi $. With $F_ i=\bar z_ i+\epsilon z_1\delta _{i1}$, its complex Hessian is

\[ (\log \Phi )_{i\bar j} =\frac{\delta _{ij}}{\Phi } -\frac{F_ i\overline{F_ j}}{\Phi ^2}. \]

Relative to $\omega $, the eigenvalues are $1$ with multiplicity $n-1$ and $h=1-|F|^2/\Phi $. Writing $z_1=x+\mathrm i y$ and $B=\sum _{j>1}|z_ j|^2$, one finds

\[ h=\frac{\epsilon ((1-\epsilon )y^2-(1+\epsilon )x^2)}{(1+\epsilon )x^2+(1-\epsilon )y^2+B}, \qquad -\epsilon \leq h\leq \epsilon . \]

At $(1,0,\ldots ,0)$ the spectrum is $-\epsilon ,1,\ldots ,1$, hence is indefinite. The bounded smooth function $u=\log \rho -\log \Phi $ is deck invariant, and

\[ \kappa _\lambda +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \rho \geq 0, \]

by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the Hessian of $\log \rho $. The correction also gives the standard Hopf conformal representative: $\mathrm e^{-u}\omega =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho /\rho $.

The first boundary occurs at $t_0=1/\epsilon $. At that value,

\[ 1+h/\epsilon =\frac{2(1-\epsilon )y^2+B}{\Phi }. \]

It vanishes only on the real $z_1$ rays with the remaining coordinates zero. The other relative eigenvalues of $\omega +t_0\kappa _\lambda $ equal $1+1/\epsilon >0$. The boundary form is therefore semipositive and its top power is positive on a nonempty open set, proving positive total volume. $\square$

Example 23.2.17.EB59EA55107F For every $n\geq 2$, the same $H_ n$ carries a family of strictly LCK metrics whose least Lee-curvature eigenvalue is identically zero and whose remaining relative spectrum is nonconstant. Thus constancy of the least eigenvalue is strictly weaker than constancy of the whole spectrum.

Proof. Put $q=|z_1|^2$ and, for $0<\epsilon <1$, define

\[ \Psi =\rho +\epsilon \frac{q^2}{\rho },\qquad \omega _\epsilon =\frac{\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi }{\rho },\qquad \lambda =-\mathrm d\log \rho . \]

For $a=q/\rho \in [0,1]$, direct differentiation gives

\[ \left(\frac{q^2}{\rho }\right)_{i\bar j} =\frac{2}{\rho }(q_ i-a\rho _ i)(q_{\bar j}-a\rho _{\bar j}) +2a\delta _{i1}\delta _{j1}-a^2\delta _{ij}. \]

The first two terms are positive semidefinite and $a^2\leq 1$, so $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Psi \geq (1-\epsilon )\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho >0$. The potential is smooth on the punctured space and scales by $1/4$ under the deck map. Hence $\omega _\epsilon $ descends, satisfies the LCK equation and has Lee period $2\log 2$.

Its Lee curvature is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log \rho $, which is semipositive of rank $n-1$. The least relative eigenvalue is consequently zero everywhere. At $(1,0,\ldots ,0)$, the Hessian of $q^2/\rho $ is $\operatorname {diag}(1,-1,\ldots ,-1)$, giving the spectrum

\[ 0,(1-\epsilon )^{-1},\ldots ,(1-\epsilon )^{-1}. \]

At $(0,1,0,\ldots ,0)$ the term $q^2/\rho $ vanishes to fourth order, so its Hessian is zero and the spectrum is $0,1,\ldots ,1$. These spectra differ for every $\epsilon >0$. $\square$

23.2.18 Ordinary and twisted potentials435B9E1C507B

Let $\omega $ be an LCK metric with Lee form $\lambda $.

The Kähler covering provides a potential calculus compatible with the Lee equation. Conjugating the ordinary differential operators by the local conformal factor produces a twisted complex Hessian on $X$.

For the fixed closed one-form $\lambda $, define

\[ \mathrm{d}_\lambda =\mathrm{d}-\lambda \wedge , \qquad \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}_\lambda =\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}-\lambda ^{\mathrm c}\wedge , \qquad D_\lambda =\mathrm{d}_\lambda \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}_\lambda . \]

On a chart where $\lambda =\mathrm{d}f$, these operators satisfy

A1632B661AC9 23.2.19
\begin{equation} \mathrm{d}_\lambda =\mathrm{e}^ f\mathrm{d}\mathrm{e}^{-f}, \qquad \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}_\lambda =\mathrm{e}^ f\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\mathrm{e}^{-f}, \qquad D_\lambda =\mathrm{e}^ f\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{e}^{-f}. \end{equation}

These identities show that $D_\lambda u$ is a real $(1,1)$-form for every smooth real function $u$, and that $\mathrm{d}_\lambda D_\lambda u=0$.

Proposition 23.2.20.B365A2A75E69 Let $u$ be a smooth real function. Then

\[ \mathrm{d}(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)-\lambda \wedge (\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u) =-\lambda \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u, \]

whereas

\[ \mathrm{d}(\omega +D_\lambda u) =\lambda \wedge (\omega +D_\lambda u). \]

Consequently, an ordinary potential preserves the LCK equation with the same Lee form precisely when $\lambda \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=0$. A twisted potential always preserves that equation, and it gives an LCK metric whenever $\omega +D_\lambda u>0$.

Proof. The first identity follows from $\mathrm{d}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=0$ and $\mathrm{d}\omega =\lambda \wedge \omega $. The second follows from $\mathrm{d}_\lambda \omega =0$ and (23.2.19). $\square$

On the Kähler covering, a twisted potential becomes the automorphic function $\widetilde u=\mathrm{e}^{-F}\pi ^*u$, and

48546D806E67 23.2.21
\begin{equation} \mathrm{e}^{-F}\pi ^*(\omega +D_\lambda u) =\widetilde\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde u. \end{equation}

The top power $(\widetilde\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde u)^ n$ transforms by $\chi ^ n$ under deck transformations, so its ratio to $\widetilde\omega ^ n$ is invariant and descends to $X$. A measure on $X$ is obtained from this ratio only after a metric in the conformal class has been fixed; the automorphic top-degree form cannot simply be integrated over the noncompact cover.

The two kinds of potentials also differ in their normalization. Expanding the twisted Hessian gives

F2EE69C448EB 23.2.22
\begin{equation} D_\lambda u =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u-\mathrm{d}u\wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c} -\lambda \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u +u\bigl (\kappa _\lambda +\lambda \wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c}\bigr ). \end{equation}

In particular, $D_\lambda 1=\kappa _\lambda +\lambda \wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c}$ need not vanish, so adding a constant to $u$ can change the metric $\omega +D_\lambda u$. The usual normalization $\sup _ Xu=0$ is therefore not harmless for twisted equations.

For a chosen Lee form $\lambda $ in its de Rham class, the real twisted Bott–Chern group in bidegree $(1,1)$ is

\[ H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC},\lambda }(X,\mathbb R) =\frac{\{ \eta \in C^\infty (X,\Lambda ^{1,1}_{\mathbb R}): \mathrm{d}_\lambda \eta =0\} }{D_\lambda C^\infty (X,\mathbb R)}. \]

This is Bott–Chern cohomology with values in the weight bundle [Section 18.5, OV24LCK]. It differs both from ordinary Bott–Chern cohomology and from the quotient of all backgrounds by ordinary $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$-terms considered in section 10.30.

23.2.23 The Vaisman metric on the Hopf manifoldD1CB266917DA

Return to the Hopf threefold and the forms $s$, $\alpha $, $\beta $ and $\omega =\alpha +\beta $ of section 23.1; recall that $s=\log |z|^2$ on the covering $\mathbb C^3\setminus \{ 0\} $. The radial description makes the Lee form explicit and shows that this Gauduchon metric is also Vaisman.

Proposition 23.2.24.32084430C8AD The Gauduchon form $\omega =\alpha +\beta $ of section 23.1 is Vaisman, with Lee form $\lambda =-\mathrm{d}s$, and

\[ \kappa _\lambda =\alpha , \qquad D_\lambda 1=\omega . \]

In particular, the bounded mass property of definition 13.14.14 fails for the ordinary potential theory of a background that is simultaneously Gauduchon and Vaisman.

Proof. Using $\alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s$ and $\beta =\mathrm{d}s\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s$, direct differentiation gives

\[ \mathrm{d}\omega =-\mathrm{d}s\wedge \alpha =-\mathrm{d}s\wedge \omega , \qquad \mathrm{e}^ s\omega =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{e}^ s)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2. \]

Although $s$ is not a function on the quotient, $\mathrm{d}s$ descends to it. Its integral along the loop induced by $z\mapsto \mathrm{e}^{-1}z$ is $-2$, so the Lee form is not exact.

Let $g_{S^5}$ be the round metric on the unit sphere. Up to a constant factor, the Riemannian metric associated with $\omega $ on the cover is

\[ \frac{|\mathrm{d}z|^2}{|z|^2} =\mathrm{d}t^2+g_{S^5},\qquad t=\log |z|. \]

The one-form $\mathrm{d}t$ is parallel for this product metric, which proves the Vaisman condition. Finally, $\kappa _\lambda =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}s=\alpha $ and $\lambda \wedge \lambda ^{\mathrm c}=\beta $, so $D_\lambda 1=\omega $ by (23.2.22). The unbounded masses are given by theorem 23.1.1. $\square$

The unbounded masses arise in the ordinary family $\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$, whose members need not satisfy the Lee equation. The volume problem within the class of Vaisman metrics imposes this additional geometric constraint.

23.2.25 Volume equations in Gauduchon gauge29CDAA9CF041

Let $X$ again be a compact LCK manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$. Prescribing the volume of an LCK metric in Gauduchon gauge couples two equations: one for the determinant and one for the conformal factor. To compare metrics with a fixed Lee class, we first identify the twisted groups for cohomologous Lee forms. For a closed real one-form $\lambda $, smooth real functions $a,u$ and a smooth differential form $\eta $, multiplication by $\mathrm{e}^{-a}$ gives

\[ \mathrm{d}_\lambda (\mathrm{e}^{-a}\eta ) =\mathrm{e}^{-a}\mathrm{d}_{\lambda +\mathrm{d}a}\eta , \qquad D_\lambda (\mathrm{e}^{-a}u)=\mathrm{e}^{-a}D_{\lambda +\mathrm{d}a}u. \]

We use this identification when comparing classes; the additive constant in $a$ corresponds to a constant rescaling of the weight bundle.

23.2.67 Strongly Gauduchon metrics5A635830FDEB

As in subsection 23.2.55, $X$ is an arbitrary connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 2$. The closed form underlying a strongly Gauduchon metric pairs positively with every nonzero closed positive $(1,1)$-current, and this pairing depends only on the de Rham class of the current. It therefore bounds the mass of closed positive currents in terms of their de Rham classes and excludes nonzero positive de Rham-exact currents. The Lee–Gauduchon cone, by contrast, tests only the de Rham-exact Bott–Chern classes in the image of $j_ X$. Definition 23.2.68.ADA9E5EAD64C A Hermitian metric $\gamma $ is strongly Gauduchon if $\gamma ^{n-1}$ is the $(n-1,n-1)$-component of a smooth closed real $(2n-2)$-form.

Write the closed real form in the definition as

\[ \Theta =\gamma ^{n-1}+\beta +\overline\beta , \qquad \beta \text{ of type }(n,n-2). \]

Then $\partial (\gamma ^{n-1})=-\bar\partial \beta $. Conversely, this equation and its conjugate give $\mathrm{d}\Theta =0$, because all other components of $\mathrm d\Theta $ have a type with an entry exceeding $n$. Thus $\gamma $ is strongly Gauduchon if and only if $\partial (\gamma ^{n-1})$ is $\bar\partial $-exact. Applying $\bar\partial $ shows that a strongly Gauduchon metric is Gauduchon. Every balanced metric is strongly Gauduchon, with $\Theta =\gamma ^{n-1}$.

Proof. Put $\Gamma =\gamma ^{n-1}$ and use $\beta $ as above. Then

\[ \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\Gamma =\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi } (\bar\partial \beta -\partial \overline\beta ) =\mathrm{d}\! \left(\frac{\mathrm i}{4\pi } (\beta -\overline\beta )\right). \]

Thus $[\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\Gamma ]=0$.

\[ \int _ XT\wedge \Gamma =\int _ XT\wedge \Theta =0. \]

But the first integral is strictly positive because $T\ne 0$ and $\Gamma >0$, a contradiction. Thus a strongly Gauduchon metric excludes nonzero positive exact currents.

Conversely, suppose that no strongly Gauduchon metric exists. In the real Fréchet space of smooth $(2n-2)$-forms, let $\mathcal Z$ be the closed subspace of closed forms, and let $\mathcal P$ be the open convex cone of forms with strictly positive $(n-1,n-1)$-part. This cone is nonempty, since it contains $h^{n-1}$ for any Hermitian metric $h$. The two sets are disjoint, because the positive $(n-1)$st root of the $(n-1,n-1)$-part of a form in their intersection would be a strongly Gauduchon metric. The Hahn–Banach separation theorem therefore gives a nonzero real current $T$ of degree two such that

\[ T|_{\mathcal Z}=0,\qquad T(\Theta )\geq 0\quad (\Theta \in \mathcal P). \]

Since $T$ annihilates every exact test form, it is closed. All these pairings vanish, so $T=\mathrm{d}S$ for a real current $S$. Thus $T$ is a nonzero positive exact current, which proves the converse. $\square$

Under this condition, the family of closed positive $(1,1)$-currents whose de Rham classes belong to a fixed compact set is weakly compact, and the image of the pseudoeffective Bott–Chern cone in $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)$ is a closed convex cone containing no line.

Proof. Suppose that $\gamma $ is strongly Gauduchon, and choose a smooth closed real form $\Theta $ whose $(n-1,n-1)$-part is $\gamma ^{n-1}$. By compactness, $h^{n-1}\leq A\gamma ^{n-1}$ for some $A>0$. Positivity and type considerations then give

\[ \int _ XT\wedge h^{n-1} \leq A\int _ XT\wedge \gamma ^{n-1} =A\langle [T]_{\mathrm{dR}},[\Theta ]_{\mathrm{dR}}\rangle . \]

The last pairing is a fixed linear functional on the finite-dimensional de Rham group, which proves (23.2.71). Conversely, this bound forces every closed positive de Rham-exact current to vanish, and proposition 23.2.69 gives a strongly Gauduchon metric.

For the compactness assertion, the bound gives uniformly bounded trace masses. A weak limit of such currents is positive and closed, and its de Rham class is the limit of their classes, as one sees by pairing with a finite basis of smooth closed forms of complementary degree and using Poincaré duality. Thus the family is weakly closed, and the usual weak compactness of positive currents with bounded mass proves the assertion.

Let $c_ j$ be a convergent sequence in the image cone, and choose closed positive currents $T_ j$ with $[T_ j]_{\mathrm{dR}}=c_ j$. By the compactness assertion, a subsequence converges to a closed positive current representing the limit of the classes. Its Bott–Chern class is pseudoeffective, so the image cone is closed. Convexity follows from addition and nonnegative scaling. Finally, if both $c$ and $-c$ lie in the image, choose closed positive currents $T$ and $S$ representing them. Their sum is de Rham exact, so (23.2.71) gives $T+S=0$, and positivity gives $T=S=0$, hence $c=0$. $\square$

Example 23.2.72.1EE117A3B0B0 There is a connected compact complex threefold $X$ with

\[ \operatorname {LG}(X)=\{ 0\} ,\qquad V_ X=K_ X=W_ X=0, \]

but with no strongly Gauduchon metric. Moreover, $X$ has a nonzero pseudoeffective Bott–Chern class whose de Rham image is zero; this class is not in the image of $j_ X$.

Proof. Consider the holomorphic action of the additive group $\mathbb C$ on

\[ \widetilde X=(\mathbb C^2\setminus \{ 0\} )\times (\mathbb C^2\setminus \{ 0\} ), \qquad t\cdot (z,w)=(\mathrm{e}^ t z,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm i t}w). \]

The action is free, since $\mathrm{e}^ t=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm i t}=1$ forces $t=0$. It is also proper. Indeed, if both $(z,w)$ and $t\cdot (z,w)$ range in compact subsets of $\widetilde X$, then the norms of the four vectors $z$, $w$, $\mathrm e^ tz$ and $\mathrm e^{\mathrm it}w$ in $\mathbb C^2$ are bounded above and away from zero. Writing $t=a+\mathrm i b$, the ratios of these norms bound $\mathrm{e}^ a$ and $\mathrm{e}^{-b}$ above and away from zero, so $a$ and $b$ stay bounded. A free proper holomorphic action has local holomorphic slices, and its quotient $X$ is a Hausdorff complex manifold of dimension three.

Every orbit meets $S^3\times S^3$ exactly once, at the image of $(z,w)$ under the group element with

\[ a=-\log |z|,\qquad b=\log |w|. \]

These expressions depend smoothly on $(z,w)$, so the slice $S^3\times S^3$ identifies the quotient with $S^3\times S^3$ as a smooth manifold. In particular, $X$ is compact and connected, and

\[ H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)=H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R) =H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)=0. \]

It follows that $V_ X=K_ X=W_ X=0$ and $\operatorname {LG}(X)=\{ 0\} $; the latter cone is nonempty by Gauduchon's theorem and lies in the zero group $H^5_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)$.

The map $(z,w)\mapsto ([z],[w])$ is invariant under the action and descends to a holomorphic map

\[ p:X\longrightarrow \mathbb P^1\times \mathbb P^1. \]

It is a submersion, because the map on $\widetilde X$ is a submersion and the orbits of the action lie in its fibers. Let $\eta $ be the sum of the Fubini–Study forms on the two factors. The smooth form

\[ \alpha =p^*\eta \]

is closed, semipositive and of rank two everywhere. It is nonzero and de Rham exact, since $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb R)=0$. By proposition 23.2.69, $X$ admits no strongly Gauduchon metric.

The Bott–Chern class of $\alpha $ is nonzero: otherwise $\alpha =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ for a smooth real function $f$, and the integral of $\alpha $ against any Gauduchon form would vanish, contradicting $\alpha \geq 0$ and $\alpha \ne 0$. The class is pseudoeffective, since $\alpha $ is a positive representative. Finally, $j_ X$ has zero domain, so this class is not in its image. $\square$

Thus $0\in \operatorname {LG}(X)$ does not imply the existence of a strongly Gauduchon metric, and hence does not imply the existence of a balanced metric.

On the threefold of proposition 12.19.18, which carries a balanced and hence strongly Gauduchon metric, let $\alpha ,\beta $ be the two pulled-back Fubini–Study forms. The nonzero class $\{ \beta -\alpha \} $ has zero de Rham image, and neither it nor its negative is pseudoeffective, by proposition 10.19.2. Every Hermitian form on this threefold has zero lower mass in the sense of definition 13.14.14. Thus the existence of a strongly Gauduchon metric guarantees neither injectivity of the map from Bott–Chern to de Rham cohomology nor the positive lower mass needed for solvability with every finite non-pluripolar measure.
