16.9 The $d_ S$-pseudometric45BED73AEFE3

45BED73AEFE3

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta $ be a closed real smooth $(1,1)$-form on $X$ representing a big cohomology class. The goal of this section is to study a pseudometric on the space $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

16.9.1 The definition of the $d_ S$-pseudometricBB740CB4BE99

Recall that for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the geodesic ray $\ell ^{\varphi }\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta )$ is defined in example 17.10.92.

Definition 16.9.2.240B1899916C For $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we define

\[ d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )\coloneqq d_1(\ell ^{\varphi },\ell ^{\psi }). \]

When we want to be more specific, we write $d_{S,\theta }$ instead of $d_ S$.

The $d_1$ distance of geodesic rays is defined in definition 17.10.80.

Proposition 16.9.3.1ABAE06071CD The function $d_ S$ defined in definition 16.9.2 is a pseudometric on $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Proof. This follows immediately from theorem 17.10.81. $\square$

When studying a pseudometric, the first thing is to understand when the distance between two elements vanishes.

We first prove a preparation:

Lemma 16.9.4.2490CFA511EC Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

\[ d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )\leq d_ S(\varphi ,\varphi \lor \psi )+d_ S(\psi ,\varphi \lor \psi ) \leq C_ n d_ S(\varphi ,\psi ), \]

where $C_ n=3(n+1)2^{n+2}$.

We shall use the notations introduced in example 17.10.92.

Proof. We claim that

FE54EE184B7A 16.9.5
\begin{equation} \ell ^{\varphi }\lor \ell ^{\psi }=\ell ^{\varphi \lor \psi }. \end{equation}

Recall that $\lor $ is defined in definition 17.10.85. Note that this assertion implies our desired inequality by 17.10.90.

In proving this assertion, we may assume that $\varphi ,\psi \leq 0$ since

\[ \ell ^{\varphi +C}=\ell ^{\varphi },\quad \ell ^{\psi +C}=\ell ^{\psi },\quad \ell ^{(\varphi +C)\lor (\psi +C)}=\ell ^{\varphi \lor \psi } \]

for any $C\in \mathbb {R}$.

In fact, it is clear that

\[ \ell ^{\varphi }\leq \ell ^{\varphi \lor \psi },\quad \ell ^{\psi }\leq \ell ^{\varphi \lor \psi }, \]

so the $\leq $ direction in (16.9.5) holds.

Conversely, if $\ell '\in \mathcal{R}^1(X,\theta )$ and $\ell '\geq \ell ^{\varphi }\lor \ell ^{\psi }$, then for each $t\geq 0$,

\[ \ell '_ t\geq \Bigl ((V_{\theta }-t)\lor \varphi \Bigr ) \lor \Bigl ((V_{\theta }-t)\lor \psi \Bigr )=(V_{\theta }-t)\lor (\varphi \lor \psi ). \]

See (17.10.96) for the first inequality. Therefore, thanks to the definition of the Perron envelopes (cf. (17.10.21)), we have

\[ \ell '_ s\geq \ell ^{\varphi \lor \psi ,t}_ s \]

for any $0\leq s\leq t$. It follows from (17.10.96) that $\ell '_ s\geq \ell ^{\varphi \lor \psi }_ s$ for any $s\geq 0$. $\square$

Proposition 16.9.6.C1D8A9BD3B3B Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. CCA0DECFB7E5

    $\varphi \sim _ P \psi $;

  2. A3A85D64324F

    $d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )=0$.

In particular, $d_ S(\varphi ,P_{\theta }[\varphi ])=0$ for all $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Proof. By 16.8.13, we have $\varphi \sim _ P \psi $ if and only if $\varphi \sim _ P \varphi \lor \psi $ and $\psi \sim _ P \varphi \lor \psi $. By 16.9.4, $d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )=0$ if and only if $d_ S(\varphi ,\varphi \lor \psi )=0$ and $d_ S(\psi ,\varphi \lor \psi )=0$. So it suffices to prove the assertion when $\varphi \leq \psi $. Assuming this, by proposition 17.10.83, 2 holds if and only if

\[ \mathbf{E}\left(\ell ^{\varphi }\right)= \mathbf{E}\left(\ell ^{\psi }\right), \]

where $\mathbf{E}$ is introduced in definition 17.10.82. But by (17.10.94), this holds if and only if

\[ \sum _{j=0}^ n \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\sum _{j=0}^ n \int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \]

Thanks to the monotonicity theorem theorem 13.13.11, this holds if and only if for all $j=0,\ldots ,n$,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}, \]

which is equivalent to 1 by proposition 16.8.7. $\square$

Lemma 16.9.7.03FCA1AB2751 Suppose that $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \preceq _ P \psi $. Then

\[ d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n \left(\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\right). \]

Proof. Set $\chi \coloneqq \varphi \lor \psi $. By 16.8.13, we have $\chi \sim _ P\psi $. Hence proposition 16.9.6 gives $d_ S(\chi ,\psi )=0$, and the pseudometric property gives

\[ d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )=d_ S(\varphi ,\chi ). \]

Since $\varphi \leq \chi $, (17.10.94) and proposition 17.10.83 give

\[ d_ S(\varphi ,\chi )=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n \left(\int _ X \theta _{\chi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}\right). \]

Finally, proposition 16.8.7 applied to $\psi \leq \chi $ gives

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\chi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j} \]

for all $j=0,\ldots ,n$. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.8.255A623EA393 Suppose that $\varphi ,\psi ,\eta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \preceq _ P\psi \preceq _ P \eta $. Then

\[ d_ S(\varphi ,\eta )\geq d_ S(\varphi ,\psi ),\quad d_ S(\varphi ,\eta )\geq d_ S(\psi ,\eta ). \]

Proof. This is an immediate consequence of 16.9.7 and the monotonicity theorem proposition 16.8.22. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.9.B29DCA70A9E6 For any $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, we have

0AC35440957F 16.9.10
\begin{equation} \begin{split} d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )\leq & \frac{1}{n+1}\sum _{j=0}^ n \left( 2\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}-\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j} \right) \\ \leq & C_ n d_ S(\varphi ,\psi ), \end{split} \end{equation}

where $C_ n=3(n+1)2^{n+2}$.

In particular, if $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $d_ S$-limit $\varphi $, then for each $j=0,\ldots ,n$,

\[ \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}=\lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}. \]

Proof. The estimates (16.9.10) follow from the combination of 16.9.7 and 16.9.4.

Suppose that $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Then $\varphi _ i\lor \varphi \xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $ by 16.9.4. Therefore, theorem 13.13.11 and 16.9.7 imply that

\[ \lim _{i\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i\lor \varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j}= \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ j\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-j} \]

for any $j=0,\ldots ,n$. The last assertion now follows from (16.9.10) and theorem 13.13.11. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.11.94B2CC4C30BB Let $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ be a proper bimeromorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold. Given a net $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, the following are equivalent:

  1. $\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $;

  2. $\pi ^*\varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S}\pi ^*\varphi $.

Proof. It suffices to observe that the middle term in (16.9.10) is invariant under $\pi ^*$. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.12.77738CEC8071 Suppose that $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is an increasing net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, uniformly bounded from above. Then

\[ \varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S} \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i. \]
If the $\varphi _ i$'s are all model potentials in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, then so is $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ i$, as we have seen in proposition 16.4.9.

Proof. Write $\varphi =\operatorname *{sup}_{j\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ j$. Recall that by proposition 5.14.2, $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. By 16.9.7, it suffices to show that for each $k=0,\ldots ,n$, we have

\[ \lim _{j\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k} = \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}. \]

The latter follows from corollary 13.13.12. $\square$

Corollary 16.9.13.D47345F0C3C8 Let $\varphi ,\psi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. Then

\[ \left| \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n \right|\leq D_ n d_ S(\varphi ,\psi ), \]

where $D_ n=3(n+1)C_ n$ with $C_ n$ being the same constant as in 16.9.4.

In other words, the non-pluripolar mass is uniformly continuous with respect to the $d_ S$-pseudometric.

Proof. We compute

\[ \begin{aligned} \left| \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n \right|\leq & \left|2\int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n\right| + 2\left| \int _ X \theta _{\varphi \lor \psi }^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n\right|\\ \leq & (n+1)C_ n d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )+2(n+1) d_ S(\varphi ,\varphi \lor \psi )\\ \leq & (n+1)C_ n d_ S(\varphi ,\psi )+2(n+1)C_ n d_ S(\varphi ,\psi ), \end{aligned} \]

where the first line is just the triangle inequality, the second line follows from corollary 16.9.9 and the third line follows from 16.9.4. $\square$

By contrast to the increasing case, for nets decreasing with respect to the $P$-preorder, the situation is different:

Corollary 16.9.14.F84D53FDA599 Suppose that $(\varphi _ i)_{i\in I}$ is a net in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

\[ \varphi _ j\preceq _ P\varphi _ i \]

whenever $j\geq i$. Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ be such that $\varphi \preceq _ P\varphi _ i$ for all $i\in I$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. 3D1A7D703ADF

    We have

    \[ \varphi _ i\xrightarrow {d_ S} \varphi ; \]
  2. 919198E0409A

    for each $k=0,\ldots ,n$, we have

    E95AEF6203E2 16.9.15
    \begin{equation} \lim _{j\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k} = \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ k\wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}. \end{equation}

If we assume furthermore that $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$, then the above conditions are equivalent to the following:

  1. 8DEC59327E72

    We have

    \[ \lim _{j\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

When $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$ and these equivalent conditions are met, we also have

FC1B825A9516 16.9.16
\begin{equation} P_{\theta }[\varphi ]=\inf _{j\in I}P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]. \end{equation}

Proof. 1 $\iff $ 2. This follows immediately from 16.9.7.

Assume that $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n>0$.

2 $\implies $ 1. This is the case $k=n$ in (16.9.15).

1 $\implies $ 2. Assume (3). If for some $i\in I$, $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n= \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n$, then for all $j\geq i$ we have

\[ \varphi \preceq _ P\varphi _ j\preceq _ P\varphi _ i. \]

Hence $\int _ X\theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\int _ X\theta _{\varphi }^ n$ by proposition 16.8.22. It follows from proposition 16.8.7 that $\varphi _ j\sim _ P\varphi $, and (2) follows from proposition 16.3.10. So in the sequel, we shall assume that for all $i\in I$, we have $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ i}^ n>\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n$. In particular, each $\varphi _ i$ has positive mass.

Let $(b_ j)_{j\in I}$ be a net converging to $\infty $ such that

\[ b_ j\in \left( 1, \left(\frac{\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n}{\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n}\right)^{1/n} \right). \]

This can be achieved for example by taking $b_ j$ as the midpoint of this interval. By theorem 16.8.23, for each $j\in I$, we can find $\eta _ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

\[ b_ j^{-1}\eta _ j +\left(1-b_ j^{-1}\right)\varphi _ j \leq \varphi . \]

It follows from the monotonicity theorem theorem 13.13.11 that for any $k=0,\ldots ,n$,

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ k \wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}\geq \left(1-b_ j^{-1}\right)^ k\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ k \wedge \theta _{V_{\theta }}^{n-k}. \]

Taking the limit, we conclude the $\leq $ direction in (16.9.15). The $\geq $ direction follows from proposition 16.8.22.

Finally, we argue (16.9.16). Let $\psi _ j=P_{\theta }[\varphi _ j]$. It follows from corollary 16.3.20 that $\psi _ j$ is a model potential. Since the net $(\varphi _ j)_{j\in I}$ is decreasing with respect to the $P$-preorder, $(\psi _ j)_{j\in I}$ is decreasing pointwise. Let

\[ \psi =\inf _{j\in I}\psi _ j\geq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]. \]

By proposition 16.4.3, $\psi $ is a model potential. Since $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]\leq \psi \leq \psi _ j$ for all $j\in I$, the monotonicity theorem theorem 13.13.11 and proposition 16.3.10 give

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n=\int _ X\theta _{P_{\theta }[\varphi ]}^ n\leq \int _ X \theta _{\psi }^ n\leq \lim _{j\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\psi _ j}^ n=\lim _{j\in I}\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n=\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n. \]

Hence $\psi $ has the same mass as $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$. As $\psi $ is model and $\psi \geq P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$, theorem 16.3.15 gives $\psi =P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$. $\square$

Having understood the increasing and decreasing cases, we shall handle general convergent sequences. In fact, since $d_ S$ is a pseudometric, the topology is completely determined by convergent sequences, so we do not need to consider nets in general.

Proposition 16.9.17.1DEED63B61B0 Let $\varphi _ j,\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ ($j\geq 1$), $\varphi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $. Assume that there is $\delta >0$ such that

\[ \int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n\geq \delta \]

for all $j$ and the $\varphi _ j$'s are all model potentials. Then up to replacing $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ by a subsequence, there is a decreasing sequence $(\psi _ j)_ j$ and an increasing sequence $(\eta _ j)_ j$ in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ such that

  1. 36F21B225E6D

    $\psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $, $\eta _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $;

  2. 1EE77849B2C2

    $\psi _ j\geq \varphi _ j\geq \eta _ j$ for all $j$.

In fact, for any $j\geq 1$, we will take

\[ \eta _ j=\inf _{k\in \mathbb {N}}\varphi _ j\land \varphi _{j+1}\land \cdots \land \varphi _{j+k},\quad \psi _ j=\operatorname *{sup}_{k\geq j}{}\! {}^{*}\varphi _ k. \]

Proof. First note that $\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n\geq \delta $ as well, due to the $d_ S$-continuity of non-pluripolar masses as proved in corollary 16.9.13. Replacing $\varphi $ by $P_{\theta }[\varphi ]$, we may then assume that $\varphi $ is a model potential in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

We are free to replace $(\varphi _ j)_ j$ by a subsequence. So we may assume that

\begin{equation} d_ S(\varphi _ j,\varphi _{j+1})\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-4}}{D_ n C_ n^{j+1}},\quad d_ S(\varphi ,\varphi _ j)\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-4}}{D_ n}, \end{equation}

where $C_ n$ is the constant in corollary 16.9.9, $D_ n$ is the constant in corollary 16.9.13.

In particular, by corollary 16.9.13,

\begin{equation} \left|\int _ X \theta _{\varphi _ j}^ n-\int _ X \theta _{\varphi }^ n\right|\leq \delta 2^{-j-4}. \end{equation}

By iteration, we find

\[ \begin{split} d_ S(\varphi _ j,\varphi _ j\lor \varphi _{j+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{j+k})\leq \sum _{a=j}^{j+k-1}C_ n^{a+1-j}d_ S(\varphi _ a,\varphi _{a+1})\\ \leq \frac{C_ n^{-j}}{D_ n}\sum _{a=j}^{j+k-1}\delta 2^{-a-4}\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-3}}{D_ n}. \end{split} \]

Using corollary 16.9.12, we have

\[ \varphi _ j\lor \varphi _{j+1}\lor \cdots \lor \varphi _{j+k}\xrightarrow {d_ S}\psi _ j \]

as $k\to \infty $. Hence

\begin{equation} d_ S(\varphi _ j,\psi _ j)\leq \frac{\delta 2^{-j-3}}{D_ n}. \end{equation}

We conclude that $\psi _ j\xrightarrow {d_ S}\varphi $.

\begin{equation} \varphi =\inf _{j\geq 1} P_{\theta }[\psi _ j] \end{equation}

Using (16.9.18), (16.9.20) and corollary 16.9.13, we have

for all $j\geq 1$. Taking (16.9.19) into account, we have
