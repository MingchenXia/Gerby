14.24 Bounded uniqueness and connectednessE0DB623401C5

E0DB623401C5

For a continuous density whose positive set is connected and dense, bounded solutions are unique up to a constant. An energy measure of $w=u-v$ recovers the information supplied by differentiability. Its pushforward under $w$ has a Lipschitz density vanishing at the extreme values. If $w$ were nonconstant, these properties would force both extremal sets to have zero mass for the common Monge–Ampère measure. A smooth test potential with a larger density near one of these sets then contradicts modified comparison.

Theorem 14.24.1.98EC31947E4C Let $F\in C^0(X)$ satisfy $F\geq 0$, $F\not\equiv 0$, with $U=\{ F>0\} $ connected and dense. If bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u,v$ satisfy

\[ \omega _ u^ n=\omega _ v^ n=F\omega ^ n, \]

then $u-v$ is constant. In particular, $\sup _ Xu=\sup _ Xv$ implies $u=v$.

Continuity of $F$ supplies the local positive lower bounds and the neighborhoods of the extremal set used below. The auxiliary test densities are chosen smooth independently of $F$. Bounded solutions are continuous by corollary 14.19.29, so we may use pointwise extrema throughout the proof. We shall also use modified comparison in the following form. If $b$ is continuous and $\omega _ b\geq \varepsilon \omega $ for some $0<\varepsilon <1$, then $b/(1-\varepsilon )$ is $\omega $-plurisubharmonic, so theorem 14.19.6 applies with $(1-\varepsilon )v=b$. Thus its thin-sublevel estimate applies directly to such a strictly positive comparison potential $b$, and the correction factor tends to one as the height of the sublevel set tends to zero, with $\varepsilon $ fixed.

Proposition 14.24.2.2C3AA01F259B Let $u,v$ be continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions with $\omega _ u^ n=\omega _ v^ n=F\omega ^ n$, where $F\geq 0$ is bounded and measurable. Let $q$ be a smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function, and write $\omega _ q^ n=G\omega ^ n$. Then for every $0<\kappa <1$ and $\eta >0$,

C7BC3EE4D543 14.24.3
\begin{equation} F\omega ^ n\bigl (\{ u-v<\min _ X(u-v)+\eta \} \cap \{ F>\kappa G\} \bigr )>0. \end{equation}

The analogous assertion holds for the set $\{ u-v>\max _ X(u-v)-\eta \} $.

Proof. Choose $\delta \in (0,1]$ with $\omega _ q\geq \delta \omega $. For $0<t<1$, put $b_ t=(1-t)v+tq$, $m_ t=\min _ X(u-b_ t)$, and $E_ s=\{ u<b_ t+m_ t+s\} $. Then $\omega _{b_ t}\geq t\delta \omega $. Write $\mu =F\omega ^ n$, $d=\kappa ^{-1/n}-1$, and $\gamma =nd$. By (5.28.9),

\[ \omega _{b_ t}^ n\geq \bigl ((1-t)F^{1/n}+tG^{1/n}\bigr )^ n \omega ^ n. \]

On $L=\{ F\leq \kappa G\} $ this is at least $(1+\gamma t)\mu $, since $G^{1/n}\geq (1+d)F^{1/n}$ there, and on $R=\{ F>\kappa G\} $ it is at least $(1-nt)\mu $. Choose $s>0$ so small that the reverse comparison factor in (14.19.7), with $\varepsilon =t\delta $, is at most $1+\gamma t/2$. Modified comparison on $E_ s$, with $x=\mu (E_ s\cap L)$ and $y=\mu (E_ s\cap R)$, gives

\[ (1+\gamma t)x+(1-nt)y\leq (1+\gamma t/2)(x+y), \qquad \text{and hence}\qquad y\geq \frac{1-\kappa ^{1/n}}2\, \mu (E_ s). \]

The open set $E_ s$ is nonempty by the definition of $m_ t$. As $\omega _{b_ t}^ n\geq (t\delta )^ n\omega ^ n$, (14.19.7) forces $\mu (E_ s)>0$, hence $y>0$.

Let $a=\min _ X(u-v)$ and $\Lambda =\| v-q\| _\infty $. Since $u-b_ t=u-v+t(v-q)$, we have $|m_ t-a|\leq t\Lambda $ and $E_ s\subset \{ u-v<a+2t\Lambda +s\} $. Given $\eta $, choose $t$ and then $s$ with $2t\Lambda +s<\eta $. Since $y=\mu (E_ s\cap R)>0$, this proves (14.24.3). Interchanging $u$ and $v$ gives the assertion for the near-maximum set. $\square$

14.24.4 The energy of the difference42826D458068

In this subsection, $u,v$ are continuous $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions with $A^ n=B^ n=F\omega ^ n$ for a bounded measurable function $F\geq 0$, and $w=u-v$. Although the cofactor $T$ is now only a current, its product with $\mathrm dw\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w$ still defines a positive measure. The identity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$ determines how this energy is distributed over the level sets of $w$.

Proposition 14.24.5.25FABE38E60B There is a finite positive measure $E=\mathrm{d} w\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge T$, defined below as a limit of regularizations, with the following properties.

For every smooth real function $\chi $ on $\mathbb R$,

5EA1FD990FD5 14.24.6
\begin{equation} \int _ X\chi ''(w)\, \mathrm{d} E=\int _ X\chi (w)\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T. \end{equation}

The signed current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T$ is a finite measure. If $a=\min _ Xw$, $b=\max _ Xw$, and

\[ k(t)=\int _ X(w-t)_+\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T, \]

then $k$ is nonnegative, globally Lipschitz, supported on $[a,b]$, and

B3915B7974CA 14.24.7
\begin{equation} w_*E=k(t)\, \mathrm{d} t. \end{equation}

In particular, $E$ charges no level set of $w$.

Let $V$ be a relatively compact coordinate domain such that $F$ has a positive essential lower bound on a neighborhood of $\overline V$. Then there is $C_ V<\infty $ such that every piecewise $C^1$ Lipschitz function $p$ on $[a,b]$ satisfies

C45BFBEEAF7F 14.24.8
\begin{equation} \int _ V|\mathrm{d}(p(w))|\, \mathrm{d}\lambda \leq C_ V\left(\int _ V|p'(w)|^2\, \mathrm{d} E\right)^{1/2}, \end{equation}

where $\mathrm{d}\lambda $ is Euclidean coordinate volume.

Proof. Locally, expand $T$ into powers of the smooth form $\omega $ multiplied by closed Bedford–Taylor products of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$. The operator $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ then acts only on the powers of $\omega $, and the local mass bounds in section 5.27 show that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T$ is a finite signed measure.

We first construct $E$ and prove (14.24.6). On a coordinate ball, convolve $u$, $v$ and the coefficients of $\omega $ with a common nonnegative radial mollifier. Write the resulting smooth positive forms as $A_\rho =\omega _\rho +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\rho $ and $B_\rho =\omega _\rho +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\rho $, and let $T_\rho $ be their mixed sum. On a slightly larger ball, add the same fixed multiple of $|z|^2$ to each potential to obtain plurisubharmonic functions. Their radial regularizations decrease to the originals, so Bedford–Taylor continuity applies to all closed Hessian products. Since $u,v$ are continuous, $u_\rho ,v_\rho ,w_\rho $ converge uniformly on compact subsets.

For a closed smooth product $S_\rho $ appearing in $T_\rho $, the identity

D3E407319B3E 14.24.9
\begin{equation} \mathrm{d} w_\rho \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w_\rho \wedge S_\rho =\tfrac 12\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(w_\rho ^2S_\rho )-w_\rho \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\rho \wedge S_\rho \end{equation}

has a distributional limit: both Hessian products on the right converge weakly with uniformly bounded local variation, and their weights converge uniformly. The same holds after multiplication by the convergent smooth factors coming from $\omega _\rho $. Thus the positive measures $E_\rho =\mathrm{d} w_\rho \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w_\rho \wedge T_\rho $ converge to a finite positive measure $E$. By (14.24.9), this limit does not depend on the regularization, so the local limits agree on overlapping charts.

For smooth regularizations, the chain rule gives

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi (w_\rho )\wedge T_\rho =\chi ''(w_\rho )E_\rho +\chi '(w_\rho )\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\rho \wedge T_\rho . \]

The last current tends to zero, because $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w_\rho \wedge T_\rho =A_\rho ^ n-B_\rho ^ n$ tends weakly to $A^ n-B^ n=0$ with uniformly bounded variation, and $\chi '(w_\rho )$ converges uniformly. Passing to the limit and integrating by parts on the compact manifold $X$ gives (14.24.6). A partition of unity justifies this passage without a global regularization; the terms containing derivatives of the cutoff functions cancel after summation.

Let $\sigma =w_*(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T)$ and $\nu =w_*E$. Equation (14.24.6) says that $\nu ''=\sigma $ as distributions on $\mathbb R$. Stokes' theorem and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0$ give

\[ \sigma (\mathbb R)=\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=0,\qquad \int _{\mathbb R}s\, \mathrm{d}\sigma (s)=\int _ Xw\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w\wedge T=0. \]

Consequently $k(t)=\int (s-t)_+\, \mathrm{d}\sigma (s)$ is globally Lipschitz and vanishes outside $[a,b]$. It satisfies $k''=\sigma $, so the compactly supported distribution $\nu -k(t)\, \mathrm{d} t$ has second derivative zero and therefore vanishes. Positivity of $\nu $ and continuity of $k$ imply $k\geq 0$. This proves (14.24.7), and the assertion about level sets follows because $\nu $ is absolutely continuous.

We now prove (14.24.8). On a coordinate neighborhood of $\overline V$, set $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and choose $c_ V>0$ with $F\omega ^ n\geq c_ V\beta ^ n$. Let $A_{\mathrm{ac}}$ denote the absolutely continuous part of the current $A$, whose coefficients are locally integrable functions. Applying lemma 5.28.10 with the constant function $g=c_ V^{1/n}$ and differentiating the coefficient measures, we obtain $\det _\beta A_{\mathrm{ac}}\geq c_ V$ almost everywhere. The local Sobolev regularity in section 5.27 gives $w_\rho \to w$ strongly in $W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, so a subsequence of the gradients converges almost everywhere. Moreover, $A_\rho \to A_{\mathrm{ac}}$ almost everywhere, by differentiation of the coefficient measures.

Since $T_\rho $ contains the term $A_\rho ^{n-1}$ and all its terms are positive, Fatou's lemma, applied to $E_\rho $, yields the measure inequality

7EF7549DDD02 14.24.10
\begin{equation} E\geq \mathrm{d} w\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}w\wedge A_{\mathrm{ac}}^{n-1}. \end{equation}

The cofactor matrix of $A_{\mathrm{ac}}$ has smallest eigenvalue at least $\det A_{\mathrm{ac}}/\operatorname {tr}A_{\mathrm{ac}}\geq c_ V/\operatorname {tr}A_{\mathrm{ac}}$, where traces and determinants are taken with respect to $\beta $. The trace is locally integrable because $A$ has locally finite mass. The chain rule for Sobolev functions, (14.24.10) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give

\[ \int _ V|\mathrm{d}(p(w))|\, \mathrm{d}\lambda \leq C\left(\int _ V|p'(w)|^2\, \mathrm{d} E\right)^{1/2} \left(\int _ V\frac{\operatorname {tr}A_{\mathrm{ac}}}{c_ V}\, \mathrm{d}\lambda \right)^{1/2}. \]

The last factor is finite and is absorbed into $C_ V$. The values of $p'$ at the finitely many corners of $p$ are immaterial, because $E$ charges no level set of $w$. $\square$

Lemma 14.24.11.4FB9051BD00E In the setting of proposition 14.24.5, assume in addition that $F$ is continuous and that $U=\{ F>0\} $ is connected and dense. If $w=u-v$ is nonconstant, then

\[ F\omega ^ n(\{ w=\min _ Xw\} )=F\omega ^ n(\{ w=\max _ Xw\} )=0. \]

Proof. Put $a=\min _ Xw<b=\max _ Xw$, and let $k$ be the function of proposition 14.24.5. It is Lipschitz and nonnegative, and $k(a)=0$. Choose $\epsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ with $a+\epsilon _ j<b$. For $\delta >0$, set

\[ J_ j(\delta )=\int _ a^{a+\epsilon _ j}\frac{\mathrm{d} t}{k(t)+\delta }. \]

If $L$ is a Lipschitz constant for $k$, then $k(t)\leq L(t-a)$ on this interval, so $J_ j(\delta )\to \infty $ as $\delta \downarrow 0$. Choose $\delta _ j>0$ with $J_ j=J_ j(\delta _ j)\geq j$. Let $p_ j=0$ on $(-\infty ,a]$, $p_ j=1$ on $[a+\epsilon _ j,\infty )$, and

\[ p_ j'(t)=\frac{1}{J_ j(k(t)+\delta _ j)}\quad (a<t<a+\epsilon _ j). \]

The pushforward identity (14.24.7) gives

\[ \int _ X|p_ j'(w)|^2\, \mathrm{d} E =\frac{1}{J_ j^2}\int _ a^{a+\epsilon _ j}\frac{k(t)}{(k(t)+\delta _ j)^2}\, \mathrm{d} t \leq \frac1{J_ j}\longrightarrow 0. \]

Every point of $U$ has a coordinate neighborhood $V$ as in proposition 14.24.5, since $F$ is continuous. By (14.24.8), therefore, $\mathrm{d}(p_ j(w))\to 0$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. The bounded functions $p_ j(w)$ converge pointwise and in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ to $\mathbf1_{\{ w>a\} }$, whose distributional gradient therefore vanishes on $U$. Since $U$ is connected, this indicator function is almost everywhere constant on $U$. As $w$ is nonconstant, $\{ w>a\} $ is a nonempty open set, and its intersection with the dense open set $U$ is nonempty and open; hence the constant is one. Thus $\{ w=a\} \cap U$ has zero volume, and since $F=0$ off $U$, the first assertion follows. Applying the argument to $-w$ gives the assertion for the maximum set. $\square$

14.24.12 Concentrating smooth test potentialsE597C8D6FBF4

The next proposition produces smooth test potentials whose Monge–Ampère density dominates a fixed multiple of a prescribed function $g$, with a constant independent of $\sup _ Xg$ and of the derivatives of $g$. This uniformity allows the test densities to concentrate near a set of measure zero.

Proposition 14.24.13.7F590FD94846 For every $B_0>\| 1\| _{L^2(\omega ^ n)}$ there is $\varepsilon =\varepsilon (X,\omega ,B_0)>0$ such that every smooth function $g\geq 1$ with $\| g\| _{L^2(\omega ^ n)}\leq B_0$ admits a smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function $q$ satisfying

6928CCD52E25 14.24.14
\begin{equation} \omega _ q^ n\geq \varepsilon g \omega ^ n. \end{equation}

Proof. Choose finitely many coordinate balls $B_ j=\{ |z_ j|<R_ j\} $, with closures in their charts, such that the concentric smaller balls $\{ |z_ j|<r_ j\} $ cover $X$. On $B_ j$, let $\psi _ j$ solve the Dirichlet problem

768EAB7723D9 14.24.15
\begin{equation} (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ j)^ n=g\omega ^ n,\qquad \psi _ j|_{\partial B_ j}=0. \end{equation}

The density of $g\omega ^ n$ with respect to coordinate volume is smooth and strictly positive. The classical smooth Dirichlet theorem therefore applies, since $C_ j(|z_ j|^2-R_ j^2)$ is a smooth strict subsolution with zero boundary values for sufficiently large $C_ j>0$. Thus $\psi _ j$ is smooth and strictly plurisubharmonic on $\overline B_ j$, and the maximum principle gives $\psi _ j\leq 0$.

We need a lower bound for $\psi _ j$ independent of $\sup _ Xg$. Write the coordinate equation as $\det (\partial _ a\bar\partial _ b\psi _ j)=D_ j$, and put $M_ j=-\min _{B_ j}\psi _ j$. Let $x_ j$ be a minimum point. For each real slope $p$ with $|p|<M_ j/(2R_ j)$, the function $\psi _ j-p\cdot (x-x_ j)$ attains its minimum at an interior point, because its value at $x_ j$ is $-M_ j$, while its boundary values are larger than $-M_ j$. At such a contact point the real Hessian $S$ of $\psi _ j$ is nonnegative, and the gradient of $\psi _ j$ maps the contact set onto the ball of these slopes. The area formula and the matrix inequality

\[ \det _{\mathbb R}S\leq 4^ n\bigl (\det _{\mathbb C}(\partial _ a\bar\partial _ b\psi _ j)\bigr )^2=4^ nD_ j^2 \]

therefore give $M_ j\leq C(n,R_ j)\| D_ j\| _{L^2(B_ j)}^{1/n}$. To prove the matrix inequality, let $J$ be the standard complex structure on the real coordinate space and average $S$ with $J^\mathsf TSJ$. Concavity of $\log \det $, and the fact that the eigenvalues of this $J$-invariant average occur in pairs, give the displayed factor; singular matrices are handled by approximation. Since the ratio of $\omega ^ n$ to coordinate volume is fixed and bounded, $\| D_ j\| _2$ is controlled by $B_0$. Consequently

7044361ECDC0 14.24.16
\begin{equation} -M_0\leq \psi _ j\leq 0\quad \text{on every }B_ j, \end{equation}

where $M_0$ depends only on $X,\omega ,B_0$ and the chosen finite cover.

Next we glue the local solutions. Put $\beta _ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z_ j|^2$. Choose $a_ j>0$ such that $a_ j\beta _ j\leq \omega /2$ on $B_ j$, and let $t_ j=(R_ j^2+r_ j^2)/2$. Fix $\tau >0$ so small that $\tau M_0<a_ j(R_ j^2-r_ j^2)/4$ for every $j$. The smooth function

\[ s_ j=\tau \psi _ j+a_ j(t_ j-|z_ j|^2) \]

is bounded below by a fixed positive constant on the smaller ball, negative on a collar of $\partial B_ j$, and satisfies $\omega _{s_ j}\geq \omega /2+\tau \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ j$. Choose a smooth convex function $\phi _ j:\mathbb R\to \mathbb R$ approximating $s\mapsto \max (s,0)$, equal to zero below a small negative threshold and to $s$ above the corresponding positive threshold, with $0\leq \phi _ j'\leq 1$ and $\phi _ j''\geq 0$; the thresholds are chosen smaller in absolute value than both margins of $s_ j$. Set $Q_ j=\phi _ j(s_ j)$ on $B_ j$ and extend it by zero. This extension is smooth, and

\[ \omega _{Q_ j}=\phi _ j'(s_ j)\omega _{s_ j}+\bigl (1-\phi _ j'(s_ j)\bigr )\omega +\phi _ j''(s_ j)\, \mathrm{d} s_ j\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}s_ j\geq \omega /2. \]

On the smaller ball, $Q_ j=s_ j$ and $\omega _{Q_ j}\geq \tau \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ j$. If $N$ is the number of balls, put $q=N^{-1}\sum _ jQ_ j$. Then $\omega _ q=N^{-1}\sum _ j\omega _{Q_ j}\geq \omega /2$ everywhere. Each point belongs to at least one smaller ball $\{ |z_ j|<r_ j\} $, so positivity of the other summands and (14.24.15) give

\[ \omega _ q^ n\geq (\tau /N)^ n(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi _ j)^ n=(\tau /N)^ n g \omega ^ n. \]

Thus (14.24.14) holds with $\varepsilon =(\tau /N)^ n$, independent of $\sup g$ and of all derivatives of $g$. $\square$

Proposition 14.24.2 asserts that $\mu (\{ w<a+\eta \} \cap \{ F>\kappa G\} )>0$. By the preceding inclusion, however, this set is empty. This contradiction shows that $w$ is constant. If $\sup _ Xu=\sup _ Xv$, the constant is zero. $\square$

For a bounded measurable density, a positive lower bound gives the same conclusion. In this case coercivity holds on all of $X$, and the concentrating test potential can dominate the essential upper bound of the density on a whole neighborhood of the extremal set.

Corollary 14.24.17.527FB17149BB If $0<c_0\leq F\leq C_0<\infty $ almost everywhere and bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions satisfy $\omega _ u^ n=\omega _ v^ n=F\omega ^ n$, then $u-v$ is constant.

Proof. The functions $u,v$ are continuous by corollary 14.19.29, and in dimension one the assertion follows from $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-v)=0$ and the maximum principle. Let $n\geq 2$, suppose that $w=u-v$ is nonconstant, and put $K=\{ w=\min _ Xw\} $. The proof of lemma 14.24.11 applies with $U=X$, since proposition 14.24.5 gives the pushforward identity and local coercivity on every chart. Thus $K$ has zero volume.

Fix $B_0>\| 1\| _{L^2(\omega ^ n)}$ and the corresponding $\varepsilon $ in proposition 14.24.13. Choose $A>1$ with $\varepsilon A>C_0$, and a smooth cutoff function $\chi $, equal to one on a neighborhood $W$ of $K$ and supported in an open set of volume so small that $g=1+(A-1)\chi $ satisfies $\| g\| _2<B_0$. The resulting test potential $q$ has density $G\geq \varepsilon g$. Choose $\kappa <1$ with $C_0<\kappa \varepsilon A$. Then $F<\kappa G$ almost everywhere on $W$. By continuity of $w$, a sufficiently thin set $\{ w<\min _ Xw+\eta \} $ lies inside $W$, which contradicts proposition 14.24.2. Hence $w$ is constant. $\square$
