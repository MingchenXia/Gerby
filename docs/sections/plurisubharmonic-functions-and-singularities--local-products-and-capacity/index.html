5.27 Local products and capacity5E507B6993DC

5E507B6993DC

Bedford–Taylor products of bounded plurisubharmonic functions, and the capacity defined through them, measure the exceptional sets of plurisubharmonic functions and control bounded approximation. We recall these classical facts of local pluripotential theory from 7.2 Bounded potentials and recursive products. The plurifine topology is the coarsest topology that makes all local plurisubharmonic functions continuous. It is finer than the ordinary topology; in particular, strict superlevel sets of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions are plurifine open.

Theorem 5.27.1 (Bedford–Taylor).8B1218AE4FDC Let $1\leq k\leq n$. If $p_1,\ldots ,p_ k$ are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\mathbb C^ n$, their mixed product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p_ k$ is a positive closed current of order zero. It is symmetric, multilinear in its current factors, and continuous under simultaneous locally bounded decreasing approximation. It does not charge pluripolar sets and is local in the plurifine topology.

Here plurifine locality means that if two families of such functions agree on a plurifine open Borel set, then their products have the same restriction to that set.

Products multiplied by a bounded potential need more than the weak convergence of the unweighted products in theorem 5.27.1. An auxiliary complex variable recovers the weight from an ordinary mixed product in one more dimension, including at top degree.

Lemma 5.27.2.2A60C2B3B4C3 Let $a_ j\downarrow a$ and $b_{i,j}\downarrow b_ i$, $1\leq i\leq r\leq n$, be locally uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions on a domain in $\mathbb C^ n$. Then

\[ a_ j\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_{1,j}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_{r,j} \longrightarrow a\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ r \]

weakly as currents. The same holds for quasi-plurisubharmonic functions that become plurisubharmonic after fixed smooth corrections, and after wedging with smooth forms that converge locally in $C^\infty $.

Proof. First let $r=n$, and write $\mu _ j=\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_{i,j}$ and $\mu =\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ i$. Work over a relatively compact ball on which $-M\leq a_ j,a\leq M$. On the product of this ball with a disk of radius $R$ in an auxiliary variable $w$, where $\log R^2>M+1$, set

\[ v_ j(z,w)=\max \{ a_ j(z),\log |w|^2\} ,\qquad v(z,w)=\max \{ a(z),\log |w|^2\} . \]

These are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions, also at $w=0$, and $v_ j\downarrow v$. Pulling back the functions $b_{i,j}$ and $b_ i$ to the product and applying theorem 5.27.1 in dimension $n+1$, we obtain

\[ T_ j=\mu _ j\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j\longrightarrow T=\mu \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v. \]

Since $\mu $ is closed, $T=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v\mu )$. As $\mu $ has top degree in the base variables, only derivatives in $w$ contribute. Testing against product test functions and applying Fubini's theorem therefore identifies $T$ with $\mu (\mathrm dz)$ times the circle measure

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}_ w\max \{ a(z),\log |w|^2\} =\sigma _{\exp (a(z)/2)}. \]

Here $\sigma _ t$ is the uniform probability measure on the circle $|w|=t$: the radial derivative jumps from zero to $2/t$ there, so the Euclidean Laplacian has total mass $4\pi $, and our normalization of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ divides that mass by $4\pi $. This distributional computation is valid for every bounded Borel function $a$; no continuity in $z$ is needed. The same description holds for $T_ j$.

Choose a smooth radial compactly supported function $\vartheta (w)$, supported away from zero and from the circle $|w|=R$, that equals $\log |w|^2$ on all circles with $-M\leq \log |w|^2\leq M$. For every smooth compactly supported test function $\xi $ on the base,

\[ \langle T_ j,\xi \vartheta \rangle =\int \xi a_ j\, \mathrm d\mu _ j, \qquad \langle T,\xi \vartheta \rangle =\int \xi a\, \mathrm d\mu . \]

The convergence $T_ j\to T$ therefore proves the top-degree assertion.

For $r<n$, wedge with $n-r$ constant positive $(1,1)$-forms, each the Hessian of a positive quadratic function, and apply the top-degree case. By polarization, the products of positive constant $(1,1)$-forms span the real constant forms of bidegree $(n-r,n-r)$, and multiplying them by smooth test functions recovers every coefficient of the currents. For quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, add the fixed smooth corrections to the decreasing sequences, apply the result to the corrected functions, and expand; the term involving the correction to $a_ j$ converges by the ordinary decreasing continuity of the products. Finally, the local Chern–Levine–Nirenberg bounds show that these finitely many products have uniformly bounded variation on compact sets. This allows smooth forms converging locally in $C^\infty $ to replace fixed forms in the tests. $\square$

For concentric coordinate balls $B'\Subset B$ and a Borel set $E\subset B'$, the relative Bedford–Taylor capacity is

\[ \operatorname {cap}(E,B)=\sup \Bigl \{ \int _ E(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n:v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B),\ 0\leq v\leq 1\Bigr \} . \]

If $v$ is bounded, nonconstant and plurisubharmonic on $B$, applying the definition to $(v-\inf _ Bv)/(\sup _ Bv-\inf _ Bv)$ gives

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n(E)\leq (\sup _ Bv-\inf _ Bv)^ n\operatorname {cap}(E,B); \]

for constant $v$ both sides vanish. Write $\operatorname {Vol}(E)=\int _ E\mathrm{d}V$, where $\mathrm{d}V$ is a fixed smooth positive volume form. We use two standard local estimates on these balls, with constants $C,a>0$ depending only on the nested balls and on $\mathrm{d}V$:

  1. If $w\leq 0$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$ and $v$ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $B$, then

    \[ \int _{B'}|w|(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n \leq C\| w\| _{L^1(B)}\| v\| _{L^\infty (B)}^ n. \]

    Since adding a constant to $v$ does not change $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$, one may first normalize $v$ by such a constant. This is the weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality.

  2. For every Borel set $E\subset B'$,

    \[ \operatorname {Vol}(E)\leq C\exp \bigl (-a\operatorname {cap}(E,B)^{-1/n}\bigr ), \]

    where the right-hand side is zero when the capacity is zero. This is the classical local volume–capacity estimate.

Both are classical results of local pluripotential theory 7.2 Bounded potentials and recursive products; the conventions for bounded products and capacity in agree with these up to fixed constants.

The dependence on the range of the potential can be read off from the usual cutoff argument. Let $M>0$ and let $p$ be plurisubharmonic on $B$ with $0\leq p\leq M$. Let $\kappa =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ be the Euclidean form, and choose a cutoff function $\chi $ supported in $B$, with $0\leq \chi \leq 1$, equal to one on a smaller ball and satisfying $-C\kappa \leq \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \leq C\kappa $. Integration by parts gives, for $1\leq j\leq n$,

\[ \begin{split} \int \chi (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} & =\int p\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^{j-1}\wedge \kappa ^{n-j}\\ & \leq CM\int _{\operatorname {supp}\chi }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^{j-1}\wedge \kappa ^{n-j+1}. \end{split} \]

Iterating this estimate with nested cutoffs between $B'$ and $B$, and stopping at degree one or at degree zero, we obtain respectively

F138661713A9 5.27.3
\begin{equation} \begin{split} \int _{B'}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} & \leq C_ jM^{j-1}\int _ B\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p\wedge \kappa ^{n-1},\\ \int _{B'}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}p)^ j\wedge \kappa ^{n-j} & \leq C_ jM^ j. \end{split} \end{equation}

The constants depend only on the nested balls and on $j$. For bounded $p$, the integrations by parts are justified by local decreasing regularization.

Definition 5.27.4.9D64513AE343 For a compact set $E$ in an open Euclidean ball $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$, the relative extremal function is

\[ h_{E,B}=\sup \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(B):-1\leq u\leq 0,\ u|_ E\leq -1\} . \]

The constant $-1$ is a competitor, and the family is bounded above by zero. The local upper-envelope theorem therefore gives $h_{E,B}^*\in \mathrm{PSH}(B)$ with $-1\leq h_{E,B}^*\leq 0$. Allowing all competitors $u\leq 0$ with $u|_ E\leq -1$ gives the same supremum, because the truncation $\max (u,-1)$ of such a competitor is a competitor in the original family.

A sequence of functions $f_ j$ converges to $f$ in local capacity if $\operatorname {cap}(K\cap \{ |f_ j-f|>\epsilon \} ,B)\to 0$ for every $\epsilon >0$, every coordinate ball $B$ and every compact set $K\subset B$. An assertion holds quasi-everywhere, abbreviated q.e., if it holds outside a pluripolar set; in particular, differences of functions that may both be $-\infty $ are considered only off such a set. A positive measure is non-pluripolar if it gives zero mass to every pluripolar set.

A function is quasi-continuous if, for every $\epsilon >0$, it is continuous outside an open set of capacity less than $\epsilon $. Plurisubharmonic functions have this property for the local Bedford–Taylor capacity, and a Borel set has zero capacity if and only if it is pluripolar. A locally uniformly bounded monotone sequence of plurisubharmonic functions converges in capacity: to its limit if it decreases, and to the upper semicontinuous regularization of its limit if it increases. If uniformly bounded plurisubharmonic functions converge in capacity, their local products converge weakly, and this remains true after the products are multiplied by uniformly bounded quasi-continuous coefficients converging in capacity. These local results, recalled in 7.5 Quasi-continuity and convergence in capacity, also apply to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions after a common smooth correction.

Theorem 5.27.5 (Mixed Monge–Ampère inequality).DBC85A3C1608 Let $U\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a domain, and let $\beta $ be its Euclidean Hermitian form. Let $a,b$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $U$, and let $f,g$ be nonnegative measurable functions on $U$ with

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a)^ n\geq f\beta ^ n, \qquad (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^ n\geq g\beta ^ n. \]

Then, for $0\leq k\leq n$,

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a)^ k\wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^{n-k} \geq f^{k/n}g^{(n-k)/n}\beta ^ n. \]

The comparison principle of Bedford and Taylor also has mixed forms, recalled in 7.14 Gradients against positive currents. Let $a,b$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $U$ such that $E=\{ a<b\} \Subset U$, and let $S$ be a positive closed $(n-1,n-1)$-current that is a product of Hessians of bounded plurisubharmonic functions and smooth closed positive forms. Then

5E980A5776A2 5.27.6
\begin{equation} \int _ E\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\wedge S\leq \int _ E\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a\wedge S. \end{equation}

Indeed, apply the Bedford–Taylor integration by parts formula to $b_\epsilon =\max \{ a,b-\epsilon \} $, and let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, using monotone continuity to pass to the limit. Replacing one factor at a time, we obtain, with $P=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ and $Q=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b$,

A9D318047BDA 5.27.7
\begin{equation} \int _ E P^{n-j}\wedge Q^ j\leq \int _ E P^ n, \qquad 0\leq j\leq n. \end{equation}

The local envelope theorem for bounded obstacles, recalled in 7.7 The homogeneous Dirichlet problem and balayage, shows where an envelope can carry Monge–Ampère mass. Let $B$ be a ball with a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic defining function $h<0$, and let $f$ be a bounded quasi-continuous obstacle with $Ah\leq f\leq 0$ for some $A>0$. Then the envelope

\[ q=\bigl (\sup \{ v\in \mathrm{PSH}(B):v\leq f\ \text{q.e.}\} \bigr )^* \]

satisfies $Ah\leq q\leq 0$, tends to zero at the boundary, and

F547D6DF0352 5.27.8
\begin{equation} \mathbf1_{\{ q<f\} }(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)^ n=0. \end{equation}

For a locally bounded quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$ and a smooth convex increasing function $F$ on a neighborhood of the range of $v$, the bounded Bedford–Taylor chain rule reads

B4EA93FC61EE 5.27.9
\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}F(v)=F'(v)\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v+F''(v)\, \mathrm{d}v\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}v. \end{equation}

The gradient term, and its products with positive Bedford–Taylor factors of bounded potentials, are nonnegative. These identities belong to the bounded local calculus recalled in 7.14 Gradients against positive currents; they follow by decreasing regularization, and bounded potentials have the local Sobolev regularity needed to define the gradient term. In particular, inequalities derived from this formula survive local smoothing with a lower curvature error tending to zero.

Lemma 5.27.10.9149838224AE Let $B$ be a coordinate ball and let $\tau $ be a finite non-pluripolar measure with compact support in $B$. Then there is a bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ such that $\tau \ll (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$.

Proof. For $n\geq 2$, we use Cegrell's decomposition theorem. On a bounded hyperconvex domain in $\mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 2$, let $\mathcal E_0$ consist of the bounded negative plurisubharmonic functions with zero boundary values and finite total Monge–Ampère mass. The theorem states that every positive compactly supported measure $\tau $ has a decomposition

\[ \tau =g(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n+\tau _ s, \qquad v\in \mathcal E_0,\qquad 0\leq g\in L^1((\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n), \]

where $\tau _ s$ is a positive measure carried by a pluripolar set. If $\tau $ is non-pluripolar, then $\tau _ s=0$, so $\tau \ll (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$.

In dimension one, the assertion follows directly from the Green potential. Let $a\leq 0$ be the Green potential of $\tau $ on the disk, normalized by $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a=\tau $. Then $v=\mathrm{e}^ a$ is bounded and subharmonic, and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq \mathrm{e}^ a\tau . \]

To justify this inequality, set $a_ M=\max (a,-M)$. The chain rule (5.27.9) in dimension one gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{e}^{a_ M}\geq \mathrm{e}^{a_ M}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ M$, and the right-hand side is at least $\mathrm{e}^ a\tau $ on $\{ a>-M\} $. Let $M\to \infty $, using the decreasing convergence of the bounded subharmonic functions $\mathrm{e}^{a_ M}$ on the left and monotone convergence on the right. Since $\{ a=-\infty \} $ is polar and $\tau $ charges no polar set, $\mathrm{e}^ a>0$ almost everywhere for $\tau $. Thus $\tau \ll \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v$. $\square$

For a signed measure $\nu $, write $|\nu |$ for its total variation measure and $\| \nu \| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}=|\nu |(B)$. On a curve, the Green representation controls capacity by the total variation of the Laplacian, together with an $L^1$ term for the harmonic remainder.

Lemma 5.27.11.C56C5CBA0E0D Let $B'\Subset B$ be concentric disks in $\mathbb C$. There is a constant $C$, depending only on these disks, such that for all quasi-subharmonic functions $u,v$ on a neighborhood of $\overline B$ and all $t>0$,

EBF435BDB6A0 5.27.12
\begin{equation} \operatorname {cap}(B'\cap \{ |u-v|>t\} ,B) \leq \frac Ct\left(\| u-v\| _{L^1(B)} +\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u-v)\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right). \end{equation}

Here the difference $u-v$ is formed off the pole sets of $u$ and $v$.

Proof. Put $f=u-v$ and write the Jordan decomposition on $B$ as $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f=\alpha ^+-\alpha ^-$. Let $g^\pm \leq 0$ be the Green potentials of $\alpha ^\pm $ on $B$ with zero boundary values. The $L^1(B)$ norm of the Green kernel of the disk is bounded uniformly in its pole, so

\[ \| g^+\| _{L^1(B)}+\| g^-\| _{L^1(B)} \leq C\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}. \]

Indeed, on the unit disk the kernel is $\log |(z-\zeta )/(1-\overline\zeta z)|^2$; its logarithmic singularity is uniformly integrable, and $|1-\overline\zeta z|\leq 2$. Scaling gives the bound on $B$.

The distribution $h=f-g^++g^-$ is harmonic, and the interior mean-value estimate gives

\[ \| h\| _{L^\infty (B')} \leq C\left(\| f\| _{L^1(B)}+\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right). \]

The identity $u+g^-=v+g^++h$ holds almost everywhere, hence everywhere by lemma 5.24.3. Thus $|u-v|\leq -g^+-g^-+|h|$ off the polar sets of $u$ and $v$. Let $0\leq a\leq 1$ be a subharmonic test function on $B$. Since the measure $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a$ charges no polar set, this pointwise bound, the weighted Chern–Levine–Nirenberg estimate and (5.27.3) yield

\[ \int _{B'}|u-v|\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a \leq C\left(\| f\| _{L^1(B)}+\| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\| _{\mathrm{TV}(B)}\right). \]

Restricting the integral to $B'\cap \{ |u-v|>t\} $, dividing by $t$ and taking the supremum over $a$ proves (5.27.12). $\square$

5.27.13 Polar parts on curves1953F5546AC6

Every locally finite signed measure $\nu $ on a Riemann surface splits uniquely as the sum of its polar part $\nu ^{\mathrm{pol}}$, which is carried by a polar Borel set, and a measure that charges no polar set. To construct the splitting locally, choose countably many polar Borel sets whose $|\nu |$-masses approach the supremum over all polar Borel sets, and restrict $\nu $ to their union, which is again polar. Uniqueness allows these local decompositions to be patched together and shows that $\nu \mapsto \nu ^{\mathrm{pol}}$ is linear.

For a locally quasi-subharmonic function $u$, the polar curvature of $u$ is the positive measure $\mu _ u=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{\mathrm{pol}}=\mathbf1_{\{ u=-\infty \} }\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$. The second equality holds because smooth corrections do not change polar parts, while bounded truncations and plurifine locality show that $\mathbf1_{\{ u>-j\} }\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ charges no polar set. The following inverse maximum principle shows that an upper bound for the difference of two potentials orders their polar curvatures.

Lemma 5.27.14.B54F79E95767 Let $B\subset \mathbb C$ be a disk and let $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)$ have a distributional Laplacian that is a signed Radon measure. If $f\leq 0$ almost everywhere, then $(\Delta f)^{\mathrm{pol}}\geq 0$.

Proof. It suffices to prove $\Delta f(K)\geq 0$ for every compact polar set $K\subset B$; restriction to smaller disks and inner regularity then give the assertion. Choose open neighborhoods $O_ j$ of $K$ with smooth boundaries such that $\overline O_{j+1}\subset O_ j\Subset B$ and $\bigcap _ j\overline O_ j=K$. Let $h_ j$ be the function that equals one on $\overline O_ j$, is harmonic on $B\setminus \overline O_ j$, and vanishes on $\partial B$. The Dirichlet problem on this smooth domain shows that $h_ j$ is continuous with $0\leq h_ j\leq 1$. Its outward normal derivative on $\partial O_ j$ is nonpositive, so the jump of the derivative across $\partial O_ j$ gives $\Delta h_ j\leq 0$ on $B$ in the sense of distributions. The maximum principle gives $h_{j+1}\leq h_ j$.

Since $0\leq h_ j\leq 1$, the sequence converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)$. Let $h$ be the superharmonic representative of this limit; it may differ from the pointwise limit on $K$. It is harmonic off $K$, so its Laplacian is supported on the polar set $K$. On the other hand, $-h$ is bounded and subharmonic, so its Laplacian charges no polar set, by theorem 5.27.1. Hence $h$ is harmonic on $B$. Since $0\leq h\leq h_1$ and $h_1$ tends to zero at $\partial B$, the maximum principle gives $h=0$. It follows that $h_ j\to 0$ smoothly on compact subsets of $B\setminus K$, while $h_ j=1$ on $K$.

Choose $0\leq \chi \in C_ c^\infty (B)$ equal to one near $K$. For large $j$, regularize $h_ j$ by convolution on a neighborhood of $\operatorname {supp}\chi $, obtaining smooth functions $h_{j,\epsilon }$ with $\Delta h_{j,\epsilon }\leq 0$. Since $f\leq 0$,

\[ \begin{split} \int _ B\chi h_{j,\epsilon }\, \mathrm{d}(\Delta f) & =\int _ B f\, \Delta (\chi h_{j,\epsilon })\, \mathrm{d}x\, \mathrm{d}y\\ & \geq \int _ B f\bigl (2\nabla \chi \cdot \nabla h_{j,\epsilon } +h_{j,\epsilon }\Delta \chi \bigr )\, \mathrm{d}x\, \mathrm{d}y. \end{split} \]

For fixed $j$, the convolutions converge uniformly to $h_ j$ on $\operatorname {supp}\chi $, because $h_ j$ is continuous. On the support of the derivatives of $\chi $, the function $h_ j$ is harmonic for all large $j$. We may therefore first let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ and then let $j\to \infty $. The smooth convergence away from $K$ makes the right-hand side tend to zero, and dominated convergence for the signed measure $\Delta f$ makes the left-hand side tend to $\Delta f(K)$. This proves $\Delta f(K)\geq 0$. $\square$

Corollary 5.27.15.F9517F96057F Let $u,v$ be locally quasi-subharmonic functions on a Riemann surface. If $u\leq v+C$ on an open set for some constant $C$, then $\mu _ u\geq \mu _ v$ there. Moreover,

6FC7AD4CB464 5.27.16
\begin{equation} \mu _{\max (u,v)}=\mu _ u\wedge \mu _ v, \end{equation}

where the right-hand side is the greatest measure dominated by both $\mu _ u$ and $\mu _ v$.

Proof. Apply lemma 5.27.14 to $u-v-C$ in local coordinates. Since smooth functions have zero polar part, linearity of the polar part gives the first assertion. Applying it to $u-\max (u,v)$ and to $v-\max (u,v)$ gives the inequality $\leq $ in (5.27.16).

For the reverse inequality, add a common smooth correction to make $u$ and $v$ subharmonic on a coordinate disk. On a smaller disk, let $g$ be the Green potential of the restriction of $\lambda =\mu _ u\wedge \mu _ v$ to that disk, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g=\lambda $. The distributions $u-g$ and $v-g$ have positive Hessians and hence subharmonic representatives $a$ and $b$. Since $\max (u,v)=g+\max (a,b)$ almost everywhere, the Hessian of $\max (u,v)$ dominates $\lambda $. Restricting this inequality to a polar Borel set carrying $\lambda $ proves the reverse inequality on the smaller disk, and these local statements give it on the whole surface. $\square$

Dividing by a fixed positive threshold and taking the supremum over $w$ gives convergence in local capacity. Each $u_ j$ is bounded and lies below $b$, and it is smooth when $b$ is smooth. One sequence serves all finite $p$, and no common local mass bound on the $\nu _ j$ is needed.

The error in the first $j$ coordinates and the tail of the series yield

\[ d_ v\bigl ((\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j)^ n,\nu _ j\bigr )\leq 2/j+2^{-j}\longrightarrow 0. \]

By the remark following lemma 5.27.38, the two sequences of measures have the same limits along corresponding subsequences. Hence their full vague cluster sets coincide. $\square$

For example, on the unit ball the function $b=|z|^2-1$ has nonzero Monge–Ampère measure. For every point $a$ of the ball, the theorem allows the cluster sets $\{ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^ n\} $, $\{ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b)^ n+\delta _ a\} $ and the empty set, but it excludes $\{ 0\} $; this exclusion relies on the convergence in local capacity. In dimension one, $L^1$ convergence alone forces $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\to \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b$ vaguely, so the full cluster set is always $\{ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b\} $.
