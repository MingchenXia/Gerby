5.5 Holomorphic zeros and the Poincaré–Lelong formula0C34F7796300

0C34F7796300

Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on a domain in $\mathbb C^ n$, not identically zero. Away from the zero set of $f$, the function $\log |f|^2$ is pluriharmonic, so the current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2$ is supported on the zero set. We show that it is the current of integration over the zero set, each smooth sheet counted with the vanishing order of $f$ along it. The main difficulty is the singular part of the zero set, where several sheets may meet. Using the Weierstrass preparation theorem in several directions, we show that in suitable linear coordinates this part lies over a Lebesgue null set in every coordinate projection, which is enough to see that it carries no mass.

Lemma 5.5.1.AD4CF8180187 Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on a domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\ge 1$, that is not identically zero. Then the zero set of $f$ has Lebesgue measure zero. Let $P\subset \Omega $ be a polydisc, let $1\leq j\leq n$, and write $P=P'\times D$ and points of $P$ as $(z',z_ j)$, where $D\subset \mathbb C$ is a disc in the variable $z_ j$ and $z'$ consists of the other coordinates. Then the set of points $z'\in P'$ such that $z_ j\mapsto f(z',z_ j)$ vanishes identically on $D$ is a Lebesgue null subset of $\mathbb C^{n-1}$; for $n=1$ it is empty. Moreover, for every zero $a$ of $f$ there is a complex linear coordinate system centered at $a$ such that $f$ does not vanish identically on any of the coordinate axes through $a$.

Proof. We prove the first two assertions together by induction on $n$. For $n=1$, every zero of $f$ is isolated: at a zero $a$, the Taylor expansion has a first nonzero term $c(z-a)^ m$, since otherwise $f$ would vanish near $a$ and hence on $\Omega $ by the identity theorem; so $f(z)=(z-a)^ mg(z)$ with $g(a)\neq 0$. Hence the zero set meets every compact subset of $\Omega $ in a finite set, and since $\Omega $ is a countable union of compact sets, it is countable, hence null. Moreover, $f$ does not vanish identically on a disc $D\subset \Omega $, by the identity theorem, which proves the second assertion for $n=1$.

Let $n\geq 2$, and let $P=P'\times D$ be as in the statement; after permuting the coordinates, which preserves Lebesgue measure, we may assume that $j=n$, and we write $w=z_ n$ and $w_0$ for the center of $D$. The functions $c_ k(z')=\partial _ w^ kf(z',w_0)/k!$ are holomorphic on $P'$, and, by the one-variable Taylor expansion of $w\mapsto f(z',w)$ on the disc $D$ centered at $w_0$, this function vanishes identically on $D$ if and only if all $c_ k(z')$ vanish. Since $f$ does not vanish identically on the nonempty open set $P$, by the identity theorem, some $c_ k$ is not identically zero on the polydisc $P'$, and its zero set $N\subset P'$ is null by the first assertion in dimension $n-1$. This proves the second assertion in dimension $n$, since the set in question is contained in $N$. For $z'\notin N$, the zero set of $w\mapsto f(z',w)$ in $D$ is null by the case $n=1$. By Tonelli's theorem, applied to the indicator function of the relatively closed zero set of $f$ in $P$, this zero set is null; and countably many such polydiscs, for instance those with rational centers and radii contained in $\Omega $, cover $\Omega $.

For the last assertion, translate $a$ to the origin and let $P_ m$ be the first nonzero term of the homogeneous expansion of $f$ at $0$; it exists because $f$ does not vanish identically near $0$, and $m\geq 1$ because $f(0)=0$. The set $\{ P_ m\neq 0\} $ is open and dense in $\mathbb C^ n$, because a polynomial vanishing on a nonempty open set is zero: its derivatives at a point $b$ of that set vanish, and the polynomial is the sum of its finite Taylor expansion at $b$. Choose $v_1\in \{ P_ m\neq 0\} $, and inductively $v_ j\in \{ P_ m\neq 0\} $ outside the linear span of $v_1,\ldots ,v_{j-1}$; this is possible because the complement of a proper linear subspace is open and dense, and the intersection of two dense open sets is dense. Then $v_1,\ldots ,v_ n$ is a basis, and $f(tv_ j)=t^ mP_ m(v_ j)+O(t^{m+1})$ by (5.2.2), so $t\mapsto f(tv_ j)$ does not vanish identically near $t=0$. Taking $v_1,\ldots ,v_ n$ as coordinate axes proves the assertion. $\square$

Theorem 5.5.2 (Weierstrass preparation).F54A645FF399 Let $f$ be holomorphic on a neighborhood $U$ of $0$ in $\mathbb {C}^{n-1}\times \mathbb {C}$, $n\geq 1$, and suppose that $w\mapsto f(0,w)$ has a zero of order $m\ge 1$ at $w=0$. Then there are polydiscs $P'\times \{ |w|<\rho \} \subset U$ centered at $0$ with arbitrarily small radii, and numbers $\rho _1\in (0,\rho )$, such that on $P'\times \{ |w|<\rho \} $

\[ f(z',w)=g(z',w)W(z',w),\qquad W=w^ m+a_1(z')w^{m-1}+\cdots +a_ m(z'), \]

where $g$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero, the coefficients $a_ j$ are holomorphic on $P'$ with $a_ j(0)=0$, and for every $z'\in P'$ the $m$ roots of $W(z',\cdot )$ lie in $\{ |w|<\rho _1\} $.

Proof. Since the zeros of $f(0,\cdot )$ are isolated, there is $\rho _0>0$ such that $\{ 0\} \times \{ |w|\leq \rho _0\} \subset U$ and $f(0,w)\neq 0$ for $0<|w|\leq \rho _0$. Let $0<\rho <\rho _0$, and choose $\rho _1<\rho <\rho _2\leq \rho _0$. Then $f(0,w)\neq 0$ on the compact annulus $\{ \rho _1\leq |w|\leq \rho _2\} $. Since $|f(0,\cdot )|$ has a positive minimum on this annulus and $f$ is uniformly continuous on a compact neighborhood of $\{ 0\} \times \{ \rho _1\leq |w|\leq \rho _2\} $ in $U$, there is a polydisc $P'$ centered at $0$, as small as we wish, such that $P'\times \{ |w|\leq \rho _2\} \subset U$ and $f(z',w)\neq 0$ for $z'\in P'$ and $\rho _1\leq |w|\leq \rho _2$. By the argument principle, the number of zeros of $f(z',\cdot )$ in $\{ |w|<\rho \} $, counted with multiplicity, is

\[ \frac{1}{2\pi \mathrm{i}}\int _{|w|=\rho }\frac{f_ w(z',w)}{f(z',w)}\, \mathrm{d}w, \]

a continuous integer-valued function of $z'$ on the connected set $P'$; hence it equals its value $m$ at $z'=0$. Denote these zeros by $\lambda _1(z'),\ldots ,\lambda _ m(z')$, in any order; they lie in $\{ |w|<\rho _1\} $.

By the residue theorem, their power sums are

\[ p_ k(z')=\sum _{l=1}^ m\lambda _ l(z')^ k=\frac{1}{2\pi \mathrm{i}}\int _{|w|=\rho } w^ k\frac{f_ w(z',w)}{f(z',w)}\, \mathrm{d}w,\qquad 1\leq k\leq m, \]

which are holomorphic functions on $P'$, by differentiation under the integral sign. The elementary symmetric functions $e_ k$ of the roots satisfy Newton's identities

\[ ke_ k=\sum _{j=1}^ k(-1)^{j-1}e_{k-j}p_ j,\qquad e_0=1. \]

To prove them, put $E(t)=\prod _ l(1+t\lambda _ l)=\sum _ ke_ kt^ k$ and compare the coefficients of $t^{k-1}$ in $E'(t)=E(t)\sum _{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j-1}p_ jt^{j-1}$, which is the expansion of the logarithmic derivative $E'/E=\sum _ l\lambda _ l/(1+t\lambda _ l)$. By induction on $k$, every $e_ k$ is holomorphic on $P'$. Hence $W(z',w)=\prod _ l(w-\lambda _ l(z'))$ is a monic polynomial in $w$ with holomorphic coefficients $a_ k=(-1)^ ke_ k$, and $a_ k(0)=0$ because all $\lambda _ l(0)=0$.

It remains to show that $f/W$ extends to a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function on $P'\times \{ |w|<\rho \} $. For fixed $z'\in P'$, the functions $f(z',\cdot )$ and $W(z',\cdot )$ have the same zeros, with the same multiplicities, in $\{ |w|<\rho _2\} $; so $f/W$ and $W/f$ extend to holomorphic functions of $w$ there. On the annulus $\{ \rho _1<|w|<\rho _2\} $ both quotients are holomorphic in $(z',w)$. The Cauchy integral

\[ G(z',w)=\frac{1}{2\pi \mathrm i}\int _{|\zeta |=\rho }\frac{(f/W)(z',\zeta )}{\zeta -w}\, \mathrm d\zeta ,\qquad |w|<\rho , \]

is holomorphic in $(z',w)$, by differentiation under the integral sign, and equals $f/W$ by Cauchy's formula in $w$. Defining $\widetilde G$ in the same way from $W/f$, we obtain $G\widetilde G=1$. Thus $g=G$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero, and $f=gW$. $\square$

Proposition 5.5.3.37EEAF275A32 Let $f$ be holomorphic on a domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, and not identically zero, and put $\log |f|^2=-\infty $ on the zero set of $f$. Then $\log |f|^2$ is locally integrable, it is the canonical subharmonic representative of its class, and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2\geq 0$. Moreover, let $P\subset \Omega $ be a polydisc, let $1\leq j\leq n$, and write points of $P$ as $(z',z_ j)$, where $z'$ consists of the coordinates other than $z_ j$. Then for every test function $\varphi $ on $P$,

FF48D92EEC58 5.5.4
\begin{equation} \big \langle (\log |f|^2)_{j\bar j},\varphi \big \rangle =\pi \int _{\mathbb {C}^{n-1}}\sum _{a:\, f(z',a)=0} m(z',a)\varphi (z',a)\, \mathrm{d}V_{2n-2}(z'), \end{equation}

where the sum runs over the zeros $a$ of $z_ j\mapsto f(z',z_ j)$ in the fiber of $P$ over $z'$, and $m(z',a)$ is the order of that zero; the integrand is defined outside the null set of those $z'$ over which $f$ vanishes identically on the fiber. In particular, if $\pi _ j$ is the projection of $P$ omitting $z_ j$, the positive measure $(\log |f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ gives zero mass to $\pi _ j^{-1}(N)$ for every Lebesgue null set $N$.

Proof. Local integrability. Near a point where $f\neq 0$, the function $\log |f|^2$ is smooth. Let $a$ be a zero of $f$. After a translation and a complex-linear change of coordinates, which preserve local integrability, we may assume that $a=0$ and that $f(0,w)$ does not vanish identically in the last variable $w$ (lemma 5.5.1). Then $f(0,\cdot )$ has a zero of some order $m\geq 1$ at $0$, and by theorem 5.5.2, $f=gW$ on a polydisc $P'\times \{ |w|<\rho \} $, where $g$ is a holomorphic unit and $W(z',w)=\prod _{l=1}^ m(w-\lambda _ l(z'))$ with all roots in $\{ |w|<\rho _1\} $ for some $\rho _1<\rho $. Choose $r\in (\rho _1,\rho )$ and replace $P'$ by a smaller concentric polydisc with compact closure in $P'$; since $g$ is continuous and nowhere zero, $\log |g|^2$ is then bounded on $P'\times \{ |w|<r\} $. For $|\lambda |\leq \rho _1$ we have

\[ \int _{|w|<r}\big |\log |w-\lambda |\big |\, \mathrm{d}V_2(w)\le C, \]

with $C$ independent of $\lambda $: the positive part of $\log |w-\lambda |$ is at most $\max \{ \log (r+\rho _1),0\} $, and after the translation $\zeta =w-\lambda $ the integral of its negative part is at most $\int _{|\zeta |<1}(-\log |\zeta |)\, \mathrm dV_2(\zeta )$. Since $|\log |W(z',w)||\leq \sum _ l|\log |w-\lambda _ l(z')||$, it follows that $\int _{|w|<r}|\log |W(z',w)||\, \mathrm dV_2(w)\leq mC$ for every $z'\in P'$. (The estimate holds for each fiber separately, so we need not choose the roots as measurable functions of $z'$.) By Tonelli's theorem $\log |W|^2$, and hence $\log |f|^2$, is integrable on $P'\times \{ |w|<r\} $, a neighborhood of $a=0$.

The slice formula. For $n=1$, example 3.4.15 gives $\Delta \log |f|^2=4\pi \sum _ am(a)\delta _ a$ locally, that is, $(\log |f|^2)_{z\bar z}=\pi \sum _ am(a)\delta _ a$; this is (5.5.4) with $\mathbb C^0$ a point. In general, write $P=P'\times D$ with $D$ a disc in the variable $z_ j$. By lemma 5.5.1, the set $N_0$ of points $z'\in P'$ over which $f$ vanishes identically on $D$ is null. By Fubini's theorem,

\begin{align*} \big \langle (\log |f|^2)_{j\bar j},\varphi \big \rangle & =\int _ P\log |f|^2\, \partial _{z_ j}\partial _{\bar z_ j}\varphi \, \mathrm dV_{2n}\\ & =\int _{P'}\Big(\int _ D\log |f(z',w)|^2\, \partial _ w\partial _{\bar w}\varphi (z',w)\, \mathrm dV_2(w)\Big)\mathrm dV_{2n-2}(z'), \end{align*}

the inner integral being an integrable function of $z'$. For $z'\notin N_0$, the one-variable case applied to $f(z',\cdot )$ on $D$ shows that the inner integral equals $\pi \sum _ am(z',a)\varphi (z',a)$. This proves (5.5.4); the integrand on the right is measurable and integrable, being equal almost everywhere to the inner integral.

Positivity. By (5.5.4), $(\log |f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ is nonnegative on nonnegative test functions supported in polydiscs contained in $\Omega $, hence on all nonnegative test functions, which are finite sums of such functions by lemma 2.5.2. This holds in every system of complex linear coordinates, since $f$ remains holomorphic after a linear change of coordinates. Applying this after a unitary change of coordinates, as in the matrix criterion of section 5.4, we see that $\sum _{j,k}(\log |f|^2)_{j\bar k}\xi _ j\bar\xi _ k$ is a positive measure for every $\xi \in \mathbb C^ n$. By the matrix criterion, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2\geq 0$, and by theorem 5.3.7 its coefficients are measures.

Null sets. Let $\nu =(\log |f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ on $P=P'\times D$, and let $\eta \geq 0$ be a test function on $D$. By (5.5.4), for every nonnegative test function $\psi $ on $P'$,

\[ \int _ P\psi (z')\eta (z_ j)\, \mathrm d\nu =\int _{P'}\psi \, h_\eta \, \mathrm dV_{2n-2},\qquad h_\eta (z')=\pi \sum _ am(z',a)\eta (a)\geq 0. \]

Both sides define positive Radon measures on $P'$ in $\psi $, the function $h_\eta $ being locally integrable by the identity itself. By the uniqueness in theorem 2.5.6, the image measure $A\mapsto \int _{\pi _ j^{-1}(A)}\eta \, \mathrm d\nu $ of $\eta \nu $ under $\pi _ j$ is therefore $h_\eta \, \mathrm dV_{2n-2}$. Every null set is contained in a Borel null set, by outer regularity of Lebesgue measure, so we may assume that $N$ is a Borel set. Then $\int _{\pi _ j^{-1}(N)}\eta \, \mathrm d\nu =\int _ Nh_\eta \, \mathrm dV_{2n-2}=0$. Letting $\eta $ increase to $1$ on $D$ along a sequence, we obtain $\nu (\pi _ j^{-1}(N))=0$ by monotone convergence.

The canonical representative. The trace of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2$ is positive, so by theorem 3.4.12 the class of $\log |f|^2$ has a canonical subharmonic representative $v$, given by the limits of ball averages in (3.4.13). At a point where $f\neq 0$, the function $\log |f|^2$ is continuous, so $v$ agrees with it there. At a zero $a$, there is $C$ with $|f(z)|\leq C|z-a|$ near $a$, since $f$ is smooth and $f(a)=0$; hence the average of $\log |f|^2$ over $B(a,r)$ is at most $\log C^2+2\log r$, which tends to $-\infty $ as $r\to 0$. Thus $v(a)=-\infty =\log |f(a)|^2$, and $v=\log |f|^2$ everywhere. $\square$

To describe $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2$, let $f$ be as in proposition 5.5.3, and consider the zero set $V=f^{-1}(0)$. Its regular part $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is the set of points $p\in V$ having a neighborhood on which

\[ f=g\, a^ m,\qquad g\ne 0,\quad \mathrm{d}a\ne 0,\quad m\ge 1, \]

with $a$ and $g$ holomorphic. In a chart around $p$ we have $\mathrm da=\sum _ k\partial _{z_ k}a\, \mathrm dz_ k$, so the complex Jacobian matrix of $a$ at $p$ is a nonzero row, and proposition 5.2.4(2), applied with $r=1$, shows that $a$ is the first coordinate of a holomorphic chart near $p$, in which $V=\{ a=0\} $ is a coordinate hyperplane. The integer $m$ depends only on $f$ and $p$. Indeed, let $f=\widetilde g\, \widetilde a^{\widetilde m}$ be a second such representation near $p$. Since $g$ and $\widetilde g$ do not vanish and $m,\widetilde m\geq 1$, both $a$ and $\widetilde a$ vanish exactly on $V$ near $p$. We use holomorphic coordinates $(t,x')$ centered at $p$ with $t=a$, defined on a polydisc. Since $\widetilde a(0,x')=0$, we have $\widetilde a(t,x')=t\, b(t,x')$ with the holomorphic function $b(t,x')=\int _0^1\partial _ t\widetilde a(st,x')\, \mathrm ds$, and $\mathrm d\widetilde a_ p=b(p)\, \mathrm da_ p$, so that $b(p)\neq 0$. On the line $x'=0$, the function $t\mapsto f(t,0)$ is $g(t,0)t^ m$ and also $\widetilde g(t,0)b(t,0)^{\widetilde m}t^{\widetilde m}$, with nonvanishing factors in front of the powers of $t$ near $t=0$; comparing the orders of vanishing at $t=0$ gives $\widetilde m=m$.

We call $m$ the multiplicity, or transverse vanishing order, of $f$ along $V$ at $p$; it is locally constant on $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. The set $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is open in $V$, so $S=V\setminus V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is closed, and $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a complex hypersurface, that is, a submanifold of dimension $n-1$, of the open set $\Omega \setminus S$. These definitions are local and invariant under holomorphic changes of coordinates, so they apply equally to holomorphic functions on complex manifolds.

Lemma 5.5.5.116B90FD9752 Let $f$ be as in proposition 5.5.3, and let $z_0$ be a zero of $f$. There are complex linear coordinates centered at $z_0$ and a polydisc $P$ centered at $z_0$ such that, for every $j=1,\ldots ,n$, the projection $\pi _ j$ of $P$ omitting the coordinate $z_ j$ satisfies $S\cap P\subset \pi _ j^{-1}(N_ j)$ for a Lebesgue null set $N_ j$.

Proof. Choose coordinates as in lemma 5.5.1, so that $f$ does not vanish identically on any coordinate axis through $z_0=0$. Fix $j$, and write points as $(z',w)$ with $w=z_ j$. By theorem 5.5.2 in the variable $w$, there is a polydisc $P_ j=P'_ j\times \{ |w|<\rho \} $ in the domain of $f$ on which $f=gW$, where $g$ is a holomorphic unit and $W$ is a monic polynomial in $w$ of some degree $m\geq 1$ whose coefficients are holomorphic on $P'_ j$ and whose roots lie in $\{ |w|<\rho _1\} $ for some $\rho _1<\rho $. We shall find a holomorphic function $h$ on $P'_ j$, not identically zero, such that every zero of $f$ in $P_ j$ lying over $\{ h\neq 0\} $ belongs to $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Then $S\cap P_ j\subset \pi _ j^{-1}(N_ j)$ with $N_ j=\{ h=0\} $, which is null by lemma 5.5.1 if $n\geq 2$, and empty if $n=1$, when $P'_ j$ is a point. Any polydisc $P$ centered at $0$ and contained in $P_1\cap \cdots \cap P_ n$ then has the required property.

For $z'\in P'_ j$, let $\lambda _1(z'),\ldots ,\lambda _ m(z')$ be the roots of $W(z',\cdot )$, repeated according to multiplicity, and put $p_ k(z')=\sum _ l\lambda _ l(z')^ k$ for $k\geq 0$. By the residue theorem,

\[ p_ k(z')=\frac1{2\pi \mathrm i}\int _{|w|=\rho '}w^ k\frac{\partial _ wW(z',w)}{W(z',w)}\, \mathrm dw\qquad (\rho _1<\rho '<\rho ), \]

so $p_ k$ is holomorphic on $P'_ j$, by differentiation under the integral sign. Let $s$ be the largest number of distinct roots of $W(z',\cdot )$ for $z'\in P'_ j$, and let $h$ be the determinant of the $s\times s$ matrix $(p_{a+b})_{0\leq a,b\leq s-1}$; it is holomorphic on $P'_ j$. For fixed $z'$, this matrix is $A^{\mathsf T}A$, where $A$ is the $m\times s$ matrix with entries $A_{la}=\lambda _ l^ a$.

To compute $h(z')$, note that the $b$th column of $A^{\mathsf T}A$ is $\sum _ l\lambda _ l^ bv_ l$, where $v_ l=(\lambda _ l^0,\ldots ,\lambda _ l^{s-1})$ is the $l$th row of $A$. Expanding the determinant by multilinearity in the columns, discarding the terms in which some $v_ l$ occurs twice, and grouping the remaining terms according to the set $L$ of indices that occur, we find that each group contributes $(\det A_ L)^2$. Indeed, if $L=\{ l_1<\cdots <l_ s\} $, the terms of this group are indexed by the permutations $\pi $ of $\{ 1,\ldots ,s\} $, the term of $\pi $ taking $\lambda _{l_{\pi (b+1)}}^ bv_{l_{\pi (b+1)}}$ from the $b$th column. Reordering the columns multiplies the determinant by $\operatorname {sign}(\pi )$, so the group contributes $\det A_ L\sum _\pi \operatorname {sign}(\pi )\prod _{b=0}^{s-1}\lambda _{l_{\pi (b+1)}}^ b$, and this sum is the Leibniz expansion of $\det A_ L$. Thus we obtain the Cauchy–Binet formula

\[ h(z')=\det (A^{\mathsf T}A)=\sum _ L(\det A_ L)^2=\sum _ L\prod _{l<l',\ l,l'\in L}(\lambda _ l-\lambda _{l'})^2, \]

where $L$ runs over the $s$-element subsets of $\{ 1,\ldots ,m\} $, $A_ L$ is the square matrix formed by the rows of $A$ with indices in $L$, and the last equality is the Vandermonde formula. If $W(z',\cdot )$ has fewer than $s$ distinct roots, every set $L$ contains two indices with equal roots, and $h(z')=0$. If $W(z',\cdot )$ has exactly $s$ distinct roots $\mu _1,\ldots ,\mu _ s$, of multiplicities $k_1,\ldots ,k_ s$, then the nonzero terms are those of the $k_1\cdots k_ s$ sets $L$ containing exactly one index of each root, and each of them equals $\prod _{c<c'}(\mu _ c-\mu _{c'})^2\neq 0$; hence $h(z')\neq 0$. By the choice of $s$, the function $h$ is not identically zero.

Now let $h(z'_0)\neq 0$, and let $(z'_0,w_0)\in P_ j$ be a zero of $f$. Then $W(z'_0,w_0)=0$, since $g$ is a unit, and $W(z'_0,\cdot )$ has exactly $s$ distinct roots $\mu _1,\ldots ,\mu _ s$, of multiplicities $k_1,\ldots ,k_ s$; say $w_0=\mu _1$. Choose disjoint closed discs $E_ c$ centered at $\mu _ c$ in $\{ |w|<\rho \} $, and a connected neighborhood $Y$ of $z'_0$ in $P'_ j$ such that $W(z',\cdot )$ has no zeros on the boundary circles $\partial E_ c$ for $z'\in Y$; this is possible because $W(z'_0,\cdot )$ has no zeros on these compact circles and $W$ is continuous. By the argument principle, the number of roots of $W(z',\cdot )$ in $E_ c$, counted with multiplicity, is $(2\pi \mathrm i)^{-1}\int _{\partial E_ c}\partial _ wW/W\, \mathrm dw$, a continuous integer-valued function on $Y$, hence equal to $k_ c$. Thus each $E_ c$ contains at least one root of $W(z',\cdot )$. Since $W(z',\cdot )$ has at most $s$ distinct roots, each $E_ c$ contains exactly one, $\mu _ c(z')$, of multiplicity $k_ c$, and since $k_1+\cdots +k_ s=m$, there are no other roots. By the residue theorem,

\[ \mu _ c(z')=\frac1{2\pi \mathrm ik_ c}\int _{\partial E_ c}w\, \frac{\partial _ wW(z',w)}{W(z',w)}\, \mathrm dw, \]

which is holomorphic on $Y$, and $W(z',w)=\prod _ c(w-\mu _ c(z'))^{k_ c}$ for $z'\in Y$, both sides being monic polynomials in $w$ with the same roots and multiplicities. Near $(z'_0,w_0)$ the function $U=\prod _{c\geq 2}(w-\mu _ c(z'))^{k_ c}$ does not vanish, so $f=(gU)(w-\mu _1(z'))^{k_1}$, where $gU$ is a holomorphic unit and $\mathrm d(w-\mu _1(z'))\neq 0$. That is, $(z'_0,w_0)\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}$. $\square$

Theorem 5.5.6 (Poincaré–Lelong).8D4BA339507E Let $f$ be a holomorphic function on a complex manifold $X$ that is not identically zero on any connected component of $X$, and let $V$, $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and $S$ be as above. Then the current $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ on $X\setminus S$, the integration over $V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ weighted by the multiplicity $m$, has locally finite mass near $S$: its coefficient measures have finite total variation on $K\setminus S$ for every compact subset $K$ of a chart. Extending its coefficient measures by zero on $S$, we obtain a closed positive $(1,1)$-current $[\operatorname{div}(f)]$ on $X$, and

9E4726681500 5.5.7
\begin{equation} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2=[\operatorname{div}(f)]. \end{equation}

Proof. If $X$ has dimension zero, then $f$ has no zeros and both sides vanish. Let $X$ have dimension $n\geq 1$. The set of points near which $f$ vanishes identically is open, and it is closed by the identity theorem, applied in connected coordinate neighborhoods; since $f$ does not vanish identically on any component of $X$, this set is empty. Hence proposition 5.5.3 applies in every connected chart: the function $\log |f|^2$ is locally integrable, and $T=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2$, given by $T(\varphi )=\int _ X\log |f|^2\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ independently of coordinates, is a positive current with measure coefficients. It is closed because $\mathrm d\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}=\mathrm d\mathrm d\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}=0$. We show that $T$ gives zero mass to $S$ and agrees with $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ outside $S$.

Step 1: $T$ gives $S$ zero mass. Let $x_0\in S$, and choose coordinates and a polydisc $P$ centered at $x_0$ as in lemma 5.5.5. For each $j$, the last assertion of proposition 5.5.3 shows that the diagonal coefficient $(\log |f|^2)_{j\bar j}$ vanishes on $S\cap P\subset \pi _ j^{-1}(N_ j)$. By the computation of the trace measure in section 5.4, the sum of these diagonal coefficients is a positive multiple of the trace measure of $T$ in these coordinates, which therefore vanishes on $S\cap P$; by theorem 5.3.7 so do all coefficient measures of $T$ in these coordinates. By (5.3.6), the vanishing of the coefficient measures on a Borel set does not depend on the chart. Every compact subset of $S\cap U$, for a chart $U$, is covered by finitely many such polydiscs $P$, so by inner regularity all coefficient measures of $T$ vanish on $S\cap U$.

Step 2: $T=m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ on $X\setminus S$. Since $T$ is defined without reference to coordinates, we may compute it in any holomorphic coordinates. Away from $V$ the function $\log |f|^2$ is pluriharmonic, so $T=0$ there. Near a point $p\in V_{\mathrm{reg}}$ write $f=g\, a^ m$ as above, and use holomorphic coordinates $(t,x')$ with $t=a$. Then $\log |f|^2=\log |g|^2+m\log |t|^2$, where $\log |g|^2$ is pluriharmonic. In one variable, $\partial _ t\partial _{\bar t}\log |t|^2=\pi \delta _0$ by example 3.4.15. Since $\log |t|^2$ does not depend on $x'$, Fubini's theorem shows that its distributional derivatives involving $x'$ or $\bar x'$ vanish and that $\langle (\log |t|^2)_{t\bar t},\psi \rangle =\pi \int \psi (0,x')\, \mathrm dV_{2n-2}(x')$ for test functions $\psi $; thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |t|^2=(\mathrm i/2\pi )(\log |t|^2)_{t\bar t}\, \mathrm dt\wedge \mathrm d\bar t$. Let $\varphi $ be a test form of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$, and let $\varphi _0=c(t,x')\bigwedge _ l(\frac{\mathrm i}2\mathrm dx'_ l\wedge \mathrm d\bar x'_ l)$ be its component without $\mathrm dt$ and $\mathrm d\bar t$, the only one that contributes. Since $(\mathrm i/2\pi )\, \mathrm dt\wedge \mathrm d\bar t\wedge \varphi _0=\pi ^{-1}c\, \mathrm dV_{2n}$, we obtain

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |t|^2)(\varphi )=\int c(0,x')\, \mathrm dV_{2n-2}(x')=\int _{\{ t=0\} }\varphi |_{\{ t=0\} }, \]

that is, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |t|^2=[\{ t=0\} ]$ in the sense of example 5.3.9, the hypersurface carrying its complex orientation. Therefore $T=m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ near $p$.

Step 3: extension across $S$. Let $U$ be a chart and $K\subset U$ compact. By Step 2, the trace measure of the positive current $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ on $U\setminus S$ is the restriction of $\mu _ T$, so its mass on $K\setminus S$ is at most $\mu _ T(K)<\infty $. By the coefficient bound of theorem 5.3.7, applied on $U\setminus S$, the coefficient measures of $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$, a positive current of bidegree $(1,1)$, have total variation at most $C_{n,1}\mu _ T(K)$ on $K\setminus S$. Hence they extend by zero on $S$ to complex Radon measures on $U$. Indeed, each coefficient measure $\sigma $ is a complex combination of the positive and negative variations of $\operatorname{Re}\sigma $ and $\operatorname{Im}\sigma $, which are positive Radon measures on $U\setminus S$ bounded by $|\sigma |$; extended by zero on $S$, they become Borel measures on $U$ that are finite on compact sets, hence Radon by proposition 2.2.2. By Step 2 the coefficient measures of $T$ agree with those of $m[V_{\mathrm{reg}}]$ on $U\setminus S$, and by Step 1 they vanish on $S$. Thus the extensions by zero are the coefficient measures of the current $T$ of order zero; we denote this current by $[\operatorname{div}(f)]$. This proves (5.5.7), and $[\operatorname{div}(f)]=T$ is positive and closed. $\square$

Example 5.5.8.DB42A2BDBC5B Let $1\leq r\leq n$ and $f(z)=z_1^{a_1}\cdots z_ r^{a_ r}$ on $\mathbb C^ n$, with positive integers $a_ j$. Then $\log |f|^2=\sum _ ja_ j\log |z_ j|^2$ everywhere, both sides being $-\infty $ on the zero set, and theorem 5.5.6 applied to each $z_ j$ gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |f|^2=\sum _{j=1}^ r a_ j[\{ z_ j=0\} ]$ on all of $\mathbb C^ n$, including the intersections of the coordinate hyperplanes. More generally, let $g$ and $h$ be holomorphic and not identically zero on a connected coordinate neighborhood. The function $\log |g|^2-\log |h|^2$, defined outside the null set where $g$ or $h$ vanishes, is locally integrable, and its complex Hessian is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\log |g|^2-\log |h|^2)=[\operatorname{div}(g)]-[\operatorname{div}(h)]$. This current depends only on the quotient $g/h$: if $g',h'$ is another such pair with $gh'=g'h$, the two logarithmic differences agree almost everywhere, hence as distributions. We regard the difference only as an element of $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$; in particular, it is assigned no values at the common zeros of $g$ and $h$.
