15.18 Interior smoothness on the regular locus899B82DE5E84

899B82DE5E84

Throughout this section, $\theta $ is a smooth big real $(1,1)$-form, and $\rho $, $Z=\{ \rho =-\infty \} $ and $\Omega =X\setminus Z$ are the strict analytic barrier and its regular locus fixed in section 15.13. Thus $\rho \leq 0$ has analytic singularities with smooth remainder, and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq \omega $. If $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has minimal singularities, then $\varphi \geq V_\theta -C\geq \rho -C$ for a constant $C$. Hence $\varphi $ is locally bounded on $\Omega $, and its Monge–Ampère measure there is the bounded-potential product.

For such a potential, theorem 15.17.1 gives local Hölder continuity on $\Omega $ when the density belongs to $L^ p$ for some $p>1$. We show that a smooth strictly positive density on the whole of $\Omega $ then makes the potential smooth on $\Omega $. Local information alone cannot give this: example 15.21.15 below is a locally Lipschitz plurisubharmonic function with constant Monge–Ampère density that is not $C^1$. The argument therefore uses the barrier globally. By the proper gap of lemma 5.40.7, the difference between the solution and the barrier attains a minimum on the closed set where smoothness fails, and strict positivity of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho $ turns this minimum into a quadratic separation. Near the minimum, smooth Dirichlet approximants have traces of polynomial growth; the separation improves this growth until a uniform trace bound yields elliptic regularity.

Theorem 15.18.1.43AC522E5ED8 Suppose $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ has minimal singularities and is locally Hölder continuous on $\Omega $. Let $c>0$ and let $f$ be smooth and strictly positive on $\Omega $. Assume

\[ (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n=cf\, \omega ^ n\quad \text{on }\Omega . \]

Then $\varphi \in C^\infty (\Omega )$ and $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi >0$ there.

The Hölder hypothesis holds when, in addition, $f\in L^ p(X,\omega ^ n)$ for some $p>1$, by theorem 15.17.1; see corollary 15.18.50. The theorem also applies when local Hölder continuity is obtained by another argument.

The proof is by contradiction and occupies the rest of the section. Let $\mathcal S\subset \Omega $ be the closed set of points near which $\varphi $ is not smooth, suppose that $\mathcal S\neq \varnothing $, and let $p\in \mathcal S$ minimize $\varphi -\rho $ on $\mathcal S$. On a small ball centered at $p$ the argument has four steps.

  1. A comparison principle for continuous solutions (subsection 15.18.2) and the smooth Dirichlet problem give smooth solutions $u_ j$ of the equation that converge uniformly to $\varphi $ at a polynomial rate in $j$, while the traces of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$, measured in Euclidean coordinates, grow at most polynomially in $j$ (subsection 15.18.7).

  2. Near $p$, the quadratic separation along $\mathcal S$, together with uniform trace bounds on compact sets where $\varphi $ is already smooth, improves the trace growth to $O(j^\gamma )$ for every $\gamma >0$. Interpolation with the convergence rate then gives uniform $C^{1,\alpha }$ bounds for every $\alpha <1$ (subsection 15.18.24).

  3. A third-order estimate whose constants depend polynomially on the ellipticity turns uniform $C^{1,\alpha }$ bounds, for $\alpha $ close to one, into a uniform trace bound (subsection 15.18.34).

  4. A continuous solution with bounded trace is smooth (subsection 15.18.4). Hence $\varphi $ is smooth near $p$, contradicting $p\in \mathcal S$ (subsection 15.18.49).

15.18.2 Local comparison7A246CDE0880

The comparison principle below, for continuous sub- and supersolutions on a bounded domain, identifies the given continuous potential with limits of smooth Dirichlet solutions. Since the background need not be closed, the classical Bedford–Taylor comparison does not apply directly. We freeze the background at a point, apply the closed-current comparison formula (5.27.7) to quadratic modifications, and control the error by the mixed inequalities of lemma 5.28.7, lemma 5.28.10.

The local arguments of this section take place in coordinate balls, always with compact closure in a larger coordinate chart. There we put $\beta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$, write $\partial _ i=\partial /\partial z_ i$ and $\partial _{\bar i}=\partial /\partial \bar z_ i$, and take coefficient matrices of real $(1,1)$-forms relative to $\beta $. For such a form $A$, $\operatorname {tr}_\beta A$ and $\det _\beta A$ denote the trace and the determinant of its coefficient matrix, so that $A^ n=(\det _\beta A)\beta ^ n$, and $\Delta _\beta =\operatorname {tr}_\beta \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$. For a positive form $T$, $\operatorname {tr}_ T$ is the trace computed with the inverse of the coefficient matrix of $T$. Indices run from $1$ to $n$, repeated indices in tensor expressions are summed, and $I$ denotes the identity matrix. In local statements, $\vartheta $ denotes a smooth real $(1,1)$-form, not necessarily closed, which plays the role of $\theta $.

Proposition 15.18.3.D2F8F8FD5A17 Let $D\Subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a bounded domain and let $\vartheta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form near $\overline D$. Suppose $u,v\in C(\overline D)$ are $\vartheta $-plurisubharmonic,

\[ (\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n\geq F\beta ^ n, \qquad (\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\leq F\beta ^ n, \]

where $F>0$ is continuous on $D$. If $u\leq v$ on $\partial D$, then $u\leq v$ in $D$.

Proof. Step 1: $v$ admits no strict smooth lower test. Suppose, to the contrary, that a $C^2$ function $q$ touches $v$ from below at a point $p\in D$ and that, at $p$,

\[ \vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q>0, \qquad \det _\beta (\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q)>F. \]

Choose a sufficiently small $a>0$. After replacing $q$ by $q-a|z-p|^2$, which makes the contact strict, and restricting to a small ball $B_ r(p)$, there are fixed $\lambda ,\eta >0$ such that

\[ A:=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \lambda \beta , \qquad A^ n\geq (F+\eta )\beta ^ n. \]

To compare with closed currents, freeze the background at $p$: put $\kappa =\sup _{B_ r}\lVert \vartheta -\vartheta (p)\rVert _\beta $ and $B_0=\vartheta (p)+\kappa \beta $. Then $S=B_0-\vartheta $ satisfies $0\leq S\leq 2\kappa \beta $. For small $s>0$ the set $E=\{ v<q+s\} $ is nonempty and relatively compact in $B_ r$. If $Q_0$ is a quadratic potential for the constant form $B_0$, then

\[ P=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(v+Q_0)=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v+S, \qquad Q=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(q+s+Q_0)=A+S\geq \lambda \beta \]

are closed positive Hessians. Applying (5.27.7), expanding $P$, and using $S\leq 2\kappa \beta \leq 2\kappa \lambda ^{-1}Q$, we obtain

\[ \int _ E P^ n\leq \int _ E F\beta ^ n+e_\kappa \int _ E P^ n, \qquad e_\kappa =\left(1+\frac{2\kappa }{\lambda }\right)^ n-1. \]

On the other hand, (5.27.7) with $j=n$ gives

\[ \int _ E P^ n\geq \int _ E Q^ n\geq \int _ E(F+\eta )\beta ^ n. \]

If $M$ bounds $F$ on the ball, the two estimates give $(1-e_\kappa )\eta \leq e_\kappa M$. Since $\kappa \to 0$, and hence $e_\kappa \to 0$, as $r\downarrow 0$, this is impossible for small $r$. Thus no such lower test exists.

Step 2: comparison.

Suppose that $\max _ D(u-v)>0$. We regularize $u$ to produce a forbidden lower test for $v$. Let $\chi _\delta $ be a nonnegative smooth mollifier of mass one and radius $\delta >0$. For small $t>0$ and $C\geq \sup _ D|z|^2$, set

\[ u_ t=u+t(|z|^2-C). \]

Then $u_ t\leq v$ on $\partial D$, the positive maximum persists, and $T_ t=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ t\geq t\beta $. Lemma 5.28.7 gives

\[ T_ t^ n\geq (F^{1/n}+t)^ n\beta ^ n. \]

Choose a relatively compact neighborhood $W$ of the positive maximum set. By lemma 5.28.10,

\[ (T_ t*\chi _\delta )^ n \geq \bigl ((F^{1/n}+t)*\chi _\delta \bigr )^ n\beta ^ n. \]

Since

\[ \vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ t*\chi _\delta ) =T_ t*\chi _\delta +(\vartheta -\vartheta *\chi _\delta ), \]

the convolution satisfies $T_ t*\chi _\delta \geq t\beta $. If $\epsilon _\delta =\| \vartheta -\vartheta *\chi _\delta \| _{C^0(W)}$, then

\[ \vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ t*\chi _\delta ) \geq (1-\epsilon _\delta /t)(T_ t*\chi _\delta ). \]

For fixed $t>0$, uniform convergence of the convolved density and $\epsilon _\delta \to 0$ show that the left-hand side is positive and has determinant strictly larger than $F$ on $W$ for small $\delta $. Since $u_ t*\chi _\delta \to u_ t$ uniformly on $W$, the difference $u_ t*\chi _\delta -v$ still attains its maximum over $\overline W$ at an interior point of $W$ for small $\delta $. Subtracting this maximum from $u_ t*\chi _\delta $ produces a smooth function touching $v$ from below with the properties excluded in Step 1, a contradiction. $\square$

15.18.4 Bounded traces and smoothness5FD96F70C06E

We begin with the criterion used in the last step of the proof. A bounded trace of a positive current bounds all its coefficient measures, so a continuous solution with bounded trace is a strong solution with $W^{2,q}$ regularity for every finite $q$. The equation then bounds the eigenvalues from below, so it becomes uniformly elliptic. Since the solution is not yet known to be $C^2$, the Evans–Krylov theory is applied to it as a viscosity solution of a concave extension of the operator.

Proposition 15.18.5.7DF8FE8A18D7 Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a coordinate ball, let $\vartheta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form there and let $F\in C^\infty (B)$ be strictly positive. Suppose $u$ is continuous, $T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq 0$, and

7A41D9785A7C 15.18.6
\begin{equation} T^ n=F\beta ^ n. \end{equation}

For a point $p\in B$, the following conditions, each required on some neighborhood of $p$, are equivalent:

  1. $u$ is smooth;

  2. $T\geq \delta \beta $ for some $\delta >0$;

  3. $\operatorname {tr}_\beta T$ has a locally bounded measure density.

Proof. Choose $C$ so that $v=u+C|z|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and use nonnegative radial mollifiers for which $v_\eta \downarrow v$. The smooth matrix convolutions $T_\eta =T*\chi _\eta $ can be written as

\[ T_\eta =(\vartheta *\chi _\eta -C\beta )+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_\eta . \]

Bedford–Taylor monotone continuity and smooth convergence of the background imply $T_\eta ^ k\to T^ k$ for every $k$.

(2)$\Rightarrow $(3). Assume (2). For every Hermitian matrix $M\geq \delta I$,

\[ \det M\geq \delta ^{n-1}\bigl (\operatorname {tr} M-(n-1)\delta \bigr ). \]

Apply this to the regularizations and pass to currents. Equation (15.18.6) gives

\[ \operatorname {tr}_\beta T\leq \delta ^{1-n}F+(n-1)\delta , \]

which is (3).

(3)$\Rightarrow $(1). Assume (3), say $\operatorname {tr}_\beta T\leq L$. Positivity makes every coefficient of the matrix-valued current $T$ a bounded density. Hence the Euclidean Laplacian of $u$ is locally bounded, and interior Poisson estimates give $u\in W^{2,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for every finite $q$. Mollification converges in $W^{2,q}$; taking $q>2n$ identifies the bounded-potential determinant with the ordinary almost-everywhere determinant. Thus $\det _\beta T=F$ almost everywhere. On a smaller ball with $F\geq m>0$, all eigenvalues of $T$ are at most $L$ and their product is at least $m$, so

\[ \frac{m}{L^{n-1}}I\leq T\leq LI \]

almost everywhere.

It remains to show that this strong solution is smooth. Let $P$ be the real linear map from real symmetric $2n\times 2n$ Hessian matrices to Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices, sending $D^2u$ to the coefficient matrix of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ relative to $\beta $, and let $\Theta (x)$ be the matrix of $\vartheta $. With $\delta =mL^{1-n}$, define the concave extension

\[ G(x,M)=\inf _{\delta I\leq A\leq LI} \left\{ \log \det A+\operatorname {tr}\! \left[A^{-1}\bigl (\Theta (x)+P(M)-A\bigr )\right]\right\} -\log F(x). \]

For fixed $A$, write $\mathcal E_ A(x,M)$ for the expression in braces minus $\log F(x)$. The tangent-plane formula for $\log \det $ gives

\[ G(x,D^2u)=0\quad \text{almost everywhere}. \]

The operator $G$ is concave and uniformly elliptic on real symmetric matrices. The affine operators $\mathcal E_ A$ have constant principal matrices with eigenvalues bounded above and below in terms of $\delta $ and $L$. Their $x$-dependence is smooth uniformly in $A$, so $G$ has a uniform local Lipschitz modulus in $x$.

We claim that $u$ is a viscosity solution of $G(x,D^2u)=0$. Suppose that a smooth function $\psi $ touches $u$ from above at $x_0$ and that $G(x_0,D^2\psi )<0$. Some fixed matrix $A$ then satisfies $\mathcal E_ A(x,D^2\psi )<-\eta $ on a small ball. Since $G(x,D^2u)=0$ almost everywhere, $\mathcal E_ A(x,D^2u)\geq 0$ there. Thus $u-\psi $ has an interior maximum and satisfies

\[ \operatorname {tr}\! \left(A^{-1}P(D^2(u-\psi ))\right)\geq \eta \quad \text{almost everywhere}. \]

After a small quadratic perturbation makes the contact strict, this contradicts the Aleksandrov maximum principle [Chapter 9, Theorem 9.1, GT01]. If instead $\psi $ touches $u$ from below and $G(x_0,D^2\psi )>0$, then $\mathcal E_ A(x,D^2\psi )>\eta $ for every admissible $A$ on a small ball. At almost every point choose an admissible $A(x)$ with $\mathcal E_{A(x)}(x,D^2u)<\eta /2$. A measurable choice is obtained by taking the first qualifying element of a countable dense family. The function $\psi -u$ then violates the same maximum principle for the measurable uniformly elliptic coefficients $A(x)^{-1}$. This proves the claim.

The classical variable-coefficient Evans–Krylov theorem gives $u\in C^{2,\alpha }_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for some $0<\alpha <1$. The original equation is now uniformly elliptic with smooth data. Difference quotients satisfy linear equations whose principal coefficients are averages of inverse matrices along positive definite segments. Interior Schauder estimates first give $u\in C^{3,\alpha }_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and then, by iteration, $u\in C^\infty $. This proves (3)$\Rightarrow $(1).

(1)$\Rightarrow $(2). If $u$ is smooth, then $T$ is a smooth semipositive form with determinant $F>0$, so it is positive definite; on a smaller neighborhood this gives (2). $\square$

15.18.7 Smooth exact approximantsE9933083FDD7

We now construct the approximants of the first step. The smooth Dirichlet theorem, lemma 15.7.1, applies to every smooth positive density $F$. Approximating the Hölder boundary values of $\varphi $ by smooth functions therefore gives smooth solutions, and the point is to control them quantitatively. The estimates below bound the trace of a smooth solution by a power of the $C^4$ norm of its boundary value. The $C^4$ norms of the boundary approximations grow polynomially while their uniform errors decay polynomially, so both the convergence and the trace growth have polynomial rates.

In this subsection balls are centered at the origin. On the boundary sphere, $\nu $ is the outward Euclidean unit normal and $\nabla _{\tan }$ denotes tangential differentiation. For a positive form $T$, inner products $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle _ T$ and norms $|\cdot |_ T$ of $(1,0)$-covectors are computed with the inverse of the coefficient matrix of $T$.

Lemma 15.18.8.3EBB652C42D7 Let $B$ be a coordinate ball, let $\vartheta $ and $F>0$ be smooth near $\overline B$, and let $q$ be smooth with $R=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q\geq \varepsilon \beta $ for a fixed $\varepsilon >0$. Fix a finite $L\geq 0$. If $u\in C^\infty (B)$ satisfies

\[ T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0, \qquad T^ n=F\beta ^ n, \qquad |u-q|\leq L, \]

put $S=\operatorname {tr}_\beta T$ and $v=u-q$. For $A>0$, define

\[ Q_ A=\log S-Av+\mathrm{e}^{-v}. \]

There are $A_0,C$, depending only on the fixed data and $L$, such that at every interior local maximum of $Q_ A$, with $A\geq A_0$,

59253C0B8B5A 15.18.9
\begin{equation} S\leq C\sqrt A. \end{equation}

The constants do not depend on any bound for the gradient of $u$.

Proof. This is the maximum-principle computation behind the classical second-order estimate. The non-closedness of $\vartheta $ produces a first-order error term, which is absorbed by the convex term $\mathrm{e}^{-v}$.

At the point in question make a constant unitary change of coordinates so that the matrix of $T$ is diagonal, with eigenvalues $\lambda _ i$. Write

\[ \Delta _ T=\sum _ i\lambda _ i^{-1}\partial _ i\partial _{\bar i}, \qquad H=\operatorname {tr}_ T\beta . \]

Differentiating $\log \det T=\log F$ and commuting derivatives gives

8A24C1B85BE1 15.18.10
\begin{equation} \Delta _ TS=\Delta _\beta \log F+P+E_0, \quad P=\sum _{k,i,j}\frac{|\partial _ kT_{i\bar j}|^2}{\lambda _ i\lambda _ j}, \quad |E_0|\leq C_0H. \end{equation}

The non-closedness of $\vartheta $ enters through the bounded vector

\[ E_ p=\sum _ k(\partial _ p\vartheta _{k\bar k}-\partial _ k\vartheta _{p\bar k}). \]

Since $\partial _ pS-E_ p=\sum _ k\partial _ kT_{p\bar k}$, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives $|\partial S-E|_ T^2\leq SP$. From (15.18.10),

1894AFF4178E 15.18.11
\begin{equation} \Delta _ T\log S \geq -\frac{C_0(1+H)}S- \frac2S\operatorname {Re}\langle \partial \log S,E\rangle _ T. \end{equation}

Set $g_ A(s)=-As+\mathrm{e}^{-s}$. At a local maximum of $Q_ A$, $\partial \log S=-g_ A'(v)\partial v$ and $\Delta _ Tv=n-\operatorname {tr}_ TR$. Combining these identities with (15.18.11), then completing the square in $\partial v$, yields

9C906B419E9F 15.18.12
\begin{equation} 0\geq \left[\varepsilon A-\frac{C_2}{S}-\frac{C_2(A+1)^2}{S^2}\right]H -C_3(A+1)-\frac{C_2}{S}. \end{equation}

Here $|E|_ T^2\leq CH$ and the bounds for $v$ have been used. If $S\geq K\sqrt A$ and $K$ is large, the bracket is at least $\varepsilon A/2$. Thus $H\leq C$ independently of $A$ and $u$. The determinant upper bound then gives $S\leq C'$. Enlarging $K$ makes this incompatible with $S\geq K\sqrt A$, proving (15.18.9). $\square$

We shall also use the following variant, in which a gradient bound replaces the convex term. If $|\nabla (u-q)|_\beta $ is bounded, then at an interior maximum of $\log S-A(u-q)$ the trace is bounded independently of $A$ for all large $A$. Indeed, at the maximum $\partial \log S=A\partial (u-q)$, and (15.18.11) gives

\[ 0\geq \left[A\varepsilon -\frac CS-\frac{CA}{S}\right]H-nA-\frac CS. \]

For $S$ larger than a fixed constant the bracket is at least $A\varepsilon /2$; the determinant again controls $S$.

15.18.13 A polynomial boundary trace estimate94BAFACA36D1

Proposition 15.18.14.6586F9B3B128 Let $B$ be the unit ball. Fix smooth $\vartheta $, smooth $F>0$ near $\overline B$, and $M<\infty $. There are $C,N<\infty $, depending only on these fixed data, such that every smooth solution $u$ with boundary value $g\in C^\infty (\partial B)$ of

\[ T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0, \qquad T^ n=F\beta ^ n, \qquad \lVert u\rVert _{L^\infty (B)}\leq M, \qquad u|_{\partial B}=g, \]

satisfies

F89A683ED708 15.18.15
\begin{equation} \sup _{\overline B}\operatorname {tr}_\beta T \leq C\bigl (1+\lVert g\rVert _{C^4(\partial B)}\bigr )^ N. \end{equation}

Proof. We follow the classical boundary estimates for the Dirichlet problem on a ball and keep track of the dependence on $L=1+\lVert g\rVert _{C^4}$. Barriers bound the first derivatives, and differentiation along rotations bounds the mixed normal–tangential second derivatives on the boundary. A lower bound for the tangential block of $T$ then bounds the double normal derivative through the equation, and lemma 15.18.8 reduces the interior bound to the boundary bound.

Step 1: first derivatives. Fix a sufficiently large constant $K>0$, depending only on the fixed data. Extend $g$ to $G$ on $\overline B$ with $\lVert G\rVert _{C^4}\leq CL$. For $A=CL$ large enough,

\[ \underline u=G+A(|z|^2-1), \qquad \underline T=\vartheta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\underline u\geq K\beta , \qquad \underline T^ n\geq F\beta ^ n. \]

Proposition 15.18.3 gives $\underline u\leq u$. If $h$ solves $\Delta _\beta h=-C_\vartheta $ with $h=g$ on $\partial B$ and $C_\vartheta \geq \sup \operatorname {tr}_\beta \vartheta $, then $u\leq h$. Boundary differentiation of these barriers gives

A09942AD8934 15.18.16
\begin{equation} |\partial _\nu u|+|\nabla _{\tan }u|\leq CL \quad \text{on }\partial B. \end{equation}

Let $H=\operatorname {tr}_ T\beta $, $\mathcal L=\operatorname {tr}_ T\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$, and $w=u-\underline u\geq 0$, the linearized operator and the barrier function used below. Taking $K$ fixed and sufficiently large gives

F834489897B3 15.18.17
\begin{equation} \mathcal Lw=n-\operatorname {tr}_ T\underline T\leq -(1+H). \end{equation}

Let $E=\sum _ k(z_ k\partial _{z_ k}+\bar z_ k\partial _{\bar z_ k})$ be the real radial field. Write $\Theta $ for the coefficient matrix of $\vartheta $. Differentiating $\log \det T=\log F$ and commuting $E$ with complex second derivatives yields

\[ \mathcal L(Eu)=E(\log F)+2n-\operatorname {tr}_ T(E\Theta +2\Theta ). \]

Hence $|\mathcal L(Eu)|\leq C(1+H)$. The same estimate holds for $Zu$ when $Z$ is an infinitesimal unitary rotation. Applying the minimum principle to $CL+C'w\pm Yu$, with $Y=E$ or $Z$, gives

13E61CA163BF 15.18.18
\begin{equation} |Eu|+|Zu|\leq CL\quad \text{on }B. \end{equation}

Step 2: mixed second derivatives on the boundary. For a rotation $Z$, the function $R_ Z=Z(u-G)$ vanishes on $\partial B$ and satisfies $|\mathcal LR_ Z|\leq CL(1+H)$. The barriers $CLw\pm R_ Z$ and (15.18.17) imply $|\partial _\nu R_ Z|\leq CL^2$ on the boundary. Rotations span the real tangent space of $\partial B$, so all real mixed normal–tangential second derivatives are $O(L^2)$. Proposition 15.18.31 therefore gives a uniform trace bound for the $u_ j$ on every compact subset of $B_0\setminus \mathcal S$.
