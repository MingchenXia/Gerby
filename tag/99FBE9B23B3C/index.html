Fourier analysis and incidence of complex lines

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 6 Fourier analysis and incidence of complex lines

This chapter develops the Fourier analysis on Euclidean space that the complex theory of 2 requires, and applies it to functions of several complex variables. Let $D\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be open, with $n\geq 2$, and let $u$ be a function on $D$ whose restriction to every complex line is subharmonic. On each line $u$ is upper semicontinuous, and hence bounded above on compact subsets of that line, but the hypothesis gives no control of these bounds as the line varies. We prove that a Borel function with this property is nevertheless locally bounded above on $D$ (corollary 6.5.4). In chapter 8 this is the step that makes upper semicontinuity automatic for functions that are subharmonic on complex lines (theorem 8.1.3).

The proof is by contradiction. Suppose that $u(x_ j)\to \infty $ along a sequence $x_ j\to p$. By the submean inequality, $u\geq u(x_ j)$ on a subset of positive length of every sufficiently small circle centered at $x_ j$ in a complex line through $x_ j$. Choosing one such point on each circle, we obtain a probability measure on an annulus about $x_ j$, carried by the set where $u\geq u(x_ j)$, whose Riesz energy of order $2n-2$ is bounded independently of $j$ (theorem 6.4.2).

By Plancherel's theorem, energy of this order is a constant multiple of the average, over all complex hyperplanes $H$ through the origin, of the squared $L^2$ norms of the densities of the measures projected onto $H$ (theorem 6.5.2). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, a projected measure with a density of controlled $L^2$ norm cannot be concentrated on a set of small measure. An incidence argument then produces a single complex line that meets infinitely many of the sets $\{ u\geq u(x_ j)\} $ near $p$ (theorem 6.5.3), which contradicts the upper bound of $u$ on that line. The Fourier analysis is developed in section 6.1, and the invariant measures used to average over directions and over hyperplanes are constructed in section 6.3.

The same Fourier methods give $L^ p$ estimates for second derivatives. In section 6.2 we show that, for $1<p<\infty $, the $L^ p$ norm of the Laplacian of a test function bounds the $L^ p$ norm of its full real Hessian. Together with Morrey's inequality, these estimates supply the real regularity of functions with bounded Laplacian that is used in chapter 16 to compute Monge–Ampère measures of envelopes below nonsmooth obstacles.

Historical remarks and references for this chapter are collected in section B.6.
