Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag 1D58A228EF5A

Pencil ideal envelope

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 27.18.3

Theorem 27.18.3. For these $K$ and $w$, the partial envelope (27.4) is

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\theta ,K}[\psi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)& =P=g(s)-\varphi _ X,\\ g(s)& =\sup _{0\leq t\leq 1}(ts-t^2) =\begin{cases} 0,& s\leq 0,\\ s^2/4,& 0\leq s\leq 2,\\ s-1,& s\geq 2. \end{cases}\end{aligned} \]

Here $P$ is given by this formula on the torus and extends to a $\theta $-plurisubharmonic function on $X$ with $P(p_\pm )=-\infty $ and $\psi -1\leq P\leq \psi $.

Proof. For an arbitrary competitor $v$, lemma 27.18.2 gives, in terms of the affine coordinate $\xi $ of $\mathbb P^1$,

\[ \chi (\xi )=\log (1+|\xi |^2)+u(\xi ), \qquad v=\chi (z_1z_2)-\varphi _ X. \]

On the interior of $K$, the obstacle condition, which holds quasi-everywhere, becomes an inequality everywhere, by the submean inequality and the continuity of $w+\varphi _ X$. Evaluating at $x_1=x_2=s/2$, with $s=\log |\xi |^2$, gives

\[ \chi (\xi )\leq s^2/4\quad (-2<s<4) \]

for every argument of $\xi $. At $s=0$ this bounds $\chi $ by zero on $|\xi |=1$, and the maximum principle for the subharmonic function $\chi $ bounds it by zero on the whole disk $|\xi |\leq 1$. At infinity use the local potential

\[ \chi _\infty (\eta )=\chi (1/\eta )+\log |\eta |^2. \]

The obstacle at $s=2$ bounds it by $-1$ on $|\eta |=\mathrm e^{-1}$, and the same maximum principle gives $\chi (\xi )\leq s-1$ for $s\geq 2$. Together with the obstacle for $0\leq s\leq 2$, this proves $\chi \leq g$, without any invariance assumption on the competitor.

Conversely, $g$ is convex with slopes in $[0,1]$, so $g(\log |\xi |^2)-\log (1+|\xi |^2)$ is an $\omega _{\rm FS}$-plurisubharmonic function on $\mathbb P^1$. By lemma 27.18.2, it defines the global function $P$. Directly,

\[ \max (0,s)-1\leq g(s)\leq \max (0,s),\qquad g(s)\leq s^2/4\leq (x_1^2+x_2^2)/2. \]

Thus $P$ belongs to the competitor class of lemma 27.18.1 and satisfies the obstacle condition everywhere on $K$. Since it dominates every competitor, it is the envelope, including its values $-\infty $ at the poles. $\square$
