Siu's divisorial decomposition

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Theorem 14.3.7 (Siu's divisorial decomposition)

Theorem 14.3.7 (Siu's divisorial decomposition). Let $T$ be a closed positive $(1,1)$-current on $X$. Then $\nu (T,D)>0$ for at most countably many prime divisors $D$, the series below converges weakly, and

14.19
\begin{equation} \label{eq:siu-divisorial-decomposition} T=R+\sum _ D\nu (T,D)[D], \end{equation}

where $R$ is a closed positive current with $\nu (R,D)=0$ for every prime divisor $D$. In particular $T-\nu (T,D)[D]\geq 0$ for every $D$. The decomposition is unique: if $T=R'+\sum _ Db_ D[D]$ for a weakly convergent series with coefficients $b_ D\geq 0$, at most countably many of them nonzero, and a closed positive current $R'$ with $\nu (R',D)=0$ for every $D$, then $b_ D=\nu (T,D)$ for every $D$ and $R'=R$.

Proof. We first subtract one divisor, then show that only countably many divisors occur and that the series converges, and finally identify the residual current and prove uniqueness.

Step 1: one divisor. Let $D$ be a prime divisor and $c=\nu (T,D)$; we show that $T-c[D]\geq 0$. Near a regular point of $D$ choose coordinates with $D=\{ z_1=0\} $ on a coordinate polydisc $W$ with $|z_1|<1$, and a plurisubharmonic potential $u$ of $T$ on $W$. Since the Lelong numbers of $u$ at the points of $D\cap W$ are at least $c$, proposition 12.3.6 gives $u\leq c\log |z_1|^2+C$ near the point. Off $D$ the function $u-c\log |z_1|^2$ is plurisubharmonic, and it is bounded above near $D$; by theorem 8.4.1 it extends to a plurisubharmonic function $v$. Since $v=u-c\log |z_1|^2$ almost everywhere and $[D]=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\log |z_1|^2$ near the point (theorem 7.4.5), we get $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v=T-c[D]$ there. Hence $T-c[D]$ is positive on a neighborhood of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and it equals $T$ on $X\setminus D$; so it is positive on the open set $X\setminus D_{\mathrm{sing}}$.

To extend the positivity across $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$, let $\phi $ be a strongly positive test form of bidegree $(n-1,n-1)$. Since $T$ and $[D]$ have measure coefficients (theorem 7.2.5), there is a real measure $m_\phi $ on $X$, a combination of the coefficient measures with the coefficients of $\phi $ as densities, such that $(T-c[D])(\chi \phi )=\int \chi \, \mathrm dm_\phi $ for test functions $\chi $. The restriction of $m_\phi $ to the open set $X\setminus D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is a positive measure, because $(T-c[D])(\chi \phi )\geq 0$ for test functions $\chi \geq 0$ supported there; and its restriction to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is that of the positive measure $\chi \mapsto T(\chi \phi )$, because $[D]$ gives no mass to $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$. Hence $(T-c[D])(\phi )=m_\phi (X)\geq 0$, that is, $T-c[D]\geq 0$.

Step 2: the series. By theorem 12.3.4, each set $E_ j=\{ x:\nu (T,x)\geq 1/j\} $ is an analytic subset of $X$ different from $X$. Being compact, $E_ j$ has finitely many irreducible components $Z$ by corollary A.2.13. We first show:

14.20
\begin{equation} \label{eq:prime-divisor-component} \begin{gathered} \text{a prime divisor $D$ contained in an analytic subset $E\neq X$}\\ \text{is an irreducible component of $E$.} \end{gathered} \end{equation}

An analytic subset of the connected manifold $X$ with an interior point is all of $X$, since the set of its interior points is open and, by the identity theorem applied to local defining functions, closed. Hence $E$ has no interior points, so its germs, and their branches, have dimension at most $n-1$, and $E_{\mathrm{sing}}$ has dimension at most $n-2$ at every point (proposition A.2.7, corollary A.2.10). At a point $y\in D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ the germ of $D$ has dimension $n-1$, so by monotonicity of dimension (see proposition A.2.7 and the remarks following it) it is not contained in the germ of $E_{\mathrm{sing}}$; thus there are points $y'\in D_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap E_{\mathrm{reg}}$. Near such a point $E$ is a complex submanifold of dimension at most $n-1$ containing the $(n-1)$-dimensional submanifold $D$, so $D$ and $E$ coincide near $y'$.

Let $F$ be the connected component of $E_{\mathrm{reg}}$ containing $y'$. The analytic subset $D\cap F$ of the connected manifold $F$ has an interior point, so it is all of $F$, and $D$ contains the closure $Z$ of $F$, an irreducible component of $E$ (corollary A.2.13). Since $D$ is compact and the components of $E$ form a locally finite family, $D$ meets only finitely many of them and is the union of its intersections with these. By induction on the number of sets, an irreducible set is not a finite union of analytic subsets different from itself; hence $D=D\cap Z'$ for a component $Z'$. Then $Z\subset D\subset Z'$, and since no component is contained in another, $D=Z$. This proves (14.20). In particular, two distinct prime divisors are not contained in one another, and every prime divisor $D$ with $\nu (T,D)\geq 1/j$, being contained in $E_ j$, is one of its finitely many components. Therefore at most countably many prime divisors have $\nu (T,D)>0$. Let $D_1,D_2,\ldots $ be an enumeration of them and put $S_ N=\sum _{k\leq N}\nu (T,D_ k)[D_ k]$.

We claim that $T-S_ N\geq 0$ for every $N$, and argue by induction; suppose that $T-S_{N-1}\geq 0$. Choose a point $x\in D_ N$ outside the proper analytic subsets $D_ N\cap D_ k$, $k<N$, and outside the exceptional sets of proposition 12.3.5 for $T$ and $T-S_{N-1}$ along $D_ N$. Such points exist, because by that proposition the complement in $D_ N$ of a countable union of proper analytic subsets of $D_ N$ is dense in $D_ N$. The currents $[D_ k]$, $k<N$, vanish near $x$, so $T-S_{N-1}=T$ near $x$, and therefore $\nu (T-S_{N-1},D_ N)=\nu (T-S_{N-1},x)=\nu (T,x)=\nu (T,D_ N)$. Step 1, applied to $T-S_{N-1}$, gives $T-S_ N\geq 0$. Consequently the trace measures of the $S_ N$ in every chart are bounded by that of $T$, and the masses $\int _ XS_ N\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$ are bounded by $\int _ XT\wedge \omega ^{n-1}$.

We now show that the sequence $(S_ N)$ converges weakly. Every test form is a finite linear combination of test forms $g\gamma $, where $g\geq 0$ is a test function supported in a chart and $\gamma $ is a strongly positive form with constant coefficients in that chart. To see this, use a partition of unity (proposition 7.1.3), the fact that strongly positive forms span the real forms (proposition 7.2.1), and write a real test function $g$ as $(g+M\psi )-M\psi $ with $\psi \geq 0$ a test function equal to one on $\operatorname{Supp}g$ and $M\geq \sup |g|$. For such $g\gamma $ the numbers $S_ N(g\gamma )$ increase with $N$, since $S_{N+1}-S_ N\geq 0$, and they are bounded by $T(g\gamma )$; hence they converge, and so does $S_ N(\phi )$ for every test form $\phi $. By the compactness assertion of theorem 7.2.5, a subsequence converges weakly to a closed positive current $S$, so $S_ N(\phi )\to S(\phi )$ for every $\phi $. Finally $R=T-S$ is closed and positive, as a weak limit of the closed positive currents $T-S_ N$.

Step 3: properties of $R$ and uniqueness. Fix a prime divisor $D$ and write $T=R+\nu (T,D)[D]+S_ D$, where $S_ D$ is the sum of the terms of the series for the other divisors. Lelong numbers are additive and nonnegative (proposition 12.1.4), and $\nu ([D],x)=\nu (\log |f|^2,x)=1$ at regular points $x$ of $D$, where $f$ is a local coordinate defining $D$. Hence, at a point of $D_{\mathrm{reg}}$ outside the countable union of proper analytic subsets where one of the currents $T$, $R$, $S_ D$ has a nongeneric Lelong number (such points exist by proposition 12.3.5, since $D_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is also a proper analytic subset of $D$),

\[ \nu (T,D)=\nu (R,D)+\nu (T,D)+\nu (S_ D,D)\geq \nu (R,D)+\nu (T,D). \]

Hence $\nu (R,D)=0$.

Now consider any decomposition $T=R'+\sum _ Db_ D[D]$ as in the statement, fix $D$, and let $S'_ D$ be the sum of the terms for the other divisors. We first show that the coefficient measures of $S'_ D$ give no mass to $D$. If $D'\neq D$ is another prime divisor, then $D\cap D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is an analytic subset of the connected manifold $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (corollary A.2.13) which is not all of it, since otherwise $D'\subset D$, and then $D'=D$ by (14.20). Hence $D\cap D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a null set for the surface measure of $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (lemma 7.4.1, in local coordinates of $D'_{\mathrm{reg}}$), and $[D']$ gives no mass to $D$. Now let $K\subset D$ be a compact subset of a chart, and for large $m$ let $\chi _ m$ be test functions with $0\leq \chi _ m\leq 1$, equal to one on $K$ and supported in the $1/m$-neighborhood of $K$ (lemma 1.4.1), all supported in a fixed compact subset of the chart, on which a test function $\psi $ equals one. For the trace measures in the chart, the weak convergence of the series gives $\mu _{S'_ D}(\chi _ m)=\sum _{D'}b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(\chi _ m)$. As $m\to \infty $, $\chi _ m\to \mathbf1_ K$ pointwise, and dominated convergence, applied to the measure $\mu _{S'_ D}$ and to the series, whose terms are bounded by $b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(\psi )$ with $\sum _{D'}b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(\psi )=\mu _{S'_ D}(\psi )<\infty $, gives $\mu _{S'_ D}(K)=\sum _{D'}b_{D'}\mu _{[D']}(K)=0$. Since $D$ is covered by countably many such compact sets, the trace measures of $S'_ D$, and hence its coefficient measures (theorem 7.2.5), vanish on $D$.

By Step 1, $S'_ D-\nu (S'_ D,D)[D]\geq 0$; comparing the trace measures of both sides on $D$, where $[D]$ has positive trace mass, forces $\nu (S'_ D,D)=0$. Additivity of Lelong numbers at generic points of $D$, applied to $T=R'+b_ D[D]+S'_ D$, gives $\nu (T,D)=0+b_ D+0$. Thus $b_ D=\nu (T,D)$ for every $D$, and $R'=R$. $\square$
