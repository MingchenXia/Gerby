Non-pluripolar products · Tag D73416504271

Weighted nonpluripolar capacity limits

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Corollary 15.6.8

Corollary 15.6.8. Let $T_{j,k}$, $T_ j$, $1\leq j\leq p\leq n$, and $R_ k$, $R$ be as in theorem 15.6.5. Let $\chi _ k$ and $\chi $ be Borel functions on $X$ with $0\leq \chi _ k,\chi \leq A<\infty $, where $\chi $ is quasi-continuous, and suppose that $\chi _ k\to \chi $ locally in capacity. Then for every smooth strongly positive $(n-p,n-p)$-form $\eta $ on $X$,

\[ \varliminf _ k\int _ X\chi _ k R_ k\wedge \eta \geq \int _ X\chi R\wedge \eta . \]

If moreover

\[ \varlimsup _ k\int _ XR_ k\wedge \omega ^{n-p} \leq \int _ XR\wedge \omega ^{n-p}, \]

then $\chi _ kR_ k\to \chi R$ weakly as currents, and weakly as Radon measures when $p=n$; this holds in particular when $\int _ XR_ k\wedge \omega ^{n-p}\to \int _ XR\wedge \omega ^{n-p}$. The lower inequality also holds locally for finite local products whose potentials are locally uniformly bounded above. By proposition 15.6.7, the hypothesis of extended capacity convergence is satisfied when, for each $j$, the potentials $u_{j,k}$ decrease to $u_ j$, or increase to $u_ j$ almost everywhere.

Proof. Lower inequality. Let $B$ be a coordinate ball such that $\overline B$ lies in a larger coordinate ball, add fixed smooth local potentials of the $\theta _ j$, defined near $\overline B$, and denote the resulting plurisubharmonic functions on $B$ by $v_{j,k}$ and $v_ j$. By lemma 15.6.3, $v_{j,k}\to v_ j$ in extended capacity on $B$, and the $v_{j,k}$ are uniformly bounded above on $B$, because the suprema of the $u_{j,k}$ are bounded above; the local products are finite by theorem 15.4.3. For $C,\varepsilon >0$ let $v_{j,k}^ C$, $v_ j^ C$ and $a_ k$, $a$ be the truncations and cutoff weights of lemma 15.6.4; thus $0\leq a_ k,a\leq 1$, $a$ is quasi-continuous and $a_ k\to a$ locally in capacity. We may assume $A>0$. The bounded Borel weights $\chi _ ka_ k$ converge locally in capacity to the bounded quasi-continuous function $\chi a$, because $|\chi _ ka_ k-\chi a|\leq A|a_ k-a|+|\chi _ k-\chi |$, so that the set where $|\chi _ ka_ k-\chi a|>\delta $ is contained in the union of the sets where $|a_ k-a|>\delta /(2A)$ and where $|\chi _ k-\chi |>\delta /2$. The product $\chi a$ is quasi-continuous, since $\chi $ and $a$ are continuous outside open sets of arbitrarily small capacity and capacity is subadditive.

By lemma 15.6.4(2) and $0\leq a_ k\leq 1$,

\[ \chi _ kR_ k\geq \chi _ ka_ kR_ k =\chi _ ka_ k\bigwedge _{j=1}^ p\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_{j,k}^ C. \]

The functions $v_{j,k}^ C\geq -C$ are uniformly bounded on $B$ and converge to $v_ j^ C$ locally in capacity (lemma 15.6.3). By the weighted capacity continuity of bounded products (theorem 9.4.8), the right side converges weakly to $\chi a\bigwedge _ j\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v_ j^ C=\chi aR$, the last identity by lemma 15.6.4(2). Testing against $g\eta $ for a nonnegative test function $g$ on $B$, we obtain $\int g\chi aR\wedge \eta \leq \varliminf _ k\int g\chi _ kR_ k\wedge \eta $, and we let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and then $C\to \infty $. By lemma 15.6.4(3), the weight $a$ increases to one outside the union of the pole sets, which $R$ does not charge, so monotone convergence gives the local lower inequality, and a finite partition of unity (proposition 7.1.3) gives the global one. The local argument uses only the local upper bounds, the extended capacity convergence and the finiteness of the local products.

Convergence. Under the additional mass hypothesis, theorem 15.6.5 gives $R_ k\to R$ weakly, and in particular $\int _ XR_ k\wedge \eta \to \int _ XR\wedge \eta $. Apply the lower inequality also to the weights $A-\chi _ k$, which converge in capacity to $A-\chi $. Since

\[ \int _ X\chi _ kR_ k\wedge \eta =A\int _ XR_ k\wedge \eta -\int _ X(A-\chi _ k)R_ k\wedge \eta , \]

this gives the matching upper limit, so $\int _ X\chi _ kR_ k\wedge \eta \to \int _ X\chi R\wedge \eta $.

Every smooth real $(n-p,n-p)$-form $\eta $ on $X$ is the difference of two smooth strongly positive forms. Indeed, if $(\rho _ i)$ is a finite partition of unity subordinate to coordinate charts (proposition 7.1.3), then $\rho _ i\eta =\sum _ lf_ le_ l$ with smooth real functions $f_ l$ supported in $\operatorname{Supp}\rho _ i$ and constant strongly positive forms $e_ l$, which span the real forms by proposition 7.2.1. If $\kappa _ i\geq 0$ is a test function of the chart equal to one on $\operatorname{Supp}\rho _ i$ and $c_ l\geq \sup |f_ l|$, then $f_ le_ l=(f_ l+c_ l\kappa _ i)e_ l-c_ l\kappa _ ie_ l$ is a difference of smooth strongly positive forms. Hence the convergence holds for all smooth real forms, and by linearity for all smooth forms, so $\chi _ kR_ k\to \chi R$ weakly as currents. Since $\chi _ k\leq A$ and the trace masses of $R_ k$ are bounded, the coefficient measures of $\chi _ kR_ k$ have bounded total variation; when $p=n$, approximating continuous functions on the compact manifold $X$ uniformly by smooth ones, using a partition of unity and lemma 1.4.1, upgrades the convergence to weak convergence of Radon measures. Finally, for monotone sequences the extended capacity hypothesis follows from proposition 15.6.7, applied to the local potentials. $\square$
