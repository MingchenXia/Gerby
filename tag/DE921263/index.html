Conventions

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Conventions

In the whole book, we adopt the following conventions:

  • A complex space is always assumed to be reduced, paracompact and Hausdorff.

  • A modification of a complex space $X$ is a proper bimeromorphic morphism $\pi \colon Y\rightarrow X$ that is locally obtained from a finite composition of blow-ups with smooth centers.

  • A subnet of a net refers to a Willard subnet. More precisely, if $f\colon I\rightarrow X$ and $f'\colon I'\rightarrow X$ are nets, namely, maps from directed sets $I$ and $I'$ to the same set $X$, we say $f'$ is a subnet of $f$ if there is an increasing final function $h\colon I'\rightarrow I$ such that $f\circ h=f'$.

  • A domain in $\mathbb {R}^ d$ or $\mathbb {C}^ n$ refers to a connected open subset.

  • A complex manifold is assumed to be paracompact.

  • A submanifold of a complex manifold means a closed complex submanifold.

  • A neighborhood is not necessarily open.

  • The set $\mathbb {N}$ of natural numbers includes $0$.

  • Increasing functions and decreasing functions are not necessarily strictly monotone.

  • Positive forms are not necessarily strictly positive.

  • The function $\mathbb {R}\ni x \mapsto x^2$ is convex instead of concave. This is different from the convention in [CLS11].

  • We say a positive measure $\mu $ on a measurable space $X$ is carried by a measurable subset $Y$ if $\mu (X\setminus Y)=0$.

  • $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ means $(2\pi )^{-1}\mathrm{i}\partial \overline{\partial }$.

We also use the following notation throughout the book.

  • The indicator function of a set $E$ is denoted by $\mathbf1_ E$.

  • For subsets $A$ and $B$ of a topological space, $A\Subset B$ means that the closure of $A$ is a compact subset of $B$; we then say that $A$ is relatively compact in $B$.

  • The closure, interior and boundary of a set $E$ in its ambient space are denoted by $\overline E$, $\operatorname{Int}E$ and $\partial E$. The support of a function, measure, distribution or current is denoted by $\operatorname{Supp}$.

  • For an open set $\Omega $ in $\mathbb R^ d$ or in a manifold, $C^ k(\Omega )$ is the space of $k$ times continuously differentiable functions, where $0\leq k\leq \infty $, and $C^ k_ c(\Omega )$ is its subspace of functions with compact support. We write $L^ p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega )$ for the functions whose restrictions to compact subsets of $\Omega $ are $p$-integrable.

  • The imaginary unit is $\mathrm{i}$, and $\operatorname{Re}z$ and $\operatorname{Im}z$ are the real and imaginary parts of a complex number $z$.
