Global potentials and envelopes

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Global potentials and envelopes

On a compact Kähler manifold, a smooth closed real $(1,1)$-form fixes a cohomology class, and a potential changes the current within that class. Local positivity must now be reconciled with this global constraint. Elliptic theory for the scalar Laplacian provides global potentials, and compactness and approximation describe pseudo-effective and big classes, including their boundary behavior. Singular Hermitian metrics express the same positivity in the language of line bundles.

An envelope is the largest admissible potential lying below a given obstacle. When an additional constraint on the singularities is imposed, the envelope selects a canonical representative of that constraint. Its Monge–Ampère measure is carried by the set where the envelope touches the obstacle, but proving this requires local replacement arguments and careful passages to the limit. Relative capacities and integration by parts then lead to the relative energy, to Monge–Ampère equations with prescribed singularities and to Perron segments joining two potentials. As an application we prove in theorem 17.5.3 the identity between ceilings and singularity envelopes of [Theorem 1.2, PSWZ26] in every big class, together with the idempotence of the singularity envelope; the case of zero mass is obtained from positive-mass perturbations together with a uniform estimate of their tails. Finally, convex duality and real Monge–Ampère measures provide the corresponding foundations for invariant potentials on the open torus, and the projective toric manifold of a Delzant polytope, with its sections and the Guillemin metric, provides the ample toric setting.
