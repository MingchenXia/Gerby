Singularity types and envelopes

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Singularity types and envelopes

Multiplier ideals record the holomorphic functions locally square integrable against $\mathrm e^{-ku}$, for every $k>0$. This gives a second equivalence relation on potentials, coarser than ordinary singularity type. To compare the two, fix $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and take envelopes in the same background. The ordinary envelope $P_\theta [u]$ is the upper semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of the $\theta $-plurisubharmonic $h\leq 0$ with $h\leq u+C$ for some constant $C$. The multiplier-ideal envelope $P_\theta [u]_{\mathcal I}$ uses instead the $h\leq 0$ with the same multiplier ideals as $u$ at every positive weight.

In a closed big class on a compact Kähler manifold, for potentials of positive mass, the equality of these two envelopes characterizes $\mathcal I$-good singularities and is equivalent to a mass identity and approximation by analytic singularities. In the Hermitian setting, analytic approximation still relates the envelopes even when a mass identity is unavailable. Correcting the curvature errors keeps the dyadic approximants in a positive Hermitian background, and their envelopes decrease to the multiplier-ideal envelope. In such a positive background, comparing the two envelopes defines a uniform topology on ordinary classes in which their equality characterizes the closure of the analytic classes, including classes with infinite-mass representatives (theorem 16.6.3). This topology can identify distinct ordinary classes.

The obstacle in an envelope is part of this construction. A smooth change $\theta \mapsto \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi $ transforms potentials by $u\mapsto u-\chi $ and transforms their obstacles as well. Keeping only the obstacle zero need not give an invariant topology; allowing all smooth obstacles does. Whether equality of the envelopes at zero implies equality at every smooth obstacle is therefore a substantive question. It holds in a smooth big background with bounded mass when the multiplier-ideal envelope has positive lower volume, as well as on curves and on certain product loci without those volume conditions (proposition 30.8.2, corollary 30.13.2, corollary 16.6.44). The general case at zero volume remains open.

Envelopes and mass give complementary ways to compare singularities. An ordinary model is a fixed point $P_\theta [u]=u$, and the infimum and supremum of mass over an ordinary type define its lower and upper singularity volumes. These volumes need not agree. In a smooth big background with bounded mass, volume comparison gives a completely metrizable topology on the models of positive lower volume. At zero volume, the comparison can fail to distinguish models, so an extension must retain information that mass has lost.

On curves, the polar curvature—the part of $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ carried by the pole set—determines a model. Total variation of this measure gives a complete metric that includes zero-volume models, with the ideal models as the closure of the analytic models. The construction extends to certain higher-dimensional product loci without an ambient mass bound (proposition 16.6.43). A general higher-dimensional model contains more information: residual top-degree mass may lie on a diffuse pole set although every polar curvature current on every modification vanishes. Distinct models can even have the same residual measure and both volumes, while their comparison distance is positive (proposition 16.6.78). Thus a classification by measures already has limitations in closed Kähler backgrounds.

Restriction raises a related question. A potential may be identically $-\infty $ on a submanifold even though its generic Lelong number there is zero. In this case, the restrictions of its ambient multiplier ideals define finite divisorial data on every modification. Trace envelopes turn this data into constraints on potentials of the submanifold. Exact realization is possible on curves and in sufficiently large backgrounds on Kähler targets, and projective pullback data also admit exact realizations on Hopf manifolds of unbounded mass (theorem 19.8.2). Thus failure of a mass bound does not itself prevent realization. Exact divisorial data nevertheless need not determine the Monge–Ampère mass, or a single ordinary type.
