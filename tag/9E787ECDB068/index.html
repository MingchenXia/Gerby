Fine topology and balayage · Tag 9E787ECDB068

Fine positive measure

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Proposition 5.1.4

Proposition 5.1.4. Let $N$ be a fine neighborhood of a point $x\in \Omega $. Then every Euclidean ball centered at $x$ contains a Borel subset of $N$ of positive Lebesgue measure. In particular, the fine topology has no isolated points.

Proof. By proposition 5.1.3, $N$ contains a set $\{ y\in V:u(y)>a\} $ with $u\in \mathrm{SH}(V)$ and $u(x)>a$. It suffices to consider balls $B(x,r)$ with $\overline{B(x,r)}\subset V$. The set $E=\{ y\in B(x,r):u(y)>a\} $ is a Borel subset of $N$, because $u$ is Borel measurable. Let $M$ be a finite upper bound for $u$ on $\overline{B(x,r)}$; then $M\geq u(x)>a$. Since $u\leq M$ on $E$ and $u\leq a$ on $B(x,r)\setminus E$, the ball submean inequality of theorem 3.1.3 gives

\[ u(x)\leq \frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int _{B(x,r)}u(y)\, \mathrm d y\leq a+(M-a)\frac{|E|}{|B(x,r)|}. \]

Hence $|E|\geq |B(x,r)|\, (u(x)-a)/(M-a)>0$. As a singleton has Lebesgue measure zero, it is not a fine neighborhood of its point. $\square$
