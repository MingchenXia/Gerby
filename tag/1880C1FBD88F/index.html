Proposition 4.1.6

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · Proposition 4.1.6

Proposition 4.1.6. Let $\varphi _0,\varphi _1\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$ be a subgeodesic from $\varphi _0$ to $\varphi _1$. Then for each $x\in X$, $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is a convex function. Furthermore,

\[ \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ),\quad \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t\leq \varphi _0\land \varphi _1. \]

Proof. Let $\Phi $ be the complexification of $(\varphi _ t)_{t\in (0,1)}$.

For each $x\in X$, the map

\[ \Bigl \{ z\in \mathbb {C}: \operatorname{Re}z\in (0,1) \Bigr \} \rightarrow [-\infty ,\infty ),\quad z\mapsto \Phi (x,z) \]

is either subharmonic or constantly $-\infty $, as follows from definition 4.1.1 1 and proposition 1.1.13. In the latter case, the convexity of $[0,1] \ni t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is trivial. In the former case, the convexity on the interval $(0,1)$ follows from proposition 1.1.9.

In order to verify the convexity at the boundary, let us fix $s\in (0,1)$. We need to show that

4.1
\begin{equation} \label{eq:varphisconvextemp1} \varphi _ s(x)\leq s\varphi _1(x)+(1-s)\varphi _0(x) \end{equation}

for all $x\in X$. Thanks to proposition 1.2.6, it suffices to prove this for almost all $x$.

Choose sequences $a_ j\searrow 0$ and $b_ j\nearrow 1$ such that $\varphi _{a_ j}\to \varphi _0$ and $\varphi _{b_ j}\to \varphi _1$ almost everywhere. Outside a set $Z\subseteq X$ of zero Lebesgue measure, all these convergences hold and all the values involved are finite. For $x\in X\setminus Z$ and $j$ large enough that $a_ j<s<b_ j$, the convexity on $(0,1)$ gives

\[ \varphi _ s(x)\leq \frac{b_ j-s}{b_ j-a_ j}\varphi _{a_ j}(x)+\frac{s-a_ j}{b_ j-a_ j}\varphi _{b_ j}(x). \]

Letting $j\to \infty $ gives (4.1) almost everywhere.

Let us prove the last assertion. Let

\[ \varphi \coloneqq \inf _{t\in (0,1)}\varphi _ t. \]

By Kiselman's principle proposition 1.2.11, we know that $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} $. Since the complexification $\Phi $ is not identically $-\infty $, Fubini's theorem shows that for almost every $x\in X$, the function $t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is finite for almost every $t\in (0,1)$. For such an $x$, the convexity just proved implies that $t\mapsto \varphi _ t(x)$ is a finite convex function on $(0,1)$, hence it is bounded from below on $(0,1)$. Thus $\varphi \not\equiv -\infty $, and so $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

For any $t\in (0,1)$, using the convexity established above, we have

\[ \varphi \leq (1-t)\varphi _0+t\varphi _1. \]

It follows that $\varphi \leq \varphi _0$ and $\varphi \leq \varphi _1$ almost everywhere and hence everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. Our assertion follows. $\square$
