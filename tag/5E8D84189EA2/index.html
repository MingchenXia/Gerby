Lelong numbers and multiplier ideals · Tag 5E8D84189EA2

Divisorial slice bound

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Lemma 12.6.6

Lemma 12.6.6. Let $\psi $ be a weight on a complex manifold $Y$, let $E\subset Y$ be a prime divisor, let $p\in E_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and let $h$ be holomorphic near $p$ with $|h|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi }$ integrable near $p$. If $k$ is the order of $h$ along $E$ at $p$, then $k+1>\nu (\psi ,E)$, where $\nu (\psi ,E)$ is the generic Lelong number of proposition 12.3.5.

Proof. We may assume $k<\infty $. Choose coordinates $w=(w_1,w')$ at $p$ with $E=\{ w_1=0\} $, and write $h=w_1^ kv$ with $v$ holomorphic and not divisible by $w_1$; then $v(0,\cdot )$ does not vanish identically near $p$, by lemma 12.6.1(2). Moving $p$ slightly along $E$, we may assume that $v(p)\neq 0$, so that $|v|\geq \delta >0$ on a neighborhood $\{ |w_1|<\rho \} \times W'$ of $p$ on which $|h|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi }$ is integrable. By Fubini's theorem, for almost every $w'\in W'$ the function $w_1\mapsto |w_1|^{2k}|v|^2\mathrm e^{-\psi (w_1,w')}$ is integrable on $|w_1|<\rho $; moreover, for almost every $w'$ the function $u=\psi (\cdot ,w')$ is not identically $-\infty $ on this disc, since the pole set of $\psi $ is a null set.

Fix such a $w'$. By proposition 8.2.3, $u$ is subharmonic on the disc, and its Lelong number at $0$ is at least $\nu (\psi ,(0,w'))$: restricting the bound $\psi (w)\leq c\log \bigl (|w-(0,w')|^2/r_0^2\bigr )+C$ of (12.2), with $c=\nu (\psi ,(0,w'))$, to the line through $(0,w')$ in the $w_1$-direction gives $u(w_1)\leq c\log (|w_1|^2/r_0^2)+C$. Moreover $\nu (\psi ,(0,w'))\geq \nu (\psi ,E)$, the latter being an infimum over $E$. Hence, by (12.2) for $u$, there are $r_1\in (0,\rho ]$ and $c'>0$ with $\mathrm e^{-u(w_1)}\geq c'|w_1|^{-2\nu (\psi ,E)}$ for $0<|w_1|<r_1$. Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \infty & >\int _{|w_1|<r_1}|w_1|^{2k}|v|^2\mathrm e^{-u}\, \mathrm dV_2 \geq \delta ^2c'\int _{|w_1|<r_1}|w_1|^{2k-2\nu (\psi ,E)}\, \mathrm dV_2\\ & =2\pi \delta ^2c'\int _0^{r_1}t^{2k-2\nu (\psi ,E)+1}\, \mathrm dt, \end{aligned} \]

which forces $2k-2\nu (\psi ,E)+2>0$, that is, $k+1>\nu (\psi ,E)$. $\square$
