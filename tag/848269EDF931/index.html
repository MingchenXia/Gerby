Partial envelopes on a weighted compact set

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.3 Partial envelopes on a weighted compact set

Two constraints act on limits of partial Fubini–Study potentials. The continuous weight is an obstacle on the set where sections are measured, and the selecting potential imposes a multiplier-ideal constraint on the whole manifold. The partial envelope is the largest potential satisfying both. Since an obstacle on a non-pluripolar compact set bounds every competitor globally, the partial envelope lies below a translate of the multiplier-ideal envelope of the selecting potential; conversely, a translate of that envelope lying below the obstacle is a competitor. The partial envelope therefore differs from the multiplier-ideal envelope by a bounded function.

Let $\theta $ be a smooth real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $w\in C(K,\mathbb R)$. Recall that theorem 1.16.3 characterizes the order $\preceq _{\mathcal I}$ by Lelong numbers on local modifications. As in section 1.19, an inequality holds quasi-everywhere if it holds outside a pluripolar set. The partial envelope of $\varphi $ with obstacle $w$ on $K$ is

27.4
\begin{equation} \label{eq:hermitian-partial-i-envelope} \begin{aligned} E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w) & =\Bigl (\sup \bigl \{ a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ): a\preceq _{\mathcal I}\varphi ,\\ & \hspace{52pt}a\leq w\text{ quasi-everywhere on }K\bigr \} \Bigr )^*. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

Theorem 27.3.1. The function $E=E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and it is the largest member of its defining family. Let $q=P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}$, and let $C$ be a constant as in lemma 1.19.1, so that $\sup _ Xa\leq C$ for every $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $a\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$. Then

\[ q+\min _ Kw\leq E\leq q+C. \]

In particular, $E\sim q$ and

\[ E\sim _{\mathcal I}\varphi . \]

Proof. By theorem 16.2.2, $q\leq 0$ and $q\sim _{\mathcal I}\varphi $. Hence $q+\min _ Kw$ belongs to the defining family. Lemma 1.19.1 bounds the entire family above by $C$, so its regularized supremum $E$ is $\theta $-plurisubharmonic, and $E\geq q+\min _ Kw$.

For the upper bound, let $a$ be a member of the family. Since $\sup _ Xa\leq C$, proposition 16.2.4, proposition 16.2.6 give

\[ a-C\leq a-\sup _ Xa\leq P_\theta [a]_{\mathcal I} \leq P_\theta [\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}=q. \]

Taking the regularized supremum over $a$ proves $E\leq q+C$.

The two bounds show that $E-q$ is bounded. Hence $E$ and $q$ have the same multiplier ideals, and these are the multiplier ideals of $\varphi $.

It remains to show that $E$ itself satisfies the obstacle condition, which is not automatic after regularization. By Choquet's lemma, $E$ is the regularization of the supremum of a countable subfamily. Replace its members by their successive finite maxima. Finite maxima are again members of the family: the multiplier-ideal order is preserved by theorem 1.16.3, and a finite union of pluripolar exceptional sets is pluripolar. We obtain an increasing sequence $a_ j$ in the family whose limit has regularization $E$. Outside a countable union of pluripolar subsets of $K$, every $a_ j$ is at most $w$, and hence so is $\lim _ ja_ j$. By the local upper-envelope theorem recalled in section 1.19, $E$ differs from $\lim _ ja_ j$ only on a pluripolar set. Thus $E\leq w$ quasi-everywhere on $K$.

We have shown that $E$ belongs to its defining family. It dominates every member by construction, so it is the largest member. In particular, the supremum in (27.4) equals $E$ at every point even before regularization. $\square$

Proposition 27.3.2. The partial envelope depends only on the multiplier-ideal class of $\varphi $, and it is increasing in the obstacle and in $\varphi $ with respect to $\preceq _{\mathcal I}$. For $w,w'\in C(K)$ and $c\in \mathbb R$,

\[ \begin{split} E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w+c) & =E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)+c,\\ \bigl |E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w) -E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w')\bigr | & \leq \| w-w'\| _{C(K)}. \end{split} \]

The second line stands for the two corresponding pointwise inequalities, which make sense also at the poles. Let $\theta _0,\theta _1$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$, and let $\varphi _ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ i)$ and $w_ i\in C(K)$ for $i=0,1$. For $0<t<1$ put $\theta _ t=(1-t)\theta _0+t\theta _1$, and define $\varphi _ t$ and $w_ t$ in the same way. Then

\[ (1-t)E_{\theta _0,K}[\varphi _0]_{\mathcal I}(w_0) +tE_{\theta _1,K}[\varphi _1]_{\mathcal I}(w_1) \leq E_{\theta _ t,K}[\varphi _ t]_{\mathcal I}(w_ t). \]

Finally, for a smooth real function $f$, changing the background and the obstacle gives the identity

\[ E_{\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f,K}[\varphi -f]_{\mathcal I}(w-f|_ K) =E_{\theta ,K}[\varphi ]_{\mathcal I}(w)-f. \]

Proof. The first two assertions hold because the defining families are equal or nested. Adding a constant $c$ is a bijection between the families for $w$ and $w+c$, which proves the translation rule. Applying monotonicity and translation to $w'-\| w-w'\| _{C(K)}\leq w\leq w'+\| w-w'\| _{C(K)}$ gives the two inequalities in the second line.

For concavity, the left-hand side is $\theta _ t$-plurisubharmonic, and it lies below $w_ t$ outside the union of two pluripolar exceptional sets. By theorem 27.3.1, its two summands have the multiplier-ideal classes of $\varphi _0$ and $\varphi _1$, so the addition and scaling rules of lemma 1.16.4 show that the left-hand side has the multiplier-ideal class of $\varphi _ t$. It is therefore a member of the family defining the right-hand side. Finally, $a\mapsto a-f$ is a bijection between the two families in the last identity, and regularization commutes with subtraction of a continuous function. $\square$

In the next section the partial envelope bounds every limit of partial Fubini–Study potentials. The point is to show that both constraints, the multiplier-ideal order and the obstacle on $K$, survive $L^1$ limits.
