Cone angular radial cancellation

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Example A.6.3

Example A.6.3. Without the assumption $\Psi =0$, a nonconstant $k$ does not force a nonconstant solution. Take $\Psi =\log 2$ and $k(s)=2/(2-s)^{n+1}$. Then $s=2x/(1+x)$, $\ell =\log (1+x)$ and

\[ \frac{B_\psi ^ n}{\beta ^ n}=\frac{s^ n(1-s)}{x^ n(1-x)}=\frac{(2-s)^{n+1}}2. \]

Thus $\nu =\beta ^ n$. Since $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} s}(s/(2-s))^ n=nk(s)s^{n-1}$, one has $I=1/n$, $M=d$ and $c_ b=1$. The formulas (A.19) give $R=1/(2-s)$ and $Q(s)=\log (2-s)$, hence $U=\log 2$. The normalized solution is zero, although $k$ is nonconstant.
