Preface

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Preface

[Introduction]

A plurisubharmonic function is the several-variable analogue of a subharmonic function: an upper semicontinuous function whose restriction to every complex line is subharmonic. Such functions arise as local potentials of closed positive currents, as weights of singular Hermitian metrics on line bundles and as solutions of complex Monge–Ampère equations. They may take the value $-\infty $, and much of the geometric information they carry is concentrated in their singularities. Pluripotential theory studies these functions, the nonlinear measures they define, and the ways in which their singularities can be measured and compared.

This book develops the foundations of the theory from the beginning. It is written as a companion to Part I of Singularities in global pluripotential theory [XiaSampleBook], and its purpose is to give complete proofs of the analytic results on which that text relies. We start from classical potential theory in $\mathbb R^ d$ and end with the machinery behind non-pluripolar products, singularity envelopes, relative energy and the ample toric setting. The chapters are arranged in the order in which the proofs depend on one another: weighted holomorphic estimates come before multiplier ideals, regularization comes before the study of global positive classes, and the Monge–Ampère operator on bounded functions comes before any product involving unbounded potentials.
