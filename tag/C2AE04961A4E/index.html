Big backgrounds under bounded mass

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 32 Big backgrounds under bounded mass

Let $X$ be a connected compact complex manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, let $\theta $ be a smooth big real $(1,1)$-form on $X$, not necessarily closed or semipositive, and let $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$. As in chapter 31, we solve the equations $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u}\mu $, $\lambda >0$, and $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (u)=c\mu $, $c>0$, for an arbitrary nonzero finite non-pluripolar measure $\mu $, with $u$ in the relative class of $\varphi $. We replace the smaller-background model condition used there by upper and positive lower mass bounds.

Throughout this chapter we make two standing assumptions. First, $X$ has the bounded mass property of definition 6.5.1: $v_+(\omega )<+\infty $ for one, and hence every, Hermitian form $\omega $ on $X$ (proposition 6.5.2). Second, $\varphi $ satisfies (30.10): it is an ordinary model, $P_\theta [\varphi ]=\varphi $ (definition 1.19.4), and its lower singularity volume is positive. Recall from definition 13.3.1 that the lower and upper volumes $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ and $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)$ are the infimum and the supremum of $\int _ X\mathrm{MA}_\theta (v)$ over all $v\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ with $v\sim u$. Fix a Hermitian form $\omega $ for capacity and a smooth positive volume form $\mathrm{d}V$.

The two assumptions give the two bounds needed for approximation: every member of the relative class of $\varphi $ has Monge–Ampère mass at least $\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )>0$ and at most $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )<\infty $. With these bounds, first moments relative to a reference prevent collapse and keep limits in the relative class, while higher moments control convergence against a smoothed measure. This first gives solutions in the class of the potential $V_\theta $ with minimal singularities.

To pass to the reference $\varphi $, we project a solution in the relative class of $V_\theta $ onto the relative class of $\varphi $. The projection keeps only part of the exponentially weighted measure, but weighted addition can fill any remaining deficit; maximizing the weighted mass therefore gives the required equation. The same contact estimates keep limits in the relative class when the model reference varies in the volume comparison of section 30.6.

For general finite non-pluripolar data the solutions lie in the relative full-mass class; for $L^ p$ densities, a stronger moment estimate shows that they differ from $\varphi $ by a bounded function. A positive exponent determines the solution uniquely; at exponent zero only the scalar is unique.

For $a\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$, write

\[ \mathcal E_\varphi =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ) =\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi ), \qquad \theta _ a=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a,\qquad \mu _ a=\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a). \]

Here $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{vol}}(X,\theta ,\varphi )$ is the relative full-mass class of definition 30.2.3, consisting of the $u\preceq \varphi $ with $\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (u)=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )$; $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(X,\theta ,\varphi )$ is the relative envelope class of definition 13.4.1; and the two classes coincide by theorem 30.2.4. As in definition 1.12.1, $u\preceq v$ means $u\leq v+C$ for a constant $C$, and $u\sim v$ means $u\preceq v\preceq u$. The measure $\mathrm{MA}_\theta (a)$ is the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure of theorem 6.1.2; it is finite by proposition 6.5.3. Put

\[ \ell =\underline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )>0, \qquad M=\overline{\operatorname {vol}}_\theta (\varphi )<\infty . \]

By theorem 30.2.4, every $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $ satisfies

32.1
\begin{equation} \label{eq:big-relative-mass-interval} \ell \leq \mu _ u(X)\leq M. \end{equation}

We use the stretching envelopes $Q_ b(u,w)=P_\theta (bu-(b-1)w)$ introduced before definition 13.4.1, the rooftop envelopes $P_\theta (u_1,\ldots ,u_ N)=P_\theta (\min _ iu_ i)$, and $V_\theta =P_\theta (0)$ (section 1.19). For $a\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, proposition 13.4.5 gives $P_\theta [a]=\varphi $, and hence, as in (31.2),

\[ a-\sup _ Xa\leq \varphi . \]

We refer to this inequality as the envelope identity. Finally, zero domination refers to theorem 30.3.1 with coefficient zero: if $u\in \mathcal E_\varphi $, $v\preceq \varphi $ and $\mu _ u(\{ u<v\} )=0$, then $u\geq v$.
