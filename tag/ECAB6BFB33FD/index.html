Radial positive volume nonbig

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Theorem 26.13.2

Theorem 26.13.2. The background $\theta $ on $X$ is smooth, semipositive, nef and nonclosed. It is not big, but

26.84
\begin{equation} \label{eq:radial-positive-volume-nonbig} \lambda _\mu (\theta )=n^{1/n},\qquad \mathcal V_\mu (\theta )=n. \end{equation}

For $\theta _\epsilon =\omega +(a+\epsilon )w$, one has

\[ \mathcal V_\mu (\theta _\epsilon )=\frac n{H_\epsilon }, \]

and the smooth positive metrics

26.85
\begin{equation} \label{eq:radial-volume-minimizing-metrics} \gamma _\epsilon =(a+\epsilon )^{-1/m} \bigl (A_\epsilon +(a+\epsilon )w\bigr ) \end{equation}

are balanced and minimize the defining quotient at $\theta _\epsilon $.

Proof. The coefficient $a$ is nonnegative, and $\theta +\eta h>0$ for every $\eta >0$; this proves semipositivity and nefness. Since $a$ is nonconstant, $\mathrm{d}\theta =\mathrm{d}a\wedge w\ne 0$.

Suppose that $\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\geq \delta h$ for a quasi-plurisubharmonic function $v$ and some $\delta >0$. Choose a nonzero smooth function $f\geq 0$ on $B$ supported in $\{ a<\delta \} $. Its pullback $S=f\omega ^ m$ is a smooth semipositive $(m,m)$-form, closed because it has top degree on the base. Positivity and distributional Stokes would give

\[ 0\leq \int _ X(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v-\delta h)\wedge S =\int _ B f(a-\delta )\omega ^ m<0. \]

This contradiction shows that $\theta $ is not big, since every Hermitian form on $X$ dominates a positive multiple of $h$.

For the lower bound on the volume, pull back the bounded potential $\varphi $ of lemma 26.13.1. The positive current $\Omega =A+a w=\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi $ is a smooth strictly positive form off $Z=\{ p\} \times \mathbb P^1$, where

\[ \Omega ^ n=n a A^ m\wedge w=n\mu . \]

Let $\gamma $ be a Gauduchon metric. Since $\varphi $ is bounded, distributional Stokes lets us replace $\theta $ by $\Omega $ in the numerator of its quotient. The set $Z$ has zero smooth volume and $\Omega \wedge \gamma ^{n-1}$ is a positive measure, so the pointwise arithmetic–geometric mean inequality off $Z$ gives

\[ \int _ X\theta \wedge \gamma ^{n-1} \geq n^{1/n}\int _ X (\mu /\gamma ^ n)^{1/n}\gamma ^ n =n^{1/n}D_\mu (\gamma ). \]

This lower bound holds for every test in definition 26.1.1; no power of the current $\Omega $ along $Z$ is needed.

For the upper bound, put $c_\epsilon =n/H_\epsilon $ and $\Omega _\epsilon =A_\epsilon +(a+\epsilon )w=\theta _\epsilon +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi _\epsilon $. Then $\Omega _\epsilon ^ n=c_\epsilon \mu $. The metrics in (26.85) satisfy

\[ T_\epsilon =\gamma _\epsilon ^ m =(a+\epsilon )^{-1}A_\epsilon ^ m +m A_\epsilon ^{m-1}\wedge w. \]

The first term has top degree on the base and the second is a product of closed forms, so $T_\epsilon $ is closed; that is, $\gamma _\epsilon $ is balanced, and in particular Gauduchon. Smooth Stokes and (26.83) give

\[ \int _ X\theta _\epsilon \wedge T_\epsilon =c_\epsilon \int _ X(a+\epsilon )^{-1}\mu =n, \qquad D_\mu (\gamma _\epsilon )=c_\epsilon ^{m/n}H_\epsilon , \]

so the quotient of $\gamma _\epsilon $ at $\theta _\epsilon $ equals $c_\epsilon ^{1/n}$. The same arithmetic–geometric mean argument, applied to the smooth representative $\Omega _\epsilon $, bounds every Gauduchon quotient at $\theta _\epsilon $ below by $c_\epsilon ^{1/n}$. This proves that $\gamma _\epsilon $ minimizes, and gives the formula for $\mathcal V_\mu (\theta _\epsilon )$. Since $\theta \leq \theta _\epsilon $, the metrics $\gamma _\epsilon $, used as tests for $\theta $, also give $\lambda _\mu (\theta )\leq c_\epsilon ^{1/n}$. Letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ proves (26.84). By theorem 26.10.1, this value is also the limit of the volumes of the Hermitian perturbations $\theta +\epsilon h$. $\square$
