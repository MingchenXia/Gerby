Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag 1B6E50A3B70B

General auxiliary all obstacle criterion

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Corollary 16.6.44

Corollary 16.6.44. For the arbitrary curve potentials $u_ i$ of the lemma, put $R=\Psi +\sum _ ir_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ p$. The following are equivalent: $P_{\Theta _\alpha }[U]=P_{\Theta _\alpha }[U]_{\mathcal I}$; for each $i$, the polar part of the curvature measure of $u_ i$ equals its atomic part; $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta _\alpha ,R)$; and the ordinary and ideal envelopes of $U$ agree below every smooth real obstacle on $M$.

Proof. By the lemma and the normalization of the coordinates, equality of the envelopes below zero is equivalent to $q_ i=r_ i$ for every $i$. By corollary 30.13.2, this is equivalent to equality of the polar and atomic measures, and in that case $u_ i\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(C_ i,\sigma _ i,r_ i)$. Choose stretching competitors in these curve classes. The sum of their pullbacks plus $\Psi $ has positive current, since the contributions of the curves, the form $\varpi ^*\alpha $ and the current of the divisor are all positive. Adding the convex stretching inequalities, which is possible also at poles, gives a stretching competitor relative to $R$ for every finite stretch, and adding the ordinary bounds gives $U\preceq R$. Thus $U\in \mathcal E_{\mathrm{env}}(M,\Theta _\alpha ,R)$, and proposition 16.6.8 gives equality below every smooth obstacle. Taking the zero obstacle recovers the first condition, which proves the equivalence. $\square$
