Several curve factors

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.6.8 Several curve factors

A single curve factor produces a model space isometric to the space of models on one curve. Products of several curves give larger complete model spaces at zero volume. The envelopes of a sum of curve models are again computed coordinatewise: the supremum of a competitor over all factors but one is a potential on the remaining curve, and restriction to slices treats the other factors by induction. The sum of the polar distances of the factors is then a complete metric on the induced product models.

Let $C_1,\ldots ,C_ m$ be connected compact curves with smooth positive forms $\eta _ i$, where $m\geq 2$, and put $A_ i=\int _{C_ i}\eta _ i$. Set

\[ Y=\prod _{i=1}^ m C_ i,\qquad \beta =\sum _{i=1}^ m\pi _ i^*\eta _ i, \]

where $\pi _ i:Y\to C_ i$ are the projections. For normalized ordinary models $q_ i$ on $(C_ i,\eta _ i)$, write

\[ w=\sum _{i=1}^ m q_ i\circ \pi _ i,\qquad q_{i,\mathcal I}=P_{\eta _ i}[q_ i]_{\mathcal I}. \]

Lemma 16.6.29. The function $w$ is a normalized ordinary model on $(Y,\beta )$, and

\[ P_\beta [w]_{\mathcal I} =\sum _{i=1}^ m q_{i,\mathcal I}\circ \pi _ i. \]

Proof. The function $w$ is $\beta $-plurisubharmonic and has supremum zero. We prove $P_\beta [w]=w$ by induction on the number of factors, the case of one factor being the identity $P_{\eta _1}[q_1]=q_1$. Let $v\leq 0$ be an ordinary competitor, and replace it by $\max (v,w)$. This preserves $v\preceq w$ and ensures that the restrictions of $v$ to slices are not identically $-\infty $ wherever the corresponding curve potentials are finite.

Write $Y=Y'\times C_ m$, and take the regularized partial supremum

\[ a(t)=\left(\sup _{y'\in Y'}v(y',t)\right)^*. \]

The restrictions of $v$ to the slices $\{ y'\} \times C_ m$ are $\eta _ m$-plurisubharmonic or identically $-\infty $. They are bounded above by zero, and at least one of them is not identically $-\infty $, since $v\geq w$. The local upper-envelope theorem from chapter 1 therefore makes $a$ an $\eta _ m$-plurisubharmonic function. The global inequality $v\leq w+C$ gives $a\leq q_ m+C$, because the other normalized potentials have supremum zero. Thus $a$ is a competitor for $P_{\eta _ m}[q_ m]$, and $a\leq P_{\eta _ m}[q_ m]=q_ m$.

For $t$ with $q_ m(t)$ finite, the function $v(\cdot ,t)-q_ m(t)$ is a potential in the product background on $Y'$, not identically $-\infty $; it is nonpositive and at least as singular as $\sum _{i<m}q_ i\circ \pi _ i$. The induction hypothesis bounds it by that sum. Hence $v\leq w$ off the poles of $q_ m$. At those poles the original bound $v\leq w+C$ gives the same inequality. Taking the envelope proves the ordinary identity.

For the ideal identity, let $E_ i$ be the atomic part of the polar curvature of $q_ i$, and take the Green potentials $g_ i$ of lemma 19.5.2. They satisfy

\[ q_ i\sim _{\mathcal I}g_ i,\qquad q_{i,\mathcal I}\sim g_ i,\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_ i=E_ i-\frac{E_ i(C_ i)}{A_ i}\eta _ i. \]

By lemma 1.16.6, lemma 1.16.4, $g=\sum _ i g_ i\circ \pi _ i$ is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $w$. Its complex Hessian is a closed countable sum of the divisors $\pi _ i^{-1}(x)$ minus the smooth form $\sum _ i E_ i(C_ i)\pi _ i^*\eta _ i/A_ i$. Each coordinate fiber has constant mass for the product metric, so the divisor part has finite mass. Moreover $g$ is $\beta $-plurisubharmonic since $E_ i(C_ i)\leq A_ i$. Therefore corollary 16.2.8 gives $P_\beta [g]_{\mathcal I}=P_\beta [g]$. Since $g$ is ordinarily equivalent to $\sum _ iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ \pi _ i$, the ordinary identity, applied to the models $q_{i,\mathcal I}$, shows that this envelope is $\sum _ iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ \pi _ i$. As $w\sim _{\mathcal I}g$, the ideal envelope of $w$ is the same, which proves the assertion. $\square$

The same coordinatewise computation applies to arbitrary curve potentials, to curve backgrounds that need not be positive, and to smooth obstacles that are sums of functions of the separate coordinates. Keep the curves $C_ i$, the product $Y$ and its projections $\pi _ i$. Let $\sigma _ i$ be smooth real $(1,1)$-forms on $C_ i$ with $\mathrm{PSH}(C_ i,\sigma _ i)\ne \varnothing $, that is, with $\int _{C_ i}\sigma _ i\geq 0$ by lemma 1.8.14, and choose $u_ i\in \mathrm{PSH}(C_ i,\sigma _ i)$ and $F_ i\in C^\infty (C_ i,\mathbb R)$. Put

\[ \gamma =\sum _ i\pi _ i^*\sigma _ i,\qquad u=\sum _ i u_ i\circ \pi _ i,\qquad F=\sum _ i F_ i\circ \pi _ i. \]

Proposition 16.6.30. For the preceding data,

16.27
\begin{equation} \label{eq:separated-curve-obstacle-projections} \begin{aligned} P_{\gamma ,F}[u] & =\sum _ i P_{\sigma _ i,F_ i}[u_ i]\circ \pi _ i,\\ P_{\gamma ,F}[u]_{\mathcal I} & =\sum _ i P_{\sigma _ i,F_ i}[u_ i]_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi _ i. \end{aligned} \end{equation}

These identities also hold when some of the curve backgrounds have zero area.

Proof. Write $e_ i=P_{\sigma _ i,F_ i}[u_ i]$. Sums of ordinary competitors in the separate coordinates are ordinary competitors on the product, and their regularized supremum is $\sum _ i e_ i\circ \pi _ i$. This gives the lower bound in the first identity.

For the upper bound we use induction on the number of factors, the case of one factor being trivial. Let $v\leq F$ be an ordinary competitor, so that $v\leq u+C$ for some constant $C$. Choose a constant $K$ with $u-K\leq F$ and replace $v$ by $\max (v,u-K)$. This preserves both bounds and ensures that the restrictions of $v$ to the slices used below are not identically $-\infty $.

Write $Y=Y'\times C_ m$, and on $Y'$ put $u'=\sum _{i<m}u_ i\circ \pi _ i$ and $F'=\sum _{i<m}F_ i\circ \pi _ i$. The regularized partial supremum

\[ a(t)=\left(\sup _{y'\in Y'}\bigl (v(y',t)-F'(y')\bigr )\right)^* \]

is $\sigma _ m$-plurisubharmonic by the local upper-envelope theorem of chapter 1: the functions $t\mapsto v(y',t)-F'(y')$ have the common upper bound $F_ m$, and at least one of them is not identically $-\infty $, by the lower bound $v\geq u-K$. The bound $v\leq u+C$ gives

\[ a\leq u_ m+C+\sup _{Y'}(u'-F'),\qquad a\leq F_ m. \]

Thus $a$ is a competitor for $e_ m$, and $a\leq e_ m$. If $u_ m(t)$ is finite, so is $e_ m(t)$, since a sufficiently low translate of $u_ m$ is a competitor for $e_ m$. The function $v(\cdot ,t)-e_ m(t)$ is then a potential in the product background on $Y'$, not identically $-\infty $, lying below $F'$ and at least as singular as $u'$. By induction, $v(\cdot ,t)\leq \sum _{i<m}e_ i\circ \pi _ i+e_ m(t)$. At a pole of $u_ m$, the bound $v\leq u+C$ already gives $v=-\infty $. Every competitor is therefore bounded by $\sum _ i e_ i\circ \pi _ i$, and taking the regularized supremum proves the ordinary identity.

For the ideal identity, put $B_ i=\int _{C_ i}\sigma _ i\geq 0$ and $\widehat\eta _ i=\eta _ i/A_ i$. By lemma 1.8.14, choose smooth functions $\chi _ i$ with

\[ \sigma _ i=B_ i\widehat\eta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ i. \]

Let $E_ i$ be the atomic part of $\sigma _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$. If $B_ i>0$, apply lemma 19.5.2 to $u_ i+\chi _ i$ in the positive background $B_ i\widehat\eta _ i$. Subtracting $\chi _ i$ from the resulting atomic Green potential gives a $\sigma _ i$-plurisubharmonic function $g_ i$ such that

\[ \begin{gathered} u_ i\sim _{\mathcal I}g_ i,\qquad P_{\sigma _ i}[u_ i]_{\mathcal I}\sim g_ i,\\ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_ i=E_ i-E_ i(C_ i)\widehat\eta _ i-\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi _ i; \end{gathered} \]

the second relation uses the smooth gauge bounds of proposition 16.3.1. If $B_ i=0$, lemma 1.8.14 gives $u_ i=-\chi _ i+c_ i$ for a constant $c_ i$, and $E_ i=0$; taking $g_ i=-\chi _ i$ gives the same conclusions. In either case,

\[ \sigma _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}g_ i =E_ i+(B_ i-E_ i(C_ i))\widehat\eta _ i\geq 0. \]

Put $g=\sum _ i g_ i\circ \pi _ i$. Pullback and addition, lemma 1.16.6, lemma 1.16.4, give $g\sim _{\mathcal I}u$. The complex Hessian of $g$ is a countable sum of divisor currents supported on coordinate fibers, minus a smooth form. This divisor current has finite mass, because each coordinate fiber has constant mass for a fixed product metric and each $E_ i$ has finite mass. Thus lemma 1.17.4 applies, even when some $B_ i$ vanish. Every ideal competitor $v\leq F$ for $u$ therefore satisfies $v\preceq g$, and is bounded by $P_{\gamma ,F}[g]$.

Set $r_ i=P_{\sigma _ i,F_ i}[u_ i]_{\mathcal I}$. Invariance of the ordinary envelope under ordinary equivalence and proposition 16.6.8 give

\[ P_{\sigma _ i,F_ i}[g_ i] =P_{\sigma _ i,F_ i}[P_{\sigma _ i}[u_ i]_{\mathcal I}] =r_ i. \]

By the ordinary identity already proved, applied to $g$, the ideal envelope of $u$ is therefore at most $\sum _ i r_ i\circ \pi _ i$. Conversely, each $r_ i$ lies below $F_ i$ and is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $u_ i$, again by proposition 16.6.8. Their sum is a $\gamma $-plurisubharmonic ideal competitor for $u$ below $F$, which proves the reverse inequality. No strict positivity of the backgrounds is needed in this comparison. $\square$

The proof of (16.27) uses that the obstacle is a sum of functions of the separate coordinates; for an arbitrary smooth obstacle on $Y$, the coordinatewise bounds are not available. Equality of the two envelopes below such mixed obstacles is treated in 16.6.11.

We now return to the positive product background $\beta $ and the normalized curve models $q_ i$, and add a divisorial factor as in 16.6.7. Let $Z$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $k\geq 1$, and let $D\subset Z$ be a nonempty smooth reduced divisor, with a Kähler form $\kappa $ and a normalized potential $\psi $ satisfying $\kappa +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\psi =[D]$. On $M=Y\times Z$, write $p,r$ for the projections, set $h_ i=\pi _ i\circ p$, and put

\[ \Theta =p^*\beta +r^*\kappa ,\qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r,\qquad \Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m)=\Psi +\sum _ i q_ i\circ h_ i. \]

Let $\mathcal S_\Psi ^{(m)}$ be the set of these potentials. For the polar curvatures $\mu _ i=\mathbf1_{\{ q_ i=-\infty \} }(\eta _ i+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}q_ i)$ and the analogous measures $\nu _ i$ of models $b_ i$, define

16.30
\begin{equation} \label{eq:separated-product-polar-metric} \rho _\Psi ^{(m)}(\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m),\Phi (b_1,\ldots ,b_ m)) =\sum _ i\| \mu _ i-\nu _ i\| _{\mathrm{TV}}. \end{equation}

Proposition 16.6.31. The set $\mathcal S_\Psi ^{(m)}$ consists of normalized ordinary models and is a complete geodesic metric space for $\rho _\Psi ^{(m)}$. Its ideal models form a closed geodesically convex subspace, equal to the metric closure of its models with analytic singularities. Ideal projection is the unique nearest-point retraction onto this subspace and is $1$-Lipschitz, and the distance to the subspace is the sum of the nonatomic polar masses. Metric convergence implies $L^1(M)$ convergence for every smooth positive volume form. The upper-volume comparison $d_\Theta $ vanishes on every pair of points of this locus.

Proof. The preceding lemma and proposition 16.6.13 give

\[ \begin{gathered} P_\Theta [\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m)]=\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m),\\ P_\Theta [\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m)]_{\mathcal I} =\Phi (q_{1,\mathcal I},\ldots ,q_{m,\mathcal I}), \end{gathered} \]

since the induced zero obstacle is zero; independence of the factors gives normalization. Restricting to sections on which the other potentials are finite shows that if two product potentials are equal, then each coordinate difference is constant, and normalization makes every such constant zero. Thus $\Phi $ identifies this locus with the finite product of the spaces of curve models, equipped with the sum of their metrics.

Completeness follows coordinate by coordinate from corollary 30.12.3. Interpolating all polar measures linearly with the same parameter gives a constant-speed geodesic for the sum metric. Ideal projection takes the atomic part in each coordinate, so the assertions on closedness, convexity, unique nearest points and the Lipschitz bound follow coordinate by coordinate from corollary 30.12.3, proposition 30.12.6, and the distances add. Finite atomic approximations with rational coefficients in each coordinate have local logarithmic coefficients whose denominators, together with the unit coefficient of $D$, can be cleared in a product monomial. They give analytic models that are dense in the ideal subspace. Conversely, every ordinary model with analytic singularities is its own ideal projection by proposition 16.2.7, so the closure of these models is exactly the ideal subspace.

By theorem 30.12.4, metric convergence gives $L^1$ convergence on each curve. Off the pole sets, the difference of two product potentials is the sum of the pulled-back coordinate differences. Integrating each term along the compact fibers of $h_ i$ bounds its $L^1$ norm on $M$ by a constant times its $L^1$ norm on the curve, as in proposition 16.6.26, and the finite sum tends to zero.

Finally, since $\dim Y=m<\dim M$, proposition 16.6.13 shows that every model in this locus has zero upper singularity volume. A pairwise maximum is still $\Psi $ plus the pullback of a $\beta $-plurisubharmonic function on $Y$, so the same proposition gives zero upper volume for it. All three terms of $d_\Theta $ therefore vanish. $\square$

As in corollary 16.6.27, corollary 16.6.28, an auxiliary curve factor with a variable coefficient makes the background nonclosed, and lets it degenerate, without changing this metric locus.

Keep $Y,\beta ,\pi _ i$ and $Z,\kappa ,D,\psi $ from above. Let $C_0$ be another connected compact curve with a smooth positive form $\tau $. On $M=Y\times C_0\times Z$, write $p,s,r$ for the projections. For a smooth real function $f\geq 0$ on $Y$, put

\[ \Theta _ f=p^*\beta +(f\circ p)s^*\tau +r^*\kappa , \qquad \Psi =\psi \circ r, \]

and set $\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m)=\Psi +\sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ p$.

Corollary 16.6.32. For every tuple of normalized ordinary curve models, one has

\[ \begin{gathered} P_{\Theta _ f}[\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m)]=\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m),\\ P_{\Theta _ f}[\Phi (q_1,\ldots ,q_ m)]_{\mathcal I} =\Phi (q_{1,\mathcal I},\ldots ,q_{m,\mathcal I}). \end{gathered} \]

On the locus of these potentials, use the sum of polar distances (16.30). All the metric conclusions of proposition 16.6.31 hold, and the diameter is $2\sum _ i A_ i$. The locus, its metric, ideal projection and the analytic closure are independent of $f$, including at $f=0$. The background is positive and not closed when $f$ is positive and nonconstant. The background $\Theta _0$ obtained for $f=0$ is not big.

Proof. On $B=Y\times C_0$, let $\pi ,s_0$ be the projections and put $\beta _ f=\pi ^*\beta +(f\circ \pi )s_0^*\tau $. For $w=\sum _ iq_ i\circ \pi _ i$, an ordinary competitor $v\leq 0$ for $w\circ \pi $ may be replaced by its maximum with $w\circ \pi $. Its restriction to every section $Y\times \{ z\} $ is then a $\beta $-plurisubharmonic competitor for $P_\beta [w]=w$, not identically $-\infty $, by lemma 16.6.29. Thus

\[ P_{\beta _ f}[w\circ \pi ]=w\circ \pi . \]

Take the atomic Green potentials $g_ i$ from the proof of the lemma and put $g=\sum _ i g_ i\circ \pi _ i\circ \pi $. Its complex Hessian is a closed countable divisor current of finite mass minus a smooth form. It is $\beta _ f$-plurisubharmonic for every $f\geq 0$, and it is multiplier-ideal equivalent to $w\circ \pi $. Put $w_{\mathcal I}=\sum _ iq_{i,\mathcal I}\circ \pi _ i$. Since $g\sim w_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi $, every ideal competitor $v\leq 0$ is bounded above by

\[ P_{\beta _ f}[g] =P_{\beta _ f}[w_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi ] =w_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi . \]

Here lemma 1.17.4 gives $v\preceq g$, and the ordinary identity applies to $w_{\mathcal I}$. Conversely, $w_{\mathcal I}\circ \pi $ is a normalized ideal competitor, because ideal projection preserves the ideal type on each curve and the ideal type is compatible with pullback and addition. This computes the ideal envelope on $B$ without requiring $\beta _ f$ to be positive.

Apply proposition 16.6.13 to $M\to B$ and the divisor $B\times D$. Its induced zero obstacle is zero, which gives the two identities upstairs and normalization. Restriction to a fixed section of the auxiliary curve identifies this locus isometrically with the finite product of the spaces of curve models. Completeness, geodesics and the nearest-point property of ideal projection therefore follow as in proposition 16.6.31, and the same rational atomic monomials give analytic density. For $f=1$, the background is positive and proposition 16.2.7 shows that every analytic model lies in the ideal subspace. Analytic singularities and the computed ideal projection are independent of $f$, so this analytic-closure assertion holds for every nonnegative coefficient. Fiber integration, now including the factor $C_0$, gives the same $L^1$ convergence upstairs. Each model and each pairwise maximum is $\Psi $ plus a pullback from $B$, whose dimension is smaller than that of $M$, so their upper volumes vanish by proposition 16.6.13; the upper-volume comparison of definition 30.6.1 therefore vanishes for every $f$.

Each polar measure $\mu _ i$ has mass at most $A_ i$, so the sum distance is at most $2\sum _ iA_ i$. By lemma 30.12.1, this bound is attained by placing mass $A_ i$ at two distinct points of each $C_ i$. Hence the diameter does not change, even at the limiting coefficient.

Finally, $\mathrm d\Theta _ f=p^*(\mathrm df)\wedge s^*\tau $ is nonzero for nonconstant $f$, and $\Theta _ f$ is strictly positive when $f>0$. To see that $\Theta _0$ is not big, put $n=m+1+k$ and use the smooth closed positive $(n-1,n-1)$-form $T$ and the Hermitian form $\Omega $ given by

\[ T=p^*\left(\bigwedge _ i\pi _ i^*\eta _ i\right)\wedge r^*(\kappa ^ k), \qquad \Omega =p^*\beta +s^*\tau +r^*\kappa . \]

Then $\int _ M\Theta _0\wedge T=0$, whereas $\int _ M\Omega \wedge T>0$. By Stokes' theorem, a lower bound $\Theta _0+\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \varepsilon \Omega $ with $\varepsilon >0$ would contradict these identities. Thus the background $\Theta _0$ is not big. $\square$
