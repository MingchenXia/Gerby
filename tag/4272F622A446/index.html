Bounded potentials and recursive products

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 9.1 Bounded potentials and recursive products

Throughout this section and the next, $\Omega \subset \mathbb {C}^ n$ is open, $n\ge 1$, and $T$ is a closed positive current on $\Omega $ of bidimension $(r,r)$, where $0\le r\le n$ is an integer; thus $T$ has bidegree $(n-r,n-r)$. Let $\beta =(\mathrm{i}/2)\sum _ j\mathrm{d}z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_ j$ be the form of (7.4). For a positive current $S$ of bidimension $(s,s)$ on $\Omega $ and a compact set $K\subset \Omega $ we write

\[ \| S\| _ K=\int _ KS\wedge \frac{\beta ^ s}{s!} \]

for the mass of $S$ on $K$; it is $1/s!$ times the value on $K$ of the trace measure of $S$ (definition 7.2.4). By theorem 7.2.5, $S$ is a current of order zero, and the total variations of its coefficient measures are bounded by a constant multiple, depending only on $n$ and $s$, of its trace measure. Since plurisubharmonic functions are upper semicontinuous, they are bounded above on compact sets; hence a plurisubharmonic function is locally bounded if and only if it is bounded below on compact sets.

Definition 9.1.1. Suppose that $r\ge 1$, and let $u$ be a locally bounded plurisubharmonic function on $\Omega $. Let $uT$ be the current of order zero obtained by multiplying the coefficient measures of $T$ by the locally bounded Borel function $u$ (section 7.2), and define

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge T=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(uT). \]

For an integer $1\le q\le r$ and locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots ,u_ q$ on $\Omega $, define recursively

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\bigl (u_1\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T\bigr ). \]

Here $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T$ is a closed positive current, hence of order zero, by proposition 9.1.2 below applied to $q-1$ factors; so its product with $u_1$ is defined as before. The product with no factors is $T$. When $T=1$ (so that $r=n$), $q=n$ and $u_1=\cdots =u_ n=u$, the resulting positive current of bidegree $(n,n)$ is a positive measure, the Monge–Ampère measure $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ of $u$.

Proposition 9.1.2. Every product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T$ of definition 9.1.1 is a closed positive current, and it has the following properties.

  1. If $u_1,\ldots ,u_ q$ are smooth, it coincides with the exterior product of the smooth forms $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j$ with $T$.

  2. The construction is local: if $u_ j=v_ j$ for every $j$ and $T=T'$ on an open set $U\subset \Omega $, then the two products agree on $U$.

  3. It is additive and positively homogeneous in each $u_ j$ and in $T$, whenever the summands are locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions, respectively closed positive currents of bidimension $(r,r)$, and it does not change if a real constant is added to some $u_ j$.

  4. If $\gamma $ is a smooth closed form of bidegree $(s,s)$, $q\le r-s$, such that $T\wedge \gamma $ is positive (for instance, $\gamma $ strongly positive), then

    \[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge (T\wedge \gamma )=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T)\wedge \gamma . \]

  5. It is invariant under biholomorphic maps: if $F\colon \Omega '\to \Omega $ is biholomorphic and $F^*$ denotes the pullback of currents of proposition 7.5.2, then

    \[ F^*\bigl (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T\bigr )=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_1\circ F)\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ q\circ F)\wedge F^*T. \]

Proof. We first record two consequences of the Leibniz rule of section 7.2. Let $R$ be a current of even degree on an open subset of $\mathbb C^ n$ such that $\partial R=\bar\partial R=0$; this holds if $R$ is closed and of pure bidegree. If $f$ is a smooth function, then $\mathrm d(fR)=\mathrm df\wedge R$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(fR)=\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge R$, and applying $\mathrm d$ to the latter gives

9.1
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bt-smooth-factor-leibniz} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(fR)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f\wedge R. \end{equation}

If $\gamma $ is a smooth closed form of pure bidegree, then $\partial \gamma $ and $\bar\partial \gamma $ are the components of $\mathrm d\gamma =0$ of two different bidegrees, so they vanish, and the Leibniz rule gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(R'\wedge \gamma )=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R'\wedge \gamma $ for every current $R'$.

We now prove positivity and closedness, first for a single factor $u$. By proposition 8.2.2, the regularizations $u_\varepsilon =u*\rho _\varepsilon $ by the radial mollifiers of section 1.4 are smooth plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega _\varepsilon $ with positive semidefinite complex Hessians, and they decrease to $u$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. On each compact set the $u_\varepsilon $ are bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon $, because $u\le u_\varepsilon \le u_{\varepsilon _0}$ for $\varepsilon \le \varepsilon _0$ and $u$ is locally bounded. Dominated convergence with respect to the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$ therefore gives $u_\varepsilon T\to uT$ weakly. By (9.1), $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_\varepsilon T)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon \wedge T$. Since weak and strong positivity agree in bidegree $(1,1)$ (section 7.2), the positive smooth form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon $ is strongly positive, so its product with the positive current $T$ is positive; it is also closed. As $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ is continuous for weak convergence, $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_\varepsilon \wedge T\to \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(uT)$ weakly, and positivity and closedness pass to the limit. Thus $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge T$ is positive and closed. Applying this with $T$ replaced by the closed positive current $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T$, we obtain the first assertion by induction on $q$.

(1) If the $u_ j$ are smooth, (9.1) shows at each step of the recursion that the product is the exterior product of smooth forms with $T$.

(2), (3) The definition uses only multiplication of coefficient measures by functions and the operator $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$; both operations are local and linear, and no product of two measures occurs. This gives locality, and additivity and positive homogeneity in $u_1$ and in $T$; for the other factors they follow by induction on $q$, since multiplication by $u_1$ and $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ are linear. If $c$ is a constant and $R=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T$, then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}((u_1+c)R)=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_1R)+c\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_1R)$, since $R$ is closed; for the other factors the invariance under constants follows again by induction.

(4) The product $T\wedge \gamma $ is closed, and multiplication of coefficient measures by $u_1$ commutes with the exterior product by $\gamma $. By the identity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(R'\wedge \gamma )=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}R'\wedge \gamma $ and induction on $q$, every step of the recursion commutes with $\wedge \gamma $.

(5) The pullback $F^*T$ is closed and positive and commutes with $\partial $ and $\bar\partial $, hence with $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$ (proposition 7.5.2), and the functions $u_ j\circ F$ are locally bounded and plurisubharmonic (proposition 8.2.3). We claim that $F^*(uT)=(u\circ F)F^*T$ for a locally bounded plurisubharmonic $u$. For a smooth function $f$ we have $F^*(fT)=(f\circ F)F^*T$: both sides are weakly continuous in $T$, they agree when $T$ is smooth, and on every relatively compact open subset of $\Omega $ the current $T$ is the weak limit of its smooth regularizations (discussion following theorem 7.2.5). Applying this to the regularizations $u_\varepsilon $ on relatively compact open subsets, and letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, we obtain the claim: $u_\varepsilon T\to uT$ as shown above, so $F^*(u_\varepsilon T)\to F^*(uT)$, while $(u_\varepsilon \circ F)F^*T\to (u\circ F)F^*T$ by dominated convergence with respect to the coefficient measures of the positive current $F^*T$. Consequently every step of the recursion commutes with $F^*$, which proves (5). In particular, by (2) and (5), products of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions with closed positive currents on a complex manifold can be computed in holomorphic charts: the products defined in two charts agree on their overlap. $\square$

Let $u\in C^2(\Omega )$ be real, and write $a_{jk}=u_{j\bar k}$, so that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _{j,k}a_{jk}\, \mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$ by (7.5). The $2$-forms $\alpha _{jk}=\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ k$ commute with each other, and $\alpha _{j_1k_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha _{j_ nk_ n}=0$ unless $(j_1,\ldots ,j_ n)$ and $(k_1,\ldots ,k_ n)$ are permutations of $(1,\ldots ,n)$. If $j_ i=\sigma (i)$ and $k_ i=\tau (i)$ for permutations $\sigma ,\tau $, we may reorder the commuting factors so that the first indices increase; with $\kappa =\tau \circ \sigma ^{-1}$ we get $\mathrm dz_1\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_{\kappa (1)}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm dz_ n\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_{\kappa (n)}$, and permuting the $1$-forms $\mathrm d\bar z_{\kappa (i)}$ among themselves gives the sign $\operatorname {sgn}\kappa $. Hence

\[ \Bigl (\sum _{j,k}a_{jk}\alpha _{jk}\Bigr )^ n =\sum _{\sigma ,\tau }\prod _{i=1}^ na_{\sigma (i)\tau (i)}\, \operatorname {sgn}(\tau \sigma ^{-1})\, \bigwedge _{j=1}^ n\alpha _{jj} =n!\det (a_{jk})\bigwedge _{j=1}^ n\alpha _{jj}, \]

because for each of the $n!$ permutations $\sigma $ the sum over $\tau $ is $\sum _\kappa \operatorname {sgn}\kappa \prod _ ja_{j\kappa (j)}=\det (a_{jk})$. Since $\mathrm{i}\, \mathrm{d}z_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}\bar z_ j=2\, \mathrm{d}x_ j\wedge \mathrm{d}y_ j$, we have $\bigwedge _ j(\mathrm i\alpha _{jj})=2^ n\mathrm dV_{2n}$, and therefore

9.2
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bt-determinant-normalization} (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=\Bigl (\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\Bigr )^ nn!\det (u_{j\bar k})\bigwedge _{j=1}^ n\alpha _{jj}=\frac{n!}{\pi ^ n}\det (u_{j\bar k})\, \mathrm{d}V_{2n}. \end{equation}

Here $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ is the exterior power of the continuous form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$; when $u$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic, it is the Monge–Ampère measure of $u$, by proposition 9.1.2(1), and the same holds for plurisubharmonic $u\in C^2(\Omega )$ (see the remark after proposition 9.1.4). In particular $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2=\frac{\mathrm i}{2\pi }\sum _ j\mathrm dz_ j\wedge \mathrm d\bar z_ j=\beta /\pi $ and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2)^ n=n!\pi ^{-n}\, \mathrm dV_{2n}$. (The formula also holds for $n=0$: the empty product is the constant $1$, and both sides are the unit mass $\mathrm{d}V_0$ on the point $\mathbb C^0$.)

Theorem 9.1.3 (Chern–Levine–Nirenberg). Let $K,L\subset \Omega $ be compact sets with $K\subset \operatorname{Int}L$, and let $0\le q\le r$ be an integer. There is a constant $C_{K,L,q}$, depending only on $K$, $L$, $n$ and $q$, such that

\[ \| \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T\| _ K \le C_{K,L,q}\| T\| _ L\prod _{j=1}^ q\| u_ j\| _{L^\infty (L)} \]

for every closed positive current $T$ of bidimension $(r,r)$ on $\Omega $ and all locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions $u_1,\ldots ,u_ q$ on $\Omega $. Here $\| u_ j\| _{L^\infty (L)}=\sup _ L|u_ j|$ is the pointwise supremum.

Proof. For $q=0$ the estimate holds with constant $1$, since $K\subset L$. Let $q=1$ and write $u=u_1$. By lemma 1.4.1 there is a smooth function $0\le \chi \le 1$ with compact support in $\operatorname{Int}L$ which equals $1$ on a neighborhood of $K$; it depends only on $K$ and $L$. Since $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge T\wedge \beta ^{r-1}$ is a positive measure, $\beta $ is closed, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}S)(\chi )=S(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi )$ for currents $S$ of bidimension $(1,1)$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ K\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge T\wedge \beta ^{r-1} & \le \int \chi \, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(uT)\wedge \beta ^{r-1} =\bigl (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(uT\wedge \beta ^{r-1})\bigr )(\chi )\\ & =\int uT\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \wedge \beta ^{r-1} \le C\| u\| _{L^\infty (L)}\| T\| _ L. \end{aligned} \]

The form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\chi \wedge \beta ^{r-1}$ is smooth with support in $L$, though in general not positive; its coefficients are bounded by a constant depending only on $\chi $ and $n$. The last inequality therefore holds because the total variations of the coefficient measures of $T$ on $L$ are bounded by a constant multiple of $\| T\| _ L$, the multiple depending only on $n$ and $r$ (theorem 7.2.5). Dividing by $(r-1)!$ and taking the largest of the constants obtained for $r=1,\ldots ,n$, we obtain the case $q=1$ with a constant depending only on $K$, $L$ and $n$.

For $q>1$, let $\delta >0$ be the distance from $K$ to $\mathbb C^ n\setminus \operatorname{Int}L$ (if $K=\varnothing $ there is nothing to prove), and put $L_ j=\{ z:\operatorname {dist}(z,K)\le j\delta /(q+1)\} $ for $0\le j<q$ and $L_ q=L$. These are compact sets, depending only on $K$, $L$ and $q$, with $K=L_0$ and $L_{j-1}\subset \operatorname{Int}L_ j$ for $1\le j\le q$. By proposition 9.1.2, the currents $S_ j=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{j+1}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T$, $0\le j\le q$, are positive and closed, and $S_{j-1}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ j\wedge S_ j$. The case $q=1$, applied to $S_ j$ and the pair $L_{j-1}\subset \operatorname{Int}L_ j$, gives $\| S_{j-1}\| _{L_{j-1}}\le C_ j\| u_ j\| _{L^\infty (L)}\| S_ j\| _{L_ j}$ for $j=1,\ldots ,q$, since $\| u_ j\| _{L^\infty (L_ j)}\le \| u_ j\| _{L^\infty (L)}$. Combining these $q$ inequalities, we obtain the estimate with $C_{K,L,q}=C_1\cdots C_ q$. $\square$

For continuous potentials, uniform convergence is enough to pass to the limit in the products.

Proposition 9.1.4. Let $T_ k\to T$ weakly, where $T_ k$ and $T$ are closed positive currents on $\Omega $ of bidimension $(r,r)$, and let $0\le q\le r$. For $1\le j\le q$, let $(u_{j,k})_{k\ge 1}$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega $ converging locally uniformly, as $k\to \infty $, to a continuous function $u_ j$, and let $(u_{0,k})_{k\ge 1}$ be locally bounded Borel functions on $\Omega $ converging locally uniformly to a continuous function $u_0$. Then $u_1,\ldots ,u_ q$ are plurisubharmonic, and

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{1,k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{q,k}\wedge T_ k & \longrightarrow \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T,\\ u_{0,k}\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{1,k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{q,k}\wedge T_ k & \longrightarrow u_0\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ q\wedge T \end{aligned} \]

weakly as $k\to \infty $.

Proof. We first show that $u_ j$ is plurisubharmonic for $1\le j\le q$. Since plurisubharmonicity is a local property, it suffices to prove this on every ball $W\Subset \Omega $. Choose $k_1<k_2<\cdots $ such that $|u_{j,k_ m}-u_ j|\le 2^{-m}$ on $W$, and put $w_ m=u_{j,k_ m}+3\cdot 2^{-m}$. These functions are plurisubharmonic on $W$, and they decrease, because

\[ w_{m+1}\le u_ j+2^{-m-1}+3\cdot 2^{-m-1}=u_ j+2^{1-m}\le u_{j,k_ m}+2^{-m}+2^{1-m}=w_ m. \]

Their limit $u_ j$ is finite, so it is plurisubharmonic on $W$ by theorem 8.3.1.

Since the $u_{j,k}$ converge locally uniformly to continuous functions, they are bounded on each compact set uniformly in $k$. Since $T_ k\to T$ weakly, the masses $\| T_ k\| _ L$ are bounded for each compact set $L$: if $\chi \in C^\infty _ c(\Omega )$ satisfies $0\le \chi \le 1$ and $\chi =1$ on $L$, then $\| T_ k\| _ L\le T_ k(\chi \beta ^ r)/r!$, and the right-hand side converges. By theorem 9.1.3, the masses of all the products considered below are therefore bounded on compact sets uniformly in $k$, and so are the total variations of their coefficient measures (theorem 7.2.5).

We claim: if $S_ k\to S$ weakly, where $S_ k$ and $S$ are closed positive currents whose masses are bounded on compact sets uniformly in $k$, and if locally bounded Borel functions $v_ k$ converge locally uniformly to a continuous function $v$, then $v_ kS_ k\to vS$ weakly. Applying this to $S_ k=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{2,k}\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{q,k}\wedge T_ k$ and $v_ k=u_{1,k}$, and then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}$, which is weakly continuous, proves the first assertion by induction on $q$; the case $q=0$ is the hypothesis. The second assertion is the claim with $v_ k=u_{0,k}$.

To prove the claim, fix a test form $\varphi $ with compact support $K$ and $\varepsilon >0$. By lemma 1.4.1(1), applied to $v$ on a relatively compact open neighborhood of $K$, there is a smooth function $v_\varepsilon $ on a neighborhood of $K$ with $|v-v_\varepsilon |\le \varepsilon $ on $K$. Write

\[ v_ kS_ k-vS=(v_ k-v)S_ k+(v-v_\varepsilon )S_ k +v_\varepsilon (S_ k-S)+(v_\varepsilon -v)S. \]

Evaluated on $\varphi $, a term $fS_ k$ with a bounded Borel function $f$ is at most $C_\varphi \sup _ K|f|$ times the total variation on $K$ of the coefficient measures of $S_ k$, where $C_\varphi $ bounds the coefficients of $\varphi $; the same holds for $S$. Hence the first term tends to zero, since $v_ k\to v$ uniformly on $K$; the second and fourth terms are bounded by a constant times $\varepsilon $, uniformly in $k$; and the third tends to zero as $k\to \infty $, since $v_\varepsilon \varphi $ is a test form. Letting first $k\to \infty $ and then $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ proves the claim. $\square$

If $u\in C^2(\Omega )$ is plurisubharmonic, its Monge–Ampère measure is given by (9.2). Indeed, the regularizations $u_\varepsilon $ are smooth and plurisubharmonic on $\Omega _\varepsilon $ (proposition 8.2.2) and converge to $u$ locally uniformly (lemma 1.4.1(1)), and their second derivatives are the regularizations of the continuous second derivatives of $u$ (proposition 1.4.3), so they also converge locally uniformly. Hence $\det ((u_\varepsilon )_{j\bar k})\to \det (u_{j\bar k})$ locally uniformly. Applying (9.2) to $u_\varepsilon $ and proposition 9.1.4 on each $\Omega _{\varepsilon _0}$, along a sequence $\varepsilon _ k\downarrow 0$ with $\varepsilon _ k<\varepsilon _0$, we obtain the formula for $u$ on every $\Omega _{\varepsilon _0}$, hence on $\Omega $.
