Chapter 15 · Multiplier-ideal models and partial equilibrium · Tag EA11DD744637

Polar model gauge transport

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Corollary 16.6.63

Corollary 16.6.63. The comparison $\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}$ descends to ordinary classes. For a smooth real function $f$ and $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$, the map

\[ G_ f(p)=P_{\omega '}[p-f] \]

is a bijection between the normalized ordinary models in the two backgrounds, with inverse $q\mapsto P_\omega [q+f]$. If the same Gauduchon forms are used in both backgrounds, $G_ f$ preserves $\delta ^{\mathrm{pol}}$, analytic models and ideal models.

Proof. Boundedly equivalent potentials have boundedly equivalent pullbacks on every modification, with the same pole set. Proposition 1.25.3 therefore gives equality of their polar currents and of their measures $\mu _\pi $ in every coordinate, which proves descent to ordinary classes.

If $p=P_\omega [p]$, the version of proposition 16.3.1 for ordinary envelopes gives

\[ p+\min _ Xf\leq G_ f(p)+f\leq p+\max _ Xf. \]

Thus $G_ f(p)\sim p-f$. Since ordinary envelopes depend only on the ordinary type, $P_{\omega '}[G_ f(p)]=P_{\omega '}[p-f]=G_ f(p)$, and similarly $P_\omega [G_ f(p)+f]=P_\omega [p]=p$. The same argument applied to models in background $\omega '$ proves surjectivity and the formula for the inverse. Invariance of the polar coordinates and the gauge identity of proposition 16.6.61 now give

\[ \delta _{\omega '}^{\mathrm{pol}}(G_ f(p),G_ f(q)) =\delta _\omega ^{\mathrm{pol}}(p,q). \]

Subtraction of a smooth function and bounded equivalence preserve analytic singularities. If $p=P_\omega [p]_{\mathcal I}$, the gauge estimate for ideal envelopes gives $P_{\omega '}[p-f]_{\mathcal I}\sim p-f\sim G_ f(p)$. By corollary 16.2.4, this ideal envelope is an ordinary model, and two ordinary models of the same ordinary type coincide. Hence it equals $G_ f(p)$, which shows that $G_ f$ maps ideal models to ideal models; the inverse map gives the converse. $\square$
