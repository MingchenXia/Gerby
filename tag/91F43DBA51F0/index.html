Multifactor mixed discriminant

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Lemma 9.7.5

Lemma 9.7.5. Let $n\geq 1$. For positive semidefinite Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices $A_1,\ldots ,A_ n$,

\[ D(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)\geq \prod _{i=1}^ n(\det A_ i)^{1/n}. \]

Proof. For $n=1$ both sides equal $A_1$. Let $n\geq 2$, and assume first that all $A_ i$ are positive definite. By lemma 7.2.2(3), $D$ is positive on positive definite matrices.

We first derive the reverse Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Fix positive definite matrices $A_3,\ldots ,A_ n$, and consider the symmetric bilinear form

\[ q(A,B)=D(A,B,A_3,\ldots ,A_ n) \]

on Hermitian matrices. Let $A,B$ be positive definite, put $c=q(A,B)/q(B,B)$ and $M=A-cB$. Then $q(M,B)=0$, so $q(M,M)\leq 0$ by lemma 7.2.3. Since $q(M,M)=q(A,A)-q(A,B)^2/q(B,B)$, we obtain

9.32
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mixed-discriminant-reverse-cauchy} q(A,B)^2\geq q(A,A)q(B,B). \end{equation}

Put $\widetilde A_ i=(\det A_ i)^{-1/n}A_ i$, so that $\det \widetilde A_ i=1$. By multilinearity it suffices to prove that $D(\widetilde A_1,\ldots ,\widetilde A_ n)\geq 1$. Consider the finitely many ordered $n$-tuples whose entries belong to the set $\{ \widetilde A_1,\ldots ,\widetilde A_ n\} $, and let $F>0$ be the smallest mixed discriminant of such a tuple. A tuple whose entries all equal $\widetilde A_ i$ has discriminant $\det \widetilde A_ i=1$, so $F\leq 1$. The multiplicity of a matrix in a tuple is the number of entries equal to it. Among the tuples with discriminant $F$, choose one for which the sum of the squares of the multiplicities is largest.

Suppose that this tuple contains two different matrices $A$ and $B$. By symmetry of $D$ we may place them in the first two slots, so that $F=q(A,B)$, where $q$ is formed with the remaining entries of the tuple. Replacing the pair $(A,B)$ by $(A,A)$ or by $(B,B)$ gives tuples with discriminants $q(A,A)\geq F$ and $q(B,B)\geq F$, whereas (9.32) gives $q(A,A)q(B,B)\leq F^2$. Hence $q(A,A)=q(B,B)=F$, and both new tuples are minimizing. If $A$ and $B$ have multiplicities $r$ and $s$, the two replacements change the sum of the squared multiplicities by $2(r-s)+2$ and $2(s-r)+2$, respectively. These two numbers add up to $4$, so one of them is positive, which contradicts the choice of the tuple. Therefore all entries of the chosen tuple are equal, and $F=1$. In particular $D(\widetilde A_1,\ldots ,\widetilde A_ n)\geq 1$.

Finally, for positive semidefinite $A_ i$, apply the inequality to $A_ i+\varepsilon I$ and let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Both sides are continuous: $D$ is a polynomial in the matrix entries, and $(\det A)^{1/n}$ is continuous on positive semidefinite matrices. $\square$
