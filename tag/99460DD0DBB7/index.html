Degenerate curvature and growing vanishing orders

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 27.12 Degenerate curvature and growing vanishing orders

In the compact models above, the vanishing orders are proportional to the tensor degree. To see the transition when the order grows more slowly, we pass to a model space of entire functions. There the local metric fixes the scale of the full kernel, while the selecting ideal creates a second, larger radius. Grouping the monomials according to the residues of their degrees expresses the transition at that radius through Poisson tails.

In this section $r$ and $q$ denote new integers. Fix an integer $r\geq 1$. On $\mathbb C$, with coordinate $z=x+\mathrm{i}y$ and area measure $\mathrm{d}A=\mathrm{d}x\, \mathrm{d}y$, put $\phi (z)=|z|^{2r}$. For a real $m>0$, let $H_{m,r}$ be the Hilbert space of entire functions with norm

\[ \| f\| _{m,r}^2=\int _{\mathbb C}|f|^2\mathrm{e}^{-m\phi }\, \mathrm{d}A. \]

For an integer $q\geq 0$, let $H_{m,r}^{(q)}$ consist of the functions vanishing to order at least $q$ at zero. Since the weight is bounded below on each fixed disk, the local submean and Cauchy inequalities bound point and jet evaluations, so this subspace is closed. Write $K_{m,r}^{(q)}(z,w)$ for its reproducing kernel, holomorphic in $z$ and antiholomorphic in $w$, and put

\[ B_{m,r}^{(q)}(z)=\mathrm{e}^{-m\phi (z)}K_{m,r}^{(q)}(z,z). \]

Thus the ideal $(z^ q)$ selects the functions, while the smooth weight determines the norm. We use $\Gamma (a)=\int _0^\infty t^{a-1}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\, \mathrm{d}t$ for $a>0$.

Proposition 27.12.1. For every $m>0$ and integer $q\geq 0$,

27.61
\begin{equation} \label{eq:degenerate-fock-series} K_{m,r}^{(q)}(z,w)=\frac r\pi \sum _{k=q}^\infty \frac{m^{(k+1)/r}(z\bar w)^ k}{\Gamma ((k+1)/r)}. \end{equation}

The series and all its derivatives converge uniformly on compact sets. For $\zeta \in \mathbb C$,

27.62
\begin{equation} \label{eq:degenerate-fock-scaling} m^{-1/r}B_{m,r}^{(q)}(m^{-1/(2r)}\zeta ) =\frac r\pi \mathrm{e}^{-|\zeta |^{2r}} \sum _{k=q}^\infty \frac{|\zeta |^{2k}}{\Gamma ((k+1)/r)}. \end{equation}

Consequently, if $m$ tends to infinity through positive integers and $q=q_ m\to \infty $, the left side tends to zero uniformly on compact $\zeta $-sets.

Proof. The monomials are orthogonal by rotational invariance. For every integer $k\geq 0$, polar integration with radial variable $\rho $ and the substitution $t=m\rho ^{2r}$ give

\[ \| z^ k\| _{m,r}^2=2\pi \int _0^\infty \rho ^{2k+1}\mathrm{e}^{-m\rho ^{2r}}\, \mathrm{d}\rho =\frac\pi r m^{-(k+1)/r}\Gamma ((k+1)/r). \]

For an entire function $f(z)=\sum _{k\geq 0}a_ kz^ k$, Parseval's identity on circles and Tonelli's theorem yield $\| f\| _{m,r}^2=\sum _{k\geq 0}|a_ k|^2\| z^ k\| _{m,r}^2$. Thus the normalized monomials form an orthonormal basis, and those with $k\geq q$ form an orthonormal basis of the selected subspace. This proves (27.61). By Stirling's formula, the reciprocal Gamma coefficients decay faster than every fixed geometric progression, even after multiplication by any fixed power of $k$; this gives uniform convergence on compact sets together with all derivatives. Substituting $z=w=m^{-1/(2r)}\zeta $ gives (27.62). On a disk $|\zeta |\leq R$ of fixed radius, the series in (27.62) is bounded by the tail, from the index $q$ on, of the convergent series with $|\zeta |$ replaced by $R$. This tail tends to zero as $q\to \infty $, proving the last assertion. $\square$

For $r=1$ and $q=0$ the density is the constant $m/\pi $. For general $r$ and $q=0$ its value at zero is $rm^{1/r}/(\pi \Gamma (1/r))$, whereas it vanishes at zero when $q>0$. The scale $m^{-1/(2r)}$ therefore describes fixed vanishing orders, but a growing order requires a further rescaling.
