Multiplier ideals and logarithmic poles

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 1.16 Multiplier ideals and logarithmic poles

For a logarithmic pole along a submanifold, weighted integrability of a holomorphic germ is determined by its vanishing order in the normal directions. For general singularities, the valuative characterization of multiplier ideals and the strong openness theorem, both recalled in [SGPT], make the comparison of multiplier ideals compatible with maxima, sums and increasing limits.

In local coordinates $(z,w)\in \mathbb C^ d\times \mathbb C^ k$, with $d\geq 0$ and $k\geq 1$, let $Y=\{ w=0\} $ and denote its ideal by $\mathcal I_ Y$.

Lemma 1.16.1. If a quasi-plurisubharmonic function has the form $u=c\log |w|^2+O(1)$ with $c>0$ and locally bounded remainder, then for every $t>0$,

\[ \mathcal I(tu)=\mathcal I_ Y^{\max \{ \lfloor tc\rfloor -k+1,0\} }. \]

The zeroth power is the full structure sheaf.

Proof. The bounded remainder changes the weight only by bounded positive factors. Expand a nonzero holomorphic germ $g(z,w)$ in homogeneous polynomials in $w$, and let $a$ be the smallest degree whose term does not vanish identically. On smaller product neighborhoods, $|g(z,w)|\leq C|w|^ a$. Hence $a+k>tc$ suffices for weighted integrability, since the corresponding radial integral in $\rho =|w|$ is

\[ \int _0^\varepsilon \rho ^{2a-2tc+2k-1}\, \mathrm{d}\rho <+\infty . \]

Conversely, let $z$ be a point near the origin at which the term of degree $a$ does not vanish identically in $w$. Integration of $|g(z,w)|^2$ over the spheres $|w|=\rho $ gives a positive leading term of order $\rho ^{2a}$, so the radial integral in $w$ diverges when $a+k\leq tc$, logarithmically in the case of equality. Such points $z$ form a nonempty open set in every sufficiently small neighborhood, and Fubini's theorem shows that $g$ is not weighted-integrable near the origin. For $d=0$ this is the radial calculation at a point. Thus $g$ is weighted-integrable exactly when $a\geq \lfloor tc\rfloor -k+1$, that is, when $g$ lies in the power of $\mathcal I_ Y$ given in the statement. $\square$

Cutoff functions allow us to insert small isolated logarithmic poles into any Hermitian background.

Lemma 1.16.2. Let $\omega $ be a Hermitian form on $X$, and fix distinct points $p_1,\ldots ,p_ N$ of $X$. There are constants $C_ j>0$ such that, whenever $a_ j>0$ and $\sum _ jC_ ja_ j<1$, there is $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$, smooth off these points, with

\[ u=a_ j\log |z|^2+O(1)\quad \hbox{near }p_ j,\qquad \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\geq \left(1-\sum _ jC_ ja_ j\right)\omega . \]

Here $z$ denotes local coordinates centered at $p_ j$. The function can be chosen to vanish outside arbitrarily small disjoint coordinate neighborhoods, with the constants depending on those neighborhoods.

Proof. Choose disjoint coordinate balls centered at the points $p_ j$ and smooth cutoffs $\chi _ j$ supported in them and equal to one near their centers. The function $\chi _ j\log |z|^2$, extended by zero, is plurisubharmonic near its pole and smooth elsewhere, so its complex Hessian is bounded below by $-C_ j\omega $. Set $u=\sum _ ja_ j\chi _ j\log |z|^2$. These lower bounds give the asserted current inequality, and the remaining properties follow from the disjointness of the supports. $\square$

Theorem 1.16.3. Let $u,v$ be plurisubharmonic functions near a point of $\mathbb C^ n$. By a local modification we mean a modification $\pi $, with smooth source, of a neighborhood of this point.

  1. The inclusions $\mathcal I(ku)\subseteq \mathcal I(kv)$ for every real $k>0$ are equivalent to the inequalities $\nu (u\circ \pi ,y)\geq \nu (v\circ \pi ,y)$ at every point $y$ of the source of every local modification $\pi $. Likewise, equality of the multiplier ideals at every positive weight is equivalent to equality of all these Lelong numbers.

  2. If $p_ j$ is an increasing sequence of plurisubharmonic functions converging almost everywhere to a plurisubharmonic function $p$, then, for each point $x$ and each $\lambda >0$,

    \[ \mathcal I(\lambda p_ j)_ x=\mathcal I(\lambda p)_ x \quad \text{for all sufficiently large }j. \]

In the first item, the neighborhoods may be shrunk as necessary; we never need to extend a local modification to a modification of a compact manifold. The second item is the increasing-limit form of the strong openness theorem used in [SGPT]. Since adding a smooth function changes neither multiplier ideals nor Lelong numbers, both items apply to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions after the local correction of proposition 1.1.2.

Lemma 1.16.4. Let $u,u',v,v'$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on $X$. For any quasi-plurisubharmonic $h$, one has

\[ u+h\sim _{\mathcal I}v+h \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad u\sim _{\mathcal I}v. \]

If $u\sim _{\mathcal I}v$ and $u'\sim _{\mathcal I}v'$, then

\[ \max (u,u')\sim _{\mathcal I}\max (v,v'),\qquad u+u'\sim _{\mathcal I}v+v',\qquad a u\sim _{\mathcal I}a v\quad (a>0). \]

Proof. Fix a coordinate neighborhood $U\subset X$ and a modification $\pi :Y\to U$ with smooth source. At every $y\in Y$, the elementary rules for the Lelong numbers of maxima, sums and positive multiples give

\[ \begin{aligned} \nu (\max (u,u')\circ \pi ,y) & =\min \{ \nu (u\circ \pi ,y),\nu (u'\circ \pi ,y)\} ,\\ \nu ((u+u')\circ \pi ,y) & =\nu (u\circ \pi ,y)+\nu (u'\circ \pi ,y),\\ \nu (a u\circ \pi ,y)& =a\nu (u\circ \pi ,y). \end{aligned} \]

These rules are unaffected by smooth local corrections. Since $u\sim _{\mathcal I}v$ and $u'\sim _{\mathcal I}v'$, the first item of theorem 1.16.3 shows that the right-hand sides do not change when $u,u'$ are replaced by $v,v'$. The converse direction of the same item, applied on each coordinate neighborhood, gives the three asserted equivalences.

For the cancellation rule, suppose that $u+h\sim _{\mathcal I}v+h$. By the first item of theorem 1.16.3, the pullbacks $(u+h)\circ \pi $ and $(v+h)\circ \pi $ have the same Lelong numbers. The common summand $h\circ \pi $ is quasi-plurisubharmonic, so its Lelong numbers are finite, and subtracting them shows that $u\circ \pi $ and $v\circ \pi $ have the same Lelong numbers. Hence $u\sim _{\mathcal I}v$ by the same item. The reverse implication is the addition rule proved above. $\square$

A residual with zero Lelong numbers can still be unbounded, but it does not change any multiplier ideal, even after addition to another potential.

Lemma 1.16.5. Let $q$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic on a complex manifold and suppose that $\nu (q,x)=0$ at every point. Then $\mathrm e^{-tq}$ is locally integrable for every $t>0$. For every quasi-plurisubharmonic $v$ and every $t>0$,

\[ \mathcal I\bigl (t(v+q)\bigr )=\mathcal I(tv). \]

Proof. Fix a point $x$ and add a smooth correction to make $q$ plurisubharmonic on a bounded pseudoconvex coordinate neighborhood. Form the weighted Bergman space of proposition 1.14.1 at weight $t$. If every member vanished at $x$, the Cauchy estimates, together with the local upper bound for $q$, would give

\[ \sup _{\| g\| _ t\leq 1}|g(z)|\leq C|z-x| \]

near $x$. Its Bergman potential would consequently have Lelong number at least $1/t$, contradicting (1.37). There is therefore a weighted square-integrable holomorphic function nonzero at $x$. On a smaller neighborhood it is bounded away from zero, so its weighted norm proves integrability of $\mathrm e^{-tq}$. Smooth corrections do not change this conclusion. Thus $q\sim _{\mathcal I}0$; the addition rule in lemma 1.16.4 gives $v+q\sim _{\mathcal I}v$, which is precisely the asserted equality at every weight. $\square$

Let $v,w$ be quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on an open set, and let $Y$ be a complex submanifold on which $v|_ Y$ is not identically $-\infty $. The restriction and subadditivity theorems give the two inclusions recalled in [SGPT] and [Section 15, DemAM11]:

1.41
\begin{equation} \label{eq:trace-local-ideal-inputs} \mathcal I(v|_ Y)\subseteq \mathcal I(v)\mathcal O_ Y, \qquad \mathcal I(v+w)\subseteq \mathcal I(v)\mathcal I(w). \end{equation}

The first follows from the local Ohsawa–Takegoshi extension theorem. For the second, the weighted Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on a product is the Hilbert tensor product of the two weighted spaces, so the multiplier ideal of $v(x)+w(y)$ on the product is generated by products of generators of $\mathcal I(v)$ and $\mathcal I(w)$; the first inclusion, applied to the diagonal, then gives the second. These local results for plurisubharmonic functions extend to quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, in the weight convention of this book, by smooth corrections.

Multiplier ideals commute with pullback by holomorphic submersions. Here the extension $\mathcal I\mathcal O_ Z$ of an ideal sheaf $\mathcal I$ on $Y$ is the ideal sheaf on $Z$ generated by the pullbacks of its local sections.

Lemma 1.16.6. Let $p:Z\to Y$ be a holomorphic submersion of complex manifolds and let $u\in \mathrm{QPSH}(Y)$. For every $t>0$,

\[ \mathcal I(tu\circ p) =\mathcal I(tu)\mathcal O_ Z. \]

Proof. Work in product coordinates $(z,\zeta )$ with $p(z,\zeta )=z$. Write $\alpha $ for a multi-index in the fiber variables and $\mathrm{d}\lambda _\zeta $ for their Euclidean volume. A germ in the ideal on the right is a finite sum of bounded holomorphic multiples of pullbacks of germs that are square integrable against $\mathrm{e}^{-tu(z)}$. Fubini's theorem shows that it is weighted-integrable on a sufficiently small product neighborhood.

Conversely, let $F(z,\zeta )$ be a weighted-integrable holomorphic germ and expand it in the fiber variables,

\[ F(z,\zeta )=\sum _\alpha f_\alpha (z)\zeta ^\alpha . \]

The coefficient estimate for a holomorphic function on a polydisk gives, on smaller product neighborhoods,

\[ |f_\alpha (z)|^2\leq C_\alpha \int |F(z,\zeta )|^2\, \mathrm{d}\lambda _\zeta . \]

Multiplying by $\mathrm{e}^{-tu(z)}$ and integrating in $z$ shows that each coefficient germ $f_\alpha $ belongs to $\mathcal I(tu)$. Let $J$ be the extension of this ideal to the local ring $R$ of holomorphic germs on the product. Every finite Taylor sum of $F$ belongs to $J$, and the remainder of total fiber degree at least $N$ belongs to $(\zeta )^ N$. Thus the class of $F$ in $R/J$ lies in $(\zeta )^ N(R/J)$ for every $N$. By the Krull intersection theorem for the finitely generated module $R/J$ over the Noetherian local ring $R$, the intersection of these submodules is zero. Hence $F\in J$. Smooth corrections and smooth positive volume densities do not change the integrability conditions. $\square$

Example 1.16.7. Exact multiplier-ideal pullback can fail at a critical fiber. On a polydisk in $\mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, let $f(z_0,\ldots ,z_{n-1})=z_0^2$ and put $v(t)=\tfrac 12\log |t|^2$. At the origin,

\[ \mathcal I(v)=\mathcal O,\qquad \mathcal I(v\circ f)=(z_0) \ne \mathcal I(v)\mathcal O. \]

Proof. On the curve the coefficient is $1/2$, so the multiplier ideal is the zeroth power of the maximal ideal. Upstairs $v\circ f=\log |z_0|^2$ has coefficient one along the smooth divisor $\{ z_0=0\} $. Apply lemma 1.16.1 in codimension one to get its first ideal power. $\square$

This calculation concerns a fixed weight. For a surjective holomorphic map from a connected compact manifold to a curve, with connected fibers, pullback still preserves and reflects all-weight ideal types by 1.22.6.
