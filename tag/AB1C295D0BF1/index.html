Radial volumes on scalar Hopf manifolds

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 29.4.2 Radial volumes on scalar Hopf manifolds

Let $n\geq 2$, put $m=n-1$, and consider the scalar Hopf manifold and its projection to $\mathbb P^ m$,

\[ H_ n=(\mathbb C^ n\setminus \{ 0\} )/\langle z\mapsto \mathrm e z\rangle , \qquad q:H_ n\longrightarrow \mathbb P^ m. \]

Let $\omega _{\rm FS}$ be the Fubini–Study form on $\mathbb P^ m$, normalized by $\int _{\mathbb P^ m}\omega _{\rm FS}^ m=1$. The function $r=\log |z|^2$ is well defined modulo $2$ on $H_ n$, so its differential is a global one-form. Set

\[ \alpha =q^*\omega _{\rm FS}=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}r,\qquad \beta =\mathrm dr\wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}r. \]

Both forms are defined on $H_ n$. The form $\alpha $ has rank $m$, and its kernel is the complex radial line; the form $\beta $ has rank one, and its kernel is the complementary horizontal subspace. Thus $\alpha ^ m\wedge \beta $ is a positive volume form. On each elliptic fiber of $q$, where $z$ moves along a complex line through the origin, $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}r=\mathrm d\arg z/(2\pi )$, so

\[ \int _{q^{-1}(x)}\beta =2,\qquad \int _{H_ n}\alpha ^ m\wedge \beta =2. \]

We consider all metrics of the radial form

29.20
\begin{equation} \label{eq:radial-lck-family} \omega =C(r)\alpha +D(r)\beta ,\qquad C,D\in C^\infty (\mathbb R/2\mathbb Z),\quad C,D>0. \end{equation}

The circle $\mathbb R/2\mathbb Z$ of values of $r$ is oriented by increasing $r$. The Lee period of such a metric is the integral of its Lee form over a radial loop along which $r$ increases from $0$ to $2$.

Lemma 29.4.4. Every metric of the form (29.20) is strictly LCK. Its Lee form and Lee period are

\[ \lambda =l(r)\, \mathrm dr,\qquad l=\frac{C'-D}{C},\qquad L=-\int _0^2\frac{D}{C}\, \mathrm dr<0. \]

The metric is Gauduchon if and only if $C^{m-1}(C'-D)=k$ for a constant $k$. In terms of the positive smooth $2$-periodic function $F=C^ mD$, this condition is equivalent to

29.21
\begin{equation} \label{eq:radial-lck-periodic-ode} C^ mC'=kC+F,\qquad D=\frac{F}{C^ m}. \end{equation}

Every positive periodic solution has $k<0$, and

29.22
\begin{equation} \label{eq:radial-lck-period-identities} \begin{gathered} k\int _0^2C\, \mathrm dr+\int _0^2F\, \mathrm dr=0, \\ l=kC^{-m},\qquad L(C)=-\int _0^2\frac{F}{C^{m+1}}\, \mathrm dr. \end{gathered} \end{equation}

Proof. The differential identities are

\[ \begin{gathered} \alpha ^{m+1}=\beta ^2=0,\qquad \mathrm d\beta =-\mathrm dr\wedge \alpha ,\\ \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\beta =-\alpha \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}r,\qquad \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\beta =-\alpha ^2. \end{gathered} \]

They give $\mathrm d\omega =(C'-D)\mathrm dr\wedge \alpha =\lambda \wedge \omega $. The closed form $\lambda $ is the Lee form, since wedging one-forms with $\omega $ is injective. The integral of $C'/C$ over a period is zero, which proves the formula for $L$; since $L\neq 0$, the Lee form is not exact. Two radial Lee forms have the same de Rham class exactly when their periods agree: a difference with zero period has a periodic primitive, and the radial loop detects the converse.

For a smooth function $G$ of $r$, the chain rule gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}G=G'\alpha +G''\beta $. Also $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}(G\beta )=-G\alpha \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}r$, so

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(G\beta )=-G'\alpha \wedge \beta -G\alpha ^2. \]

Expanding the powers of $\omega $ therefore gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \omega ^ m& =C^ m\alpha ^ m+mC^{m-1}D\alpha ^{m-1}\wedge \beta ,\\ \omega ^ n& =nC^ mD\alpha ^ m\wedge \beta , \end{aligned} \]

and

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ m)= \bigl [(C^ m)''-m(C^{m-1}D)'\bigr ]\alpha ^ m\wedge \beta . \]

Since $(C^ m)''-m(C^{m-1}D)'=m\bigl (C^{m-1}(C'-D)\bigr )'$, this vanishes exactly when $C^{m-1}(C'-D)$ is constant. Multiplying the identity $C^{m-1}(C'-D)=k$ by $C$ gives (29.21). Since $C^ mC'$ has zero integral over a period, integrating (29.21) gives the first identity in (29.22), which forces $k<0$. The formulas for $l$ and $L$ follow from the formula for the Lee form. $\square$

Lemma 29.4.5. For fixed $F>0$ and each $k<0$, there is at most one positive $2$-periodic solution of (29.21). If $k_2>k_1$, the corresponding solutions satisfy $C_2>C_1$ everywhere and $L(C_2)>L(C_1)$.

Proof. Suppose $k_2>k_1$, and put $R=C_2-C_1$. At a zero of $R$, the equation gives $R'=(k_2-k_1)C_1^{1-m}>0$. Such zeros are isolated, so there are finitely many on the circle. A periodic differentiable function cannot cross zero only upwards, since returning around the circle would require a downward crossing. Hence $R$ has no zero and a constant strict sign. The identity $\int _0^2C_ i\, \mathrm dr=-\int _0^2F\, \mathrm dr/k_ i$ shows that this sign is positive, and the formula for $L(C)$ in (29.22) then gives $L(C_2)>L(C_1)$.

For equal $k$, uniqueness for the smooth scalar equation $C'=kC^{1-m}+FC^{-m}$ on $C>0$ shows that two solutions which meet coincide. Two solutions which never meet are strictly ordered, which contradicts the equality of their integrals. This proves uniqueness for fixed $k$. $\square$

Theorem 29.4.6. For every positive smooth $2$-periodic function $F$ and every $L_0<0$, there is exactly one metric of the form (29.20) which is Gauduchon, has Lee period $L_0$, and satisfies

\[ \omega ^ n=nF(r)\alpha ^ m\wedge \beta . \]

Proof. Fix $k<0$, put $c=-k$, and let $f_0=\min F>0$ and $f_1=\max F$. The constant functions $f_0/c$ and $f_1/c$ are a subsolution and a supersolution, respectively, of $C'=C^{-m}(F-cC)$. By comparison, the forward flow of this equation preserves the compact interval $I_ k=[f_0/c,f_1/c]$ of positive numbers, and existence and uniqueness for smooth ordinary differential equations give a continuous time-two map $I_ k\to I_ k$. Its difference from the identity is nonnegative at the left endpoint and nonpositive at the right one, so the intermediate value theorem gives a fixed point; if $f_0=f_1$, the constant $C=f_0/c$ is the solution. Since the equation is periodic, the solution through a fixed point extends to a smooth positive periodic solution. It is unique by lemma 29.4.5 and satisfies

29.25
\begin{equation} \label{eq:radial-lck-ode-barriers} \frac{f_0}{-k}\leq C_ k(r)\leq \frac{f_1}{-k}. \end{equation}

By lemma 29.4.5, the period $L(k)=-\int _0^2F/C_ k^{m+1}\, \mathrm dr$ is strictly increasing in $k$. It is also continuous. Indeed, for $k$ in a compact neighborhood of any $k_0<0$, (29.25) gives uniform positive lower and finite upper bounds for $C_ k$, and the equation then bounds all derivatives. Any sequence $k\to k_0$ therefore has smoothly convergent subsequences, and the equation and uniqueness identify every limit with $C_{k_0}$. Thus $C_ k\to C_{k_0}$ uniformly, which gives continuity of the integral.

The same bounds imply $L(k)\to 0$ as $k\uparrow 0$ and $L(k)\to -\infty $ as $k\to -\infty $. Hence exactly one $k$ has period $L_0$. For this $k$, set $D=F/C_ k^ m$. The identities of lemma 29.4.4 show that $C_ k\alpha +D\beta $ has the required properties. Conversely, every metric of the form (29.20) with these properties gives a positive periodic solution of the same equation, so the ordering of the periods also proves uniqueness. $\square$

Proposition 29.4.7. Let $\omega $ be a metric as in theorem 29.4.6, with Lee form $\lambda $. There is a positive smooth function $U$ on $H_ n$ with $\omega =D_\lambda U$; in particular, the class of $\omega $ in $H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC},\lambda }(H_ n,\mathbb R)$ is zero. If $\omega _1,\omega _2$ are two such metrics, with positive potentials $U_1,U_2$ and cohomologous Lee forms $\lambda _1$ and $\lambda _2=\lambda _1+\mathrm da$, then for every choice of the additive constant in $a$,

\[ \mathrm e^{-a}\omega _2-\omega _1=D_{\lambda _1}(\mathrm e^{-a}U_2-U_1). \]

In particular, among metrics of the form (29.20), a solution of (29.11) is determined by its volume form and Lee class, even if the potential $u$ in (29.11) is not assumed radial.

Proof. Write $l=kC^{-m}<0$ and $f(r)=\int _0^ rl(s)\, \mathrm ds$ for real $r$. Then $f(r+2)=f(r)+L_0$. The solution of $U'-lU=C$ given by

\[ \begin{aligned} U(0)& =\frac{\mathrm e^{L_0}}{1-\mathrm e^{L_0}} \int _0^2\mathrm e^{-f(s)}C(s)\, \mathrm ds,\\ U(r)& =\mathrm e^{f(r)} \left(U(0)+\int _0^ r\mathrm e^{-f(s)}C(s)\, \mathrm ds\right) \end{aligned} \]

is positive on $[0,2]$ and satisfies $U(2)=U(0)$. Since the equation is periodic, all derivatives also match at the endpoints, so $U$ defines a positive smooth function on $H_ n$. By (29.8), its twisted Hessian is

\[ \begin{split} D_\lambda U & =(U'-lU)\alpha + \bigl [U''-2lU'+(l^2-l')U\bigr ]\beta \\ & =C\alpha +(C'-lC)\beta =\omega . \end{split} \]

This proves that the twisted class is zero. The identity $\mathrm e^{-a}D_{\lambda _2}U_2=D_{\lambda _1}(\mathrm e^{-a}U_2)$ of section 29.4 gives the formula for the difference. For two metrics of the form (29.20), equal volume forms give the same function $F$, and equal Lee classes give the same period, so theorem 29.4.6 applies. This uniqueness concerns the metric, not the potentials representing it. $\square$

Proposition 29.4.8. If $F$ is nonconstant, the metric of theorem 29.4.6 is not Vaisman.

Proof. The radial complex line is orthogonal to $\ker \mathrm dr\cap \ker \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}r$. On a radial complex line, use a logarithmic coordinate $w=x+\mathrm i y$, so that $r=2x+\text{constant}$ and $\beta =\pi ^{-1}\mathrm dx\wedge \mathrm dy$. The Riemannian metric of $\omega $ restricts there to $D\pi ^{-1}(\mathrm dx^2+\mathrm dy^2)$, whence

\[ |\mathrm dr|_\omega ^2=\frac{4\pi }{D},\qquad |\lambda |_\omega ^2=4\pi k^2\frac{C^{-2m}}{D}. \]

A parallel Lee form has constant norm. Since $k\ne 0$, the Vaisman condition would therefore imply $D=Q C^{-2m}$ for a constant $Q>0$. At a maximum and at a minimum of $C$, where $C'=0$, the Gauduchon identity would then give $kC^{m+1}+Q=0$. This equation has only one positive root, so the maximum and the minimum of $C$ coincide. Thus $C$, $D$ and $F=C^ mD$ would all be constant, a contradiction. $\square$

Example 29.4.9. For

\[ \begin{gathered} 0<\varepsilon <\min \left\{ \frac12, \frac{(3/2)^{1-m}}{4\pi }\right\} , \\ C=1+\varepsilon \cos (\pi r),\qquad D=C'+C^{1-m}, \end{gathered} \]

the metric (29.20) is Gauduchon and non-Vaisman, with Lee form $\lambda =-C^{-m}\mathrm dr$ and density $F=C^ mC'+C$. The probability volume $\mathrm dV=\frac12\alpha ^ m\wedge \beta $ gives $\omega ^ n=2nF\, \mathrm dV$ and $\int _{H_ n}F\, \mathrm dV=1$.

Proof. Since $1/2<C<3/2$ and $m\geq 1$, we have $D\geq (3/2)^{1-m}-\pi \varepsilon >\frac34(3/2)^{1-m}>0$. The Gauduchon identity holds with $k=-1$, giving the Lee form and the positive density $F=C^ mD$. Its values $F(0)=1+\varepsilon $ and $F(1)=1-\varepsilon $ differ, so proposition 29.4.8 applies. The integral of $C^ mC'$ over a period is zero and the average of $C$ is one, so integration along the fibers of $q$ gives $\int _{H_ n}F\, \mathrm dV=1$. $\square$

The volume form in this example is not invariant under the flow of the Lee field of the Vaisman metric $\alpha +\beta $, which translates $r$, so theorem 29.4.2 does not apply to it. For the new metric, the Gauduchon condition states that $lF/D=lC^ m=k$ is constant; up to a constant factor, this quantity is the flux of its Lee field through the level sets of $r$, and here it equals $-1$. The radial classification answers 29.4.1 within the family (29.20); uniqueness among arbitrary LCK metrics remains open.
