Absorbing the curvature error

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 16.5.2 Absorbing the curvature error

When $\theta =\omega $ is Hermitian, we can take $h=\omega $, and dividing the approximants by $1+\epsilon _ j$ absorbs the curvature error. If the error bounds are rational, the analytic coefficients remain rational, so the whole approximation takes place in the fixed class $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$.

Corollary 16.5.6. Let $\omega $ be Hermitian and let $u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$. There are $b_ j\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\omega )$ with analytic singularities and decreasing ordinary singularity types, such that $b_ j\to u$ in $L^1$ and

\[ P_\omega [b_ j]=P_\omega [b_ j]_{\mathcal I}\searrow P_\omega [u]_{\mathcal I}. \]

Consequently the classes of potentials with analytic singularities are dense in the whole metric space of ideal classes of proposition 16.4.1. The equality $P_\omega [u]=P_\omega [u]_{\mathcal I}$ is equivalent to convergence of these ordinary envelopes to $P_\omega [u]$ in $L^1$.

Proof. Use $h=\omega $ in (16.3). Enlarge the error bounds to positive rational numbers $\epsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, and set

\[ c_ j=\frac1{1+\epsilon _ j},\qquad b_ j=c_ ja_ j. \]

Then $c_ j\uparrow 1$ is rational and

\[ \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}b_ j =c_ j\bigl ((1+\epsilon _ j)\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}a_ j\bigr )\geq 0. \]

The analytic coefficient of $b_ j$ is $c_ j/m_ j$. Moreover $b_ j\to u$ in $L^1$, since $a_ j\to u$ there and the sequence has bounded $L^1$ norm. Because $a_{j+1}\preceq a_ j$, $c_{j+1}\geq c_ j$, and each $a_ j$ is bounded above, one has $b_{j+1}\preceq b_ j$.

Put $q_ j=P_{(1+\epsilon _ j)\omega }[a_ j]=P_{(1+\epsilon _ j)\omega }[a_ j]_{\mathcal I}$. The preceding theorem gives $q_ j\searrow P_\omega [u]_{\mathcal I}$ pointwise and in $L^1$. Since $b_ j$ has analytic singularities, proposition 16.2.7 and the homogeneity in proposition 16.2.6 give

\[ r_ j:=P_\omega [b_ j]=P_\omega [b_ j]_{\mathcal I}=c_ jq_ j. \]

Here $q_ j\leq 0$ decreases and $c_ j\uparrow 1$, so $r_ j$ also decreases, with pointwise limit $P_\omega [u]_{\mathcal I}$. Moreover

\[ P_\omega [u]_{\mathcal I}\leq q_ j\leq r_ j\leq 0. \]

Since the lower bound is integrable, dominated convergence gives convergence in $L^1$.

The distance in proposition 16.4.1 from $[b_ j]_{\mathcal I}$ to $[u]_{\mathcal I}$ is exactly the $L^1$ distance from $r_ j$ to $P_\omega [u]_{\mathcal I}$, so it tends to zero. The last assertion follows because the limit of the $r_ j$ has been identified. $\square$
