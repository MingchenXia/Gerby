Two ways of measuring a singularity

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Two ways of measuring a singularity

The first way is local and holomorphic. The Lelong number of a plurisubharmonic function measures its logarithmic concentration at a point, while its multiplier ideals record which holomorphic functions remain square integrable against the corresponding exponential weight. The proofs that multiplier ideals are coherent, and that integrability persists after a small increase of the weight, rely on holomorphic extension. Weighted $L^2$ estimates for the $\bar\partial $ operator are the basic tool: they correct the error introduced by a cutoff function, prescribe values along slices and produce the holomorphic functions used in approximation. Potentials with analytic singularities, defined by finitely many holomorphic functions, make the resulting growth explicit; on a resolution of singularities it is described by divisors.

The second way is nonlinear. For locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions, Bedford and Taylor defined the exterior products of their curvature currents and proved that these products are continuous along decreasing sequences. For an unbounded function one first truncates the potential and then discards the part of the product carried by its pole set. When the products obtained in this way have locally bounded mass, their limit is the non-pluripolar product. This operation can lose mass, and the relation between this loss and the singularities of the potentials is one of the central themes of the global theory.

The two measurements need not agree. Using an atom-free probability measure carried by a polar Cantor set (proposition 4.2.4), we construct in example 15.3.8 a potential on the projective line whose Lelong numbers all vanish and whose multiplier ideals are all trivial, but whose non-pluripolar mass is zero. This example, which is [Example 6.1.3, XiaSampleBook], shows that neither Lelong numbers nor multiplier ideals can replace the mass theory developed in the later chapters.
