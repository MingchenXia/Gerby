Convex envelope algebra

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Proposition 18.2.3
Proposition 18.2.3.

  1. The pointwise supremum of a family of convex functions is convex. The pointwise limit of a decreasing net of convex functions is convex; it may take the value $-\infty $.

  2. Let $(F_ i)_{i\in I}$ be a nonempty family of proper convex functions on $N_{\mathbb R}$, and put

    \[ H(x)=\inf \sum _{j=1}^ q\lambda _ jF_{i_ j}(x_ j), \]

    where the infimum is taken over the values $\sum _ j\lambda _ jF_{i_ j}(x_ j)$ of all admissible combinations for $x$, that is, over all $q\geq 1$, $i_ j\in I$, $x_ j\in \operatorname {dom}F_{i_ j}$ and $\lambda _ j>0$ with $\sum _ j\lambda _ j=1$ and $\sum _ j\lambda _ jx_ j=x$; we set $H(x)=+\infty $ if no such combination exists. Then $H$, the convex envelope of the family, is the largest convex function below $\inf _ iF_ i$, and $H^*=\sup _ iF_ i^*$. If $H$ has an affine minorant, then its lower semicontinuous regularization $\overline H$ is the largest lower semicontinuous convex function below $\inf _ iF_ i$, and $\overline H=(\sup _ iF_ i^*)^*$.

  3. For proper convex functions $F,G$ on $N_{\mathbb R}$, the infimal convolution

    \[ (F\mathbin \square G)(x)=\inf _{y+z=x}(F(y)+G(z)) \]

    is convex, and $(F\mathbin \square G)^*=F^*+G^*$.

  4. Let $F,G$ be finite convex functions on $N_{\mathbb R}$ and $p\in M_{\mathbb R}$. Then

    \[ (F+G)^*(p)=\min _{q+r=p}(F^*(q)+G^*(r)) \]

    whenever the right-hand side is finite, the minimum being attained; and $(F+G)^*(p)=+\infty $ if $F^*(q)+G^*(r)=+\infty $ for every decomposition $p=q+r$.

Proof. (1) The epigraph of a supremum is the intersection of the epigraphs. Let $(F_ i)$ be a decreasing net of convex functions with limit $F=\inf _ iF_ i$, let $x,y\in N_{\mathbb R}$ and $0<\lambda <1$, and let $a>F(x)$ and $b>F(y)$ be real numbers. Choose indices $i_1,i_2$ with $F_{i_1}(x)<a$ and $F_{i_2}(y)<b$, and an index $i$ following both. Then $F_ i(x)<a$ and $F_ i(y)<b$, and convexity of $\operatorname {epi}F_ i$ gives

\[ F((1-\lambda )x+\lambda y)\leq F_ i((1-\lambda )x+\lambda y)\leq (1-\lambda )a+\lambda b. \]

Since $a$ and $b$ are arbitrary, this shows that $\operatorname {epi}F$ is convex; this also covers the case where $F(x)$ or $F(y)$ equals $-\infty $.

(2) If $H(x)<a$ and $H(x')<a'$, we can choose admissible combinations for $x$ and $x'$ with values below $a$ and $a'$; mixing them with weights $1-\lambda $ and $\lambda $ gives an admissible combination for $(1-\lambda )x+\lambda x'$ with value below $(1-\lambda )a+\lambda a'$. Hence $H$ is convex. Combinations with $q=1$ show $H\leq F_ i$ for each $i$. If $G$ is convex and $G\leq F_ i$ for all $i$, then for every admissible combination the points $(x_ j,F_{i_ j}(x_ j))$ lie in $\operatorname {epi}G$, and so does their convex combination with the weights $\lambda _ j$, since a convex set contains the convex combinations of its points; that is,

\[ G\Bigl (\sum _ j\lambda _ jx_ j\Bigr )\leq \sum _ j\lambda _ jF_{i_ j}(x_ j). \]

Hence $G\leq H$. For the conjugate, the supremum defining $H^*(p)$ is the supremum over all admissible combinations of

\[ \Bigl \langle p,\sum _ j\lambda _ jx_ j\Bigr \rangle -\sum _ j\lambda _ jF_{i_ j}(x_ j)=\sum _ j\lambda _ j\bigl (\langle p,x_ j\rangle -F_{i_ j}(x_ j)\bigr )\leq \sup _ iF_ i^*(p), \]

and combinations with $q=1$ give equality. Since affine functions are convex, an affine function lies below $H$ if and only if it lies below $\inf _ iF_ i$. Suppose that $H$ has an affine minorant. Then $H$ is proper, so $\overline H$ is a closed proper convex function by proposition 18.1.4, and $\overline H=H^{**}=(\sup _ iF_ i^*)^*$ by theorem 18.2.2. A lower semicontinuous convex function below $\inf _ iF_ i$ lies below $H$, and therefore below $\overline H$.

(3) Convexity of $F\mathbin \square G$ follows as in (2), by mixing decompositions $x=y+z$ and $x'=y'+z'$ whose values $F(y)+G(z)$ are close to the infima. The two suprema over $y$ and $z$ in

\[ (F\mathbin \square G)^*(p)=\sup _{y,z}\bigl (\langle p,y\rangle -F(y)+\langle p,z\rangle -G(z)\bigr )=F^*(p)+G^*(p) \]

are independent, which gives the conjugate formula.

(4) If $n=0$, then $F$ and $G$ are constants and both sides equal $-F(0)-G(0)$; so let $n\geq 1$. Let $J(p)=\inf _{q+r=p}(F^*(q)+G^*(r))$. Each conjugate is bounded below by minus its value at the origin: $F^*\geq -F(0)$ and $G^*\geq -G(0)$. Moreover, for every $R>0$, choosing $x$ with $|x|=R$ and $\langle q,x\rangle =R|q|$ gives

\[ F^*(q)\geq R|q|-\max _{|x|\leq R}F(x), \]

and the analogous bound holds for $G^*$. Consequently, if $p$ stays bounded and $F^*(q)+G^*(r)$ stays bounded above, then $q$ and $r=p-q$ stay bounded: for instance $F^*(q)\leq F^*(q)+G^*(r)+G(0)$, and the last bound with $R=1$ controls $|q|$. Lower semicontinuity of $F^*$ and $G^*$ therefore implies that the infimum defining $J(p)$ is attained whenever it is finite: a minimizing sequence of decompositions has a convergent subsequence. The same argument, applied to $p_ k\to p$ and decompositions attaining $J(p_ k)$, shows that $J$ is lower semicontinuous. By (3), $J=F^*\mathbin \square G^*$ is convex; it is bounded below by $-F(0)-G(0)$, and it is proper, since $J(q_0+r_0)\leq F^*(q_0)+G^*(r_0)<+\infty $ for subgradients $q_0\in \partial F(0)$ and $r_0\in \partial G(0)$. Finite convex functions are continuous, so $F$ and $G$ are closed and $F^{**}=F$, $G^{**}=G$. By (3), $J^*=F^{**}+G^{**}=F+G$, and theorem 18.2.2 gives $J=J^{**}=(F+G)^*$. $\square$
