Chapter 13 · Singularity types, envelopes, and models · Tag 6EFE0C8DBCE3

Big regular obstacle density

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Corollary 16.2.32

Corollary 16.2.32. Suppose that $\{ \theta \} $ is big, and let $f$ be a continuous real function with bounded metric Laplacian. Then

\[ \langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}P_\theta (f))^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ P_\theta (f)=f\} }(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ n. \]

In particular $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}V_\theta )^ n\rangle =\mathbf1_{\{ V_\theta =0\} }\theta ^ n$. The same identities hold in every pseudo-effective class; this is corollary 17.4.22 in the next chapter, which adds a zero-mass argument for classes that are not big.

Proof. A constant shift of $V_\theta $ lies below the bounded function $f$, so the competitor family of $P_\theta (f)$ is nonempty, and it is bounded above by $\max _ Xf$. Since $f$ is continuous, $u=P_\theta (f)$ satisfies $u\leq f$ everywhere, by proposition 16.1.4. Theorem 16.2.31, with all $\theta _ j=\theta $, $u_ j=u$ and $f_ j=f$, identifies the restriction of $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n\rangle $ to $\{ u=f\} $ with that of $(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f)^ n$. By theorem 16.2.5, applied to the continuous and hence quasi-continuous obstacle $f$, the measure $\langle (\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n\rangle $ is carried by $\{ u=f\} $. This proves the first identity. For $f=0$ the envelope is $V_\theta $ by definition, which gives the second. $\square$
