Chapter 5 · Plurisubharmonic functions and positive currents · Tag F031A16FF76F

Compact obstacle polar invariance

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Proposition 1.19.6

Proposition 1.19.6. Let $\theta $ be big, let $K\subseteq X$ be compact and non-pluripolar, and let $\psi $ be a bounded real function on $K$. Set

\[ \begin{aligned} R& =\sup \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\leq \psi \text{ on }K\} ,\\ Q& =\sup \{ u\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ):u\leq \psi \text{ q.e. on }K\} . \end{aligned} \]

Then $R^*=Q^*$ belongs to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ and has minimal singularities; it is bounded if $\theta \geq 0$. Moreover, this regularized envelope does not change if the pointwise constraint is imposed only on $K\setminus E$, for any pluripolar subset $E\subseteq K$.

Proof. Put $m=\inf _ K\psi $ and $M=\sup _ K\psi $. Since $\theta $ is big, $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ is nonempty, and $V_\theta \leq 0$; hence $V_\theta +m$ belongs to the family defining $R$. Applying lemma 1.19.1 with the constant obstacle $M$ gives a common upper bound $C$ for the larger family defining $Q$. The upper-envelope argument at the beginning of section 1.19 therefore shows that $R^*$ and $Q^*$ belong to $\mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$.

Now fix a competitor $u$ for $Q$ and define a bounded function on $X$ by

\[ f(x)= \begin{cases} \psi (x),& x\in K,\\ \max \{ \sup _ Xu,M\} ,& x\notin K. \end{cases} \]

Then $u\leq f$ quasi-everywhere. By lemma 1.19.5, there are $u_ t\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )$ satisfying $u_ t\leq f$ everywhere and $u_ t\to u$ almost everywhere. Each $u_ t$ is a competitor for $R$, so $R^*\geq u$ almost everywhere, and lemma 1.3.3 gives this inequality everywhere. Taking the supremum over $u$ and regularizing yields $R^*\geq Q^*$; the inclusion of the competitor families gives the reverse inequality.

Write $F=R^*=Q^*$. Since $F\leq C$, the potential $F-C$ is a competitor for $V_\theta $. Thus

\[ V_\theta +m\leq F\leq V_\theta +C, \]

which proves minimal singularities and, when $\theta \geq 0$, boundedness.

Finally, if $E\subseteq K$ is pluripolar, the family constrained on $K\setminus E$ lies between the families defining $R$ and $Q$, so its regularized supremum also equals $F$. Note that this argument uses the upper bound obtained from the compact set $K$, even when $K\setminus E$ is not compact. $\square$
