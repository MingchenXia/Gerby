From distributions to pointwise potentials

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · From distributions to pointwise potentials

A locally integrable function has distributional derivatives of every order, but as a distribution it cannot see its values on a set of measure zero. Potential theory recovers these values from mean-value inequalities. If the distributional Laplacian of a function is a positive measure, the function has a canonical subharmonic representative, whose value at each point is the limit of its averages over small balls. Harmonic replacement on balls then controls maxima, boundary values and upper envelopes. The exceptional sets that occur in these constructions are measured by capacity rather than by Lebesgue measure. For this reason measure theory, the Dirichlet problem, energy and the fine topology together form the first part of the book.

In several variables a further difficulty appears. Plurisubharmonicity requires the submean inequality on every complex line, and an upper bound obtained separately on each line need not be uniform on a neighborhood. The Fourier and incidence estimates of chapter 6 provide this uniformity. With them we show in theorem 8.1.3 that upper semicontinuity is automatic for a function that is subharmonic on every complex line, and we identify plurisubharmonic functions with the canonical representatives of distributions whose complex Hessian is positive. Compactness and envelope arguments can therefore be carried out for genuine functions, not only for almost-everywhere classes.
