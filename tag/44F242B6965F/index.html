Positive mixed mass bound

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Proposition 15.8.2

Proposition 15.8.2. The numbers of definition 15.8.1 do not depend on the chosen smooth representatives or minimal potentials, nor on the Kähler form within its class. The limit in definition 15.8.1 exists for all pseudo-effective classes, does not depend on the Kähler class used for the perturbation, and equals the number defined by the display when all $\alpha _ j$ are big. For closed positive currents $T_ j\in \alpha _ j$,

\[ \int _ X\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ p\rangle \wedge \omega ^{n-p} \leq I_\omega (\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ p). \]

The numbers are positively homogeneous in each class and increase when a pseudo-effective class is added to any argument. For nef classes they equal the cohomological intersection numbers $\int _ X\theta _1\wedge \cdots \wedge \theta _ p\wedge \omega ^{n-p}$. Big classes have positive volume.

Proof. Big classes. Any two potentials with minimal singularities in a fixed big class differ by a bounded function, and a change of smooth representative changes each potential by a smooth function. Hence corollary 15.7.5 shows that $I_\omega $ does not depend on these choices. The same corollary and minimality give the upper bound for closed positive currents in big classes. If $\omega '=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ is another Kähler form in the class of $\omega $, with $f$ smooth, then $\omega '^{n-p}-\omega ^{n-p}=\mathrm d\bigl (\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}f\wedge \sum _{i=0}^{n-p-1}\omega '^ i\wedge \omega ^{n-p-1-i}\bigr )$ is a smooth exact form. Its integral against the closed current $\langle T_1\wedge \cdots \wedge T_ p\rangle $ vanishes, because $S(\mathrm d\psi )=\pm (\mathrm dS)(\psi )=0$ for a closed current $S$ and a smooth form $\psi $ on the compact manifold $X$. Hence the numbers for big classes do not depend on $\omega $ within its class, and neither do their limits, since the perturbing classes $\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ are the same.

Let $\alpha $ be big and $\gamma $ pseudo-effective, represented by a closed positive current $S$. Adding $S$ to a current with minimal singularities in $\alpha $ gives a current in the big class $\alpha +\gamma $, which by the upper bound has mixed mass at most the number with $\alpha $ replaced by $\alpha +\gamma $. By multilinearity and positivity, its mixed mass is at least the number for $\alpha $. Hence, when all arguments are big, replacing an argument $\alpha $ by $\alpha +\gamma $ increases the number. Homogeneity for big classes follows by scaling a minimal potential together with its background: if $c>0$, then $cV_\theta $ has minimal singularities in $\mathrm{PSH}(X,c\theta )$, and the product is homogeneous in each factor by proposition 15.1.5.

Pseudo-effective classes. By this monotonicity for big classes, the perturbed numbers decrease as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$; they are nonnegative, so the limit exists. Every Kähler form $\kappa $ satisfies $c^{-1}\omega \leq \kappa \leq c\omega $ for some $c\geq 1$ (lemma 13.1.1), so monotonicity places the perturbation by $\varepsilon \kappa $ between the perturbations by $c^{-1}\varepsilon \omega $ and $c\varepsilon \omega $, with the same fixed test form $\omega ^{n-p}$; this proves independence of the perturbing class. The same comparison shows that perturbing the arguments by different fixed multiples of $\varepsilon \omega $ gives the same limit, which extends homogeneity to pseudo-effective classes. Monotonicity also extends: if $\gamma $ is pseudo-effective, then for every $\varepsilon >0$ the number with an argument $\alpha +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ replaced by $\alpha +\gamma +\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ is at least the original one, by the big case, and we let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$.

If all $\alpha _ j$ are big, choose $\delta >0$ such that each $\alpha _ j$ contains a Kähler current $R_ j\geq \delta \omega $. Then $(1+\varepsilon /\delta )\alpha _ j-(\alpha _ j+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )=(\varepsilon /\delta )\{ R_ j-\delta \omega \} $ is pseudo-effective, so monotonicity and homogeneity give

\[ I_\omega (\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ p)\leq I_\omega (\alpha _1+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} ,\ldots ,\alpha _ p+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} )\leq (1+\varepsilon /\delta )^ pI_\omega (\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ p), \]

and the limit is $I_\omega (\alpha _1,\ldots ,\alpha _ p)$. For arbitrary closed positive currents $T_ j\in \alpha _ j$, the upper bound in the big classes $\alpha _ j+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $, applied to $T_ j+\varepsilon \omega $, and multilinearity give $\int _ X\langle \bigwedge _ jT_ j\rangle \wedge \omega ^{n-p}\leq I_\omega (\alpha _1+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} ,\ldots )$, and letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ proves the upper bound.

Nef and big classes. If the $\alpha _ j$ are nef, their perturbations are Kähler classes: $\alpha _ j+\varepsilon \{ \omega \} $ contains $\theta _{j,\varepsilon /2}+\varepsilon \omega \geq \varepsilon \omega /2$, where $\theta _{j,\varepsilon /2}\geq -(\varepsilon /2)\omega $ is a smooth representative of $\alpha _ j$ given by definition 14.3.1. If $\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}f$ is a Kähler form with $f$ smooth, then $f\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega )$, so $V_{\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega }\geq f-\sup _ Xf$ by lemma 14.2.7; since also $V_{\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega }\leq 0$ by the same lemma, the minimal potentials are bounded. By proposition 15.4.1, the perturbed numbers are the integrals $\int _ X\bigwedge _ j(\theta _ j+\varepsilon \omega )\wedge \omega ^{n-p}$, and their limits are the cohomological intersection numbers. Finally, let $\alpha $ be big and $T_0\in \alpha $ a Kähler current with $T_0\geq \delta \omega $. Expanding $T_0=\delta \omega +(T_0-\delta \omega )$ by multilinearity gives $\langle T_0^ n\rangle \geq \delta ^ n\omega ^ n$, so $\operatorname {vol}(\alpha )\geq \delta ^ n\int _ X\omega ^ n>0$ by the upper bound. $\square$
