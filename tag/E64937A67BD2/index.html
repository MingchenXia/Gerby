Positive capacity domination

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · Lemma 12.10.9

Lemma 12.10.9. On $K\Subset U$, the positive shifted currents $Q_ p(u_1,\ldots ,u_ p)$, $1\leq p\leq m$, for globally bounded admissible potentials have trace and coefficient variations bounded on Borel $E\subset K$ by $C c_ m^*(E)$. The constants are uniform when all potentials have a common bound.

Proof. First take smooth potentials, put $\sigma _ i=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$, and choose the fixed local correction $\tau =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho \geq 2\omega $. Then $\beta _ i=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u_ i+\rho )=\sigma _ i+(\tau -\omega )$ lies in the same local cone. Since $\tau -\omega \geq 0$, mixed positivity gives

\[ 0\leq \int _ E Q_ p\wedge \omega ^{m-p} \leq \int _ E\beta _1\cdots \beta _ p\omega ^{n-p} \leq C c_ m(E),\qquad E\subset K. \]

Here $Q_ p\wedge \omega ^{m-p}=\sigma _1\cdots \sigma _ p\omega ^{n-p}$. The last bound follows from smooth mixed-trace domination at $s=m$ for the bounded corrected potentials and a normalized bounded sum with a fixed strictly plurisubharmonic function. Positivity of $Q_ p$ bounds each of its coefficient variations on the compact coordinate set by a fixed multiple of its trace measure.

For bounded potentials choose the smooth decreasing global approximations already established in the chapter. The joint convergence in 12.10.8 identifies their positive-current limit with the previously constructed $Q_ p$. On a compact neighborhood of $K$ inside $U$, lower semicontinuity on open sets preserves trace and coefficient variation estimates. For an arbitrary open $G\supset E$ intersect with a fixed relatively compact neighborhood of $K$ before taking this limit; its capacity is at most $c_ m(G)$. Therefore every such trace or variation measure $\mu $ satisfies $\mu (E)\leq Cc_ m(G)$. Infimize over $G$ to obtain the asserted outer-open bound. The constants of the smooth estimates are independent of the approximation index. The argument does not multiply a rough current of positive order or use an unproved rough diagonal polynomial expansion. $\square$
