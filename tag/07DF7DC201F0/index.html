Mixed products for a common measure

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 9.11 Mixed products for a common measure

Corollary 9.7.6 bounds a mixed Monge–Ampère product from below when the pure Monge–Ampère measures of the factors have continuous lower densities with respect to Lebesgue measure. For non-pluripolar products in chapter 15 we need the same inequality relative to an arbitrary positive Radon measure $\mu $, which may be singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. Instead of regularizing the potentials by convolution, as in the proof of that corollary, we restrict each pure Monge–Ampère measure to a compact set and replace it by its cube averages, as in proposition 9.10.3. The averaged measures have piecewise constant densities, so the case of Lebesgue measure applies on each cube, and Hölder's inequality compares the resulting bound with the cube averages of the expected lower bound. The convergence results of section 9.10 then allow us to pass to the limit.

Theorem 9.11.1. Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$ be open, $n\geq 1$, and let $u_1,\ldots ,u_ n$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega $. Let $\mu $ be a positive Radon measure on $\Omega $, and let $f_1,\ldots ,f_ n$ be nonnegative Borel functions on $\Omega $. If

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n\geq f_ i\mu \qquad (1\leq i\leq n), \]

then

9.75
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bounded-multifactor-common-measure} \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n \geq \left(\prod _{i=1}^ nf_ i^{1/n}\right)\mu . \end{equation}

For $n=1$ the assertion is the hypothesis.

Proof. Let $n\geq 2$, and put $\lambda =(\prod _ if_ i^{1/n})\mu $. We first prove the theorem when $\Omega $ is a ball $B=B(a_0,R)$ and all $u_ i$ belong to $\mathcal E_0(B)$ (Steps 1 and 2), and then reduce the general case to this one by modifying the potentials near the boundary of a ball (Step 3). For a ball $B=B(a_0,R)$ we write $\rho (z)=|z-a_0|^2-R^2$.

We use the generalized Hölder inequality

9.76
\begin{equation} \label{eq:generalized-holder-geometric-mean} \int _ A\prod _{i=1}^ ng_ i^{1/n}\, \mathrm d\nu \leq \prod _{i=1}^ n\Bigl (\int _ Ag_ i\, \mathrm d\nu \Bigr )^{1/n} \end{equation}

for a positive measure $\nu $, a measurable set $A$ and nonnegative measurable functions $g_ i$. If one of the integrals $c_ i=\int _ Ag_ i\, \mathrm d\nu $ vanishes, then $g_ i=0$ $\nu $-almost everywhere on $A$ and both sides vanish, with the convention $0\cdot \infty =0$; if none vanishes and one is infinite, the right side is infinite. Otherwise, integrating the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality $\prod _ i(g_ i/c_ i)^{1/n}\leq \frac1n\sum _ ig_ i/c_ i$ over $A$ gives $\int _ A\prod _ i(g_ i/c_ i)^{1/n}\, \mathrm d\nu \leq 1$, which is (9.76). For every compact $K\subset \Omega $ we have $\int _ Kf_ i\, \mathrm d\mu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n(K)<\infty $ by hypothesis, so (9.76) shows that $\lambda $ is a positive Radon measure.

Step 1. Exhaustion by compactly supported measures. Choose closed balls $K_ l\subset B$ concentric with $B$, increasing with $l$ and exhausting $B$, and set

\[ \alpha _{i,l}=\mathbf1_{K_ l}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n. \]

These measures are finite since $u_ i\in \mathcal E_0(B)$, they have compact support in $B$, and they are dominated by the Monge–Ampère measure of the bounded plurisubharmonic function $u_ i$. By theorem 9.10.12, the equation $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A)^ n=\alpha _{i,l}$ has a bounded solution $A_{i,l}\in \mathcal E_0(B)$ with zero boundary values, and $A_{i,l}$ is the largest bounded plurisubharmonic function $v$ on $B$ with $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\geq \alpha _{i,l}$ and upper boundary limits at most zero. The function $u_ i$ has these properties for every $l$, and so has $A_{i,l+1}$, because $\alpha _{i,l+1}\geq \alpha _{i,l}$. Therefore

\[ u_ i\leq A_{i,l+1}\leq A_{i,l}\leq 0. \]

We claim that $A_{i,l}\downarrow u_ i$ as $l\to \infty $. The decreasing limit $A_{i,\infty }$ satisfies $u_ i\leq A_{i,\infty }\leq 0$, so it is a bounded plurisubharmonic function (theorem 8.3.1) with zero boundary values. By theorem 9.2.5, $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,\infty })^ n$ is the weak limit of $\alpha _{i,l}$, that is, $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,\infty })^ n=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n$, and this measure has finite total mass. Let $\varepsilon >0$ and $S=\{ u_ i<A_{i,\infty }+\varepsilon \rho \} $. Since $u_ i-(A_{i,\infty }+\varepsilon \rho )$ tends to zero at $\partial B$, the comparison theorem (theorem 9.2.8) applies. Expanding $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(A_{i,\infty }+\varepsilon \rho ))^ n$ and dropping the mixed terms, which are positive, we obtain

\[ \int _ S(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n+\varepsilon ^ n\int _ S(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^ n \leq \int _ S\bigl (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(A_{i,\infty }+\varepsilon \rho )\bigr )^ n \leq \int _ S(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n. \]

The integrals of $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n$ are finite, so $S$ is Lebesgue null. Hence $u_ i\geq A_{i,\infty }+\varepsilon \rho $ almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere on $B$, by the uniqueness assertion of theorem 8.2.1 applied to $\max \{ u_ i,A_{i,\infty }+\varepsilon \rho \} $ and $u_ i$. Letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ gives $A_{i,\infty }=u_ i$, as claimed.

Step 2. A common grid. Fix $l$. Choose a sequence of partitions of $\mathbb C^ n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ into half-open cubes whose side lengths decrease to zero, the same for all $i$. Let $\alpha _{i,l,j}$ be the corresponding cube averages of $\alpha _{i,l}$, as in proposition 9.10.3, and let $A_{i,l,j}\in \mathcal E_0(B)$ be the corresponding canonical approximants of $\alpha _{i,l}$, that is, the zero-boundary solutions of $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l,j})^ n=\alpha _{i,l,j}$; they are defined for all large $j$. Discarding finitely many indices $j$, we may assume that they are defined for all $j$ and all $i$, and that every cube meeting $K_ l$ has its closure in $B$. By theorem 9.10.12, $A_{i,l,j}\to A_{i,l}$ locally in capacity as $j\to \infty $. By proposition 9.10.3, applied with the bounded subsolution $u_ i$, the energies $e_1(A_{i,l,j})$ are bounded uniformly in $j$; since there are only finitely many indices $i$, they have a common bound. (If $\alpha _{i,l}=0$, all these solutions vanish.) Therefore corollary 9.10.14 gives

9.77
\begin{equation} \label{eq:common-measure-grid-mixed-limit} \bigwedge _{i=1}^ n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l,j} \longrightarrow \bigwedge _{i=1}^ n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l} \end{equation}

weakly on $B$.

Let $\lambda _ l=\mathbf1_{K_ l}\lambda $, a finite measure, and let $\lambda _{l,j}$ be its cube averages. For every cube $Q$, the hypothesis gives $\alpha _{i,l}(Q)\geq \int _{Q\cap K_ l}f_ i\, \mathrm d\mu $, and (9.76) then gives

9.78
\begin{equation} \label{eq:common-measure-grid-holder} \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\frac{\alpha _{i,l}(Q)}{|Q|}\right)^{1/n} \geq \frac1{|Q|}\int _{Q\cap K_ l}\prod _{i=1}^ nf_ i^{1/n}\, \mathrm d\mu =\frac{\lambda _ l(Q)}{|Q|}. \end{equation}

Let $Q$ be a cube with $\lambda _ l(Q)>0$; it meets $K_ l$, so its interior lies in $B$. There the measures $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l,j})^ n=\alpha _{i,l,j}$ have the constant densities $\alpha _{i,l}(Q)/|Q|$ with respect to Lebesgue measure $\mathrm dV$. Recall that $\eta ^ n=c_ n\, \mathrm dV$ with $\eta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2$ and $c_ n=n!\pi ^{-n}$, by (9.2). Applying corollary 9.7.6 on the open set $\operatorname{Int}Q$ to the constant densities $c_ n^{-1}\alpha _{i,l}(Q)/|Q|$ with respect to $\eta ^ n$, and using (9.78), we obtain on $\operatorname{Int}Q$

\[ \bigwedge _{i=1}^ n\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l,j} \geq \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\frac{\alpha _{i,l}(Q)}{c_ n|Q|}\right)^{1/n}\eta ^ n =\prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\frac{\alpha _{i,l}(Q)}{|Q|}\right)^{1/n}\mathrm dV \geq \lambda _{l,j}. \]

The factor $c_ n$ cancels because the $n$ exponents $1/n$ sum to one. The measure $\lambda _{l,j}$ vanishes on the cubes with $\lambda _ l(Q)=0$ and does not charge the cube boundaries, which are Lebesgue null. Since the mixed product is positive, we conclude that $\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l,j}\geq \lambda _{l,j}$ on $B$.

As $j\to \infty $, $\lambda _{l,j}\to \lambda _ l$ weakly. Indeed, for $\chi \in C_ c(B)$, replacing the values of $\chi $ by its cube averages changes $\int \chi \, \mathrm d\lambda _ l$ by at most $\lambda _ l(B)$ times the modulus of continuity of $\chi $ at the cube diameter. Letting $j\to \infty $ and using (9.77), we obtain $\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l}\geq \lambda _ l$. Finally let $l\to \infty $. By Step 1, $A_{i,l}\downarrow u_ i$ with $u_ i\leq A_{i,l}\leq 0$, so theorem 9.2.5 gives $\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}A_{i,l}\to \bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$ weakly, while $\lambda _ l$ increases to $\lambda $. Testing with nonnegative functions in $C_ c(B)$ and using monotone convergence on the right, we obtain (9.75) for potentials in $\mathcal E_0(B)$.

Step 3. Localization. Now let $u_1,\ldots ,u_ n$ be locally bounded on $\Omega $. Choose a ball $B=B(a_0,R)$ with $\overline B\subset \Omega $ and a compact set $K\subset B$. Since adding constants does not change the products, we may assume that $u_ i\leq -1$ on $B$. With constants $C_ i>0$ so large that $C_ i\rho <\inf _ Bu_ i$ on a neighborhood of $K$, put

\[ \widetilde u_ i=\max \{ u_ i,C_ i\rho \} . \]

Then $\widetilde u_ i=u_ i$ on a neighborhood of $K$, and $\widetilde u_ i=C_ i\rho $ near $\partial B$, because $C_ i\rho \to 0$ at $\partial B$ while $u_ i\leq -1$. Thus $\widetilde u_ i\in \mathcal E_0(B)$: its Monge–Ampère measure is smooth near $\partial B$ and locally finite on the rest of $B$. By locality of the products on the open neighborhood of $K$ where $\widetilde u_ i=u_ i$, we have $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde u_ i)^ n\geq f_ i\mathbf1_ K\mu $ on $B$. The case already proved, applied to the measure $\mathbf1_ K\mu $, gives $\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\widetilde u_ i\geq \mathbf1_ K\lambda $, and on a neighborhood of $K$ the left side equals $\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$ by locality. Thus $P=\bigwedge _ i\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i$ satisfies $P(E)\geq \lambda (E)$ for every Borel set $E\subset K$. The interiors of such compact sets $K$ cover $\Omega $, and since $\Omega $ has a countable basis, countably many of them, say $\operatorname{Int}L_1,\operatorname{Int}L_2,\ldots $, already cover $\Omega $. A Borel set $E\subset \Omega $ is the disjoint union of the Borel sets $E_ m=(E\cap L_ m)\setminus \bigcup _{m'<m}L_{m'}$, and therefore $P(E)=\sum _ mP(E_ m)\geq \sum _ m\lambda (E_ m)=\lambda (E)$. $\square$

Corollary 9.11.2. Let $u,v$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, and let $\mu $ be a positive Radon measure on $\Omega $. If $f,g$ are nonnegative Borel functions on $\Omega $ and

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n\geq f\mu ,\qquad (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n\geq g\mu , \]

then, for each integer $1\leq k\leq n-1$,

9.79
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bounded-common-measure-mixed} (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ k\wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^{n-k} \geq f^{k/n}g^{(n-k)/n}\mu . \end{equation}

For $k=0$ and $k=n$ the corresponding inequalities are the hypotheses; for $n=1$ only these occur.

Proof. Apply theorem 9.11.1 to $k$ copies of $u$ with density $f$ and $n-k$ copies of $v$ with density $g$. $\square$

Corollary 9.11.3. Under the hypotheses of corollary 9.11.2,

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u+v))^ n\geq (f^{1/n}+g^{1/n})^ n\mu . \]

Proof. By additivity and symmetry of the products, $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(u+v))^ n=\sum _{k=0}^ n\binom nk(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ k\wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^{n-k}$. Bound the terms with $1\leq k\leq n-1$ by corollary 9.11.2 and the terms with $k=0,n$ by the hypotheses; the binomial formula then gives the assertion. The density on the right is locally $\mu $-integrable, since $f^{k/n}g^{(n-k)/n}\leq f+g$ by the weighted arithmetic–geometric mean inequality, and $f$ and $g$ are locally $\mu $-integrable because $f\mu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ and $g\mu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v)^ n$. $\square$
