The mass polynomial

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 2.2 The mass polynomial

For a smooth real function $u$ on $X$, write $\omega _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$, and set

\[ \mathcal H_\omega =\{ u\in C^\infty (X,\mathbb R):\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u>0\} , \qquad \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n. \]

The set $\mathcal H_\omega $ of smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions is convex and open in the $C^2$ topology, while the mass $\mathrm M_\omega (u)$ is defined for every smooth real $u$. Expanding it along a smooth direction gives a polynomial whose nonconstant coefficients detect the complex Hessians of the powers of the background.

Theorem 2.2.1. For every smooth real function $u$,

2.1
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mass-expansion} \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\int _ X\omega ^ n+ \sum _{k=1}^{n-1}\binom nk \int _ X u(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{k-1}\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-k}). \end{equation}

Proof. Expand $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ by the binomial theorem. The term with $k=n$ integrates to zero, since it is exact:

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=\mathrm{d}\bigl (\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}u\wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{n-1}\bigr ). \]

For $1\leq k\leq n-1$, apply lemma 2.1.1 with $f=u$ and $T=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{k-1}\wedge \omega ^{n-k}$. Since both $\mathrm{d}$ and $\mathrm d^{\mathrm c}$ annihilate $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$,

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}T=(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{k-1}\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-k}), \]

which gives the $k$th term of (2.1). When $n=1$, the sum is empty and the mass is constant. $\square$

If every $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ j)$ with $1\leq j\leq n-1$ vanishes, (2.1) shows that the mass is constant. Conversely, varying the potential in several independent directions recovers each of these forms by polarization.

Theorem 2.2.2 (Chiose). Assume $n\geq 2$. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ is independent of $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $;

  2. $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ j)=0$ for $1\leq j\leq n-1$;

  3. $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$ and $\mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega =0$.

They are also equivalent to the condition that $\int _ X(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ be the same for all bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions $u$, where $(\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ is the Bedford–Taylor product of section 1.7. Under these conditions the common mass is $\int _ X\omega ^ n$.

Proof. Step 1: the first condition implies the second. Let $u$ be a smooth real function. For $|t|$ small, $tu$ belongs to $\mathcal H_\omega $ for both signs of $t$, so the polynomial $t\mapsto \int _ X(\omega +t\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n$ is constant on an interval and its nonconstant coefficients vanish. For $1\leq j\leq n-1$ this gives

\[ \int _ X\omega ^ j\wedge (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^{n-j}=0. \]

Replace $u$ by a linear combination of $n-j$ smooth real functions $u_1,\ldots ,u_{n-j}$ and compare the coefficients of the monomial in which every parameter appears once. We obtain

\[ \int _ X\omega ^ j\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n-j}=0. \]

Integrating by parts in the first factor and using that $u_1$ is arbitrary, we obtain the pointwise identity

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ j)\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_2\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n-j}=0 \]

for every choice of the remaining functions. At a given point, the complex Hessians of these functions can be prescribed arbitrarily: multiply a quadratic polynomial in a coordinate chart by a cutoff function equal to one near that point. Since the identity is multilinear, we may complexify it and use the quadratic monomials $z_ a\bar z_ b$. Wedge products of their complex Hessians give all coordinate forms of the complementary bidegree, and pairing with these forms shows that every coefficient of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^ j)$ vanishes. When $j=n-1$ there are no remaining factors, and the same conclusion follows directly from the arbitrariness of $u_1$. This proves the second condition.

Step 2: the remaining equivalences for smooth potentials. The second condition implies the first by theorem 2.2.1. For $n\geq 3$, the identities for $j=1,2$ and lemma 2.1.2 give $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$ and

\[ 0=\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)=2\omega \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega +2\mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega , \]

so the second condition implies the third. For $n=2$, the second condition is $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$, while $\mathrm{d}\omega \wedge \mathrm d^{\mathrm c}\omega $ vanishes for degree reasons; the implication is again immediate. Conversely, lemma 2.1.2 derives all the identities of the second condition from the third.

Step 3: bounded potentials. Assume the first condition and let $u$ be a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function. By theorem 1.11.1, there are uniformly bounded functions $u_ j\in \mathcal H_\omega $ decreasing to $u$. Their masses all equal $\int _ X\omega ^ n$, and the continuity of the bounded products of section 1.7 along such sequences passes this common value to the limit. Conversely, every element of $\mathcal H_\omega $ is a bounded $\omega $-plurisubharmonic function, so constancy for bounded potentials implies the first condition. The zero potential shows that the common mass is $\int _ X\omega ^ n$. $\square$

In particular, a closed background has invariant mass. Under the equivalent conditions above, (2.1) even gives $\mathrm M_\omega (u)=\int _ X\omega ^ n$ for every smooth real $u$, not only for $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $. In dimension one the mass is always $\int _ X\omega $.

Mass invariance can also hold in a nonclosed background. In theorem 26.17.6, a pluriclosed metric with nonzero exterior derivative satisfies all the lower-power identities above. Those identities fix its bounded-potential mass, even though a family of scalar perturbations can have its least relative eigenvalue tend to zero.

The first variation of the mass involves only the complex Hessian of the power $\omega _ u^{n-1}$ of the perturbed form.

Proposition 2.2.3. For smooth real functions $u,v$ on $X$,

2.2
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mass-first-variation} \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\mathrm M_\omega (u+tv) =n\int _ X v\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _ u^{n-1}). \end{equation}

If $\mathrm M_\omega $ is constant on $\mathcal H_\omega $, then $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _ u^{n-1})=0$ for every $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $.

Proof. Since $\mathrm M_\omega (u+tv)$ is a polynomial in $t$, its derivative at $t=0$ is

\[ n\int _ X\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}v\wedge \omega _ u^{n-1}, \]

and lemma 2.1.1 turns this into the right-hand side of (2.2). For the second assertion, fix $u\in \mathcal H_\omega $. For every smooth real direction $v$, the segment $u+tv$ lies in $\mathcal H_\omega $ when $|t|$ is sufficiently small, so the right-hand side of (2.2) vanishes for all smooth real $v$. Hence the smooth top-degree form $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega _ u^{n-1})$ vanishes. $\square$

Corollary 2.2.4. If $n=2$, then for every smooth real function $u$ on $X$,

\[ \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\int _ X\omega ^2+2\int _ Xu\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega . \]

Consequently, $\mathrm M_\omega $ is constant on $\mathcal H_\omega $ if and only if $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$.

Proof. The formula is the case $n=2$ of (2.1), and it shows that $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$ implies constancy. Conversely, if the mass is constant, proposition 2.2.3 at $u=0$ gives $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega =0$. $\square$

The identity $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^{n-1})=0$ makes the first variation vanish at $u=0$; Hermitian forms satisfying it are the Gauduchon metrics of chapter 4. On a surface this identity already gives mass invariance. In dimension three, however, (2.1) reads

\[ \mathrm M_\omega (u)=\int _ X\omega ^3 +3\int _ Xu\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2) +3\int _ Xu\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u\wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega . \]

Vanishing of $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}(\omega ^2)$ removes the linear term, while $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\omega $ still governs the quadratic term. Thus stationarity of the mass at one potential must be distinguished from its invariance on $\mathcal H_\omega $; an explicit example is given in example 4.8.3.
