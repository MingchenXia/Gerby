Operations on test curves

From Singularities in global pluripotential theory · 9.4 Operations on test curves

Let $X$ be a connected compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $\theta ,\theta ',\theta ''$ be smooth closed real $(1,1)$-forms on $X$ representing big cohomology classes.

In this section, we develop several general operations on test curves, anticipating the applications in non-Archimedean geometry in chapter 13. One may read chapter 13 first and consult this section when necessary.

Definition 9.4.1. Given $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$, we say $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$ if $\Gamma _{\max }\leq \Gamma '_{\max }$ and, for all $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }$, we have

9.66
\begin{equation} \label{eq:GammatauGammap} \Gamma _{\tau }\preceq _ P \Gamma '_{\tau }. \end{equation}
Observe that (9.66) actually holds for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ if $\theta =\theta '$. Here we use the convention that $-\infty \preceq _ P \varphi $ for any $\varphi \in \mathrm{QPSH}(X)\cup \{ -\infty \} $. The relation $\leq $ defines a partial order on $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Lemma 9.4.2. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\omega $ be a closed real smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. $\Gamma \leq \Gamma '$;

  2. $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]\leq P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']$.

Proof. This follows from example 6.1.3. $\square$

  1. We set

  2. for any $\tau <(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\max }$, we define

Lemma 9.4.4. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma ' \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$. Then we have $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$. In particular, for any $\tau <(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\max }$,

\[ \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}} \left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right)\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta '). \]

This potential is $\mathcal{I}$-good if $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma ' \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$.

In particular, (9.67) in definition 9.4.3 makes sense.

Proof. Let

\[ \eta _{\tau }=\sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}} \left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right)=\sup _{\tau -\Gamma '_{\max }<\delta <\Gamma _{\max }}\left(\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right) \]

for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. Set

\[ Z=\left\{ x\in X: \Gamma _{\upsilon }(x)=-\infty \, \forall \upsilon \in \mathbb {R}\right\} \cup \left\{ x\in X: \Gamma '_{\upsilon }(x)=-\infty \, \forall \upsilon \in \mathbb {R}\right\} . \]

It follows from proposition A.2.11 that for any $x\in X\setminus Z$, we have

\[ \eta ^*_ t(x)=\Gamma ^*_ t(x)+\Gamma '^*_ t(x) \]

for all $t>0$. The same trivially holds when $x\in Z$, so the equation holds everywhere. In particular, by corollary A.2.4 and proposition 1.2.11, we have

\[ \eta _{\tau }=\left(\Gamma ^*+\Gamma '^*\right)^*_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ') \]

when $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$.

We now check that $\Gamma +\Gamma '$ is a test curve. The family $\tau \mapsto \eta _\tau $ is decreasing, and the monotonicity of the $P$-envelope proposition 3.1.24 3 gives the decreasingness of $\Gamma +\Gamma '$. If $\tau =(1-s)\tau _0+s\tau _1$, then for arbitrary $\delta _0,\delta _1\in \mathbb {R}$ and $\delta =(1-s)\delta _0+s\delta _1$,

\[ \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\geq (1-s)(\Gamma _{\delta _0}+\Gamma '_{\tau _0-\delta _0})+s(\Gamma _{\delta _1}+\Gamma '_{\tau _1-\delta _1}). \]

Taking suprema and using proposition 3.1.24 gives the concavity of $\Gamma +\Gamma '$.

Fix $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$. Choose $\delta \in \mathbb {R}$ with $\delta <\Gamma _{\max }$ and $\tau -\delta <\Gamma '_{\max }$. Then

\[ \eta _\tau \geq \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }. \]

By 9.1.3, $\Gamma _\delta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$. Hence $\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }$ has positive $(\theta +\theta ')$-mass by the multi-linearity of non-pluripolar products proposition 2.2.2 6. It follows from the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8 that $\eta _\tau \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$. By corollary 3.1.12,

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_\tau =P_{\theta +\theta '}[\eta _\tau ] \]

is a model potential. Finally, $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{-\infty }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ')$ by proposition 1.2.2; since it dominates $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_\tau $ for any $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$, it has positive mass by another application of the monotonicity theorem theorem 2.4.8. Thus $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$.

Next, assume that $\Gamma $ and $\Gamma '$ are $\mathcal{I}$-model. Fix $\tau <\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }$. Then for each $\delta \in \mathbb {R}$ such that $\delta <\Gamma _{\max }$ and $\tau -\delta <\Gamma '_{\max }$, we know that $\Gamma _\delta \in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$ by 9.1.3. It follows from example 7.1.7 that $\Gamma _\delta $ and $\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }$ are both $\mathcal{I}$-good, hence so is $\Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$ by proposition 7.2.1. These sums are bounded above by $0$, so $\eta _{\tau }$ is $\mathcal{I}$-good by proposition 7.2.3. Since $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')_\tau =P_{\theta +\theta '}[\eta _\tau ]$, we conclude that $\Gamma +\Gamma '$ is $\mathcal{I}$-model. $\square$

Proposition 9.4.5. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma ' \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$. Then $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$. Moreover,

9.68
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sumGammaGammap} (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{-\infty }=P_{\theta +\theta '}\left[\Gamma _{-\infty }+\Gamma '_{-\infty }\right]. \end{equation}

When $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$, we have $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$.

The operation $+$ is commutative and associative.

Proof. It follows immediately from 9.4.4 that $\Gamma +\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$. It lies in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta +\theta ')_{>0}$ if $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$.

We argue (9.68). By definition, for any small enough $\tau $, we have

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{-\infty }\geq (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{2\tau }\succeq _ P \Gamma _{\tau }+\Gamma '_{\tau }. \]

Letting $\tau \to -\infty $ and applying proposition 6.2.24 and theorem 6.2.25, we find that

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{-\infty }\succeq _ P \Gamma _{-\infty }+\Gamma '_{-\infty }. \]

On the other hand, for each small enough $\tau $, we have

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\tau }\sim _ P \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}}\left( \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right) \preceq _ P \Gamma _{-\infty }+\Gamma '_{-\infty } \]

by proposition 6.1.19 and proposition 6.2.24. We apply proposition 6.2.24 again, we conclude that

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{-\infty } \preceq _ P \Gamma _{-\infty }+\Gamma '_{-\infty }. \]

So (9.68) follows.

Finally, let us show that $+$ is commutative and associative. Commutativity follows from the symmetry of the defining supremum. Let $\Gamma ''\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta '')_{>0}$. Then we want to show that

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')+\Gamma ''=\Gamma +(\Gamma '+\Gamma ''). \]

First observe that

\[ \left((\Gamma +\Gamma ')+\Gamma '' \right)_{\max }=\left( \Gamma +(\Gamma '+\Gamma '')\right)_{\max }. \]

Fix $\tau $ less than this common value. We compute that

\[ \begin{aligned} & \left( (\Gamma +\Gamma ')+\Gamma ''\right)_{\tau }\\ =& P_{\theta +\theta '+\theta ''}\left[\sup _{\delta _1\in \mathbb {R}} \left((\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\delta _1}+\Gamma ''_{\tau -\delta _1}\right)\right]\\ \sim _ P & \sup _{\delta _1\in \mathbb {R}} \left((\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\delta _1}+\Gamma ''_{\tau -\delta _1}\right)\\ \sim _ P & \sup _{\delta _1,\delta _2\in \mathbb {R}} \left(\Gamma _{\delta _2}+\Gamma '_{\delta _1-\delta _2}+\Gamma ''_{\tau -\delta _1}\right), \end{aligned} \]

where in the last line, we applied proposition 6.2.24 and proposition 6.1.19. Similarly, for $\left( \Gamma +(\Gamma '+\Gamma '')\right)_{\tau }$, we get the same expression. The associativity follows. $\square$

Lemma 9.4.6. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\Gamma ' \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$. Then for any closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-forms $\omega $ and $\omega '$ on $X$, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\theta '+\omega +\omega '}[\Gamma +\Gamma ']=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]+P_{\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma ']. \]

Proof. Observe that

\[ \begin{aligned} P_{\theta +\theta '+\omega +\omega '}[\Gamma +\Gamma ']_{\max }=& \left(P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]+P_{\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma '] \right)_{\max }\\ =& \Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }. \end{aligned} \]

Take $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ less than this common value. We need to verify that

\[ (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\tau }\sim _ P \Bigl ( P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]+P_{\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma ']\Bigr )_{\tau }. \]

By definition, this means that

\[ \sup _{\tau -\Gamma '_{\max }<\delta <\Gamma _{\max }}\left(\Gamma _{\delta }+\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }\right)\sim _ P \sup _{\tau -\Gamma '_{\max }<\delta <\Gamma _{\max }}\left(P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma _{\delta }]+P_{\theta '+\omega '}[\Gamma '_{\tau -\delta }]\right). \]

This is a consequence of proposition 6.1.19 and proposition 6.1.20. $\square$

Proposition 9.4.8. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$ and $C,C'\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

  1. $(\Gamma +\Gamma ')+C=\Gamma +(\Gamma '+C)=(\Gamma +C)+\Gamma '$;

  2. $\Gamma +(C+C')=(\Gamma +C)+C'$.

Proof. 1 We first observe that

\[ \left((\Gamma +\Gamma ')+C\right)_{\max }=\left( \Gamma +(\Gamma '+C) \right)_{\max }=\left( (\Gamma +C)+\Gamma ' \right)_{\max }=\Gamma _{\max }+\Gamma '_{\max }+C. \]

Take any $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ less than this common value. We compute

\[ \begin{aligned} \left((\Gamma +\Gamma ')+C\right)_{\tau }=& (\Gamma +\Gamma ')_{\tau -C} = P_{\theta +\theta '}\left[ \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}}\left( \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -C-\delta } \right) \right],\\ \left( \Gamma +(\Gamma '+C) \right)_{\tau }=& P_{\theta +\theta '}\left[ \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}}\left( \Gamma _\delta +(\Gamma '+C)_{\tau -\delta } \right) \right]\\ =& P_{\theta +\theta '}\left[ \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}}\left( \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -C-\delta } \right) \right],\\ \left((\Gamma +C)+\Gamma '\right)_{\tau }=& P_{\theta +\theta '}\left[ \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}}\left( (\Gamma +C)_{C+\delta }+\Gamma '_{\tau -C-\delta } \right) \right]\\ =& P_{\theta +\theta '}\left[ \sup _{\delta \in \mathbb {R}}\left( \Gamma _\delta +\Gamma '_{\tau -C-\delta } \right) \right]. \end{aligned} \]

2 Observe that

\[ \left( \Gamma +(C+C') \right)_{\max }=\left( (\Gamma +C)+C' \right)_{\max }=\Gamma _{\max }+C+C'. \]

For any $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ less than this value, we have

\[ \left( \Gamma +(C+C') \right)_{\tau }=\Gamma _{\tau -C-C'}=\left( (\Gamma +C)+C' \right)_{\tau }. \]

This completes the proof. $\square$

\[ \psi _{\tau }=\operatorname{CE}\left( \rho \mapsto \Gamma _{\rho }\lor \Gamma '_{\rho } \right)_{\tau } \]

for all $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$. Thanks to proposition A.2.10, for any fixed $x\in X$, we have

\[ \psi _{\tau }=(\psi ^*_{\bullet })^*_{\tau }\in \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta )\cup \{ -\infty \} . \]

\[ \begin{split} \psi _{\tau }(x)=\sup \left\{ \lambda \Gamma _{\rho }(x)+(1-\lambda )\Gamma '_{\rho '}(x): \lambda \in (0,1),\rho ,\rho '\in \mathbb {R},\lambda \rho +(1-\lambda )\rho '=\tau \right\} \end{split} \]

Lemma 9.4.12. Let $\Gamma ,\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\omega $ be a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma \lor \Gamma ']=P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]\lor P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']. \]

\[ \left(P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma \lor \Gamma ']\right)_{\max }=\left( P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]\lor P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']\right)_{\max }=\Gamma _{\max }\lor \Gamma '_{\max }. \]

\[ (\Gamma \lor \Gamma ')_{\tau }\sim _ P \left(P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]\lor P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma '] \right)_{\tau }. \]

We need the formula (9.83) proved in the proof of 9.4.10:

\[ \begin{split} (\Gamma \lor \Gamma ')_{\tau }\sim _ P\operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}\left\{ \lambda \Gamma _{\rho }+(1-\lambda )\Gamma '_{\rho '}: \lambda \in (0,1),\rho ,\rho '\in \mathbb {R},\lambda \rho +(1-\lambda )\rho '=\tau \right\} . \end{split} \]

A similar result holds with $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]$ and $P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ']$ in place of $\Gamma $ and $\Gamma '$. So our assertion is a direct consequence of proposition 6.1.19 and proposition 6.1.20. $\square$

  2. for any $\tau < \sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{\max }$, we let

    \[ \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{\tau }\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i}_{\tau }. \]

Proposition 9.4.14. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be an increasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Then $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i}$ as defined in definition 9.4.13 lies in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Moreover, if $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ for all $i\in I$, then $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i}$ lies in $\mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ as well.

Moreover, we have

9.88
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Gammiminf} \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{-\infty }=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_{-\infty }. \end{equation}

Proof. Fix $\tau <\sup _ i\Gamma ^ i_{\max }$. Choose $i_0$ such that $\tau <\Gamma ^{i_0}_{\max }$. On the tail $i\geq i_0$, the potentials $\Gamma ^ i_\tau $ form an increasing net of model potentials of positive mass, uniformly bounded above by $0$. Hence proposition 3.1.28 shows that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_\tau = \operatorname *{sup}_{i\geq i_0}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_\tau \]

is model. Its mass is positive by monotonicity, since it dominates $\Gamma ^{i_0}_\tau $. The decreasingness and concavity in $\tau $ follow from the directedness of the net and the corresponding properties of the curves $\Gamma ^ i$. Thus $\operatorname *{sup}_{}{}\! {}^{*}_ i\Gamma ^ i$ is a test curve. If all $\Gamma ^ i$ are $\mathcal I$-model, the same tail argument and proposition 3.2.19 show that the supremum is $\mathcal I$-model.

It remains to argue (9.88). Fix any $i_0\in I$. Since the curves are decreasing and are extended as in 9.1.2, the value at $-\infty $ can be computed by taking the supremum over rational $\tau <\Gamma ^{i_0}_{\max }$.

By proposition 1.2.5, there is a pluripolar set $Z\subseteq X$ such that for any $x\in X\setminus Z$,

\[ \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{-\infty }(x)=\sup _{\mathbb {Q}\ni \tau < \Gamma ^{i_0}_{\max }}\left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_{\tau }\right)(x)=\sup _{\mathbb {Q}\ni \tau < \Gamma ^{i_0}_{\max },i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\tau }(x)=\sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{-\infty }(x). \]

So they are equal everywhere by proposition 1.2.6. $\square$

Lemma 9.4.15. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be an increasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Assume that $\omega $ is a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right]= \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]. \]

Proof. Observe that

\[ \left( P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right] \right)_{\max }= \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]\right)_{\max }=\sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }. \]

Fix $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ less than this common value.

It suffices to show that

\[ \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{\tau }\sim _ P \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[\Gamma ^{i}\right] \right)_{\tau }. \]

This is an immediate consequence of proposition 6.1.20. $\square$

Definition 9.4.16. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Let $\operatorname{Fin}(I)$ denote the net of non-empty finite subsets of $I$, ordered by inclusion. Then we define

9.89
\begin{equation} \label{eq:generalsupstestcurv} \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\coloneqq \operatorname *{sup}_{J\in \operatorname{Fin}(I)}{}\! {}^{*} \left( \bigvee _{j\in J} \Gamma ^ j\right). \end{equation}

Observe that by definition 9.4.9, we have

\[ \sup _{J\in \operatorname{Fin}(I)} \left( \bigvee _{j\in J} \Gamma ^ j\right)_{\max }=\sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }<\infty . \]

So (9.89) makes sense. In particular,

9.90
\begin{equation} \label{eq:testcursupmax} \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\right)_{\max }=\sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }. \end{equation}

It is clear that definition 9.4.16 extends both definition 9.4.13 and definition 9.4.9.

Proposition 9.4.17. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Then $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Moreover, if $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ for all $i\in I$, then so is $\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i$.

Finally, we have

9.91
\begin{equation} \label{eq:supsminfty} \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i \right)_{-\infty }=P_{\theta }\left[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_{-\infty } \right]. \end{equation}

Proof. The first assertion and the second follow from proposition 9.4.14 and corollary 9.4.11.

It remains to argue (9.91). For this purpose, it suffices to show that

\[ \left( \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i \right)_{-\infty }\sim _ P \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_{-\infty }. \]

For any $J\in \operatorname{Fin}(I)$, it follows from corollary 9.4.11 and proposition 6.1.20 that

\[ \left(\bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j\right)_{-\infty }\sim _ P \bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j_{-\infty }. \]

From this, applying proposition 3.1.28, proposition 6.1.20 and proposition 9.4.14, we conclude our assertion. $\square$

Lemma 9.4.18. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Assume that $\omega $ is a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^{i} \right]= \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]. \]

Proof. This is a direct consequence of 9.4.15 and 9.4.12. $\square$

We prove a version of Choquet's lemma.

Proposition 9.4.19. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Then there is a countable subset $I'\subseteq I$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i =\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i. \]

Proof. We may assume that $I$ is infinite.

Set

\[ M\coloneqq \sup _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }. \]

For each rational number $q<M$, apply Choquet's lemma proposition 1.2.3 to the locally uniformly bounded above family

\[ \left\{ \left(\bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j\right)_ q:J\in \operatorname{Fin}(I)\right\} \subseteq \mathrm{PSH}(X,\theta ). \]

We obtain a countable subfamily $\mathcal J_ q\subseteq \operatorname{Fin}(I)$ such that

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{J\in \operatorname{Fin}(I)}{}\! {}^{*}\left(\bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j\right)_ q = \operatorname *{sup}_{J\in \mathcal J_ q}{}\! {}^{*}\left(\bigvee _{j\in J}\Gamma ^ j\right)_ q. \]

Let

\[ I'\coloneqq \bigcup _{q\in \mathbb Q, q<M} \bigcup _{J\in \mathcal J_ q}J. \]

Then $I'$ is countable and, for every rational $q<M$,

\[ \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\right)_ q= \left(\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\right)_ q. \]

Moreover, $\sup _{i\in I'}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }=M$, since the last equality is non-trivial for every rational $q<M$. Let $\Gamma '=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I'}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i$. Then clearly, $\Gamma '\leq \Gamma $. We claim that they are actually equal.

Thanks to proposition 6.1.4 and 9.1.3, it suffices to show that for any $\tau <\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i_{\max }$, we have

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma '_{\tau }\right)^ n=\int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma _{\tau }\right)^ n. \]

The equality is already known for all rational $\tau <M$. Since both

\[ \tau \mapsto \log \int _ X\theta _{\Gamma '_\tau }^ n,\quad \tau \mapsto \log \int _ X\theta _{\Gamma _\tau }^ n \]

are continuous on $(-\infty ,M)$ by proposition 9.1.4, the equality of masses holds for every $\tau <M$. $\square$

Proposition 9.4.20. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a non-empty family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Let $C\in \mathbb {R}$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} (\Gamma ^ i+C)=\operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*} \Gamma ^ i+C. \]

Suppose that $(\Gamma '^ i)_{i\in I}$ is another family in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$ satisfying (9.87). Suppose that $\Gamma ^ i\leq \Gamma '^ i$ for all $i\in I$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma ^ i\leq \operatorname *{sup}_{i\in I}{}\! {}^{*}\Gamma '^ i. \]

Proof. This is immediate by definition. $\square$

Definition 9.4.21. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\lambda >0$, we define $\lambda \Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\lambda \theta )_{>0}$ as follows:

  1. We set

    \[ \left(\lambda \Gamma \right)_{\max }=\lambda \Gamma _{\max }; \]

  2. for any $\tau < \left(\lambda \Gamma \right)_{\max }$, we set

    \[ (\lambda \Gamma )_{\tau }=\lambda \Gamma _{\lambda ^{-1}\tau }. \]

Proposition 9.4.22. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ and $\lambda >0$. Then $\lambda \Gamma $ as defined in definition 9.4.21 lies in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\lambda \theta )_{>0}$. Moreover, if $\Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, then $\lambda \Gamma \in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\lambda \theta )_{>0}$.

We have

9.92
\begin{equation} (\lambda \Gamma )_{-\infty }=\lambda \Gamma _{-\infty }. \end{equation}

Proof. This is immediate by definition. $\square$

Proposition 9.4.23. Let $\Gamma \in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$, $\Gamma '\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta ')_{>0}$, $C\in \mathbb {R}$ and $\lambda ,\lambda '>0$, we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \lambda (\Gamma +\Gamma ')=& \lambda \Gamma +\lambda \Gamma ',\\ (\lambda \lambda ')\Gamma =& \lambda (\lambda '\Gamma ),\\ \lambda (\Gamma +C)=& \lambda \Gamma +\lambda C. \end{aligned} \]

\[ P_{\lambda \theta +\lambda \omega }[\lambda \Gamma ]=\lambda P_{\theta +\omega }[\Gamma ]. \]

Definition 9.4.25. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$. Assume that

9.96
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Gammaiinffinite1} \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{\max }>-\infty , \end{equation}

and

9.97
\begin{equation} \label{eq:massnondeg} \inf _{i\in I} \int _ X \left( \theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right)^ n>0,\quad \textup{for some }\tau < \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{\max }. \end{equation}

Then we define $\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i}\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ as follows:

  1. We set

    \[ \left( \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{\max }=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{\max }; \]

  2. for any $\tau < \left( \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right)_{\max }$, we let

Proposition 9.4.26. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.96) and (9.97). Then $\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i}\in \mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$.

Moreover, if $\Gamma ^ i\in \mathrm{PSH}^{\mathrm{NA}}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ for all $i\in I$, then so is $\inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i}$.

Proof. The first assertion is an immediate consequence of proposition 3.1.25, except for the positivity of the mass at $-\infty $. Choose $\tau _0$ as in (9.97). Applying proposition 3.1.26 gives

\[ \int _ X \left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^ i_{\tau _0}\right)^ n =\lim _{i\in I}\int _ X\left(\theta +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma ^{i}_{\tau _0}\right)^ n>0. \]

Hence the value at $-\infty $ has positive mass by monotonicity. The last assertion follows from proposition 3.2.18. $\square$

In general, it is not true that

\[ \left( \inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i} \right)_{-\infty }=\inf _{i\in I}\Gamma ^{i}_{-\infty }. \]

Lemma 9.4.27. Let $(\Gamma ^ i)_{i\in I}$ be a decreasing net in $\mathrm{TC}(X,\theta )_{>0}$ satisfying (9.96) and (9.97). Assume that $\omega $ is a closed smooth positive $(1,1)$-form on $X$. Then

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right]= \inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]. \]

Proof. First observe that

\[ \left( P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^{i} \right] \right)_{\max }= \left(\inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^{i} \right]\right)_{\max }. \]

Let $\tau \in \mathbb {R}$ be less than this common value. Then we need to show the following:

9.98
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Pthetaomega_inf} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{i\in I} \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]\sim _ P \inf _{i\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]. \end{equation}

Applying proposition 3.1.26 gives the mass convergence required in corollary 6.2.11. Hence $\Gamma ^ i_{\tau }\xrightarrow {d_ S}\inf _{j\in I} \Gamma ^ j_{\tau } $. Thanks to corollary 6.2.19 and corollary 6.2.11, we have

\[ P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]\xrightarrow {d_ S} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \inf _{j\in I} \Gamma ^ j_{\tau } \right],\quad P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ i_{\tau } \right]\xrightarrow {d_ S} \inf _{j\in I} P_{\theta +\omega }\left[ \Gamma ^ j_{\tau } \right]. \]

Hence, (9.98) follows from proposition 6.2.4. $\square$
