Local energy moments

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 9.10 Local energy moments

To solve $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u)^ n=\mu $ for a measure $\mu $ which need not be absolutely continuous, we approximate $\mu $ by the measures $\mu _ j$ obtained by averaging $\mu $ over the cubes of finer and finer grids, and we solve the equations for $\mu _ j$ by theorem 9.9.2. Each $\mu _ j$ has a bounded density, but the bounds are not uniform in $j$, so the arguments of the preceding section do not apply.

A bounded subsolution provides a substitute. If $\mu \le (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ for a bounded plurisubharmonic function $w$, then $\mu $ integrates the powers $(-\varphi )^ p$ of plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi $ with zero boundary values, with a bound in terms of the local energy moments $\int (-\varphi )^ p(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n$ (theorem 9.10.2). The cube averages inherit this control (proposition 9.10.3). In the subsections that follow, this control replaces the common density bound. It yields convergence of the energy integrals and convergence in capacity of the solutions for $\mu _ j$, and hence the solution of the Dirichlet problem for compactly supported measures $\mu $ of this kind (theorem 9.10.12). The section closes with an inequality for mixed energies (theorem 9.10.13), which is needed in section 9.11. Throughout this section, $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ is a ball, $n\geq 1$.

Definition 9.10.1. Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, be a ball. We denote by $\mathcal E_0(B)$ the class of bounded plurisubharmonic functions $\varphi \leq 0$ on $B$ such that

\[ \lim _{B\ni z\to \zeta }\varphi (z)=0\quad (\zeta \in \partial B), \qquad \int _ B(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n<\infty . \]

For $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$ and real $p\geq 1$, the local energy moment of order $p$ is

\[ e_ p(\varphi )=\int _ B(-\varphi )^ p(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n. \]
This class is due to Cegrell; see section B.9. The zero function belongs to $\mathcal E_0(B)$. Since $\varphi $ is bounded and its Monge–Ampère measure has finite mass, every moment $e_ p(\varphi )$ is finite. Since $\partial B$ is compact, the boundary limit is uniform: for every $s>0$ there is a compact set $K_ s\subset B$ such that $\varphi >-s$ on $B\setminus K_ s$. Indeed, every $\zeta \in \partial B$ has an open neighborhood $N_\zeta $ with $\varphi >-s$ on $N_\zeta \cap B$; finitely many of these neighborhoods cover $\partial B$, and we may take for $K_ s$ the complement of their union in $\overline B$. In particular, each set $\{ \varphi \leq -s\} $, $s>0$, lies in a compact subset of $B$.

Theorem 9.10.2. Let $B\subset \mathbb C^ n$ be a ball, and let $\mu $ be a finite positive Borel measure on $B$ such that $\mu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$ for some bounded plurisubharmonic function $w$ on $B$. Put $m=\mu (B)$ and $M=\sup _ Bw-\inf _ Bw$, and for real $p\geq 1$ put

\[ \kappa _{n,p}=2^{n+p}(n+1)!M^ n, \qquad A_ p=\Bigl (1+\frac pn\Bigr )\kappa _{n,p}^{p/(n+p)}m^{n/(n+p)}. \]

Then, for every $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$, every real $p\geq 1$ and every $t>0$,

9.42
\begin{equation} \label{eq:local-energy-measure-tail} \mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} ) \leq \min \{ m,\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )t^{-(n+p)}\} . \end{equation}

Consequently,

9.43
\begin{equation} \label{eq:local-energy-measure-moment} \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu \leq A_ p\, e_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}. \end{equation}

The constant $A_ p$ depends only on $n$, $p$, $m$ and $M$.

Proof. We prove the tail bound by applying Błocki's estimate (proposition 9.3.5) to truncations of $\varphi $, and then integrate it. For $s>0$ let

\[ h_ s=\max \{ \varphi ,-s\} ,\qquad d_ s=h_ s-\varphi =(-\varphi -s)_+. \]

By the remark following definition 9.10.1, there is a compact set $K_ s\subset B$ with $\varphi >-s$ on $B\setminus K_ s$, and since $K_ s$ is compact there is a ball $B_ s\Subset B$, concentric with $B$, which contains $K_ s$. Thus $h_ s=\varphi $ on $B_ s\setminus K_ s$, which is a collar of $\partial B_ s$, and $d_ s$ vanishes outside $K_ s$. The functions $\varphi $, $h_ s$ and $w$ are bounded and plurisubharmonic near $\overline{B_ s}$, $\varphi \le h_ s$, and the oscillation of $w$ on $B_ s$ is at most $M$. Błocki's estimate on $B_ s$, together with $\mu \leq (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n$, therefore gives

\[ \int _ B d_ s^{n+1}\, \mathrm d\mu \leq \int _{B_ s}d_ s^{n+1}(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}w)^ n \leq (n+1)!M^ n\int _{B_ s}d_ s(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n. \]

Now take $s=t/2$. On $\{ \varphi <-t\} $ we have $d_ s>t/2$. On the other hand, $0\le d_ s\le -\varphi $, and $d_ s=0$ where $-\varphi \le t/2$; since $p\geq 1$, we have $-\varphi \le (t/2)^{1-p}(-\varphi )^ p$ where $-\varphi >t/2$, and hence

\[ \int _{B_ s}d_ s(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n \leq \int _{\{ -\varphi >t/2\} }(-\varphi )(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\varphi )^ n \leq (t/2)^{1-p}e_ p(\varphi ). \]

Combining the two estimates and dividing by $(t/2)^{n+1}$, we obtain

\[ \mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )\le (n+1)!M^ n(t/2)^{-n-p}e_ p(\varphi )=\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )t^{-(n+p)}. \]

Since also $\mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )\le m$, this proves (9.42).

If $m=0$, both sides of (9.43) vanish. If $\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )=0$, then (9.42) shows that $\mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )=0$ for every $t>0$, so the left side of (9.43) vanishes. Otherwise put

\[ T=\left(\frac{\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )}m\right)^{1/(n+p)}. \]

The first equality below is the layer-cake formula, which follows from Tonelli's theorem applied to the nonnegative function $(z,t)\mapsto pt^{p-1}\mathbf1_{\{ \varphi (z)<-t\} }$ on $B\times (0,\infty )$. Using (9.42) with the bound $m$ for $t<T$ and the second bound for $t\ge T$, we obtain

\[ \begin{aligned} \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu & =p\int _0^\infty t^{p-1}\mu (\{ \varphi <-t\} )\, \mathrm dt \leq mT^ p +p\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )\int _ T^\infty t^{-n-1}\, \mathrm dt\\ & =mT^ p+\frac pn\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )T^{-n}. \end{aligned} \]

By the choice of $T$,

\[ mT^ p=\kappa _{n,p}e_ p(\varphi )T^{-n}=\kappa _{n,p}^{p/(n+p)}m^{n/(n+p)}e_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}, \]

so the right-hand side equals $(1+p/n)mT^ p=A_ pe_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}$. This proves (9.43). $\square$

We now show that the cube averages of a compactly supported measure $\mu $ as in theorem 9.10.2 satisfy the same moment estimate up to a constant depending only on $n$ and $p$. We call the solutions $U_ j$ of $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ j)^ n=\mu _ j$ with zero boundary values constructed in the following proposition the canonical approximants of $\mu $ (for the chosen grids).

Proposition 9.10.3. Let $\mu $, $w$, $m$ and $A_ p$ be as in theorem 9.10.2, and assume moreover that the support $K$ of $\mu $ is a compact subset of $B$. Extend $\mu $ by zero to $\mathbb C^ n$. Let $h_ j\downarrow 0$, and for each $j$ let $\mathcal Q_ j$ be a partition of $\mathbb C^ n=\mathbb R^{2n}$ into half-open cubes of side length $h_ j$. Put

\[ \mu _ j=\sum _{Q\in \mathcal Q_ j}\frac{\mu (Q)}{|Q|}\mathbf1_ Q\, \mathrm dV, \]

where $|Q|=h_ j^{2n}$ is the volume of $Q$ and $\mathrm dV=\mathrm dV_{2n}$ is Lebesgue measure. For $z\in \mathbb C^ n$ and $r>0$ let $P(z,r)=\{ \zeta \in \mathbb C^ n:|\zeta _ k-z_ k|<r,\ 1\le k\le n\} $ be the polydisc of radius $r$ centered at $z$. Then $\mu _ j\to \mu $ weakly. Moreover, there are an index $j_0$ and, for every real $p\geq 1$, a constant $D_{n,p}>0$ depending only on $n$ and $p$, such that the following hold for all $j\geq j_0$ and $p\geq 1$.

  1. The measure $\mu _ j$ has a bounded density and compact support in $B$, and $\mu _ j(B)=m$.

  2. If $Q\in \mathcal Q_ j$ is a cube which meets $K$ and $z\in Q$, then $P(z,4\sqrt2\, h_ j)\subset B$, and every plurisubharmonic function $v\le 0$ on $P(z,4\sqrt2\, h_ j)$ satisfies

    9.46
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:canonical-grid-polydisc-average} \frac1{|Q|}\int _ Q(-v)^ p\, \mathrm dV \leq D_{n,p}(-v(z))^ p, \end{equation}

    where the right side is $+\infty $ if $v(z)=-\infty $.

  3. Every $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$ satisfies

    9.47
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:canonical-grid-energy-moment} \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu _ j \leq D_{n,p}A_ p e_ p(\varphi )^{p/(n+p)}. \end{equation}

  4. The bounded plurisubharmonic solution $U_ j$ of $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}U_ j)^ n=\mu _ j$ with zero boundary values, given by theorem 9.9.2, belongs to $\mathcal E_0(B)$ and satisfies $e_ p(U_ j)\leq (D_{n,p}A_ p)^{(n+p)/n}$.

Finally, the sequence $(U_ j)_{j\geq j_0}$ is relatively compact in $L^1(B)$, and the limit of every subsequence converging in $L^1(B)$ has a nonpositive plurisubharmonic representative.

Proof. If $m=0$, then $\mu _ j=0$ and $U_ j=0$ for all $j$, and all assertions hold. Assume that $m>0$, put $d=\operatorname {dist}(K,\mathbb C^ n\setminus B)>0$, and choose $j_0$ such that $h_ j<d/(6\sqrt{2n})$ for $j\geq j_0$; in the rest of the proof we consider only $j\geq j_0$. Since $\mu $ is a finite Borel measure, it is a Radon measure (proposition 1.1.2), so $\mu $ vanishes outside its support $K$. Hence a cube $Q$ with $\mu (Q)>0$ meets $K$, and as its diameter is $\sqrt{2n}\, h_ j<d/6$, it lies in the $(d/6)$-neighborhood of $K$, which is contained in $B$. These cubes are disjoint, each has volume $h_ j^{2n}$, and they lie in a bounded set; so there are only finitely many of them. Therefore $\mu _ j$ is a finite sum of measures with constant densities on cubes contained in $B$, so it has a bounded density and compact support in $B$, and $\mu _ j(B)=\sum _ Q\mu (Q)=m$. This proves (1).

For the weak convergence, let $g$ be a continuous function on $\mathbb C^ n$. Then

\[ \int g\, \mathrm d\mu _ j-\int g\, \mathrm d\mu =\sum _{Q\in \mathcal Q_ j}\int _ Q\left(\frac1{|Q|}\int _ Qg\, \mathrm dV-g\right)\mathrm d\mu , \]

and on every cube with $\mu (Q)>0$ the integrand is bounded by the modulus of continuity of $g$ at $\sqrt{2n}\, h_ j$ on the closed $(d/6)$-neighborhood of $K$. Hence the difference tends to zero.

To prove (2), fix a cube $Q\in \mathcal Q_ j$ which meets $K$ and a point $z\in Q$, and put $\varrho =4\sqrt2\, h_ j$. Every point of $Q$ differs from $z$ by less than $\sqrt2\, h_ j$ in each complex coordinate, so $Q\subset P(z,\varrho /2)$. Moreover $P(z,\varrho )$ lies in the Euclidean ball of radius $4\sqrt{2n}\, h_ j$ about $z$, and $\operatorname {dist}(z,K)\le \sqrt{2n}\, h_ j$, so this polydisc lies in the $(5d/6)$-neighborhood of $K$ and hence in $B$. Let $v\le 0$ be a plurisubharmonic function on $P(z,\varrho )$; if $v(z)=-\infty $, there is nothing to prove, so we assume that $v(z)>-\infty $. The function $\zeta \mapsto v(z+\varrho \zeta )$ is plurisubharmonic and nonpositive on the unit polydisc (proposition 8.2.3), with the finite value $v(z)$ at the origin. The first estimate of proposition 8.3.6, with its constant $C_{n,p}$, and the change of variables $\zeta \mapsto z+\varrho \zeta $, whose Jacobian is $\varrho ^{2n}$, give

\[ \int _ Q(-v)^ p\, \mathrm dV\le \int _{P(z,\varrho /2)}(-v)^ p\, \mathrm dV\le \varrho ^{2n}C_{n,p}^ p(-v(z))^ p. \]

Since $|Q|=h_ j^{2n}$ and $(\varrho /h_ j)^{2n}=32^ n$, we obtain (9.46) with $D_{n,p}=32^ nC_{n,p}^ p$.

For (3), let $\varphi \in \mathcal E_0(B)$. Since $\varphi \le 0$ is plurisubharmonic on $B$, (9.46) applies to $v=\varphi $ at every point $z$ of every cube with $\mu (Q)>0$. Integrating over $z\in Q$ with respect to $\mu $ and summing over the cubes, we obtain

\[ \int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu _ j =\sum _{Q\in \mathcal Q_ j}\frac{\mu (Q)}{|Q|}\int _ Q(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm dV \leq D_{n,p}\int _ B(-\varphi )^ p\, \mathrm d\mu , \]

and (9.47) follows from (9.43).

For (4), note that $\mu _ j$ equals $f_ j\eta ^ n$ for a bounded density $f_ j\ge 0$, so the solution $U_ j$ exists by theorem 9.9.2, applied with zero boundary values. Since $U_ j$ is subharmonic (theorem 8.2.1) with boundary limits $0$, the maximum principle (proposition 3.1.4) gives $U_ j\le 0$, and its Monge–Ampère measure has finite mass $\mu _ j(B)=m$; hence $U_ j\in \mathcal E_0(B)$. Taking $\varphi =U_ j$ in (9.47), we get

\[ e_ p(U_ j)=\int _ B(-U_ j)^ p\, \mathrm d\mu _ j\le D_{n,p}A_ pe_ p(U_ j)^{p/(n+p)}. \]

If $e_ p(U_ j)>0$, dividing by $e_ p(U_ j)^{p/(n+p)}$ gives the asserted bound; if $e_ p(U_ j)=0$, there is nothing to prove.

Finally write $B=B(a,R)$ and $\rho (z)=|z-a|^2-R^2$. The function $\rho $ is bounded and plurisubharmonic on $B$, and $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^ n=\eta ^ n=n!\pi ^{-n}\, \mathrm dV$. Applying theorem 9.10.2 to the finite measure $(\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\rho )^ n$ on $B$, with $p=2$, and using the uniform bound for $e_2(U_ j)$, we obtain $S=\sup _ j\int _ BU_ j^2\, \mathrm dV<\infty $. Since $U_ j\le 0$, theorem 8.3.4 shows that every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(B)$ to a plurisubharmonic function $u$; convergence to $-\infty $ is excluded by the $L^2$ bound. Exhausting $B$ by compact sets and passing to a diagonal subsequence, we may assume that $U_ j\to u$ almost everywhere on $B$, so Fatou's lemma gives $\int _ Bu^2\, \mathrm dV\le S$. For a compact set $L\subset B$, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\[ \int _{B\setminus L}|U_ j-u|\, \mathrm dV\le 2S^{1/2}|B\setminus L|^{1/2}, \]

which is small when $|B\setminus L|$ is small. Since $U_ j\to u$ in $L^1(L)$, it follows that $U_ j\to u$ in $L^1(B)$ along this subsequence. In particular, if a subsequence of $(U_ j)$ converges in $L^1(B)$, its limit is represented by such a function $u$. Finally $u\le 0$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, by the uniqueness assertion of theorem 8.2.1 applied to $\max \{ u,0\} $ and $0$. $\square$
