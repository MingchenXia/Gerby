Monge–Ampère energy and torsion

From Singularities in Hermitian pluripotential theory · 3 Monge–Ampère energy and torsion

Throughout this chapter, $\omega $ is a Hermitian form on $X$, and $\omega _ u=\omega +\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u$ and the space $\mathcal H_\omega $ of smooth strictly $\omega $-plurisubharmonic functions are as in chapter 2.

For a Kähler form, the Monge–Ampère operator is the gradient of an energy: there is a functional on $\mathcal H_\omega $ whose derivative at $u$ in every smooth real direction $v$ is $\int _ Xv\, \omega _ u^ n$. For a Hermitian form, such a primitive exists exactly when $\omega $ is closed. Symmetry of its mixed second derivatives first forces $\mathrm d(\omega _ u^{n-1})=0$, and varying the potential then forces $\mathrm d\omega =0$; this is stronger than the mass invariance of chapter 2. For a nonclosed background, integrating the Monge–Ampère one-form along radial segments still defines a functional, and its first variation shows which torsion terms enter the corresponding Euler equation. A different energy is available for Hermitian symplectic forms on fourfolds: along potential variations, this completion energy changes by minus the change of the volume. It is therefore constant along potential variations exactly when the mass is invariant, although even then the Monge–Ampère one-form has no primitive unless $\omega $ is closed.
