Bedford–Taylor products and local capacity · Tag 7E603FDE8D9A

Several independent factors

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 9.7.1 Several independent factors

Corollary 9.7.4 concerns products in which only two Hessians occur, each repeated several times. For a product $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n$ of $n$ arbitrary potentials, the coefficients $M_ k$ of lemma 9.7.1 are replaced by the mixed discriminant $D(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)$ of Hermitian matrices. Recall from the paragraph before lemma 7.2.2 that $D(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)$ is $1/n!$ times the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_ n$ in $\det (\sum _ jt_ jA_ j)$; it is symmetric and real multilinear, and $D(A,\ldots ,A)=\det A$ (lemma 7.2.2(1)). We prove the geometric-mean inequality for $D$ by means of a reverse Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, which follows from the signature of $D$ computed in lemma 7.2.3. Convolution then extends it to locally bounded potentials as before.

Lemma 9.7.5. Let $n\geq 1$. For positive semidefinite Hermitian $n\times n$ matrices $A_1,\ldots ,A_ n$,

\[ D(A_1,\ldots ,A_ n)\geq \prod _{i=1}^ n(\det A_ i)^{1/n}. \]

Proof. For $n=1$ both sides equal $A_1$. Let $n\geq 2$, and assume first that all $A_ i$ are positive definite. By lemma 7.2.2(3), $D$ is positive on positive definite matrices.

We first derive the reverse Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Fix positive definite matrices $A_3,\ldots ,A_ n$, and consider the symmetric bilinear form

\[ q(A,B)=D(A,B,A_3,\ldots ,A_ n) \]

on Hermitian matrices. Let $A,B$ be positive definite, put $c=q(A,B)/q(B,B)$ and $M=A-cB$. Then $q(M,B)=0$, so $q(M,M)\leq 0$ by lemma 7.2.3. Since $q(M,M)=q(A,A)-q(A,B)^2/q(B,B)$, we obtain

9.32
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mixed-discriminant-reverse-cauchy} q(A,B)^2\geq q(A,A)q(B,B). \end{equation}

Put $\widetilde A_ i=(\det A_ i)^{-1/n}A_ i$, so that $\det \widetilde A_ i=1$. By multilinearity it suffices to prove that $D(\widetilde A_1,\ldots ,\widetilde A_ n)\geq 1$. Consider the finitely many ordered $n$-tuples whose entries belong to the set $\{ \widetilde A_1,\ldots ,\widetilde A_ n\} $, and let $F>0$ be the smallest mixed discriminant of such a tuple. A tuple whose entries all equal $\widetilde A_ i$ has discriminant $\det \widetilde A_ i=1$, so $F\leq 1$. The multiplicity of a matrix in a tuple is the number of entries equal to it. Among the tuples with discriminant $F$, choose one for which the sum of the squares of the multiplicities is largest.

Suppose that this tuple contains two different matrices $A$ and $B$. By symmetry of $D$ we may place them in the first two slots, so that $F=q(A,B)$, where $q$ is formed with the remaining entries of the tuple. Replacing the pair $(A,B)$ by $(A,A)$ or by $(B,B)$ gives tuples with discriminants $q(A,A)\geq F$ and $q(B,B)\geq F$, whereas (9.32) gives $q(A,A)q(B,B)\leq F^2$. Hence $q(A,A)=q(B,B)=F$, and both new tuples are minimizing. If $A$ and $B$ have multiplicities $r$ and $s$, the two replacements change the sum of the squared multiplicities by $2(r-s)+2$ and $2(s-r)+2$, respectively. These two numbers add up to $4$, so one of them is positive, which contradicts the choice of the tuple. Therefore all entries of the chosen tuple are equal, and $F=1$. In particular $D(\widetilde A_1,\ldots ,\widetilde A_ n)\geq 1$.

Finally, for positive semidefinite $A_ i$, apply the inequality to $A_ i+\varepsilon I$ and let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Both sides are continuous: $D$ is a polynomial in the matrix entries, and $(\det A)^{1/n}$ is continuous on positive semidefinite matrices. $\square$

Corollary 9.7.6. Let $u_1,\ldots ,u_ n$ be locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions on an open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb C^ n$, $n\geq 1$, and let $f_1,\ldots ,f_ n\geq 0$ be continuous functions on $\Omega $ such that

\[ (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ i)^ n\geq f_ i\eta ^ n\quad (1\leq i\leq n), \qquad \eta =\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}|z|^2. \]

Then

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n \geq \left(\prod _{i=1}^ n f_ i^{1/n}\right)\eta ^ n. \]

Proof. For $n=1$ this is the hypothesis, so let $n\geq 2$. Put $u_{i,\varepsilon }=u_ i*\rho _\varepsilon $, with the same radial mollifiers for all $i$. By the last assertion of proposition 9.7.3, applied to each $u_ i$ with the density $f_ i$,

\[ \det \mathcal H(u_{i,\varepsilon })\geq (f_ i^{1/n}*\rho _\varepsilon )^ n\qquad (1\leq i\leq n). \]

By (9.2), applied to the smooth function $\sum _ it_ iu_{i,\varepsilon }$, the exterior powers of smooth forms satisfy

\[ \Bigl (\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}\sum _{i=1}^ nt_ iu_{i,\varepsilon }\Bigr )^ n=\det \Bigl (\sum _{i=1}^ nt_ i\mathcal H(u_{i,\varepsilon })\Bigr )\eta ^ n\qquad (t_1,\ldots ,t_ n\in \mathbb R). \]

Both sides are polynomials in $t_1,\ldots ,t_ n$. Since $2$-forms commute, the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_ n$ on the left is $n!\, \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{1,\varepsilon }\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n,\varepsilon }$, and on the right it is $n!\, D(\mathcal H(u_{1,\varepsilon }),\ldots ,\mathcal H(u_{n,\varepsilon }))\eta ^ n$. Since the Bedford–Taylor product of smooth functions is the exterior product of the forms $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{i,\varepsilon }$ (proposition 9.1.2(1)), we obtain

\[ \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{1,\varepsilon }\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_{n,\varepsilon } =D\bigl (\mathcal H(u_{1,\varepsilon }),\ldots ,\mathcal H(u_{n,\varepsilon })\bigr )\eta ^ n \geq \prod _{i=1}^ n(f_ i^{1/n}*\rho _\varepsilon )\, \eta ^ n, \]

where the inequality is lemma 9.7.5, applied to the complex Hessians of the smooth plurisubharmonic functions $u_{i,\varepsilon }$, which are positive semidefinite (proposition 8.2.2). As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, the density on the right converges locally uniformly to $\prod _ if_ i^{1/n}$, and the left side converges weakly to $\mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \mathrm{dd}^{\mathrm{c}}u_ n$ by theorem 9.2.5, since $u_{i,\varepsilon }\downarrow u_ i$ for each $i$ (proposition 8.2.2). $\square$
