Prerequisites and how to read this book

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · Prerequisites and how to read this book

We assume undergraduate real analysis, including the differential calculus of several real variables (in particular the inverse and implicit function theorems), Lebesgue integration with its convergence theorems and the change of variables formula, linear algebra, point-set topology and the elementary theory of holomorphic functions of one variable. Distribution theory, the functional analysis needed for weighted estimates and elliptic equations, holomorphic functions of several variables, the commutative algebra used for ideals of holomorphic germs, complex differential geometry and convex analysis are developed in the text. 1 establishes the linear theory and the theory of exceptional sets; 2 develops the local complex theory and then passes to singular potentials on compact manifolds. Each part opens with a short introduction describing its chapters.

Chapter A collects the facts about complex spaces, normalization and modifications that are needed for multiplier ideals and resolutions; it can be consulted when these tools first appear. Two existence theorems are used without proof: the resolution of singularities of complex spaces and the principalization of coherent ideals. They are stated precisely as theorem A.5.4, theorem A.5.5, and every consequence of them used in the book is proved. The historical and bibliographical notes in chapter B give the sources of the results and the precise versions of the cited works, with references to the corresponding statements in the text.

Our notation and normalizations follow [XiaSampleBook]. The global conventions are listed on the following pages; all other notation is introduced where it is first needed. A few definitions and constructions of [XiaSampleBook] are repeated here when a complete argument requires them, while the classification of singularities and its applications are left to that book.
