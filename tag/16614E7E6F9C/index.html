Variational Monge–Ampère equations

From Foundations of pluripotential theory · 17.4 Variational Monge–Ampère equations

We keep the big class of $\theta $, the model potential $\phi $ and its mass $m=\int _ X\langle \theta _\phi ^ n\rangle >0$, and write $E=E_\theta ^\phi $. We solve the equation $\mu _ u=\nu $ for non-pluripolar measures $\nu $ of mass $m$ (definition 17.4.1) by a variational method. By the tangent bounds, a potential $u$ with $\mu _ u=\nu $ maximizes $v\mapsto E(v)-\int _ X(v-\phi )\, \mathrm d\nu $. For the converse we perturb a maximizer $u$ in the direction of a continuous function $f$. Since $u+tf$ need not be $\theta $-psh, we replace it by its envelope $P_\theta (u+tf)$ and compute the derivative of the energy along these envelopes (proposition 17.4.3).

Maximizers exist by the compactness of corollary 17.3.4 when the integral term of the functional is continuous for $L^1$ convergence, as it is for measures with bounded densities. For an arbitrary non-pluripolar measure $\nu $ we replace the linear term by the exponential term $\lambda ^{-1}\int _ X\mathrm e^{\lambda v}\, \mathrm d\nu $, $\lambda >0$, which is continuous for $L^1$ convergence of potentials, and then let $\lambda \to 0$. The remaining subsections prove uniqueness (section 17.4.2) and the boundedness of $u-\phi $ for $L^ p$ densities (section 17.4.3), and derive two consequences of the existence theorem (section 17.4.4, section 17.4.5).
